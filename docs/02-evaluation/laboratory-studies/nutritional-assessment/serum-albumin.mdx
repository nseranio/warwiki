# Serum Albumin

Serum albumin is the **most widely used and most prognostic preoperative laboratory marker** in the surgical literature, including across reconstructive urology and urogynecology. It is also one of the **most misinterpreted** — albumin is a negative acute-phase reactant and falls in inflammation independent of nutritional status, so a low value reflects a composite of catabolism, inflammation, and surgical risk rather than a pure nutritional readout.

## Key Cutoffs

- **< 3.5 g/dL** — abnormal
- **< 3.0 g/dL** — severe nutritional risk (ACS NSQIP / AGS)
- **≤ 3.3 g/dL** — the threshold associated with **36.5% complication rate vs 10.4%** in a 200,015-patient plastic-surgery cohort

## In Reconstruction

- Strongest preoperative predictor of complications across major reconstructive series (radical cystectomy, free flap, autologous breast reconstruction, head-and-neck reconstruction).
- Hypoalbuminemia + obesity has a **compounding** effect on wound complications.
- Optimization before elective major reconstruction when timeline permits.
