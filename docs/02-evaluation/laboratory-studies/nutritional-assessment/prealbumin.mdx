# Prealbumin (Transthyretin)

Transthyretin is a liver-synthesized protein that transports thyroid hormone and complexes with retinol-binding protein. Its approximately two-day half-life explains rapid concentration changes, but does not make it a reliable measure of nutritional status or response to feeding.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Interpreting a Result

Reference intervals vary by assay and laboratory. A low result, sometimes defined as < 15 or < 20 mg/dL in individual studies, is **not a diagnostic cutoff for malnutrition**. Inflammation, liver disease, fluid balance and renal disease can alter the concentration; kidney dysfunction may increase it.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Prealbumin can remain normal despite severe nutritional deprivation. A low result may instead reflect acute or chronic disease, and improvement can accompany resolving inflammation. These limitations also apply to albumin.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Why Routine Nutrition Monitoring Is Inappropriate

ASPEN advises against using prealbumin as a proxy for body protein or muscle mass, or as a basis for changing nutrition support. Shorter half-life does not overcome the inflammatory confounding.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In adults receiving enteral nutrition, Davis and colleagues found that changes related to CRP rather than calorie or protein delivery.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

Monitor the actual nutrition plan: delivered intake, tolerance, weight and fluid context, physical findings, function and clinical recovery. Adding CRP does not turn prealbumin into a validated test of nutritional adequacy.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Prognostic and Exploratory Evidence

| Evidence | Finding | Practical limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Nichols 2020:** retrospective cohort of 926 ICU patients | Patients who died had a larger fall in prealbumin; baseline concentration was not associated with mortality or length of stay | A prognostic association does not establish benefit from targeting the concentration |
| **Kim 2023:** single-surgeon cohort of 69 lower-extremity free transfers for chronic wounds | Preoperative prealbumin < 20 mg/dL was not associated with flap healing or time to healing | Does not support serial prealbumin / CRP testing to time secondary flap procedures |
| **Dellière 2021:** retrospective derivation followed by small prospective groups stratified by CRP and transthyretin | Proposed thresholds of 0.17 and 0.12 g/L for nutritional classification | Assessed against file-based nutritional status; not a replacement for GLIM or an endorsement of routine prealbumin-directed care |

Sources: Nichols,<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Kim,<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> and Dellière.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Application to Reconstruction

- Diagnose and treat malnutrition using an accepted clinical assessment, not a low prealbumin result.
- Do not routinely order serial prealbumin to judge response to supplements, immunonutrition or parenteral nutrition.
- GLP-1 receptor agonist exposure alone does not establish an indication for serial prealbumin / CRP testing. Assess intake, weight trajectory, symptoms and operative risk directly.
- A prealbumin threshold should not determine whether a patient proceeds with reconstruction. Use the overall nutrition assessment, disease urgency and perioperative plan.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
