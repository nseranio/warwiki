# Transferrin

Transferrin is a liver-synthesized iron-transport protein with a half-life of about 7–8 days. Each molecule binds two ferric ions. Serum concentration depends on iron status, inflammation, hepatic function and protein losses, so transferrin is not a stand-alone test of nutritional status.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Iron Studies

- **Total iron-binding capacity (TIBC)** relates to transferrin concentration. It often rises in iron deficiency and falls with inflammation or impaired hepatic synthesis.
- **Transferrin saturation (TSAT)** is serum iron divided by TIBC, multiplied by 100, using compatible units.
- TSAT is interpreted with ferritin, CBC, the clinical context and the laboratory method. Iron overload and iron deficiency use different thresholds, and a single normal range does not answer every clinical question.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

Iron deficiency may raise transferrin while concurrent inflammation lowers it, so a normal concentration does not exclude deficiency. A low TIBC can also make calculated TSAT appear relatively high despite reduced circulating iron.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### Preoperative Anemia

ICCAMS recommends investigating the cause of anemia before surgery. Its iron-deficiency criteria include **ferritin < 30 ng/mL and/or TSAT < 20%**; with inflammation, **ferritin < 100 ng/mL** may indicate deficiency. These criteria define iron deficiency. They are not an instruction to give IV iron to every patient with a low TSAT. The panel states that IV iron is preferable to oral iron in preoperative iron-deficiency anemia, and the time available before surgery affects the choice.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

The [Iron & Ferritin](/docs/evaluation/laboratory-studies/nutritional-assessment/iron-ferritin) and [Preoperative Labs](/docs/evaluation/laboratory-studies/preoperative-labs) pages cover the wider workup. Dialysis, heart failure and other inflammatory diseases have their own iron-treatment frameworks, and their ferritin thresholds do not transfer to every surgical pathway.

### Soluble Transferrin Receptor

Soluble transferrin receptor (sTfR), sometimes combined with log ferritin as the sTfR/log ferritin index, can help when routine tests leave iron deficiency uncertain in an inflammatory setting. A prospective multicenter study supported its added value in separating iron-deficiency anemia from anemia of chronic disease.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> Assay availability, method-specific cutoffs and altered erythropoiesis limit interchangeability. sTfR is an adjunct to ferritin and TSAT, not a replacement.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Why It Is Not a Nutritional Marker

Like albumin and prealbumin, transferrin is a negative acute-phase protein. Iron deficiency can raise its concentration while inflammation or hepatic disease lowers it, obscuring nutritional interpretation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Historical studies illustrate this limitation:

- **Ingenbleek 1975:** transferrin had intermediate sensitivity for malnutrition, lower than the prealbumin/retinol-binding protein complex. Iron deficiency raises transferrin synthesis and protein deficiency lowers it, which partly invalidates it as an index of protein depletion alone.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Roza 1984:** correlation with body cell mass at the group level did not produce reliable individual classification; reported false-positive and false-negative rates were 60% and 31%. Transferrin did not change significantly after two weeks of parenteral nutrition in the treated subgroup.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Morlese 1997:** kinetic measurements in 14 children with severe protein-energy malnutrition showed only fair correlations with wasting indices. This is pediatric mechanistic evidence and not an adult operative decision rule.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

Nutrition is screened and assessed with weight and intake history, examination and an accepted clinical framework. Nutrition support is not titrated to transferrin concentration.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Liver Disease and Dialysis

A mechanistic study that included liver and serum samples from 40 patients with advanced liver disease linked transferrin with hepatic HNF4α activity. Serum transferrin correlated with hepatic transferrin expression (r = 0.51, p = 0.01).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> In a kinetic study of 11 alcoholic patients, transferrin synthesis was lower in the 5 with cirrhosis than in 6 controls (0.9 vs 1.8 mg/kg/h), and higher in the 6 with fatty liver.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> This is exploratory biomarker evidence and does not establish transferrin as a preoperative test of hepatic reserve.

In a 59-patient hemodialysis cohort, mean TIBC was 276, 217 and 176 mg/dL in well, moderately and poorly nourished patients by SGA (p &lt; 0.00001). The authors noted that a lower TIBC in poorly nourished patients can falsely raise calculated TSAT and mask iron deficiency.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Iron indices are read together, and TIBC alone does not diagnose nutritional status or guide iron prescribing.

## Comparison with Other Visceral Proteins

| Feature | Albumin | Prealbumin | Transferrin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Approximate half-life | 20 days | 2 days | 7–8 days |
| Negative acute-phase response | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Measures nutritional intake or muscle stores reliably | No | No | No |
| Important confounding | Distribution, dilution, synthesis and losses | Inflammation and renal / hepatic disease | Iron status, inflammation, hepatic synthesis and losses |
| Clinical use | Risk assessment in context | Selected prognostic information; no routine nutrition-monitoring target | Iron-status assessment as part of a panel |

Laboratory-specific reference intervals apply. A short half-life does not make a visceral protein a better therapeutic target.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Reconstructive Relevance

Transferrin is rarely the right marker for a nutritional decision in reconstructive urology or urogynecology. Its use is in the preoperative iron-status workup.

- **Major reconstruction with expected blood loss** (cystectomy with diversion, complex prolapse repair, phalloplasty) commonly involves preoperative anemia. Iron deficiency is identified with ferritin and TSAT using the ICCAMS criteria above, and IV iron is preferred over oral iron when iron-deficiency anemia is confirmed.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Cancer survivors and chronic inflammatory disease** can have anemia of chronic disease with superimposed iron deficiency. The sTfR/log ferritin index can help when ferritin and TSAT are equivocal.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Hemodialysis patients undergoing reconstruction** need ferritin as well as TSAT, because low TIBC can falsely normalize TSAT.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Cirrhosis** lowers transferrin, and the value may add prognostic information about hepatic synthetic function. Its incremental value over albumin and INR is not established.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Malnutrition** is diagnosed with validated tools (NRS-2002, GLIM, SGA) and nutrition-focused examination, not with transferrin.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
