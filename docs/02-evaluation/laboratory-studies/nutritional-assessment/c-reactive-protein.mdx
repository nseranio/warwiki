# C-Reactive Protein (CRP)

CRP is a liver-synthesized **positive acute-phase protein**. It can support assessment of inflammation, but does not identify its source or diagnose malnutrition. High-sensitivity CRP has a separate role in longer-term cardiovascular risk assessment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Biology and Assays

CRP binds phosphocholine on microbial or damaged-cell surfaces and participates in complement and innate immune responses. IL-6 is a major driver of hepatic synthesis. Hepatic synthesis begins 6 to 8 hours after an inflammatory stimulus, and peak concentrations are reached at about 36 to 50 hours. Serum concentrations can rise up to 1000-fold above physiologic levels. The plasma half-life is approximately 19 hours, and levels fall as the stimulus resolves.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The lag means a very early sample can be falsely reassuring.

Standard CRP and hsCRP measure the same protein. High-sensitivity assays quantify lower concentrations more precisely; detection limits depend on the laboratory platform. Neither an absolute concentration nor a simple range reliably separates bacterial infection from viral disease or sterile postoperative inflammation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

For cardiovascular interpretation, hsCRP ≥ 2 mg/L is a risk-enhancing signal. A result obtained during infection, trauma or recovery from surgery should not be treated as the patient's stable cardiovascular baseline; repeat after the acute process resolves when clinically appropriate.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Postoperative Inflammation and Infection

CRP can help follow a clinical trajectory, but surgery itself raises it. In 51 patients after uncomplicated elective total hip arthroplasty, mean CRP was 13-fold above baseline on day 1 and peaked at 33-fold (10.7 mg/dL) on day 3. It was still 26-fold above baseline on day 7 and 1.9-fold on day 30. Procalcitonin peaked earlier and lower, on day 1 at about 3-fold, and never reached concentrations typical of bacterial infection.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> These figures come from arthroplasty. They are a reference for the shape of the response, not a normal curve for every pelvic or GU operation.

A persistently elevated or rising value may justify reassessment alongside symptoms, examination, vital signs, wound or drain findings and other tests. It does not mandate CT on a particular postoperative day, and a low value cannot safely exclude an early serious complication.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

SCCM/IDSA 2023 guidance for new ICU fever permits CRP or procalcitonin as an adjunct when bacterial infection probability is low to intermediate and no focus is apparent, with very low-certainty evidence. In a high-probability presentation, neither should be used to rule out bacterial infection. The guideline excludes severely immunocompromised patients; it does not establish that procalcitonin is unaffected by immunodeficiency.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Nutrition and Visceral Proteins

Inflammation can raise CRP while lowering albumin, prealbumin and transferrin. This explains part of their prognostic association, but measuring them together does not determine whether nutritional depletion is present.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

In a prospective study of 2,465 emergency department patients, elevated CRP (&gt; 20 mg/L) was the stronger correlate of low albumin (adjusted OR 10.51, 95% CI 7.51–14.72), compared with increased nutritional risk on NRS-2002 (adjusted OR 2.87, 95% CI 1.98–4.15). Each of the three independently predicted 30-day mortality.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> In 111 older men in a recuperative care unit, markers of inflammation accounted for 56% of the variance in prealbumin change and discharge protein intake for 6%. Monitoring prealbumin change was not an adequate substitute for a detailed nutritional assessment.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

| Pattern | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Low albumin, high CRP | Inflammation may contribute; assess nutrition and other causes of low albumin independently |
| Low albumin, normal CRP | Consider synthesis, losses, fluid balance and nutrition; this does not prove pure nutritional depletion |
| Normal albumin, high CRP | Inflammation may be present; normal albumin does not establish preserved nutritional stores |
| Both normal | Neither result excludes malnutrition or every inflammatory condition |

The 2025 GLIM update permits clinical disease burden to establish the inflammatory etiologic criterion. This is the etiologic criterion that distinguishes inflammation-associated from starvation-associated malnutrition. CRP can clarify uncertainty but is not mandatory. A diagnosis still needs an appropriate phenotypic criterion, such as weight loss, low BMI or reduced muscle mass. Albumin and prealbumin are not GLIM diagnostic criteria.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Cardiovascular Prevention

The 2025 ACC scientific statement supports broad hsCRP screening in primary and secondary cardiovascular prevention.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> This concerns long-term prevention and is not proof that adding hsCRP to every preoperative panel improves surgical outcomes or provides a new surgical-clearance threshold.

In JUPITER, 17,802 participants without known cardiovascular disease, with LDL < 130 mg/dL and hsCRP ≥ 2 mg/L, were randomized to rosuvastatin or placebo. The primary composite cardiovascular endpoint was reduced by 44% (HR 0.56, 95% CI 0.46–0.69). The often-quoted 47% reduction concerned the narrower composite of myocardial infarction, stroke or cardiovascular death.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> The trial did not test perioperative hsCRP screening.

An observational UK Biobank analysis of 448,653 participants without baseline ASCVD found that hsCRP &gt; 3 versus &lt; 1 mg/L was associated with a 34% higher risk of major adverse cardiovascular events, 61% higher cardiovascular death and 54% higher all-cause death after adjustment. This supports prognostic relevance but does not establish that CRP itself is a treatment target in reconstructive surgery.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

## CRP and Procalcitonin: Practical Differences

| Feature | CRP | Procalcitonin |
|---|---|---|
| Source during systemic inflammation | Predominantly hepatic synthesis | Extra-thyroid tissues contribute substantially during systemic bacterial inflammation; thyroid cells alone do not explain the septic response |
| Interpretation | Nonspecific inflammatory marker | May support bacterial-infection assessment but is not an independent diagnostic or exclusion test |
| Confounding | Tissue injury, inflammatory disease, liver dysfunction and timing | Tissue injury, kidney dysfunction, timing and other noninfectious causes |
| Cardiovascular use | hsCRP has an established risk-assessment role | No corresponding routine prevention role |

Interpret either marker within its clinical setting; do not assume fixed assay turnaround times, universal peaks or immunity to confounding.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Application to Reconstruction

- After cystectomy with diversion, complex prolapse repair, gender-affirming phalloplasty or vaginoplasty, posterior urethroplasty or free-flap reconstruction, CRP is used as a bedside trend marker for leak, abscess and surgical-site infection. A value that fails to fall after about postoperative day 3 to 4, or that rises, prompts clinical reassessment and imaging when the examination supports it. The kinetic reference is from arthroplasty, and no threshold has been validated for these operations.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- Investigate the suspected complication rather than treating the number.
- Use CRP with GLIM to classify malnutrition as inflammation-associated when a patient is being worked up for elective reconstruction.
- Assess malnutrition independently. Routine paired CRP / prealbumin testing is not a validated method to titrate nutrition or schedule flap surgery.
- Keep cardiovascular prevention assessment, including hsCRP in older patients before major elective reconstruction, separate from the immediate surgical evaluation. It informs statin decisions, not surgical clearance.
- An early infection, or severe hepatic dysfunction that impairs CRP synthesis, can produce a deceptively low result.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
