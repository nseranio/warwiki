# Nutritional Screening Tools

Four tools matter for the reconstructive practice. **MUST** and **NRS-2002** are the highest-yield screens; **MNA-SF** is the geriatric workhorse; **GLIM** is the **diagnostic** framework that follows a positive screen.

## At-a-Glance

| Tool | Type | Best in | Inputs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MUST** (Malnutrition Universal Screening Tool) | Screen | Surgical adults — highest pooled diagnostic accuracy (sensitivity 86%, specificity 89%) | BMI, unintentional weight loss, acute-illness-no-intake |
| **NRS-2002** (Nutritional Risk Screening) | Screen | ESPEN standard; broad inpatient surgical use | Nutritional status + disease severity (composite) |
| **MNA-SF** (Mini Nutritional Assessment — Short Form) | Screen | Geriatric surgical patients; AUC 0.83 | 6 items, < 5 min |
| **GLIM** (Global Leadership Initiative on Malnutrition) | **Diagnosis** | After positive screen | Phenotypic + etiologic two-step |

## GLIM Quick Reference

A formal malnutrition diagnosis requires **at least one phenotypic + at least one etiologic** criterion:

| Phenotypic (any 1) | Etiologic (any 1) |
|---|---|
| Unintentional weight loss ≥ 5% in 6 months (or > 10% beyond 6 months) | Reduced food intake or assimilation |
| Low BMI (< 20 if age < 70; < 22 if ≥ 70) | Disease burden / inflammation (acute or chronic) |
| Reduced muscle mass | — |

Severity grading uses weight loss, low BMI, and muscle-mass loss.
