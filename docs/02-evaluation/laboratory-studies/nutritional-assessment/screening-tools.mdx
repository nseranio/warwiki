# Nutritional Screening and Assessment Tools

Use a validated screening tool to identify nutritional risk, then assess the cause, severity and treatment needs. **Screening, malnutrition diagnosis and sarcopenia assessment are different steps.** Choose a tool suited to the population and local workflow; no single surgical cohort establishes a universal best tool for every reconstructive procedure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Screening Tools by Setting

| Tool | Typical setting | Core information | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MST** | Adult hospital or outpatient care | Unintentional weight loss and reduced appetite | Score ≥2 prompts comprehensive nutritional assessment |
| **MUST** | Adult community or hospital care | BMI, weight loss, acute disease effect | 0 low, 1 medium, ≥2 high risk; act according to the associated care pathway |
| **NRS-2002** | Hospitalized adults | Nutritional impairment, illness severity and age | ≥3 indicates nutritional risk; ≥5 denotes high risk in the ESPEN surgical framework |
| **MNA-SF** | Adults ≥65 years | Intake, weight, mobility, acute illness/stress, neuropsychological status, BMI or calf circumference | 12–14 normal, 8–11 at risk, 0–7 malnourished by this instrument |
| **PG-SGA Short Form** | Adult oncology outpatients | Patient-reported weight, intake, symptoms and activity | Use the validated screening version and its threshold; do not substitute a full PG-SGA intervention score |
| **mNUTRIC** | Critically ill adults | Age, APACHE II, SOFA, comorbidity count, hospital-to-ICU interval | 5–9 high nutrition risk; not a diagnosis of malnutrition |

The Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics recommends **MST for adult screening across settings**. Other validated tools remain useful in setting-specific pathways. Use one consistently and ensure a positive result leads to assessment and a care plan.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### MUST

BAPEN's tool scores BMI, unintentional weight loss over 3–6 months, and the acute disease effect. The latter applies when the patient is **acutely ill and has had, or is likely to have, no nutritional intake for more than five days**. BMI scoring is 0 above 20, 1 at 18.5–20 and 2 below 18.5 kg/m²; weight-loss scoring is 0 below 5%, 1 at 5–10% and 2 above 10%. Use the [original tool and management pathway](https://www.bapen.org.uk/pdfs/must/must_full.pdf).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

A 2024 systematic review found pooled MUST sensitivity/specificity of **84%/85% against SGA**, with accuracy varying by reference standard and study quality. A 2025 prospective cohort of **1,649 major abdominal-surgery patients** favored MUST for combined diagnostic/prognostic performance. These findings support its use but do not prove that it is superior for every GU reconstruction or that every score ≥1 requires delaying surgery.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### NRS-2002

Initial screening asks about BMI below 20.5, recent weight loss, reduced intake and severe illness. If indicated, complete the nutritional impairment and disease-severity scoring, adding one point at age ≥70. A score ≥3 identifies risk. The **ESPEN 2025 surgical guideline** includes NRS ≥5 among high metabolic-risk criteria; planned nutritional optimization and operative timing require the broader assessment, not the screening score alone.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### MNA-SF

The six-item form was developed for older adults and permits calf circumference when BMI cannot be obtained. The instrument's categories should trigger an appropriate assessment and intervention pathway. Sensitivity and specificity depend on population and comparator; avoid presenting one study's figures as universal performance. Obtain the current [MNA-SF form and guide](https://www.mna-elderly.com/mna-forms).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### Oncology Screening

ASPEN's **2024 systematic review** identified **six validated screening tools**, including MST, MUST, NRS-2002, NUTRISCORE, the Mini Nutritional Assessment and PG-SGA Short Form. It did not select PG-SGA Short Form as the only acceptable option. Use a tool validated in the intended oncology population and repeat screening when the clinical situation changes.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

The full scored **PG-SGA** includes clinician assessment and an intervention-triage score. Its **≥9 urgent-intervention threshold** must not be applied to the patient-only short-form screen; validated short-form thresholds differ by study and version.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Assessment and Diagnosis

### Subjective Global Assessment

**SGA** combines weight and intake history, gastrointestinal symptoms, function, disease burden and examination for muscle/fat loss or fluid accumulation. Categories are A (well nourished), B (moderate or suspected malnutrition) and C (severe malnutrition). Classification requires trained clinical judgment; a particular weight-loss percentage does not independently assign the category.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

SGA and GLIM are related assessment approaches, rather than interchangeable numerical scores. Use the diagnostic framework required by the clinical service and document the actual findings; a screening result alone does not establish a GLIM diagnosis.

### GLIM — 2025 Update

Following risk screening, GLIM diagnosis requires **at least one phenotype and one etiologic criterion**. Assess all five criteria when feasible; severity is based on the phenotypic abnormalities.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

| Phenotypic criteria | Etiologic criteria |
|---|---|
| Unintentional weight loss &gt;5% within six months or &gt;10% beyond six months | Intake ≤50% of requirements for &gt;1 week, any reduction for &gt;2 weeks, or impaired assimilation |
| Low BMI: &lt;20 if age &lt;70 or &lt;22 if ≥70; use appropriate regional thresholds | Inflammation or disease burden |
| Reduced muscle mass assessed using a validated method | At least one of the two etiologic criteria is required |

**Muscle strength is not a substitute for muscle mass.** If technical body-composition assessment is unavailable, use validated anthropometry or trained examination with awareness of adiposity, edema and reference-population limits. **CRP is not required for every GLIM diagnosis**: clinical disease context may establish inflammation; CRP helps when uncertainty remains.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

A negative initial screen does not override substantial clinical concern. In a 328-patient medical/surgical cohort, NRS-2002 missed 37 of 114 GLIM-defined cases; this supports further assessment when findings conflict with the screen, rather than an automatic imaging requirement for everyone.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## ICU: NUTRIC and Modified NUTRIC

The original **NUTRIC** combines age, APACHE II, SOFA, number of comorbidities, days from hospital admission to ICU admission, and IL-6. **mNUTRIC omits IL-6** and uses the remaining five variables. Mechanical-ventilation days are not an input; replacing APACHE II with SAPS 3 produces a studied modification, not the standard score.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

The usual high-risk ranges are **6–10 for original NUTRIC** and **5–9 for mNUTRIC**. These scores incorporate illness severity and prognosis; they do not measure muscle depletion or establish malnutrition. Associations between nutrition delivery and outcome in observational analyses do not prove that aggressively reaching a calorie target reduces mortality for every high-scoring patient.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

Use the ICU nutrition pathway, including hemodynamic stability, feeding tolerance, refeeding risk and appropriate reassessment. Do not prescribe feeding intensity from mNUTRIC alone.

## Sarcopenia Case-finding: SARC-F

SARC-F asks about strength, walking assistance, rising from a chair, climbing stairs and falls. The conventional **≥4** result triggers further assessment. It does **not** establish probable sarcopenia by itself: in EWGSOP2, that stage requires measured low strength. Its low-to-moderate sensitivity means a negative result cannot reliably rule out sarcopenia when clinical suspicion remains.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

The **AWGS 2025** update also retains SARC-F ≥4 among older-adult case-finding options, while emphasizing age-specific strength and muscle-mass assessment. See [Handgrip Strength](/docs/evaluation/laboratory-studies/nutritional-assessment/handgrip-strength) for the current frameworks.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

## Reconstructive Workflow

1. **Screen early enough to act** using the institution's validated tool; review weight trajectory, intake and barriers to eating.
2. **Assess a positive screen or persisting concern**, including nutrition-focused examination and an appropriate diagnostic framework.
3. **Assess strength and function separately** when sarcopenia or frailty is suspected. Use existing suitable imaging when it adds useful information rather than ordering a scan automatically.
4. **Treat identified problems and reassess.** Nutrition support and operative timing depend on severity, urgency, expected benefit and multidisciplinary judgment. A positive screen is not a fixed surgical contraindication.

Serum albumin, prealbumin and transferrin should not be used alone to diagnose malnutrition or judge feeding adequacy. They are affected by inflammation and disease physiology; see the [nutritional assessment overview](/docs/evaluation/laboratory-studies/nutritional-assessment), [albumin](/docs/evaluation/laboratory-studies/nutritional-assessment/serum-albumin) and [prealbumin](/docs/evaluation/laboratory-studies/nutritional-assessment/prealbumin) pages.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
