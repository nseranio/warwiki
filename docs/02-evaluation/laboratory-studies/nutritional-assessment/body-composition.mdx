# Body Composition Assessment

Body composition assessment measures fat, lean tissue, muscle size, bone mineral and body water. It can identify muscle depletion that weight or BMI misses. In reconstructive practice, its main role is to complement nutrition, strength and functional assessment when the result can inform care.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Low muscle mass on an image is not, by itself, a clinical diagnosis of sarcopenia. EWGSOP2 identifies probable sarcopenia through low strength, confirms it with low muscle quantity or quality, and uses physical performance to grade severity. The AWGS 2025 framework requires concurrent low strength and low muscle mass, with physical performance treated as an outcome rather than a severity category.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Choosing a Method

| Method | Main information | Useful application | Interpretation limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Anthropometry** | Weight trajectory, BMI, waist or calf circumference | Initial assessment; trained bedside assessment when equipment is unavailable | BMI does not distinguish muscle from fat; edema and adiposity complicate circumference measurements |
| **DXA** | Fat mass, appendicular lean soft tissue, bone mineral | Widely used muscle-mass estimate in sarcopenia assessment | Lean tissue includes water and nonmuscle tissue; scanner differences and fluid shifts affect results |
| **BIA** | Impedance-derived body-water and lean-mass estimates; phase angle | Portable assessment using an appropriate validated equation | Does not directly measure muscle; hydration, device and reference population matter |
| **CT** | Muscle cross-sectional area and attenuation; visceral/subcutaneous fat | Secondary analysis of a recent clinically indicated scan | Radiation precludes routine new scanning solely for this purpose; contrast and acquisition protocol affect measurements |
| **MRI** | Muscle volume and fat infiltration | Selected research or specialist assessment | Cost, access and protocol variation; technical capability does not establish a routine screening indication |
| **Ultrasound** | Regional muscle thickness, area and echogenicity | Bedside assessment by trained operators | Operator and site dependence; cutoffs and clinical validation remain less standardized |

These methods are not interchangeable. The 2026 ACR criteria rate DXA as usually appropriate for suspected sarcopenia and support opportunistic analysis of available CT scans; a recent suitable CT may avoid an additional DXA examination. ACR does not support ordering a new CT or MRI solely for routine initial sarcopenia assessment.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Compartment Models and Measurement Conditions

A two-compartment model divides the body into fat and fat-free mass. Three-compartment models additionally account for body water; four-compartment models incorporate bone mineral and residual components. More detailed models reduce dependence on fixed assumptions but do not eliminate biological or technical error. Validation in healthy volunteers or athletes does not establish the same accuracy in edema, renal failure or major postoperative fluid shifts.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

For serial assessment, document the device, equation, anatomical site, positioning, hydration and acquisition conditions. Changes between scanners or after substantial fluid administration can resemble tissue change. Avoid interpreting a single small change as confirmed nutritional recovery.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Bioelectrical Impedance and Phase Angle

BIA measures resistance and reactance to a small alternating current. Predictive equations estimate body compartments. Single-frequency, multifrequency, spectroscopy and segmental systems provide different outputs and cannot automatically share cutoffs.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

**Phase angle**, expressed in degrees, is derived from arctangent(reactance/resistance). It is associated with cellular and hydration characteristics, but is not a direct assay of membrane integrity or nutritional adequacy. The 2020 ASPEN validity guideline found insufficient evidence to recommend BIA or ultrasound for body-composition assessment across clinical populations; subsequent consensus use of validated methods in specified populations should not be confused with universal device accuracy.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

A systematic review of critically ill patients found an association between low phase angle and mortality (pooled RR 1.82, 95% CI 1.46–2.26). Certainty was low to very low and more than 40% of included studies had high risk of bias. Proposed sarcopenia cutoffs vary by study, population and measurement protocol.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

**There is no established universal phase-angle threshold for postponing or permitting reconstructive surgery.** Cardiac-surgery or ICU prognostic associations do not validate a 4.5°–5° clearance rule for urethroplasty, prolapse repair or gender-affirming surgery. If used locally, phase angle is an adjunct to a broader assessment.

## Opportunistic CT Muscle Assessment

An axial L3 skeletal muscle area can be indexed to height squared to obtain **SMI (cm²/m²)**. Muscle attenuation in Hounsfield units provides different information about tissue composition. Record the muscle group, level, segmentation method, reference population and contrast phase.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

The often-quoted 52.4 cm²/m² in men and 38.5 cm²/m² in women are historical oncology research cutoffs and not universal EWGSOP2 diagnostic thresholds or validated operative-clearance limits for every GU procedure. Other cancer studies use BMI-dependent or population-specific thresholds. Interpret low SMI as an imaging finding and assess strength before diagnosing clinical sarcopenia.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

A meta-analysis of 38 studies including 7,843 patients with solid tumors associated low muscle mass with higher hazards of all-cause death (HR 1.44, 95% CI 1.32–1.56) and cancer-specific death (HR 1.93). These are observational hazard associations across heterogeneous definitions, not absolute survival losses of 44% or 93%, and not procedure-specific complication estimates.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

When L3 is unavailable, another vertebral level may be usable with a validated method. A systematic review found substantial methodological variability and insufficient evidence for a definitive interchangeable alternative; do not apply an L3 cutoff directly to T4 or T12.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

## Sarcopenia, Malnutrition and Obesity

These conditions overlap but answer different clinical questions:

- **Sarcopenia:** assess strength together with muscle quantity. Use the selected consensus framework consistently; see [Handgrip Strength](/docs/evaluation/laboratory-studies/nutritional-assessment/handgrip-strength).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **GLIM malnutrition:** at least one phenotypic criterion plus one etiologic criterion. Reduced muscle mass is one possible phenotype; weight loss or low BMI may provide another. The 2025 update explicitly excludes grip strength as a substitute for muscle mass. Validated anthropometry or trained physical assessment can be used when technical measurement is unavailable, accounting for sex, population and adiposity. CRP is helpful when inflammatory status is uncertain, but is not mandatory.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **Sarcopenic obesity:** impaired muscle function and reduced muscle mass together with excess adiposity. Normal BMI with low SMI alone does not establish sarcopenic obesity.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

CT or MRI can quantify visceral adipose tissue, while DXA and BIA provide different estimates. In a 4,558-person UK Biobank comparison, the association with MRI-derived visceral fat was stronger for DXA (R² 0.94) than BIA (R² 0.49); these are coefficients of determination and not correlation coefficients. Visceral-fat measurements alone do not determine operative eligibility.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

## Application Before and After Reconstruction

Use a suitable existing staging or surveillance scan when muscle assessment is clinically relevant. Combine it with intake and weight history, examination, [strength](/docs/evaluation/laboratory-studies/nutritional-assessment/handgrip-strength), mobility and [frailty assessment](/docs/foundations/perioperative-care/preoperative-assessment/frailty). An abnormal result should prompt assessment of reversible contributors and appropriate dietetic or rehabilitation input. It does not create an automatic waiting period or a requirement to normalize SMI before surgery.

After bowel-based urinary reconstruction, evaluate persistent acidosis and other bone-health risks. Bone-density DXA and whole-body composition DXA are distinct examinations. Select bone-density testing according to fracture risk and clinical indications; there is no universal annual DXA requirement established by the body-composition literature. See [Renal Function & Metabolic Surveillance](/docs/evaluation/laboratory-studies/renal-function-metabolic-surveillance) and [Vitamin D](/docs/evaluation/laboratory-studies/nutritional-assessment/vitamin-d).
