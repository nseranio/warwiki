# Body Composition Assessment

Quantitative muscle-mass measurement is the **muscle-mass phenotypic criterion** of GLIM and the basis for diagnosing **sarcopenia**. Sarcopenia is a strong, independent predictor of postoperative complications in the surgical literature — distinct from BMI, which can mask sarcopenia in the obese ("sarcopenic obesity").

## Modalities (GLIM-preferred order)

| Method | Strength | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| **CT-based skeletal muscle index (SMI) at L3** | Gold standard; often **already available** from staging scans in oncologic patients (bladder cancer pre-cystectomy, renal cell carcinoma, prostate cancer) | Requires CT; software for quantification not universal |
| **Bioelectrical impedance analysis (BIA)** | Portable, inexpensive, widely available; reasonable in outpatient clinic | Hydration-dependent; less accurate in obese or fluid-overloaded patients |
| **Dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA)** | Accurate; widely validated | Less routine preoperatively; radiation; not portable |
| **Anthropometrics + trained physical exam** | Always available; GLIM-endorsed when imaging unavailable | Lower accuracy; operator-dependent |

## CT-SMI Cutoffs (commonly cited)

- **Men: SMI < 52.4 cm²/m²**
- **Women: SMI < 38.5 cm²/m²**
- Population-specific thresholds (Prado, Martin, EWGSOP2) — anchor to your local reference

## In Reconstruction

- **Pre-cystectomy CT staging** routinely captures L3 — quantify SMI when the radiology workflow supports it.
- Pre-free-flap workup — sarcopenia in head-and-neck flap patients predicts failure.
- Pre-major-reconstruction in oncologic populations (cystectomy, exenteration, RPLND).
