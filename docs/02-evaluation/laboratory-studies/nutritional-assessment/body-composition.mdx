# Body Composition Assessment

Body composition assessment measures fat, lean tissue, muscle size, bone mineral and body water. It can identify muscle depletion that weight or BMI misses. In reconstructive practice, its main role is to complement nutrition, strength and functional assessment when the result can inform care.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Low muscle mass on an image is not, by itself, a clinical diagnosis of sarcopenia. EWGSOP2 identifies probable sarcopenia through low strength, confirms it with low muscle quantity or quality, and uses physical performance to grade severity. The AWGS 2025 framework requires concurrent low strength and low muscle mass, with physical performance treated as an outcome rather than a severity category.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Why Body Composition Matters Beyond BMI

BMI does not separate fat from lean mass or describe fat distribution. In a meta-analysis of 25 studies (31,968 patients), commonly used BMI cutoffs for obesity had a pooled sensitivity of 0.50 (95% CI 0.43–0.57) and specificity of 0.90 (0.86–0.94) for high body adiposity.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> Normal or high BMI also does not exclude muscle depletion. In a single-center CT series of 117 patients with pancreatic cancer, low SMI was found in 79.7% of those with normal BMI and in 50% of overweight patients.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> That cohort is not a GU population. It shows why a patient who looks well nourished by weight can still have low muscle mass, a finding that is reported as an imaging result and confirmed with strength testing.

## Choosing a Method

| Method | Main information | Useful application | Interpretation limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Anthropometry** | Weight trajectory, BMI, waist or calf circumference | Initial assessment; trained bedside assessment when equipment is unavailable | BMI does not distinguish muscle from fat; edema and adiposity complicate circumference measurements |
| **DXA** | Fat mass, appendicular lean soft tissue, bone mineral | Widely used muscle-mass estimate in sarcopenia assessment | Lean tissue includes water and nonmuscle tissue; scanner differences and fluid shifts affect results. ASPEN 2020 recommended DXA for fat mass but found the validity of DXA lean-mass measurement in clinical populations unknown<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **Air displacement plethysmography (BodPod)** | Body volume and density, converted to fat mass in a two-compartment model | Radiation-free whole-body fat estimate in stable outpatients | Needs dedicated equipment; assumes fixed fat-free mass density and hydration, which may not hold in illness, edema or older age |
| **BIA** | Impedance-derived body-water and lean-mass estimates; phase angle | Portable assessment using an appropriate validated equation | Does not directly measure muscle; hydration, device and reference population matter |
| **CT** | Muscle cross-sectional area and attenuation; visceral/subcutaneous fat | Secondary analysis of a recent clinically indicated scan | Radiation precludes routine new scanning solely for this purpose; contrast and acquisition protocol affect measurements |
| **MRI** | Muscle volume and fat infiltration | Selected research or specialist assessment | Cost, access and protocol variation; technical capability does not establish a routine screening indication |
| **Ultrasound** | Regional muscle thickness, area and echogenicity | Bedside assessment by trained operators | Operator and site dependence; cutoffs and clinical validation remain less standardized |

These methods are not interchangeable. The 2026 ACR criteria rate DXA as usually appropriate for suspected sarcopenia and support opportunistic analysis of available CT scans; a recent suitable CT may avoid an additional DXA examination. ACR does not support ordering a new CT or MRI solely for routine initial sarcopenia assessment.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Compartment Models and Measurement Conditions

A two-compartment model divides the body into fat and fat-free mass. Three-compartment models additionally account for body water; four-compartment models incorporate bone mineral and residual components. More detailed models reduce dependence on fixed assumptions but do not eliminate biological or technical error. A simplified four-compartment model that combines DXA and BIA takes about 10 minutes of patient time. In 23 healthy adults it matched the criterion four-compartment estimate of percent fat with R² 0.96, which does not establish accuracy in patients.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> Validation in healthy volunteers or athletes does not establish the same accuracy in edema, renal failure or major postoperative fluid shifts.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

For serial assessment, document the device, equation, anatomical site, positioning, hydration and acquisition conditions. Changes between scanners or after substantial fluid administration can resemble tissue change. Standardized conditions matter. In 32 athletic men, non-fasted afternoon testing changed fat-free-mass estimates substantially for BIA spectroscopy and for the three- and four-compartment models, and only slightly for DXA. DXA and air displacement remained acceptable when acute food and fluid intake stayed below 500 g.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> A single small change is not interpreted as confirmed nutritional recovery.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Bioelectrical Impedance and Phase Angle

BIA measures resistance and reactance to a small alternating current. Predictive equations estimate body compartments. Fat-free mass is a good conductor because of its water and electrolyte content, and fat is a poor one. Single-frequency systems (usually 50 kHz) estimate total-body impedance. Multifrequency systems and bioimpedance spectroscopy separate intracellular from extracellular water. Segmental systems measure arms, legs and trunk separately. These systems give different outputs and cannot automatically share cutoffs.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

**Phase angle**, expressed in degrees, is derived from arctangent(reactance/resistance). It is associated with cellular and hydration characteristics, but is not a direct assay of membrane integrity or nutritional adequacy. The 2020 ASPEN validity guideline found insufficient evidence to recommend BIA or ultrasound for body-composition assessment across clinical populations; subsequent consensus use of validated methods in specified populations should not be confused with universal device accuracy.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

A systematic review of critically ill patients found an association between low phase angle and mortality (pooled RR 1.82, 95% CI 1.46–2.26). Certainty was low to very low and more than 40% of included studies had high risk of bias. Proposed sarcopenia cutoffs vary by study, population and measurement protocol.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

Phase angle has been associated with outcomes in other populations, and each association is observational:

- In a systematic review of 13 studies (7,668 subjects), sarcopenia was associated with lower phase angle, and proposed cutoffs for identifying sarcopenia ranged from 4.05° to 5.05°.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- In 100 outpatients with cirrhosis, a phase angle of 4.6° or less was associated with more hospitalization (35% vs 11%), falls (41% vs 11%) and mortality (26% vs 3%) over 2 years.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- In 4,452 community-dwelling adults aged 65 or older, low phase angle (4.95° or less in men, 4.35° or less in women) was associated with incident disability over 24 months (HR 1.95, 95% CI 1.37–2.78).<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
- In a retrospective cohort of 858 patients having elective cardiovascular surgery, higher preoperative phase angle was independently associated with lower mortality after discharge (HR 0.91 per 0.1° increment).<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

No universal phase-angle threshold exists for postponing or permitting reconstructive surgery. The cardiac-surgery, cirrhosis and ICU associations do not validate a 4.5° to 5° clearance rule for urethroplasty, prolapse repair or gender-affirming surgery. If phase angle is used locally, it is an adjunct to a broader assessment, and serial values can track response to prehabilitation.

## Opportunistic CT Muscle Assessment

An axial L3 skeletal muscle area can be indexed to height squared to obtain **SMI (cm²/m²)**. Muscle attenuation in Hounsfield units provides different information about tissue composition. Record the muscle group, level, segmentation method, reference population and contrast phase.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

Reconstructive patients often already have a suitable scan: pre-cystectomy or pre-RPLND staging CT, pelvic imaging before exenteration, trauma CT in pelvic fracture urethral injury, or surveillance CT urography. Automated segmentation at the workstation is moving this analysis from research into clinical use.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

The often-quoted 52.4 cm²/m² in men and 38.5 cm²/m² in women are historical oncology research cutoffs and not universal EWGSOP2 diagnostic thresholds or validated operative-clearance limits for every GU procedure. Other cancer studies use BMI-dependent or population-specific thresholds. Interpret low SMI as an imaging finding and assess strength before diagnosing clinical sarcopenia.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

A meta-analysis of 38 studies including 7,843 patients with solid tumors associated low muscle mass with higher hazards of all-cause death (HR 1.44, 95% CI 1.32–1.56) and cancer-specific death (HR 1.93). These are observational hazard associations across heterogeneous definitions, not absolute survival losses of 44% or 93%, and not procedure-specific complication estimates.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

When L3 is unavailable, another vertebral level may be usable with a validated method. A systematic review found substantial methodological variability and insufficient evidence for a definitive interchangeable alternative; do not apply an L3 cutoff directly to T4 or T12.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

## Sarcopenia, Malnutrition and Obesity

These conditions overlap but answer different clinical questions:

- **Sarcopenia.** Strength is assessed together with muscle quantity, and the selected consensus framework is applied consistently; see [Handgrip Strength](/docs/evaluation/laboratory-studies/nutritional-assessment/handgrip-strength).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **GLIM malnutrition.** Diagnosis needs at least one phenotypic criterion and one etiologic criterion. Reduced muscle mass is one possible phenotype; weight loss or low BMI may provide another. The 2025 update states that handgrip testing should not be used as a surrogate for muscle mass. Calf or arm anthropometry and subjective muscle assessment by trained clinicians are acceptable when advanced devices are unavailable. CRP is recommended for confirmation when inflammatory status is uncertain, and clinical judgment may suffice otherwise.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **Sarcopenic obesity.** It combines impaired muscle function and reduced muscle mass with excess adiposity. Normal BMI with low SMI alone does not establish it.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

CT or MRI can quantify visceral adipose tissue, while DXA and BIA provide different estimates. In a 4,558-person UK Biobank comparison, the association with MRI-derived visceral fat was stronger for DXA (R² 0.94) than BIA (R² 0.49); these are coefficients of determination and not correlation coefficients. Visceral-fat measurements alone do not determine operative eligibility.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

## Application Before and After Reconstruction

Use a suitable existing staging or surveillance scan when muscle assessment is clinically relevant. Combine it with intake and weight history, examination, [strength](/docs/evaluation/laboratory-studies/nutritional-assessment/handgrip-strength), mobility and [frailty assessment](/docs/foundations/perioperative-care/preoperative-assessment/frailty). An abnormal result should prompt assessment of reversible contributors and appropriate dietetic or rehabilitation input. It does not create an automatic waiting period or a requirement to normalize SMI before surgery. Muscle assessment is most often considered before major bowel-based diversion, complex pelvic reconstruction, gender-affirming surgery and graft-based urethroplasty. Frailty assessment incorporates the result. The sources cited here do not support deferring surgery on body-composition data alone.

After bowel-based urinary reconstruction, evaluate persistent acidosis and other bone-health risks. Bone-density DXA and whole-body composition DXA are distinct examinations. Select bone-density testing according to fracture risk and clinical indications; there is no universal annual DXA requirement established by the body-composition literature. See [Renal Function & Metabolic Surveillance](/docs/evaluation/laboratory-studies/renal-function-metabolic-surveillance) and [Vitamin D](/docs/evaluation/laboratory-studies/nutritional-assessment/vitamin-d).
