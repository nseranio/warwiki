# Handgrip Strength

A bedside dynamometer measurement of grip force — **inexpensive, fast, and reproducible**. Grip strength is a validated **functional marker of nutritional status and sarcopenia**, predicts postoperative complications, prolonged LOS, and delayed ambulation across surgical populations, and is one of the easiest GLIM muscle-mass surrogates to deploy in clinic.

## Cutoffs (commonly cited)

- **Men < 27 kg** — abnormal
- **Women < 16 kg** — abnormal
- Population- and dynamometer-specific norms exist; reference EWGSOP2 / AWGS thresholds

## In Reconstruction

- Most evidence comes from GI / oncologic surgery, but the mechanism generalizes — sarcopenic patients have worse outcomes after any major operation.
- Especially valuable when CT or BIA-based muscle-mass measurement is unavailable (most outpatient clinics).
- Trends matter — serial measurements track response to prehabilitation.
