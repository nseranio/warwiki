# Thiamine (Vitamin B1)

Essential cofactor for **glucose metabolism** (pyruvate dehydrogenase, transketolase, α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase). Deficiency causes **Wernicke encephalopathy** (acute, reversible) and **Korsakoff psychosis** (chronic, often irreversible). The single most important context for the reconstructive practice is **refeeding syndrome** — administering carbohydrate to a thiamine-depleted patient acutely precipitates Wernicke.

## Key Test

- **Erythrocyte transketolase (ETK) activity coefficient** — > 25% increase after thiamine pyrophosphate addition indicates deficiency
- Whole-blood or RBC thiamine assays are also available
- In urgent / acute settings, **empiric repletion** is more important than waiting for lab confirmation

## In Reconstruction

- **Refeeding-syndrome risk** — give thiamine before starting nutrition support (PN or aggressive EN) in any patient who has had prolonged NPO, malnutrition, or significant weight loss.
- Alcoholism — universal screen and empiric repletion.
- Bariatric surgery (especially RYGB, BPD-DS, SG with persistent vomiting).
- Hyperemesis or significant postoperative emesis.
