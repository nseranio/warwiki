# Urogenital Physical Exam

A thorough urogenital examination is foundational in reconstructive urology. The physical exam confirms clinical suspicion, grades severity, establishes baseline anatomy, and often dictates operative approach before a single imaging study is ordered. This page covers the systematic examination of the male and female urogenital systems with particular attention to pelvic organ prolapse quantification (POP-Q), urethral assessment, and neurological evaluation of the pelvic floor.

---

## General Principles

- Examinations should be performed in a standardized position (lithotomy for females, supine/standing for males)
- Adequate lighting and exposure are mandatory — perineal pathology is routinely missed on cursory examination
- Document findings using validated, reproducible systems (POP-Q for prolapse; urethral caliber in French)
- Pelvic floor neurological exam should be performed in every patient with voiding dysfunction, incontinence, or pelvic floor descent

---

## Male Urogenital Exam

### External Genitalia

**Penis:**
- Inspect meatus: caliber, stenosis, hypospadias, epispadias, scarring, lichen sclerosus (white plaques at meatus or glans)
- Palpate urethra along ventral penile shaft: cord, nodule, or tender segment suggests periurethral fibrosis
- Prepuce: retractability, phimosis, paraphimosis, lichen sclerosus under foreskin
- Glans: erosions, tumors, condyloma, Balanitis Xerotica Obliterans (BXO/LS) — waxy white sclerotic patches

:::tip Lichen Sclerosus (BXO)
White sclerotic plaques involving the meatus, glans, or prepuce in any male presenting with stricture should prompt biopsy of the affected tissue. LS extends proximally in 20–40% of patients and will recur after urethroplasty if diseased tissue is not excised. Do **not** use penile skin as graft in LS patients.
:::

**Urethral meatus calibration:**
- Pass bougie-à-boule or calibration sounds to 24 Fr as screening for meatal/fossa stricture
- A meatus admitting ≥18 Fr is functionally adequate for voiding
- Document in French (Fr); 1 Fr = 0.33 mm

**Scrotum:**
- Transillumination for hydrocele
- Testicular size and consistency — atrophy, firmness (tumor), epididymal fullness (obstruction/epididymitis)
- Varicocele: Valsalva-enhanced palpation of spermatic cord in standing position

### Perineal Exam (Male)

- Inspect perineum for prior surgical scars (prior urethroplasty, fistula repair, perineal trauma)
- Palpate bulbar urethra through perineum: firmness or tenderness at the penobulbar junction may indicate spongiofibrosis
- Assess scrotal-perineal-urethral fistulae: small openings that may have been missed by the patient
- Perianal inspection: fistula-in-ano, warts, prolapsed hemorrhoids relevant to rectourethral fistula evaluation

### Digital Rectal Exam (Male)

- Prostate: size (estimated grams — normal 20–30 g), consistency, symmetry, tenderness, nodularity
- Prostatic floor: palpate for induration suggesting post-radiation fibrosis or cancer
- Sphincter tone: resting tone (internal anal sphincter — autonomic) and squeeze (external anal sphincter — somatic/pudendal)
- Bulbocavernosus reflex (BCR): see neurological section below

## Female Urogenital Exam

### External Inspection

**Vulva and perineum:**
- Atrophic changes: pallor, friability, loss of rugae (assess for hypoestrogenism)
- Lichen sclerosus: white atrophic patches, architectural distortion (fusion of labia minora, loss of clitoral hood)
- Scarring from prior repair, episiotomy, obstetric injury
- Fistula openings: may be visible at fourchette, perineal body, or vaginal wall

**Urethral meatus:**
- Caruncle vs. prolapse vs. stricture — urethral prolapse presents as annular red mucosal eversion
- Meatal stenosis: whitening, scarring, or inability to pass 14 Fr catheter
- Hypermobility: Q-tip test (see below)

### Vaginal Examination — Prolapse Assessment

Prolapse examination is best performed with the patient at maximum Valsalva (strain). The standard position is lithotomy or left lateral decubitus. All compartments must be assessed systematically.

**Anterior compartment:**
- Anterior vaginal wall descent: cystocele (most common prolapse in women)
- Grade by how far the leading edge descends relative to hymen

**Apical compartment:**
- Uterine descent or post-hysterectomy vaginal vault descent
- Enterocele: small bowel-filled apical prolapse — feels soft and reducible; may be seen on straining

**Posterior compartment:**
- Rectocele: posterior vaginal wall bulge; finger in vaginal and rectal canals simultaneously confirms
- Perineal body integrity: assess perineal body thickness (normal ≥3 cm from vaginal introitus to anal verge)

## POP-Q: Pelvic Organ Prolapse Quantification

The POP-Q system is the internationally standardized, reproducible method for grading pelvic organ prolapse adopted by the International Continence Society (ICS) in 1996. It uses the **hymen** as the fixed anatomical reference plane (zero point), with measurements in centimeters.

:::info Reference Plane
All POP-Q measurements are made relative to the hymen:
- **Negative values** = proximal (above the hymen, inside the vagina)
- **Zero** = at the hymen
- **Positive values** = distal (beyond the hymen)
:::

### The Nine POP-Q Measurement Points

| Point | Location | Normal Value | What It Represents |
|-------|----------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Aa** | Anterior vaginal wall, 3 cm proximal to urethral meatus | −3 cm | Anterior wall at fixed reference |
| **Ba** | Most distal position of anterior vaginal wall between Aa and apex | −3 cm | Leading edge of cystocele |
| **C** | Most distal edge of cervix or vaginal cuff (post-hysterectomy) | Variable | Apical descent |
| **D** | Posterior fornix (only in women with intact uterus) | Variable | Uterosacral ligament attachment |
| **Ap** | Posterior vaginal wall, 3 cm proximal to hymen | −3 cm | Posterior wall at fixed reference |
| **Bp** | Most distal position of posterior vaginal wall between Ap and apex | −3 cm | Leading edge of rectocele |
| **gh** | Genital hiatus — external urethral meatus to posterior midline hymen | 2–4 cm | Hiatal size |
| **pb** | Perineal body — posterior hymen to midanal opening | 2–4 cm | Perineal body integrity |
| **tvl** | Total vaginal length — with prolapse fully reduced | 8–10 cm | Vaginal capacity |

### How to Perform POP-Q

1. **Position**: Patient in lithotomy (dorsal) position, comfortable bladder fill
2. **Instruments**: Bivalve speculum or ruler; single-toothed tenaculum optional for cervical traction; centimeter ruler or calibrated probe
3. **Valsalva**: All measurements made at maximum reproducible straining — ask patient to "bear down as if having a bowel movement"
4. **Measure each point** sequentially using the hymen as zero reference
5. **Reduce prolapse** to measure tvl (total vaginal length)
6. **gh**: Measure from external urethral meatus to posterior midline hymen
7. **pb**: Measure from posterior midline hymen to midanal opening
8. **Record**: Use the 3×3 grid format for documentation

**Standard 3×3 POP-Q Grid:**

```
Aa  |  Ba  |  C
----|------|----
gh  |  pb  | tvl
----|------|----
Ap  |  Bp  |  D
```

### POP-Q Staging

| Stage | Definition |
|-------|-----------|
| **0** | No prolapse; Aa, Ba, Ap, Bp = −3; C or D ≤ −(tvl − 2) |
| **I** | Leading edge &gt;1 cm above hymen (above −1 cm) |
| **II** | Leading edge within 1 cm proximal or distal to hymen (−1 to +1 cm) |
| **III** | Leading edge &gt;1 cm below hymen but &lt;(tvl − 2) cm |
| **IV** | Complete vaginal eversion; leading edge ≥ (tvl − 2) cm |

:::tip Clinical Correlation
Stage II prolapse is the most common finding in parous women and does not necessarily require treatment. The decision to intervene is based on **symptom bother**, not stage alone. Stages III–IV are typically symptomatic and warrant surgical counseling.
:::

## Urethral Assessment in Women

### Q-Tip Test (Urethral Hypermobility)

The Q-tip test quantifies urethral hypermobility, which indicates deficiency of the pubourethral ligaments and anterior vaginal wall support — the anatomical basis for stress urinary incontinence.

**Technique:**
1. Place a lubricated cotton-tipped applicator into the urethra to the level of the urethrovesical junction (approximately 4–5 cm)
2. Measure the resting angle of the applicator from horizontal (0°)
3. Ask the patient to perform maximum Valsalva or cough
4. Measure the straining angle from horizontal
5. Calculate the **deflection angle** = straining angle − resting angle

**Interpretation:**
| Deflection | Interpretation |
|-----------|---------------|
| &lt;30° | No significant hypermobility — consider intrinsic sphincter deficiency (ISD) if incontinent |
| ≥30° | Urethral hypermobility — anatomic stress urinary incontinence |

A positive Q-tip test (≥30°) in a symptomatic woman supports the diagnosis of stress urinary incontinence and guides surgical approach (mid-urethral sling is appropriate). A flat Q-tip with severe incontinence points to intrinsic sphincter deficiency — low Valsalva leak point pressure (&lt;60 cmH₂O on UDS) confirms.

### Urethral Calibration

Pass progressively larger dilators (handheld or Hegar) to determine urethral caliber:
- **Normal female urethra**: ≥18–24 Fr
- **Stricture threshold**: &lt;14 Fr — pathognomonic for urethral stricture
- **14–18 Fr**: Borderline; correlate with symptoms and VCUG wine-glass sign
- Calibration should be performed under controlled conditions without forceful dilation

### Cough Stress Test

- Patient standing (preferred) or lithotomy with comfortably full bladder
- Single forceful cough
- Observe for immediate (synchronous) urinary leakage from the urethra
- **Positive**: Immediate leakage synchronous with cough = stress urinary incontinence
- **Delayed leak or continuous dribble** = urgency incontinence or detrusor overactivity — not a positive cough stress test

## Neurological Examination of the Pelvic Floor

Pelvic floor neurological integrity must be assessed in every patient with voiding dysfunction. The relevant neural pathways:

| Reflex Arc | Afferent | Efferent | Nerve | Clinical Test |
|-----------|---------|---------|-------|--------------|
| Bulbocavernosus reflex | Dorsal nerve of penis/clitoris | Pudendal nerve (S2–S4) | Pudendal | BCR |
| Anal wink reflex | Perianal skin | Pudendal (S2–S4) | Pudendal | Anal cutaneous reflex |
| Cremasteric reflex | Inner thigh skin | Genitofemoral (L1–L2) | Genitofemoral | — |
| Plantar reflex | Sole of foot | Corticospinal | Upper motor neuron | Babinski |

### Bulbocavernosus Reflex (BCR)

The BCR tests the integrity of the S2–S4 sacral reflex arc, which forms the neural substrate for the somatic pelvic floor, external urethral sphincter, and external anal sphincter.

**Technique (Male):**
1. With patient supine or in lateral decubitus, place a gloved index finger in the rectum
2. Squeeze the glans penis firmly (or catheter tug if catheterized)
3. Normal: palpable contraction of the external anal sphincter

**Technique (Female):**
1. Finger in rectum or two fingers in vagina
2. Tap or squeeze the clitoris
3. Normal: palpable contraction of the external anal sphincter or bulbocavernosus muscle

**Interpretation:**
- **Present BCR**: S2–S4 reflex arc intact; lower motor neuron injury not the cause of dysfunction
- **Absent BCR**: Lower motor neuron lesion at S2–S4 level (cauda equina, pudendal neuropathy, sacral cord) or spinal shock
- **BCR absence in a patient with stress incontinence**: Consider ISD from pudendal neuropathy (obstetric, radiation, chronic straining)

:::warning BCR in Spinal Cord Injury
In acute spinal cord injury (spinal shock), BCR may be transiently absent even with upper motor neuron lesions. BCR returns within 1–12 weeks in complete upper motor neuron injuries. Delayed return of BCR in the setting of pelvic fracture suggests pudendal nerve recovery after neuropraxia.
:::

### Anal Wink Reflex (Anocutaneous Reflex)

- Lightly stroke perianal skin with a cotton tip or pin
- Normal: visible contraction of external anal sphincter ("wink")
- Same S2–S4 arc as BCR — confirms intact sacral dermatome
- More sensitive for detecting subtle sacral nerve root injury than BCR

### Perineal Sensation

- Test light touch and pinprick in dermatomes S2 (posterior thigh/perineum), S3 (perineum/genitalia), S4 (perianal)
- Saddle anesthesia (S2–S5 numbness) = cauda equina pathology — requires urgent MRI spine
- Unilateral perineal hypoesthesia after pelvic surgery = pudendal nerve injury

### Levator Ani Assessment

- Insert two fingers vaginally (female) or perform bimanual pelvic exam
- Ask patient to contract ("squeeze as if stopping urine flow") and relax
- **Modified Oxford Scale** for levator ani strength:

| Grade | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 0 | No contraction |
| 1 | Flicker |
| 2 | Weak — no lift |
| 3 | Moderate — some lift and squeeze |
| 4 | Good — lifts fingers against resistance |
| 5 | Strong — holds for &gt;3 seconds against resistance |

- Document resting tone (hypertonicity = pelvic floor overactivity/pain) vs. hypotonicity (weakness, neurogenic)
- Levator tenderness on palpation: score 0–10 per side (levator ani syndrome, pudendal neuralgia)

## Genital Sensory and Vascular Assessment

### Penile/Clitoral Sensation

- Monofilament testing over dorsal penile shaft (dorsal nerve of penis, S2–S4)
- Biothesiometry (vibration threshold) if available — elevated thresholds suggest pudendal neuropathy
- Important baseline before urethroplasty in patients with pre-existing erectile dysfunction

### Peyronie's Disease Exam

- Measure erect penile length (preferably with intracavernosal vasoactive agent or after natural erection photograph)
- Palpate dorsal and lateral surfaces for Peyronie's plaques: firm, sometimes calcified nodules
- Estimate curvature direction and degree from patient-provided photographs
- Assess for hourglass deformity or hinge defect

## Integrated Examination Approach by Presentation

| Presentation | Key Exam Components |
|-------------|-------------------|
| Male urethral stricture | Meatal inspection, urethral palpation, meatal calibration, DRE |
| Female urethral stricture | Urethral calibration, VCUG (wine-glass), Q-tip test |
| Stress urinary incontinence (female) | POP-Q, Q-tip test, cough stress test, levator assessment |
| Pelvic organ prolapse | Full POP-Q with all 9 points, perineal body measurement, levator tone |
| Neurogenic bladder | BCR, anal wink, perineal sensation, perianal tone, Babinski |
| Rectourethral fistula | DRE, anoscopy, perineal inspection, fistula tract probe |
| Pelvic fracture urethral injury | Perineal inspection (hematoma, ecchymosis), urethral blood at meatus, DRE |
| Penile curvature (Peyronie's) | Plaque palpation, erect photograph, penile length, girth symmetry |
