## Contributions

Inouye's cited work covers extirpative surgery for the end-stage irradiated bladder in prostate cancer survivors, including pubovesical fistula with pubic bone osteomyelitis, and buccal graft reharvest for recurrent urethral stricture.[^4][^5][^6]

- **Extirpative surgery for the end-stage irradiated bladder** (2023). Senior author of a review of cystectomy with urinary diversion for urosymphyseal fistula, radiation cystitis and rectourethral fistula after prostate radiation, concluding that it is a safe option in selected patients.[^4] Cited on WARWIKI: [Primary Repair (No Flap) for USF](/docs/surgical-techniques/04h-fistula-repair/male/usf-primary-repair-no-flap), [Radiation and Hemorrhagic Cystitis](/docs/special-populations/05d-cancer-survivorship/radiation-hemorrhagic-cystitis).
- **Pubic symphysis resection and cystectomy for pubovesical fistula** (2022). Led a study of 18 prostate cancer survivors in which paired SF-12 physical composite scores rose from 30.6 to 45.5 after surgery and median annual opioid prescribing fell from 103.1 to 34.3 morphine milligram equivalents.[^5]
- **Buccal graft reharvest** (2022). Led a review of 5 ipsilateral reharvests from previously closed buccal sites in 4 men with recurrent stricture; median postoperative oral health scores were 0 of 56, the same as after first-time harvest.[^6]
