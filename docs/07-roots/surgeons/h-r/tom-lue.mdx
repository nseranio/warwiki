## Biography

Tom Lue arrived at UCSF expecting a one-year research fellowship before private practice.[^3] He turned from a long-term bladder neuroprosthesis project to penile physiology after finding that stimulation of certain pelvic nerve branches produced erection without the bladder and bowel responses of broader sacral stimulation.[^3] Emil Tanagho offered him a clinical instructor position at the end of the year.[^3]

Experiments in dogs and monkeys, along with studies of fresh human cadaver tissue, helped Lue and colleagues describe cavernous smooth muscle relaxation, arterial inflow, and venous compression in erection.[^3] After Giles Brindley's demonstration of injection-induced erection, Lue and Richard Schmidt explored intracavernous papaverine.[^3] An early patient had a prolonged erection, and an attempted reversal caused acute hypertension.[^3]

The physiology work led to clinical studies of penile blood flow and to operations for Peyronie's disease and priapism.[^3] In 1998, he and colleagues established a molecular urology laboratory at UCSF with visiting fellows and basic scientists.[^3]

## Key Publications

Lue's cited work examines penile erection anatomy and vascular physiology, erectile dysfunction treatment, and vascular surgery. His cited Peyronie's disease work describes venous patch grafting, tunical plication, and extra-tunical grafting.<sup>[P5](#publication-3)</sup><sup>[P6](#publication-4)</sup><sup>[P7](#publication-5)</sup><sup>[P8](#publication-6)</sup><sup>[P9](#publication-7)</sup>
