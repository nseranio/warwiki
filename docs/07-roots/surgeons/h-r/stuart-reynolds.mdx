## Training and career

<div className="sp-facts">

- **Current position** Program director, Clinical and Translational Research in Women's Urology, Division of Kidney, Urologic, and Hematologic Diseases, National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases (NIDDK), and program director for urologic training and career development programs (from 2025)[^1][^2]
- **Medical school** Penn State Hershey College of Medicine (2003)[^1][^2]

- **Fellowship** Urogynecology and reconstructive pelvic surgery, Vanderbilt University Medical Center (2011); MPH, Vanderbilt (2011)[^1]
- **Earlier posts** Assistant professor (2011–2018) and associate professor (2018–2024) of urology, Vanderbilt University Medical Center[^1]

- **Fellows trained** Fellowship director from 2020 for urology FPMRS fellows including Stephanie Gleicher, who graduated in 2023, and Rosa Park[^3][^4]

## Key Publications

His cited work comes from the Vanderbilt functional urology group: mesh complication surgery, onabotulinumtoxinA outcomes, sacral neuromodulation, and textbook chapters on slings and outlet obstruction after incontinence surgery.<sup>[P1](#publication-5)</sup><sup>[P2](#publication-6)</sup><sup>[P3](#publication-7)</sup><sup>[P4](#publication-8)</sup>

<ol className="sp-pubs">

<KeyPublication publication={publicationData.keyPublications[0]} citations={citations}>

**Obturator foramen dissection for transobturator mesh excision** (2012). Led a series of 8 women with intractable groin or obturator pain after transobturator mesh in which residual mesh was found and removed through a lateral groin incision in every case, and 5 were cured of pain or infection at a mean 6 months.

</KeyPublication>

<KeyPublication publication={publicationData.keyPublications[1]} citations={citations}>

**Urinary retention after onabotulinumtoxinA for overactive bladder** (2015). Senior author of a clinical-practice review of 160 patients in which 35% developed retention after a first injection; a preoperative postvoid residual of 100 mL or more was followed by retention in 94%.

</KeyPublication>
