## Biography

At Cardinal Hill Hospital in Kentucky, John Mulcahy treated patients with spinal cord injury and spina bifida, using intermittent catheterization and oxybutynin to manage continence.[^2] Visits to Hardy Hendron and Brantley Scott exposed him to reconstructive techniques and implant surgery.[^2] After moving to Indiana University, his interest in urologic prosthetics grew through courses, clinical trials, and referrals for complex cases.[^2]

When a penile implant infection arose during an operation, Mulcahy tried removing the device, washing the operative spaces, and replacing it in the same procedure, following an approach Scott had previously described.[^2] His first patient did well, and he repeated the method in subsequent infections.[^2] Other surgeons were concerned about the legal implications of placing a device in an infected field.[^2]

Over a decade he accumulated more than 100 salvage cases and reported an 84% success rate.[^2] He taught an AUA course on penile implant complications, returning with updates each year for 25 years.[^2]
