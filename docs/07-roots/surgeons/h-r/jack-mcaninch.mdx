## Principal Contributions

His UCSF team studied sonourethrography and magnetic resonance imaging for urethral injury and stricture evaluation.[^1] He created a genitourinary trauma database at San Francisco General Hospital in 1977.[^2] His textbook was titled Traumatic and Reconstructive Urology.[^1]

## The UCSF Fellowship

McAninch trained fellows in male genitourinary trauma and reconstruction at UCSF and San Francisco General beginning in 1989.[^2]

## Recognition

UCSF reported his retirement from the San Francisco General chief position in July 2013 after 36 years.[^2]
