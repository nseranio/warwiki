## Principal Contributions

Webster and Juan Ramón reported a sequential elaborated perineal repair in 74 patients with posterior urethral distraction defects after pelvic fracture.[^4] The reported maneuvers were distal urethral mobilization, corporal separation, inferior pubectomy, and supracrural urethral rerouting.[^4] At Duke, Webster developed video urodynamic work and bladder reconstruction strategies for neurogenic bladder.[^2] He described the transcorporal approach to artificial urinary sphincter implantation in the 1990s.[^2]

## The Duke Fellowship

Webster started the Duke fellowship in 1982 and reported training 32 fellows in consecutive years.[^2] Andrew C. Peterson, a 2003 Duke reconstructive fellow, later directed the Duke Reconstructive Urology and Genitourinary Cancer Survivorship Fellowship.[^5]
