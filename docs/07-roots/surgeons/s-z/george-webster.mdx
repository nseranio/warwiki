## Principal Contributions

Webster and Juan Ramón reported a sequential elaborated perineal repair in 74 patients with posterior urethral distraction defects after pelvic fracture.[^4] The reported maneuvers were distal urethral mobilization, corporal separation, inferior pubectomy, and supracrural urethral rerouting.[^4] At Duke, Webster developed video urodynamic work and bladder reconstruction strategies for neurogenic bladder.[^2] He described the transcorporal approach to artificial urinary sphincter implantation in the 1990s.[^2]

- **Progressive perineal repair of pelvic-fracture urethral defects** (1991). Developed a sequential perineal approach using additional maneuvers as needed to achieve tension-free urethral anastomosis.[^6] Cited on WARWIKI: [Combined Abdominoperineal Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/combined-abdominoperineal).
- **Neurogenic-bladder Hostility Score** (1992). Co-developed a urodynamic score used to assess upper-tract risk and outcomes after augmentation in children with myelodysplasia.[^7]
- **Transcorporal artificial urinary sphincter cuff placement** (2002). Developed a transcorporal cuff approach for artificial urinary sphincter implantation in complex revision cases.[^8] Cited on WARWIKI: [Artificial Urinary Sphincter (Procedure)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter).

## The Duke Fellowship

Webster started the Duke fellowship in 1982 and reported training 32 fellows in consecutive years.[^2] Andrew C. Peterson, a 2003 Duke reconstructive fellow, later directed the Duke Reconstructive Urology and Genitourinary Cancer Survivorship Fellowship.[^5]
