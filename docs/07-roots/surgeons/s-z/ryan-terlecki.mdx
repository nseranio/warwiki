## Contributions

Terlecki's cited work covers stricture location and the choice between excision and grafting in bulbar urethroplasty, urethral rest before anterior urethroplasty, and revision strategies for the artificial urinary sphincter.[^4][^5][^6]

- **Excision and primary anastomosis in the proximal bulb** (2010). Led a single-surgeon review of 112 bulbar urethroplasties: all 72 proximal bulbar strictures were treated by excision and primary anastomosis with 1 recurrence (1.4%), and among intermediate-length strictures recurrence was 3.0% after excision and primary anastomosis vs 38% after graft repair.[^4] Cited on WARWIKI: [Excision and Primary Anastomosis (EPA)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/excision-primary-anastomosis).
- **Urethral rest before anterior urethroplasty** (2011). Of 128 anterior urethroplasties, 28 men first had a median 3 months of urethral rest after recent manipulation; 75% then underwent focal or complete excision, and recurrence (14%) was similar to the rest of the cohort (10%).[^5] Cited on WARWIKI: [Principles of Urethral Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/urethral-reconstruction-principles).
- **Revision of the artificial urinary sphincter** (2022). Lead author, with Steven Wilson, of a review of cuff downsizing or relocation, pressure-regulating balloon changes, urethral wrapping, tandem cuffs and transcorporal placement for recurrent incontinence after sphincter implantation.[^6] Cited on WARWIKI: [Artificial Urinary Sphincter (Procedure)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter), [Artificial Urinary Sphincter (Device)](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/prosthetics/artificial-urinary-sphincter).
