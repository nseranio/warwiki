## Biography

Leonard Zinman's interest in genitourinary reconstruction grew during urology training at Massachusetts General Hospital, where Wyland Leadbetter and W. Hardy Hendren encouraged that direction.[^1] He joined Lahey Clinic in 1964, trained with Richard Turner-Warwick in London from 1967 to 1968, and founded Lahey's Center for Reconstructive Urologic Surgery in 1973; he remained at Lahey for more than 50 years.[^2][^1]

Zinman described his group's work as addressing radiation injuries and long, high-risk strictures with combinations of oral mucosa grafts, muscle or myocutaneous flaps and other graft materials.[^1] The same obituary records reconstruction for male genital cancer and adjunct use of injectable agents intended to limit fibrosis.[^1]

Zinman directed Lahey's residency program, later chaired urology, and helped establish the Society of Genitourinary Reconstructive Surgeons with Gerald Jordan, Charles Devine and Charles Horton.[^1] Colleagues remembered his persistent inquiry and his affection for patients and colleagues; he trained many residents.[^1]

## Key Publications

A 2020 conference abstract credited his group with gracilis muscle interposition for rectourethral fistula repair and with using gracilis muscle as a vascular bed for oral mucosa urethral grafts.[^3] The same abstract documented work on urethral squamous cell carcinoma, bladder reconstruction, and recalcitrant bladder neck contractures.[^3]
