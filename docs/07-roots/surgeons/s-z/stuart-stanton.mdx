## Biography

Stuart Stanton developed a urogynecology service for female urinary incontinence and pelvic floor disorders at St George's Hospital.[^2] Under the influence of Richard Turner-Warwick, he learned Burch colposuspension, and his research and influence helped make colposuspension widely used for stress urinary incontinence from the 1970s to the 1990s.[^2]

Stanton believed research was vital to the development of urogynecology.[^2] He helped found the International Continence Society and the International Urogynecological Association, worked on ICS standardisation committees, and convened the ICS annual scientific meeting in London in 1985.[^2] His department studied female incontinence and pelvic floor disorders while attracting trainees from Britain and abroad, many of whom went on to establish their own departments and research programs.[^2] Peter Dwyer described Stanton's department as a model for the units those trainees later built.[^2]

Dwyer, a former trainee, remembered a demanding mentor who expected high standards and was known for his humor and humanity.[^2] He described a busy theatre with observers and open discussion, where surgical work and teaching happened together.[^2] In later life Stanton moved to Jerusalem, continued to work in medicine, and regularly returned to London to visit family.[^2]

## Professional Work

- **UK urogynecology subspecialty training**. Set up the first UK subspecialty training program in urogynecology, according to the cited source.[^4]
- **Founding member of continence and urogynecology societies**. Was a founding member of the International Continence Society, International Urogynecological Association, and British Society of Urogynaecology.[^5]
