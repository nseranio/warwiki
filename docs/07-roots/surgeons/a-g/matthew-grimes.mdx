## Contributions

Grimes studies the pathophysiology of urethral stricture disease and lichen sclerosus, including stricture histology and collagen structure, and reports ureteral reconstruction outcomes in radiated and non-radiated patients.[^7][^8][^9]

- **Histopathology of anterior urethral strictures** (2019). Led a blinded pathology review of 100 urethroplasty specimens: 21% had two or more lichen sclerosus features and 44% showed chronic inflammation, which was largely absent from isolated bulbomembranous strictures (9%); the 11% failure rate did not differ with inflammation.[^7] Cited on WARWIKI: [Male Urethral Stricture](/docs/clinical-conditions/03b-voiding-outlet/urethral-stricture).
- **Ureteral reconstruction after radiation** (2022). In 47 consecutive single-surgeon ureteral reconstructions, radiated patients more often needed complex repairs (ileal ureter or autotransplantation; 35% vs. 7%), and success was 94% in radiated and 100% in non-radiated patients at a median follow-up of 30 months.[^8]
- **Collagen in lichen sclerosus** (2023). As senior author, showed greater collagen content and less homogeneous, more disorganized collagen structure in urethral, vulvar and foreskin tissue affected by lichen sclerosus than in unaffected tissue.[^9]
