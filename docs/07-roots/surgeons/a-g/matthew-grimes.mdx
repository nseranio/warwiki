## Key Publications

Grimes studies the pathophysiology of urethral stricture disease and lichen sclerosus, including stricture histology and collagen structure, and reports ureteral reconstruction outcomes in radiated and non-radiated patients.<sup>[P1](#publication-7)</sup><sup>[P2](#publication-8)</sup><sup>[P3](#publication-9)</sup>

<ol className="sp-pubs">

<KeyPublication publication={publicationData.keyPublications[0]} citations={citations}>

**Histopathology of anterior urethral strictures** (2019). Led a blinded pathology review of 100 urethroplasty specimens: 21% had two or more lichen sclerosus features and 44% showed chronic inflammation, which was largely absent from isolated bulbomembranous strictures (9%); the 11% failure rate did not differ with inflammation.

</KeyPublication>

<KeyPublication publication={publicationData.keyPublications[1]} citations={citations}>

**Ureteral reconstruction after radiation** (2022). In 47 consecutive single-surgeon ureteral reconstructions, radiated patients more often needed complex repairs (ileal ureter or autotransplantation; 35% vs. 7%), and success was 94% in radiated and 100% in non-radiated patients at a median follow-up of 30 months.

</KeyPublication>

<KeyPublication publication={publicationData.keyPublications[2]} citations={citations}>

**Collagen in lichen sclerosus** (2023). As senior author, showed greater collagen content and less homogeneous, more disorganized collagen structure in urethral, vulvar and foreskin tissue affected by lichen sclerosus than in unaffected tissue.

</KeyPublication>

</ol>
