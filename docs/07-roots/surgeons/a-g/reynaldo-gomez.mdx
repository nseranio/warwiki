## Contributions

Gomez's cited work covers posterior urethroplasty after pelvic fracture urethral injury, including bulbar artery sparing and the timing of repair, and dorsal buccal graft reconstruction of the female urethra.[^7][^8][^9][^10]

- **Bulbar artery sparing in pelvic fracture urethral injury repair** (2016). Described mobilizing the bulb from one side to preserve at least one bulbar artery during anastomotic repair; all 26 patients were voiding normally and stricture free at a mean follow-up of 20 months. His 2025 long-term series of 127 vessel-sparing non-transecting posterior repairs reported 95% success at a median of 43 months, with no cuff erosion in the 14 men who later received an artificial urinary sphincter.[^7][^8] Cited on WARWIKI: [Jordan Vessel-Sparing EPA](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/jordan-vessel-sparing-epa).
- **Timing of delayed posterior urethroplasty** (2018). Senior author of a 39-patient series in which repair at 6 weeks or sooner after pelvic fracture urethral injury had failure, incontinence and erectile dysfunction rates similar to repair at 12 weeks or later.[^9] Cited on WARWIKI: [Pelvic Fracture Urethral Injury (PFUI)](/docs/special-populations/05a-trauma-emergencies/pfui).
- **Female urethral reconstruction with dorsal buccal mucosa graft** (2020). Led a review and technique report on female urethral stricture that recommends grafting the entire urethra and gives a collected dorsal graft success rate of 86% at a mean of 21 months.[^10] Cited on WARWIKI: [Female Dorsal Onlay Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-dorsal-onlay-urethroplasty).
