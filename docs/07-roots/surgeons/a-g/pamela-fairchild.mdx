## Contributions

Her research covers recovery of pelvic floor function after childbirth, pelvic floor injury from childbirth, and surgical education.[^1] She coauthored the Fundamentals of Vaginal Surgery pilot study, which developed and validated a simulation system and proficiency score for basic vaginal surgical skills.[^3]

- **Colpopexy and colporrhaphy at hysterectomy for prolapse** (2016). Led a Michigan statewide database study of 1557 hysterectomies with prolapse as an indication; 43.1% were hysterectomy alone and 24.1% included a colpopexy, which was more likely when the surgeon specialized in urogynecology (odds ratio 8.2).[^4] Cited on WARWIKI: [Anterior Colporrhaphy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/anterior-colporrhaphy).
- **Normal pelvic floor recovery after high-risk vaginal birth** (2020). Led a prospective cohort of 82 women after vaginal births with risk factors for pelvic floor injury and 30 after cesarean; using posterior wall support, Kegel force and levator ani tear on imaging, 46.4% met criteria for pelvic floor injury at 6 weeks and 29.6% at 6 months.[^5]
- **Surgery in a postpartum pelvic floor clinic** (2020). Coauthored a review of 1138 patients seen in the Michigan Healthy Healing After Delivery clinic; 9.1% had surgery, most often anal sphincteroplasty, perineal laceration revision or rectovaginal fistula repair.[^6] Cited on WARWIKI: [Obstetric Perineal Lacerations](/docs/clinical-conditions/03c-pelvic-support/obstetric-perineal-injury).
