## Contributions

Her research covers sexual function after prolapse surgery, uterine preservation at prolapse repair, and complications of sacrocolpopexy. She was second author of a systematic review with clinical practice guidelines comparing uterine preservation with hysterectomy at prolapse surgery.[^4]

- **Vaginal dilators after posterior colporrhaphy** (2013). Led a randomized trial in 60 sexually active women; daily dilator use from weeks 4 to 8 did not change de novo dyspareunia rates, overall sexual function or global improvement scores at 3 and 6 months.[^5] Cited on WARWIKI: [Posterior Colporrhaphy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/posterior-colporrhaphy).
- **Sexual activity and function after prolapse surgery** (2020). Led a systematic review of 67 studies in which sexual function improved or was unchanged after every type of reconstructive prolapse surgery, and de novo dyspareunia ranged from 0% to 9% outside posterior repair.[^6] Cited on WARWIKI: [Posterior Compartment Prolapse](/docs/clinical-conditions/03c-pelvic-support/posterior-compartment).
- **Lumbosacral discitis after sacrocolpopexy** (2024). Reported 3 cases and reviewed 30 published cases; the complication followed abdominal, laparoscopic and robotic sacrocolpopexy, and most patients needed mesh excision together with prolonged intravenous antibiotics.[^7] Cited on WARWIKI: [Sacrocolpopexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrocolpopexy), [Sacrocolpopexy Tacker](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/sacrocolpopexy-tacker).
