## Contributions

Brant's cited work covers priapism shunting, artificial urinary sphincter erosion in the TURNS multicenter cohort and infection after inflatable penile prosthesis placement.[^4][^5][^6]

- **T-shaped shunt for ischemic priapism** (2009). Led a series of 13 men treated under local anesthesia with the T-shunt and intracavernous tunneling: cavernous blood flow was restored in all but 1, and all but 2 recovered erectile function.[^4] Cited on WARWIKI: [Priapism Shunts & Decompression](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/priapism-shunts-decompression).
- **Artificial urinary sphincter erosion** (2014). Led the TURNS prospective study of 386 sphincters placed at 8 institutions: 31 (8.0%) were explanted, with higher rates after prior radiation, prior UroLume stent placement and 3.5 cm cuffs.[^5] Cited on WARWIKI: [Artificial Urinary Sphincter (Device)](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/prosthetics/artificial-urinary-sphincter).
- **Inflatable penile prosthesis infection** (2025). Led an analysis of 18,475 US hospital implantations: the infection rate was 3.1% at a median follow-up of 3 years, and type 2 diabetes, spinal cord injury, obesity and concomitant circumcision were associated with higher rates.[^6] Cited on WARWIKI: [Penile Prosthesis Infection](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/penile-implants/infection), [Inflatable Penile Prosthesis](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/prosthetics/inflatable-penile-prosthesis).
