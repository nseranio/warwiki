## Biography

In a Norfolk operating room, Charles Devine and Charles Horton reconsidered how a hypospadias repair was being done.[^2] Gerald Jordan's memorial account describes that encounter as the start of years of reconstructive work together.[^2] The two surgeons developed operations for hypospadias, epispadias, Peyronie disease, genital trauma and phallic reconstruction.[^2] Devine also worked with his brother Patrick on urethral stricture reconstruction, including patch graft techniques.[^2]

Their work grew out of a local practice that also built a urology residency and helped establish Eastern Virginia Medical School, where Charles became the first department chair.[^2] In 1975 the Devine brothers founded the adult and pediatric genitourinary reconstructive surgery fellowship at EVMS.[^1] Devine, Patrick Devine and Horton held surgical teaching clinics in Norfolk where visiting urologists and plastic surgeons watched operations over periods that could last weeks.[^2] The clinics showed primary and complex hypospadias operations as well as repairs after genital trauma.[^2]

Devine carried those lessons to postgraduate courses and international meetings, explaining tissue handling across reconstructive problems.[^2] He was among the founders of the Society of Genitourinary Reconstructive Surgeons.[^2]

## Key Publications

Their work also addressed Peyronie disease, penile and genital trauma, and phallic reconstruction.[^2] Devine and his brother Patrick developed patch graft urethroplasty techniques for urethral stricture.[^2]
