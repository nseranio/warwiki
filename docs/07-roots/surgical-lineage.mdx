# Surgical Genealogy

**Coming soon — and we need your help to build it.**

The reconstructive subspecialties were built by tight networks of surgeons who trained one another across generations. **GURS** (Genitourinary Reconstructive Surgery) and **URPS** (Urogynecology & Reconstructive Pelvic Surgery) remain, more than most fields, apprentice–master traditions: technique passes from mentor to fellow in the operating room, not from textbook to reader. A complete map of those mentor–trainee relationships — who trained whom, where the named techniques originated, which dynasties produced today's leading surgeons — would make explicit what every fellow currently learns by word of mouth, and would give residents and applicants a tool to navigate the field that does not exist anywhere else.

We have started this work but we are not close to finished. Building it accurately requires institutional memory that lives in the surgeons themselves — fellowship rosters, dates, and the chain of teachers behind any given technique.

## How to help

If you trained in GURS or URPS, were trained by someone who should be profiled, or remember the lineage at your institution, we would like to hear from you. Concretely we are looking for:

- **Fellowship rosters** (GURS or URPS, any era) — names, years, program director.
- **Mentor–trainee links** that should appear on the tree, especially pre-2000.
- **Biographical detail** on individual surgeons — training pathway, signature techniques, institutional appointments.
- **Corrections** to anything we have already published elsewhere on the site.

Email **nseranio@gmail.com** or open an issue on the [GitHub repository](https://github.com/nseranio/warwiki/issues). Contributors will be credited.
