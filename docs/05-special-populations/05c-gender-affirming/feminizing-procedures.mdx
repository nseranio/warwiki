# Feminizing Procedures

Feminizing genital surgery encompasses procedures that eliminate male-typical anatomy and reconstruct feminine-appearing external and internal genitalia. Operations range from orchiectomy alone to complete vaginoplasty with vulvar reconstruction. Penile inversion vaginoplasty remains the most commonly performed and most studied technique.[^2][^3][^4]

## Orchiectomy

Bilateral orchiectomy is the removal of both testicles. It may be performed as a standalone procedure or as a component of vaginoplasty.

**As a standalone procedure:**
- Allows cessation of anti-androgen therapy, substantially reducing medication burden
- Permits lower estrogen doses with equivalent feminizing effect
- Shorter, lower-risk procedure compared to vaginoplasty — suitable for patients not seeking or not yet ready for vaginoplasty
- Performed through a single scrotal midline incision; bilateral spermatic cords are ligated and divided

**In preparation for vaginoplasty:**
- When performed as a separate first stage, scrotal skin is preserved for later use in neovaginal construction or labial formation
- Allows hormonal stabilization and confirmation of patient goals prior to more complex surgery

## Vaginoplasty

Vaginoplasty creates a vaginal canal and reconstructs external feminine genitalia. The primary techniques differ in the tissue used to line the neovaginal canal.

### Penile Inversion Vaginoplasty

Penile inversion is the established gold standard, used in the majority of primary vaginoplasties.[^2][^3][^4]

**Technique:**
- A neovaginal cavity is dissected between the rectum posteriorly and the urethra and bladder anteriorly in the rectovesical/rectourethral space
- Penile skin is inverted to line the canal; scrotal skin is deepithelialized and used to extend depth or form the labia majora
- The glans penis is used to construct a neoclitoris, preserving the dorsal neurovascular bundle to maintain erogenous sensation[^1]
- The urethra is shortened and repositioned at the introitus
- Labia minora are fashioned from residual penile frenulum or preputial tissue

**Neovaginal depth** typically ranges from 10–15 cm, though varies with preoperative penile and scrotal skin availability. Prior circumcision, prolonged pre-pubertal hormone use, or lichen sclerosus reduce available skin and may limit achievable depth.

**Preoperative requirement:** Complete depilation of penile shaft and scrotal skin selected for canal lining — typically 6–12+ months of laser or electrolysis treatment.

### Peritoneal Flap Vaginoplasty

Peritoneal (Davydov-type) vaginoplasty uses the peritoneal lining of the abdominal cavity — harvested laparoscopically or robotically — to augment or replace skin-lined canal depth.[^7]

**Advantages:**
- Self-lubricating mucosa (peritoneal secretions)
- Mean neovaginal depth ~14.7 cm; high satisfaction (96%) reported[^7]
- Preferred option when penile/scrotal skin is insufficient (post-puberty-blocker use, circumcision, limited genital tissue)

**Considerations:**
- Requires laparoscopic or robotic component, increasing operative complexity
- Most commonly used as an adjunct to penile inversion rather than standalone

### Intestinal Vaginoplasty

A segment of sigmoid colon or ileum is isolated on its mesenteric vascular pedicle and transposed to form the neovaginal canal.

**Advantages:**
- Provides spontaneous mucus secretion and self-lubrication
- Suitable depth independent of genital skin availability

**Disadvantages:**
- Requires bowel resection and reanastomosis — carries risk of anastomotic leak, bowel obstruction, and ileus
- Mucus discharge can be copious and odorous
- Reserved primarily for revision cases or patients in whom skin-based techniques are not feasible[^6]

### Vulvoplasty (Zero-Depth Vaginoplasty)

Vulvoplasty creates feminine external genitalia — clitoris, labia, and urethral meatus — without constructing a vaginal canal.[^3][^8]

**Indications:**
- Patients who do not desire or anticipate receptive vaginal intercourse
- Patients seeking to avoid the lifelong dilation requirement
- Lower surgical risk profile than full vaginoplasty (shorter operative time, no rectovaginal dissection)

Vulvoplasty provides clitoral and labial reconstruction with equivalent external aesthetics to full vaginoplasty and eliminates the most common source of long-term complications (vaginal stenosis from inadequate dilation).

## Postoperative Care

### Dilation Protocol

After vaginoplasty, patients must maintain neovaginal patency through consistent dilation:

- Initial dilation begins at 1–2 weeks postoperatively under clinical supervision
- Frequency is highest early: typically 3× daily for 30 minutes each session in the first months
- Frequency decreases over time but dilation is required **indefinitely** — even years post-surgery, the neovagina will stenose if dilation is abandoned
- Patients who are sexually active with receptive intercourse may be able to reduce formal dilation frequency, but cessation of all mechanical dilation carries stenosis risk

**The single most important determinant of long-term vaginal depth and caliber is patient adherence to the dilation protocol.**

### Hygiene

- Low-pressure douching maintains cleanliness of the skin-lined canal, which does not self-clean like native vaginal epithelium
- Secretions differ by canal type: skin-lined canals require active hygiene; peritoneal and intestinal canals are more self-maintaining

### Follow-Up

- First postoperative visit typically 1–2 weeks (wound check, dilation initiation)
- Urethral calibration to assess for meatal stenosis
- Serial dilation coaching and cavity assessment at 6 weeks, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, then annually

## Complications

Overall complication rates range from 28–70%, though most are minor and managed non-operatively.[^15][^3][^16][^17]

### Common Complications

| Complication | Approximate Rate | Management |
|---|---|---|
| Granulation tissue | 26% | Silver nitrate cauterization, topical steroid |
| Intravaginal scarring | 20% | Dilation optimization; surgical lysis if severe |
| Wound dehiscence / SSI | 9% | Local wound care; antibiotics if infected |
| Prolonged pain | 20% | Multimodal analgesia; pelvic floor PT |
| Introital stenosis | Variable | Progressive dilation; surgical revision |
| Urethral meatal stenosis | Common | Calibration; urethral dilation; meatoplasty |

### Serious Complications

| Complication | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Rectovaginal fistula | 0.9–3% | Major complication; requires diversion and staged repair |
| Urethrovaginal fistula | Uncommon | Urethral reconstruction or catheter management |
| Severe complications (Clavien-Dindo 3–4) | ~5.5% | No deaths reported in major multicenter study[^15] |

### Risk Factors for Complications

Elevated BMI, higher ASA class, diabetes, hypertension, and surgeon inexperience are associated with increased complication rates.[^15] Surgeon volume is particularly relevant — after approximately 50 cases, rates of delayed complications and revision surgery fall substantially.[^8]

## Outcomes

Patient satisfaction following vaginoplasty is consistently high:

- **94%** of patients report they would undergo surgery again[^18]
- **94%** feel positively about their genitals postoperatively[^18]
- **71%** report resolution of gender dysphoria[^18][^16]
- Quality of life improves significantly across mental health (PHQ-9, GAD-7), body image, social function, and sexual function domains[^18]
