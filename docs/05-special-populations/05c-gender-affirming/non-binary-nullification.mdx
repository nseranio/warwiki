# Non-Binary & Nullification Procedures

Not all gender-diverse patients seek reconstruction toward a binary male or female phenotype. Non-binary, agender, and gender-nonconforming individuals may desire partial, neutral, or customized anatomical configurations that do not map neatly onto standard feminizing or masculinizing surgical pathways. The reconstructive urologist's role is to understand the range of options and tailor the surgical plan to each patient's specific goals — not to fit the patient into a predefined algorithm.

## Principles of Care

- **Goal-directed planning:** The operative plan should be derived from the patient's stated anatomical goals, not from a categorical assumption about their gender identity. A non-binary patient may desire some elements of feminizing surgery and not others, or may wish to eliminate all external genital structures.
- **Informed consent for irreversibility:** All genital procedures result in permanent changes. Patients must clearly understand which aspects of the anatomy are affected and that return to baseline anatomy is not possible after gonadectomy, vaginoplasty, or phalloplasty.
- **No requirement for binary social role:** SOC8 does not require patients to adopt a binary social role as a criterion for surgery. Non-binary patients who meet criteria for sustained gender incongruence and satisfy other criteria are candidates for surgical intervention.

## Genital Nullification

Genital nullification refers to the surgical removal or neutralization of all external genital structures, creating a smooth perineum without male- or female-typical anatomy (sometimes called a "smooth" or "neuter" configuration). Procedures may include:

### In AMAB (Assigned Male at Birth) Patients

- **Bilateral orchiectomy:** Removal of testes; eliminates androgens
- **Penectomy:** Removal of the penile shaft; can be combined with glans excision
- **Urethral repositioning:** The urethra is brought to a perineal position, allowing seated urination
- **Scrotectomy:** Removal of the scrotal sac
- **Labiaplasty:** Optional creation of labial folds without a vaginal canal (distinct from full vulvoplasty)

The resulting anatomy allows perineal voiding, eliminates visible male structures, and creates a neutral perineum.

### In AFAB (Assigned Female at Birth) Patients

- **Hysterectomy and oophorectomy:** Eliminates menstruation and internal reproductive organs
- **Vaginectomy (colpocleisis):** Closure of the vaginal canal
- **Metoidioplasty without urethral lengthening:** Releases and advances the hypertrophied clitoris without creating a standing-void urethra — provides a small phallus with preserved sensation, without the complications of urethral extension
- **Minimal scrotoplasty or no scrotoplasty:** Depending on patient preference

## Partial Procedures

Many non-binary patients desire specific components of feminizing or masculinizing surgery without the complete reconstruction. Common requests include:

| Procedure | Population | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Orchiectomy alone | AMAB | Gonadal removal; hormone reduction; no penile/scrotal reconstruction |
| Vulvoplasty without vaginoplasty | AMAB | Feminine external appearance; no vaginal canal or dilation requirement |
| Hysterectomy alone | AFAB | Menstruation cessation; no external genital change |
| Metoidioplasty without urethral lengthening | AFAB | Phallus creation with preserved erogenous sensation; minimal complication burden |
| Chest surgery only | AMAB or AFAB | Masculine or neutral chest contour; no genital surgery |

These partial procedures are not "incomplete" — they represent the complete surgical goal for that patient. Surgeons should be careful not to suggest that additional procedures are needed or that the patient's choices are insufficient.

## Pelvic Floor Considerations

Regardless of the specific genital configuration achieved, non-binary patients may have pelvic floor dysfunction warranting attention:

- **Post-vaginectomy:** Scar tissue in the vaginal remnant or perineum can cause dyspareunia, constipation, or pelvic pain
- **Post-metoidioplasty without urethral lengthening:** Perineal voiding position is preserved; pelvic floor therapy may help with muscle tension around the perineal meatus
- **Post-orchiectomy:** Low androgen state can affect pelvic floor muscle tone and libido; primary care and endocrine follow-up relevant

## Documentation and Communication

Surgeons working with non-binary patients should use accurate, patient-preferred language in documentation and communication. The operative note and clinical correspondence should describe anatomical structures clearly (e.g., "clitoris," "vaginal canal," "scrotum") while using the patient's preferred pronouns and name. This is both a matter of professional courtesy and medical accuracy.
