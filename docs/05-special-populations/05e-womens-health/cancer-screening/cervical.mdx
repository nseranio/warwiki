# Cervical Cancer Screening

Cervical cancer screening is a well-established preventive strategy that can reduce the lifetime risk of cervical cancer from up to **5% in unscreened populations to less than 0.5%** with effective screening and treatment of precancers.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> All major U.S. guideline organizations now endorse **primary high-risk HPV (hrHPV) testing as the preferred screening method** for most individuals — a major shift from cytology-based screening.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Screening methods

Three strategies are endorsed:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- **Primary HPV testing** (preferred) — uses an FDA-approved hrHPV test alone. Sensitivity for detecting precancer exceeds **90%**, vs. **50–70% for cytology**. A recent large RCT showed a **17% lower risk** of invasive cervical cancer with primary HPV screening compared with cytology with reflex to HPV.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Cotesting** — HPV testing combined with cytology. Provides increased sensitivity and long-term negative predictive value vs. cytology alone, but adds limited incremental benefit over HPV testing alone since 97% of precancers are HPV-positive.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Cytology alone** (Pap test) — acceptable when HPV testing is unavailable. Requires more frequent screening (every 3 years) due to lower sensitivity.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Current guideline recommendations by age

| Age | USPSTF | ACS |
|---|---|---|
| **&lt;21 years** | No screening | No screening |
| **21–24** | Cytology every 3 years | No screening before age 25 |
| **25–29** | Cytology every 3 years | Primary HPV every 5 years (clinician-collected) or 3 years (self-collected) |
| **30–65** | Primary HPV every 5 years (preferred); cotesting every 5 years or cytology every 3 years acceptable | Same — primary HPV every 5 years preferred |
| **&gt;65** | Discontinue with adequate prior negative results | Negative primary HPV (preferred) or cotesting at ages 60 and 65 = discontinue |
| **After hysterectomy with cervix removal** | No screening if no history of CIN2+ or cervical cancer | Same |

<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

Key points of agreement and divergence:

- **Under 21 years** — no screening recommended by any guideline.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Ages 21–24** — USPSTF recommends cytology every 3 years; ACS recommends no screening before age 25.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Ages 25–29** — USPSTF continues cytology every 3 years; ACS recommends primary HPV testing every 5 years (clinician-collected) or every 3 years (self-collected).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Ages 30–65** — both agree that **primary HPV testing every 5 years is preferred**. Cotesting every 5 years or cytology every 3 years are acceptable alternatives.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Over 65** — screening can be discontinued with adequate prior negative results. ACS now recommends a forward-looking approach: negative primary HPV testing (preferred) or negative cotesting at ages **60 and 65**.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **After hysterectomy with cervix removal** — no screening if no history of CIN2+ or cervical cancer.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## HPV self-collection — a major recent advance

The FDA approved the first self-collected vaginal specimens for HPV testing in clinical settings in **May 2024**, and the first at-home self-collection device (Teal Wand) in **May 2025**.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- Self-collected and clinician-collected samples show **similar accuracy** for detecting high-grade cervical lesions (pooled overall agreement ~89%).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Clinician-collected specimens remain preferred** because they allow reflex cytology from the same sample if HPV-positive. Self-collected specimens require a return visit for cytology if HPV-positive.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- After a negative self-collected HPV test, repeat screening is recommended in **3 years** (vs. 5 years for clinician-collected) — a margin of safety while U.S. longitudinal data accrue.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Self-collection is **not appropriate** for immunocompromised patients, those with HIV, DES exposure, history of cervical cancer, or those requiring cytology-based surveillance.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- HRSA guidelines effective January 2027 require insurance coverage of self-collected hrHPV testing without cost-sharing.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Management of abnormal results — ASCCP risk-based framework

The 2019 ASCCP guidelines introduced a paradigm shift from results-based to **risk-based management**, using the estimated risk of CIN3+ (CIN3, adenocarcinoma in situ, or cancer) to guide clinical action:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

| Immediate CIN3+ risk | Recommended action |
|---|---|
| **&lt;4%** | Surveillance (1, 3, or 5 years depending on result) |
| **4–24%** | Colposcopy |
| **25–59%** | Colposcopy or excisional treatment acceptable |
| **≥60%** | Expedited treatment (excision without prior colposcopy) preferred |

<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

A prior negative HPV test within 5 years reduces the CIN3+ risk by approximately **50%** for new low-grade abnormalities, potentially allowing deferral of colposcopy.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> After treatment for CIN2 / CIN3, surveillance with HPV testing or cotesting at 3-year intervals is recommended for at least **25 years**.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

---

## Special populations

More frequent screening is recommended for **immunocompromised patients** — those with HIV, solid-organ transplant, hematopoietic stem-cell transplant, systemic lupus erythematosus, inflammatory bowel disease, and those on immunosuppressive therapies.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> These individuals should not use self-collection and require both HPV and cytology testing.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Screening recommendations apply regardless of HPV vaccination status or sexual history.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

---

## Screening gaps and disparities

Despite effective screening, over **50% of cervical cancers** in the U.S. are diagnosed in individuals overdue for screening.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Up-to-date screening rates have declined to approximately 75% and have not rebounded post-pandemic.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Disparities persist among uninsured, rural, and racial / ethnic minority populations — making self-collection a particularly promising strategy for these groups.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
