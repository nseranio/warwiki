# Simple Interrupted

The **simple interrupted** stitch is the foundation suturing pattern — each throw is an independent knot with its own tails, cut after tying. Local failure of one stitch does not propagate to the rest of the closure.

## Technique

- Enter the tissue perpendicular to the wound edge
- Emerge symmetrically on the opposite side at the same depth
- Tie with the preferred knot (square, surgeon's, instrument)
- Repeat at spaced intervals

## Key Uses

- Skin closure (fine suture)
- Fascial closure when each stitch needs to be independent (contaminated field)
- Bowel anastomosis — single-layer interrupted closure
- Urethral anastomosis — multiple interrupted throws at the 12, 3, 6, 9 o'clock positions
- Any situation where tension varies along the wound and uniform continuous tension would be inappropriate

## Advantages / Disadvantages

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Isolated failure | Slower than running |
| Tension can vary by stitch | More knots, more suture |
| Easier to revise | Less watertight |
