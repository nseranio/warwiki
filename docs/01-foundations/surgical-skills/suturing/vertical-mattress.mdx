# Vertical Mattress

The **vertical mattress** stitch is an **everting** suture pattern — two passes through the same wound (one deep, one superficial) that bring the wound edges together while lifting them slightly out of the plane of the surrounding skin.

## Technique

- **First pass (deep):** enter far from the wound edge, take a deep bite, exit far from the opposite edge
- **Second pass (superficial):** reverse direction, enter close to the wound edge on the exit side, take a shallow bite, exit close to the edge on the entry side
- Tie — the two passes cinch the wound together and evert the edges

"Far-far-near-near" is the common mnemonic.

## Key Uses

- **Skin closure over tension** — scalp, back, perineum
- **Wound edges prone to inversion** — where eversion is cosmetically and functionally important
- **Complex skin closures** after flap inset or extensive undermining
- Perineal skin closure after urethroplasty

## Advantages

- Good edge eversion (skin healing is best with slightly everted edges)
- Wound tension distributed between deep and superficial passes
- Holds under tension better than a simple interrupted

## Disadvantages

- More suture per stitch
- Marked skin track (cross-hatching visible) if left in too long
- Slower than simple
