# Lembert Stitch

The **Lembert stitch** is an **inverting seromuscular** suture — the classic **outer layer** of a two-layer hand-sewn bowel anastomosis. It takes only the serosa and muscularis (not the mucosa), and when tightened inverts the tissue, pressing serosa against serosa for a water- and air-tight closure. Named for **Antoine Lembert** (1826), who described it for gastrointestinal reconstruction and thereby founded the modern technique of bowel anastomosis.

## Technique

- **Seromuscular bite only** — needle enters the serosa, passes through the muscular layer, and emerges back on the serosa without penetrating the mucosa
- Place paired bites on each side of the wound: enter on one side, exit on the same side; then cross and enter the opposite side, exit the opposite side
- Tie — the serosa inverts on both sides, yielding serosa-to-serosa apposition
- Can be placed as interrupted or continuous

## Key Uses

- **Outer layer of two-layer bowel anastomosis** — the classic pairing with the [Connell](/docs/foundations/surgical-skills/suturing/connell-stitch) inner layer
- **Reinforcement** of any bowel closure (staple-line overclosure)
- **Urinary diversion** — bowel segment construction and reimplantation reinforcement
- **Ureteroileal anastomosis** — bowel-side closure

## Historical Significance

Lembert's 1826 stitch was transformative — previously, bowel anastomosis carried catastrophic leak rates because of mucosa-to-mucosa apposition. Lembert's insight that **serosa-to-serosa healing is the basis of reliable bowel closure** defined modern gastrointestinal surgery.
