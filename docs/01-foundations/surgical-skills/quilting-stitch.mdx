# Quilting Stitch

The **quilting stitch** tacks an overlying flap or skin layer to the underlying tissue bed at multiple points — like the stitching of a quilt — **eliminating dead space and reducing seroma formation**. Borrowed from plastic surgery (abdominoplasty) into reconstructive urology for flap-based reconstruction and complex skin closures.

## Technique

- After the flap is inset on the skin edges, place multiple **interrupted** or **running** sutures from the underside of the flap to the underlying fascia or tissue bed
- Sutures placed in a grid pattern, typically 2–3 cm apart
- Absorbable suture (Vicryl 3-0 or 4-0 is typical)
- The flap is compressed against the bed at multiple points, obliterating the potential space

## Key Uses in Reconstructive Urology

- **Scrotal reconstruction** after Fournier's gangrene — quilting the advancement or STSG to the underlying cord structures
- **Perineal reconstruction** with local advancement flaps
- **Phalloplasty skin closure** — reducing fluid collection beneath mobilized skin
- **Abdominal wall reconstruction** adjacent to complex urologic cases
- **Groin / inguinal skin reconstruction** where dead-space seroma is a known complication

## Benefits

- Reduces seroma formation (the principal benefit)
- Reduces flap ischemia from fluid collection beneath
- Distributes tension across the flap-bed interface
- Largely eliminates drain need in some applications
