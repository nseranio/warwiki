# Quilting Stitch

The **quilting stitch** tacks an overlying flap, skin graft, or mucosal graft to the underlying tissue bed at multiple points, like the stitching of a quilt, to reduce dead space and hold a graft spread against its bed. In reconstructive urology it is used for flap fixation and graft fixation in urethroplasty (buccal mucosa or skin graft); the cited urethroplasty series do not establish outcomes for every flap application.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

![Graft-bed contact and quilting: Generic free-graft interface, conceptual section](/img/diagrams/quilting-stitch.svg)

*Fluid or shear separating a graft from its recipient bed threatens incorporation. Fixation brings the graft into close apposition and limits dead space. Rat imbibition evidence supports a mechanism, not a human clinical success rate; bed vascularity, infection and graft handling also matter. (Original WARWIKI schematic)*

---

## Principles of Graft Survival

Free graft incorporation is commonly described in overlapping, approximate phases; timing varies with the graft and recipient bed, and the cited experimental models are not human timing studies:<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

1. **Plasmatic imbibition** (0–48 h). Graft absorbs plasma from recipient bed
2. **Inosculation** (48–72 h). Graft vessels align with recipient bed vessels
3. **Neovascularization** (days 4–7). Capillary ingrowth from recipient bed
4. **Maturation** (weeks to months). Graft remodeling and epithelialization

**Why quilting matters:**<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

- Reduces dead space and the opportunity for fluid to separate graft from bed
- Maintains graft–bed contact needed for early incorporation
- Holds the graft spread and limits folding or retraction ("hanging" the graft)<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- Adds fixation points across the graft instead of relying only on the graft edges

## Suture Quilting in Urethroplasty

Urethroplasty is the main reconstructive urology application of graft quilting. Several named techniques define how the graft is placed and fixed.

### Standard technique

1. **Graft preparation**. Trim to size, remove excess submucosa to improve imbibition
2. **Apex fixation**. Secure proximal and distal apices first
3. **Edge suturing**. Continuous or interrupted sutures along lateral margins
4. **Quilting sutures**. Multiple interrupted sutures through graft center into the underlying bed (tunica albuginea, periosteum, or urethral margins)

### Suture selection

Examples of absorbable sutures used for fine graft fixation; choice and size depend on tissue, approach and surgeon preference; there is no universal quilting specification.

| Material | Size | Application | Advantages |
|---|---|---|---|
| Polyglactin (Vicryl) | 4-0 to 5-0 | Edge and quilting | Braided; good handling; predictable absorption |
| Polydioxanone (PDS) | 5-0 to 6-0 | Spread fixation | Monofilament; longer tensile-strength retention |
| Poliglecaprone (Monocryl) | 5-0 to 6-0 | Delicate quilting | Monofilament; minimal tissue reaction |

### Dorsal onlay — Barbagli technique

Urethra mobilized from the corpora cavernosa, opened dorsally, graft quilted to the tunica albuginea.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

- Quilting sutures pass through the graft, then the tunica albuginea, then back through the graft.
- Urethra rotated back to cover the graft
- Reported success varies with stricture site, case mix, outcome definition and follow-up; systematic reviews do not supply a single technique-specific 88%-at-42-month estimate applicable to all dorsal onlays.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

### Dorsal inlay — Asopa technique

Urethra opened ventrally, then incised dorsally through the stricture without mobilization; graft quilted into the dorsal defect through the ventral approach.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

- The urethra is not mobilized.
- In one 47-patient randomized comparison (25 dorsal onlay, 22 dorsal inlay), mean operative time was 128 versus 205 min and mean blood loss 105 versus 228 mL for inlay versus onlay.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- Success was 88% for onlay and 86.4% for inlay in that study, at mean follow-up of 22.6 versus 24.2 months by the authors' no-reintervention and flow criteria. The similar rates do not establish formal equivalence.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

### Ventral onlay

Urethra opened ventrally, graft quilted to the ventral bed with spongiosal coverage.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

- **Spongioplasty**. Corpus spongiosum advanced and closed over the graft
- **Pseudospongioplasty**. When spongiosum is inadequate distally, periurethral tissue flaps are mobilized over the graft<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- In a retrospective series comparing two ventral coverage methods, pseudospongioplasty succeeded in 37/46 (80%) versus 47/56 (84%) with conventional spongioplasty; these are not ventral-versus-dorsal rates.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- A separate retrospective comparison in sexually active men with primary nontraumatic proximal bulbar strictures found erectile dysfunction at 3 months in 5% after ventral versus 25% after dorsal onlay (60 versus 73 men), and at 12 months in 1.7% versus 13.7%. Overall success was similar (91.7% versus 90.4%). The center switched from dorsal to ventral during the study period, so chronology and other confounding limit causal inference; do not generalize the difference to all urethroplasties.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

### Double graft — Palminteri technique

For tight or obliterative bulbar strictures, both dorsal inlay and ventral onlay grafts are used.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

1. Urethra opened ventrally
2. Dorsal urethra incised in midline to create raw area on tunica albuginea
3. First graft quilted dorsally (dorsal inlay) to corpora
4. Second graft quilted ventrally (ventral onlay) to urethral margins
5. Spongiosum closed over ventral graft

In the two Palminteri series, success was 43/48 (89.6%) at mean 22 months (range 13–59) and 64/73 (88%) at mean 48.9 months. Success was defined as normal voiding without any postoperative procedure.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

### Transurethral quilting — fossa navicularis

A minimally invasive approach for distal strictures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

1. Transurethral ventral urethrotomy to excise cicatrix
2. Double-arm 6-0 PDS delivers triangular BMG to the proximal urethrotomy
3. Graft secured to meatus with 5-0 polyglactin
4. Additional 6-0 double-arm PDS sutures quilt the graft for spread fixation
5. 16 Fr silicone catheter

In a 44-patient retrospective series with at least one year of follow-up, 95% were patent without further intervention at mean follow-up 36 months (IQR 22–50); this is not a fixed 36-month endpoint.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Quilting to periosteum — VUAS repair

For vesicourethral anastomotic stricture after radical prostatectomy, the graft can be quilted to the pubic bone periosteum.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- Dorsal BMG sutured to bladder neck at 11, 12, and 1 o'clock
- Interrupted dorsal quilting through periosteum over pubic bone using 4-0 Vicryl
- Graft sutured to urethra in continuous fashion
- In the reported four-patient series, all were patent at 3 months, but all remained incontinent; this is early feasibility, not durable 100% success.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Fibrin Glue Fixation

Fibrin sealants combine fibrinogen and thrombin to form an adherent clot and have been used to supplement or partly replace suture quilting in small urethroplasty series.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)[[20]](#ref20)[[21]](#ref21)</sup> The penile-glue series below used a cyanoacrylate adhesive, not fibrin glue.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

Avoiding central quilting sutures may simplify graft fixation, but these series do not prove faster surgery, better graft take or angiogenesis versus sutures alone.

### Dorsal onlay BMG with fibrin glue (Barbagli)

1. Urethra mobilized and opened dorsally
2. 2 mL fibrin glue applied to corpora cavernosa (recipient bed)
3. BMG placed on glue bed
4. Only 2 interrupted 5-0 polyglactin sutures fix graft apices to tunica albuginea
5. Additional fibrin glue injected over urethra after rotation

In this six-patient series, mean operative time was 100 min and no anastomotic restrictures were detected at the reported 6- and 12-month assessments. Mean follow-up was 16 months (range 12–24); there was no suture-only comparison group.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

### One-stage penile urethroplasty with cyanoacrylate glue

Barbagli and colleagues used Glubran 2 (N-butyl-2-cyanoacrylate/methacryloxy sulfolane), a different adhesive from fibrin sealant, to fix an oral graft without central quilting sutures:<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

- Median operative time: 60 min
- Success was 85.7% (12/14) at median follow-up 16 months (IQR 12–32), in an uncontrolled retrospective series
- No fistula or sacculation on the 2-week VCUG; three intraoperative tunica injuries and one postoperative UTI were reported
- No patient *complained* of chordee or sexual dysfunction in this small series

### Experimental — fibrin glue + MSCs

In a rabbit urethral injury model, fibrin glue combined with autogenic bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells increased neovascularization and smooth muscle formation, and no fistulae developed (two in untreated controls).<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> No human data exist.

## Barbed Sutures

Unidirectional or bidirectional barbs grip tissue, eliminating knot tying and maintaining uniform tension.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)[[25]](#ref25)[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

### Vesicourethral anastomosis

This is the best-studied urologic application. A meta-analysis of three randomized trials and six observational studies (786 patients) found:<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

- Anastomosis time was shorter by a weighted mean 3.98 min (p = 0.0001).
- Operative time was shorter by 10.06 min (p = 0.0003).
- Leakage rate did not differ significantly.
- Continence rates did not differ significantly at 4–6 weeks, 3 months and 6–12 months.

In a single-surgeon randomized trial of 70 RARP cases (Zorn), reconstruction time was 13.1 min with barbed suture versus 20.8 min with monofilament, the need to readjust tension was 6% versus 24% (p = 0.03), and cost was CAN$48 versus CAN$70 per case. Pad-free continence at 1, 3 and 6 months was similar.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> A 64-patient randomized trial found a 26% shorter anastomosis time with barbed suture and equivalent 6-week functional outcomes.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup> A prospective cohort of 80 patients found equal urethral stenosis (2.5% in each arm) and equal 12-month continence, and its authors described the barbed technique as economical.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup> In a 100-patient comparison, barbed suture alone (without rhabdosphincter reconstruction) was associated with better immediate continence (76% versus 40%) and one-month continence (92% versus 76%) than poliglecaprone with rhabdosphincter reconstruction; three-month continence was similar (96% versus 94%).<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup>

### Barbed sutures for graft quilting

Graft-specific clinical benefit is unestablished. The cited self-anchoring-suture strength and watertightness experiment concerns urinary-tract reconstruction, not graft quilting; it cannot be used as graft-fixation outcome evidence.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup> Knotless spread fixation and time savings are theoretical here; assess tissue response and exposed barbs before applying this approach to a graft.

## Staged Urethroplasty

### First stage — graft inlay and maturation

Graft quilted to recipient bed and left to mature before tubularization.<sup>[[30]](#ref30)[[31]](#ref31)[[32]](#ref32)[[33]](#ref33)</sup>

1. Stricture excised or urethra opened ventrally
2. **Graft quilted** to urethral bed / corporal bodies
3. **Lateral edges sutured** to skin margins (marsupialization)
4. **Maturation**: 4–6 months (BMG) or 8–12 weeks (mesh graft)<sup>[[30]](#ref30)[[31]](#ref31)</sup>

In one nonrandomized comparison, triangular extension at the proximal and/or distal stoma was associated with fewer first-stage stomal revisions (0/23 versus 5/24 controls) and higher mean maximum flow after the second stage (21.5 versus 15.8 mL/s, p = 0.027). Both groups had 19/20 (95%) success among those reaching second-stage surgery; the study does not prove prevention of all restenosis.<sup>[[34]](#ref34)</sup>

### Second stage — tubularization

1. Urethral plate mobilized from surrounding skin
2. Plate rolled into tube over catheter
3. Multi-layer closure (mucosa, dartos, skin)
4. Additional graft inlay if needed

**Long-term outcomes.** In a 49-patient series with median follow-up of 57 months, long-term success was 96.4% for BMG-only staged repairs and 53% when split-thickness skin graft was used in the first stage. All recurrences occurred after the 4-month cystoscopy, at a median of 78 months.<sup>[[33]](#ref33)</sup>

## Skin Graft Fixation

### Tie-over bolster

Traditional method for irregular surfaces.<sup>[[35]](#ref35)[[36]](#ref36)[[37]](#ref37)[[38]](#ref38)</sup>

1. Graft sutured to wound edges with long suture tails
2. Bolster material (gauze, foam, proflavine-soaked gauze) placed over graft
3. Suture tails tied over the bolster to compress graft to bed

**TODGA** (Tie-Over Dressing for Graft Application, distal penectomy or glans resurfacing): in the authors' standardized 29-operation phase, a proflavine-soaked gauze bolster was left for 10 days and patients could mobilize immediately; one patient required re-grafting. This is not a measured 97% graft-take comparison with NPWT or quilting.<sup>[[38]](#ref38)</sup> Modified extra-wound fixation variants aim to limit graft-edge trauma during dressing take-down.<sup>[[39]](#ref39)</sup>

### Negative pressure wound therapy (NPWT)

In a randomized trial of 86 skin-graft recipients (43 per arm), NPWT improved early graft take versus tie-over bolster, particularly at irregular or high-mobility sites; the endpoint was postoperative days 5–7, not long-term urethral graft success.<sup>[[36]](#ref36)</sup>

- **Overall graft take:** 97.2% (NPWT) versus 90.2% (tie-over), p = 0.005
- **Irregular or high-mobility areas:** 97.6% versus 81.7%, p &lt; 0.001

### Eggcrate foam bolster — penile/genital reconstruction

A structured alternative for high-risk genital reconstruction:<sup>[[40]](#ref40)</sup>

1. Bacitracin on nonadherent dressing
2. Saline-moistened silver dressing
3. Gauze layer
4. Eggcrate foam (spikes toward wound)
5. Penis extended; cylindrical bolster placed and stapled

In a retrospective series of 21 men (median follow-up 50 days), median graft take was 100% through POD 14 and 95% at days 15–30; 95% had at least one impaired wound-healing risk factor.<sup>[[40]](#ref40)</sup>

## Flap Quilting — Scrotal and Perineal Reconstruction

Distinct from graft quilting but using a related mechanical principle, selected deep fixation can reduce flap–bed dead space and shear. The applicability and outcome benefit depend on flap design and recipient bed.

**Technique.** After flap inset, selected buried fixation sutures can attach its deep surface to suitable fascia or a recipient bed where dead space remains. An interrupted or running pattern and spacing and material should be adapted to flap thickness, perfusion, tissue strength and local protocol; a universal 2–3 cm grid or 3-0/4-0 polyglactin standard is not established by the cited urethroplasty studies.

**Uses:**

- **Scrotal reconstruction**. After Fournier's gangrene, quilting the advancement or STSG to cord structures
- **Perineal reconstruction** with local advancement flaps
- **Phalloplasty skin closure**. Reduces fluid collection beneath mobilized skin
- **Abdominal wall reconstruction** adjacent to complex urologic cases
- **Groin / inguinal skin reconstruction** where dead-space seroma is a known complication

**Rationale:** reduce dead space and shear at the flap–bed interface. These graft-fixation references do not demonstrate a quantified seroma benefit or justify omitting a drain for a particular scrotal or perineal flap; drainage and perfusion decisions remain procedure-specific.

## Technical Pearls

**Graft preparation:**<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

- Trim excess submucosa to improve imbibition
- Fenestrate graft if seroma risk is high
- Keep graft moist during preparation

**Recipient bed:**<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

- Ensure adequate hemostasis
- Create a well-vascularized bed
- Excise obstructing scar as required for the planned reconstruction while preserving viable support and perfusion

**Suture placement:**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

- **"Hanging" sutures**. Anchor graft to surrounding tissue to prevent folding/retraction<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Spread fixation**. Multiple quilting sutures distribute graft evenly<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Avoid excessive tension**. Prevents graft ischemia
- **Mattress sutures** can be useful for selected tissue geometry, but avoid excess pressure or a blanket assumption of superior graft take
- **Mechanical "sewing-machine" devices**. A handheld battery-powered suturing tool has been described to accelerate BMG quilting in deep, narrow fields (dorsal-onlay membranous urethroplasty, robotic subtrigonal inlay for BNC, augmented perineal urethrostomy) where conventional needle-driver quilting is slow and ergonomically unfavorable.<sup>[[41]](#ref41)</sup>

**Postoperative:**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[38]](#ref38)</sup>

- Catheter drainage 1–3 weeks depending on procedure
- Avoid pressure on graft site
- Monitor for hematoma or seroma

## Summary — method selection

| Method | Best applications | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Suture quilting | Selected urethroplasty grafts (Barbagli, Asopa, Palminteri); selected flap fixation | Direct spread fixation; tailor bite depth and spacing |
| Fibrin glue | Reported for dorsal onlay BMG | Small series; may pair with limited sutures; comparative benefit unproved |
| Cyanoacrylate glue | Reported for one-stage penile oral-mucosal graft inlay | Different chemistry from fibrin; uncontrolled 14-patient series |
| Barbed suture | Robotic VUA; graft quilting is proposed rather than clinically established | VUA evidence cannot be transferred directly to graft fixation |
| Tie-over bolster (incl. TODGA) | Penile skin grafts; glans resurfacing | External compression; do not infer a cross-site take-rate ranking |
| NPWT | Selected complex or irregular/high-mobility skin-graft wounds | Early take benefit in one 86-patient RCT versus tie-over |
| Eggcrate foam | Penile/genital reconstruction in a small series | Structured non-NPWT bolster; no direct comparison with NPWT |
