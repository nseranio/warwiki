# Locking Stitch

A locking stitch is a running suture in which successive loops are locked as the line advances. It is a descriptive closure pattern, not a standalone operation, and its effect depends on the tissue, material, tension, and indication.

## Evidence orientation

The strongest retrieved evidence in this recovery review concerns repair of episiotomy and second-degree perineal tears, not general reconstructive surgery. A Cochrane review reported less short-term pain, less analgesic use, and fewer removals with continuous techniques than with interrupted repair; the included studies were heterogeneous and the findings should not be carried over automatically to other tissues or procedures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

No general rule about locking for hemostasis, edema, pain, or anastomotic security is made here. A procedure-specific plan should follow current specialty guidance, tissue assessment, and supervised surgical judgment.

## Evidence boundary

The inherited page and the PubMed record/abstract for the cited Cochrane review were read. Full source packages, current specialty guidance, and the inherited ACOG and technical-source claims were not adjudicated. This is not clinical verification or a repair protocol.
