# Connell Stitch

The **Connell stitch** is a **continuous full-thickness inverting** suture — the classic **inner layer** of a two-layer hand-sewn bowel anastomosis. It passes through all layers of the bowel wall and, when tightened, inverts the mucosa into the lumen, apposing serosa to serosa.

## Technique

- Pass the needle **from outside to inside** (serosa to mucosa) on one bowel segment
- Cross the lumen to the opposite segment
- Pass **from inside to outside** (mucosa to serosa)
- Continue running in this pattern around the anastomosis

Each throw creates a U-loop on the outside and a parallel stitch on the inside, inverting the wound edges.

## Key Uses in Reconstructive Urology

- Inner layer of two-layer bowel anastomosis during urinary diversion (ileal conduit, neobladder, pouch)
- Ureterointestinal anastomosis (bowel side)
- Continent channel construction (Mitrofanoff, Monti)
- Bowel segment closures in complex abdominal reconstruction

## Named For

**F. Gregory Connell** (American surgeon, early 20th century) described the stitch for gastrointestinal anastomosis.
