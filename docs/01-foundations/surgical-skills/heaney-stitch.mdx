# Heaney Stitch

The **Heaney stitch** is a transfixing pedicle suture-ligature used in hysterectomy, particularly for a clamped uterosacral pedicle. A Heaney-type transfixion suture can serve as a pedicle ligature or, in Cruikshank's described vaginal-hysterectomy training technique, as a second tie after a simple uterine-vessel ligature; he also considers a single-clamp, single-suture method adequate.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> It is associated with Noble Sproat Heaney and the [instruments bearing his name](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/needle-holders/heaney).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Technique

- Identify and protect nearby structures, especially the ureter, while placing a Heaney or suitable pedicle clamp close to the uterus; divide the pedicle on the specimen side.
- For a selected uterosacral pedicle, pass a suture near the clamp tip and through the pedicle midportion, then tie and tag it (for example with a Kocher clamp) for later cuff support where applicable.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In the Atlas of Pelvic Surgery cuff-fixation technique, an absorbable suture passes from inside the ligament near the clamp tip out through vaginal mucosa, then returns through mucosa and the ligament midportion beneath the clamp to plicate the pedicle to the cuff.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- In the cited vaginal-hysterectomy sequence, the vascular cardinal pedicle receives a simple suture ligature without a fixation stitch because of hematoma concern. The tubo-ovarian–round-ligament pedicle receives a separate fixation ligature after a simple tie. These are pedicle-specific choices, not an obligatory second wrap of every Heaney stitch.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The two passes, one at the clamp tip and one through the pedicle midportion, distinguish the described Heaney-type stitch from a simple free tie. The exact trajectory varies by pedicle and operative approach.

## Why It Works

The described stitch combines three elements:

1. **Transfixion.** The suture passes through the pedicle tissue near the clamp tip and through its midpoint.
2. **Clamp-assisted control.** The pedicle is held during division and placement of the suture.
3. **Fixation where intended.** In a uterosacral pedicle, the retained suture can assist later identification and cuff suspension.

Choice of ligature and whether an additional tie is needed depend on the specific pedicle and exposure.

## Key Uses

- **Vaginal hysterectomy.** The stitch has been described for the uterosacral or other selected nonvascular pedicle. Vascular pedicles may require a different ligature strategy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Open abdominal hysterectomy.** Transfixing sutures may secure selected clamped pedicles, but the two-pass uterosacral pattern is not universal. In Wheeless and Roenneburg's abdominal sequence, uterine-vessel and cardinal pedicles are ligated without a pass through their vascular midportion because of hematoma or vessel-tearing risk.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Laparoscopic clips and energy devices are different methods.
- **Associated cuff support.** A retained uterosacral suture can contribute to cuff fixation in the described vaginal hysterectomy sequence; formal prolapse suspension is a separate procedure.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
