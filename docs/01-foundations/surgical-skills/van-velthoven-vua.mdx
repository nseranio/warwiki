# Van Velthoven Running Vesicourethral Anastomosis

The **Van Velthoven technique** is a **continuous running vesicourethral anastomosis** using a **double-armed suture** (two needles attached to opposite ends of a single suture length). Described by Roland Van Velthoven in 2003, it became the de facto standard VUA technique for robotic radical prostatectomy and is widely adapted for open and laparoscopic prostatectomy as well.

## Setup

- **Two needles** tied to opposite ends of a single suture length — typically 12 cm long with each needle
- **Contrasting suture colors** on the two halves (e.g., one 3-0 Monocryl, one 3-0 V-Loc dyed differently) help the surgeon track position
- The suture begins at the **posterior midline** (6 o'clock) of the urethra / bladder neck

## Technique

1. **Posterior midline anchor** — a throw is placed from urethra to bladder at the 6 o'clock position
2. **Run clockwise** with the right-hand needle — sequential bites from urethra-to-bladder at 5 → 3 → 1 o'clock
3. **Run counterclockwise** with the left-hand needle — bites at 7 → 9 → 11 o'clock
4. Meet at the 12 o'clock position
5. Tie the two needle tails to each other at 12 o'clock — single knot closes the anastomosis

## Advantages

- **Watertight** running closure
- **Single knot** (at 12 o'clock) minimizes knot burden
- **Faster than interrupted** anastomosis
- **Uniform tension** around the anastomotic circumference
- **Cystoscopically smooth** — no multiple knot clusters protruding into the lumen

## Modifications

- **Barbed suture** (V-Loc, Stratafix) — allows the knot to be eliminated entirely; self-locking running closure
- **Posterior rhabdosphincter reconstruction** (Rocco stitch) before the VUA — often combined with Van Velthoven anastomosis for continence optimization
- **Double-cast needle configuration** — some surgeons use two distinct suture colors to make the running path visible

## Key Uses

- **Robotic radical prostatectomy** — the dominant VUA technique
- **Laparoscopic radical prostatectomy**
- **Open radical prostatectomy** (adapted)
- **Robotic / laparoscopic salvage prostatectomy** after radiation
- **Robotic cystoprostatectomy** with orthotopic neobladder-urethral anastomosis (modified)
