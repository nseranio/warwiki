# Cushing Stitch

The **Cushing stitch** is a **continuous seromuscular inverting** pattern — a continuous version of the Lembert, taking seromuscular bites parallel to the wound axis. An alternative outer layer for bowel closure; sometimes preferred for its speed.

## Technique

- Bites parallel to the wound axis (not perpendicular as in Lembert)
- Run continuously along the length of the closure
- Stays in the seromuscular plane throughout
- Closes into an inverting, serosa-to-serosa apposition

## Key Uses

- **Continuous outer layer** of two-layer bowel closure (when the surgeon prefers continuous over interrupted)
- **Gastric closure**
- **Uretero-intestinal junction reinforcement**

Named for **Harvey Cushing** (neurosurgeon), who described the technique in his early general surgical work.
