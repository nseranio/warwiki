# Suture Lubrication

Ad hoc exposure of sutures to saline, petroleum products, or antibiotic solutions is a material-handling question rather than a validated universal technique. Product instructions for use and local infection-prevention policy should govern preparation and use.

## Evidence orientation

An in-vitro study of four knotted materials reported lower failure tension after petroleum-gauze exposure than after saline exposure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> This is bench evidence and does not establish clinical wound, infection, or anastomotic outcomes.

Another in-vitro study found that bacitracin-soaked sutures inhibited tested organisms, but the commercial antimicrobial comparator showed larger inhibition zones. The study did not measure clinical surgical-site infection outcomes.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The site’s [Sutures](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/sutures) page records the separately reviewed high-quality clinical meta-analysis of triclosan-coated sutures.

## Evidence boundary

The inherited page and the cited PubMed records/abstracts were read. Full source packages, product instructions, current infection-prevention guidance, and clinical outcome studies applicable to a particular procedure were not adjudicated. This page does not recommend a lubricant, antibiotic exposure, or antimicrobial suture strategy.
