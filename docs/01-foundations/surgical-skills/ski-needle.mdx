# The Webster Ski Needle (J-Hook)

The **ski needle**, or **J-hook**, is a standard curved taper needle that the reconstructive surgeon **manually re-bends on the back table** to create an exaggerated, recurved tip for deep, narrow-field perineal suturing — most importantly during the apical anastomotic sutures of bulboprostatic anastomotic urethroplasty for pelvic-fracture urethral injury.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The technique is transmitted through operative mentorship at Duke and through hands-on courses; it is rarely depicted in print, and the geometry — a tighter pivot radius that lets the needle "scoop" mucosa in a confined wound — is felt rather than measured.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Why the modification exists

George D. Webster's **step-based progressive perineal approach** to bulboprostatic anastomotic urethroplasty for pelvic-fracture urethral injury (PFUI) sequentially shortens the gap between the bulbar and prostatic urethral ends through four maneuvers:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

1. Bulbar urethral mobilization
2. Crural separation
3. Inferior wedge pubectomy
4. Supracrural urethral rerouting

By Steps 2–4, the prostatic stump sits at the depth of an obliquely angled, narrow perineal wound. Placing the **circumferential anastomotic sutures at the prostatic apex** — particularly the posterior 4-, 6-, and 8-o'clock bites — requires the needle to enter, pivot through tissue, and emerge in a working space that is too tight for the lateral excursion a standard curved needle demands.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The hand-bent J-hook collapses the arc that the needle tip travels, allowing it to scoop mucosa in a confined field. Andrich and Mundy's standing observation that posterior urethroplasty technique includes irreducible **"art"** that resists full codification is a fair description of this maneuver.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Starting needle and modification

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Suture** | 3-0 or 4-0 polyglactin (Vicryl) — the size supported by reported anastomotic outcomes<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Needle** | Standard taper-point, half- or three-eighths-circle (e.g., Vicryl SH or RB-1) |
| **Bend location** | Distal third of the needle body, at the start of the existing curve |
| **Bend angle** | Roughly **30–45° of additional upturn** in the same direction as the existing concavity |
| **Result** | Exaggerated recurved tip — the "ski" or "J-hook" — with a tighter pivot radius |

**Bench technique:**

1. Grasp the needle at the **mid-body** with a heavy needle driver
2. With a second heavy driver or sturdy forceps, grasp the **distal third** near the swage-tip junction
3. Roll the tip **toward the existing concavity** by ~30–45° to deepen the curve
4. Inspect the tip — it should retain a sharp point and a smooth arc; discard if the metal kinks or the tip is blunted

The maneuver is performed by the surgeon on a fresh needle for each anastomotic suture pass, since the bend opens slightly with each tissue passage. Most operators bend several needles up front and keep them on a separate Mayo stand.

---

## Suture placement

After the prostatic stump has been mobilized and the bulbar end is ready to be brought up, the apical sutures are placed **before** approximating the two ends:<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- **Six circumferential bites** at the prostatic mucosa — typically 12, 2, 4, 6, 8, and 10 o'clock — through full-thickness mucosa and submucosa
- The J-hook is most useful for the **posterior bites (4, 6, 8 o'clock)** where wound depth and the angle of attack are worst
- Sutures are tagged, drawn out of the field, then sequentially passed through the spatulated bulbar end and tied to complete the anastomosis under direct vision

The same J-hook geometry is useful for any deep, narrow-field anastomosis where a standard needle cannot complete its arc — selected vesicourethral anastomoses, redo bladder-neck reconstruction, sphincter-preserving membranous urethral stricture repairs.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

---

## Pitfalls

- **Over-bending** kinks the metal and predisposes to needle fracture mid-tissue — discard any needle that looks deformed
- **Bending in the wrong direction** (away from the concavity) defeats the purpose
- **Holding the tip directly** with the needle driver dulls or notches it — grip just behind the tip
- **Reusing a J-hook** after several passes — the bend opens and the geometry is lost; bend new ones rather than trying to re-bend the same one repeatedly
