# Barbed Suture Closure

**Barbed sutures** have microscopic barbs cut into the suture filament at regular intervals. Once the suture is pulled through tissue, the barbs engage the tissue **in one direction only** — the suture is **self-retaining and knotless**, requiring no tied knots to maintain closure.

## Devices

- **V-Loc** (Covidien / Medtronic) — unidirectional barbed, with welded end stop
- **Stratafix** (Ethicon) — bidirectional or unidirectional, multiple configurations
- **Quill** — earlier-generation, less commonly used now

## Absorbable vs Non-Absorbable

- **Absorbable** (V-Loc 180, Stratafix Monocryl) — polyglyconate or polyglactin; degrades over 3–6 months; standard for most reconstructive applications
- **Non-absorbable** (V-Loc PBT, Stratafix polypropylene) — permanent; specific fascial or load-bearing closures

## Key Uses in Reconstructive Urology

- **Robotic vesicourethral anastomosis** — replacing or modifying the [Van Velthoven technique](/docs/foundations/surgical-skills/special-techniques/van-velthoven-vua)
- **Robotic urethroplasty** (e.g., buccal mucosa graft onlay)
- **Bladder closure** after partial cystectomy or robotic cystoplasty
- **Fascial closure** (port site, Pfannenstiel)
- **Vaginal cuff closure** during robotic or laparoscopic hysterectomy-adjacent work
- **Bowel wall closures** in urinary diversion (particularly robotic)

## Advantages

- **No knots** — eliminates the most common failure point of running closures
- **Uniform tension** along the closure — barbs hold each pass in place before the next
- **Faster** than conventional running-with-knot closure
- **Hands-free** — single-handed tension maintenance is built into the suture

## Considerations &amp; Caveats

- **Tissue trauma** — barbs are cut into the filament, slightly reducing tensile strength vs smooth suture; not recommended where maximum strength is needed
- **Removal difficulty** — if a closure must be taken down, barbed suture is harder to remove than smooth
- **Tissue reaction** — some reports of increased inflammation at the barbed surface
- **Knot placement options** — some barbed sutures allow a starting loop or a welded end stop; correct handling of the starting configuration is essential
- **Infection / biofilm** — limited but growing evidence of no increased infection risk compared with smooth suture
