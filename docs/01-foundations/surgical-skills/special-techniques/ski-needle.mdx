# Ski Needle Technique

The **ski needle** is a specialized needle with a **straight shank and a curved tip** — resembling the profile of a ski — designed to **pass through a laparoscopic cannula while still providing a curved geometry** at the tissue interface. The technique is essential for deep-pelvic laparoscopic suturing before robotic platforms democratized intracorporeal suturing.

## Needle Design

- **Straight proximal shank** (~30 mm) — fits through a 5 mm or 10 mm cannula without catching
- **Curved distal tip** (~10–15 mm, typical half-circle) — negotiates the tissue bite angle
- **Taper or cutting tip** depending on tissue

## Use Cases — Classic Laparoscopic Pelvic Suture Placement

- Laparoscopic Burch colposuspension
- Laparoscopic sacrocolpopexy attachments
- Deep pelvic ligation when a standard curved needle cannot pass through the cannula
- Laparoscopic hernia repair adjacent to urologic reconstruction

## In the Robotic Era

Robotic platforms (EndoWrist) allow the articulated needle driver to manipulate standard curved needles inside the abdomen, **largely displacing the ski needle** for contemporary reconstructive work. The ski needle retains a role for:

- Centers still performing traditional laparoscopic reconstruction
- **Cost-sensitive settings** where robotic access is not universal
- Specific procedures where the ski geometry fits the tissue geometry better

## Contemporary Repurposing

The term **"ski needle"** is sometimes used loosely for **any needle-shank configuration** that accommodates an unusual cannula approach — including in niche open reconstructive work where the ski tip provides leverage in a narrow field.
