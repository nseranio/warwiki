# Suture Ligature (Tie-on-a-Passer)

A **suture ligature** is a suture delivered around a pedicle using a passer — most commonly a right-angle clamp ("right angle") or a dedicated ligature carrier. Used when the pedicle is clamped but a simple free tie cannot physically reach around it.

## Technique

- Clamp the pedicle with a hemostat or right-angle clamp
- Pass a **right-angle clamp** behind the pedicle
- Load the free suture onto the right-angle clamp
- Pull the suture through; the right-angle leads the suture around the back of the pedicle
- Tie the suture at the front
- Alternatively, the primary clamp can be exchanged for the ligature suture by the "tie behind the clamp" maneuver

## Key Uses

- Deep pelvic pedicles during pelvic dissection
- Hepatic and retroperitoneal pedicles
- Any clamped pedicle where the free tie does not reach
- Standard technique for hysterectomy, prostatectomy, and complex pelvic reconstruction

## Limitations

- Still susceptible to slippage on large or firm pedicles — use [transfixion](/docs/foundations/surgical-skills/ligatures/transfixion-stitch) for security
- The right-angle maneuver requires the clamp to fully encircle the pedicle without injury to adjacent structures

See also: [Free Tie](/docs/foundations/surgical-skills/ligatures/free-tie), [Transfixion Stitch](/docs/foundations/surgical-skills/ligatures/transfixion-stitch), [Heaney Stitch](/docs/foundations/surgical-skills/special-techniques/heaney-stitch).
