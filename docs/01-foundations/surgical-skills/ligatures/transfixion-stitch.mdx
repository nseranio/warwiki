# Transfixion Stitch (Stick Tie)

A **transfixion stitch** — colloquially the **stick tie** — passes the suture **through the substance of the pedicle itself** before tying around it. The suture mechanically anchors in the pedicle tissue, **preventing the knot from slipping off** even under tension.

## Technique

- Clamp the pedicle
- Pass the suture needle **through the middle of the pedicle** (through the substance of the clamped tissue)
- Loop the suture around both halves of the pedicle and tie
- The knot is now mechanically held in place by the suture passing through the tissue

## Key Uses

- **Large pedicles** — ovarian or uterine artery-vein complex, renal hilar pedicles, uterine-cervical pedicles
- **Firm or fibrous pedicles** that resist ligature grip
- **Short pedicles** where sliding room is limited
- **High-pressure vessels** where slip would be catastrophic

## Why It's Standard for the Heaney Stitch

The [Heaney stitch](/docs/foundations/surgical-skills/special-techniques/heaney-stitch) — the signature pelvic pedicle ligature of vaginal and abdominal hysterectomy — is a transfixion stitch delivered with a double throw around the clamp. Its security derives from the transfixion element.
