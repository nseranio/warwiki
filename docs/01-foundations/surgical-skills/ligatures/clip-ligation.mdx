# Clip Ligation

**Titanium or polymer clips** applied across a vessel or pedicle provide fast, reliable ligation without suture. The workhorse hemostatic technique in laparoscopic and robotic surgery.

## Types

- **Titanium clips** (Ligaclip, Horizon) — classic; radiopaque; durable; some MRI compatibility
- **Polymer clips** (Hem-o-lok, LAPRA-TY) — larger vessel size; curved inner jaw prevents slip; MRI-compatible
- **Absorbable clips** (LAPRA-TY polydioxanone) — resorb over time; used for ureteral and vascular work where permanent clip is undesired

## Key Uses in Reconstructive Urology

- Vascular pedicles during laparoscopic / robotic nephrectomy, partial nephrectomy
- Ureteral transection and anastomotic preparation
- Mesenteric branches during robotic urinary diversion
- Vasal ligation during robotic prostatectomy (Hem-o-lok is the standard)
- Hilar vessels in renal reconstruction

## Considerations

- **Hem-o-lok on the renal artery** — FDA boxed warning regarding use as the primary renal-artery closure in living-donor nephrectomy (historical slippage cases)
- **Clip thrown into anastomosis** — always account for clips placed near the anastomotic bed
- **Imaging** — titanium clips are radiopaque; may create artifact on CT/MRI planning
