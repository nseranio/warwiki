# Two-Handed Square Knot

The **two-handed square knot** is the foundation knot of open surgery — throws alternated in **opposite directions** produce a flat, **symmetric** knot that locks reliably under tension. The square knot is the conventional reference against which self-locking knots and the surgeon's knot are compared.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Biomechanics

- **Loop-holding capacity:** 37–150 N depending on suture material<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Minimum 4 throws** for security — most studies converge on this threshold<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Additional throws beyond 5 do not increase security** in most configurations
- **Symmetric (opposite-rotation) throws** are critical — asymmetric throws produce a granny knot, the weakest of the standard knots<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- The **optimal square-knot configuration** is **2=1=1** (surgeon's-knot first throw followed by two square throws) or **2=1=1=1** for high-tension closures<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Knot security is independent of suture size** — a 4-0 and a 2-0 of the same material with equivalent throws behave similarly<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

See [Self-Locking Knots](/docs/foundations/surgical-skills/knot-tying/self-locking-knots) for the knot biomechanics terminology and loop-security values.

## Construction

- **First throw** — suture passed from one hand to the other, one direction
- **Second throw** — suture passed back in the opposite direction; throws lie flat against each other
- **Additional throws** depending on suture material:
  - Monofilament: 5–6 throws minimum
  - Braided absorbable: 3–4 throws
  - Silk: 3 throws

## Key Uses

- Any open surgical suture closure where tail length permits
- Teaching knot — the foundation for all other knot variations

## Technique Pearls

- **Throws must lie flat** — if they stack, it's a granny knot (slips)
- **Tension both tails symmetrically** while tying to prevent knot migration
- **Lay the throws down** — don't tie in mid-air
