# Laparoscopic Knot / Knot Pusher

**Laparoscopic knot tying** describes the techniques for securing suture in a minimally-invasive field without direct hand access to the knot. Two general approaches: **extracorporeal knot tying with a knot pusher**, and **intracorporeal knot tying** (pure laparoscopic) with needle drivers.

## Extracorporeal Technique

- Suture placed through tissue laparoscopically
- Both tails exteriorized through the port
- Knot constructed **outside the body** — typically a Roeder or fisherman's knot (slip knot that tightens one direction only)
- **Knot pusher** — a rigid rod with a slotted tip — advanced through the port along the suture to push the pre-formed knot down to the tissue

## Intracorporeal Technique

- Suture and knot tied **inside the body** using the laparoscopic needle drivers
- Mirrors instrument-tie mechanics but performed through cannulas
- Standard for robotic surgery (robot's EndoWrist replicates intracorporeal tying natively)

## Displacement by Robotic Surgery

Robotic platforms largely replaced laparoscopic knot-tying in contemporary reconstructive urology — the EndoWrist restores full needle-driver articulation, making intracorporeal ties as ergonomic as open instrument ties. Pure laparoscopic knot-tying persists in centers and cases without robotic access.

## Key Uses (Historical and Current)

- Laparoscopic urinary diversion and radical cystectomy
- Laparoscopic sacrocolpopexy (increasingly robotic)
- Laparoscopic hernia repair in urologic patients
- **Knot-pusher** technique retains a role in specific applications — e.g., laparoscopic Burch or retropubic suspension
