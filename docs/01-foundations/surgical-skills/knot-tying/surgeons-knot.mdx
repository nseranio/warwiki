# Surgeon's Knot

The **surgeon's knot** is a **double-throw first knot** — the initial throw wraps the suture around itself twice (a **half-knot H2**) instead of once, creating friction that prevents the knot from slipping before the second throw is laid down.

## Biomechanics

The surgeon's knot consistently outperforms the standard square knot in comparative testing:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- **Strongest standard knot** in comparative testing with nylon sutures — greater load-to-failure than square, granny, and random knots<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Superior elongation at yield** compared with square, granny, and random knots<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Greater knot security** than square and sliding knots (P&lt;0.05)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Minimum 4 throws** for security — the same threshold as the square knot<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

See [Self-Locking Knots](/docs/foundations/surgical-skills/knot-tying/self-locking-knots) for the broader knot biomechanics framework.

## When to Use

- **Slippery suture materials** — monofilament polypropylene, PDS, Prolene
- **Tissue under tension** — fascia, urethra-to-bladder-neck anastomosis, bowel corners
- **Situations where a single first throw will loosen** before the second throw locks it

## Construction

- **First throw: double-wrap** the suture around itself (instead of the usual single wrap)
- Lay the throw flat — the double-wrap grips itself under tension
- **Second throw: single wrap** in the opposite direction — converts to a square knot
- Additional throws to secure per suture material

## Key Uses

- Vesicourethral anastomosis
- Tension fascial closure
- Bowel anastomosis at corners
- Any suture material known to slip

## Technique Pearls

- The surgeon's knot is **not a substitute** for good throw technique — it compensates for slippage between the first and second throws, not for poor throw construction
- **Don't over-wrap** — two wraps is the surgeon's knot; three is the Aberdeen variant, not a stronger knot
