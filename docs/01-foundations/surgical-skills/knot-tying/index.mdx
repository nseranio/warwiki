# Knot Tying

Every operation is held together by knots. A reconstructive surgeon needs a **small set of reliable knots that can be tied in any field, at any depth, with any suture material**. Mastery of two-handed and one-handed ties, the instrument tie, and the surgeon's knot covers the vast majority of open reconstruction; the self-locking knot family (Aberdeen, Forwarder, Loop knot, Nice, Cow hitch) finishes and stabilizes running closures with less bulk and higher knot-holding capacity than conventional square ties.

**Core principles of knot security** (see [Self-Locking Knots](/docs/foundations/surgical-skills/knot-tying/self-locking-knots) for detailed biomechanics):

- **Minimum 4 throws** for square or surgeon's knots
- **Knot security is independent of suture size** — material and configuration matter, not gauge
- **Polyfilament &gt; monofilament** for knot-holding capacity
- **Symmetric (opposite rotation) &gt; asymmetric (same rotation)** — the **granny knot** (same-rotation throws) is the weakest standard knot and should be avoided as a final knot
- **Additional throws beyond 5 do not increase security** in most configurations
- **2 throws are never indicated** as a final knot

  <li>

    <span className="section-stack-desc">Foundation knot — bilateral hand participation, maximum control, lowest failure rate.</span>

  <li>
    <a href="/docs/foundations/surgical-skills/knot-tying/one-handed-tie" className="section-stack-title section-stack-link">One-Handed Tie</a>
    <span className="section-stack-desc">Efficient single-hand throws used when the opposite hand is occupied with tissue or suture tension.</span>
  </li>
  <li>
    <a href="/docs/foundations/surgical-skills/knot-tying/instrument-tie" className="section-stack-title section-stack-link">Instrument Tie</a>
    <span className="section-stack-desc">Needle-holder-based knot — the default for microsurgery, fine suture, short tails, and deep restricted fields.</span>
  </li>
  <li>
    <a href="/docs/foundations/surgical-skills/knot-tying/surgeons-knot" className="section-stack-title section-stack-link">Surgeon's Knot</a>
    <span className="section-stack-desc">Double-throw first knot that locks under tension — used for slippery suture material or tissue under tension.</span>
  </li>
  <li>
    <a href="/docs/foundations/surgical-skills/knot-tying/slip-knot" className="section-stack-title section-stack-link">Slip Knot</a>
    <span className="section-stack-desc">Tensioning knot that can be progressively tightened before locking — useful for apical suspension and deep-pelvic work.</span>
  </li>
  <li>
    <a href="/docs/foundations/surgical-skills/knot-tying/aberdeen-knot" className="section-stack-title section-stack-link">Aberdeen Knot</a>
    <span className="section-stack-desc">Terminal knot for running continuous closure — secures the end without a tail-on-tail tie.</span>
  </li>
  <li>
    <a href="/docs/foundations/surgical-skills/knot-tying/self-locking-knots" className="section-stack-title section-stack-link">Self-Locking Knots (Aberdeen, Forwarder, Middler)</a>
    <span className="section-stack-desc">The self-locking knot family — Aberdeen and Forwarder as terminal knots (Forwarder with higher knot-holding capacity under tension), Middler / loop-lock for mid-run tensioning, plus sliding extracorporeal knots for MIS use.</span>
  </li>
</ul>
