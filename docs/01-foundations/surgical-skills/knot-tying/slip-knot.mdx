# Slip Knot

A **slip knot** can be **progressively tightened** before being locked with a final throw. The knot slides down along its own tail under tension, allowing the surgeon to set the final tension after the throws are in place — particularly useful in deep fields where the final knot depth cannot be reached with the fingers.

## When to Use

- **Deep pelvic suture placement** where tying at depth requires incremental tensioning
- **Apical suspension** — uterosacral or sacrospinous ligament suspensions where the suture tail must reach from the deep ligament anchor up to the vaginal cuff
- **Post-prostatectomy vesicourethral anastomosis** — tightening the anastomosis in steps as each suture is placed
- Laparoscopic and robotic applications — pre-formed extracorporeal slip knots are the foundation of laparoscopic knot-tying

## Types

- **Sliding square knot** — initial throws tied loosely, then slid down as a unit
- **Tension-setting knot** — first throw slides until desired tension, then locked with alternating throws

## Key Considerations

- **Slip-knot integrity** depends on correct alternating throws once tension is set
- **Not all suture materials slip equally** — monofilament slides smoothly; braided suture resists sliding
