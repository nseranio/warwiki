# The Abdominal Wall

The abdominal wall provides the muscular and fascial envelope of the abdomen and an operative access and donor-site region. Its anterior and lateral components include the rectus abdominis and the external oblique, internal oblique, and transversus abdominis muscles. Their aponeuroses meet at the linea alba and form the rectus sheath around rectus abdominis.[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)

This page is an anatomic orientation, not an incision, port-placement, flap-design, hernia-repair, analgesia, or postoperative-management protocol. Those decisions require indication-specific planning and current specialty guidance.

For adjacent anatomy, see [Retropubic Space](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/pelvis-support/retropubic-anatomy), [Pelvic Vascular Anatomy](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/pelvis-support/pelvic-vascular-anatomy), [Pelvic Neuroanatomy](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/pelvis-support/pelvic-neuroanatomy), and [The Perineum](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/pelvis-support/perineum).

## Layers and landmarks

From superficial to deep, the anterior wall includes skin, subcutaneous tissue (including Camper and Scarpa layers), the muscular-aponeurotic complex, transversalis fascia, extraperitoneal fat, and peritoneum. The exact appearance and thickness of these structures vary by region and individual.[[2]](#ref2)

The rectus sheath is formed by contributions from the three flat lateral muscles. Above the arcuate line, rectus abdominis has anterior and posterior sheath components. Inferiorly, the lateral aponeuroses pass anterior to rectus and transversalis fascia lies directly posterior to it. The level and appearance of that transition vary; it should not be treated as a fixed surface measurement.[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)

The linea alba joins the paired rectus sheaths at the midline. The linea semilunaris marks the lateral rectus border. Together with the inguinal region and the lower abdominal-wall fascia, these landmarks orient assessment of anterior-wall defects and surgical planes.[[2]](#ref2)

## Muscles, vessels, and nerves

The external oblique, internal oblique, and transversus abdominis form the layered anterolateral wall; rectus abdominis is paired and vertical within its sheath. Coordinated contraction supports trunk movement and increases intra-abdominal pressure during actions such as coughing and defecation.[[2]](#ref2)

The superior epigastric artery arises from the internal thoracic system, and the inferior epigastric artery arises from the external iliac system. They supply the rectus region and communicate within it. The inferior epigastric vessels course on the deep surface of rectus before entering the sheath near the arcuate-line region.[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)

Thoracoabdominal and subcostal nerves supply much of the anterolateral wall; iliohypogastric and ilioinguinal nerves contribute inferiorly. A transversus abdominis plane is the tissue plane between internal oblique and transversus abdominis in which segmental nerves travel.[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)

## Reconstructive context

The abdominal wall can be an access site, a source of vascularized tissue, and a potential donor-site morbidity site. Rectus-based and perforator-based flap options depend on the individual vascular anatomy, prior operations, tissue condition, intended recipient defect, and reconstructive expertise. This overview does not establish eligibility, pedicle choice, dimensions, or operative steps for any flap.[[2]](#ref2)

## Clinical boundaries

- Hernia classification, mesh-plane selection, component separation, incision choice, trocar configuration, flap harvest, regional blocks, and treatment of abdominal-wall complications are outside this page's verified scope.
- Anatomic variation is clinically relevant. Named landmarks should support—not replace—direct intraoperative assessment and procedure-specific planning.
- New pain, a wound concern, a bulge, obstructive symptoms, or systemic illness after surgery warrants timely clinical assessment rather than self-management based on an anatomy reference.
