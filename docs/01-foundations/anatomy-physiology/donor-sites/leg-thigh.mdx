# The Leg & Thigh

For genitourinary reconstruction, the medial thigh can provide gracilis tissue for selected coverage or interposition, and the tibial nerve near the medial ankle is relevant to tibial-nerve neuromodulation. This page provides anatomic orientation only; it is not a flap-harvest, fistula-repair, continence, implant, or stimulation-treatment protocol.

See [The Perineum](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/pelvis-support/perineum), [Neuromodulation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/neuromodulation), and [Neurogenic Lower Urinary Tract Dysfunction](/docs/clinical-conditions/03d-nlutd/neurogenic-bladder) for related context.

## Medial thigh and gracilis

Gracilis is a long, superficial muscle in the medial thigh. It runs from the pubic region to the medial proximal tibia as part of the pes anserinus. It lies in the adductor compartment and receives motor innervation from the obturator nerve.[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)

The muscle has a segmental vascular pattern, including a dominant proximal pedicle and smaller pedicles. Published anatomic descriptions vary in the number, caliber, and measured position of these branches. Those measurements are not interchangeable with a patient-specific operative map.[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)

In selected pelvic and perineal reconstruction, gracilis can be transferred as vascularized tissue to separate repaired structures or fill a defect. Suitability depends on defect anatomy, prior operations and radiation, donor anatomy, recipient vascularity, infection or inflammation, patient goals, and the reconstructive team's experience. This overview does not establish indications, technical approach, expected success, or donor-site risk for a particular case.

## Medial ankle and tibial nerve

The tibial nerve descends in the deep posterior leg compartment and passes posterior to the medial malleolus through the tarsal tunnel. The flexor retinaculum forms the roof of this fibro-osseous region. The posterior tibial vessels and flexor tendons are adjacent; distal branching patterns vary.[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)

In the tarsal-tunnel region, the tibial nerve typically divides into medial and lateral plantar branches, with calcaneal branches arising variably. The close relation of nerve, vessels, tendons, and retinaculum matters for examination, imaging, regional procedures, and device planning.[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)

Tibial-nerve stimulation is a lower-urinary-tract treatment modality addressed in dedicated clinical pages. This anatomy reference does not specify a target point, needle depth, device choice, stimulation settings, treatment schedule, candidacy, efficacy, or safety profile.

## Clinical boundaries

- Do not use this page to plan gracilis harvest, a perineal flap, fistula repair, dynamic graciloplasty, a posterior-tibial injection, PTNS, or an implantable device.
- Individual vascular and neural anatomy, prior surgery, radiation, wound status, anticoagulation, and foot or ankle pathology can materially change procedural risk.
- New weakness, sensory loss, foot pain, wound concerns, fever, or postoperative symptoms require clinical assessment.
