# Male Urethra

The male urethra is a **tubular channel approximately 18–20 cm in length** that serves as both a **urinary conduit and reproductive passage**, extending from the bladder neck to the external urethral meatus.

![Diagram of the male urethra and its segments](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0e/Scheme_male_reproductive_system-en.svg/800px-Scheme_male_reproductive_system-en.svg.png)
*Overview of male urogenital anatomy showing the relationship between the urethra, bladder, prostate, and penile structures.*

## Anatomic Divisions

The urethra is categorized into segments based on anatomy and surgical function:

### Posterior Urethra (approx. 3–4 cm)

- **Prostatic Urethra (3–4 cm):** Traverses the prostate gland and receives the openings of the **ejaculatory ducts**. Its walls are intimately attached to the prostatic lobes.
- **Membranous Urethra (2–3.5 cm):** Passes through the urogenital diaphragm and represents the **narrowest portion** of the channel. It is surrounded by areolar tissue and the voluntary fibers of the **external urethral sphincter**.

### Anterior Urethra (approx. 15–16 cm)

- **Bulbar Urethra:** Contained within the bulb of the **corpus spongiosum**. Its lumen widens to form the **urethral bulb (intrabulbar fossa)**, where semen collects before expulsion.
- **Penile (Pendulous) Urethra:** Extends through the penile shaft, lying within the corpus spongiosum nearer to the dorsum than the ventrum. It terminates at the **fossa navicularis**, a dilation in the glans penis that may help convert a narrow, fast stream into a higher-pressure jet to prevent self-contamination during voiding.

### Surgical Classification

From a surgical perspective, the urethra is often divided into the **bulbomembranous** (striated sphincter and proximal bulb), **bulbospongy** (from the membranous segment to the suspensory ligament), and **pendulous/penile** segments.

## Histologic Structure and Glands

The urethral lining transitions throughout its length to suit its environmental needs:

| Segment | Epithelium |
|---|---|
| Proximal (bladder neck / prostatic) | Transitional (urothelium) |
| Membranous & bulbar | Stratified columnar |
| Penile segment | Pseudostratified columnar |
| Fossa navicularis & meatus | Stratified squamous (non-keratinized → keratinized) |

The urethra is equipped with various glands:

- **Glands of Littré:** Minute, mucus-secreting cells with ducts opening toward the meatus to provide lubrication prior to ejaculation.
- **Bulbourethral (Cowper's) Glands:** Located on either side of the membranous urethra; however, their ducts travel distally to empty into the **bulbar** segment.
- **Lacuna Magna:** A large pocket found on the roof of the fossa navicularis.

## Physiology and Sphincteric Mechanisms

Continence is maintained by a dual-sphincter complex:

- **Lissosphincter (Internal Sphincter):** Composed of smooth muscle under autonomic control, responsible for **passive continence at rest**.
- **Rhabdosphincter (External Sphincter):** An omega-shaped striated muscle loop that provides **active continence during stress** and helps propel semen during ejaculation.

The urethra is no longer viewed as a passive conduit; it **actively opens** at the start of micturition through specific muscle contractions. Furthermore, specialized cells in the proximal urethra support a **urethro-vesical reflex**, allowing the urethra to trigger detrusor contractions in the bladder.

## Neurovascular Supply

### Innervation

- **Autonomic:** The **inferior hypogastric plexus** provides sympathetic and parasympathetic fibers to the smooth muscle, reaching the urethra through dorsolateral and dorsal approaches.
- **Somatic:** The **pudendal nerve** (specifically S4) provides motor innervation to the rhabdosphincter, while sensation is transmitted via the **dorsal nerve of the penis**.

### Vascular Supply

Primary arterial supply is via the **internal pudendal artery**, which gives rise to the **penile artery**. This further branches into the **bulbar and urethral arteries**. The urethral artery supplies the corpus spongiosum and anastomoses with the dorsal artery at the glans.

| Segment | Arterial Supply |
|---|---|
| Posterior urethra | Inferior vesical artery, middle rectal artery |
| Bulbar urethra | Bulbar artery (branch of internal pudendal) |
| Penile urethra | Urethral artery (branch of internal pudendal) |
| Glans / fossa navicularis | Dorsal artery anastomosis |

### Lymphatic Drainage

- **Anterior urethra** → superficial and deep inguinal lymph nodes
- **Posterior urethra** → pelvic lymphatic channels (external iliac, obturator groups)

## Clinical Significance

:::note Stricture Disease
The bulbar segment is highly susceptible to strictures because the surrounding spongy tissue reacts to infection — often originating in the glands of Littré — with dense fibrous proliferation known as **spongiofibrosis**. The degree of spongiofibrosis determines surgical approach: superficial fibrosis may allow non-transecting repair, while deep fibrosis typically requires excision and anastomosis or graft augmentation.
:::

:::note Urethral Injury Patterns
The **membranous urethra** is the most vulnerable segment in pelvic fracture urethral injuries (PFUI), as it is tethered to the urogenital diaphragm while the prostate is displaced superiorly. The **bulbar urethra** is the most common site of straddle-mechanism blunt trauma injuries.
:::
