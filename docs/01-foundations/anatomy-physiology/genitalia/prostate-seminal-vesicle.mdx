# The Prostate & Seminal Vesicles

The prostate and seminal vesicles are accessory reproductive structures in the male pelvis. The prostate surrounds the proximal urethra below the bladder; paired seminal vesicles lie posterior to the bladder and join the vas deferens to form the ejaculatory ducts. This page provides gross-anatomy orientation, not guidance for benign prostatic hyperplasia, cancer, infertility, ejaculation disorders, or pelvic surgery.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## The prostate

The prostate is a fibromuscular gland situated inferior to the bladder and anterior to the rectum. Its base is continuous with the bladder neck; its apex rests on the external urethral sphincter. A fibrous capsule encloses the gland, and a visceral layer of pelvic fascia surrounds the capsule, vessels, and nerves. Denonvilliers' fascia separates the prostate and seminal vesicles from the rectum posteriorly.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Zonal and urethral orientation

McNeal zonal anatomy describes peripheral, central, and transition zones, together with anterior fibromuscular stroma. The peripheral zone is the largest glandular zone; the central zone surrounds the ejaculatory ducts; the transition zone surrounds part of the urethra between the bladder and verumontanum. The anterior fibromuscular stroma is predominantly non-glandular.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The prostatic urethra traverses the gland. The paired ejaculatory ducts pass from posterior-lateral to medial-inferior and open into the prostatic urethra at the seminal colliculus (verumontanum), adjacent to the prostatic utricle.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Vessels, lymphatics, and nerves

The prostate receives branches from the internal iliac system, commonly including inferior vesical, internal pudendal, and middle rectal contributions. Its venous plexus communicates with the deep dorsal penile venous system and drains to internal iliac veins. Regional lymphatic drainage includes obturator, internal iliac, and external iliac basins.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Autonomic fibers arise through the inferior hypogastric plexus and form the prostatic plexus. The neurovascular structures at the posterolateral prostate are a distributed network rather than a single isolated cord; they continue toward the erectile tissues of the penis. This relationship is clinically important but does not itself determine an operative approach or expected functional outcome.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## The seminal vesicles

The seminal vesicles are paired, coiled, blind-ending glands located posterior to the bladder, superior to the prostate, and anterior to the rectum. Each lies lateral to the ampulla of the vas deferens. Its duct joins the ampulla to create an ejaculatory duct, which traverses the prostate to open at the verumontanum.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Their arterial supply commonly arises from middle or inferior vesical and middle rectal vessels; venous drainage is through the vesical plexus to internal iliac veins. Lymphatic drainage follows to internal and external iliac nodes. Innervation is from hypogastric nerves and the inferior hypogastric plexus.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Functional orientation

Seminal-vesicle secretions contribute a substantial share of seminal fluid and include alkaline fluid, fructose, prostaglandins, and semenogelin. Semenogelin participates in post-ejaculatory coagulation; prostate-specific antigen contributes to subsequent liquefaction. Sympathetic activity contributes to emission, when seminal-vesicle and prostatic contractions move fluid into the posterior urethra.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
