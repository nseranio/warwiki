# The Testicles & Scrotum

The testes are paired reproductive glands with endocrine and sperm-producing functions. They are suspended in the scrotum by the spermatic cords, with the epididymides attached along their posterior borders. This page is a gross-anatomy orientation, not guidance for acute scrotal pain, torsion, infertility, malignancy, cryptorchidism, varicocele, hydrocele, infection, or scrotal reconstruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Testis and epididymis

Each testis is enclosed by the dense tunica albuginea and is largely covered by the double-layered tunica vaginalis, except posteriorly and superiorly where the epididymis and spermatic cord attach. Septa extend inward from the tunica albuginea to organize the testicular parenchyma. Seminiferous tubules drain toward the rete testis and efferent ductules, which enter the head of the epididymis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The epididymis lies on the posterior testis and is conventionally divided into head, body, and tail. Its duct continues from the tail as the vas deferens, which ascends in the spermatic cord through the inguinal canal and ultimately joins the duct of the seminal vesicle to form the ejaculatory duct.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Scrotal coverings and spermatic cord

The scrotum is divided by a median septum. Its superficial skin and dartos layer cover the spermatic cord and testis. The cord's coverings reflect layers of the abdominal wall: external spermatic fascia, cremasteric fascia and muscle, and internal spermatic fascia. The tunica vaginalis derives from the processus vaginalis; persistent patency can create communication between the peritoneal cavity and scrotum.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Important cord contents include the testicular artery, artery to the vas deferens, cremasteric artery, pampiniform venous plexus, vas deferens, lymphatics, and genital branch of the genitofemoral nerve. The relationship of these structures is relevant to procedures in the inguinal and scrotal regions, but procedural decisions require condition-specific evidence and expertise.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Blood, lymphatic, and nerve supply

The principal testicular arteries arise from the abdominal aorta; additional supply is provided by the artery to the vas deferens and cremasteric artery. Venous return begins through the pampiniform plexus, which converges as the testicular vein. The right testicular vein drains to the inferior vena cava and the left to the left renal vein.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Testicular lymphatics follow the testicular vessels retroperitoneally to pre-aortic and related para-aortic nodal territories, reflecting embryologic descent. Scrotal skin has separate superficial inguinal drainage. Sensory and autonomic pathways travel with the cord; symptoms from testicular pathology may be referred to lower abdominal or groin regions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Functional orientation

Leydig cells produce androgens, while seminiferous tubules support sperm production. The scrotal location and pampiniform venous plexus contribute to a cooler local environment for sperm production and maturation. These broad physiologic relationships do not establish an individual cause of infertility or indicate treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
