# The Adnexa

The adnexa include the ovaries, fallopian tubes, and their mesenteric, vascular, neural, and connective-tissue relationships. They are relevant during lateral pelvic dissection because the ovarian vessels in the infundibulopelvic ligament and the ureter are close neighbors.[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)

This is an anatomic orientation, not a protocol for adnexal surgery, cancer-risk reduction, torsion, ectopic pregnancy, infertility, ovarian-vein disease, hormone care, or cancer staging.

See also [The Uterus](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/genitalia/uterus), [The Ureters](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/urinary-tract/ureter-anatomy-physiology), and [Pelvic Vascular Anatomy](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/pelvis-support/pelvic-vascular-anatomy).

## Ovary and attachments

The ovary is an intraperitoneal gonad located lateral to the uterus. The mesovarium attaches it to the broad ligament; the utero-ovarian ligament connects it to the uterus; and the suspensory (infundibulopelvic) ligament connects it to the pelvic sidewall.[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)

The ovarian artery is a direct aortic branch that courses through the infundibulopelvic ligament. Ovarian arterial supply also communicates with uterine-artery branches through the utero-ovarian region. The right ovarian vein drains to the inferior vena cava and the left ovarian vein to the left renal vein.[[3]](#ref3)

The broad ligament is a double peritoneal layer with mesometrium, mesosalpinx, and mesovarium portions. It carries vessels, nerves, lymphatics, and related pelvic structures. The exact relation of the ureter, vessels, adnexa, and any mass must be established in the individual operative field.[[2]](#ref2)

## Fallopian tube

Each fallopian tube extends from the uterine horn toward the ipsilateral ovary in the mesosalpinx. Its anatomic regions are the uterine (intramural), isthmic, ampullary, and infundibular portions. The distal infundibulum has fimbriae adjacent to the ovary and opens to the peritoneal cavity.[[1]](#ref1)

Tubal blood supply arises through anastomoses between ovarian and uterine arterial branches. The tube contains mucosa, smooth muscle, and serosa; ciliary activity, muscular contraction, and secretory fluid contribute to gamete transport.[[1]](#ref1)

## Surgical and clinical boundaries

- The infundibulopelvic ligament, ovarian vessels, ureter, and iliac vessels require patient-specific identification in lateral pelvic surgery.
- Do not infer a safe dissection plane, vessel-control method, salpingectomy or oophorectomy approach, tumor-risk estimate, fertility prognosis, or lymph-node template from this page.
- Adnexal pain, suspected torsion or ectopic pregnancy, fever, unexpected bleeding, a new pelvic mass, or postoperative symptoms needs urgent or timely clinical assessment according to the presentation.
