# The Penis

The penis supports urination and sexual function. Its gross anatomy is organized into a root, body, and glans; the body contains paired corpora cavernosa dorsally and the corpus spongiosum with the spongy urethra ventrally. This page is a gross-anatomy orientation, not guidance for erectile dysfunction, priapism, trauma, cancer, congenital conditions, or reconstructive procedures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Gross anatomy

The root is formed by the bulb of the corpus spongiosum and the paired crura of the corpora cavernosa. The crura attach along the ischiopubic rami and are invested by ischiocavernosus; bulbospongiosus overlies the bulb. The free body contains the erectile tissues, spongy urethra, fascia, vessels, nerves, and mobile skin. Distally, the corpus spongiosum expands as the glans; the external urethral meatus is at its tip.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Erectile bodies and coverings

| Structure | Orientation and role |
| --- | --- |
| **Corpora cavernosa** | Paired dorsal erectile bodies; each is covered by tunica albuginea and contains a deep penile artery. |
| **Corpus spongiosum** | Ventral erectile body surrounding the spongy urethra; continues into the bulb proximally and glans distally. |
| **Tunica albuginea** | Fibrous covering of the cavernosa that provides structural containment during erection. |
| **Buck's fascia** | Deep penile fascia superficial to the tunica; encloses the erectile bodies and invests the dorsal neurovascular structures. |
| **Dartos and skin** | Superficial mobile coverings; the prepuce, when present, covers the glans and attaches ventrally through the frenulum. |

The penis is also supported proximally by the suspensory and fundiform ligaments. The suspensory ligament arises from the pubic symphysis; the fundiform ligament descends from the linea alba and blends with the dartos fascia.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Blood, lymphatic, and nerve supply

The internal pudendal system is the principal arterial supply. Deep penile arteries run within the corpora cavernosa and give helicine branches to erectile tissue; dorsal arteries run on the dorsum; arteries to the bulb supply the bulbous spongiosum and urethra. External pudendal branches supply the skin. Venous return includes superficial dorsal drainage from skin and subcutaneous tissue and deep dorsal drainage from the erectile tissues.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Lymphatic drainage differs by territory: penile skin and most perineal skin drain to superficial inguinal nodes, while the glans and distal spongy urethra drain to deep inguinal nodes. More proximal urethral and cavernosal drainage reaches internal iliac nodes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Innervation combines autonomic and somatic pathways. Cavernous nerves carry autonomic fibers to erectile tissue; the pudendal nerve gives rise to the dorsal nerve, which travels with dorsal vessels and supplies penile and glans sensation. Sacral parasympathetic pathways contribute to erection, while sympathetic activity participates in emission and detumescence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Functional orientation

Erection requires relaxation and filling of the cavernosal spaces with restricted venous outflow; the bulbospongiosus and ischiocavernosus muscles can augment rigidity. The corpus spongiosum remains comparatively compressible around the urethra. These relationships help explain why disorders of arterial inflow, corporal tissue, tunical containment, venous outflow, or innervation may affect erectile function, but diagnosis and treatment belong in condition-specific clinical resources.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
