# Clitoral Anatomy and Physiology

The **clitoris** is a multiplanar sensory and erectile organ composed of the glans, body, paired crura, and closely associated vestibular bulbs. The glans is its visible, densely innervated, non-erectile part; the erectile structures extend beneath the vulvar surface along the pubic arch.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Components and Terminology

| Structure | Anatomy and measured size | Operative relationship |
|---|---|---|
| **Glans** | Midline, non-erectile sensory tissue beneath the prepuce; mean length 6.40 mm and width 5.14 mm in a 21-study anatomy meta-analysis<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | The glans and its corona are the landmarks exposed by preputial retraction. |
| **Prepuce and frenulum** | The prepuce (hood) and ventral frenular attachments arise where the labia minora meet anteriorly; mean preputial length 23.19 mm in the same synthesis<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | Preputial scarring may conceal the glans or trap keratin. |
| **Body** | Paired corpora cavernosa within a tunica albuginea, joined at a septum; mean length 25.46 mm and width 9.00 mm<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | Lies beneath the hood and mons, anterior to the urethra. |
| **Crura** | Paired proximal extensions of the corpora fixed to the ischiopubic rami beneath ischiocavernosus; mean length 52.41 mm and width 8.71 mm<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | Ramal dissection or deep fixation can approach the dorsal clitoral nerves. |
| **Vestibular bulbs** | Paired spongy erectile bodies alongside the vaginal vestibule, covered by bulbospongiosus; mean length 52.00 mm and width 10.33 mm<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | Their close continuity with the clitoris and relationship to the distal urethra and vagina matter in anterior vulvar surgery. |

Pauls described the organ's overall extent as approximately 9–11 cm; this is an anatomic orientation, not a surgical measurement for an individual patient.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The Society of Gynecologic Surgeons anatomy group found 687 terms for 22 clitoral and vestibular-bulb components in its structured review. Its proposed vocabulary includes *clitoral root*, *clitoral-vestibular septum*, and *spongiosus nerve of the vestibular bulb*; established writing still uses both “vestibular bulbs” and “bulbs of the clitoris.”<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The corporal bodies contain vascular sinusoids and trabecular smooth muscle within a fibrous tunica. The glans lacks the same erectile architecture. Histologic and pharmacologic studies identify nitric-oxide synthase, soluble guanylyl cyclase, and phosphodiesterase type 5 in clitoral erectile tissue.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Clitourethrovaginal Relationships

The clitoral body and bulbs sit near the distal urethra, anterior vaginal wall, and vestibule, joined by connective-tissue supports. The *clitourethrovaginal complex* describes their anatomic proximity and dynamic interaction during sexual response; it is a functional model rather than a separate dissectible organ.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> For the surrounding surface anatomy and pelvic fascial planes, see [The Vulva](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/genitalia/female-external-genitalia) and [Perineum](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/pelvis-support/perineum).

## Neurovascular Supply and Operative Planes

The paired **dorsal nerves of the clitoris** arise from the pudendal nerve (S2–S4), ascend beside the ischiopubic rami, and pass along the dorsal clitoral body into the glans. The dorsal arteries accompany them and arise from the internal pudendal arterial system; deep arteries supply the corpora. Autonomic cavernous fibers approach through the vaginal plexus alongside the urethra and vagina, while spongiosus fibers supply the bulbs.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

In 27 cadavers, Jackson and colleagues found dorsal-nerve widths of 2–4 mm beside the lateral crura and 1–2 mm at the distal body, with greater nerve density dorsally than ventrally. A seven-cadaver immunohistochemical study likewise found the greatest somatic and autonomic density dorsal to the distal body.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> These findings favor superficial, visually controlled dissection around the prepuce and preservation of the dorsal fascial and neurovascular tissues. Deep dissection or sutures extending from the preputial midline toward the inferior pubic rami put the nerves at risk; a ventral approach is not an absolute nerve-free zone.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- **Incontinence surgery:** In an anatomic study using fetal reconstructions and 14 adult hemipelves (7 TVT and 7 TVT-O), the TVT-O lay a mean 9 mm from the dorsal nerve; the retropubic TVT needle pierced autonomic fibers alongside the urethra. The study describes anatomical proximity, not a measured clinical injury rate.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Vulvar and hood surgery:** Plan incisions against the glans, corona, prepuce, corporal body, and dorsal nerve course; preserve the investing tissues during clitoral mobilization or cyst excision.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Urethral and diverticulum surgery:** Anterior vaginal and periurethral dissection approaches autonomic fibers along the urethra; preserve the vaginal and periurethral tissue planes when feasible.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Gender-affirming vaginoplasty and clitoroplasty:** Neoclitoral construction depends on a perfused, innervated glans and its preserved dorsal bundle. See the [clitoroplasty technique](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/clitoroplasty-preputial-flap).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

For the pudendal course and pelvic plexus, see [Pelvic Neuroanatomy](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/pelvis-support/pelvic-neuroanatomy).

## Embryology and Homology

The clitoris and penis develop from the genital tubercle. In the clitoral developmental pathway, the vestibular plate canalizes and its folds remain unfused, forming the open vestibule and labia minora; the penile pathway includes androgen-dependent urethral-fold fusion. The clitoral corpora and penile corpora are homologous, as are the vestibular bulbs and penile corpus spongiosum.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> These relationships guide tissue selection and neurovascular preservation in genital reconstruction.

## Engorgement and Orgasm

Neural and endothelial nitric oxide increases cyclic GMP in clitoral smooth muscle, activating protein kinase G and potassium channels; relaxation permits corporal and bulbar engorgement. Phosphodiesterase type 5 degrades cyclic GMP. This pathway was demonstrated principally in experimental rat clitoral tissue, with human clitoral tissue showing the relevant proteins.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Somatic sensation from the glans and autonomic vasomotor input from pelvic plexuses participate in arousal; the clitoris, urethra, vaginal wall, and pelvic floor can interact during orgasm without a single obligatory stimulation route.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Imaging

High-resolution pelvic MRI depicts the clitoral body, crura, bulbs, urethra, and adjacent masses in multiple planes. On T2-weighted images, the crura course beneath the inferior pubic rami toward the body; the bulbs flank the introitus. MRI is useful when a mass, altered postoperative anatomy, or a urethral/periurethral lesion makes the examination incomplete. Ultrasound can assess a superficial focal lesion and Doppler flow when the question is vascular.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
