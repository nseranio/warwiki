# Male Urethra

The male urethra is the terminal conduit of the urinary tract and also carries semen. This page is a source-bounded anatomic orientation; it does not provide diagnostic, catheterization, trauma, cancer-staging, or operative guidance.

![Gray's Anatomy figure of the male urethra laid open on its anterior surface](/img/anatomy/gray1142-urethra.png)

*Gray's Anatomy Fig. 1142 — male urethra laid open on its anterior surface.*

## Regional organization

From the bladder outlet to the external urethral meatus, the urethra is described by the structures it traverses. Broadly, these include a pre-prostatic/bladder-neck portion, the prostatic urethra, the membranous urethra, and the spongy urethra. The spongy urethra includes bulbar and penile portions and continues into the fossa navicularis within the glans. Different clinical and anatomic sources use slightly different regional groupings; this page uses the location-based terms rather than a disease-specific classification.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The prostatic urethra passes through the prostate. The membranous urethra lies in the deep perineal region with the external urethral sphincter. The spongy urethra is surrounded by the corpus spongiosum and extends through the penis to the meatus.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Wall and surrounding tissues

The urethral lining changes by region. The most proximal portions are lined by urothelium, while more distal regions transition through columnar and stratified squamous epithelium. This regional variation is an anatomic and histologic observation, not a substitute for evaluation of a urethral disorder.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Within the penis, the corpus spongiosum surrounds the urethra and lies beneath the paired corpora cavernosa. The fossa navicularis is the distal expanded segment within the glans.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

![Lateral cross-section of the penis showing the corpus spongiosum containing the urethra](/img/anatomy/penis-lateral-cross-section.jpg)

*Lateral cross-section of the penis showing the urethra within the corpus spongiosum and the paired corpora cavernosa.*

## Sphincter and neural orientation

At the bladder outlet, smooth muscle contributes to the internal urethral sphincter. The external urethral sphincter is a striated-muscle structure associated with the membranous urethra. Its voluntary control is supplied through the pudendal nerve from sacral roots S2–S4.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The urethra also receives autonomic and sensory input through pelvic pathways. For the broader pelvic autonomic anatomy, see [Pelvic Neuroanatomy](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/pelvis-support/pelvic-neuroanatomy).
