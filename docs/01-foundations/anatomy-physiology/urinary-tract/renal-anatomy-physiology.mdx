# The Kidneys

The kidneys are paired retroperitoneal organs that filter blood, form urine, help regulate fluid, electrolyte, and acid-base balance, and contribute endocrine functions. This page is a source-bounded anatomy and physiology orientation; it does not provide imaging interpretation, renal-function assessment, operative planning, or treatment guidance.

![Kidney anatomy — internal structure and position](/img/anatomy/openstax-kidney.jpg)

*OpenStax Anatomy & Physiology — internal structure of the kidney. CC BY 4.0.*

## Position and gross structure

The kidneys sit in the retroperitoneum on either side of the vertebral column, inferior to the adrenal glands. The right kidney is generally lower than the left because of its relationship to the liver. Each kidney has an outer cortex and an inner medulla; the medulla is organized into renal pyramids whose papillae drain into the collecting system.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

At the medial hilum, the renal artery enters and the renal vein and renal pelvis leave the kidney. The renal pelvis receives the major calyces; these receive urine from minor calyces at the papillae and continue as the ureter. Calyceal number and shape vary among individuals.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The kidney is surrounded by a fibrous capsule, perirenal fat, and renal fascia. Its anterior and posterior relations differ by side and are important regional anatomy, but procedure-specific access and complication guidance belong on focused clinical and operative pages.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Blood supply and innervation

Renal perfusion is supplied by the renal arteries, which commonly divide into anterior and posterior branches and then into smaller intrarenal vessels. Accessory renal arteries can occur and may provide end-arterial supply to a renal segment. Venous blood exits through the renal veins; the left renal vein receives tributaries including the left gonadal and adrenal veins before crossing anterior to the aorta.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The renal plexus accompanies the renal vessels. It carries predominantly sympathetic and visceral-afferent fibers; renal discomfort can be referred through thoracolumbar segments. The details and clinical interpretation of renal pain require condition-specific assessment.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Nephrons and urine formation

Nephrons are the kidney's microscopic functional units. Each includes a renal corpuscle, where filtration begins, and tubular segments that modify filtrate before it enters the collecting system. Renal corpuscles and convoluted tubules lie primarily in the cortex; loops of Henle and collecting ducts extend into the medulla.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Filtration produces fluid that is subsequently reabsorbed and secreted along the tubules. The kidneys thereby excrete metabolic waste while participating in water, solute, electrolyte, and acid-base regulation. They also secrete renin and erythropoietin and convert vitamin D to its active form.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
