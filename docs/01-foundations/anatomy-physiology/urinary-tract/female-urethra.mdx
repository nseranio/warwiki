# Female Urethra

The female urethra connects the bladder neck to the external urethral meatus in the vestibule. This page is a source-bounded anatomy orientation, not a diagnostic, continence, surgical, or treatment protocol. Individual anatomy and pelvic-floor relationships vary.

## Regional relationships

The urethra lies behind the pubic bones and anterior to the vagina. Its external opening is in the vulvar vestibule, anterior to the vaginal opening. The proximal urethra and vagina traverse the perineal membrane, and their relationship to the bladder neck, anterior vaginal wall, pelvic floor, and pubic support structures is clinically important but three-dimensional and variable.

The female urethra is generally shorter than the male urethra and is not divided into the same named segments. For adjacent anatomy, see [The Bladder](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/urinary-tract/bladder-anatomy-physiology), [The Vagina](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/genitalia/vaginal-anatomy), [The Vulva](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/genitalia/female-external-genitalia), and [The Perineum](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/pelvis-support/perineum).

## Wall and sphincter complex

The urethral wall includes mucosa, submucosal tissue, smooth muscle, and outer connective and striated-muscle relationships. Epithelial type varies along the urethra. Periurethral vascular tissue and connective tissue contribute to its regional structure.

The female urogenital sphincter complex includes the sphincter urethrae, compressor urethrae, and urethrovaginal sphincter. These structures have close relationships with the perineal membrane and vagina and should not be represented as a single flat “urogenital diaphragm.”

## Vascular and neural orientation

Arterial supply arises from regional branches of the internal iliac system, including vaginal and internal-pudendal contributions. Venous and lymphatic drainage follows regional pelvic and vulvar pathways. Exact vessels, nodal territories, and their procedural relevance require focused source review.

Somatic innervation of the external urethral sphincter includes pudendal fibers from S2–S4 and contributions from sacral plexus branches. Autonomic pathways contribute to lower-urinary-tract coordination. The functional effects of individual pathways should not be inferred from regional anatomy alone.
