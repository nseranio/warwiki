# The Retropubic Space

The retropubic, prevesical, or **Retzius** space is an anterior extraperitoneal potential space between the pubic bone and urinary bladder. This page provides a source-bounded anatomic orientation. It is not a procedural guide, operative-safety map, diagnostic framework, or treatment protocol.

## Regional relationships

The space lies posterior to the pubic symphysis and anterior to the bladder. It generally contains loose connective tissue and fat. The prevesical/retropubic compartment is continuous with adjacent anterior pelvic and paravesical connective-tissue planes; its apparent size and shape vary with the individual and with bladder filling.

Commonly described boundaries include the pubic symphysis anteriorly, bladder posteriorly, parietal peritoneum superiorly, and pelvic-support structures inferiorly. The lateral relationships include the pubic rami, obturator internus region, and tendinous pelvic fascia. These landmarks are variable and should not be treated as fixed procedural measurements.

## Fascial and visceral orientation

The retropubic space is part of the anterior extraperitoneal pelvis. It is associated with fascial continuities between the anterior abdominal wall, bladder, and pelvic floor. The bladder neck and urachal remnant have important regional relationships, but organ-specific anatomy is addressed on the relevant urinary-tract pages.

The space provides an anatomic corridor to the bladder and adjacent pelvic structures. The clinical meaning of a given route depends on the procedure, prior surgery, individual anatomy, and imaging; this overview does not specify access methods, instrument trajectories, or operative decisions.

## Neurovascular orientation

Neurovascular structures are present near the lateral and inferior portions of the space. They include the obturator neurovascular region and venous networks associated with the bladder neck and genital structures. Variant vessels may cross the posterior surface of the pubic bone. These relationships matter in surgery, but this page does not provide prevalence estimates, injury predictions, or technique recommendations.

For broader context, see [Pelvic Vascular Anatomy](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/pelvis-support/pelvic-vascular-anatomy), [Pelvic Neuroanatomy](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/pelvis-support/pelvic-neuroanatomy), and [Bony Pelvic Anatomy](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/pelvis-support/bony-pelvic-anatomy).
