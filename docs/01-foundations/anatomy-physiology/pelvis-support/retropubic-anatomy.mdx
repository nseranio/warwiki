# The Retropubic Space

The retropubic space (space of **Retzius**) is the potential preperitoneal compartment behind the pubic symphysis and anterior to the bladder. It is an important operative field for retropubic radical prostatectomy, Burch colposuspension, retropubic midurethral slings, preperitoneal hernia repair, selected artificial urinary sphincter reservoir placement, and suprapubic bladder access. Hemorrhage from pelvic fracture or surgery may collect here. Its ligamentous, venous and variable arterial boundaries matter to reconstructive surgery; adjacent pelvic lymph-node dissection extends laterally beyond the central space.

See also [Bony Pelvic Anatomy](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/pelvis-support/bony-pelvic-anatomy) for Cooper's ligament / pubic anatomy; [Pelvic Vascular Anatomy](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/pelvis-support/pelvic-vascular-anatomy) for the corona mortis; [The Bladder](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/urinary-tract/bladder-anatomy-physiology) and [The Prostate](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/genitalia/prostate-seminal-vesicle) for organ pedicles; [The Penis](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/genitalia/penis-anatomy-physiology) for dorsal vein complex anatomy.

## Boundaries

The retropubic space is a **potential space** developed through a preperitoneal approach; the precise entry plane varies with the operation and prior surgery. Its boundaries:

| Boundary | Structure |
|---|---|
| **Anterior** | Posterior aspect of the **pubic bones** and **symphysis**, covered by transversalis fascia |
| **Posterior** | Anterior surface of the **bladder** and **prostate** (M) / bladder and anterior vagina (F) |
| **Superior** | Peritoneal reflection from anterior abdominal wall onto the bladder; its position changes with bladder filling and prior surgery, so a fixed fingerbreadth rule is unsafe |
| **Inferior** | Puboprostatic attachments and bladder neck (M); pubovesical / pubourethral attachments (F), with the perineal membrane farther caudal |
| **Lateral** | Obturator internus fascia; **arcus tendineus fasciae pelvis (ATFP)** continuing back to the ischial spine; obturator neurovascular bundle anterolaterally |

The retropubic space communicates laterally with the paravesical space on each side of the bladder, and superolaterally with the preperitoneal space of the anterior abdominal wall, which is why pelvic-fracture hematomas can extend both upward and laterally.

## Structural Contents

### Periosteal and ligamentous

- **Pubic symphysis and posterior pubic body.** The anterior wall.
- **Cooper's (pectineal) ligament.** Thick fibrous band along the pectineal line of the superior pubic ramus; a fixation landmark for Burch colposuspension and some preperitoneal hernia repairs.
- **Pubourethral ligaments (F).** Attachments from the posterior pubis toward the urethra and anterior vaginal wall; described subdivisions vary among anatomy studies and should not be mapped directly onto male prostatectomy.
- **Puboprostatic ligaments (M).** From the posterior pubis toward the prostatic apex and urethral sphincter region. Some continence-sparing techniques preserve these attachments when oncologically and technically feasible; this is not universal.
- **Arcus tendineus fasciae pelvis (ATFP, "white line").** Fibrous band from the posterior pubis to the ischial spine along the obturator fascia; the lateral attachment of the anterior vaginal wall (Level-II support).
- **Arcus tendineus levator ani (ATLA).** Origin of levator ani from the obturator fascia.

### Vascular

The retropubic space is highly vascular. The dominant structures:

- **Dorsal vein complex (DVC / Santorini's plexus).** Male venous network anterior to the prostatic apex and urethra that can bleed substantially during prostatectomy.
- **Deep dorsal vein of the penis.** Contributes to the male prostatic venous plexus. The deep dorsal vein of the clitoris drains toward the female vesical venous plexus, not a prostatic plexus (see [The Perineum](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/pelvis-support/perineum)).
- **Inferior vesical vessels** (M) or **vaginal branches** (F). Approach the bladder base or upper vagina from the pelvic sidewall; nearby autonomic nerves travel in their own variable fascial pathways rather than inside a fixed vascular bundle.
- **Obturator neurovascular bundle.** Nerve, artery and vein at the anterolateral edge of the space, emerging through the obturator canal.
- **Corona mortis.** Variable arterial and/or venous connection between obturator and external iliac or inferior epigastric systems across the superior pubic ramus. The adjacent [vascular-anatomy review](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/pelvis-support/pelvic-vascular-anatomy) documents a pooled estimate near 46% across 1,455 hemipelves with marked heterogeneity, not a universal 20–30% rate or safe distance. Anticipate and identify crossing vessels during Cooper's-ligament dissection.
- **Accessory pudendal arteries.** Variable supralevator arterial routes near the prostate or apex. A 23-study meta-analysis estimated presence in 28.5% of patients, whereas surgical detection and definitions differ. Preservation is desirable when feasible, but transection is not established as an independent cause of postoperative erectile dysfunction; one 880-patient cohort found no significant association after adjustment. ([Meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28202357/); [cohort](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28859491/))

### Neural

- **Obturator nerve.** Exits the obturator canal anterolaterally; supplies medial-thigh sensation and the adductor compartment. At risk during TOT sling passage, obturator lymphadenectomy, and retropubic dissection extending laterally.
- **Pelvic plexus branches to the bladder and prostate.** Enter the bladder base posterolaterally; not typically encountered in pure anterior retropubic dissection but at risk at the junction of the bladder neck and prostate.

### Adipose and lymphatic

Loose preperitoneal fat (the "Retzius fat pad") and prevesical lymphatics lie here; the obturator lymph-node basin is lateral and is one of several pelvic drainage basins relevant to bladder and prostate cancer. It is not the single first landing zone for both cancers, and pelvic lymphadenectomy templates are disease- and guideline-specific.

### Urachus

The median umbilical ligament (**urachus remnant**) runs from the bladder dome to the umbilicus in the midline across the retropubic space anteriorly. Urachal cysts, sinuses, and adenocarcinomas arise along this ligament.

## Key Fascial Planes

| Plane | Approach |
|---|---|
| **Between abdominal-wall fascia and bladder** | Prevesical plane developed for selected retropubic operations; the method and safety assessment differ across prostatectomy, Burch, sling and bladder access. |
| **Cave of Retzius (preperitoneal plane)** | Same as above — the classical eponymous name. |
| **Between bladder neck and prostate** | The plane of bladder-neck-sparing radical prostatectomy. |
| **Between urethra and pubic symphysis** | Plane around anterior urethral / prostatic attachments; division or preservation depends on the approach, anatomy and oncologic constraints. |
| **Around Cooper's ligament** | Identify variable arterial **and venous** corona-mortis connections before fixation or dissection; no single prevalence or fixed safe zone applies. |

## Operative Uses of the Retropubic Space

### Retropubic radical prostatectomy (RRP)
In the classical open approach, retropubic exposure gives access to the anterior prostate, endopelvic fascia, puboprostatic attachments, dorsal vein complex, apex and urethra. Posterior dissection then follows the prostate–rectum fascial relationship, with nerve-sparing and reconstruction adapted to tumor location and anatomy. Robotic approaches use related landmarks but different access, visualization and preservation maneuvers; the technique is not an invariant step sequence.

### Burch colposuspension
Retropubic access permits suspension of periurethral or paravaginal tissue near the bladder neck to Cooper's ligament. Suture number, material and operative approach vary. It remains a mesh-free surgical option for selected stress urinary incontinence patients, including some undergoing concurrent abdominal surgery; it has not been eliminated by midurethral slings.

### Retropubic midurethral sling (TVT)
Passage behind the pubis places a midurethral sling through the retropubic space; route and device instructions vary. Bladder or urethral perforation and bleeding are important hazards, and rare bowel or major-vessel injuries have been reported. The [AUA/SUFU female SUI guideline](https://www.auanet.org/documents/Guidelines/PDF/2023%20Guidelines/SUI%20Unabridged%20FINAL%20080223.pdf) recommends intraoperative cystoscopy with midurethral sling surgery to assess lower-tract integrity; it does not make unguided trocar passage a general safety instruction.

### Stoppa / laparoscopic pre-peritoneal hernia repair
Preperitoneal access includes the medial retropubic region and the myopectineal orifice. Mesh coverage and fixation technique depend on the repair; Cooper's ligament is a landmark, not an instruction to anchor every mesh there. Identify variant corona-mortis vessels when dissecting nearby.

### Artificial urinary sphincter (AUS)
The **pressure-regulating balloon** may occupy a retropubic or prevesical position or a selected ectopic position depending on prior pelvic surgery, radiation and device-specific planning. Pump and cuff positions vary with anatomy and indication; this regional page does not substitute for the device IFU or operative technique article.

### Male sling (urethral sling)
Some male slings use retropubic anchoring, but transobturator and other designs do not all traverse the retropubic space. The relevant risks and fixation points depend on the device and route.

### Suprapubic catheter / cystostomy
Percutaneous or open access reaches the bladder through the lower anterior abdominal or prevesical region. Adequate bladder filling can separate bowel from the intended route, but distension alone does not establish a safe trajectory, especially after lower abdominal surgery or when filling is inadequate. Ultrasound or cystoscopic guidance or an open approach may be appropriate to the patient and setting; bowel injury remains possible. ([BAUS practice-guideline notice](https://www.baus.org.uk/professionals/baus_business/publications/10/baus_suprapubic_catheter_practice_guidelines); [UK audit](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31532853/))

### Cystotomy
Selected abdominal approaches to bladder surgery may develop this anterior space for access. Vesicovaginal fistula repair, diverticulectomy and partial cystectomy have different routes and exposure requirements; not every case traverses Retzius.

### Pelvic lymphadenectomy (bladder, prostate, gynecologic)
The obturator, external iliac and internal iliac regions are common components of pelvic node dissection, with boundaries and extent determined by disease and the applicable guideline. The obturator basin is not the sole first-echelon site for bladder and prostate cancer; node work lies largely lateral to the central Retzius space. The obturator nerve is an important landmark to identify and protect.

## Sex-Specific Features

### Female
- **Puboprostatic analog** corresponds to the pubovesical or pubourethral ligaments.
- **Arcus tendineus fasciae pelvis (ATFP)** is the lateral attachment of the anterior vaginal wall; detachment produces a paravaginal defect.
- **Cooper's ligament** suture fixation is the Burch principle.
- The retropubic space sits anterior to the urethra, bladder, and anterior vaginal wall.

### Male
- **Puboprostatic ligaments** anchor the prostatic apex to the pubis.
- **DVC** receives the penile dorsal system and can be a major source of bleeding in apical dissection.
- **Accessory pudendal arteries** may pass near the apex; detection rates vary by imaging, dissection and definition. Preserve a functional vessel where feasible without claiming that division inevitably causes erectile dysfunction.
- The retropubic space lies anterior to the bladder and prostate; the seminal vesicles are farther posterior.

## Clinical Correlations for the Reconstructive Surgeon

- **DVC control in prostatectomy.** Venous bleeding can obscure apical planes; suture, stapling and athermal strategies have different tradeoffs. No single DVC maneuver guarantees preserved membranous urethral length or continence.
- **Corona mortis.** Anticipate a crossing artery and/or vein during superior-pubic-ramus dissection. Preserve it when possible or obtain controlled hemostasis if division is required; do not use a fixed coordinate or automatic clip-and-divide rule.
- **Retropubic sling complications.** Bladder perforation and retropubic hematoma are recognized risks; rare severe vascular or bowel injuries require prompt recognition. Cystoscopy assesses urinary-tract injury, not vessel or bowel integrity.
- **Pubic osteomyelitis / osteitis pubis.** Persistent pubic pain after retropubic surgery has a broad differential; infection or inflammatory change may require imaging and specialist evaluation. Antibiotics, drainage or implant removal depend on the established diagnosis and source control.
- **Infection spread.** Severe perineal infection can extend along contiguous fascial planes, but retropubic involvement is not inevitable; surgical exploration follows the observed disease extent.
- **Preperitoneal pelvic packing.** In selected pelvic-fracture hemorrhage, packs can be placed in the preperitoneal or paravesical region as part of a trauma-control pathway; sequencing with other hemorrhage control depends on physiology and local protocol.
- **Urachal pathology.** Remnants and rare tumors can arise along the median umbilical ligament. Management of a suspected urachal carcinoma requires staging and multidisciplinary planning; en-bloc urachus and umbilicus resection with an appropriate bladder margin is a common surgical approach, not a universal instruction for every lesion.
- **Anatomy preservation for post-prostatectomy continence.** Membranous urethral length, apical dissection and anterior attachments are relevant, but individual preservation maneuvers have different evidence strength and must not compromise cancer control. A 2023 RCT review found the clearest early-continence signal for Retzius-sparing surgery, with lower-certainty support for some other maneuvers. ([RCT review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37597197/))
- **Retropubic access in the radiated / reoperative pelvis.** Scarred planes and altered vascular anatomy call for case-specific planning; routine ureteric stenting or a particular dissection path cannot be prescribed from this regional-anatomy page.
- **Robotic Retzius-sparing radical prostatectomy.** Posterior access preserves more anterior retropubic attachments and is associated with faster early continence recovery in pooled studies. Positive-margin signals vary by stage and study; long-term oncologic and functional choices need patient-specific review. ([2025 synthesis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40444077/))
