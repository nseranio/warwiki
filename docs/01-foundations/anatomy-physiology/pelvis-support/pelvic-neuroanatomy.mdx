# Pelvic Neuroanatomy

This page is a source-bounded orientation to the pelvic autonomic and somatic nerve networks. It is not a surgical-dissection guide, diagnostic framework, or treatment protocol. Pelvic neural anatomy is variable; use procedure-specific anatomy, imaging, and current specialty guidance when making clinical decisions.

## Autonomic pelvic network

The **inferior hypogastric (pelvic) plexus** is a bilateral network in the extraperitoneal connective tissue of the pelvic sidewall. It lies alongside the rectum, medial to the internal iliac vessels, and communicates with organ-specific plexuses. It contains sympathetic, parasympathetic, and visceral-afferent fibers.

Sympathetic fibers reach the pelvis through the superior hypogastric plexus and paired hypogastric nerves. Parasympathetic fibers reach the pelvic plexus through the pelvic splanchnic nerves from S2–S4. This convergent arrangement helps explain why pelvic-visceral innervation is not captured by a single, fixed nerve trunk.

The plexus gives rise to smaller plexuses associated with pelvic viscera, including rectal, prostatic, and uterovaginal networks. Its relationship to the rectum, vagina, prostate, bladder, and pelvic sidewall should be understood as a variable three-dimensional relationship rather than a universal set of measurements or operative landmarks.

## Somatic perineal pathway

The paired **pudendal nerves** arise principally from the ventral rami of S2–S4. Each nerve leaves the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen, courses around the region of the ischial spine and sacrospinous ligament, re-enters through the lesser sciatic foramen, and continues in the pudendal canal on the medial obturator-internus fascia.

![Course of the pudendal nerve from the sacral roots through the pudendal canal to its terminal branches](/img/diagrams/pudendal-nerve.svg)

*Schematic course of the pudendal nerve. Individual branch patterns and relationships vary.*

Within or near the pudendal canal, commonly described terminal branches are the inferior rectal nerve, perineal nerve, and dorsal nerve of the penis or clitoris. The pudendal nerve has sensory and motor roles in the perineum and external genitalia, including innervation related to the external urethral and anal sphincters. The exact branching pattern and target structures vary between individuals.

## Functional orientation

Autonomic and somatic pathways work with central neural control and pelvic-floor structures. For that reason, bladder, bowel, sexual, and sensory functions cannot be assigned reliably to a single peripheral structure in isolation. This page intentionally does not convert anatomy into symptom diagnoses, lesion-level predictions, procedural targets, or recommendations for blocks, neuromodulation, or surgery.
