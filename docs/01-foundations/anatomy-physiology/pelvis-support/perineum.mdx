# The Perineum

The **perineum** is the diamond-shaped region of the pelvic outlet below the pelvic diaphragm. It is the operative field for bulbar urethroplasty, radical perineal prostatectomy, AUS placement, perineorrhaphy and OASIS repair, pelvic-floor reconstruction, Fournier's debridement, vaginal and vulvar reconstruction and perineal-hernia repair, and it is the dissection route for most posterior-approach pelvic procedures. This article describes surgical anatomy (boundaries, fascial pouches, neurovascular geometry), the sex differences that shape male and female reconstructive technique, and the physiology of continence, sexual function, defecation and parturition to the depth needed in the operating room.

See also [The Vulva](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/genitalia/female-external-genitalia), [Male Urethra](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/urinary-tract/male-urethra), [Female Urethra](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/urinary-tract/female-urethra), [The Vagina](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/genitalia/vaginal-anatomy), and [Pelvic Neuroanatomy](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/pelvis-support/pelvic-neuroanatomy).

## Boundaries and the Diamond

The perineum is bounded by four bony and ligamentous landmarks, and a transverse line between the ischial tuberosities divides it into two triangles:

| Triangle | Anterior boundary | Posterior boundary | Lateral boundary | Contents |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Urogenital (UG)** | Pubic symphysis + inferior pubic rami | Interischial line | Ischiopubic rami | Urethra, perineal membrane; vagina in women; root of penis / clitoris; Bartholin / Cowper's glands |
| **Anal** | Interischial line | Coccyx + sacrotuberous ligaments | Ischial tuberosities | Anal canal, external anal sphincter, ischiorectal fossae, pudendal canal |

The ischial tuberosities, pubic symphysis, and coccyx together form the four corners of the diamond. In lithotomy, the UG triangle is anterior and the anal triangle is posterior.

## The Perineal Body — The Structural Anchor

The **perineal body** is a pyramidal fibromuscular structure in the midline between the UG and anal triangles, anterior to the anus.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> Textbook dimensions are about 20–40 mm craniocaudally in women, and a scoping review found substantial heterogeneity in how ultrasound studies measure it. It receives the insertions of:

- **Bulbospongiosus / bulbocavernosus**
- **Superficial transverse perinei**
- **Deep transverse perinei**
- **External anal sphincter** (subcutaneous and superficial parts)
- **Puborectalis** (superiorly)
- **Posterior vaginal wall fibromuscularis** (in women)
- **Perineal membrane**

A dissection of 56 cadaveric specimens (46 adults, 10 neonates) described the perineal body as a three-layer criss-crossing scaffold and not a simple insertion point:<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

1. **Superficial layer.** External-anal-sphincter fleshy fibers continuous across the midline with bulbospongiosus or bulbocavernosus.
2. **Middle layer.** Tendinous extension of the superficial transverse perinei in a criss-cross pattern.
3. **Deep layer.** Tendinous fibers of the deep transverse perinei decussating with the contralateral muscle.

Some muscle fibers cross the midline in a figure-8 pattern instead of terminating at the perineal body. Diffusion-tensor imaging in 10 nulliparous women and four men, with a male cadaveric micro-CT for validation, showed continuity between superficial perineal and external-anal-sphincter fibers. Cadaveric dissection of 43 specimens found bulbospongiosus and the superficial EAS continuous, with common innervation, in 92.3%.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> These findings support a connected anatomic model. They do not establish shared function.

**The Perineal Complex Triad and urogenital hiatus closure.** DeLancey and colleagues propose that the levator ani, perineal membrane and perineal body form a **perineal complex triad** that closes the urogenital hiatus. MRI of 30 nulliparous women and dissection of 10 cadavers indicated a cranioventral levator line of action, and in cadavers loss of the connection between the left and right perineal membranes through the perineal body widened the hiatus. This is an anatomic hypothesis. It does not establish that perineal-body loss alone causes prolapse, rectocele or stress incontinence.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

**Why the perineal body matters in reconstruction.** The perineal body connects the posterior vaginal wall, perineal membrane and superficial anal-sphincter region. It is generally wider in women (about 20–40 mm) than in men (about 10–15 mm). Obstetric tears and prior surgery may leave a deficient perineum associated with a wide genital hiatus or posterior-compartment symptoms. Perineorrhaphy repairs selected distal support defects. Apical prolapse and sphincter injury are evaluated and treated separately.

## The Anal Triangle

| Structure | Dimensions / features | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal anal sphincter (IAS)** | **~0.3–0.5 cm thick**; thickened circular smooth muscle of the rectum<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> | Under **autonomic** control; major contributor to resting anal tone.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[16]](#ref16)</sup> Injury can contribute to passive leakage, but symptoms are multifactorial.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> |
| **External anal sphincter (EAS)** | **~0.6–1.0 cm thick**; commonly described in subcutaneous / superficial / deep portions<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>; predominantly slow-twitch, fatigue-resistant fibers | Somatic sacral innervation through variable pudendal and direct branches; voluntary squeeze reinforces closure.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> Injury can contribute to urgency-associated leakage, but does not by itself predict a symptom pattern.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> |
| **Puborectalis** | Levator-ani sling drawing anorectum anteriorly | Contributes to the **anorectal angle**, which changes during squeeze and defecation; measured angles vary with technique and individual anatomy.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| **Ischiorectal (ischioanal) fossa** | Fat-filled paired spaces lateral to the anal canal; bounded by obturator internus laterally, levator ani superomedially, and the anal canal medially | Communicates posteriorly via a **deep postanal space** — the anatomic path of **horseshoe abscesses** |
| **Pudendal (Alcock's) canal** | Fascial tunnel on the medial surface of obturator internus | Carries the **pudendal nerve and internal pudendal vessels** |
| **Anococcygeal body (ligament)** | Fibromuscular raphe between anal canal and coccyx | Receives fibers of levator ani and EAS |

The entire sphincter complex extends approximately 4 cm up the anal canal, with the EAS overlapping the distal IAS by approximately 1–2 cm.

### EAS innervation varies by subdivision

In the 43-cadaver series of Plochocki and colleagues, an inferior rectal nerve arising from S3 and S4 separately from the pudendal nerve innervated only the subcutaneous EAS in 89.2%. The superficial EAS received perineal-nerve branches and the deep EAS a branch from the nerve to levator ani.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> The authors interpret this as potential functional independence of the three EAS portions.

## Vascular Supply

### Arterial

The dominant supply is the **internal pudendal artery** (anterior division of the internal iliac). It exits the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen, wraps around the ischial spine, re-enters the perineum through the lesser sciatic foramen, and runs through Alcock's canal on the medial obturator internus. It gives the following branches:

| Branch | Territory |
|---|---|
| **Inferior rectal artery** | Anal canal, EAS, perianal skin |
| **Perineal artery** (→ posterior scrotal / labial, transverse perineal) | Superficial pouch, scrotum / labia, perineal body |
| **Artery of the bulb of the penis (M) / vestibular bulb (F)** | Bulb of spongiosum / vestibular bulb |
| **Urethral artery and dorsal / deep artery of the penis (M) / clitoris (F)** | Urethral corpus spongiosum; dorsal NVB; cavernosal / corporeal engorgement |

The **external pudendal arteries** (from the femoral) supply anterior scrotum, anterior labia majora and mons skin. The Martius labial fat-pad flap can be based superiorly on external-pudendal branches or inferiorly on internal-pudendal branches, and pedicle choice sets its reach.

### Venous and lymphatic

Venous drainage parallels the arteries. The deep dorsal vein of the penis drains into the prostatic (Santorini's) plexus; the deep dorsal vein of the clitoris drains into the vesical venous plexus, with communicating female pelvic plexuses.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> Superficial veins drain toward the external pudendal or saphenous system.

Lymphatic drainage is split:

- **Perineal skin and superficial structures** drain to the superficial inguinal nodes
- **Deeper structures** (bulb, urethra, deep pouch) drain to the internal iliac basins
- This split drives the inguinal-LND template in vulvar / penile SCC and the pelvic-LND template in posterior urethral cancer.

## Innervation — The Pudendal Nerve Dominates

### Pudendal nerve (S2–S4)

The principal nerve of the perineum. After traversing Alcock's canal it gives three terminal branches:

| Branch | Territory / function |
|---|---|
| **Inferior rectal nerve** | EAS motor; perianal sensation |
| **Perineal nerve — deep** | Bulbospongiosus, ischiocavernosus, transverse perinei, **external urethral rhabdosphincter** (via **Onuf's nucleus**) |
| **Perineal nerve — superficial (posterior scrotal / labial)** | Posterior scrotum / labia majora sensation |
| **Dorsal nerve of the penis / clitoris** | Dominant sensory input to the glans |

Additional peripheral contributions:

- **Ilioinguinal** and **genital branch of genitofemoral** (L1–L2). Anterior scrotum, labia majora and mons
- **Posterior femoral cutaneous, perineal branch.** Lateral perineum and upper medial thigh
- **Autonomic fibers** from the inferior hypogastric plexus. Erectile tissue, glands, and pelvic viscera; cavernous and clitoral nerves ride with the lateral NVB and with the posterior route (see [The Penis](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/genitalia/penis-anatomy-physiology))

### Onuf's nucleus

A sacral (S2–S4) somatic motor-neuron pool that innervates the striated external urethral and external anal sphincters. Relative preservation in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis has been reported. Sphincter function in advanced disease still needs its own assessment.

### Dorsal penile / clitoral nerve — the sensory-critical branch

Runs along the dorsal erectile structures after passing beneath the pubic arch (see [The Penis](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/genitalia/penis-anatomy-physiology) / [The Vulva](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/genitalia/female-external-genitalia)). Branching and crossing vary, so no clock-face position is a reliably nerve-free dissection or injection plane. Injury during genital or perineal reconstruction can cause sensory loss.

## Physiology

The perineum supports four overlapping functions, and preserving or reconstructing one can trade off against another.

### Continence

- **External urethral sphincter** (voluntary, pudendal) provides phasic closure on cough and Valsalva and a tonic contribution to resting urethral pressure. The **guarding reflex** recruits the sphincter reflexively before abdominal pressure rises.
- **Anal continence** depends on a layered mechanism:<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)[[18]](#ref18)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
  - **Mechanical.** IAS tone (bulk of resting pressure), EAS squeeze (voluntary reinforcement), puborectalis-generated anorectal angle (flap-valve), and the sealing effect of anal mucosal folds and vascular cushions.
  - **Sensory.** Pelvic-floor stretch receptors detect rectal distension; anal-transition-zone receptors discriminate gas, liquid, and solid ("rectoanal sampling").
  - **Reflex.** The rectoanal inhibitory reflex relaxes the IAS on rectal distension, allowing sampling. Involuntary EAS, puborectalis and sphincter-of-O'Beirne contractions, together with retrograde propagating sigmoid and rectal motor patterns, prevent continuous anal outflow between defecations.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **Pelvic-floor and perineal support.** The perineal body, perineal membrane, levator-ani complex, urethral support and sphincter function interact during rises in abdominal pressure. Support defects may contribute to female stress urinary incontinence, and no single perineal structure explains most cases (see [The Vagina](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/genitalia/vaginal-anatomy)).

### Sexual function

- **Ischiocavernosus.** Contracts during sexual response and compresses the crura, contributing to penile rigidity; the cited study is in mammalian penile tissue, so its role in clitoral engorgement is not established.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Bulbospongiosus (bulbocavernosus).** In men, contracts to expel semen and urine through the urethra and maintains intraspongiosal pressure; in women, contributes to introital narrowing, clitoral engorgement via vestibular-bulb compression, and the rhythmic contractions of orgasm.
- **Erectile tissue of the clitoris, vestibular bulbs, corpora cavernosa, and corpus spongiosum.** Autonomic-driven engorgement during arousal.
- **Glans sensation.** Carried prominently by dorsal penile or clitoral nerve branches, with variable branching and overlapping sensory supply.
- **Three-part reflex control:** erection is parasympathetic-dominant (S2–S4, NO-mediated vasodilation); emission is sympathetic-dominant (T10–L2, contraction of vas deferens, seminal vesicles, and prostate); ejaculatory expulsion uses somatic S2–S4 and pudendal pathways and rhythmic perineal-muscle contractions. A postmortem study of galaninergic neurons (6 men, 6 women) and 384 men with clinically complete spinal cord injury support a spinal ejaculation generator at L3–L5. Injury involving L3–L5 was the sole independent predictor of failed ejaculation with penile vibratory stimulation.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)[[20]](#ref20)[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

### Defecation

- Voluntary relaxation of puborectalis opens the anorectal angle.
- Voluntary relaxation of EAS combined with reflex IAS relaxation on rectal distension permits evacuation.
- **Perineal-body support** contributes to posterior vaginal-wall support. A distal support deficit can coexist with rectocele or obstructed defecation, and symptoms also depend on other pelvic structures and bowel function.

### Childbirth

- The perineum stretches markedly during the second stage of labor.
- The perineal body is the most common site of obstetric laceration.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Episiotomy** is not recommended routinely. When performed, a mediolateral cut is associated with a lower risk of extension to a 3rd- or 4th-degree tear than a midline cut.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Obstetric anal sphincter injuries (OASIS).** 3rd-degree tears involve the EAS, with or without IAS injury (3c includes both); 4th-degree tears involve EAS, IAS and anorectal mucosa. Primary repair includes separate IAS repair when identifiable; partial-thickness EAS injury is approximated end-to-end, while full-thickness EAS injury can be repaired end-to-end or by overlap. Later fecal-incontinence treatment follows evaluation and is not set by tear grade alone.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)[[25]](#ref25)</sup>
- **Levator ani avulsion** is an often unrecognized component of birth injury. In an MRI study, 32 of 160 vaginally primiparous women (20%) had a visible levator defect and none of 80 nulliparous women did. Half of the primiparous women had stress incontinence, so the sample was not population-based.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> Levator avulsion is associated with later prolapse.

## Vascular Anatomy of Perineal Perforator Flaps

Beyond the internal and external pudendal supply described above, a microdissection of 11 adult cadavers (22 sides) mapped four relatively constant perforating arteries relevant to perforator-flap planning. Vascular anatomy is confirmed before a flap is planned.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

1. **Inguinal perforating branch** of the superficial external pudendal artery
2. **Perineal perforating branch** of the superficial external pudendal artery
3. **Perforating branch** of the anterior cutaneous branch of the obturator artery
4. **Perforating branch** of the lateral branch of the posterior scrotal / labial artery

These form upper, middle, and lower anastomoses in the deep fascia above the adductor compartment, the anatomic substrate for the pudendal-thigh (Singapore), internal pudendal artery perforator (IPAP), and anterior obturator artery perforator flaps used in vaginal, vulvar, and perineal reconstruction.

## Sex Differences for the Reconstructive Surgeon

| Feature | Men | Women |
|---|---|---|
| **UG-triangle contents** | Single pass-through (urethra); bulb + crura of penis | Double pass-through (urethra + vagina); vestibular bulbs + clitoral crura |
| **Perineal body** | Narrower (~10–15 mm); less structural role | Wider (~20–40 mm); **central reconstructive target** for posterior compartment and EAS support |
| **Sphincter geometry in deep pouch** | Rhabdosphincter as anterolateral omega-loop around membranous urethra | Striated sphincter complex U-shaped in distal 2/3 of urethra, embedded in perineal membrane |
| **Homologue glands** | Cowper's (bulbourethral) — in the **deep** pouch | Bartholin (greater vestibular) — in the **superficial** pouch |
| **Scrotum / labia majora** | Scrotum + dartos | Labia majora + dartos; Martius flap territory |
| **Erectile-organ homology** | Penis has glans, shaft, paired crura and a dorsal sensory nerve | Clitoris has glans, body, paired crura and a dorsal sensory nerve; urethra remains anatomically separate |
| **Operative targets** | Bulbar urethroplasty, AUS, radical perineal prostatectomy, PFUI repair | Perineorrhaphy, OASIS repair, vestibulectomy, Martius flap harvest, vaginal reconstruction |

## Clinical Correlations for the Reconstructive Surgeon

- **Bulbar urethroplasty.** The bulbar urethra sits in the superficial perineal pouch invested by bulbospongiosus. Perineal incision, then midline split of bulbospongiosus, then mobilise the corpus spongiosum, then perform anastomotic or augmentation repair. The central perineal tendon (perineal body) is preserved to avoid postoperative anterior ejaculatory dysfunction and perineal weakness.
- **Artificial urinary sphincter (AUS) cuff placement.** Perineal approach to the bulbar urethra; cuff placed around the corpus spongiosum distal to the crura. Transcorporal cuff placement (through the tunica of the corpora cavernosa) reinforces a compromised spongiosum in reoperative or radiation cases.
- **Radical perineal prostatectomy and perineal approach to the prostate.** Curved incision anterior to the anus, through the central tendon, between ischiocavernosus muscles, exposing the prostate posterior to the bulb. Useful historically and in selected patients where abdominal access is contraindicated.
- **Pelvic-fracture urethral injury (PFUI) repair.** Perineal approach with crural separation, infrapubic resection, and anastomotic urethroplasty; deep-pouch dissection required for proximal exposure. Supracrural or transpubic extensions for long or complex defects.
- **Perineorrhaphy and OASIS repair.** Reconstruct the disrupted perineal-body muscles according to the defect. In OASIS, identify and repair the IAS separately when torn; use end-to-end EAS approximation for partial-thickness tears and either end-to-end or overlap for full-thickness tears, then repair the perineal muscles and overlying tissue.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>
- **Vestibulectomy for vulvodynia.** Excision of painful vestibule with vaginal advancement flap, the neuroproliferative vestibulodynia operation. See [The Vulva](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/genitalia/female-external-genitalia).
- **Martius labial fat-pad flap.** Interposition flap for VVF, RVF, UVF, urethral diverticulectomy, complex perineal wounds. Harvested through a vertical labia-majora incision; pedicle choice (superior external pudendal versus inferior internal pudendal) dictates reach. See [The Vulva](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/genitalia/female-external-genitalia).
- **Gracilis muscle flap.** Reliable regional flap for large perineal defects (rectovaginal / rectourethral fistulae, perineal hernia after APR, vulvar reconstruction). Pedicled on the medial circumflex femoral artery.
- **Pudendal-thigh (Singapore) fasciocutaneous flap.** Axial flap from the groin crease supplied by branches of the internal pudendal artery; used for vaginal, vulvar, and perineal reconstruction.
- **Fournier's gangrene.** Necrotizing fasciitis of the dartos / Colles' / Scarpa's plane; testes and cord usually spared. Emergency wide debridement, broad-spectrum antibiotics, diversion as needed, delayed reconstruction.
- **Perineal hernia.** Post-APR / post-exenteration defect through the levator and perineal floor; repair may use mesh, regional flap tissue (for example gracilis or VRAM), or a combination selected for the defect and tissue quality.
- **Ischiorectal fossa abscess / horseshoe abscess.** Drained through the anal triangle; attention to the deep postanal space where horseshoe abscesses bridge. Unrecognised horseshoe extensions produce recurrent abscess / complex fistula.
- **Pudendal neuralgia and Alcock's canal entrapment.** Perineal or genital pain may worsen while sitting. The Nantes criteria support a clinical diagnosis, and neither symptoms nor a block alone establish the entrapment site. Blocks and decompression are specialist options in selected patients.
- **Sacrospinous ligament fixation (SSLF).** Vaginal-vault suspension to the sacrospinous ligament near the ischial spine, directly adjacent to the pudendal NVB in Alcock's canal. Injury produces gluteal or perineal pain and pudendal neuropathy.
- **Radical vulvectomy and perineal reconstruction.** After wide local excision or radical vulvectomy ± inguinal LND. Reconstruction ladder: primary closure, then local V-Y or rhomboid flaps, then Singapore (pudendal-thigh) flap, then gracilis or VRAM myocutaneous flap for large defects.
- **Cauda equina and S2–S4 lesions.** May cause saddle sensory change and bladder, bowel or sexual dysfunction, with patterns varying by lesion. New saddle sensory symptoms, retention or progressive deficits warrant urgent evaluation, and incomplete saddle anesthesia does not exclude cauda equina syndrome.
- **Male sling procedures.** Selected transobturator slings for post-prostatectomy SUI support or reposition the bulbar urethra, with mechanisms and fixation that vary by device.
- **Pelvic radiation sequelae.** Radiation can impair tissue healing and complicate fistula repair. Well-vascularized interposition tissue (Martius, gracilis or omentum) is often used in complex irradiated repairs.
- **Gender-affirming surgery (perineal component).** Phalloplasty and metoidioplasty use perineal tissue for urethral lengthening; vaginoplasty creates the neovaginal cavity in the plane between urethra and prostate (anterior) and rectum (posterior), with the perineal body preserved to support the introitus.
