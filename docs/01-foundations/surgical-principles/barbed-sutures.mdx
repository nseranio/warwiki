# Barbed Sutures

Barbed sutures self-anchor along a running closure, reducing knot tying and the need to maintain tension between passes. This reference brings together device characteristics, operative handling, clinical comparisons, and laboratory studies. Manufacturer specifications describe the product; clinical trials address patient outcomes; animal and bench findings explain possible mechanisms without establishing human complication rates. For the broader materials framework, see [Sutures](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/sutures).

## Biomechanics — What the experiments measured

A bench comparison of specific 4-0 Quill and PDS samples reported the following measurements; these are experimental results, not values for all barbed products:<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

| Property | Barbed (Quill 4-0) | Conventional (PDS 4-0) | Clinical implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Breaking strength** | **11.57 N** | 18.41 N | Lower breaking strength in these samples; does not define a universal gauge conversion |
| **Stiffness** | **172.8 N/mm²** | 128.9 N/mm² | Greater stiffness in this test; relevance depends on tissue and device |
| **Suture-line shortening** | **26.6%** | 40.7% | Less shortening in this experiment; clinical anastomotic patency was not established |

**Holding strength with Hem-o-lok / Lapra-Ty clips** for renorrhaphy (Paka 2017):<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

- **2-0 V-Loc: 15.9 N** in this test
- 2-0 Stratafix: 13.8 N
- 2-0 Vicryl: 5.7 N (lower measured holding strength; p &lt; 0.05)

### Closure-system testing — Gupta 2016

**Stratafix PGA-PCL with Lapra-Ty clips at 3 weeks.**

**Gupta 2016** laboratory functional-duration study of closure systems at 4 N tension (not a patient failure rate):<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup>

- **Stratafix PGA-PCL with Lapra-Ty/Hem-o-lok: 82% failure at 3 weeks**
- **V-Loc 180 with Lapra-Ty/Hem-o-lok: 24% failure at 3 weeks** (p &lt; 0.0001)

These results concern the tested formulation, clip arrangement, load and laboratory conditions. They are useful for understanding system failure and do not establish an 82% clinical renorrhaphy failure rate or a current brand-wide contraindication. The exact closure system and its manufacturer instructions matter.<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup>

A 100-patient comparison by Cakici and Canda examined bidirectional barbed closure against poliglecaprone with rhabdosphincter reconstruction. Because the comparison included different reconstruction methods, its continence findings cannot isolate the effect of barbs alone.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup>

## The Upper-Tract Controversy — Pyeloplasty

Small series and a meta-analysis describe conflicting pyeloplasty experiences. Their technique, product, age group, denominator and failure definitions are important; the reports remain useful despite their limited size.

### The alarm signals

| Source | Population | Suture | Finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Liatsikos 2013 multi-center** | 6 adults, laparoscopic pyeloplasty | **Quill SRS** | **5 / 6 (83%) recurrent stricture** requiring further intervention. The authors advised against the application pending clarification; this was a six-patient signal, not a randomized estimate for all devices.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Radford 2018 pediatric** | 10 children, robotic pyeloplasty | V-Loc for pelvic closure | **4 / 10 (40%) worsening hydronephrosis** requiring restenting (n=1) or redo-pyeloplasty (n=3). **All 4 were asymptomatic** — failures detected only on imaging. Histology: **"plaque-like" inflammatory reaction** at the suture line. Vicryl group: 1 / 15 (6.7%) redo<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |

### The counter-evidence

| Source | Population | Suture | Finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sorokin 2016** | 13 adults, robotic pyeloplasty | 4-0 V-Loc | Barbed (n = 13) and nonbarbed (n = 13) groups; postoperative renography showed no obstruction in 10 of 11 patients with follow-up imaging in each group. A small comparative series; it does not establish equivalence<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **Anand 2022 meta** | 5 studies | — | **OR time shorter** with barbed (p = 0.01); **redo-pyeloplasty trend higher but not significant** — **RR 6.00** (95% CI 0.78–46.14; p = 0.09); no difference in total complications; **significant heterogeneity**<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |

**Interpretation.** The wide CI on the redo-pyeloplasty point estimate (0.78–46.14) means the pooled data neither confirm nor refute a 6-fold risk. Product matters. Quill SRS was involved in the 83% failure series, and V-Loc has produced mixed results. Whether this reflects the bidirectional versus unidirectional mechanism, the PDO versus polyglyconate material, or the learning curve is not resolved.

### Proposed mechanisms and preclinical observations

| Mechanism | Why it matters in UPJ / ureter |
|---|---|
| **Suture-line shortening** (26.6%) | A bench measure of shortening raises a concern about constriction; it is not a measured clinical UPJ diameter change |
| **Tissue cutting by barbs** | Tissue trauma is a proposed mechanism; clinical causation is not established by a stiffness test |
| **Plaque-like inflammatory reaction** | Described in the Radford series; not proof that every barbed device produces the same reaction<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Stone formation on exposed barbs** | Yalcin 2018 rabbit bladder: **4 / 7 on V-Loc vs 1 / 7 on Vicryl at 6 wk** (p = 0.039)<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Suture migration** | **3 / 7 on V-Loc at 6 wk** (p = 0.050)<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Irreversible overtightening** | The line resists sliding back; an unsatisfactory pass may need revision rather than further traction |
| **Learning curve** | Liatsikos noted failures occurred "at least in the initial cases of each surgeon"<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

### The fibrosis paradox

In Yalcin's rabbit-bladder experiment, the tested barbed suture produced less inflammation and fibrosis histologically than Vicryl or Monocryl (p = 0.010 at 3 wk; p = 0.002 at 6 wk).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> In contrast, the small upper-tract series described above reported fibrotic narrowing or inflammatory failure after particular barbed-suture repairs. Different species, tissues, products and outcome definitions prevent resolving this apparent contrast from these studies alone. Mechanical effects are hypotheses, not proven explanations of individual clinical strictures.

## Vesicourethral anastomosis after minimally invasive prostatectomy

The accessible 2015 systematic review included three randomized and six observational studies (786 patients) comparing unidirectional barbed with conventional suture for VUA. It found shorter anastomosis time (mean difference −3.98 minutes) and total operative time (−10.06 minutes), but no statistically significant difference in leak, catheter duration, or continence at the reported follow-up intervals.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> The authors describe the intended VUA as watertight, optimally apposed, and tension-free; that does not support deliberately leaving a gap between the edges.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

The pooled leak estimate was imprecise (7.08% versus 3.37%; OR 2.17, 95% CI 0.86–5.50), and the review notes variation in technique and limited long-term data. Therefore, barbed suture is an available option for an experienced VUA team, and it is not a site-wide “standard of care” conclusion or a substitute for a validated anastomotic technique.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

Individual VUA studies include the randomized comparisons by Zorn, Williams and Sammon and the synthesis by Bai. Williams highlights the importance of tension and technique; Haga examined postoperative MRI tissue findings and urinary outcomes in an observational cohort. These provide useful technical and mechanistic context alongside the pooled clinical evidence.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

### Practical safety framing

- Follow the procedure-specific technique, device instructions, and institutional practice.
- Aim for apposition without ischemic traction. If approximation is unsatisfactory, correct it instead of relying on a generic “run loose” or intentional-gap rule.
- Do not infer a continence, stricture, or leak advantage from reduced suturing time alone.

## Renorrhaphy during partial nephrectomy

In a 2018 systematic review and meta-analysis of eight cohort studies (431 patients; no eligible randomized trials), barbed suture was associated with lower warm-ischemia time (mean difference −6.55 minutes) and operative time (−11.29 minutes). The same review reported fewer postoperative complications with barbed suture (OR 0.44, 95% CI 0.24–0.80), in contrast to the higher complication rate in the cross-specialty randomized meta-analysis below. Renal-function outcomes were not pooled.<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup> Confounding by tumour complexity, surgeon preference, and learning curve remains important.

A later single-centre randomized study assigned 50 selected T1 RAPN patients to double-needle barbed suture or Vicryl. It reported shorter mean warm-ischemia time (11.76 versus 14.56 minutes) and differences in short-term laboratory outcomes, but it was small, limited to selected T1 tumors, and explicitly called for larger multicentre trials.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

**Interpretation:** barbed suture is a reasonable technique to evaluate within an established renorrhaphy workflow. Present evidence does not establish a universal product preference, clip strategy, gauge conversion, or renal-function benefit.


The broader Bertolo review compares several renorrhaphy strategies, including running versus interrupted and single- versus double-layer repair. Those comparisons do not isolate the effect of barbs from the rest of the technique.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

### Additional clinical and technical reports

| Study | Design | n | WIT (barbed vs conventional) | Finding |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Olweny 2012** | Retrospective | 78 | **26.4 vs 32.8 min** (p = 0.001) | Bleeding requiring intervention: 3.4% vs 18.4% (p = 0.06)<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |
| **Erdem 2013** | Matched-pair | 66 | **19 vs 28 min** (p = 0.037) | Inner-layer renorrhaphy time 350 vs 505 sec (p = 0.004)<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> |
| **Liu 2015** | PADUA-matched | 82 | **20.7 vs 27.5 min** (p &lt; 0.05) | **GFR reduction 20.8% vs 29%** (p &lt; 0.05) — observational association<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> |
| **Hu 2025** | RCT | 50 | **11.76 vs 14.56 min** (p &lt; 0.05) | Short-term findings in a small selected cohort<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> |
| **Di Franco 2025** | Prospective | 55 | 22.5 min (mean) | Novel R-BEP technique with elongated PTFE pledgets; Feasibility findings in the reported cohort<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> |


These reports preserve useful operative experience. Differences in case selection, tumor complexity, closure method and learning curve limit causal comparisons; a process-time advantage does not establish a preferred closure for every renal defect.

## Bladder Closure — Mixed Evidence

**Advantages:**

- **Gözen 2012** pig bladder model: running barbed suture had the largest filling volume before leakage (419.7 mL), compared with running Vicryl (353.8 mL) and interrupted Vicryl (276.2 mL) (p &lt; 0.05)<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
- **Yalcin 2018:** less inflammation and fibrosis histologically than Vicryl or Monocryl<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

**Concerns (rabbit bladder model, Yalcin 2018):**<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

- Stone formation: 4 / 7 on V-Loc versus 1 / 7 on Vicryl at 6 wk (p = 0.039)
- **Suture migration: 3 / 7 on V-Loc at 6 wk** (p = 0.050)

These animal results inform mechanism and device-development discussions. They do not estimate human stone or migration risk, and the filling volumes above are not pressure measurements.

## Early preclinical urinary reconstruction

**Weld 2006** (earliest preclinical porcine evaluation) tested a prototype self-anchoring suture for pyeloplasty and bladder-neck anastomosis:<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

- **In-vitro failure force:** no difference (13.2 ± 2.6 N versus 14.1 ± 3.1 N; p = 0.22)
- **OR time:** no difference
- **Radiographic integrity:** no extravasation at 1 wk in any animal
- Histopathology showed more fibrotic reaction at the experimental bladder-neck anastomosis; this is an animal-model observation, not proof of a diversion contraindication.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

## Small Bowel Obstruction — Reported Risk and Denominator Evidence

**Clapp 2020 review of the literature and the MAUDE database** identified 18 published cases and 14 MAUDE reports of SBO secondary to barbed suture:<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

| Feature | Data |
|---|---|
| **Mechanism** | Exposed barbs on suture tail or cut end snag adjacent bowel → internal hernia or volvulus |
| **Mean time to presentation** | **26.3 days postop** (range 1–196 d) |
| **Presentation** | Abdominal pain 88.9%; vomiting 33.3%; distension 27.8% |
| **CT findings** | Mesenteric volvulus 5 cases; strangulation 2 |
| **Management in the reported cases** | The review concluded that these suture-related obstructions required exploration; laparoscopy succeeded in most |

The later **Sassun 2026** single-institution retrospective cohort identified 20,260 intra-abdominal operations using barbed suture. Of 264 patients diagnosed with intestinal obstruction, 102 underwent reoperation for obstruction; across 279 abdominal reoperations for any indication, operative-note review attributed none of the obstructions to barbed-suture entrapment. This supplies a large exposed denominator, but it has no non-barbed comparator and cannot rule out rare events, nonoperatively managed events, or those not captured by the institution's review. The specific published entrapment cases remain clinically relevant.<sup>[[34]](#ref34)</sup>

**Handling near mobile bowel:** cover or terminate the line according to the specific device and operation, avoiding unintended exposed barbed material. A loop or stopper does not eliminate risk from a remaining exposed tail. Case reports and MAUDE provide mechanism signals, not incidence denominators; the large cohort above should not be interpreted as proof of zero risk.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)[[34]](#ref34)</sup>

## Cross-Field Safety Meta-Analysis

**Lin 2016 meta** (17 RCTs, n = 1,992, across all surgical specialties):<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

- **Suture time** ↓ (SMD −0.95; p = 0.0001)
- **Operative time** ↓ (SMD −0.28; p = 0.003)
- Blood loss: no difference
- **Postoperative complications ↑ (OR 1.43; 95% CI 1.05–1.96; p = 0.03)**

**Su 2023 cosmetic-surgery meta** (14 studies, n = 2,259):<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>

- **Wound dehiscence ↑** (OR 1.60; p = 0.02)
- **Suture extrusion ↑** (OR 3.97; p = 0.0001), particularly with unidirectional barbed suture
- Infection, delayed healing, hematoma: no difference

These cross-specialty syntheses provide context; they cannot determine the complication rate or best material for an individual urinary reconstruction.

## Decision summary

| Setting | What the evidence can support | What it cannot support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| VUA | Modest time savings with no demonstrated pooled difference in the reported leak, catheter, or continence outcomes. | A universal standard-of-care designation, intentional gapped closure, or superior functional outcome. |
| Renorrhaphy | Time-related benefit is plausible; a small randomized study and older cohort synthesis support further comparative evaluation. | A universal device, clip, gauge, or long-term renal-function rule. |
| UPJ/ureter/ureteroenteric repair | Do not extrapolate VUA or renal-parenchymal data to a tubular urinary anastomosis. | A blanket ban, brand ranking, or universal risk estimate from small heterogeneous reports. |
| Bladder/bowel-adjacent closure | Handle exposed material thoughtfully and follow the specific procedure/device guidance. | Human complication rates inferred from animal, bench, case-report, or surveillance data. |

For injury-specific reconstruction, the ACS genitourinary-trauma guideline is a companion reference. Device choice is considered within the relevant repair and is not inferred from a different anastomosis.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup>
