# Reconstructive Ladder

The reconstructive ladder is a conceptual way to organize closure and tissue-replacement options, from simpler wound management through grafts, local or regional flaps, and free-tissue transfer. The later "reconstructive elevator" framing emphasizes selecting an option that fits the individual defect rather than using a fixed stepwise sequence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## What the framework can—and cannot—do

This framework can support structured discussion of available options. It does not by itself determine the right treatment. Defect characteristics, tissue vascularity, contamination, functional requirements, prior operations or radiation, donor-site effects, patient goals, and local expertise all matter.

An accessible wound-closure review describes the ladder as a spectrum from primary closure to more complex reconstruction, with the simplest effective option considered when appropriate.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> It does not support a universal sequence, a specific flap choice, or a prediction of success for an individual wound.
