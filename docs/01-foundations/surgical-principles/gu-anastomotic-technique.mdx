# GU Anastomotic Technique — Evidence &amp; Technique Reference

Urologic reconstruction lives and dies by its anastomoses. This article is the **cross-cutting evidence and technique reference** for how we sew at each reconstructive site — VUA (running vs interrupted, barbed, Rocco, Van Velthoven step-by-step, microskills that predict watertightness), Bricker vs Wallace ureteroileal with step-by-step detail, Anderson-Hynes pyeloplasty with bite-depth and ureter-first data, bladder closure (single- vs double-layer leak pressures, barbed-suture-stone caution), ureteral repair by location including the Lich-Gregoir 10-step, urethroplasty graft fixation (Barbagli dorsal onlay, ventral, transurethral ventral inlay), and the cross-cutting principles. It complements [Sutures](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/sutures) (materials and selection), [Needles](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/needles) (needle anatomy), [Bowel anastomosis](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/bowel-anastomosis) (bowel-specific technique), and the named techniques in [Surgical skills](/docs/foundations/surgical-skills) (per-stitch detail including Van Velthoven VUA, ski needle, Heaney, Parker-Kerr, SEXI stance exchange).

## 1. The Vesicourethral Anastomosis (VUA)

The most technically demanding step of radical prostatectomy and the best-studied anastomosis in urology.

### Van Velthoven — step-by-step (standard for MIS)

The **Van Velthoven single-knot running suture** is the dominant VUA technique in laparoscopic and robotic prostatectomy. In a series of 2,115 patients, bladder-neck-contracture (BNC) rate was **1.4%**, with 78% diagnosed within the first year. **Prior TURP and external-beam radiotherapy were independent BNC risk factors.**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

1. **Two sutures** (typically 3-0 poliglecaprone or 3-0 barbed polyglyconate) are **tied together at their tails** to create a double-armed suture
2. The first arm: **outside-in on the bladder at 5 o'clock**, then **inside-out on the urethra** at the same position
3. Proceeding **clockwise**, place 2–3 additional posterior bites
4. **Cinch down** — "parachute" the bladder neck down to the urethral stump
5. The second arm runs **counterclockwise** from 5 o'clock, completing the anterior wall
6. The two arms meet anteriorly at 12 o'clock and are **tied together** — hence "single-knot"<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

**Continuous-tension modification (Meeks 2009, n = 154):** assistant and third robotic arm maintain continuous tension on the sutures throughout. Eliminates Lapra-Ty clip requirement, reduces urine leaks, BNC, and migrated clips — equivalent continence, no additional learning curve.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

**Simone "Single Knot–Single Running" modification (n = 155):** a single suture (not two tied together) starting at 6 o'clock posteriorly, **incorporating posterior musculofascial reconstruction into the same running suture**. Significantly better continence at 1, 3, and 6 months vs conventional Van Velthoven (p = 0.01 at all time points), comparable operative time.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### Running vs interrupted — conflicting evidence, approach-dependent

| Study | Design | n | Comparison | Finding |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Can 2025** | RCT (open RRP) | 70 | Running vs interrupted | Running: **faster (21 vs 33 min; p = 0.001)**, less leakage (9% vs 23%). Interrupted: **better early Qmax at 1 mo** (23.3 vs 17.2 mL/s; p = 0.003). Continence similar.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Matsuyama 2015** | RCT (open RRP) | 60 | Running vs interrupted | Running: earlier catheter removal (HR 5.23; p = 0.003), **higher 1-mo pad-free rate** (20.7% vs 3.3%; p = 0.046), **0% vs 10% stricture rate**<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Köhler 2020** | Retrospective (open RRP) | 211 | Running vs interrupted | Interrupted: **dramatically better early continence** (7.7% vs 42.2% incontinence; p &lt; 0.001); shorter catheterization (4 vs 5 d); similar extravasation<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **Poulakis 2006** | Prospective (endoscopic extraperitoneal RP) | 250 | Running vs interrupted | Similar continence (91.5% vs 93% at 6 mo); similar extravasation and stricture; **running faster (16 vs 24 min; p &lt; 0.001)** and easier (difficulty 1 vs 3)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |

**Interpretation.** Running is faster and technically simpler. The "purse-string" concern motivates the interrupted camp and may explain Köhler's strong early-continence signal in open RRP. **By 3–6 months continence equalizes.** Approach usually decides the technique: **running dominates in robotic and laparoscopic surgery** because intracorporeal knot-tying is the rate-limiting step of interrupted work.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### Number of sutures — does quantity matter?

**Gallo 2007 RCT** (n = 90) compared **2-suture**, **4-suture**, and **6-suture** interrupted VUA:<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

- **No significant differences** in blood loss, drain removal time, hospitalization, stricture rate, erectile function, or continence
- **Only anastomosis time differed: 3.6 min (2-suture) vs 16.6 min (4-suture) vs 23.5 min (6-suture)** (p &lt; 0.001)

**Bottom line:** suture quantity does not predict anastomotic quality — **precision of placement matters more than number of stitches**.

### Microskills that predict a watertight VUA

**Dadashian 2026 *J Surg Educ* RCT** (n = 19 residents, 170 simulated VUAs) used the **EASE (End-to-End Assessment of Suturing Expertise)** tool to identify the **top 5 suturing microskills** that predict watertightness:<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

1. **Needle hold angle** — the angle at which the needle is loaded on the driver
2. **Depth of needle hold** — where on the needle body the driver grasps
3. **Needle repositions** — fewer re-grasps = better
4. **Wrist rotation during needle withdrawal** — smooth pronation following the needle's arc
5. **Needle driving smoothness** — absence of jerky or interrupted needle passage

**These metrics predicted watertightness with 78% sensitivity and 72% specificity.** Critically, **tailored feedback on individual deficient microskills achieved and sustained watertight VUAs in 100% (7/7) of trainees**, vs 17% with conventional feedback and 0% with video-only training (p &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

This is the most actionable training paradigm in the modern VUA literature — worth knowing both for one's own self-assessment and for structured feedback to trainees.

### Biomechanical considerations — tensile strength

**Perera 2017 porcine model** tested VUA tensile strength across suture techniques:<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

- **3-point interrupted fixation** significantly more likely to rupture (p = 0.025) vs 6-point interrupted or 6-point continuous
- **6-point interrupted ≈ 6-point continuous** — equivalent tensile strength
- **Catheter balloon shape matters:** round-balloon catheters were less likely to dislodge through the VUA than oval-balloon catheters (p = 0.002)

### Urethral-fixation technique

**Ficarra 2017** (n = 121) described fixing the **urethral stump laterally to the levator ani muscle and posteriorly to the medial dorsal raphe at 6 o'clock** using 3-0 PDS **before completing the VUA** with 8 interrupted sutures. Prevents urethral retraction and deviation:<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

- Continence at 1 week: **45.7% vs 19.6%** (p = 0.01)
- Continence at 4 weeks: **65.7% vs 31.4%** (p = 0.001)
- Continence at 8 weeks: **84.3% vs 41.2%** (p &lt; 0.001)

### Barbed (V-Loc) vs conventional — robotic standard

**Barbed suture has become the default for VUA during robotic prostatectomy.** Supported by two meta-analyses and a pivotal RCT:

- **Li 2015 meta** (9 studies, n = 786) — ↓ anastomosis time (WMD −3.98 min; p = 0.0001), ↓ total operative time (WMD −10.06 min; p = 0.0003), ↓ posterior-reconstruction time (WMD −0.93 min; p = 0.002); **no difference** in leakage, continence at any time point, or catheterization<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Bai 2015 meta** (10 studies, n = 747) — shorter operative time (MD −10.54 min; p &lt; 0.001)<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Zorn 2012 RCT** (n = 70) — reconstruction time 13.1 vs 20.8 min; p &lt; 0.001<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Massoud 2013** — no urethral-stenosis increase; cost-effective<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

:::warning[Barbed suture — the overtightening trap]
The self-anchoring property that eliminates knot-tying also eliminates the **self-correcting slack** of a conventional running line. **Williams 2010 RCT** (n = 81) found barbed suture associated with **more frequent cystogram extravasation (20% vs 2.8%; p = 0.019)** and longer catheterization (11.1 vs 8.3 d). After the authors modified technique to deliberately avoid overtightening, the leak rate dropped to 6.3%.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> **Run the line loose. If it looks snug, it is too tight.**
:::

### Knotless alternatives

**Zarrelli 2013 "Three-U-Stitches"** — three interrupted 2-0 Monocryl U-stitches tied tension-free; **98.9% continence at 6 mo, 0% BNC**. Useful alternative when barbed suture is unavailable or contraindicated.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

### Posterior (Rocco) reconstruction

Posterior musculofascial plate reconstruction is one of the most studied technical modifications in prostatectomy. The evidence shows a **modest benefit for early continence only** — no long-term effect and no complications.

| Source | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Rosenberg 2021 Cochrane** (8 RCTs, n = 963) | **1-week continence:** may improve (RR 1.25; 95% CI 0.90–1.73; low certainty). **3-month:** little to no effect. **Serious AEs** probably no difference. PSM likely similar.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)[[20]](#ref20)</sup> |
| **Grasso 2016 meta** (21 studies) | Continence improved at **3–7 d, 30 d, and 90 d**; significantly lower anastomotic leakage. **No benefit at 180 d or beyond.**<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> |
| **Cui 2017 meta** | **Posterior reconstruction alone** provided the most consistent benefit; anterior suspension alone showed no benefit.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> |
| **Student 2017 (ARVUS RCT)** | n = 66; levator-incorporating ARVUS technique: **86.7% vs 61.3% continence at 12 mo** (p = 0.04) — outlier positive trial<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> |

### VUA technique summary table

| Technique | Type | Suture | Anastomosis time | BNC rate | Advantage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Van Velthoven (running, double-armed)** | Continuous | 3-0 Monocryl × 2 tied | 16–21 min | **1.4%** | Standard for MIS; single knot; low BNC<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Interrupted (6-stitch, open)** | Interrupted | 3-0 PDS or Monocryl × 6 | 23–33 min | 9–10% | Better early MFR; avoids purse-string<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |

## 2. Ureteroileal Anastomosis — Bricker vs Wallace

Two dominant techniques for ureteral anastomosis to the ileal conduit or neobladder: **Bricker (separate, end-to-side)** and **Wallace (conjoined, end-to-end)**. Per-patient stricture rates are similar, but the **critical difference is bilateral stricture risk** because Wallace converts two independent renal units into a single anastomotic site.

### Bricker — step-by-step<sup>[[24]](#ref24)[[25]](#ref25)[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

1. **Ureteral preparation:** mobilize each ureter with periureteral adventitia preserved (blood supply). Debride distal ureter to healthy bleeding tissue; **spatulate 1–1.5 cm** on the antimesenteric side.
2. **Ileal preparation:** small full-thickness opening on the **antimesenteric border** of the proximal ileal conduit, sized to the spatulated ureter.
3. **Anastomosis:** **interrupted 4-0 or 5-0 absorbable sutures** (polyglactin or poliglecaprone), **mucosa-to-mucosa**, typically 6–8 sutures per anastomosis.
4. **Stenting:** single-J or feeding-tube stent through each anastomosis, brought out through the stoma.
5. Each ureter is anastomosed **independently** — the left ureter is typically tunneled beneath the sigmoid mesentery to reach the conduit.

**Advantages:** independent anastomoses — a stricture affects only one kidney. Technically simpler when ureteral lengths are disparate (e.g., obese patients).<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

### Wallace — step-by-step<sup>[[24]](#ref24)[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

1. **Ureteral preparation:** mobilize both ureters; **spatulate 2–3 cm** on their medial aspects (**Wallace I**) or lateral aspects (**Wallace II**).
2. **Conjoining:** suture the spatulated edges of both ureters together with a **continuous 4-0 or 5-0 absorbable** suture → single wide plate of ureteral tissue.
3. **Ileal anastomosis:** the conjoined ureteral plate is anastomosed **end-to-end** to the proximal opening of the ileal conduit with interrupted or continuous absorbable sutures.
4. **Stenting:** both ureters are stented.

**Advantages:** wider anastomotic lumen; faster (single anastomosis). **Critical disadvantage:** stricture at the conjoined site obstructs **both kidneys simultaneously** — **bilateral stricture in 69.2% of Wallace strictures** vs rare in Bricker.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

### Comparative evidence

| Study | Design | n | Bricker | Wallace | Insight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Al-Nader 2024** (multi-institutional, propensity-matched) | 418 (209 each) | 12% patients | 14.4% patients (p = 0.56) | **Bilateral stricture: 1 Bricker vs 15 Wallace** (p = 0.00); more affected renal units with Wallace (10.7% vs 6.2%)<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup> |
| **Krafft 2022** (retrospective) | 135 | 5.9% renal units | 16.6% renal units (p &lt; 0.001) | Bilateral stricture 69.2% in Wallace (p &lt; 0.001); prior chemo and Clavien ≥ III predict stricture<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup> |
| **Kouba 2007** (single-surgeon) | 186 | 3.7% ureters | **0%** (p = 0.015) | Wallace had zero strictures; Bricker group had higher BMI<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup> |
| **Evangelidis 2006** (retrospective) | 198 | 1.85% | 4.46% (p = 0.21) | No significant difference<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup> |
| **Carreno 2025** (robotic) | 96 | 13.8% chronic stricture | 0% (p = 0.055) | Trend favoring Wallace in robotic setting<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup> |
| **Ren 2025** (single-surgeon, BMI-matched) | 461 | 1.1% hydronephrosis | 0% (p = 0.148) | Wallace: **shorter OR time** (281 vs 302 min; p &lt; 0.001) and LOS<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup> |

### Individualized selection — Liu 2014 algorithm<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup>

1. **Exclude Wallace** if tumor involves the ureteral orifice or distal ureter (risk of positive margin at conjoined site), or if ureteral anomalies (duplication, ectopia) are present.
2. **Prefer Wallace** when ureters are of similar length and reach the conduit without tension.
3. **Prefer Bricker** when significant ureteral length discrepancy exists (common in obese patients after retrosigmoidal tunneling), or when one ureter is of questionable viability.

This strategy yielded an overall stricture rate of **3.1%** in the Liu series.<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup>

**Independent predictors of ureteroenteric stricture:** prior pelvic radiotherapy, T4 disease, node-positive disease, prior chemotherapy, and 90-day Clavien ≥ III complications.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

## 3. Pyeloplasty — Anderson-Hynes Dismembered

The **Anderson-Hynes dismembered pyeloplasty** is the gold-standard repair for UPJ obstruction.<sup>[[33]](#ref33)[[34]](#ref34)</sup>

### Core steps

1. **Excise the obstructed UPJ segment** completely
2. **Spatulate the proximal ureter laterally** for 1–2 cm → wide funnel-shaped opening
3. **Reduce the redundant pelvis** to create a dependent, non-dilated pelvis
4. **Apex stitch** — **the most critical suture** — placed between the lowest point of the spatulated ureter and the most dependent corner of the trimmed pelvis. **This stitch determines the geometry of the entire anastomosis.**
5. **Posterior wall:** continuous or interrupted absorbable sutures (outside-in on pelvis, inside-out on ureter)
6. **Double-J stent** inserted before completing the anterior wall
7. **Anterior wall** completed with same technique

### Suture-bite depth — deeper is better

**Gu &amp; Luo 2024 prospective comparative** (n = 90 children, 5-0 Vicryl continuous): **1 mm vs 0.5 mm** suture-bite depth:<sup>[[35]](#ref35)</sup>

- **1 mm depth: significantly less postoperative drainage volume, shorter time to stent removal, faster hydronephrosis resolution** (all p &lt; 0.05)
- **No increase in stenosis** with deeper bites

**Translation:** take a full 1 mm bite — tissue purchase matters more than shallow-bite theoretical preservation of lumen.

### Continuous vs interrupted suturing — continuous wins

**Kim 2022 meta** (5 studies): continuous suture **shorter operative time** (MD −33.1 min; p &lt; 0.001), **no difference in success rate or complications**.<sup>[[36]](#ref36)</sup>

### Ureter-first approach (Nayyar 2022)

A standardization of the dismembered repair:<sup>[[34]](#ref34)</sup>

1. **Start with the ureter** — incise just below the UPJ
2. **Spatulate laterally** through the obstruction into normal ureter
3. **Then trim the pelvis** — only after confirming the UPJ anatomy and ureteral length
4. This preserves the lower lip of the pelvis and minimizes tension

**Results:** zero redo repairs at mean 39-month follow-up; no cases deemed under tension.<sup>[[34]](#ref34)</sup>

## 4. Bladder Closure and Cystotomy Repair

### Healing biology

The bladder attains **100% of unwounded tissue strength in 14–21 days** — among the fastest-healing visceral tissues. **Absorbable suture strength retention need only match this 3-week window.**<sup>[[37]](#ref37)</sup>

### Single-layer vs double-layer — double wins on leak pressure

| Study | Model | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| **Duffy 2019** ex vivo ovine | — | **Double-layer ~ 2× higher leak pressure** than single-layer (p &lt; 0.0001), regardless of suture type<sup>[[38]](#ref38)</sup> |
| **Gözen 2012** pig, laparoscopic | — | Running > interrupted for leak pressure. **Running barbed 419.7 mL** &gt; running Vicryl 353.8 mL &gt; interrupted Vicryl 276.2 mL (p &lt; 0.05)<sup>[[39]](#ref39)</sup> |
| **Baum 1975** canine in vivo | — | Extramucosal running horizontal-mattress = ideal closure pattern<sup>[[40]](#ref40)</sup> |

**Where leaks occur:** in single-layer closure, at **suture holes**; in double-layer closure, at the **knot** (or pre-constructed end-loop in barbed suture).<sup>[[38]](#ref38)</sup>

### The barbed-suture-stone caution in the collecting system

:::warning[Barbed suture in the urinary collecting system]
**Yalcin 2018 in-vivo rabbit bladder model** — V-Loc 90 produced **less inflammation and fibrosis** than Vicryl or Monocryl but was associated with significantly **more stone formation (4/7 animals at 6 wk vs 1/7 for Vicryl; p = 0.039) and suture migration (3/7 at 6 wk).**<sup>[[41]](#ref41)</sup>

Barbed suture is well-validated for the VUA (brief urine contact) but **less validated for cystotomy / pyeloplasty / augment closures** where sustained urine contact occurs. Consider conventional braided absorbable in those settings.
:::

### Practical recommendations

- **Standard open cystotomy closure:** two-layer — inner continuous 3-0 polyglactin (full-thickness or mucosal), outer interrupted or continuous 3-0 polyglactin (seromuscular / extramucosal)<sup>[[37]](#ref37)[[42]](#ref42)</sup>
- **Laparoscopic cystotomy closure:** single-layer interrupted absorbable acceptable with 7–14 d catheter drainage — 1/19 vesicovaginal fistula reported in Nezhat 1996<sup>[[42]](#ref42)</sup>
- **Suture choice:** braided synthetic absorbable (polyglactin 910) is ideal — strength retention ~21 d matches bladder healing
- **Avoid nonabsorbable** in the urinary stream — predictable stone nidus<sup>[[37]](#ref37)</sup>

## 5. Ureteral Repair — Principles and Technique by Location

### Universal principles (ACS 2025 + WSES 2023)<sup>[[24]](#ref24)[[43]](#ref43)</sup>

- **Spatulate the ureter** — always, to widen the anastomotic circumference
- **Mucosa-to-mucosa approximation** — urothelial continuity → reduced fibrosis
- **Tension-free** — psoas hitch or Boari flap as needed
- **Stented** — double-J for 4–6 weeks
- **Absorbable sutures only** — 4-0 or 5-0 polyglactin or poliglecaprone, interrupted
- **Preserve periureteral adventitia** — ureteral blood supply runs longitudinally; skeletonization → ischemic stricture
- **Watertight but not strangulating**
- **Drain the repair** — closed suction drain near (not on) the anastomosis

### Location-based technique selection

| Injury location | Preferred repair | Technique | Success |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Distal ureter** | Ureteroneocystostomy ± psoas hitch | Spatulate; reimplant into bladder dome; refluxing or nonrefluxing; stent | **91–98%**<sup>[[24]](#ref24)[[44]](#ref44)</sup> |
| **Mid ureter** | Ureteroureterostomy | Debride to healthy tissue; spatulate both ends; interrupted 4-0/5-0 absorbable; stent | &gt; 90%<sup>[[43]](#ref43)</sup> |
| **Proximal ureter** | Ureteroureterostomy or **ureterocalicostomy** | End-to-end if feasible; nephropexy + calicostomy for long defects | &gt; 90%<sup>[[45]](#ref45)</sup> |
| **Extensive defect** | Psoas hitch + Boari flap | Mobilize bladder; hitch to psoas tendon; tubularize bladder flap for additional length | **97% (psoas hitch)**<sup>[[44]](#ref44)</sup> |
| **Bilateral or complex** | Transureteroureterostomy | Donor ureter tunneled beneath sigmoid; end-to-side; stent | High patency; last resort<sup>[[43]](#ref43)</sup> |

### Lich-Gregoir extravesical reimplantation — 10-step technique<sup>[[46]](#ref46)</sup>

1. Close the distal ureteral stump
2. Mobilize the ureter with periureteral tissue
3. Spatulate the ureter (1–1.5 cm)
4. Mobilize the bladder
5. **Incise the detrusor muscle** (3–4 cm) down to mucosa **without entering the bladder**
6. Suspend the bladder (psoas hitch if needed)
7. **Incise the bladder mucosa** (matching the spatulated ureter)
8. Perform the ureterovesical anastomosis — interrupted 4-0/5-0 absorbable, mucosa-to-mucosa
9. **Insert double-J stent**
10. **Close the detrusor muscle over the ureter** — creating the antireflux tunnel

### Top-down suturing (robotic extravesical) — Silay 2015<sup>[[47]](#ref47)</sup>

Starting the detrusor reapproximation at the **superior aspect** elevates the ureter out of harm's way, decreasing instrument contact and trauma. Useful technical modification in pediatric RALUR.

## 6. Urethroplasty — Graft Fixation Techniques

The AUA 2023 Urethral Stricture Disease Guidelines form the anchor:<sup>[[48]](#ref48)</sup>

- **Oral mucosa** (buccal or lingual) = first-choice graft material (Expert Opinion)
- **Buccal ≈ lingual mucosa** (Strong Recommendation; Grade A)
- **Urethroplasty &gt; endoscopic treatment** for strictures ≥ 2 cm bulbar

### Technique outcomes summary

| Technique | Indication | Success | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **EPA (transecting)** | Short bulbar (&lt; 2 cm) | **89.7%** (IQR 7.0) | Jasionowska 2022 SR<sup>[[49]](#ref49)</sup> |
| **Non-transecting (vessel-sparing) EPA** | Short bulbar | **90–98%** | Virasoro 2020 — potential erectile-function advantage<sup>[[50]](#ref50)</sup> |
| **Dorsal onlay BMG** | Bulbar / pan-urethral ≥ 2 cm | **86.6%** | Largest substitution evidence base<sup>[[49]](#ref49)</sup> |
| **Endoscopic (DVIU / dilation)** | Very short, simple only | ~20% if stricture &gt; 4 cm; ~40% overall | AUA: don't offer as first-line for &gt; 2 cm bulbar<sup>[[48]](#ref48)</sup> |

### Dorsal onlay — Barbagli technique with fibrin glue

**Most common graft placement — 66% of GURS surgeons prefer dorsal.**<sup>[[51]](#ref51)</sup>

1. **Perineal approach** — bulbar urethra mobilized from corpora cavernosa
2. **Dorsal urethrotomy** through the stricture into healthy tissue
3. **Graft bed** — exposed tunica albuginea of the corpora cavernosa
4. **Graft fixation — traditional:** interrupted **5-0 polyglactin** quilting the graft to the corpora, with additional sutures securing graft edges to urethrotomy margins
5. **Graft fixation — fibrin glue (Barbagli 2006):** **2 mL fibrin glue** secures the graft to corpora, with only **2 interrupted 5-0 polyglactin apex sutures**. Reduced operative time (mean 100 min), **100% success at 16-month follow-up**.<sup>[[52]](#ref52)</sup>
6. **Urethral closure** — urethra rotated back over the graft and closed with continuous absorbable suture

### Ventral onlay

1. Urethra opened ventrally through the stricture
2. Graft sutured to urethrotomy margins with **interrupted 5-0 polyglactin**
3. Graft supported by underlying **spongiosum** (vascular bed)
4. Spongiosum closed over the graft

### Transurethral ventral inlay (Sterling 2023) — fossa navicularis / distal strictures<sup>[[53]](#ref53)</sup>

1. Transurethral ventral urethrotomy to ≥ 24 Fr
2. Triangular BMG delivered to proximal extent using **double-armed 6-0 PDS**
3. Graft secured to meatus with **5-0 polyglactin**
4. Additional **6-0 PDS quilting sutures** for spread fixation
5. **95% patency at 36-month median follow-up** (n = 44)

### Suture selection for urethroplasty

- **Graft quilting / fixation:** **5-0 or 6-0 polyglactin (Vicryl) or polydioxanone (PDS)**, interrupted
- **Urethral closure over graft:** 4-0 or 5-0 polyglactin, continuous or interrupted
- **Anastomotic urethroplasty (EPA):** 4-0 or 5-0 polyglactin or poliglecaprone, **interrupted** — typically 8–12 sutures circumferentially with mucosa-to-mucosa

For graft-harvest technique details, see [Buccal mucosa](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa) and the [Grafts hub](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts-gu-reconstruction).

## 7. Suture Material in the Urinary Tract

The single most important materials fact: **sutures exposed to urine serve as a nidus for stone formation.** **Absorbable only** for any closure in contact with the urinary stream.<sup>[[37]](#ref37)</sup>

### The PDS-in-urine surprise

- **el-Mahrouky 1987** — **PDS lost all breaking strength after only 3 days in sterile urine.** Polyglycolic acid retained **36% at 10 days**; **chromic catgut maintained nearly full strength.** In *Proteus*-infected urine, **both PDS and PGA lost all strength within 1 day**, while chromic catgut lost only 5.8% at 10 days.<sup>[[54]](#ref54)</sup>
- **Cohen 1987** and **Stewart 1990** — animal models generally favored PDS over chromic for most bladder-closure scenarios.<sup>[[55]](#ref55)[[56]](#ref56)</sup>
- **Wainstein 1997** pyeloplasty model — polyglactin (Vicryl) produced the **least inflammation**.<sup>[[57]](#ref57)</sup>

### Practical implications

| Scenario | Implication |
|---|---|
| **Bladder closure, sterile urine** | Polyglactin (Vicryl) — 21-day retention matches bladder healing |
| **Standard VUA** | Poliglecaprone (Monocryl) or barbed V-Loc — smooth passage, minimal tissue drag |
| **PDS in the urinary stream** | **Caution** — rapid strength loss; prefer for fascial closure |
| **Known Proteus-infected urine** | **Chromic catgut** paradoxically outperforms synthetic absorbables despite more inflammation<sup>[[54]](#ref54)</sup> |
| **Any urine-contact closure** | **Absorbable only** — nonabsorbable → stones<sup>[[37]](#ref37)</sup> |

See [Sutures](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/sutures) for the full materials framework and procedure-by-procedure selection.

## 8. Cross-Cutting Technical Principles

| Principle | Rationale | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Always spatulate** | ↑ circumference → ↓ stricture risk | Universal across GU anastomoses<sup>[[24]](#ref24)[[44]](#ref44)</sup> |
| **Mucosa-to-mucosa** | Urothelial continuity → ↓ fibrosis | All ureteral + urethral anastomoses<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> |
| **Tension-free** | Tension → ischemia → stricture | Psoas hitch 97% success<sup>[[44]](#ref44)</sup> |
| **Absorbable only in urine** | Nonabsorbable → stones | Edlich 1987<sup>[[37]](#ref37)</sup> |
| **Stent all ureteral anastomoses** | Maintains patency during healing | Universal; 4–6 weeks standard<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> |
| **1 mm bite depth &gt; 0.5 mm in pyeloplasty** | Better purchase; less extravasation; no stenosis increase | Gu-Luo 2024<sup>[[35]](#ref35)</sup> |
| **Microskills predict watertight VUA** | Tailored feedback → 100% watertight anastomoses | Dadashian EASE RCT<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Barbed saves time but risks over-tightening and stones** | VUA ↓ 4–10 min; collecting-system stones in animal models | Multiple RCTs<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[17]](#ref17)[[41]](#ref41)</sup> |
| **Suture quantity &lt; placement precision** | 2- vs 4- vs 6-suture VUA equivalent | Gallo 2007 RCT<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **Double-layer &gt; single-layer bladder closure** | ~ 2× leak pressure regardless of suture | Duffy 2019<sup>[[38]](#ref38)</sup> |

## Practical Pearls

- **The 5 microskills that predict a watertight VUA** — needle hold angle, depth of needle hold, needle repositions, wrist rotation during withdrawal, driving smoothness — are a trainable framework. Targeted feedback on individual deficient skills achieved **100% watertight VUAs** in trainees vs 0% with video-only training.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Suture quantity does not determine anastomotic quality.** The 2-suture, 4-suture, and 6-suture VUA techniques produce identical outcomes — only anastomosis time differs.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **For pyeloplasty, use 1 mm bite depth** — simple technical change that significantly reduces postoperative extravasation without increasing stenosis.<sup>[[35]](#ref35)</sup>
- **Wallace ureteroileal carries a hidden risk:** per-patient stricture rates match Bricker, but **69% of Wallace strictures are bilateral** because the conjoined plate shares a single anastomotic site. Use Liu 2014 selection algorithm to decide.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)[[32]](#ref32)</sup>
- **Barbed suture in the urinary collecting system** — well-validated for VUA, **less validated for cystotomy / augment / pyeloplasty closure** where sustained urine contact raises stone-formation risk in animal models.<sup>[[41]](#ref41)</sup>
- **Double-layer bladder closure provides ~ 2× leak pressure** of single-layer regardless of suture type — relevant for augmentation cystoplasty and complex bladder reconstruction.<sup>[[38]](#ref38)</sup>
- **Fibrin glue + 2 apex sutures** (Barbagli dorsal onlay) is a legitimate alternative to traditional quilting and reduces operative time with 100% short-term success.<sup>[[52]](#ref52)</sup>
- **Run the Van Velthoven loose.** Barbed suture's self-anchoring property eliminates the self-correcting slack of conventional running — overtightening stays in the ring until tissue remodels.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- **Rocco posterior reconstruction** accelerates early continence recovery (first 1–3 mo) without affecting long-term outcomes — minimal added time, no complications.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- **Ureter-first pyeloplasty** (Nayyar) preserves the lower lip of the pelvis and minimizes tension — zero redo repairs in the reported series.<sup>[[34]](#ref34)</sup>
- **PDS loses all strength in 3 days in sterile urine** — surprising pharmacology worth remembering. In known *Proteus* infection, **chromic catgut** is paradoxically the most durable choice.<sup>[[54]](#ref54)</sup>
