# Stoma Site Marking

Preoperative marking and education are recommended whenever a stoma may be needed. The joint WOCN Society/AUA/ASCRS position statement includes urinary diversions and emphasizes individualized assessment by a trained clinician. An appropriate skin site must also be compatible with bowel perfusion, mesenteric reach and the planned reconstruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

See also: [Ileal Conduit](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion/ileal-conduit), [Cutaneous Ureterostomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion/cutaneous-ureterostomy), [Principles of Urinary Diversion](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion/urinary-diversion-principles), [Continent Catheterizable Channels](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/catheterizable-channels), and [Incisions & Closure](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/incisions-closure).

## What the Evidence Supports

Marking is associated with fewer complications and better self-care. A 2021 systematic review included 20 studies, with 19 contributing to quantitative synthesis: pooled odds ratios were 0.47 (95% CI 0.36–0.62) for complications and 0.34 (0.18–0.64) for self-care deficits. The complication analysis had substantial heterogeneity (I² 70.6%). These mixed-design ostomy data do not establish the same effect size for every urinary diversion.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The ASCRS 2022 guideline gives preoperative marking a strong recommendation based on moderate-quality evidence (1B), but explicitly excludes urostomies from its scope. The joint statement is the relevant multidisciplinary guidance that includes urinary stomas.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

A historical 137-patient ileal-conduit cohort found stoma complications in 20 patients (14.6%), predominantly parastomal hernia. Higher BMI was associated with complications. This is a cohort-specific estimate, not a current universal complication rate or proof that marking prevents every event.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Who and When

Arrange assessment by a certified ostomy nurse and the operating team during preoperative planning. Other appropriately trained clinicians can mark a site when specialist nursing is unavailable. There is no requirement to wait until the day before surgery. Preserve the mark with indelible ink or a waterproof cover, and document backup sites if intraoperative choices are uncertain.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Education and marking should occur together when feasible. The ASCRS guideline cites a small 42-patient randomized education trial with faster proficiency and shorter hospitalization; those results were not a pooled urinary-diversion trial.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Assessment Checklist

- Examine standing, sitting and supine, adapted to the patient's mobility. Identify folds that appear only on sitting or bending.
- Assess visibility and reach, hand function, vision, clothing and beltline, occupation, braces and habitual activities.
- Identify the rectus muscle and a flat pouching surface; avoid problematic creases, scars, bony prominences and proximity to the umbilicus. Test the intended appliance against the actual abdominal contour.
- Confirm the planned incontinent pouching system versus continent catheterizable channel. Their access and construction requirements differ.
- Mark the preferred site and, when needed, clearly ranked alternatives. Discuss these with the operating surgeon.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

A right-sided site is common for an ileal conduit, but a fixed ASIS-to-umbilicus fraction should not replace this assessment. Rectus placement is a recommended siting principle; it is not a guarantee against parastomal hernia.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Adaptation to the Patient

| Situation | Practical adjustment |
|---|---|
| **Protuberant abdomen or substantial pannus** | An upper abdominal site may improve visibility and shorten the passage through a thick abdominal wall; verify conduit reach. |
| **Wheelchair use, contractures or limited reach** | Assess in the patient's usual seated position and equipment; demonstrate access with the patient or their chosen caregiver. |
| **Prior surgery or existing stomas** | Consider scars, hernias, pouch footprints and the proposed incision together; a contralateral or higher site may be preferable. |
| **Two separate stomas** | Plan enough room for both appliances and consider different horizontal levels if belts will be used. |

These are individualized planning considerations from the joint statement, rather than fixed geometric rules.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Connect Marking With Construction and Follow-up

For an incontinent ileal conduit, adequate protrusion, perfusion and a tension-free passage help appliance fitting. A flush or retracted conduit warrants assessment for leakage and skin injury; it does not automatically mean the diversion has failed. A continent catheterizable stoma may intentionally sit at skin level, with reliable catheter access as a central objective.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

Taneja and Godoy described preparing the ileal-conduit stoma before abdominal-wall transposition in 45 consecutive cases. The uncontrolled series supports a technical option; it does not establish superiority over conventional maturation.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

Before discharge, confirm appliance or catheter skills, access to supplies and a contact for problems. Continue ostomy nursing follow-up for leakage, skin changes, changing body contour and difficulties with independent care.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
