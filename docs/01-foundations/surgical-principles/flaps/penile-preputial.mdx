# Pedicled Penile / Preputial Skin Flap

Local pedicled skin flaps harvested from the penile shaft or prepuce, based on the **dartos fascia vascular plexus** supplied by branches of the **superficial external pudendal artery**. The family encompasses several named techniques — McAninch-Morey circular fasciocutaneous, Orandi dorsal onlay, Quartey island preputial, Duckett transverse preputial island — all sharing the dartos-based vascular supply but differing in flap geometry and transfer direction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

See the overview article: [Flaps in GU Reconstruction](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps-gu-reconstruction).

---

## Anatomy &amp; Vascular Pedicle

- **Source vessel:** branches of the **superficial external pudendal artery** (femoral a. branch) via the dartos fascia plexus
- **Dartos fascia** is the loose areolar layer deep to penile skin carrying the vascular network — it is the flap's true pedicle
- **Skin types:** non-glabrous preputial skin is the preferred donor because it closely matches urethral epithelium and tolerates the wet urinary environment; hair-bearing shaft skin is **not appropriate for intraluminal use** (hair growth, stone formation)

---

## Named Techniques

### McAninch-Morey circular penile fasciocutaneous flap

The workhorse flap for **long-segment anterior urethral strictures** with limited alternative donor tissue. A circular distal penile skin flap is longitudinally split on the dorsal midline and transferred ventrally for urethroplasty.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

**Technique:**

1. Circumferential distal penile skin incision (circumcising incision)
2. Dartos fascia mobilized to preserve the vascular pedicle
3. **Flap split longitudinally on the dorsal side**
4. Transferred ventrally to cover the urethral defect
5. Deployed as **onlay** or **tubularized** configuration per stricture anatomy

**Onlay vs. tubularized — outcomes:**<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

| Configuration | Recurrence rate |
|---|---|
| **Onlay** | **13%** |
| **Tubularized** | **58%** |

**Onlay is strongly preferred** whenever the urethral plate can be preserved — tubularized reconstruction carries nearly 5× higher recurrence.

**Contemporary outcomes (Zhao 2026 modified distal penile circular flap series):**<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- Success rate **79–100%** for complex anterior urethral strictures
- Average stricture length **9.08 cm** (one of the longest anterior stricture series reported)
- Qmax: pre-op 6.3 mL/s → post-op 18.6 mL/s
- **Erectile function preserved** — IIEF-5 maintained at 23.5–23.9
- Post-micturition dribbling: 24%
- Mild penile torsion: rare (&lt;5%)

### Orandi dorsal onlay urethroplasty

A longitudinal strip of penile (usually preputial or shaft) skin raised on dartos pedicle and placed as a **dorsal onlay** onto the opened urethral plate. Preferred for penile urethral strictures where the Quartey island flap is not suitable.

### Quartey island preputial flap

A transverse island of preputial skin pedicled on dartos fascia and rotated ventrally. Commonly used for pendulous urethral strictures with reliable preputial availability.

### Duckett transverse preputial island flap (hypospadias)

The classical hypospadias repair technique: a transverse inner-preputial skin island rotated ventrally and tubularized to form the neourethra. The prototype of preputial island flap urethroplasty.

## Harvest Technique

### Design

- **Circumscribing incision** 5–10 mm proximal to the corona
- **Preserve the dartos fascia** on the flap — the dartos is the pedicle, not the skin
- **Identify and spare** the dorsal neurovascular bundle when dissecting proximally

### Dissection

1. Infiltrate the subcutaneous plane with saline or dilute epinephrine (not in the penis itself) to facilitate dissection
2. Raise the skin off the dartos distal to the planned flap boundary
3. **Preserve the dartos pedicle** running longitudinally along the shaft
4. Split the flap longitudinally (for McAninch-Morey) or raise as a longitudinal or transverse island (for Orandi, Quartey, Duckett)
5. Transfer to the recipient site — ventrally for most urethroplasty applications
6. Inset with fine absorbable suture (5-0 or 6-0 PDS / Monocryl)

---

## GU Applications

- **Anterior urethral stricture reconstruction:** Orandi dorsal onlay, Quartey island flap, McAninch-Morey circular flap
- **Long-segment / panurethral strictures:** McAninch-Morey circular — reported for strictures up to 15+ cm
- **Hypospadias repair:** Duckett transverse preputial island flap
- **Penile shaft resurfacing:** after oncologic resection or Fournier's
- **Failed urethroplasty salvage** where alternative grafts cannot take due to inadequate bed

---

## Outcomes

Summary across contemporary series:<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- **Success: 79–100%** for complex anterior urethral strictures depending on configuration
- **Onlay (13%) vs tubularized (58%) recurrence** — the defining technique choice
- **Erectile function preservation** — IIEF-5 maintained in &gt;95% of patients
- **Post-micturition dribbling** in ~24% of long-segment reconstructions
- **Mild penile torsion** in &lt;5% of cases
- **Durability** — recurrences typically occur within the first 12 months post-op

---

## Limitations &amp; Contraindications

- **Requires intact, non-scarred penile/preputial skin**
- **Limited donor availability after circumcision** — preputial options unavailable
- **Not available in reoperative or radiation-compromised fields**
- **Hair-bearing shaft skin** is unsuitable for intraluminal use
- **Penile torsion risk** in long flaps requiring extensive skin re-draping
- **Contraindicated in lichen sclerosus (BXO)** — the donor skin is itself diseased; buccal mucosa graft is preferred

---

## Comparison with Alternatives

| Donor | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|---|
| **Penile/preputial flap (this page)** | Local; reliable axial vascularity; matches urethral environment | Limited availability; unsuitable in BXO / circumcised / irradiated |
| **[Buccal mucosa graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa)** | Universal availability; excellent properties; no local donor constraint | Requires oral donor site; no intrinsic vascular supply (graft) |
| **[SCIP flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/scip)** | Thin, pliable free tissue; concealed groin donor | Free microvascular technique; learning curve |
| **[Scrotal / biaxial scrotal flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps-gu-reconstruction)** | Long flap potential (up to 20 cm); local | Hair-bearing; epilation required |
