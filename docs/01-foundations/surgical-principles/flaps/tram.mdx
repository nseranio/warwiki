# Transverse Rectus Abdominis Musculocutaneous (TRAM) Flap

The transverse rectus abdominis musculocutaneous (TRAM) flap transfers rectus abdominis muscle with a transverse abdominal skin island. Hartrampf and colleagues described a rectus-abdominis musculocutaneous island flap for postmastectomy breast reconstruction in 1982, including clinical and cadaveric anatomic work, an arc of rotation, and design alternatives.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Reported pelvic and vulvar use

A 1994 preliminary report described an inferiorly based TRAM flap in five people undergoing radical vulvectomy for invasive vulvar squamous-cell carcinoma.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> A 2008 vulvovaginal reconstruction review describes use of several random and vascular-territory flap types, including musculocutaneous flaps, but emphasizes individualized reconstruction rather than a small set of standardized procedures and notes limitations in irradiated tissue.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

In a retrospective pelvic-exenteration series, 21 TRAM and seven DIEP vaginal reconstructions were compared. The abstract reports three total and five partial necroses in the TRAM group, no flap losses in the DIEP group, and four TRAM donor-site complications; this nonrandomized, small comparison cannot establish a preferred reconstruction for an individual patient.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Evidence boundary

The sources directly reviewed for this page were PubMed records and abstracts. They do not establish vascular or perforator measurements, perfusion zones, muscle-sparing classification, flap dimensions, operative elevation or inset, donor-site closure, delay/supercharging techniques, complication rates across indications, or comparative superiority of TRAM, DIEP, VRAM, or other flaps.
