# Rhomboid (Limberg) Flap

The Limberg, or rhombic, flap is a local transposition flap used to close a rhombus-shaped skin defect. Descriptions commonly characterize it as a full-thickness random-pattern flap supplied through the dermal-subdermal plexus.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limited GU context

Published GU use includes a 1986 ten-person report of bilateral rhomboid flaps for penile elongation and external-genital reconstruction in epispadias-exstrophy. The report described satisfactory cosmetic and functional results, but is a small historical series rather than comparative evidence or a general indication.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Rhomboid flaps also appear in reconstructive discussions of vulvar, scrotal, and perineal defects. The accessible evidence reviewed for this page does not support a universal defect-size threshold, a location-specific algorithm, or a claim of superiority over other local or perforator-based flaps.

## Evidence boundary

Pilonidal-disease research evaluates off-midline closure strategies in a different anatomical and clinical setting. A 2024 Cochrane review included 33 trials (3,667 analysed participants) and found that off-midline closure probably improved several outcomes versus conventional midline closure, but found no clear advantage among the evaluated off-midline flap techniques; evidence and applicability varied by comparison.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> This does not establish outcomes, technique, or flap choice for GU reconstruction.

## What this page does not establish

This page does not provide geometric marking rules, flap dimensions, elevation or closure steps, vascular safety thresholds, irradiated-field management, complication protocols, or a genital/perineal reconstruction algorithm. Those decisions require defect-specific assessment and appropriately qualified reconstructive expertise.
