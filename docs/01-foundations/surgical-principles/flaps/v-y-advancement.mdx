# V-Y Advancement Flap

A V-Y advancement flap is a local flap design for closing an adjacent skin or soft-tissue defect. In a mechanical analysis, a triangular flap was incised beside a rectangular defect and advanced to close it, creating two smaller donor-site defects; the analysis identified flap apex angle and skin elasticity as factors affecting closure mechanics.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Reported reconstructive uses

Perforator-based V-Y variants have been reported to increase mobility beyond that of traditional V-Y flaps. One 2007 case series used 26 such flaps in 24 people for defects in several anatomic regions; two flaps had total or marginal necrosis. That uncontrolled series cannot define complication rates or superiority for other settings.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Published genitourinary-adjacent reports include:

- A 2025 single-center retrospective series of 57 patients with postoperative meatal stenosis after hypospadias surgery treated with a Y-V meatoplasty; the authors reported two early repeat operations and incomplete longer follow-up for six patients.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- A video case report of V-Y repair of a recurrent perineal defect after episiotomy dehiscence.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- A five-patient report of an interrupted V-Y design for vulvovaginal defects after treatment of extramammary Paget disease or vulvar carcinoma.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

These reports show described uses, not interchangeable indications or a procedure-selection hierarchy.

## Evidence boundary

The sources directly reviewed for this page were PubMed records and abstracts. They do not establish universal advancement distances, flap-angle rules, operative steps, Doppler or perforator requirements, outcomes across buried-penis, bladder-neck, Fournier's, vulvovaginal, pelvic, irradiated, or lichen-sclerosus settings, or comparative superiority over other local or regional flaps.
