# Martius Flap

The **Martius flap** (also known as the **Martius labial fat pad flap** or **bulbocavernosus flap**) is the **most commonly used interposition flap in female pelvic reconstructive surgery**, first described by **Heinrich Martius in 1928**. It consists of a well-vascularized fibroadipose tissue pedicle harvested from the labium majus and is used to reinforce repairs of vesicovaginal fistulas, rectovaginal fistulas, urethral diverticula, urethral strictures, and mesh erosions, with an overall complication rate of **&lt; 5%**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

This page is the foundations-level deep dive. Site-specific technique pages — VVF / RVF / UVF repair, urethral diverticulum, urethroplasty — link back here.

![Martius flap: a fibrofatty flap harvested from the labium majus on its dual pedicle, tunneled to the midline and interposed over a repair](/img/diagrams/martius-flap.svg)
*The Martius flap in two steps. A vertical incision over the (default right) **labium majus** exposes the **fibrofatty pad**, which carries a **dual blood supply** — an **anterior** pedicle (external pudendal) and a **posterior** pedicle (internal pudendal). The flap is raised on one pedicle, **tunneled subcutaneously** to the midline, and laid over the repair as a vascularized, non-overlapping suture-line buttress. Pedicle rule: keep the **posterior** pedicle for **anterior/urethral** targets (VVF, diverticulum, female urethroplasty); keep the **anterior** pedicle for **posterior** targets (RVF). (Original WARWIKI schematic)*

## Historical Context and Nomenclature

Martius originally described the flap in 1928 as a **bulbocavernosus muscle flap** for urethral reconstruction. Anatomical studies have since shown that the tissue harvested in the modern "Martius flap" is composed primarily of **fibroadipose tissue from the labium majus** — not the bulbocavernosus muscle itself. The prominent fibrous component arises from a superficial tunic of fibrous tissue analogous to the male tunica dartos, along with considerable fibrous septa within the adipose tissue.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The terms "Martius flap," "Martius fat pad," "bulbocavernosus flap," and "labial fat pad graft" are used interchangeably in the literature.

---

## Vascular Anatomy and Blood Supply

The Martius flap has a **dual blood supply**, allowing it to be pedicled either anteriorly or posteriorly:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

| Pedicle | Supply | Reach |
|---|---|---|
| **Anterior pedicle** | External pudendal artery (branch of the femoral) | Anterior / urethral targets |
| **Posterior pedicle** | Internal pudendal artery (branch of the internal iliac) | Posterior / perineal targets (RVF) |
| **Lateral contribution** | Obturator artery (variable)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | — |

These vessels form a plexus within the graft, providing robust vascularity that promotes rapid neovascularization at the recipient site.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

**Rothenberger 2025** — cadaveric neurovascular mapping in 10 cadavers:<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- **Highest vascular density** at the **lateral aspect of the right labial fat pad**
- **Highest nerve density** anterior and medial bilaterally
- The **right labium** had significantly greater vascular density (8% increase, p &lt; 0.05) — supporting right-sided harvest as the default when laterality is otherwise neutral

## Harvest Technique

Standard harvest sequence:<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

1. **Incision** — vertical incision over the labium majus from the level of the clitoral hood to the labiocrural fold (typically 4–6 cm).
2. **Flap mobilization** — the fibrofatty tissue is mobilized lateral-to-medial. The skin is preserved over the labium.
3. **Pedicle selection** — divide the flap at one pedicle and preserve the other:
  - **Posterior pedicle preserved** → for **anterior / urethral** targets (VVF, urethral diverticulum, female urethroplasty)
  - **Anterior pedicle preserved** → for **posterior** targets (RVF, perineal defects)
4. **Subcutaneous tunneling** from the labium to the recipient site, wide enough to accommodate the flap without kinking.
5. **Interposition** — flap passed through the tunnel and sutured to the underlying tissue (periurethral fascia, rectovaginal fascia, bladder wall) without tension.
6. **Closure** — vaginal wall over the flap; labial incision closed in layers. No drain typically required.

## Application 1 — Vesicovaginal Fistula Repair

The Martius flap is the most commonly used interposition flap for transvaginal VVF repair.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> It adds a well-vascularized tissue layer between the bladder and vaginal suture lines, reducing recurrence and dyspareunia.

- **Kapriniotis 2024** comprehensive review — most simple non-irradiated obstetric and iatrogenic fistulae can be closed without tissue interposition (&gt; 90% success with graftless repair). Martius flap remains valuable in **complex fistulae** — difficulty achieving tension-free closure, significant tissue loss, urethral involvement, or poorly vascularized tissues after radiotherapy — with success rates of **80–97%**.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Eilber 2003** 10-year series (207 VVFs) — Martius flap used for distal fistulas with **97% cure** (vs. 96% with peritoneal flap for proximal fistulas).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Pushkar 2009** (216 patients with radiation-induced VVF) — Martius flap used in 41%; cumulative success **80.4%** after multiple procedures.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Safan 2009 RCT** (40 patients) — fibrin glue vs. Martius flap interposition for complicated obstetric VVF: **no significant difference** (68% vs. 58%).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Rangnekar 2000** — recurrent / multiple fistulas: Martius **0% recurrence** vs. anatomic repair 16.7% for primary fistulas, and **8.3% vs. 57.1%** for recurrent fistulas.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

## Application 2 — Urethral Diverticulum Excision

The most commonly used adjunct to urethral diverticulectomy:<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

- **Malde 2017** (largest dedicated series, 70 patients) — diverticulectomy with Martius interposition: **100% complete excision**, **1.4% recurrence**, **12% persistent de novo SUI** at mean 19-month follow-up.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Osman 2021** (121 patients) — Martius flap used in **30%** of cases (36/121), typically for larger / more complex diverticula; overall recurrence 4%, de novo SUI 13%.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Ko 2017** — recurrent diverticula: **7 of 8** successfully treated with Martius interposition.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Dykes 2020** — a previously placed Martius flap can be **mobilized and repositioned** for recurrent diverticulum repair without harvesting a new flap.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

---

## Application 3 — Rectovaginal Fistula Repair

The **2022 ASCRS Clinical Practice Guidelines** provide a strong recommendation (Grade 1C) that gracilis or bulbocavernosus (Martius) flap is recommended for **recurrent or otherwise complex RVF**.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

| Study | Setting | Success | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Swindon 2024 SR/meta** (12 studies, 137 Martius flaps)<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> | Pooled | **91.4% primary RVF; 77.5% recurrent RVF; 94.6% radiation-induced RVF** | Overall complication 29%, recurrence 12% |
| **Pastier 2024** (62 patients)<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> | Martius (55) vs gracilis (24) | **Equivalent 69% vs 69%** at 23 mo | Martius shorter LOS (–2 days, p = 0.01); 27% Martius without diverting stoma without increased morbidity. Authors propose gracilis as **salvage after Martius failure** |
| **Trompetto 2019** (24 complex low RVF)<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> | Martius advancement | **91.3%** at 42 mo | Significant improvement in SF-12 and FSFI |

**Smoking** was the only negative predictive factor for Martius flap success in RVF repair (p = 0.02).<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

## Application 4 — Mid-Urethral Sling Erosion Repair

Martius interposition covers exposed or eroded mid-urethral tape while preserving sling function:<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[22]](#ref22)[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

- The **2025 ACOG guidelines on mesh / graft complications** list Martius graft transposition as an option for preserving a functioning but exposed midurethral sling when conservative management and primary reclosure have failed.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- **Mortimer 2016** — 5 TOT extrusions treated with Martius graft: **100% successful coverage** with no ipsilateral recurrence of tape extrusion.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
- **Malde** (largest series of 159 patients) — 24 patients underwent Martius interposition for MUT erosion repair, 2 had it used as a prophylactic MUT wrap for fragile urethras.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Application 5 — Female Urethroplasty for Urethral Stricture

The Martius flap provides a vascularized tissue layer over urethral repairs, particularly when combined with **buccal mucosal grafts** or **vaginal flap urethroplasty** (e.g., the [Blandy ventral vaginal flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/blandy)). 12 patients in the Malde series underwent Martius interposition as part of female urethroplasty.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Application 6 — Bladder Neck Closure for End-Stage SUI

In rare cases of complex end-stage stress urinary incontinence requiring vaginal closure of the bladder neck, the Martius flap reinforces the closure. Four such cases were reported in the Malde series.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Application 7 — Urethrovaginal Fistula Repair

The Martius flap is particularly effective for urethrovaginal fistulas that may occur after sling procedures, urethral diverticulum excision, anterior colporrhaphy, or radiation. In a series of fistulas secondary to mid-urethral sling surgery, Martius was used in **6 of 9** repairs with a 78% overall success rate.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

**Donor-site complications are remarkably low (Malde 159-patient series):**<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- Labial hematoma **1.25%** (2/159)
- Labial wound infection **0.6%** (1/159)

- **79% rated labial appearance as good or excellent**
- No significant long-term complications

Other reported complications include seroma, numbness, pain, and labial distortion, though these are uncommon.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## When Is Martius Flap Necessary vs Unnecessary?

Selective application is supported by the contemporary evidence:<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

- **Probably unnecessary** — most simple, non-irradiated obstetric fistulae within healthy tissues (graftless repair achieves &gt; 90% closure)
- **Recommended** — complex fistulae with any of:
  - Recurrent or failed prior repair
  - Significant tissue loss or scarring
  - Urethral involvement
  - Radiation-induced fistula
  - Difficulty achieving tension-free, layered closure
  - Multiple fistulae

## Key Takeaways

1. The Martius labial fat pad flap remains the **workhorse interposition flap in female pelvic reconstructive surgery** nearly a century after its original description.
2. Principal advantages: robust dual blood supply, low donor-site morbidity (&lt; 5%), short LOS, and broad applicability to VVF, RVF, urethral diverticulum, MUT erosion, and female urethroplasty.
3. Recurrent or radiation-induced RVF — Martius **77.5% / 94.6%** success; the equivalence to gracilis (Pastier 69% vs 69%) supports Martius-first with gracilis as salvage.
4. Most simple non-irradiated obstetric VVF do **not** require Martius interposition (&gt; 90% graftless success); reserve for complex / recurrent / irradiated / urethrally-involved cases.
5. Right-sided harvest (Rothenberger 2025) shows greater vascular density and is reasonable as the default unless lateralized contraindication exists.
6. Smoking is the only consistent negative predictor of Martius flap success in RVF repair.
