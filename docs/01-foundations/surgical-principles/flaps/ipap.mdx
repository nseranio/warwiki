# Internal Pudendal Artery Perforator (IPAP) Flap

The **IPAP flap** is a free-style pedicled perforator flap based on skin perforators from the internal pudendal artery, formally described by Hashimoto et al. (2014). It is the modern perforator-based evolution of the pudendal-thigh (Singapore), lotus petal, and gluteal-fold flaps and is now widely used in perineal soft-tissue reconstruction. The original series reported no total flap failures in 71 flaps (45 patients) across vulvar, vaginal, buttock, anal, and pelvic-cavity reconstruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For the genital-context treatment menu see the [Vulvar Reconstruction Atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar) and [Scrotal Reconstruction Techniques](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/scrotal-reconstruction). For related parent / sibling flaps see [Lotus Petal](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/lotus-petal), [Singapore / pudendal-thigh](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/singapore-pudendal-thigh), [IGAP / Gluteal-Fold](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/igap-gluteal-fold), [EPAP](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/epap), and [MCFAP](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/mcfap). For the broader perforator-flap framework see [SCIP / perforator flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/scip).

## Historical Development

The IPAP flap sits at the apex of an evolutionary lineage of perineal flaps sharing the same vascular territory:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

| Year | Contribution |
|---|---|
| 1996 — Yii & Niranjan | Gluteal-fold flap (lotus petal flap) described for vulvar reconstruction<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| 2001 — Hashimoto et al. | Cadaveric study (8 cadavers) of the gluteal-fold flap: blood supply is a **direct cutaneous system of the IPA** and vein, enabling flap thinning; 14 thinned flaps in 10 patients all survived<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| 2001 — Hashimoto | 3–5 perforators in the perineal anogenital triangle<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| 2009 — Jin et al. | Microdissection (22 sides / 11 cadavers) identifying 4 relatively constant perineal perforators<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| 2014 — Hashimoto et al. | **Formal IPAP description** — free-style pedicle perforator flap with propeller / transposition / V-Y advancement movements<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| 2021 — Giroux et al. | Routine preoperative ultrasound not always required<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

The IPAP is distinguished by its free-style, perforator-based approach, designed around Doppler-identified perforators rather than a fixed anatomic template.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Vascular Anatomy

### Perforator anatomy (Hashimoto 2001; Giroux 2021)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- **3–5 perforators** consistently present in the perineal anogenital triangle.
- **Ischial tuberosity** is the landmark and safe boundary of medial dissection.
- Mean distance from ischial tuberosity to perforator: 27.3 mm (Giroux Doppler study of 15 subjects, 24 perforators &gt; 5 mm).
- Perforator positions measured by orthonormal landmarks (distance from midline and along the inter-ischial-tuberosity line).

### Source artery — internal pudendal artery

The IPA is a terminal branch of the anterior division of the internal iliac artery. Relevant branches:<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- **Posterior labial (scrotal) aa.**. These are terminal branches of the superficial perineal a. (continuation of the IPA) and run deep to Colles' fascia close to the midline.
- **Direct cutaneous perforators.** These emerge through the ischiorectal fossa fat to supply the overlying gluteal-fold / perineal skin.
- **Lateral branch of the posterior labial a.** This branch contributes a perforating branch to the lower vascular anastomosis.

### Microdissection — Jin 2009<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

Four relatively constant perineal perforators were identified: inguinal and perineal branches of the superficial external pudendal a., the obturator anterior cutaneous branch, and the lateral branch of the posterior labial (pudendal) a. All were direct perforators forming upper, middle, and lower deep-fascial anastomoses above the adductor wall.

### Three-territory concept — Tham 2010<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

| Territory | Source | Plane |
|---|---|---|
| **1 (medial / base)** | Posterior labial aa. | Deep to Colles' fascia |
| **2 (middle)** | Obturator a. cutaneous branches | Superficial to Colles' fascia |
| **3 (lateral / apex)** | External pudendal a. branches | Superficial to Colles' fascia |

The pedicle is close to the midline. Lateral and apical portions of large flaps have a precarious blood supply.

### Sensory innervation

- **Pudendal nerve branches.** Primary sensory supply.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Posterior femoral cutaneous nerve.** Gluteal-fold territory.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- The flap retains cutaneous innervation and provides sensate reconstruction.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Surgical Technique (Hashimoto 2014)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

1. **Position.** Lithotomy (vulvar / vaginal / anal / pelvic cavity) or prone (buttock).
2. **Perforator identification.** Use a handheld Doppler on and around the ischiorectal fossa; arterial sounds mark the flap base.
3. **Flap design.** Free-style; skin island centered on the identified perforator(s):
   - **Propeller flap.** Most common (35/45 cases); rotated 90–180° around the perforator pivot point.
   - **V-Y advancement.** 7/45 cases; advanced on the perforator pedicle.
   - **Traditional transposition.** 3/45 cases.
4. **Incision and elevation.** Circumferential incision; suprafascial elevation preserving the perforator as the sole supply.
5. **Flap thinning.** Performed in all cases except pelvic-cavity reconstruction; adipose tissue is removed except the tissue around the pedicle vessels, an advantage over traditional designs.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
6. **Transfer.** Rotate (propeller), advance (V-Y), or transpose depending on the design.
7. **Donor closure.** Primary; scar concealed in the gluteal fold or perineal crease.

Technical points:

- Ischial tuberosity is the safe boundary of medial dissection.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- In a 15-subject color Doppler study, Giroux and colleagues concluded that routine ultrasound identification and dissection of the perforators is not always required before harvest.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Provides suitable volume for both thin reconstructions (vulvar, vaginal, anal) and bulky reconstructions (pelvic-cavity dead-space obliteration).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Variants and Modifications

| Variant | Author / year | Concept |
|---|---|---|
| **IPAT (Internal Pudendal Artery Turnover)** | Nassar 2021<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | Turnover flap requiring no perforator visualization / dissection; raised on reliable IPA vasculature and de-epithelialized to fill deep 3D defects. n = 38 — no flap or partial losses; 10/38 complications (9 minor, 1 return to theatre) |
| **Perineal Turnover Perforator (PTO)** | Chasapi 2018<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | IPA-perforator-based turnover flap; thick gluteal dermis as autologous dermal vascularized substitute for excised pelvic-floor muscles + subcutaneous bulk to obliterate dead space. n = 14 ELAPE; no flap / donor / major wound complications; median OR 49 min |
| **Bilobed pudendal artery perforator flap** | Yun 2010<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> | Bilobed design — improved arc of rotation and mobility for wide / deep defects; preserves distinct urogenital and anal-triangle tissue. n = 15 women (7 vulvar SCC, 7 EMPD, 1 RVF), bilobed in 9 and unilobed in 6; all flaps survived at a mean follow-up of 4.6 months; 3 × 4 to 13 × 12 cm |
| **Gull-wing flap** | Han 2016<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> | IPA-perforator-based gull-wing flap for 3D vulvovaginal reconstruction; perforator = pivot, rotated &gt; 150–180° internally; reconstructs labium and external vaginal wall with sufficient volume |
| **PIPAP for ischial pressure sores** | Legemate 2018<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | Skin flap along the gluteal fold; perforators marked by Doppler medially in the gluteal fold; suprafascial elevation. n = 27 patients / 34 flaps; mean OR 60 ± 21 min; 6/34 flaps (17.6%; the abstract gives 9%) had wound-healing problems that needed no further surgery; 9/34 (26.5%) required a second procedure (3 for recurrent ulcers) |
| **IPAP propeller for penoscrotal reconstruction** | Han 2018<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> | IPAP propeller from the gluteal fold rotated &gt; 90° tension-free; long axis centered on the gluteal fold. n = 10; mean flap 6.7 × 11.7 cm; partial distal necrosis in 1 (healed spontaneously); all patients satisfied; mean follow-up 19.7 mo |
| **Rotation / island / propeller variants** | Loreti 2023<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> | n = 11 APR perineal reconstructions — rotation (8), island advancement (2), propeller (1). 100% flap survival; no immediate major complications; LOS 11 d; reliable in irradiated tissues (8/11) |

## Sensibility — Coltro 2015<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

A prospective study evaluated IPAP cutaneous sensibility after perineal reconstruction:

- 25 patients undergoing APER with bilateral V-Y advancement IPAP flap reconstruction.
- Sensibility was assessed at 4 areas of the gluteal fold (flap donor area) preoperatively and at the corresponding 4 flap areas 12 mo postoperatively.
- **Tactile** (PSSD™): no significant difference between preoperative and postoperative (p &gt; 0.05 in all 4 areas).
- **Pain, thermal, vibration**: 100% preserved in all 4 areas postoperatively.

The authors conclude that cutaneous sensibility of the flap is expected to be maintained; the flap is innervated by branches of the pudendal nerve and the posterior femoral cutaneous nerve. The study was small (n = 25) and compared the flap at 12 months with the preoperative donor area.

## Comparison with Other Flaps

### IPAP vs PAP / TUG for vulvar reconstruction (Han 2023, n = 47)<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

| Parameter | IPAP (n = 31) | PAP / TUG (n = 16) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wound complications** | **12.9%** | **37.5%** | **0.04** |
| Functional complications | Similar | Similar | NS |
| 2-y oncologic recurrence | 14.9% (overall) | No significant difference | NS |

Han et al. proposed an algorithm using the vulvo-thigh junctional crease (inferior pubic ramus) as the decision point: medial defects are reconstructed with IPAP and lateral defects with PAP / TUG. Because flap choice followed defect location, the wound-complication difference reflects different defects as well as different flaps.

### Lotus petal vs V-Y (Confalonieri 2017)<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

The abstract's denominators are internally inconsistent (284 women; 234 V-Y and 128 lotus petal recipients; complication fractions of 27/128 and 14/106), so only the published percentages are shown, and no arm-level counts can be given.

| Parameter | Lotus petal | V-Y | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overall complications | **13%** | 21% | 0.588 (NS) |
| Tunneled LPF | Superior functional / cosmetic results for primary malignancies | — | — |

### IPAP vs primary closure for APR (Coltro 2017)<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

Coltro's prospective series had no primary-closure comparison arm. It reported 95% complete wound healing at 12 wk in irradiated APR patients, and the authors propose the flap as a first choice in selected patients with moderate and some large defects.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> A 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis of 18 studies (2,614 patients) comparing flap reconstruction with primary closure after APR, ELAPE or pelvic exenteration found comparable overall perineal wound complication rates (OR 0.90, 95% CI 0.56–1.47) and a significantly lower risk of pelvic abscess with flap reconstruction; the abstract reports pooled results and the authors call for further high-quality trials.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> In a randomized trial of 51 patients after IPAP reconstruction for irradiated APR defects, an adapted early mobilization program (instead of the traditional 5 days of bed rest) improved independent ambulation on day 5 (68.0% versus 38.5%, p = 0.035) and did not increase complications (68% versus 80.8%, p = 0.296).<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

### IPAP vs VRAM / gracilis<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)[[13]](#ref13)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

| Advantage | IPAP |
|---|---|
| Muscle sacrifice | No (vs gracilis / VRAM) |
| Patient repositioning | Not required (lithotomy) |
| Operative time | Median 49 min (PTO flap after ELAPE) and mean 60 min (PIPAP for pressure sores) in single-arm series; no comparative operative-time data |
| Volume adjustability | Thinned for vulvar / vaginal / anal; full-thickness for pelvic cavity |
| Donor-site morbidity | Lower (no abdominal-wall weakness or thigh weakness) |

For wide vulvovaginal defects that need more tissue to fill pelvic dead space, Negosanti and colleagues preferred a pedicled DIEP flap over the lotus petal flap; musculocutaneous flaps (VRAM, gracilis) are the other options.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

## Risk Factors for Complications (Coltro 2017, n = 73 / 122 flaps APR)<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

- Higher BMI was associated with longer healing time (p = 0.02).
- A defect of 60 cm² or more was associated with Clavien-Dindo III–IV complications (p = 0.03; OR 10.56).
- Anal SCC was associated with higher postoperative complications (p = 0.005; OR 6.09).
- Comorbidities were associated with higher postoperative complications (p = 0.04; OR 2.78).
- Postoperative complications were associated with longer LOS (p = 0.001) and healing time (p &lt; 0.001).

## Position in Reconstructive Algorithms

| Algorithm | Position |
|---|---|
| **Han 2023**<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | Vulvo-thigh junctional crease as decision point — medial → IPAP; lateral → PAP / TUG; Doppler-confirmed perforator |
| **Negosanti 2015**<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | Type IA → monolateral lotus petal; Type IB → bilateral lotus petal; Type II → pedicled DIEP |
| **Toulouse 2025**<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> | Perforator flaps are proposed as the first option, with the flap chosen by the location and size of the vulvar defect |
| **Höckel 2008**<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> | Lotus-petal / gluteal-fold / Singapore flaps (IPAP predecessors) — pudendal-thigh for lateral / hemivulvectomy (unilateral) and total vulvar (bilateral) |

## Limitations

| Limitation | Detail |
|---|---|
| Partial necrosis | 4/71 (5.6%) in Hashimoto; no total failures<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Wide defects with extensive pelvic dead space | Negosanti preferred a pedicled DIEP flap; musculocutaneous flaps (VRAM / gracilis) are alternatives<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| Defect ≥ 60 cm² | Significantly higher Clavien-Dindo III–IV complications<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Higher BMI | Longer healing time<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Anal SCC histology | Higher postoperative complications<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Learning curve | Doppler perforator identification and perforator-based design — though IPAT variant eliminates perforator dissection<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Bulkiness in obese patients | Thinning is possible but requires familiarity with the vascular anatomy<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Limited arc of rotation | Common-sheath / nerve-loop IGA-descending-branch / PFCN topology can constrain propeller rotation |
| 30-day mortality 4% in Coltro APR series | Occurred in patients undergoing extirpative surgery for anorectal tumors<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Hair-bearing skin | May cause chronic discharge / dissatisfaction when used for vaginal reconstruction |

## Relationship to Other Perineal Perforator Flaps

| Feature | **IPAP (Hashimoto 2014)** | Lotus petal (Yii 1996) | Pudendal-thigh / Singapore (Wee 1989) | Gluteal-fold (Hashimoto 2001) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Design philosophy | Free-style perforator | Fasciocutaneous transposition | Axial-pattern fasciocutaneous | Fasciocutaneous island |
| Perforator identification | Handheld Doppler | Not required | Not required | Not required |
| Flap movement | Propeller / V-Y / transposition | Transposition | Transposition / rotation | Transposition / V-Y |
| Thinning | Routine (all except pelvic cavity) | Possible | Limited (two-stage liposuction) | Possible (Hashimoto technique) |
| Fascial plane | Suprafascial (perforator-based) | Subfascial or suprafascial | Subfascial (incl. deep fascia + epimysium) | Subfascial |
| Primary indication | All perineal defects | Vulvoperineal | Vaginal reconstruction | Vulvar / buttock |

<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

The gluteal-fold and lotus petal flaps are names used for the flap Yii and Niranjan described in 1996, and Hashimoto's 2001 anatomic study defined its direct cutaneous supply. The sequence Singapore (1989), lotus petal (1996), gluteal fold (2001) and IPAP (2014) represents a progression from territory-based axial flap to free-style perforator flap, enabling greater design flexibility, routine thinning, and more versatile movement patterns while maintaining the same reliable IPA territory.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Key Takeaways

1. The IPAP flap (Hashimoto 2014) is a free-style pedicled perforator flap on IPA skin perforators identified by handheld Doppler on / around the ischiorectal fossa.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
2. The IPAP is the modern perforator-based evolution of the Singapore, lotus petal and gluteal-fold flaps, with the same IPA vascular territory and a free-style design.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
3. In Hashimoto's 71-flap series, 67 survived completely, four had partial necrosis, and none had total loss; this is not a pooled guarantee for all designs or settings.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
4. At 12 mo, tactile thresholds did not differ significantly from preoperative values, and pain, thermal and vibration sensibility were preserved in all 25 patients.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
5. Fewer wound complications than PAP / TUG in one retrospective series (12.9% vs 37.5%, p = 0.04; flaps assigned by defect location); Han 2023 vulvo-thigh-crease algorithm.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
6. Reported in irradiated tissues: 95% healing at 12 wk after irradiated APR (Coltro); all 11 flaps survived in Loreti's series, 8 of them in previously irradiated tissue.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
7. Adjustable-volume design: thinned for vulvar / vaginal / anal; full-thickness for pelvic-cavity dead-space obliteration.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
8. Toulouse 2025 algorithm: perforator flaps are proposed as the first option, chosen by defect location and size.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
