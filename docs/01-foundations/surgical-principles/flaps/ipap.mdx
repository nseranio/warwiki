# Internal Pudendal Artery Perforator (IPAP) Flap

The **internal pudendal artery perforator (IPAP) flap** is a free-style pedicled perforator-flap approach reported for vulvar, vaginal, buttock, anal, and perineal reconstruction. The name describes its vascular basis; it should not be treated as interchangeable with every pudendal-thigh, lotus-petal, or gluteal-fold flap. This page is an evidence-limited terminology and reconstruction-context overview, not an operative protocol or flap-selection algorithm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Reported clinical context

In the originating IPAP series, 71 flaps were used in 45 cases across several reconstructed regions. The authors reported complete survival in most flaps, partial necrosis in four, and no total flap loss. This single therapeutic Level IV study supports description of the reported technique and its case mix, but does not establish an outcome expectation, comparative advantage, or a general recommendation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

A prospective case series reported IPAP reconstruction after irradiated abdominoperineal resection, while explicitly noting its nonrandomized design. Its findings should be interpreted as cohort-specific rather than as a rule for selection or perioperative management.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Evidence limits

The inherited page included detailed perforator anatomy, Doppler and dissection instructions, variants, outcome tables, sensory and functional claims, complication thresholds, comparisons with other flaps, postoperative management, and selection algorithms. The broader inherited source package was not fully reviewed for this overview. Those details are intentionally omitted.
