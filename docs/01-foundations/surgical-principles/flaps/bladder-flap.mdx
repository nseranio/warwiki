# Bladder Flap Family

“Bladder flap” describes reconstructive uses of vascularized bladder tissue in selected urinary-tract, bladder-neck, or fistula repairs. The term includes several distinct operations and should not be understood as one standardized procedure. This page is a source-bounded framework overview; it does not provide selection criteria, operative technique, defect-length thresholds, outcomes, or postoperative management.

## Anatomic basis

The bladder is a distensible muscular reservoir lined by urothelium. Its arterial supply includes branches of the internal iliac system, notably superior and inferior vesical vessels, with additional variable contributions. The ureters enter the posterolateral bladder obliquely at the trigone.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

These relationships help explain why bladder tissue can be mobilized in reconstructive planning, but the amount of usable tissue, vascular preservation, and effect on bladder function are patient- and procedure-specific.

## Named approaches

Bladder-based reconstruction includes psoas-hitch and Boari-flap approaches for selected ureteral reconstruction, as well as other specialized bladder-wall techniques described for complex settings. Each has distinct indications, alternatives, technical requirements, and evidence limitations. Detailed procedure guidance belongs on the dedicated [Boari Flap & Psoas Hitch](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/boari-flap-psoas-hitch), [Ureteral Reimplantation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/ureteral-reimplantation), and condition-specific pages.

Bladder tissue has also been described in selected fistula and female outlet-reconstruction reports. These are specialized applications and should not be generalized from small series or used to infer comparative effectiveness.
