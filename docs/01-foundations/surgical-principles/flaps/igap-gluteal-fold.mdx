# Gluteal-Region Perforator Flaps (SGAP / IGAP / Gluteal-Fold)

**Superior gluteal artery perforator (SGAP)** and **inferior gluteal artery perforator (IGAP)** flaps are distinct perforator-flap families based on the superior and inferior gluteal vascular systems. A gluteal-fold or infragluteal perforator flap is a related but separately described local flap family. The names are sometimes used imprecisely; this is an anatomic orientation rather than a selection algorithm or operative guide.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Anatomic context

A cadaveric study described both superior and inferior gluteal cutaneous perforators, with differences in their number, location, and cutaneous territories. Most described perforators in that study were musculocutaneous. These findings provide an anatomic basis for both pedicled and free perforator flaps, but do not prescribe a flap design or establish clinical performance in a particular defect.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Reconstructive context

Gluteal perforator flaps have been reported in lumbosacral, perineal, and breast-reconstruction literature. A retrospective IGAP series after abdominoperineal excision reported no flap necrosis in its cohort, while also reporting major flap complications and late complications. A single series cannot establish comparative superiority or a general recommendation over other reconstruction options.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Gluteal propeller-perforator flaps have also been reported for abdominoperineal reconstruction, including both SGAP- and IGAP-based flaps. The accessible full text reports a case series with complications as well as achieved defect coverage; it does not establish a broadly applicable technical or patient-selection rule.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Evidence limits

The inherited page combined several distinct flap families with detailed vascular measurements, harvesting and transfer instructions, clinical tables, comparisons with VRAM, gracilis, PAP, and DIEP flaps, randomized-trial interpretation, and a decision algorithm. Those source packages were not fully reviewed for this page. It therefore intentionally omits numerical anatomy, procedural steps, outcome rates, rankings, and recommendations.
