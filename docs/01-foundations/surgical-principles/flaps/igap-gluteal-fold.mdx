# IGAP / SGAP / Gluteal-Fold Flap

The **gluteal-fold flap family** comprises perforator-based fasciocutaneous flaps centered on the natural gluteal crease, supplied by **inferior gluteal artery perforators (IGAP)** ± **superior gluteal artery perforators (SGAP)** ± **descending branch of the inferior gluteal artery** ± **profunda femoris artery perforators**. Variants are used for perineal, vaginal, vulvar, and scrotal reconstruction with the donor scar concealed in the gluteal fold. The **wide posterior gluteal-thigh propeller flap** incorporates both the IGA descending branch and PFA perforators as a redundant blood-supply design, addressing the ~20% absent / inadequate IGA descending branch.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Anatomy

- **IGAP (inferior gluteal artery perforators).** Musculocutaneous perforators through the inferior third of gluteus maximus; reliable axial supply to the gluteal-fold skin.
- **SGAP (superior gluteal artery perforators).** Musculocutaneous perforators through the upper gluteus maximus; better suited for posterior pelvic defects than perineum.
- **Descending branch of the IGA.** Travels caudally toward the posterior thigh; absent or inadequate in ~20% of specimens — a key vulnerability of single-pedicle posterior-thigh propeller designs.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Composition.** Skin + subcutaneous fat ± fascia; gluteus maximus generally preserved.
- **Dimensions.** Width approximates the ischial-tuberosity-to-greater-trochanter distance; length extends to within ~8 cm of the popliteal fossa.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Genital / Perineal Applications

- **Combined perineal / pelvic defects** after APR, ELAPE, exenteration.
- **Scrotal reconstruction** as part of combined perineal closure, particularly when abdominal donor sites are unavailable.
- **Posterior perineal defects** — better suited than anterior-based flaps.
- **Aesthetic penoscrotal resurfacing** — IPAP gluteal-fold propeller (Han 2018) — well-concealed scar.

---

## Outcomes

- **Ordenana 2021** — wide posterior gluteal-thigh propeller (n = 13): 100% flap survival, redundant IGA + PFA blood supply addresses ~20% IGA-descending-branch dropout.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Winterton 2013 / Thiele 2020 / Koulaxouzidis 2019** — gluteal-fold flaps for perineal reconstruction: faster elevation than gracilis (70 min unilateral / 107 min bilateral vs 105 / 163 min), 97.6% flap success in a 127-flap series, donor scar hidden in the gluteal fold.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Trade-offs.** Overall complication rate ~44% (wound-healing issues predominant); pain-free sitting takes longer; pre-operative radiotherapy prolongs time to discharge.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
