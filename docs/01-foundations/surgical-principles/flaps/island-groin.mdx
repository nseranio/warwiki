# Island Groin Flap (SCIA-Axial) and the SCIP Relationship

The island groin flap is a groin fasciocutaneous flap described around the superficial circumflex iliac artery (SCIA) system. It is related to, but is not interchangeable with, the superficial circumflex iliac artery perforator (SCIP) flap. This is a terminology and literature-context overview—not operative, flap-selection, or defect-management guidance.

## Relationship to SCIP

The literature describes the SCIP flap as a perforator-based development from the conventional free groin flap. Hsu and colleagues reported a 12-patient clinical series using a superficial branch of the SCIA, framing that approach as an evolution of the free groin flap.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Goh and colleagues later reported a retrospective 210-case SCIP series; its design and outcome observations belong to that series and should not be generalized into universal anatomic or comparative claims.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For the dedicated perforator-flap overview, see [SCIP flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/scip).

## Reported reconstruction context

Island groin flaps appear in reconstructive reports including genital and scrotal defects. Sahai and Singh reported a 29-case, single-centre scrotal-reconstruction experience over a 30-year practice period; that report is case-series evidence, not a comparative indication or outcome standard.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The clinical problem and reconstruction discussion are kept in [Scrotal Reconstruction Techniques](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/scrotal-reconstruction) and [Scrotal Flap Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/scrotal-flaps).

## Evidence boundaries

The accessible sources reviewed for this recovery are indexed records/abstracts. They do not support carrying forward detailed anatomy measurements, harvest steps, flap dimensions, complication rates, comparative rankings, or patient-selection recommendations from the inherited article. Those questions require source-specific review in their clinical and procedural contexts.
