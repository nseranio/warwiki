# Island Groin Flap (SCIA-Axial)

The **island groin flap** is the axial fasciocutaneous predecessor of the perforator-based [SCIP](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/scip) — pedicled on the **superficial circumflex iliac artery (SCIA)** as a named axial vessel rather than on individual SCIA perforators. Historically the workhorse for groin-area pedicled coverage; in genital reconstruction it remains a single-stage option for moderate-to-large scrotal defects and, when paired with bilateral superior-medial pedicle thigh flaps, can cover **total scrotal loss**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Anatomy

- **Pedicle:** superficial circumflex iliac artery (SCIA), a branch of the femoral artery arising ~2 cm below the inguinal ligament, with paired vena comitans and superficial venous drainage to the saphenous system.
- **Composition:** skin + subcutaneous fat ± deep fascia along the inguinal axis.
- **Innervation:** non-sensate by default; branches of the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve can be included for variable protective sensation.
- **Distinguished from SCIP:** the SCIA flap takes the named artery wholesale; the SCIP isolates an individual SCIA perforator and allows a much thinner skin paddle. Modern free flaps have largely shifted to SCIP because the SCIA has short pedicle length, small caliber, and variable branching — but those limitations matter less for pedicled scrotal coverage where the donor is adjacent.

---

## Genital / Scrotal Applications

- Single-stage scrotal reconstruction for &gt; 50% defects (Fournier's, avulsion).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Combined with bilateral superior-medial pedicle thigh flaps for **total scrotal loss**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Penoscrotal resurfacing in selected post-debridement defects.
