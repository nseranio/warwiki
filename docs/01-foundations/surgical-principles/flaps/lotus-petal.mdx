# Lotus Petal Flap

The lotus petal flap is a local fasciocutaneous perineal-reconstruction term also used in the literature as the gluteal-fold flap and internal pudendal artery perforator flap. Nomenclature overlaps with “Singapore flap” in some reports, so the related [Singapore / pudendal-thigh flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/singapore-pudendal-thigh) is maintained separately here. This is a terminology and evidence-context overview—not operative, flap-selection, or defect-management guidance.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Reported evidence context

An ultrasound study in 15 subjects described internal-pudendal perforators in the anogenital triangle and evaluated landmark-based harvesting; it does not establish a universal mapping, dissection, or imaging rule.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Lotus-petal reconstruction reports include vulvar and perineal defects. In a retrospective single-centre series of 59 patients and 80 flaps, Argenta and colleagues reported complications and partial flap loss; those estimates are specific to that population and setting, not a general performance standard.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> A second retrospective cohort of 93 patients and 137 flaps categorized postoperative complications, again underscoring that wound complications are material in this reconstructive setting.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Evidence boundaries

The accessible source package for this recovery consists of PubMed abstracts/records for one anatomic-imaging study and two retrospective clinical series. It does not support retaining inherited operative steps, flap dimensions, technical variants, complication pooling, comparative rankings, reconstruction algorithms, functional or quality-of-life claims, treatment recommendations, or postoperative protocols.
