# Superficial Circumflex Iliac Artery Perforator (SCIP) Flap

The superficial circumflex iliac artery perforator (SCIP) flap is a groin-region perforator flap that can be used as a pedicled or free tissue transfer. Published reconstructive reports describe thin, pliable soft-tissue coverage and a nearby donor site, but its appropriate use depends on anatomy, defect requirements, and surgical expertise.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Reported GU and vulvoperineal contexts

Small series describe pedicled SCIP use for male genital defects after conditions including Fournier gangrene, scrotal squamous-cell carcinoma, and severe acne. In a six-person 2022 series, one small wound dehiscence was reported; the study cannot establish comparative safety, function, or a preferred reconstructive pathway.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For vulvoperineal reconstruction after vulvar-cancer surgery, a 2022 retrospective cohort included 32 people and 34 flaps. The authors reported feasibility in their cohort and three wound complications, but the single-center observational design does not establish a first-line flap or a general defect-selection algorithm.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

A 2025 report describes a 10-year single-center experience using SCIP flaps for urethral and/or shaft reconstruction in gender-diverse people. The full article was blocked by a PMC download challenge in this audit, so no detailed outcomes, operative approach, or comparative claim is carried forward here.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Evidence boundary

Available source access for this review was limited to PubMed records and abstracts; the accessible full-text version of the 2025 gender-diverse series could not be retrieved. The evidence reviewed here consists of observational series, not randomized or broadly comparative trials. It does not determine flap choice across phalloplasty, genital reconstruction, fistula repair, vulvoperineal reconstruction, or lymphatic surgery.

## What this page does not establish

This page does not provide vascular anatomy measurements, imaging rules, harvest or inset instructions, flap dimensions, donor-site morbidity rates, lymph-node-transfer technique, phalloplasty urethral protocols, fistula repair methods, or a comparison with RFFF, ALT, gracilis, or other flaps. Those decisions require procedure-specific evidence and reconstructive assessment.
