# Singapore / Pudendal Thigh Flap

The Singapore, or pudendal-thigh fasciocutaneous, flap is a sensate axial-pattern flap supplied by terminal branches of the superficial perineal artery. It has been reported for vaginal, perineal, fistula, and selected urethral reconstruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Nomenclature boundary

The terms “Singapore flap,” “pudendal-thigh flap,” and related perineal perforator terminology are not used uniformly. This page concerns the pudendal-thigh design. The gluteal-fold [lotus petal flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/lotus-petal) is a distinct flap family and should not be treated as interchangeable without source-specific anatomic review.

## Reported reconstructive contexts

In a 2001 series, bilateral use was reported for congenital vaginal atresia (eight patients) and after oncologic resection (five patients), while unilateral use was reported for recurrent or complex rectovaginal fistula (four patients) and two posterior-urethral defects. The report described complete survival of 31 flaps, but this uncontrolled single-center experience does not establish comparative effectiveness or a universal indication.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

An earlier eight-person oncologic vaginal-reconstruction series reported partial apical necrosis in four people and complete bilateral necrosis in one; the abstract describes poor functional outcomes in patients with bilateral flaps or prior pelvic irradiation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> This historical series signals uncertainty and does not by itself define radiation-field management or select an alternative flap.

The 2022 obstetric-fistula follow-up reports use of Singapore and/or gracilis flaps in complex cases; the accessible abstract does not supply sufficient detail to support outcome rates, procedural criteria, or a general recommendation.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Evidence boundary

All source access for this review was limited to PubMed records and abstracts. The directly reviewed evidence is composed of case reports and retrospective series. It cannot establish harvest or inset technique, a no-dilation protocol, defect algorithms, radiation-field decisions, complication rates, or superiority over gracilis, VRAM, Martius, lotus-petal, or other reconstructive options.
