# Singapore Flap (Pudendal Thigh Fasciocutaneous Flap)

The **Singapore flap** — also called the **pudendal thigh fasciocutaneous flap** — is a pedicled fasciocutaneous flap from the medial groin and upper medial thigh based on the **posterior labial artery branch of the internal pudendal artery**. Originally described in Singapore (hence the name) for vaginal reconstruction after oncologic resection, it is the **second most commonly used regional flap for post-Fournier's reconstruction** and a workhorse for posterior vaginal reconstruction and vulvovaginal defect coverage.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Vascular Anatomy

- **Source vessel:** posterior labial artery branch of the **internal pudendal artery** (Alcock's canal)
- The pedicle enters the flap medially, near the labia majora
- Flap territory: medial groin and upper medial thigh skin
- **Bilateral flaps** commonly used for vaginal reconstruction — the two flaps are joined at the midline to form the posterior and lateral walls of the neovagina

---

## Applications in GU Reconstruction

### Vaginal reconstruction

The **primary indication.** Bilateral Singapore flaps are raised, tubularized, and brought together to create a neovagina after:

- Radical vulvectomy / partial vaginectomy
- Radiation injury with vaginal loss
- Total pelvic exenteration (when VRAM is unavailable or inadequate)
- Congenital vaginal agenesis (MRKH) — in selected reconstruction pathways

### Perineal and vulvovaginal reconstruction

- Post-Fournier's reconstruction of the **posterior perineum** (the medial thigh flap typically covers the anterior perineum)
- Vulvar reconstruction after tumor resection
- Vaginal introital stenosis reconstruction

### Urethral reconstruction (rare)

- Female urethral reconstruction in specialized contexts where vaginal wall is also deficient

---

## Design and Harvest

### Design

- **Flap outlined on the medial thigh**, centered over the axis of the posterior labial artery perforator territory
- Typically **6 × 15 cm** — length of 15 cm achievable with preserved perforator
- **Distal aspect** of the flap is the advancing edge; proximal aspect (near the vaginal introitus) contains the pedicle

### Dissection

1. Incise skin and subcutaneous tissue to the adductor fascia
2. Raise the flap **including the adductor fascia** — the fascia carries the posterior labial artery perforators
3. Dissect toward the pedicle medially, identifying and preserving the **posterior labial artery** and its venae comitantes
4. Mobilize to allow the flap to reach the perineum, vaginal introitus, or vaginal vault
5. For **neovagina construction**, the flap is tubularized with the skin surface facing inward; bilateral flaps are joined at the midline posteriorly
6. **Donor site** typically closes primarily with modest tension

### Technical points

- The **adductor fascia must be included** — the flap fails reliably if raised above the fascia
- Preserve the posterior labial artery through Doppler-guided dissection in reoperative fields
- Bilateral harvest is tolerated; donor-site morbidity is modest

---

## Outcomes

Across large series for vaginal reconstruction and vulvovaginal coverage:

- **Flap survival &gt;90%** in experienced hands
- **Partial necrosis** at the distal flap in 10–15%; managed with local wound care
- **Neovaginal patency** maintained long-term in most patients (with postoperative dilator regimen)
- **Sexual function** — variable; depends on patient anatomy, radiation status, and partner involvement

---

## Advantages

- **Pedicled** — no microvascular anastomosis
- **Tissue match** — medial thigh skin is pigmented and pliable, approximating vulvar and vaginal-canal skin character
- **Bilateral availability** — two flaps can be raised for circumferential vaginal reconstruction
- **Low donor morbidity** — thigh donor site usually closes primarily with a linear scar
- **Sensate potential** — the ilioinguinal and genitofemoral nerves can be preserved within the flap, giving the neovagina some native sensation

---

## Limitations

- **Hair-bearing** — brings pigmented and sometimes hair-bearing skin into the neovagina; preoperative hair removal can mitigate
- **Random-pattern distal necrosis** risk at the distal flap tip when flap length is maximized
- **Dependence on posterior labial artery integrity** — prior radiation, vulvectomy, or groin dissection may compromise the pedicle
- **Inadequate for large composite defects** — VRAM or gracilis preferred when bulk is needed beyond skin and fascia

## Comparison with Other Vaginal Reconstruction Flaps

| Flap | Tissue | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Singapore / pudendal thigh (this page)** | Fasciocutaneous | Bilateral neovagina, posterior perineum |
| **[Gracilis myocutaneous](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/gracilis)** | Myocutaneous | Deep vaginal reconstruction with bulk; pelvic exenteration |
| **[VRAM](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/vram)** | Myocutaneous | Full vaginal reconstruction with bulk; exenteration |
| **[ALT](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/anterolateral-thigh)** | Fasciocutaneous / perforator | Extensive vulvovaginal defects; young patients with minimal adipose |
| **[Martius](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/martius)** | Adipofascial | Fistula interposition; urethrovaginal defects |
| **[Labia majora fasciocutaneous](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/labia-majora-fasciocutaneous)** | Fasciocutaneous | VVF + vaginal-wall deficit |

---

## Technical Pearls

- **Doppler the pedicle before incision** — particularly in radiated or reoperative fields
- **Include the fascia** in the flap — suprafascial elevation leads to distal necrosis
- **Tubularize with skin inward** for neovagina construction
- **Postoperative vaginal dilator regimen** is essential for long-term neovaginal patency
- **Partner counseling** — the neovagina has different sensation and lubrication characteristics from the native vagina; expectation setting is part of standard care
