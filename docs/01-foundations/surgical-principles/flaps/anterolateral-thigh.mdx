# Anterolateral Thigh (ALT) Flap

The anterolateral thigh (ALT) flap is a perforator flap from the anterolateral thigh, commonly based on branches of the lateral circumflex femoral system. It can provide skin, subcutaneous tissue, fascia, and, in selected designs, muscle. This page is a source-bounded flap anatomy overview; it does not provide flap selection, harvesting, phalloplasty, perineal reconstruction, or donor-site management guidance.

## Vascular territory and variability

The ALT flap is generally supplied by perforators related to the descending branch of the lateral circumflex femoral artery. These perforators may follow musculocutaneous or septocutaneous courses, and their origin and pattern vary substantially. This anatomic variability is a defining feature of the flap and should not be reduced to a fixed template.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The vascular pedicle courses in the anterolateral thigh in relation to the rectus femoris and vastus lateralis. The skin territory can be designed with different tissue components, including fasciocutaneous and myocutaneous configurations; the appropriate configuration depends on the defect and is beyond this overview.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Reconstructive role

ALT tissue has been used in diverse reconstructive settings because its composition and vascular anatomy can be adapted to soft-tissue defects. In genitourinary and pelvic reconstruction, the flap may be considered in multidisciplinary planning for selected complex defects, but outcomes and indications are condition-, patient-, and center-specific.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The flap is also used in phalloplasty and other gender-affirming reconstruction. Decisions among ALT, radial forearm, abdominal, and other donor sites require dedicated current evidence, patient-centered counseling, and specialized operative expertise; this page does not rank techniques or report comparative outcomes.

## Donor site

Donor-site effects and closure options vary with flap dimensions, tissue composition, perforator anatomy, and patient factors. Systematic reviews describe generally favorable donor-site experience but also heterogeneous methods and complications; numerical thresholds and closure algorithms require focused review and are not reproduced here.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
