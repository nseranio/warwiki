# Vertical Rectus Abdominis Myocutaneous (VRAM) Flap

The vertical rectus abdominis myocutaneous (VRAM) flap is a rectus-abdominis muscle and skin-paddle reconstruction used in pelvic and perineal surgery. A 2021 systematic review assessed its use after pelvic surgery; it does not constitute a universal standard for any individual defect.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Reported pelvic and genitourinary use

A retrospective cohort of 12 selected people with complex recurrent urogenital fistulas used five VRAM and eight gracilis flaps. The authors reported final fistula treatment in all cases, with continence preserved but some residual incontinence among the three VRAM patients alive at follow-up. This small, selected series cannot determine comparative continence outcomes or flap choice.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

A four-patient report described rectus-abdominis vaginoplasty after anterior exenteration and vaginal resection for invasive bladder or urethral cancer.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

A 2022 supraregional series documented abdominal and perineal hernia after VRAM reconstruction. Its results are specific to its cohort and do not establish a universal donor-site rate or a mesh recommendation.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Evidence boundary

The sources directly reviewed for this page were PubMed records and abstracts. They do not establish anatomy measurements, pedicle flow, flap dimensions, operative harvesting or tunneling, rotation rules, donor-site closure or mesh practice, robotic technique, complication rates across indications, or comparative superiority over gracilis, ALT–VL, gluteal, omental, or other flaps.
