# Posterior Thigh Flap (Friedman / Reece)

The **posterior thigh flap** is a fasciocutaneous flap of the posterior thigh based on the **descending branch of the inferior gluteal artery** and **profunda femoris artery perforators**, designed primarily for **complex pelvic and perineal reconstruction** when laparotomy should be avoided or when both urinary and fecal diversion are already present. Friedman & Reece's published series reported successful transfer in 26/27 flaps (96.3%); early wound-healing complications are common (~53%) but only one patient required a secondary flap.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Overlaps anatomically with the [IGAP / gluteal-fold flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/igap-gluteal-fold) family (descending IGA branch + PFA perforators); the posterior thigh flap variant emphasizes a vertical posterior-thigh skin paddle rather than a gluteal-crease orientation.

---

## Anatomy

- **Pedicle.** Descending branch of the inferior gluteal artery + profunda femoris artery perforators; can be designed with a redundant dual-supply pattern.
- **Composition.** Skin + subcutaneous fat ± fascia along the posterior thigh.
- **Orientation.** Vertical along the posterior thigh; can be raised unilaterally or bilaterally.

---

## Indications

- Complex perineal wounds when [VRAM](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/vram) and [gracilis](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/gracilis) are unavailable or unsuitable.
- Situations where **laparotomy should be avoided** (e.g., dense pelvic adhesions, anticoagulation, frailty).
- Patients with **pre-existing urinary and fecal diversion** in whom abdominal donors carry additional morbidity.
- Combined scrotal-perineal coverage as an adjunct.

---

## Outcomes

Friedman & Reece (n = 27 flaps):<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- **96.3% successful transfer.**
- Early wound-healing complications **~53%**.
- Secondary procedures in **37%** of patients; only 1 required a second flap.
