# Posterior Thigh Flap (Gluteal-Thigh)

The posterior-thigh or gluteal-thigh flap is a fasciocutaneous reconstructive-flap term. Published anatomic work describes a posterior-thigh fascial plexus supplied primarily by profunda perforators and secondarily by an inferior-gluteal branch. This is a terminology and evidence-context overview—not operative, flap-selection, or defect-management guidance.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Reported evidence context

Rubin and colleagues reported an anatomic study and 24 clinical posterior-thigh flaps, including use in patients with prior inferior-gluteal-artery ligation. This is a small clinical experience alongside cadaveric work, not a universal vascular or selection standard.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Friedman and colleagues retrospectively reported 27 posterior-thigh flaps in 19 patients with complex pelvic or perineal wounds. The series included difficult, frequently previously treated clinical situations; it reported one flap failure and common early wound-healing complications. Its outcomes must not be generalized into a comparative ranking or procedure recommendation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Achauer and colleagues reported nine gluteal-thigh flaps in seven patients for immediate vaginal reconstruction after oncologic resection, further illustrating a historical, small-series use rather than a standardized approach.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Evidence boundaries

The accessible source package for this recovery consists of PubMed abstracts/records for one anatomic-plus-clinical report and two small retrospective clinical series. It does not support retaining inherited anatomy measurements, operative steps, nerve-management guidance, dimensions, outcome pooling, comparative tables, indications, reconstruction algorithms, radiation claims, technical recommendations, or postoperative protocols.
