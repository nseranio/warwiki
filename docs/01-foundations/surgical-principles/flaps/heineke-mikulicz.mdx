# Heineke-Mikulicz Technique

The **Heineke-Mikulicz principle** describes a geometric maneuver in which a longitudinal opening across a narrowing is closed transversely. It originated in pyloroplasty and has been adapted in several reconstructive settings. This page is a conceptual overview, not a procedure guide or a rule for selecting treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

![Heineke-Mikulicz geometry: Generic luminal repair, conceptual sequence](/img/diagrams/heineke-mikulicz.svg)

*Conceptual geometry only: a longitudinal opening is closed transversely, changing the local shape. Tissue mobility, vascularity, anatomy, and the particular defect determine whether any adaptation is appropriate. (Original WARWIKI schematic; see the figure source record and review limits.)*

## Urethral context

A small urethral series reported use of longitudinal stricturotomy with transverse closure in selected strictures. The study was preliminary, comprised ten patients, and its authors stated that larger cohorts were needed. It does not establish an outcome expectation for other stricture locations, severity, populations, or techniques.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The published literature also uses the principle in other urethral, genital-skin, bowel, and upper-tract contexts. Those applications have distinct anatomy, goals, and evidence bases; they should be considered only within their dedicated condition and procedural reviews.
