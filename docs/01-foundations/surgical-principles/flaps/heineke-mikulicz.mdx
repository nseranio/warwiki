# Heineke-Mikulicz Technique

The **Heineke-Mikulicz technique** is a reconstructive principle in which a **longitudinal incision** across a short luminal narrowing is **closed transversely** (at 90°), widening that lumen without graft harvest. Independently described by **Walter Hermann Heineke (1886)** and **Johann von Mikulicz-Radecki (1887)** as a pyloroplasty, the principle has been adapted to selected urethral strictures and ureteropelvic-junction obstruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In genital-skin applications, the incision and closure axes depend on the structure and the desired direction of lengthening; the penoscrotal-web repair below uses the reverse orientation. Related geometric techniques include [Y-V plasty](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/y-v-plasty) (advancement) and [Z-plasty](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/z-plasty) (transposition).

## Geometric Principle

Three ideas define the technique:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

1. **Incise long, close transverse.** A longitudinal cut along the axis of a tubular structure, carried across the stenosis into healthy tissue at both ends, is then sutured perpendicular to its original axis. The wound's midpoints are drawn apart while its ends are brought together.
2. **Caliber is traded for length.** The closure widens the cross-section at the former narrowing while modestly shortening the segment — the gain is bounded by how far the transverse closure will stretch, so the technique only works for **short** narrowings.
3. **Full transection may be avoided.** In a suitable urethral repair, the segment is incised rather than fully divided, preserving more native continuity; the actual blood supply still depends on tissue quality and dissection. This is not a guarantee of perfusion in scarred or previously operated tissue.

---

## Origin

Heineke and Mikulicz independently introduced the maneuver as a **pyloroplasty** — a gastric drainage procedure — in the 1880s.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The identical geometry remains the most common short-segment intestinal **strictureplasty**, with the Finney and Michelassi strictureplasties reserved for longer bowel segments. In genitourinary reconstruction the same principle is borrowed wherever a **short, focal** narrowing can be opened and re-closed without resection.

## Applications in Reconstructive Urology

### Male urethral stricture — non-transecting "stricturoplasty"

Lumen and colleagues formally applied the principle to the urethra as a **ventral longitudinal stricturotomy with transverse closure**.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Because the fibrotic segment is **incised rather than excised** and the urethra is never transected, it is a [non-transecting repair](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/non-transecting-bulbar) — sometimes called a *stricturoplasty* rather than a true urethroplasty.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Candidate selection is strict: a **short (&lt;1 cm), not-too-narrow, minimally fibrotic** stricture of the **bulbar urethra or fossa navicularis**. In the original series, 9 of 10 patients (90%) were treated successfully.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

A more recent extension is the **double-sided Heineke-Mikulicz urethroplasty** for short (3–20 mm) strictures around the **penoscrotal junction**: the authors describe ventral and dorsal wall incisions in a non-transecting repair without substitute tissue. In the initial 11-patient series, 9 were recurrence-free at a median 33 months; three reported slight shortening without reported sexual-activity impairment.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

Where it sits in the ladder: this is a selected small-series technique, not a named recommendation of the **AUA urethral-stricture guideline**. Guideline-level choice for a short bulbar stricture is broader and depends on location, length, spongiofibrosis, prior treatment and available tissue; longer disease may require [substitution urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/urethral-reconstruction-principles).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### Female urethral stricture — meatoplasty

For distal or meatal stenosis, the Heineke-Mikulicz **meatoplasty** opens the meatus with a **ventral longitudinal incision** carried to healthy urethral mucosa (admitting a ~20 Fr catheter), then sutures the **urethral mucosa transversely to the vaginal mucosa**, widening the outlet without a flap or graft.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> See [Female Urethral Stricture](/docs/clinical-conditions/03b-voiding-outlet/female-urethral-stricture).

### Ureter and ureteropelvic junction

The principle is the basis of the **non-dismembered Fenger pyeloplasty**: in selected short ureteropelvic-junction narrowings, a longitudinal incision is closed transversely without dividing the UPJ. The related [Foley Y-V pyeloplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/pyeloplasty) uses advancement geometry for different anatomy.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> In the ureter proper, Tsivian reported **one case** in which the maneuver bridged a gap after upper-ureteral injury, avoiding more extensive substitution in that patient; it is not a broadly validated answer to extensive ureteral loss.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> See [Ureteroureterostomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/ureteroureterostomy) and [UPJ Obstruction](/docs/clinical-conditions/03e-upper-tract/upj-obstruction).

### Genital skin

- **Preputioplasty** — a **foreskin-preserving** option for selected adults with phimosis. In one seven-patient, single-surgeon retrospective series, a dorsal vertical incision over the phimotic band (adding a ventral incision in two patients) was closed horizontally; one patient reported recurrent bothersome phimosis at a median follow-up of only 1.8 months and was treated with triamcinolone. Longer-term durability is not established by this series.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Penoscrotal web (scrotoplasty)** — a **transverse incision closed vertically** can release a tethering web and lengthen ventral shaft skin; this reverses the incision/closure axes of the classic luminal stricturoplasty. In a 40-child, two-group comparison, Heineke-Mikulicz repair took less operative time than Z-plasty, but the success-rate difference (85% versus 95%) was not statistically significant. The groups were small, and the result is not proof of equivalent long-term outcomes.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

### Urethral diverticulum

A **single** recurrent male urethral diverticulum with calculus formation — after repeated excision-and-repair — was reconstructed with defect excision followed by Heineke-Mikulicz closure; its authors reported no recurrence during their follow-up. This case does not establish a general diverticulum-repair strategy.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

### Transmasculine / gender-affirming reconstruction

For a selected short neourethral stricture after phalloplasty or metoidioplasty, the Heineke-Mikulicz repair is one reconstructive option. In one single-center retrospective cohort, six of seven such repairs were successful; the small selected group does not establish suitability for every neourethral location or scar pattern.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> See [Revision and Salvage in Gender-Affirming Surgery](/docs/special-populations/05c-gender-affirming/revision-salvage-gas).

## Patient Selection and Limitations

| | |
|---|---|
| **Potential fit** | A **short, focal** luminal narrowing with healthy, mobile tissue; site-specific evaluation remains necessary. |
| **Limits** | Long, dense, obliterative or multifocal disease may not permit a useful tension-free transverse closure; alternative reconstruction is selected by anatomy and etiology, not one universal length cutoff. |
| **Relatives** | [Y-V plasty](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/y-v-plasty) (advancement — bladder-neck contracture, VUAS, Foley pyeloplasty, meatoplasty); [Z-plasty](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/z-plasty) (transposition — scar lengthening/reorientation); Finney / Michelassi strictureplasties for longer bowel segments. |

For the classic **luminal** repair, local caliber is gained at the cost of some length, provided the tissues permit tension-free closure. Genital-skin adaptations must be described by their actual incision and closure axes rather than assuming that lumen-widening geometry applies unchanged.
