# Blandy Flap

The **Blandy flap** is an eponymous urethral reconstructive technique attributed to Sir John Blandy (1927–2011), Professor of Urology at The London Hospital and a pioneering British urologist. The eponym refers to two distinct but related techniques:

1. **Female Blandy ventral vaginal flap urethroplasty or meatoplasty** is an inverted U-shaped anterior vaginal wall advancement flap for selected female urethral strictures. This historical use is the main focus of this page. Current technique selection depends on stricture location, tissue quality and surgeon experience.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
2. **Male Blandy perineal urethrostomy** is a permanent perineal stoma made with a posteriorly based inverted-U perineal skin flap sutured to an opened bulbar urethra. It is distinct from a laterally based scrotal 7-flap.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

Sir John Blandy's contributions to urethral surgery, stone disease and oncology are memorialized in the textbook *Blandy's Urology* (now in its 3rd edition).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The "Blandy technique" for female urethral stricture was described by Romero-Maroto and colleagues as "the most common technique used" for this condition.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Female Blandy Ventral Vaginal Flap Urethroplasty

### Technique

A ventral meatoplasty / urethroplasty using the anterior vaginal wall as a local advancement flap to augment the strictured distal female urethra:

1. **Position.** Dorsal lithotomy; Foley catheter to identify the urethral lumen.
2. **Incision.** An inverted U-shaped incision on the anterior vaginal wall, with the base of the U surrounding the urethral meatus and the arms extending proximally along the periurethral vaginal mucosa.
3. **Urethrotomy.** The ventral (posterior) wall of the strictured urethra is incised longitudinally through the meatus and proximally through the full length of the stricture until healthy non-strictured urethral tissue is reached.
4. **Flap advancement.** The U-shaped vaginal mucosal flap is advanced distally and sutured into the opened urethral defect, widening the urethral lumen by incorporating vaginal-wall tissue into the ventral urethral plate.
5. **Closure.** Suture the flap to the urethral edges with absorbable suture and close the vaginal-wall donor site primarily. Leave a temporary catheter according to the actual reconstruction and healing plan; no single duration applies to every flap repair.

Like the male [Orandi penile-skin onlay flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/penile-preputial), this is tissue-augmented reconstruction after a longitudinal urethrotomy and is not a simple transverse Heineke-Mikulicz closure. Simonato's series describes a different, lateral vaginal flap and should not be used as an operative description of the inverted-U Blandy flap.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### Primary indication — female urethral stricture

Female urethral stricture is rare. Management options:

- **Urethral dilation.** The EAU guideline accepts dilation as an initial option for uncomplicated stricture; dilation-only patency was 40.1% across its summarized studies (range 7.5–51%). The often-quoted 6% first-dilation figure came from one selected 17-patient series and does not describe all women. Patency maintained by ongoing intermittent self-dilation is not cure.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Distal urethrectomy with advancement meatoplasty**
- **Blandy ventral vaginal flap meatoplasty / urethroplasty**
- **Lateral-based vaginal wall flap** (Orandi-inspired modification)
- **Dorsal or ventral buccal mucosal graft (BMG) urethroplasty**

The 2023 AUA guideline amendment states that surgeons may reconstruct female strictures using oral mucosal grafts, vaginal flaps or a combination (Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level C) and cites success rates of 69–95% across these techniques, not Blandy alone. The EAU 2026 guideline lists initial dilation to 24–41 Fr (weak recommendation) and recommends urethroplasty (strong recommendation) for a second recurrence when a woman cannot perform intermittent self-dilation or wants definitive treatment. It recommends meatotomy or meatoplasty for meatal strictures (weak).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

### Other female applications

#### Meatal reconstruction after vulvectomy

An anatomically related **vaginal-wall V-Y advancement** has been used for urethral meatus reconstruction after oncologic vulvectomy, which is a different indication from female stricture repair. In 42 reconstructions in 41 women, the authors reported three major complications: one neomeatal stenosis, one partial vaginal-flap necrosis and one circumferential neomeatal dehiscence and occlusion. The V-Y design was intended to avoid a circumferential suture line; this series does not prove that it eliminates stenosis.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

#### Distal urethral stricture in transwomen

The inverted U-flap concept has been applied to neo-vaginal advancement flap urethroplasty in transwomen with distal urethral strictures. In five patients (three inverted-U, including one with an additional buccal graft for lichen sclerosus, and two seven-flap), no recurrences were observed at median 37-month follow-up. This mixed, small series does not isolate the Blandy effect.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

### Limitations and known drawbacks

- **Retrusive meatus.** Romero-Maroto's group reported a retrusive meatus and inward-directed stream after the Blandy technique in their own patients. This motivated their lateral-based vaginal-wall flap modification, but its nine-patient single-arm series (mean follow-up 80.7 months) is not proof of comparative superiority.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Limited reach for proximal strictures.** The flap is best suited to distal and meatal strictures; lateral-based vaginal flaps, dorsal BMG or combined approaches are preferred for mid-urethral or longer strictures.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Vaginal-tissue quality.** Coexisting lichen sclerosus or vaginal atrophy may affect the results of vaginal or labial tissue and favor buccal mucosa, and prior radiation is a further practical concern for tissue quality.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **New urinary incontinence.** The EAU compilation reports 15/161 (9.3%) new UI across vaginal-flap variants, which is not a Blandy-specific SUI estimate. Hajebrahimi reported two SUI cases among 14 women (one improved by 6 months; one had a TOT at 12 months) and new-onset urgency or worsening urge incontinence during follow-up. Counsel according to baseline continence and planned reconstruction.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

### Comparison with alternative female-urethral-stricture techniques

These are descriptive options and not a ranking. The EAU estimates combine different series, patient selection and follow-up, and the small named series cannot establish one flap's superiority.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

| Technique | Reported setting | Practical feature | Tradeoff | Reported outcome (not head-to-head) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Blandy (ventral vaginal flap)** | Selected female strictures with suitable vaginal tissue | Local vascularized tissue; no oral graft harvest | Possible retrusive meatus; tissue quality/reach matter | All vaginal-flap variants: **81.4% patency in 161 women** (EAU); not a Blandy-only rate |
| **Lateral vaginal-wall flap** | Selected female strictures | Attempts to preserve meatal position | Pedicle dissection; limited comparative data | **9/9** without recurrence in Romero-Maroto's single series |
| **Dorsal oral mucosal graft** | Selected patients needing a separate tissue source | Avoids diseased vaginal donor tissue | Oral donor-site morbidity | **83%** across 319 dorsal oral-graft cases (EAU) |
| **Ventral oral mucosal graft** | Selected anatomy favoring ventral access | Graft option with or without interposition | Donor site and repair-dependent support | **90.7%** across 247 ventral oral-graft cases (EAU); not specifically Martius-supported |
| **Meatoplasty** | **Short isolated meatal stricture** | Local repair without long-segment reconstruction | Does not address proximal disease | EAU cites **48/50 girls** at 12 months; not an adult Blandy comparison |
| **V-Y vaginal-wall advancement after vulvectomy** | Post-oncologic meatal reconstruction | Avoids a circular inset | Distinct disease and reconstructive setting | **Three major complications in 42 repairs** (one stenosis, one partial flap necrosis, one dehiscence/occlusion) |

## Male Blandy Perineal Urethrostomy

The male Blandy [perineal urethrostomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/blandy-perineal-urethrostomy) uses a posteriorly based inverted-U perineal skin flap advanced to a ventrally opened bulbar urethra, which is laid open and not transected, to form a permanent perineal stoma. It is an option for selected complex anterior strictures and does not guarantee a low restenosis rate. In Lumen's comparison, 2 of 16 Blandy and 9 of 35 Johanson urethrostomies failed, with follow-up of 11.1 versus 47.9 months. The dedicated page contains the operative sequence and source-specific outcomes. The laterally based scrotal-skin 7-flap is a different operation.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

The flap's reach and local skin quality matter, and perineal skin is not inherently hairless. Prior perineal surgery, altered tissue or radiation may complicate stoma construction. This eponym should not be extended to an anterior urethral onlay or inlay graft or conflated with penile-skin Orandi reconstruction.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

## Key Takeaways

1. The female Blandy is an established ventral vaginal-tissue flap for selected female urethral strictures; the EAU's 81.4% patency combines 161 women across several vaginal-flap variants and is not a Blandy-only rate.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
2. A retrusive meatus or altered stream is a recognized concern. The lateral alternative has small uncontrolled data (9 patients, no recurrence) and no proven superiority.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
3. **Initial dilation is an option**, but recurrent stricture may warrant urethroplasty; technique choice depends on anatomy, tissue quality, patient preference and surgical expertise. Ongoing self-dilation patency is not cure.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
4. A short isolated meatal stricture may be treated by meatoplasty; no single flap or graft is the universal first choice.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
5. The male Blandy perineal urethrostomy advances posteriorly based perineal skin to an opened bulbar urethra, unlike the laterally based scrotal 7-flap.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
