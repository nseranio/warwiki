# Blandy Flap

The **Blandy flap** is an eponymous urethral reconstructive technique attributed to **Sir John Blandy** (1927–2011), Professor of Urology at The London Hospital and a pioneering British urologist. The eponym refers to two distinct but related techniques:

1. **Female Blandy ventral vaginal flap urethroplasty / meatoplasty** — an inverted U-shaped anterior vaginal wall advancement flap for selected female urethral strictures. This historical use is the main focus of this page; contemporary technique selection is individualized.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
2. **Male Blandy perineal urethrostomy** — a permanent perineal stoma made with a posteriorly based inverted-U **perineal skin** flap sutured to an opened bulbar urethra. It is distinct from a laterally based scrotal 7-flap.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

Sir John Blandy's contributions to urethral surgery, stone disease, and oncology are memorialized in the textbook *Blandy's Urology* (now in its 3rd edition).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The "Blandy technique" for female urethral stricture has been described as **"the most common technique used"** for this condition in the open-surgical era.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Female Blandy Ventral Vaginal Flap Urethroplasty

### Technique

A ventral meatoplasty / urethroplasty using the anterior vaginal wall as a local advancement flap to augment the strictured distal female urethra:

1. **Position** — dorsal lithotomy; Foley catheter to identify the urethral lumen.
2. **Incision** — an **inverted U-shaped incision** on the anterior vaginal wall, with the base of the U surrounding the urethral meatus and the arms extending proximally along the periurethral vaginal mucosa.
3. **Urethrotomy** — the ventral (posterior) wall of the strictured urethra is incised longitudinally through the meatus and proximally through the full length of the stricture until healthy non-strictured urethral tissue is reached.
4. **Flap advancement** — the U-shaped vaginal mucosal flap is advanced distally and sutured into the opened urethral defect, widening the urethral lumen by incorporating vaginal-wall tissue into the ventral urethral plate.
5. **Closure** — flap sutured to urethral edges with absorbable suture; vaginal-wall donor site closed primarily. Leave a temporary catheter according to the actual reconstruction and healing plan; no single duration applies to every flap repair.

Like the male **[Orandi penile-skin onlay flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/penile-preputial)**, this is tissue-augmented reconstruction after a longitudinal urethrotomy, **not** a simple transverse Heineke-Mikulicz closure. Simonato's series describes a different, lateral vaginal flap; it should not be used as an operative description of the inverted-U Blandy flap.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### Primary indication — female urethral stricture

Female urethral stricture is rare. Management options:

- **Urethral dilation** — an acceptable initial option for uncomplicated stricture in the EAU guideline; dilation-only patency was **40.1%** across its summarized studies (range 7.5–51%). The often-quoted 6% first-dilation figure came from **one selected 17-patient series**, not all women. Patency maintained by ongoing intermittent self-dilation is not cure.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Distal urethrectomy with advancement meatoplasty**
- **Blandy ventral vaginal flap meatoplasty / urethroplasty**
- **Lateral-based vaginal wall flap** (Orandi-inspired modification)
- **Dorsal or ventral buccal mucosal graft (BMG) urethroplasty**

The **2023 AUA guideline** supports offering female urethroplasty using oral graft, vaginal flap, or both after ineffective endoscopic treatment; its reported 69–95% range spans **different reconstructive techniques**, not Blandy alone. The **EAU guideline** accepts initial dilation, but recommends urethroplasty after a second recurrence when a woman cannot continue intermittent self-dilation or wants definitive treatment. A short isolated meatal stricture may instead be treated by meatoplasty.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

### Other female applications

#### Meatal reconstruction after vulvectomy

An anatomically related **vaginal-wall V-Y advancement** has been used for urethral meatus reconstruction after oncologic vulvectomy; this is not the same indication as female stricture repair. In 42 reconstructions in 41 women, the authors reported **one stenosis and one dehiscence/occlusion**. The V-Y design was intended to avoid a circumferential suture line; this series does not prove that it eliminates stenosis.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

#### Distal urethral stricture in transwomen

The inverted U-flap concept has been applied to neo-vaginal advancement flap urethroplasty in transwomen with distal urethral strictures. In five patients (three inverted-U, including one with an additional buccal graft for lichen sclerosus, and two seven-flap), **no recurrences were observed** at median 37-month follow-up; this mixed, small series does not isolate the Blandy effect.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

### Limitations and known drawbacks

- **Retrusive meatus** — some surgeons reported a posteriorly displaced meatus and inward-directed stream after ventral advancement. This motivated the **Romero-Maroto lateral-based vaginal-wall flap** modification, but its nine-patient single-arm series is not proof of comparative superiority.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Limited reach for proximal strictures** — best suited for distal and meatal strictures; lateral-based vaginal flaps, dorsal BMG, or combined approaches are preferred for mid-urethral or longer strictures.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Vaginal-tissue quality** — depends on healthy, well-estrogenized vaginal tissue. Lichen sclerosus, vaginal atrophy, or prior radiation may compromise the flap and favor buccal mucosa.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **New urinary incontinence** — the EAU compilation reports **15/161 (9.3%) new UI across vaginal-flap variants**, not a Blandy-specific SUI estimate. Hajebrahimi reported two SUI cases among 14 women, one transient and one treated later; counsel according to baseline continence and planned reconstruction.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

### Comparison with alternative female-urethral-stricture techniques

These are **descriptive options, not a ranking**. The EAU estimates combine different series, patient selection and follow-up; the small named series cannot establish one flap's superiority.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

| Technique | Reported setting | Practical feature | Tradeoff | Reported outcome (not head-to-head) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Blandy (ventral vaginal flap)** | Selected female strictures with suitable vaginal tissue | Local vascularized tissue; no oral graft harvest | Possible retrusive meatus; tissue quality/reach matter | All vaginal-flap variants: **81.4% patency in 161 women** (EAU); not a Blandy-only rate |
| **Lateral vaginal-wall flap** | Selected female strictures | Attempts to preserve meatal position | Pedicle dissection; limited comparative data | **9/9** without recurrence in Romero-Maroto's single series |
| **Dorsal oral mucosal graft** | Selected patients needing a separate tissue source | Avoids diseased vaginal donor tissue | Oral donor-site morbidity | **83%** across 319 dorsal oral-graft cases (EAU) |
| **Ventral oral mucosal graft** | Selected anatomy favoring ventral access | Graft option with or without interposition | Donor site and repair-dependent support | **90.7%** across 247 ventral oral-graft cases (EAU); not specifically Martius-supported |
| **Meatoplasty** | **Short isolated meatal stricture** | Local repair without long-segment reconstruction | Does not address proximal disease | EAU cites **48/50 girls** at 12 months; not an adult Blandy comparison |
| **V-Y vaginal-wall advancement after vulvectomy** | Post-oncologic meatal reconstruction | Avoids a circular inset | Distinct disease and reconstructive setting | **One stenosis and one dehiscence/occlusion in 42 repairs** |

## Male Blandy Perineal Urethrostomy

The male Blandy **[perineal urethrostomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/blandy-perineal-urethrostomy)** uses a posteriorly based inverted-U **perineal skin** flap advanced to a ventrally opened, **laid-open rather than transected** bulbar urethra to form a permanent perineal stoma. It is an option for selected complex anterior strictures, not a guarantee of low restenosis; the dedicated page contains the operative sequence and source-specific outcomes. The laterally based **scrotal-skin 7-flap** is a different operation.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

The flap's reach and local skin quality matter; **perineal skin is not inherently hairless**. Prior perineal surgery, altered tissue or radiation may complicate stoma construction. This eponym should not be extended to an anterior urethral onlay/inlay graft or conflated with penile-skin Orandi reconstruction.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

## Key Takeaways

1. The **female Blandy** is an established ventral vaginal-tissue flap for selected female urethral strictures; the EAU's **81.4% patency** combines 161 women across several **vaginal-flap variants**, not Blandy alone.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
2. A **retrusive meatus or altered stream** is a recognized concern; the lateral alternative has promising but small uncontrolled data, not proven superiority.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
3. **Initial dilation is an option**, but recurrent stricture may warrant urethroplasty; technique choice depends on anatomy, tissue quality, patient preference and surgical expertise. Ongoing self-dilation patency is not cure.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
4. A **short isolated meatal stricture** may be treated by meatoplasty; no single flap or graft is the universal first choice.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
5. The **male Blandy** perineal urethrostomy advances **posteriorly based perineal skin** to an opened bulbar urethra, distinct from the laterally based scrotal 7-flap.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
