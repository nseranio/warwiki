# Medial Circumflex Femoral Artery Perforator (MCFAP) Flap

The **MCFAP flap** is a perforator-based **muscle-sparing** flap of the superior medial thigh, vascularized by **musculocutaneous perforators arising from the gracilis pedicle of the medial circumflex femoral artery (MCFA)**. First described by **Hallock (2003)** as a local pedicled and free flap, it preserves the gracilis muscle in situ — perforators are dissected free as island branches.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Its signature niche is **scrotal reconstruction after Fournier's gangrene**, where Coskunfirat's series argued for its **superiority** based on thinness, mobility, muscle preservation, and primary donor closure in a single procedure.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

For the broader thigh-flap family see [Gracilis](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/gracilis) (musculocutaneous variant TMG/TUG), [Medial Thigh](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/medial-thigh), and [PMTP Propeller](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/pmtp-propeller); for the genital-context summary see [Scrotal Reconstruction Techniques](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/scrotal-reconstruction).

## Nomenclature

| Name | Emphasis |
|---|---|
| **MCFAP** (Hallock 2003 / 2006) | Formal perforator-flap nomenclature<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Gracilis perforator flap** | Anatomical relationship to the gracilis muscle<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Upper medial thigh perforator flap** | Freestyle suprafascial approach (Eom & Hong 2011)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **TMG / TUG flap** | Musculocutaneous variant — includes the gracilis muscle<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |

The MCFAP is a **true perforator flap** — only the musculocutaneous perforators from the gracilis pedicle are retained; **the gracilis muscle is completely preserved**, distinguishing it from the TMG / TUG.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Vascular Anatomy

### Source — Medial Circumflex Femoral Artery (MCFA)

- Origin: from the **deep femoral artery in 64.6%** and the **common femoral artery in 32.2%** (meta-analysis of 4,351 limbs).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- MCFA + LFCA both originate from the DFA in 50%; the MCFA arises directly from the CFA in 31%.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- Mean distance from inguinal ligament to MCFA origin: **41.7 mm**.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- Courses posteromedially deep to adductor longus and pectineus, supplying adductors, gracilis, and femoral head.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

### Gracilis pedicle — the perforator source

- Principal pedicle enters gracilis ~**8–10 cm below the pubic tubercle** (mean 10 ± 1 cm from the ischiopubic attachment).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- Principal pedicle artery caliber: **2.5 ± 0.5 mm** (CTA); **1.6 mm** (cadaveric dominant).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- Dominant pedicle from DFA in 87% / MCFA in 13% (Magden); DFA 45% / "artery to the adductors" 46% (Macchi).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- Mean pedicle length: **5.7 cm** (clinical MCFAP breast series, range 3–9); **6 cm** (Eom/Hong freestyle); **6.7 ± 1.0 cm** (TMG angiosome study).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- 1–4 distal accessory pedicles from the SFA (mean caliber 2.0 mm) also supply gracilis.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

### Perforator characteristics (Peek 2009 cadaveric — 43 specimens)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- **Musculocutaneous** perforators present in **100%**; ≥ 0.5 mm in 93%.
- **Septocutaneous** perforators present in **84%**; ≥ 0.5 mm in 63%.
- Most musculocutaneous perforators are concentrated in the **anterior quarter of the muscle** at the pedicle entry.
- A **constant intramuscular anastomosis** between the main and second gracilis pedicles allows an **extended perforator flap up to 27 cm**.

Eom 2011 (40 clinical cases) — **90% of perforators originated from the MCFA**; two-thirds septocutaneous, one-third musculocutaneous; mean artery diameter 0.8 mm; mean pedicle 6 cm.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### Cutaneous territory

MCFAP / TMG angiosome **74.1 ± 32.1 cm²** (Zaussinger head-to-head) — smaller than the PAP angiosome (98.5 cm²) but comparable to the infragluteal flap (77.2 cm²).<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> The gracilis perforator flap can be harvested up to **18 × 15 cm**, extended to **27 cm** with the intramuscular anastomosis.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Flap Design and Surgical Technique

### A. Hallock true MCFAP flap

**Preoperative.** Handheld Doppler maps perforator(s) ~8–10 cm below the pubic tubercle at the anterior border of gracilis; skin paddle designed transversely over the upper medial thigh centered on the perforator.

**Elevation.**

1. Incise the posterior border; dissect posterior → anterior in the **suprafascial or subfascial plane**.
2. Identify musculocutaneous perforator(s) emerging through gracilis.
3. **Intramuscular dissection** — split muscle fibers in line, preserve the gracilis intact.
4. **Independently dissect** the perforator from the gracilis muscular branches — the key muscle-preservation innovation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
5. Trace the pedicle to the MCFA for maximum length.

**Conjoint flap option.** The gracilis muscle can be separately included on its own branch — wraps and protects exposed vascular structures (e.g., infected femoral graft) while the cutaneous component closes the wound. Muscle and skin are independently mobilized on respective branches from the same source pedicle.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Donor closure.** Primary in all cases; scar concealed within the medial groin crease.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### B. Upper-medial-thigh freestyle (Eom & Hong)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- Transverse skin paddle over the upper medial thigh.
- Elevated **entirely suprafascial** — no deep fascial incision.
- Any reliable pulsating perforator encountered is preserved as the pedicle.
- Flap can be **thinned** to the recipient bed.
- Superficial nerves and veins can be included for sensate / supercharged reconstruction.
- Mean flap size 71.6 cm² (6.4 × 10.9 cm); 39/40 flaps survived (97.5%).

### C. Free gracilis perforator flap (Peek)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- Flap dimensions up to **18 × 15 cm**; **27 cm** with the intramuscular anastomosis.
- "Pliable and thin" — ideal for resurfacing.
- Gracilis fully preserved; 14/14 clinical flaps survived.

## Clinical Applications

### 1. Scrotal reconstruction after Fournier's gangrene — signature application

**Hallock 2006** — first case report of MCFAP for neoscrotum creation; muscle-sparing, functional / aesthetic scrotal coverage.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

**Coskunfirat 2011** — the largest dedicated series, n = 7 post-Fournier's:<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Perforators per flap | 1 (6 flaps); 2 (1 flap) |
| Transfer | Transposition or V-Y advancement |
| Donor closure | Primary in all |
| Major complications | **0** |
| Minor complications | 2 wound dehiscences (healed conservatively) |
| Scrotal contour | Acceptable in all |

Authors argued for **MCFAP superiority** in scrotal reconstruction on the basis of mobility, thinness, muscle preservation, and primary donor closure — all in a single procedure. Across Alammar 2026 (619 patients, 625 flaps for Fournier's), **medial-thigh-based flaps** were among the most commonly utilized, with overall flap loss only **1.6%**.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

### 2. Groin wound reconstruction (local pedicled flap)

Hallock 2003 — 4 consecutive groin wounds (vascular intervention bed) with uncomplicated healing; arc of rotation extends throughout the groin and potentially to the lateral thigh; conjoint variant wraps muscle around exposed vessels.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Arvanitakis 2019 — 54 perforator-flap groin reconstructions (ALT, PMT, vDIEP), 100% flap survival; pedicled PMT perforator flap (sharing MCFA territory) recommended as **first choice** when PFA perforators are available.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

### 3. Partial breast reconstruction (free flap)

Izumi 2013 — 15 patients with immediate free MCFAP for partial breast after BCS:<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| True perforator flaps | 3 (20%) |
| Muscle-sparing perforator flaps | 12 (80%) |
| Mean flap weight | 138.5 g (77–230 g) |
| Mean pedicle length | 5.7 cm (3.0–9.0) |
| Recipient vessels | Serratus (10), IMA (3), thoracodorsal (2) |
| Major complications | 0 |
| Partial necrosis | 2 (small firm lesions) |
| Donor seroma / lymphedema | 0 |

Suited for **partial breast** given modest volume (138.5 g); insufficient for total breast reconstruction in most patients without stacking or augmentation.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

### 4. Lower-extremity reconstruction (free flap)

Eom 2011 — upper-medial-thigh perforator flap (90% MCFA-based) for lower-extremity soft-tissue defects, n = 40, 39/40 survived (97.5%); thinned to bed; concealed donor scars closed primarily.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### 5. Perineal and vulvoperineal reconstruction

The MCFA territory underwrites the **gracilis myocutaneous flap** — the workhorse thigh-based flap for perineal reconstruction. The MCFAP perforator-only version preserves muscle for functional or staged future use.

- Singh 2016 — gracilis perineal n = 40; recipient complications 40%, donor 12.5%; obesity OR 7.5, smoking OR 9.3.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- Jenkins 2023 — bilateral pedicled gracilis muscle ELAPE n = 50; 86% complete wound healing, 14% donor complications, 22% recipient.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- Kiiski 2019 — TMG flap pelvic exenteration n = 25; complications 65.4% with flap vs 84.6% without (p = 0.191); TMG favored because it does not interfere with stoma or weaken the abdominal wall.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

### 6. Head and neck — chimeric PAP-gracilis (SIMPLE)

Yao — **SIMPLE** (Simultaneous Innervated Muscle and PAP from one Lower Extremity) chimeric harvest from the medial thigh donor for radical-parotidectomy defects needing both soft-tissue coverage and facial reanimation.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

## TMG / TUG — Musculocutaneous Counterpart

The musculocutaneous TMG variant includes the gracilis muscle and is the most widely studied member of this vascular family.

**Weitgasser 2021** (largest series, 300 TMG free flaps, breast reconstruction, 30 yr):<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

| Outcome | Rate |
|---|---|
| Mean flap weight | 320 g |
| Mean pedicle length | 70 mm |
| Flap loss | 19/300 (6.3%) |
| Cellulitis | 10/300 (3.3%) |
| Donor wound-healing disturbance | 28/300 (9.3%) |

**Schoeller 2008** — 111 patients, 154 TMG flaps; 3 complete losses (1.9%); transient posterior-thigh sensory deficit in **49/111 (44%)**.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> **Siegwart 2021** — 38 patients (59 TMG flaps); normal-to-slightly-diminished superficial sensation in **98.4%** of thigh skin; strength / mobility unimpaired in &gt; 80%; high PROMs.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

## MCFAP vs TMG vs PAP

| Parameter | MCFAP (true perforator) | TMG / TUG (musculocutaneous) | PAP (profunda perforator) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Source | MCFA → gracilis perforators | MCFA → gracilis pedicle | Profunda femoris → adductor magnus perforators |
| Gracilis | **Preserved** | Sacrificed | Preserved |
| Mean pedicle length | 5.7–6.0 cm | 6.7–7.0 cm | 8.8–10.3 cm |
| Mean pedicle diameter (artery) | 0.8 mm | 2.9 mm external | 3.6 mm external |
| Angiosome area | ~74 cm² | 74.1 ± 32.1 cm² | 98.5 ± 26.7 cm² |
| Mean flap weight (breast) | 138.5 g | 295–320 g | 242 g |
| Flap survival | 97.5–100% | 93.7–98.1% | 97–100% |
| Vascular complications | Low | 5.0% | **0.6%** (p &lt; 0.01 vs TUG) |
| Donor scar | Medial groin crease | Medial groin crease | Posterior medial thigh / gluteal crease |
| Sensory deficit | Minimal | 44% transient posterior-thigh | Minimal |
| Primary indication | Scrotal / groin reconstruction; partial breast | Breast reconstruction; perineal | Breast reconstruction; perineal |

<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)[[24]](#ref24)[[25]](#ref25)[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

**Key comparative findings.** PAP has significantly fewer vascular complications than TUG (0.6% vs 5.0%, p &lt; 0.01), a longer pedicle, and a larger angiosome — making it more versatile for free breast reconstruction.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[25]](#ref25)[[26]](#ref26)</sup> The **true MCFAP** has the shortest pedicle (5.7–6.0 cm) and smallest vessel (0.8 mm), limiting its free-flap utility but making it ideal as a **local pedicled flap** for groin and scrotal reconstruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> When PAP perforators are insufficient intraoperatively, the **gracilis perforator** is a reliable **"escape" alternative** with a perfusion area of **182 ± 42 cm²** vs PAP 204 ± 90 cm².<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

## Combined and Chimeric Configurations

- **TUGPAP (Ciudad 2019).** TUG + PAP from the same medial-thigh donor on their respective pedicles — muscle for dead-space obliteration / functional reconstruction + fasciocutaneous PAP for external resurfacing; skin paddles up to 30 × 8 cm.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>
- **Pedicled PAP + bilateral gracilis (Sharp 2021).** Vaginal and pelvic-floor reconstruction after ELAPE; 6 patients (18 flaps), all flaps survived except 1 cm PAP loss; median time to heal 4 mo.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup>
- **Bilobed gracilis (Weinstein 2020).** Second soft-tissue arm bilobed design for moderate-large perineal defects; n = 6 (5 oncologic, 1 Fournier's).<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup>

## Advantages

1. **Complete gracilis preservation** — leaves the muscle available for future functional transfer / perineal reconstruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
2. **Consistent pedicle location** — gracilis pedicle is familiar to plastic surgeons.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
3. **Thin, pliable** — matches scrotal contour and resurfacing needs.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
4. **Concealed donor scar** in the medial groin crease.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
5. **Primary donor closure** virtually always.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
6. **Minimal donor morbidity** — no seroma / lymphedema in the MCFAP series.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
7. **Conjoint flap capability** — muscle independently included for vascular protection.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
8. **Versatile** — local pedicled (groin / scrotum / perineum) or free flap (breast / extremity).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

---

## Limitations

1. **Short pedicle** (5.7–6.0 cm) — shortest among medial-thigh flaps; may need vein grafts as a free flap.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
2. **Small artery diameter** (0.8 mm) — technically demanding anastomosis.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
3. **Limited volume** (138.5 g MCFAP / 320 g TMG) — insufficient for total breast reconstruction without stacking.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[15]](#ref15)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
4. **Variable perforator anatomy** — septocutaneous ≥ 0.5 mm in only 63%.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
5. **Intramuscular dissection** required when only musculocutaneous perforators are available — added operative time / technical demand.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
6. **Limited MCFAP-specific evidence** — most large series report the musculocutaneous TMG / TUG; the true perforator flap remains a small-series / case-report literature.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
