# Medial Circumflex Femoral Artery Perforator (MCFAP) Flap

The **MCFAP flap** is a perforator-based **muscle-sparing** alternative to the [gracilis](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/gracilis) myocutaneous flap. Vascularized by **musculocutaneous perforators of the medial circumflex femoral artery** that traverse the gracilis muscle, the MCFAP captures the same skin territory as a gracilis myocutaneous flap but **preserves the muscle in situ** — perforators are dissected free of the muscle as island branches. Particularly useful when a thin, pliable skin paddle is preferred over bulky muscle, e.g., for scrotal contour reconstruction after Fournier's gangrene.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Anatomy

- **Pedicle:** musculocutaneous perforator(s) from the medial circumflex femoral artery, typically a **single dominant perforator** (occasionally two) entering the gracilis ~8–10 cm distal to the pubic tubercle.
- **Composition:** skin + subcutaneous fat over the medial thigh / groin axis; gracilis muscle is preserved.
- **Arc of rotation:** extends throughout the groin region and potentially to the lateral thigh as a transposition or V-Y advancement.

---

## Indications and Outcomes

- Moderate scrotal defects after Fournier's gangrene where muscle bulk is not required.
- Coskunfirat 2011 (n = 7) — minor wound dehiscence in 2/7 (healed conservatively); no flap losses; advocate for MCFAP as preferred over gracilis when bulk is unnecessary.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Hallock 2006 / 2003 — original description and clinical application for scrotal and groin defects.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Advantages vs Gracilis Myocutaneous

- Muscle preservation (avoids functional and donor-site morbidity).
- Thinner skin paddle for scrotal contour.
- Donor scar concealed in the medial groin.
- Single-stage; primary donor closure.
