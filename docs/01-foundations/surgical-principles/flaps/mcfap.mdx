# Medial Circumflex Femoral Artery Perforator (MCFAP) Flap

The **MCFAP/gracilis-perforator flap** is a muscle-sparing superior-medial-thigh design on perforators associated with the gracilis vascular pedicle. Although named for the medial circumflex femoral artery (MCFA), pedicle origin and perforator course vary, and some upper-medial-thigh designs use septocutaneous rather than musculocutaneous perforators. Hallock described local and free applications in 2003; the gracilis can remain functional when its muscle branches are preserved.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Coskunfirat's seven-patient post-Fournier series proposed advantages for scrotal coverage, but its title's “superiority” was not established by a comparative trial.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

For the broader thigh-flap family see [Gracilis](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/gracilis) (musculocutaneous variant TMG/TUG), [Medial Thigh](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/medial-thigh), and [PMTP Propeller](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/pmtp-propeller); for the genital-context summary see [Scrotal Reconstruction Techniques](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/scrotal-reconstruction).

## Nomenclature

| Name | Emphasis |
|---|---|
| **MCFAP** (Hallock 2003 / 2006) | Formal perforator-flap nomenclature<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Gracilis perforator flap** | Anatomical relationship to the gracilis muscle<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Upper medial thigh perforator flap** | Freestyle suprafascial approach (Eom & Hong 2011)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **TMG / TUG flap** | Musculocutaneous variant that includes the gracilis muscle<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |

The true perforator variant preserves the functioning gracilis and differs from muscle-bearing TMG/TUG; septocutaneous free-style designs and Izumi's muscle-sparing (not all true-perforator) cases need separate labels.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

## Vascular Anatomy

### Source — Medial Circumflex Femoral Artery (MCFA)

- Origin: from the deep femoral artery in 64.6% and the common femoral artery in 32.2% (meta-analysis of 4,351 limbs).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- MCFA + LFCA both originate from the DFA in 50%; the MCFA arises directly from the CFA in 31%.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- Mean distance from inguinal ligament to MCFA origin: 41.7 mm.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- Courses posteromedially deep to adductor longus and pectineus, supplying adductors, gracilis, and femoral head.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

### Gracilis pedicle — the perforator source

- Principal pedicle enters gracilis about 8–10 cm below the pubic tubercle (mean 10 ± 1 cm from the ischiopubic attachment).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- Principal pedicle artery caliber: 2.5 ± 0.5 mm (CTA); 1.6 mm (cadaveric dominant).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- The gracilis pedicle's parent artery varies in the cited anatomical samples (Magden: DFA 87% / MCFA 13%; Macchi: DFA 45% / “artery to the adductors” 46%). This does not describe the separate MCFA's own origin (DFA 64.6% / common femoral 32.2% in the meta-analysis).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- Mean pedicle length: 5.7 cm (clinical MCFAP breast series, range 3–9); 6 cm (Eom/Hong freestyle); 6.7 ± 1.0 cm (TMG angiosome study).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- 1–4 distal accessory pedicles from the SFA (mean caliber 2.0 mm) also supply gracilis.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

### Perforator characteristics (Peek 2009 cadaveric — 43 specimens)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- **Musculocutaneous** perforators present in 100%; ≥ 0.5 mm in 93%.
- **Septocutaneous** perforators present in 84%; ≥ 0.5 mm in 63%.
- Most musculocutaneous perforators are concentrated in the anterior quarter of the muscle at the pedicle entry.
- A constant intramuscular anastomosis between the main and second gracilis pedicles allows an extended perforator flap up to 27 cm.

Eom 2011 (40 clinical cases): 90% of perforators originated from the MCFA; two-thirds were septocutaneous and one-third musculocutaneous; mean artery diameter 0.8 mm; mean pedicle 6 cm.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### Cutaneous territory

The TMG angiosome measured 74.1 ± 32.1 cm² in Zaussinger's head-to-head cadaver study (13 specimens), smaller than the PAP angiosome (98.5 cm²) and comparable to the infragluteal flap (77.2 cm²); this is a TMG measurement and was not made for a perforator-only MCFAP flap.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> The gracilis perforator flap can be harvested up to 18 × 15 cm, extended to 27 cm with the intramuscular anastomosis.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Flap Design and Surgical Technique

### A. Hallock true MCFAP flap

Preoperative. Handheld Doppler maps perforator(s) about 8–10 cm below the pubic tubercle at the anterior border of gracilis; skin paddle designed transversely over the upper medial thigh centered on the perforator.

**Elevation.**

1. Incise the posterior border; dissect from posterior to anterior in the suprafascial or subfascial plane.
2. Identify musculocutaneous perforator(s) emerging through gracilis.
3. **Intramuscular dissection.** Split muscle fibers in line, preserve the gracilis intact.
4. **Independently dissect** the perforator from the gracilis muscular branches. This independent dissection is what preserves the muscle.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
5. Trace the pedicle to the MCFA for maximum length.

**Conjoint flap option.** The gracilis muscle can be separately included on its own branch. It wraps and protects exposed vascular structures (for example an infected femoral graft) while the cutaneous component closes the wound. Muscle and skin are independently mobilized on respective branches from the same source pedicle.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Donor closure.** Primary in all cases; scar concealed within the medial groin crease.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### B. Upper-medial-thigh freestyle (Eom & Hong)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- Transverse skin paddle over the upper medial thigh.
- Elevated entirely suprafascial, with no deep fascial incision.
- Any reliable pulsating perforator encountered is preserved as the pedicle.
- Flap can be thinned to the recipient bed.
- Superficial nerves and veins can be included for sensate / supercharged reconstruction.
- Mean flap size 71.6 cm² (6.4 × 10.9 cm); 39/40 flaps survived (97.5%).

### C. Free gracilis perforator flap (Peek)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- Flap dimensions up to 18 × 15 cm; 27 cm with the intramuscular anastomosis.
- "Pliable and thin," suited to resurfacing.
- Gracilis fully preserved; the authors report successful clinical application in 14 cases.

## Clinical Applications

### 1. Scrotal reconstruction after Fournier's gangrene — signature application

Hallock 2006 described a case report of MCFAP neoscrotum creation, with muscle-sparing functional and aesthetic scrotal coverage.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Coskunfirat 2011 reported a small dedicated series of 7 post-Fournier's patients:<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Perforators per flap | 1 (6 flaps); 2 (1 flap) |
| Transfer | Transposition or V-Y advancement |
| Donor closure | Primary in all |
| Major complications | **0** |
| Minor complications | 2 wound dehiscences (healed conservatively) |
| Scrotal contour | Acceptable in all |

The authors' preference reflects seven selected cases, not a head-to-head superiority estimate. The [Alammar 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41720016/) review's 1.6% loss is pooled across multiple Fournier flap types, not an MCFAP-specific outcome.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

### 2. Groin wound reconstruction (local pedicled flap)

Hallock 2003 described four groin wounds with uncomplicated healing; a conjoint variant can use a separate muscle branch to protect exposed vessels.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Arvanitakis 2019 studied 54 other groin perforator reconstructions (ALT, posteromedial thigh, vDIEP); its preferred profunda-based PMT option is not a direct MCFAP outcome or MCFA-specific recommendation.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

### 3. Partial breast reconstruction (free flap)

Izumi 2013 reported 15 patients with immediate free MCFAP flaps for partial breast reconstruction after breast-conserving surgery:<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| True perforator flaps | 3 (20%); only this subset meets a strict perforator-only definition |
| Muscle-sparing flaps | 12 (80%); not automatically identical to the true-perforator design |
| Mean flap weight | 138.5 g (77–230 g) |
| Mean pedicle length | 5.7 cm (3.0–9.0) |
| Recipient vessels | Serratus (10), IMA (3), thoracodorsal (2) |
| Major complications | 0 |
| Partial necrosis | 2 (small firm lesions) |
| Donor seroma / lymphedema | 0 |

Suited for partial breast given modest volume (138.5 g); insufficient for total breast reconstruction in most patients without stacking or augmentation.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

### 4. Lower-extremity reconstruction (free flap)

Eom 2011 used the upper-medial-thigh perforator flap (90% MCFA-based) for lower-extremity soft-tissue defects in 40 patients; 39/40 survived (97.5%). Flaps were thinned to the bed, and concealed donor scars were closed primarily.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### 5. Perineal and vulvoperineal reconstruction

The MCFA territory underwrites the gracilis myocutaneous flap, a widely used thigh-based flap for perineal reconstruction. The MCFAP perforator-only version preserves muscle for functional or staged future use.

- Singh 2016 used a muscle-bearing gracilis (not MCFAP) in 40 perineal patients; these complication estimates cannot be assigned to the perforator-only flap.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- Jenkins 2023 reported bilateral pedicled gracilis muscle flaps after ELAPE in 50 patients: 86% complete wound healing, 14% donor-site and 22% recipient-site complications.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- Kiiski 2019 reported TMG flap reconstruction after pelvic exenteration (n = 25): complications 65.4% with flap versus 84.6% without (p = 0.191). The authors favored TMG because donor-site morbidity was reasonable and it does not interfere with the enterostomy.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

### 6. Head and neck — chimeric PAP-gracilis

Yao 2023 described combined PAP-gracilis reconstruction from one thigh donor site in 10 patients after radical parotidectomy with facial nerve sacrifice (all received adjuvant radiation): a chimeric flap with one anastomosis in 3 and a double free flap in 7. There were no flap losses; 6 patients achieved dynamic animation, at a mean follow-up of 11.1 months.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

## TMG / TUG — Musculocutaneous Counterpart

The musculocutaneous TMG variant includes the gracilis muscle and is the most widely studied member of this vascular family.

**Weitgasser 2021** (retrospective two-center cohort of 300 TMG free flaps for breast reconstruction, with a 30-year literature review):<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

| Outcome | Rate |
|---|---|
| Mean flap weight | 320 g |
| Mean pedicle length | 70 mm |
| Flap loss | 19/300 (6.3%) |
| Cellulitis | 10/300 (3.3%) |
| Donor wound-healing disturbance | 28/300 (9.3%) |

Schoeller 2008 reported 111 patients with 154 TMG flaps: 3 complete losses (1.9%) and transient posterior-thigh sensory deficit in 49/111 (44%).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Siegwart 2021 studied 38 patients (59 TMG flaps): normal to slightly diminished superficial sensation in 98.4% of thigh skin, strength and mobility unimpaired in &gt; 80%, and high PROMs.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

## MCFAP vs TMG vs PAP

| Parameter | MCFAP (true perforator) | TMG / TUG (musculocutaneous) | PAP (profunda perforator) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Source | Variable gracilis-pedicle parent; often MCFA in selected freestyle series | Variable gracilis-pedicle parent | Profunda femoris → adductor magnus perforators |
| Gracilis | **Preserved** | Sacrificed | Preserved |
| Mean pedicle length | 5.7–6.0 cm | 6.7–7.0 cm | 8.8–10.3 cm |
| Mean pedicle diameter (artery) | 0.8 mm | 2.9 mm external | 3.6 mm external |
| Angiosome area | Not measured; mean clinical flap 71.6 cm² (Eom) | 74.1 ± 32.1 cm² | 98.5 ± 26.7 cm² |
| Mean flap weight (breast) | 138.5 g | 295–320 g | 242 g |
| Flap survival | Small, mixed-design series | Separate TMG cohorts | Separate PAP cohorts |
| Vascular complications | MCFAP-specific comparator not established | 5.0% in cited TUG/PAP comparison | 0.6% in that comparison, not directly compared with MCFAP |
| Donor scar | Medial groin crease | Medial groin crease | Posterior medial thigh / gluteal crease |
| Sensory deficit | Not systematically reported | 44% transient posterior-thigh (Schoeller) | Not compared in the cited sources |
| Primary indication | Scrotal / groin reconstruction; partial breast | Breast reconstruction; perineal | Breast reconstruction; perineal |

<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)[[24]](#ref24)[[25]](#ref25)[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

Comparison boundaries. The cited PAP-versus-TUG vascular-complication comparison (0.6% versus 5.0%) is not a comparison against true MCFAP. The 5.7-cm pedicle in Izumi's mixed true-perforator/muscle-sparing breast series and Eom's 0.8-mm artery in a freestyle upper-medial-thigh series are not universal MCFAP specifications.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[15]](#ref15)[[25]](#ref25)[[26]](#ref26)</sup> A gracilis perforator can be considered when PAP perforators are unsuitable, with design verified in the individual vascular anatomy.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

## Combined and Chimeric Configurations

- **TUGPAP (Ciudad 2019).** TUG and PAP from the same medial-thigh donor on their respective pedicles: muscle for dead-space obliteration or functional reconstruction plus fasciocutaneous PAP for external resurfacing. In 28 patients, skin paddles were 20–30 × 6–9 cm and all flaps survived.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>
- **Pedicled PAP + bilateral gracilis (Sharp 2021).** Vaginal and pelvic-floor reconstruction after ELAPE; 6 patients (18 flaps), all flaps survived except 1 cm PAP loss; median time to heal 4 mo.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup>
- **Bilobed gracilis (Weinstein 2020).** Second soft-tissue arm bilobed design for moderate-large perineal defects; n = 6 (5 oncologic, 1 Fournier's).<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup>

## Advantages

1. **Complete gracilis preservation.** The muscle remains available for future functional transfer or perineal reconstruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
2. **Consistent pedicle location.** Gracilis pedicle is familiar to plastic surgeons.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
3. **Thin, pliable.** Matches scrotal contour and resurfacing needs.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
4. **Concealed donor scar** in the medial groin crease.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
5. **Primary donor closure** virtually always.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
6. **Potentially limited donor morbidity.** Izumi reported no seroma/lymphedema in 15 mixed-design cases, not proof of zero risk.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
7. **Conjoint flap capability.** Muscle independently included for vascular protection.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
8. **Versatile.** Local pedicled (groin, scrotum, perineum) or free flap (breast, extremity).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

---

## Limitations

1. **Short pedicle** (5.7–6.0 cm). Shorter than the TMG (6.7 cm) and PAP (8.8 cm) pedicles in Zaussinger's study; may need vein grafts as a free flap.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
2. **Small artery diameter** (0.8 mm). Technically demanding anastomosis.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
3. **Limited volume** (138.5 g MCFAP; 320 g TMG). The volume is insufficient for total breast reconstruction without stacking.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[15]](#ref15)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
4. **Variable perforator anatomy.** Septocutaneous ≥ 0.5 mm in only 63%.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
5. **Intramuscular dissection** is required when only musculocutaneous perforators are available, adding operative time and technical demand.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
6. **Limited MCFAP-specific evidence.** Most large series report the musculocutaneous TMG / TUG; the true perforator flap remains a small-series and case-report literature.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
