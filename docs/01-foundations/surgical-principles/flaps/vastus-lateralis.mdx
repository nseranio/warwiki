# Vastus Lateralis (VL) Flap

The vastus lateralis (VL) muscle can be used in pedicled, free, and combined ALT–VL flap designs. In a 30-specimen cadaver study, its dominant pedicle was the descending branch of the lateral circumflex femoral artery; the study also described minor pedicles and femoral-nerve entry.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Reported reconstructive uses

A 2015 retrospective series reported 22 composite ALT–VL reconstructions after excluding three people without six-month follow-up. The included defects were larger than 100 cm² and involved oncologic resections or Fournier gangrene; reported complications included one total necrosis, one partial necrosis after venous congestion, and six peripheral wound dehiscences. The authors reported satisfactory coverage at follow-up, but the uncontrolled series cannot establish outcomes for other patient groups or a preferred flap.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

An 18-patient pelvic-exenteration series reported pedicled ALT–VL reconstruction when rectus abdominis was unavailable or insufficient. The authors used inguinal and perineal delivery routes, reported one flap failure attributed to pedicle tension in an obese person with a short thigh, and described five small perineal wound dehiscences that healed spontaneously.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

A four-patient post-traumatic bladder reconstruction case series used pedicled VL myocutaneous flaps for bladder and lower-abdominal or perineal soft-tissue loss. It reported intact bladders on cystography and normal voiding in three people within one year; a small uncontrolled series does not establish general effectiveness or a standard technique.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Evidence boundary

The sources directly reviewed for this page were PubMed records and abstracts. They do not establish operative harvesting or tunneling rules, perforator or pedicle measurements for clinical planning, donor-site functional outcomes, contraindications, comparative superiority over VRAM, ALT, gracilis, or other flaps, or routine use in scrotal, penile, vulvar, vaginal, or bladder reconstruction.
