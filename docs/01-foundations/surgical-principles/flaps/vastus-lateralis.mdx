# Vastus Lateralis (VL) Flap

The **pedicled vastus lateralis (VL)** and combined **anterolateral thigh–vastus lateralis (ALT-VL)** flaps provide muscle bulk for selected pelvic, perineal and groin defects, including when an abdominal donor is unsuitable. They commonly use the **lateral circumflex femoral artery (LCFA)** system also used by the ALT flap. VL may be transferred as muscle alone, with an ALT skin paddle, or in a muscle-sparing design, depending on the defect and donor anatomy; published outcomes for ALT skin-only, ALT-VL composite and VL muscle-only flaps should not be conflated.

See the overview article: [Flaps in GU Reconstruction](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps-gu-reconstruction). Related pages: [Anterolateral Thigh (ALT) Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/anterolateral-thigh), [Gracilis Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/gracilis), [VRAM](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/vram).

## Anatomy and Vascular Supply

The vastus lateralis is the largest component of the quadriceps, originating from the greater trochanter and lateral lip of the linea aspera and inserting into the quadriceps tendon.

### Dominant Pedicle — Descending Branch of the LCFA

The same pedicle that supplies the ALT flap:<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

| Feature | Value |
|---|---|
| Artery | Descending branch of the lateral circumflex femoral artery |
| Mean arterial diameter | **2.1 mm** (range 2.0–2.5 mm) |
| Pedicle length (to LCFA origin) | Mean 56.8 mm; up to **8–20 cm** with proximal dissection |
| Pedicle entry (from pubic symphysis) | **~119 mm distal** |
| Pedicle entry (from greater trochanter) | **~156 mm** |
| Venae comitantes | Paired, 2.5–4 mm caliber |

### Accessory Pedicles

- **Proximal minor pedicles** — ascending and transverse branches of the LCFA, mean 1.8–2.0 mm diameter.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Distal minor pedicles** — three large branches from the deep femoral artery, **7.9–15.4 cm proximal to the patella** (2.2–2.8 mm), providing the anatomic basis for **distally based VL flaps**.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Segmental Anatomy

The vastus lateralis has **three distinct anatomical partitions** with constant segmental neurovascular supply, enabling **muscle-sparing or segmental harvest**.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> This is the anatomic basis for the "just-enough-muscle" technique that preserves function at the donor site.

### Motor Innervation

**Femoral nerve branch** enters the muscle approximately **195 mm from the ASIS**.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Preservation matters for donor-site quadriceps function.

## GU Reconstructive Applications

Reported VL or ALT-VL uses include the following; some related ALT-only applications are covered separately on the [ALT page](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/anterolateral-thigh).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

1. **Scrotal reconstruction** — Fournier's gangrene, scrotal trauma, scrotal oncology
2. **Perineal reconstruction** — APR, pelvic exenteration, extensive radiation wounds
3. **Bladder reconstruction** — posttraumatic bladder wall + overlying soft-tissue coverage
4. **Penile reconstruction** and penoscrotal coverage
5. **Groin defects** with exposed femoral vessels
6. **Vaginal reconstruction** after oncologic resection

### When a Muscle Component May Be Useful<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- **Deep defects** requiring dead-space obliteration
- **Contaminated wounds** benefiting from well-vascularized muscle
- **Selected traumatic bladder defects** — a four-patient case series used the muscle undersurface with soft-tissue coverage; this is not established routine bladder substitution<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Exposed vital structures** (femoral vessels, testes, urethra)
- **Large surface-area defects**, when bulk as well as surface cover is required; a &gt;100 cm² threshold was an inclusion criterion in one composite-flap series, not a general selection rule<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Published Flap-Selection Scheme for Lower Abdominal, Perineal, and Groin (LAPG) Reconstruction<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The following is **Zelken and colleagues' proposed algorithm** from a retrospective 22-patient, 5 ALT-FC/8 ALT-MC/9 VL-MC series. It illustrates how bulk and skin needs influenced their choices; it is not a comparative standard or a mandate for a specific flap.

| Defect characteristics | Preferred flap | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Superficial, small–moderate size | **ALT-FC** (fasciocutaneous) | Thin, pliable, minimal donor morbidity |
| Moderate depth, moderate size | **ALT-MC** (myocutaneous with muscle cuff) | Additional bulk, reliable perfusion |
| Deep defects, large size, dead space | **VL-MC** (vastus lateralis myocutaneous) | Maximum bulk, dead-space obliteration |
| Bladder reconstruction | **VL-MC** | Muscle undersurface as bladder-wall substitute |
| Exposed femoral vessels | **Chimeric ALT + VL** | Muscle wraps vessels, skin provides closure |
| Pelvic exenteration | **ALT-VL composite** | Fills pelvic dead space, prevents herniation |

## Bladder Reconstruction

Pedicled VL-MC has been **reported in four patients** with complex traumatic bladder tissue loss plus lower abdominal or perineal soft-tissue injury. That small case series does not establish it as a routine alternative to conventional bladder repair.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

**Principle:** the **undersurface of the vastus lateralis substitutes for the missing bladder wall**; the remaining muscle and subcutaneous fat provide bulk to obliterate dead space and cover the external defect.

**Wu 2010 series (4 patients):**<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- No major complications
- **Intact bladders on postop cystogram**
- **3 of 4 (75%) voiding normally within 1 year**

In Zelken's separate **22-patient mixed ALT-FC/ALT-MC/VL-MC series**, three urinary leaks occurred in bladder-reconstruction cases. The abstract does not give a bladder-subgroup denominator or establish a VL-specific leak rate.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Scrotal, Penile, and Perineoscrotal Reconstruction

### Fournier's Gangrene and Major Scrotal Defects

The cited perineoscrotal reports primarily concern **ALT fasciocutaneous/perforator flaps**, not necessarily a harvested VL muscle. Their outcomes inform the adjacent ALT option but cannot be presented as ALT-VL-specific results:<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

- **Flap survival:** small ALT series report high survival; no universal 100% VL result follows
- Natural scrotal contour with adequate testicular coverage
- **Sexual function:** report only within the specific ALT cohort and measure used, not as a VL-muscle effect
- Minor posterior perineal wound dehiscence in ~30% due to fecal contamination, typically heals secondarily

### Systematic Review of Fournier Reconstruction (619 Patients, 625 Flaps)<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

- **Flap loss: 1.6% pooled across multiple flap designs**, not an ALT-VL-specific rate
- Most common flaps: medial thigh, pudendal thigh, ALT, gracilis
- Flap choice depends on defect, reach and available donor tissue; this review does not prove regional transfer universally superior to free transfer
- Primary indications: coverage of exposed vital structures (52%), functional restoration (39%), cosmesis (4%)

### Penile Suspensory Ligament Reconstruction<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

A composite ALT flap including **fascia lata** can reconstruct the penile suspensory ligament after Fournier's gangrene, restoring erection orientation and sexual intercourse function.

## Pelvic Exenteration — ALT-VL as a VRAM Alternative

The ALT-VL composite is a **reported alternative to VRAM** when the rectus abdominis donor is unavailable or unsuitable, including some large pelvic-exenteration defects. Reach, vascular anatomy, muscle volume and donor morbidity still require individual assessment.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### Delivery Routes<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

- **Inguinal route** — VL tunneled over the inguinal ligament into the pelvis when the perineal defect is small enough for primary closure.
- **Perineal route** — ALT-VL tunneled over the medial thigh to the defect when perineal-vaginal reconstruction is required in the same operation.
- **Bilateral approach** — one flap via each route for very large defects.

### Wong 2009 Series (18 Patients, All With Preoperative Chemoradiation)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

- Flap failure: **1 case (5.6%)** — pedicle tension in an obese patient with short thigh
- Perineal wound dehiscence: 5 cases (28%), all healed spontaneously
- **No hernias**
- **Selection caution:** one failure from pedicle tension occurred in an obese patient with a short thigh; this case alone does not define a universal contraindication

### Comparative APR Reconstruction Evidence

| Study | n | Comparison | Key finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pang 2014**<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> | 19 (10 ALT, 9 VRAM) | ALT vs VRAM, not isolated VL | No significant differences reported in small nonrandomized groups |
| **Galbraith 2023**<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> | Retrospective mixed pelvic cohort | VRAM vs gluteal vs thigh designs (including ALT/gracilis), not isolated VL | Mixed-flap outcomes cannot establish ALT-VL equivalence |
| **Nelson 2009 (MD Anderson)**<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> | 133 (114 VRAM, 19 mixed thigh) | VRAM vs **mixed thigh flaps**, not specifically ALT-VL | Major complications **15% vs 42%**, pelvic abscess **6% vs 32%**, major wound dehiscence **5% vs 21%** in this cohort |
| **Johnstone 2017 meta-analysis**<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> | Pooled | VRAM vs gluteal vs gracilis | Overall perineal complication rate **35.8% VRAM vs 43.7% gluteal vs 52.9% gracilis** (p = 0.041) |

These studies compare **different patient and flap mixes** and do not establish ALT-VL noninferiority to VRAM. They support considering donor availability, defect volume and reach, prior treatment, and the complication profile of each option rather than transporting a pooled thigh-flap rate to VL specifically.

### Transabdominal-Pelvic-Perineal (TAPP) ALT-VL — di Summa 2016<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

A novel technique for **massive anteroposterior defects after extended APR + sacrectomy**: the ALT + VL flap is delivered transabdominally through the pelvis to the perineum.

- A six-patient technical series of extensive pelvic/perineal defects
- 1 partial flap necrosis; no late fistulas or perineal hernias
- No significant flap atrophy on serial CT volumetric analysis
- The **fascia lata** component supports the pelvic floor
- Donor-site findings were reported in this small series; they do not establish absence of functional morbidity in all patients

## Vulvar / Vaginal Reconstruction

- **Gentileschi 2017** — the authors reported ALT reconstruction, with VL muscle when needed for dead-space filling, in **15 patients/16 flaps** after vulvar cancer surgery; no flap necrosis and three partial wound dehiscences were reported. Their proposed first-line use is a center-specific conclusion, not a comparative standard.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>
- **Wong & Sbitany 2020** — free ALT for posterior vaginal-wall reconstruction after APR with rectovaginal fistula in 2 female patients; 100% flap survival.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>
- The VL is also among the musculocutaneous flap options listed by Höckel and Dornhöfer for vulvovaginal reconstruction, though less commonly used than gracilis or VRAM for this indication.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>

## Phalloplasty and Urethral Reconstruction

The following are **ALT phalloplasty** reports, not evidence that the **VL muscle** must be incorporated or that its harvest improves phalloplasty. They are retained here to show the shared LCFA donor region; see the [ALT page](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/anterolateral-thigh) for procedure-specific interpretation:

- **D'Arpa 2019** — 93 ALT phalloplasties (largest series). Urethral reconstruction by tube-in-tube (5.8% of cases), combined with [SCIP flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/scip) (40.9%), or radial forearm flap (31.2%). Eventually **91.9% voided while standing**.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup>
- **Harris 2021** — bladder exstrophy: 5 patients underwent pedicled ALT phalloplasty; all flaps survived, but **4/4 survivors required later debulking**. RFFF preferred when feasible; ALT acceptable when forearm is overly thin.<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup>
- **Morrison 2014 "mushroom flap"** — a tube-in-tube ALT design with a semicircular extension creates a neoglans with natural anatomic landmarks, enabling single-stage neophallus + neoglans + neourethra construction without microsurgery.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup>
- **Ozkan 2009 prefabricated ALT** — pedicled ALT prefabricated with skin graft for full-thickness urethral reconstruction; durable coverage and patent tube passage.<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup>

See the [ALT page](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/anterolateral-thigh) for the full phalloplasty workflow including ALT vs RFFF head-to-head comparisons.

## Large Perineal Defects — Combined ALT + VL Flap

In di Summa's **retrospective series**, 23 patients underwent 25 ALT+VL reconstructions for large defects; three patients lacked six-month follow-up, leaving **20 patients/22 reconstructions** for outcome analysis (mean defect area 182 cm²; &gt;100 cm² was an inclusion criterion):<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- Flap necrosis: 4.5% (1/22)
- Partial necrosis: 1 case (venous congestion)
- Peripheral wound dehiscence: 27% (managed with debridement ± STSG)
- Satisfactory coverage at mean 14-month follow-up was reported among those analyzed, including after complementary procedures in two cases
- No functional deficit was reported in this small followed group; that is not proof of zero donor-site morbidity

**Risk factors for complications:** larger defect size, preoperative radiotherapy (significantly prolongs hospital stay).

## Surgical Technique

### Standard Pedicled VL-MC Harvest<sup>[[17]](#ref17)[[18]](#ref18)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

1. **Positioning** — supine, ipsilateral hip slightly elevated on a roll.
2. **Skin paddle design** — centered over the lateral thigh along the ASIS–patella axis. Typical 8 × 5 cm up to 11 × 18 cm; reports up to 38 × 20 cm for extensive harvests.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
3. **Incision along the anterior skin-paddle border**; dissect through subcutaneous fat to the fascia lata.
4. **Identify the intermuscular interval** between rectus femoris and vastus lateralis.
5. **Locate the descending branch of LCFA** and accompanying venae comitantes in the intermuscular septum.
6. **Dissect the pedicle proximally** to its origin from LCFA for maximum length (8–20 cm possible).
7. **Muscle harvest options:**
   - **Full muscle** — entire VL
   - **Segmental** — one of three anatomical partitions (muscle-sparing)
   - **Muscle-sparing with perforator cuff** — minimal muscle retained around perforators
8. **Flap transfer:**
   - **Proximal pedicle** — arc of rotation reaches lower abdomen, perineum, scrotum, groin
   - **Subcutaneous tunnel** or **passage beneath rectus femoris**
   - **Inguinal route** (over inguinal ligament) for pelvis; **perineal route** (over medial thigh) for perineum
9. **Donor-site closure** — primary closure feasible in **81–94%** of cases; STSG for wider skin paddles.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

### Chimeric ALT + VL<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

When the defect needs both bulk and skin coverage with **different orientations**:

- Two separate perforators — one **musculocutaneous**, one **pure muscular** — on the same LCFA pedicle.
- Muscle component wraps vessels or fills dead space.
- Fasciocutaneous component provides tension-free skin closure.
- Can be designed as a **sensate flap** by including the **lateral femoral cutaneous nerve**.

### Distally-Based VL Flap<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

Based on distal accessory pedicles arising from the deep femoral artery 7.9–15.4 cm proximal to the patella — used for knee coverage rather than urologic reconstruction, but worth knowing as an available variant.

### Preoperative Planning<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

- **CT angiography** maps perforator anatomy when ALT / ALT-VL reliability is in question.
- **Assess thigh length** in obese patients — short thighs increase pedicle-tension risk.
- **Bilateral harvest** for very large defects.

## Outcomes

Selected small series report high flap survival, but the cited studies mix ALT-FC, ALT-MC and VL-MC designs, defect sites and follow-up; a single **94–100% VL-specific survival range** is not established by them.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[9]](#ref9)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

### Complications<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

| Complication | Rate |
|---|---|
| Venous congestion (mechanical cause) | **3/9 VL-MC** in Zelken's 22-patient mixed-ALT series; not a pooled rate<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Wound infection | **6/22 patients** in the same mixed-ALT series; not isolated VL-MC<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Peripheral wound dehiscence | **6/22 followed reconstructions (27%)** in di Summa's ALT+VL composite series; some required debridement, grafting or advancement<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Total/partial flap necrosis | Report by **specific cohort and flap denominator** rather than combining heterogeneous series into one VL-specific percentage<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |

## Donor-Site Morbidity

Donor-site findings vary by whether the operation takes skin alone, a small VL cuff, a segment or most of the muscle. These sources should not be merged into one VL-only morbidity estimate.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[18]](#ref18)[[33]](#ref33)[[34]](#ref34)</sup>

| Parameter | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Reported musculoskeletal dysfunction** | Nearly one in 20 after mixed ALT/VL harvest in the background evidence summarized by Puladi; not the measured outcome of its simulation<sup>[[34]](#ref34)</sup> |
| **Lower Extremity Functional Scale (segmental harvest)** | **67.7 to 67.4**, nonsignificant, in Toia's **23 selected free-flap patients** (14 segmental VL, nine chimeric ALT-VL); not proof of no deficit after full VL harvest<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Modeled muscle reduction (elderly/fragile)** | Puladi's **predictive gait simulation** cautioned against **50% or greater** VL reduction; not a validated operative safety threshold<sup>[[34]](#ref34)</sup> |
| **Modeled muscle reduction (young/athletic)** | A **10% simulated reduction** had no relevant modeled effect; **more than 30%** produced relevant modeled quadriceps weakening, not a guaranteed individual cutoff<sup>[[34]](#ref34)</sup> |
| **Hanasono 2010 — 220 mixed ALT flaps** | 25% skin-only, 38% minimal muscle, 33% superficial half, 4% whole VL; donor seroma 5%, dehiscence 2%, hematoma 1%, infection 1% across **all designs**, not VL-only<sup>[[33]](#ref33)</sup> |
| **Sensory and gait effects** | Assess and counsel individually; the inherited 89% tactile-loss and strength/walking-speed figures need source-specific population and measurement verification before use as VL rates. |
| **Primary donor closure** | 81–94% |
| **Skin-graft cosmesis** | Can be poor — discuss at consent |

## Comparison With Alternatives

### ALT-VL vs VRAM for Pelvic Reconstruction<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

| Feature | ALT-VL | VRAM |
|---|---|---|
| Abdominal-wall integrity | **Preserved** | Compromised |
| Donor-site hernia risk | Preserves abdominal fascia, but thigh harvest has its own risks | Abdominal-wall hernia/bulge risk varies with technique, closure and follow-up; no universal 15–17% comparator is established here |
| Availability with ostomies or prior abdominal surgery | **Excellent** | May be limited |
| Pedicle length | 9–16 cm | Variable |
| Bulk for dead space | Excellent | Excellent |
| Donor morbidity | Thigh wound, sensory and quadriceps effects vary by harvested tissue | Abdominal-wall wound and hernia/bulge effects vary; no universal rank |

ALT-VL's principal advantage is **preserving the abdominal wall** — particularly important in patients with stomas, prior rectus harvest, or planned adjuvant therapy requiring intact abdominal fascia.

### VL vs Gracilis for Perineal Reconstruction

- **VL** — can provide more muscle bulk for a large dead space, at the cost of a larger quadriceps donor-site harvest
- **Gracilis** — often offers a smaller donor footprint and useful perineal reach, but less muscle volume
- Neither flap has universally low morbidity or proven superiority for all defects; compare actual reach, vessels, prior treatment and functional consequences
- Practical planning shorthand: **consider VL when bulk is limiting and gracilis when reach and smaller harvest are priorities**, then verify patient-specific suitability

## Key Takeaways

1. **VL and ALT-VL are reported VRAM alternatives for selected pelvic defects** when abdominal-wall preservation matters; comparative evidence is not VL-specific.
2. **LCFA descending branch** — the same pedicle as the ALT flap; diameter ~2.1 mm, length up to 20 cm with proximal dissection.
3. **Three anatomical partitions** permit segmental harvest; Toia's selected 23-patient cohort had no significant mean LEFS change, which does not establish zero donor morbidity after larger harvests.
4. **VL-MC supplies bulk** for selected deep defects and dead-space filling; the &gt;100 cm² threshold was one series' enrollment rule, not a universal indication.
5. **Bladder reconstruction** — a four-case traumatic series used muscle undersurface for bladder tissue loss; three patients voided normally by one year. This is preliminary case experience.
6. **Fournier / scrotal reconstruction** — ALT-only and mixed-flap reports should not be quoted as VL-specific outcomes; the 1.6% pooled loss spans multiple designs.
7. **Pelvic exenteration** — Wong's 18-patient ALT-VL series described inguinal and perineal routes; one pedicle-tension failure in an obese short-thigh patient warrants individualized reach assessment, not a universal exclusion.
8. **Chimeric ALT + VL** enables simultaneous skin + muscle coverage with different orientations on one LCFA pedicle.
9. **VL vs gracilis** — bulk and reach are useful planning considerations, not an evidence-based universal ranking.
10. **Outcome and donor-site estimates depend on the exact flap and cohort**; pedicle tension is one reported failure mechanism, alongside wound and perfusion complications.
