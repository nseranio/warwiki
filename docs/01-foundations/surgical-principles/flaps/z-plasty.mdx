# Z-Plasty

Z-plasty is a local tissue-rearrangement design in which two triangular flaps are transposed across a central limb. The result depends on the selected design, tissue characteristics, scar pattern, and operative context. This page records selected reported uses; it is not a universal geometry rule, operative protocol, or procedure-selection guide.

## Reported uses in reconstructive literature

### Pediatric genital reconstruction

A 2025 retrospective series described 100 Z-plasty procedures for pediatric penile and scrotal anomalies, including primary and revision hypospadias repairs, chordee correction, and penoscrotal or scrotal reconstruction. The abstract reports primary flap healing in 98 procedures and scheduled follow-up completion in 97%; its retrospective design and short reported follow-up range limit broader outcome inference.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Adult prepuceplasty

One comparative study analyzed consecutive adult standard prepuceplasty and foreskin Z-plasty procedures (22 and 12 procedures, respectively). The PubMed abstract supports that this was a comparison of clinical and patient-reported outcomes; its indexed record alone does not support an independent technique recommendation or a general estimate of comparative benefit.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Transverse vaginal septum

A 25-year single-center report described Garcia Z-plasty with a Grünberger modification in 13 patients with obstructed or nonobstructed transverse vaginal septa. The abstract reports a functioning vagina with at least 4-cm width at the former septum site and no postoperative contractures in that small series.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> This result should not be generalized to other anatomy, etiologies, or reconstruction plans without source- and patient-specific review.

### Posterior fourchette reconstruction

A publication describes double opposing Z-plasty with V-Y advancement of the perineum for posterior vaginal fourchette narrowing or splitting. The accessible indexed record identifies the technique and study publication but does not provide enough detail here to establish comparative effectiveness or a treatment pathway.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Evidence boundary

The inherited page included angle-specific lengthening figures, technique steps, comparative algorithms, outcomes across multiple genital and gynecologic conditions, and recommendations. Those claims require their own full-source and context-specific review and are not retained here. In particular, this page does not establish a universal length gain, an angle-selection rule, superiority over Y-V or V-Y designs, or an indication for penile, vaginal, perineal, or oncologic reconstruction.
