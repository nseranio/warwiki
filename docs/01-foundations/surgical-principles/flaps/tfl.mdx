# Tensor Fasciae Latae (TFL) Flap

The tensor fasciae latae (TFL) flap can transfer muscle, fascia lata and overlying lateral-thigh skin together, or be designed as a fascia and skin perforator variant. Its principal pedicle is the ascending branch of the lateral circumflex femoral artery (LCFA). The 1978 descriptions established the musculocutaneous design. Höckel and Dornhöfer later included it in their proposed vulvovaginal-reconstruction scheme, favoring it for selected defects extending into the groin; that is an author's algorithm, not a universal first-choice rule.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For the broader GU-reconstruction flap menu see [Flaps for GU Reconstruction](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps-gu-reconstruction). For related lateral-thigh flap pages see [Anterolateral Thigh](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/anterolateral-thigh) (LCFA descending branch) and [Vastus Lateralis](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/vastus-lateralis); for the perforator family see [SCIP / perforator flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/scip).

## Muscle Anatomy

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Origin | Outer lip of the iliac crest between the ASIS and the iliac tubercle; encapsulated within fascia lata, no distal bony attachment<sup>[[17]](#ref17)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| Insertion | Into the **iliotibial band**, which inserts distally on **Gerdy's tubercle** of the lateral tibial condyle<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> |
| Volume | ~1.8 ± 0.8 cm³ (small fusiform muscle vs gluteus medius 27.6 cm³, gluteus minimus 14.1 cm³)<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> |
| Length | ~15 cm (ASIS to musculotendinous junction) |
| Width | ~5–7 cm at widest |
| Innervation | Superior gluteal nerve (L4-S1) — homogeneous (no differential compartments)<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> |

### Function

- Hip flexion (anteromedial fibers).<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- Hip abduction: in one experimentally induced weakness study, force fell by about 15% at 30° hip flexion, without a detected effect in neutral or extension. This does not measure outcomes after flap harvest.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- Hip internal rotation (posterolateral fibers).
- Knee stabilization via tension on the iliotibial band.
- **Primary functional role**: balancing the body weight and non-weight-bearing leg during walking.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

Donor-site function depends on the amount of muscle or fascia removed, nerve preservation, baseline gait and rehabilitation; the induced-weakness result alone cannot establish minimal postoperative deficit.

## Vascular Anatomy

### LCFA branching patterns (Palackic 2021, 102 lower limbs)<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

The LCFA arises from the deep femoral (profunda) artery in most cases (occasionally directly from the femoral artery) and shows highly variable branching, with 11 trunk variations identified:

| Group | Branches | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| A | 3 branches | **49%** |
| B | 4 branches | 40% |
| C | 5 branches | 5% |
| D | 2 branches | 6% |

Branches are typically: ascending (supplies TFL), transverse, oblique, and descending (supplies ALT territory).

### DSA-based morphology (Yang 2023, n = 113)<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

LCFA usually routinely sends four main branches (ascending, transverse, oblique, descending). The ascending branch courses approximately 45° upward and outward from the horizontal axis.

### Accessory vascularization (Gandolfi 2024, cadaveric injection study)<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

The TFL has anastomotic perforasome connections with:

- **Superficial circumflex iliac artery (SCIA).** Six arteries were injected as part of the anatomy study.
- **Inferior gluteal artery (IGA).** Three arteries were injected as part of the anatomy study.

These cadaveric connections and one reported clinical reconstruction led the authors to hypothesize alternative TFL perfusion after LCFA interruption. They do not establish that a flap will survive an injured dominant pedicle in an individual patient; confirm actual inflow before relying on a collateral pathway.

### Perforator anatomy — Hubmer 2009 (definitive cadaveric study, n = 45 thighs)<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

| Parameter | Musculocutaneous | Septocutaneous |
|---|---|---|
| Average number / TFL | 2.3 (range 0–5) | **1.8 (range 1–3)** |
| Distance from ASIS | 10.9 cm (4.5–16.1) | **10.9 cm (6.2–15.7)** |
| Diameter | 0.9 mm (0.2–2) | **1.5 mm (0.5–3)** |
| Consistency | Absent in 4/45 (9%) | **Present in all** |
| Concentration | Variable | **76% between 8–12 cm from ASIS** |
| Pedicle length | — | **8.1 cm (6.5–10)** |

Septocutaneous perforators are more constant, larger, and more reliably located than the musculocutaneous perforators. All perforators arise from the ascending branch of the LCFA.

### Skin territory (Hubmer 2009 injection)<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

- Ascending-branch injection: 19.4 × 13.4 cm (range 10–24 × 7–17 cm).
- Selective septocutaneous-perforator injection: 19.2 × 13.7 cm, a similar stained area, indicating that septocutaneous perforators alone can perfuse the skin territory in cadavers.

### CTA-based anatomy — Powers 2018 (n = 59 thighs)<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

- Average 2.5 perforators per TFL (all from the ascending branch).
- **66% septocutaneous, 34% musculocutaneous**.
- Perforator size on CTA: 3 mm average.
- Pedicle length: 8.3 cm (range 6.0–11.2).
- Perforator location: 10.1 cm inferior, 8.5 cm lateral to the ASIS-to-superolateral-patella line.

### Extended-flap three-zone model (Gosain 2002, n = 10 cadavers)<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

| Zone | Source artery | Reliability |
|---|---|---|
| **Proximal** | LCFA ascending branch via musculocutaneous perforators | **Reliable** |
| **Middle** | Third perforating artery of profunda femoris | Less reliable |
| **Distal** | Superior lateral genicular artery (popliteal branch) | Unreliable |

Middle and distal zones meet 8–10 cm above the knee; the skin paddle becomes unreliable here without a delay procedure or an additional anastomosis to the superior lateral genicular artery.

### Transverse-TFL anatomy (Medot & Fissette 1993, 20 cadavers)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

- LCFA divides into 3 terminal branches as it enters the TFL.
- The posterior transverse branch is constant, making the transverse TFL flap highly reliable.
- Descending branch (basis of the longitudinal or extended TFL) absent in 5/20 (25%).
- LCFA also supplies 1–2 ascending iliac-crest periosteal branches, the basis for a potential osteomyocutaneous TFL and iliac crest flap.

### Sensory innervation

- **Lateral femoral cutaneous nerve (L2-3).** Overlying anterolateral-thigh skin.
- Sensation can be partly retained when the LFCN is integrated into the flap; sensory projection to the donor site may persist for ≥ 2 years.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Flap Variants

| Variant | Pedicle / design | Skin island | Key feature | Indication |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Standard TFL MC** | Pedicled transposition / rotation | Over the TFL muscle (~15 × 8 cm) | Muscle + fascia lata + skin composite | Trochanteric sores, groin, perineum<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Extended TFL** | Pedicled transposition | Extended distally (up to 40 × 10 cm; to within 8 cm of knee) | Distal third unreliable without delay | Large pelvic / perineal defects<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **V-Y TFL** | V-Y advancement | Over TFL muscle | Advances best-vascularized portion; avoids dog-ear | Trochanteric sores<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **Island V-Y TFL fasciocutaneous** | Dual pedicle (LCFA descending + 3rd profunda perforator) | Distal lateral thigh | Avoids muscle sacrifice | Trochanteric sores<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Subcutaneous-pedicle TFL** | Distal skin island on iliotibial tract | Distal lateral thigh | **360° arc of rotation**; subcutaneous tunneling; sensate potential | Pelvic / perineal / penile defects<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **Transverse TFL** | Constant posterior transverse branch | 20 × 18 cm superolateral thigh | Highly reliable | Various<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| **Free TFL perforator** | Free; perforator-based | &gt; 15 × 12 cm | No muscle; minimal donor morbidity | Extremity / head & neck<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **TFL perforator (septocutaneous)** | Septocutaneous perforator | ~14 × 7 cm (4.5–25 × 4.5–19) | Perforator constancy simplifies planning | Groin / extremity / head<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> |
| **Free TFL musculofasciocutaneous** | Free; muscle + fascia + skin | Variable | Largest free-flap series (Bulstrode n = 85) | Head & neck, abdominal wall, lower limb<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |

## Surgical Technique — Pedicled TFL for Vulvar / Inguinal Reconstruction

### Position

- Supine or lithotomy: allows access to both the anterolateral-thigh donor and the vulvar or inguinal defect.
- Ipsilateral lower extremity prepped circumferentially.

### Landmarks<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

- **ASIS.** Proximal landmark.
- **Greater trochanter.** Lateral landmark.
- **Lateral femoral condyle or superolateral patella.** Distal landmark.
- **ASIS-to-superolateral-patella line.** The TFL lies along its proximal portion.
- Perforator planning zone: in Hubmer's 45-thigh cadaveric study, 76% of septocutaneous perforators emerged 8–12 cm from the ASIS; the interval is not guaranteed, and each patient's perforators should be located individually.

### Design

| Design | Skin paddle |
|---|---|
| Standard | ~15 × 8 cm over the TFL muscle |
| Extended | Up to 40 × 10 cm, distally along the iliotibial band to within 8 cm of the knee |
| V-Y / transposition / rotation / island | As required by defect |

### Elevation<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

1. **Distal incision first.** Elevate distal-to-proximal.
2. **Subfascial plane.** Deep to fascia lata; harvest TFL muscle, fascia lata, subcutaneous tissue and skin as a composite.
3. **Identify the pedicle.** Ascending LCFA enters the deep TFL surface approximately 8–10 cm below the ASIS.
4. **Preserve the pedicle** as the flap is elevated proximally.
5. **Motor-nerve choice.** Superior-gluteal-nerve branch can be preserved or sacrificed depending on flap design.
6. **Distal extension.** Fascia lata and overlying skin extended distally; distal skin supplied by terminal musculocutaneous perforators.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Transfer

- Arc of rotation: the pedicle-entry region is a planning pivot; reported reach includes the groin, perineum, lower abdomen, trochanter, ischium, and sacrum, depending on flap variant, pedicle dissection and patient anatomy.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- For vulvar or inguinal defects: rotate anteromedially.
- Subcutaneous-pedicle variant: 360° arc of rotation via subcutaneous tunneling.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

### Donor closure

- Primary closure for flap widths up to approximately 8–10 cm.
- Wider flaps require split-thickness skin graft.
- Donor scar on the lateral thigh: visible but concealable by clothing.

## Applications

### Trochanteric pressure sores

A traditional option because of donor proximity, vascularized tissue and fascia-lata padding; suitability depends on pressure-relief planning and donor-site condition.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[7]](#ref7)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

### Groin reconstruction after inguinal / ilioinguinal lymphadenectomy

**Nirmal 2011 (n = 25, primary closure versus TFL flap after groin dissection):**<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

| Outcome | Primary closure (28 groins) | TFL flap (20 groins) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wound infection | 14% | 5% | 0.38 |
| **Major flap necrosis** | **25%** | **0%** | **0.01** |
| Minor flap necrosis | 25% | 15% | — |
| **Necrosis after ilioinguinal dissection** | **75%** | **17%** | **0.001** |
| Seroma | 18% | 15% | 1.0 |
| Hospital stay | 20 ± 14 d | 16 ± 3 d | — |

This 25-patient retrospective comparison led its authors to suggest prophylactic TFL coverage after selected ilioinguinal dissections. The 75% versus 17% comparison is among groins with ilioinguinal dissection, not all 48 groins or a randomized effect estimate; treatment selection and small subgroup sizes limit generalization.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

**Agarwal 2009 (n = 15 inguinal block dissection for malignancy):**<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

- Satisfactory healing in all 15 cases.
- Complications: 2 marginal necroses, 3 lymphedemas, 2 infections, 3 minor STSG losses at donor.
- No regional recurrence.

**Saito 2014 (n = 15 oncology patients across multiple defect sites):**<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

- 60% overall complications (distal necrosis 33%, ventral hernia 11% in abdominal-wall recon cases).
- No total flap loss.

### Vulvar / perineal reconstruction

| Series | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Höckel & Dornhöfer 2008**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Listed in the authors' vulvovaginal scheme and favored there for selected defects including the inguinal region; not a universal first-choice standard |
| Temple & Ketcham 1982<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | Bilateral extended TFL (10 × 40 cm each) for a 15 × 30 cm perineal defect post-exenteration; flaps delayed 2 wk preoperatively |
| Withers 1980<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | 11 TFL musculocutaneous flaps in 9 patients — exceedingly reliable; groin / perineum / abdominal wall / lower chest wall |
| Safak 1996 (subcutaneous-pedicle TFL)<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | Sensate, thin, durable fascia, extensive reach with 360° rotation arc — pelvic / perineal / penile reconstruction |

**Rationale for inguinal-region vulvar defects**: simultaneous coverage of (1) vulvar defect, (2) vascularized tissue over femoral vessels in the groin, (3) inguinal dead-space obliteration, (4) well-vascularized tissue into a potentially irradiated field.

### Other reconstructive applications

| Application | Detail |
|---|---|
| Head & neck oncologic defects | Hodea 2026 (n = 20) — 95% success; TFL perforator flap as ALT alternative when ALT perforators absent / inadequate<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup> |
| Abdominal-wall reconstruction | Bulstrode series; ventral-hernia risk noted (Saito)<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[27]](#ref27)</sup> |
| Lower-limb reconstruction | Trochanteric, knee defects, distal-extremity coverage<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Free TFL musculofasciocutaneous | Bulstrode 2006 — n = 85; 93% success, 5% partial loss, 2% total failure<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |

## Advantages

| Advantage | Detail |
|---|---|
| Reliable axial pedicle | Ascending LCFA branch with septocutaneous perforators present in 100% of dissections<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| Large skin paddle | Up to 19 × 13 cm standard; extendable to 40 × 10 cm (with delay) |
| Versatile design | Standard MC / extended / V-Y / island / transverse / subcutaneous-pedicle / perforator (pedicled or free) |
| 360° arc of rotation | With subcutaneous-pedicle variant<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| Composite tissue | Muscle + fascia lata + skin in a single elevation |
| Fascia lata structural component | Useful for abdominal-wall or pelvic-floor reconstruction |
| Sensate potential | LFCN can be incorporated<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Function-sparing potential | Induced TFL weakness reduced abduction force in one hip position; postoperative donor function requires separate assessment.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> |
| Accessory vascular network | SCIA / IGA connections were demonstrated anatomically; reliable harvest after LCFA injury remains an individual perfusion decision.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| Groin-dissection series | Selected ilioinguinal groins had less skin necrosis with TFL in one small retrospective cohort (75% versus 17%); not a randomized benefit estimate.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> |
| Inguinal-region vulvar defects | One option in Höckel and Dornhöfer's proposed scheme.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |

## Limitations

| Limitation | Detail |
|---|---|
| Distal-skin unreliability | Skin paddle unreliable 8–10 cm above the knee without delay or additional anastomosis<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Donor-site contour | Visible lateral-thigh scar; STSG required when width &gt; 8–10 cm |
| Variable LCFA branching | 11 trunk variations; descending branch absent in 25% (transverse-TFL anatomy)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[22]](#ref22)</sup> |
| Donor-site complications | Distal necrosis 33% in mixed-site oncology series (Saito); ventral hernia 11% in abdominal-wall recon<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup> |
| Sensory projection to donor | LFCN-included flap may have persistent sensory projection to donor for ≥ 2 y<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Not ideal for hairless / mucosal subunits | Hair-bearing anterolateral-thigh skin |
| Cannot reach midline above umbilicus | Pedicled arc of rotation limits anterior abdominal-wall extension |
