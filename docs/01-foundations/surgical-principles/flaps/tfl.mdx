# Tensor Fasciae Latae (TFL) Flap

The tensor fasciae latae (TFL) flap is a lateral-thigh musculocutaneous flap based on the ascending branch of the lateral circumflex femoral artery. Historical reports describe it as a pedicled, island, or free transfer for coverage of groin, perineal, abdominal-wall, pressure-injury, and other soft-tissue defects.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Reported GU context

In a retrospective 2011 study of groin dissections, 11 people receiving TFL cover were compared with 14 receiving primary closure. The cohort suggested fewer necrosis events in the TFL group, but case selection differed and the nonrandomized design cannot establish prophylactic use as a standard.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The TFL flap appears among procedures discussed for vulvovaginal reconstruction. That review emphasizes individualized reconstructive planning rather than a small set of standardized choices and notes limitations in irradiated tissue.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The source access reviewed here does not support a first-choice claim for a specific vulvar or inguinal defect.

## Evidence boundary

The sources directly reviewed for this page were PubMed records and abstracts. They do not establish muscle function after harvest, perforator anatomy, skin-paddle dimensions, arc of rotation, donor-site closure, technical elevation details, outcome rates across indications, or comparative superiority over ALT, SCIP, VRAM, gracilis, or other flaps.
