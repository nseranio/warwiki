# Labia Majora Fasciocutaneous Flap

The **labia majora fasciocutaneous flap** is a full-thickness pedicled flap from the labia majora — **distinct from the Martius flap**, which harvests only the labial fat pad. Where the Martius contributes a **vascularized interposition layer**, the labia majora fasciocutaneous flap contributes **actual vaginal wall tissue** — skin, subcutaneous fat, and underlying dartos-like fascia — to reconstruct the anterior vaginal wall itself.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

It is an option for selected **complex urogynecologic fistulas with concomitant vaginal-wall deficit** — a situation in which fistula closure alone may leave an inadequate, rigid, or absent vagina.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Vascular Basis

The labia majora receives dual blood supply:

- **Anterior labial arteries** (from the external pudendal system) — contribute to anterior labial perfusion
- **Posterior labial arteries** (from the internal pudendal system) — contribute to posterior labial perfusion

Pedicle choice and orientation depend on defect position, reach, prior incisions and the vascular territory preserved in the individual flap; a single anterior/posterior split or default fistula pedicle should not be assumed.

The epifascial and subfascial arterial networks of the perineal region permit **peninsula or island designs** in selected defects. Sensory preservation depends on the individual flap design and nerve dissection; it is not guaranteed by the flap name.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

**Rothenberger et al.** mapped the **Martius labial fat pad**, not the full-thickness fasciocutaneous flap, in 10 cadavers. The right labium had 8% greater measured vascular density than the left (p &lt; 0.001), but the medial-versus-lateral vascular-density comparison was **not significant** (p = 0.55); nerve density was higher medially. These cadaveric observations inform anatomy but do not establish a preferred incision, laterality or outcome for this distinct flap.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

Contrast with the [Martius flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/martius), which uses the same vascular system but harvests only the fat pad — not the overlying skin and fascia.

---

## Indications

The defining indication is a **complex urogynecologic fistula with vaginal deficit** — situations where the vagina itself is inadequate and the defect requires tissue replacement, not just interposition.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- **Complex VVF with rigid, non-capacious, radiated vagina** — labia majora tissue augments both the fistula repair and the vaginal wall
- **Urethrovaginal fistula with anterior vaginal wall loss and stenosis** — restores vaginal caliber while closing the fistula
- **Vesicolabial fistula** — the fistula tract communicates with the labia; flap repair is natural
- **Recurrent VVF after failed Martius interposition** — escalation to full-thickness tissue replacement
- **Post-obstetric or post-radiation vaginal-wall loss** where vaginal reconstruction is needed concurrent with fistula closure

---

## Harvest and Technique

### Design

- **Flap outlined on the labia majora** on one side (or bilaterally for larger defects)
- **Full-thickness flap** including skin, subcutaneous fat, underlying fibrofatty tissue, and investing fascia
- **Pedicle preserved** posteriorly (posterior labial a.) or anteriorly (anterior labial a.) depending on required reach
- **Flap dimensions** tailored to the defect and a tension-free, perfused inset; published dimensions should be checked in the specific operative report before being used as a template

### Dissection

1. **Perineotomy or vaginal dissection** to expose the fistula and the deficient vaginal wall
2. **Close the fistula** (cystorrhaphy, urethrorrhaphy) with a standard technique
3. **Elevate the labia majora flap** from distal to proximal, staying above the pubic periosteum and sparing the pedicle
4. **Identify and preserve the labial artery and its venae comitantes** on the undersurface of the flap
5. **Tunnel the flap** beneath the perineal skin to the vaginal defect
6. **Inset** as **vaginal wall replacement** — the flap skin becomes neovaginal epithelium
7. **Donor site** closed primarily in most cases; may require local rotation advancement if large

### Principle

The labia majora fasciocutaneous flap **simultaneously** (1) covers the fistula repair with a vascularized interposition layer (like a Martius) and (2) provides actual vaginal wall tissue — skin and fascia replacing the missing vaginal surface. This dual contribution is what distinguishes it from the Martius flap, which provides only interposition.

## Outcomes — Complex Urogynecologic Fistula

Gupta 2022 series (7 patients, complex urogynecologic fistulas):<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- The original abstract reports no postoperative leak in its radiation-associated VVF subgroup **except one patient who became dry after later colpocleisis**; it does not provide a verified 6/7 flap-only success count.
- The patient with urethrovaginal fistula and stenosis was continent with a capacious vagina and regular menstrual periods; the vesicolabial-fistula patient was dry at six months. These are **case-specific observations**, not outcomes in every patient.
- The full case-by-case outcomes and donor-site complication table remain to be checked before quoting a cohort-wide rate.

## Vulvar Reconstruction After Oncologic Resection

Labial fasciocutaneous flaps are a key component of the reconstructive armamentarium for vulvar cancer defects:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- **Small-to-medium vulvar defects** — island V-Y flaps from the pubolabial region are considered the **"workhorse flap"** for vulvar reconstruction owing to their versatility, reliability, and low complication rate.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Anterior / posterior commissure defects** — anteriorly or posteriorly based labial flaps as appropriate.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Hemivulvectomy defects** — unilateral labial or pudendal-thigh flaps.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Total vulvectomy defects** — bilateral labial flaps or the **pubolabial V-Y amplified flap of Moschella and Cordova**, which can restore the entire vulva with a single flap by combining downward advancement with bilateral medial rotation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Salgarello 2005 algorithm** structures flap selection by defect dimensions and location.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

**Kwong 2025** studied **mixed locoregional vulvar flaps**, not a labia-majora-fasciocutaneous-flap-specific cohort (136 flaps in 69 women):<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- **92.6% of flaps with none-to-mild complications at 7 days**
- **83.1% with none-to-mild complications during days 8–30**; five necrotic flaps in two women required debridement
- Patient-reported genital, urinary, sexual and body-image measures improved in assessed subsets at 12 months; the single-arm study cannot assign those changes to this particular flap or isolate a causal effect of reconstruction

**Al-Benna 2012** — postablative vulvar reconstruction with local fasciocutaneous flaps and superficial-fascial-system repair achieved durable closure with low complication rates.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Vaginoplasty and Neovagina Creation

- **Flack 1993** — a related but distinct **labia minora** flap with a Lucite dilator for Müllerian agenesis; not an outcome series for the labia majora flap.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Belloli 1997** — a separately described tissue-expanded labial-skin-flap vaginoplasty. Its reported dilation experience should not be generalized to nonexpanded labia majora flaps.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Partial vaginal defects** — labial flaps are well suited to functionally important small-volume vaginal defects, such as those resulting from fistula excision.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Tubularized Labial Urethroplasty for Complex Female Stricture

**Xu 2009** — 8 patients with **complex female urethral strictures associated with urethrovaginal fistulas after pelvic-fracture urethral injury** treated with **transpubic access + tubularized labial skin flap urethroplasty**. The flap was tubularized over a stent to create a neourethra:<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- **No postoperative complications**
- Normal micturition in **7 of 8 patients** at mean 48-month follow-up
- A salvage option when local urethral plate is destroyed and BMG alone is insufficient

---

## Recurrent Urethrovaginal Fistula

**Candiani 1993** — a **distinct island bulbocavernosus musculocutaneous flap** incorporating labium-majus skin for anterior vaginal-wall reconstruction after urethral repair in recurrent urethrovaginal fistulas.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

---

## Perineal Reconstruction

**Windhofer 2011** — a **distinct infragluteal fasciocutaneous flap** for perineal reconstruction after rectal / anal disease. Its outcomes are not labia majora flap outcomes; the reported 13 of 14 complete healings pertain to that series.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Design Variations

| Flap design | Vascular basis | Best indication | Key feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Anteriorly based labial flap** | External pudendal arteries | Posterior commissure, RVF | Rotates posteriorly<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Posteriorly based labial flap** | Posterior labial artery (internal pudendal) | Anterior defects, urethral pathology, VVF | Rotates anteriorly<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Pubolabial V-Y amplified flap (Moschella–Cordova)** | Deep pubic arterial network | Total vulvectomy defects | Single flap restores entire vulva; bilateral medial rotation<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Island fasciocutaneous flap (gluteal fold)** | Pudendal artery perforators | Hemivulvectomy, posterior defects | Scar hidden in gluteal fold; sensate<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Composite fasciocutaneous + fat pad** | Dual pudendal supply | Complex fistula with vaginal deficit | Provides both epithelial surface and interposition<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Tubularized labial flap** | Labial vascular pedicle | Female urethral stricture / PFUI fistula | Creates neourethra<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Tissue-expanded labial-skin flap** | Labial vascular pedicle | Müllerian agenesis vaginoplasty | Generates large non-hair-bearing neovaginal lining<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |

## Advantages

- **Tissue replacement, not just interposition** — addresses vaginal deficit and fistula in one operation
- **Full-thickness vascularized tissue** — may help selected scarred or previously irradiated fields when an adequate pedicle and recipient bed are available; survival is not assured
- **Donor site in the operative field** — no distant harvest
- **Bilateral harvest possible** — for very large vaginal deficits

---

## Limitations

- **Female patients only** — technique is anatomy-specific
- **Labial disfigurement/asymmetry** at the donor site — significant counseling point
- **Hair-bearing** — the flap skin carries labial hair into the neovagina; can be managed with preoperative hair removal or accepted
- **Bilateral harvest** doubles donor morbidity — reserved for the largest defects
- **Limited tissue volume** compared with thigh-based flaps for very large defects

---

## Comparison with Martius Flap

| Feature | Martius Flap | Labia Majora Fasciocutaneous |
|---|---|---|
| **Tissue harvested** | Labial fat pad only | Skin + fat + fascia (full-thickness) |
| **Primary role** | Interposition layer between repaired planes | Vaginal wall tissue replacement |
| **Donor morbidity** | Modest; labial contour change | Visible labial asymmetry |
| **Best for** | Complex VVF without vaginal-wall deficit | Complex VVF with vaginal-wall deficit |
| **Bilateral** | Possible for larger interposition | Possible for large vaginal reconstruction |

The two flaps have **complementary tissue roles**: an interposition fat pad when the vaginal surface is adequate, versus an epithelialized flap when vaginal-wall replacement is required. Choice also depends on anatomy, perfusion, defect size and reconstructive alternatives; this is not a comparative-evidence ranking.

| Flap | Tissue type | Skin island | Sensate | Best for | Limitations |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| **[Martius (labial fat pad)](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/martius)** | Fibroadipose only | No | No | Fistula interposition; sling erosion | No epithelial surface |
| **[Singapore / pudendal-thigh](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/singapore-pudendal-thigh)** | Fasciocutaneous | Yes | Yes | Partial vaginal defects; vulvar | Bulky base; hair growth |

## Technical Pearls

- **Preoperative hair removal** on the planned flap skin reduces neovaginal hair growth
- **Identify the labial artery Doppler-wise** before incision in re-operative or scarred fields
- **Donor-site closure** benefits from a small lateral undermining to reduce tension
- **Postoperative stenting, packing or dilation**, when used, should follow the specific reconstruction and surgeon's protocol; a universal five-to-seven-day schedule or prevention of contraction is not established here
