# Labia Majora Fasciocutaneous Flap

The **labia majora fasciocutaneous flap** is a full-thickness pedicled flap from the labia majora that is distinct from the Martius flap, which harvests only the labial fat pad. The Martius flap contributes a vascularized interposition layer. The labia majora fasciocutaneous flap contributes vaginal wall tissue (skin, subcutaneous fat and investing fascia) to reconstruct the anterior vaginal wall itself.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

It is an option for selected complex urogynecologic fistulas with concomitant vaginal-wall deficit, in which fistula closure alone may leave an inadequate, rigid or absent vagina.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Vascular Basis

The labia majora receives dual blood supply:

- **Anterior labial arteries** (from the external pudendal system) contribute to anterior labial perfusion.
- **Posterior labial arteries** (from the internal pudendal system) contribute to posterior labial perfusion.

Pedicle choice and orientation depend on defect position, reach, prior incisions and the vascular territory preserved in the individual flap; a fixed anterior-versus-posterior split or default fistula pedicle should not be assumed.

The epifascial and subfascial arterial networks of the perineal region permit peninsula or island designs in selected defects. Sensory preservation depends on the individual flap design and nerve dissection; it is not guaranteed by the flap name.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Rothenberger and colleagues mapped the Martius labial fat pad, not the full-thickness fasciocutaneous flap, in 10 cadavers. Vascular density was 8% greater in the right labium than the left (p &lt; 0.001), with the highest density in the right lateral labium, and the medial-versus-lateral difference was not significant (p = 0.55). Nerve density was highest in the anterior and medial fat pad. These cadaveric observations inform anatomy but do not establish a preferred incision, laterality or outcome for this distinct flap.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

Contrast with the [Martius flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/martius), which uses the same vascular system but harvests only the fat pad and not the overlying skin and fascia.

---

## Indications

The defining indication is a complex urogynecologic fistula with vaginal deficit, where the vagina itself is inadequate and the defect requires tissue replacement and not just interposition.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- **Complex VVF with a rigid, non-capacious, radiated vagina.** Labia majora tissue augments both the fistula repair and the vaginal wall.
- **Urethrovaginal fistula with anterior vaginal wall loss and stenosis.** The flap restores vaginal caliber while closing the fistula.
- **Vesicolabial fistula.** The fistula tract communicates with the labia; flap repair is natural.
- **Recurrent VVF after failed Martius interposition**, where full-thickness tissue replacement may be considered
- **Post-obstetric or post-radiation vaginal-wall loss** where vaginal reconstruction is needed concurrent with fistula closure

## Harvest and Technique

### Design

- **Flap outlined on the labia majora** on one side (or bilaterally for larger defects)
- **Full-thickness flap** including skin, subcutaneous fat, underlying fibrofatty tissue, and investing fascia
- **Pedicle preserved** posteriorly (posterior labial a.) or anteriorly (anterior labial a.) depending on required reach
- **Flap dimensions** tailored to the defect and a tension-free, perfused inset; published dimensions should be checked in the specific operative report before being used as a template

### Dissection

1. **Perineotomy or vaginal dissection** to expose the fistula and the deficient vaginal wall
2. **Close the fistula** (cystorrhaphy, urethrorrhaphy) with a standard technique
3. **Elevate the labia majora flap** from distal to proximal, sparing the pedicle
4. **Identify and preserve the labial artery and its venae comitantes** on the undersurface of the flap
5. **Tunnel the flap** beneath the perineal skin to the vaginal defect
6. **Inset** as vaginal wall replacement. The flap skin becomes neovaginal epithelium.
7. **Donor site** closed primarily in most cases; may require local rotation advancement if large

The sequence above is a general outline. Gupta's series describes several technical modifications made for individual fistulas.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Principle

The labia majora fasciocutaneous flap (1) covers the fistula repair with a vascularized interposition layer, like a Martius flap, and (2) provides vaginal wall tissue, with skin and fascia replacing the missing vaginal surface. The second contribution distinguishes it from the Martius flap, which provides only interposition.

## Outcomes — Complex Urogynecologic Fistula

Gupta 2022 series (7 patients, complex urogynecologic fistulas):<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- All patients with radiation-induced VVF had no postoperative leak, except one who became dry only after later colpocleisis. The series does not report a cohort-wide success rate.
- The patient with urethrovaginal fistula and vaginal stenosis was continent with a capacious vagina and regular menstrual periods. The patient with vesicolabial fistula was dry at six months.
- These are case-specific observations in seven selected patients, not outcomes in every patient.

## Vulvar Reconstruction After Oncologic Resection

Various skin flaps, including axial pattern, fasciocutaneous and musculocutaneous flaps, can restore vulvovaginal form and function with acceptable donor-site and recipient-site morbidity, and function is harder to restore in irradiated tissue.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Flap choice by defect:

- **Small-to-medium vulvar defects.** Salgarello's algorithm uses island V-Y flaps, island gluteal fold flaps or pedicled pudendal-thigh flaps. Among these, Salgarello preferred the island V-Y flap for its versatility, reliability and technical simplicity with a very low complication rate (31 flaps in 20 reconstructions).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Anterior and posterior commissure defects.** Anteriorly or posteriorly based labial flaps are used as appropriate.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Hemivulvectomy defects.** Unilateral labial or pudendal-thigh flaps are used.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Symmetric anterior defects.** Moschella and Cordova used a V-Y amplified sliding flap from the pubis, vascularized by the deep arterial network of the pubis and sensate through ilioinguinal nerve branches.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Posterior defects after hemivulvectomy or radical vulvectomy.** Moschella and Cordova raised a fasciocutaneous island flap from one or both gluteal folds, vascularized by pudendal artery perforators and innervated by perineal branches of the pudendal nerve.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Large or very large defects.** In Salgarello's algorithm V-Y flaps close large defects, and a distally based vertical rectus abdominis flap is recommended for very large defects extending anteriorly and posteriorly.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

Kwong studied mixed locoregional vulvar flaps, not a cohort specific to the labia majora fasciocutaneous flap (136 flaps in 69 women):<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- 92.6% (126/136) of flaps had none-to-mild complications at 7 days.
- 83.1% (113/136) had none-to-mild complications during days 8–30. Five necrotic flaps in two women required debridement.
- Patient-reported genital, urinary, sexual and body-image measures improved in assessed subsets at 12 months. The single-arm design cannot attribute those changes to a particular flap or to reconstruction itself.

Al-Benna and Tzakas reported that 12 of 13 local fasciocutaneous flaps with superficial-fascial-system repair healed primarily after vulvar ablation. Complications were two superficial wound infections and one minor dehiscence.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Vaginoplasty and Neovagina Creation

- **Flack 1993.** A related but distinct labia minora flap with Lucite dilators for vaginal agenesis, reported in three teenage girls without postoperative complications at 1 year. It is not an outcome series for the labia majora flap.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Belloli 1997.** A separately described tissue-expanded labial-skin-flap vaginoplasty in two adolescents. Its reported freedom from postoperative dilation does not apply to nonexpanded labia majora flaps.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Partial vaginal defects.** Labial flaps are well suited to functionally important small-volume vaginal defects, such as those resulting from fistula excision.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Tubularized Labial Urethroplasty for Complex Female Stricture

Xu and colleagues treated 8 women with urethral strictures and urethrovaginal fistulas after pelvic fracture using transpubic access and a pedicled labial skin flap urethroplasty. The flap (about 3 × 3.5 × 3 cm from the labia minora or majora) was tubularized over an 18–22 Fr stent in five women, and two smaller flaps were pieced together in three:<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- No postoperative complications were reported. Transient dysuria and stress incontinence resolved spontaneously.
- One patient needed urethral dilation for difficulty voiding at 3 months, and the other seven had normal micturition after catheter removal (mean follow-up 48 months, range 10–118).
- The authors describe the technique for extensive urethral damage in this setting.

---

## Recurrent Urethrovaginal Fistula

**Candiani 1993** described a distinct island bulbocavernosus musculocutaneous flap raised from the labium majus to reconstruct the anterior vaginal wall after urethral suture in one woman with a recurrent postpartum urethrovaginal fistula.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

---

## Perineal Reconstruction

**Windhofer 2011** described a distinct fasciocutaneous infragluteal flap for perineal reconstruction after rectal and anal disease. Its outcomes are not labia majora flap outcomes. Complete healing occurred in 13 of 14 patients in that series.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Design Variations

| Flap design | Vascular basis | Best indication | Key feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Anteriorly based labial flap** | External pudendal arteries | Posterior commissure, RVF | Rotates posteriorly<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Posteriorly based labial flap** | Posterior labial artery (internal pudendal) | Anterior defects, urethral pathology, VVF | Rotates anteriorly<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **V-Y amplified sliding flap from the pubis (Moschella–Cordova)** | Deep pubic arterial network | Symmetric anterior vulvar defects | Sensate through ilioinguinal branches<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Island fasciocutaneous flap (gluteal fold)** | Pudendal artery perforators | Hemivulvectomy, posterior defects | Scars in natural folds; sensate through perineal branches of the pudendal nerve<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Composite fasciocutaneous + fat pad** | Dual pudendal supply | Complex fistula with vaginal deficit | Provides both epithelial surface and interposition<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Tubularized labial flap** | Labial vascular pedicle | Female urethral stricture / PFUI fistula | Creates neourethra<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Tissue-expanded labial-skin flap** | Labial vascular pedicle | Müllerian agenesis vaginoplasty | Generates large non-hair-bearing neovaginal lining<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |

## Advantages

- **Tissue replacement and interposition.** The flap addresses vaginal deficit and fistula in one operation.
- **Full-thickness vascularized tissue.** It may help selected scarred or previously irradiated fields when an adequate pedicle and recipient bed are available; survival is not assured.
- **Donor site in the operative field.** No distant harvest is needed.
- **Bilateral harvest.** It is possible for very large vaginal deficits.

---

## Limitations

- **Female patients only.** The technique is anatomy-specific.
- **Labial disfigurement or asymmetry.** Donor-site change is a significant counseling point.
- **Hair-bearing skin.** The flap skin carries labial hair into the neovagina, which can be managed with preoperative hair removal or accepted.
- **Bilateral harvest** doubles donor morbidity and is reserved for the largest defects.
- **Limited tissue volume** compared with thigh-based flaps for very large defects

---

## Comparison with Martius Flap

| Feature | Martius Flap | Labia Majora Fasciocutaneous |
|---|---|---|
| **Tissue harvested** | Labial fat pad only | Skin + fat + fascia (full-thickness) |
| **Primary role** | Interposition layer between repaired planes | Vaginal wall tissue replacement |
| **Donor morbidity** | Modest; labial contour change | Visible labial asymmetry |
| **Best for** | Complex VVF without vaginal-wall deficit | Complex VVF with vaginal-wall deficit |
| **Bilateral** | Possible for larger interposition | Possible for large vaginal reconstruction |

The two flaps have complementary tissue roles: an interposition fat pad when the vaginal surface is adequate, versus an epithelialized flap when vaginal-wall replacement is required. Choice also depends on anatomy, perfusion, defect size and reconstructive alternatives; this is not a comparative-evidence ranking.

## Technical Pearls

- **Preoperative hair removal** on the planned flap skin may reduce neovaginal hair growth
- **Identify the labial artery Doppler-wise** before incision in re-operative or scarred fields
- **Donor-site closure** benefits from a small lateral undermining to reduce tension
- **Postoperative stenting, packing or dilation**, when used, should follow the specific reconstruction and surgeon's protocol; a universal five-to-seven-day schedule or prevention of contraction is not established here
