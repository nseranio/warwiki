# Labia Majora Fasciocutaneous Flap

This term is used for a local labia-majora tissue flap in vulvovaginal and genitourinary reconstruction literature. It should not be conflated with the [Martius flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/martius), which the accessible anatomy study describes as a vascularized labial-fat-pad interposition flap. This page is a source-bounded terminology and literature-context overview—not operative, flap-selection, or fistula-management guidance.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Reported contexts

Gupta and colleagues published a 2022 report titled *Labia Majora Fasciocutaneous Flap Reconstruction in Complex Urogynecological Fistulas With Vaginal Deficit*; the accessible indexed record establishes that report's topic and citation, but not a transferable procedure or outcome standard.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

More broadly, a review of reconstruction for neoplastic vulvovaginal disease describes several flap families and emphasizes that reconstruction requires individualized specialist assessment rather than a small set of standardized procedures.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> A separate eight-patient series reported pedicled labial-skin flaps for a highly specific female pelvic-fracture urethral-injury setting; that is not evidence for routine use or for other defects.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Anatomy evidence boundary

The accessible 2026 cadaveric mapping study concerns the labial fat pad for Martius-flap harvest, not a full labia-majora fasciocutaneous flap. It found regionally variable nerve and vascular density in ten cadavers; it does not establish a universal dissection plane, pedicle choice, flap dimensions, or safety rule for the flap named on this page.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Evidence boundaries

The source package reviewed for this recovery is limited to an indexed record for the 2022 fistula article and PubMed abstracts/records for the broader review, the cadaveric Martius study, and the eight-patient urethral-injury series. It does not support retaining inherited operative steps, flap dimensions, indications, comparative tables, complication rates, functional outcomes, hair-management claims, or postoperative protocols.
