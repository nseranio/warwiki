# External Pudendal Artery Perforator (EPAP) Hemi-Scrotal Flap

The **EPAP flap** is a **sensate perforator-based hemi-scrotal flap** harvested from one hemiscrotum, pedicled on a perforator of the **external pudendal artery** (from the femoral artery), and rotated onto the penile shaft or contralateral scrotum to resurface circumferential or hemi-scrotal defects. The flap was popularized in genital reconstruction by Tsukuura 2025 for circumferential penile-shaft skin defects, exploiting the fact that the hemiscrotum is its own well-vascularized, elastic, sensate skin territory that closely matches penile-shaft requirements.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Anatomy

- **Pedicle.** Perforator of the external pudendal artery (a branch of the femoral artery), reliably identifiable in the anterior-medial scrotal root.
- **Composition.** Hemi-scrotal skin + dartos ± external spermatic fascia — thin, elastic, sensate.
- **Innervation.** Branches of the ilioinguinal and genitofemoral nerves give the flap **protective sensation**.

---

## Genital Applications

- **Circumferential penile-shaft skin defect** — sensate single-flap coverage of the penis with native-quality skin.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Hemi-scrotal reconstruction** when the contralateral side is intact.
- **Adjunct in penile shaft reconstruction** after Fournier's gangrene, oncologic resection, or buried-penis repair when STSG / FTSG is undesirable.

---

## Advantages

- Native scrotal skin texture, color, and elasticity.
- Sensate.
- Single-stage; primary donor closure (contralateral hemiscrotum closes primarily because of native redundancy).
