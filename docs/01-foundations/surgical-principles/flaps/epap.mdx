# External Pudendal Artery Perforator (EPAP) Hemi-Scrotal Flap

The **EPAP hemi-scrotal flap** is a sensate perforator-based flap harvested from one hemiscrotum, pedicled on a perforator of the external pudendal artery with incorporation of the anterior scrotal nerve. Tsukuura et al. (2025) reported one case of circumferential penile-shaft skin defect reconstruction. The related super-thin external pudendal artery (STEPA) free flap was studied in cadavers by Phoon et al. in 2014; its mean measured thickness of 1.1 mm is not a measurement or guaranteed thickness of the clinical EPAP flap.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

For the operative-level deep-dive in scrotal flap glanuloplasty / penile resurfacing see the [EPAP Hemi-Scrotal Flap atlas page (04e)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/epap-hemi-scrotal-flap).

## Vascular Anatomy of the External Pudendal System

### External pudendal arteries

The external pudendal arteries arise from the femoral artery (occasionally the deep femoral) and exist as two distinct vessels:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- **Superficial external pudendal artery (SEPA).** It courses superficial to the spermatic cord and supplies suprapubic skin, mons pubis, and upper scrotum. It was present in 92% of specimens, with a diameter of 1.2–3.8 mm. It was duplicated in 46%, a common trunk with the deep branch in 24%, and single in 30%. Origin was 0.8–8.5 cm below the inguinal ligament.
- **Deep external pudendal artery (DEPA).** It courses deep to the spermatic cord medially; the cited anatomical studies describe branches supplying the scrotum, with vascular variation that matters in flap planning.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

**Surgical landmark.** 83% of external pudendal arteries arise between the inter-pubic-tubercle horizontal and 3 cm below it, centered on the femoral artery.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### Scrotal cutaneous territories (Carrera 2009)

Three richly inter-anastomosed territories:<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- **Two lateral territories.** Each is supplied by an inferior external pudendal artery entering at the midpoint of the scrotal root and fanning out across the entire hemiscrotum.
- **One central territory.** It is supplied by perineal-artery branches (internal pudendal system) running deep on both sides of the scrotal septum.

This dual-system supply (external pudendal laterally, internal pudendal centrally and posteriorly) is the anatomical basis for harvesting one hemiscrotum while preserving contralateral perfusion.

### Perineal perforators

Jin's microdissection of 22 sides identified 4 relatively constant perforating arteries in the perineum: the inguinal and perineal perforating branches of the SEPA, a perforating branch of the anterior cutaneous branch of the obturator artery, and a perforating branch of the lateral branch of the posterior scrotal (pudendal) artery. These formed upper, middle, and lower vascular anastomotic networks within the deep fascia above the adductor wall.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## The EPAP Flap — Innovations Over Traditional Scrotal Flaps

Traditional scrotal flaps for penile resurfacing include bilateral or total-anterior scrotal-skin designs. Tsukuura and colleagues identified possible limitations of these designs and proposed perforator isolation in their single case; those limitations are not inevitable in every conventional flap.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

1. Some bilateral designs use both external pudendal vascular territories.
2. A broad attached pedicle can tether the reconstruction and cause stretching or discomfort with erection.
3. Some designs leave a midline donor scar.

The EPAP perforator-based design addresses each:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Innovation | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Isolate the perforator** | Wider arc of rotation while retaining contralateral scrotal supply. |
| **Free the tunica vaginalis from the flap pedicle** | Aims to reduce tethering; the reported patient had no pain or stretching at seven months. |
| **Incorporate the anterior scrotal nerve** | Sensate reconstruction; the reported flap had sensation at seven months, without formal modality-specific testing in the accessible abstract. |
| **Unilateral harvest** | Primary closure of the donor with a **lateral** (rather than midline) scar. |

## Surgical Technique<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Preoperative planning

- Handheld Doppler maps the external pudendal artery perforator at the scrotal root.
- Flap designed on one hemiscrotum, pedicle at the scrotal root.

### Flap elevation

1. Incise the hemi-scrotal skin paddle and elevate above the deep fascia, as reported by Tsukuura et al.
2. Identify and isolate the external pudendal artery perforator as the vascular pedicle.
3. Identify and preserve the anterior scrotal nerve within the flap.
4. **Free the tunica vaginalis from the flap pedicle** to reduce testis–penis tethering.
5. Rotate the flap on its perforator pedicle to wrap circumferentially around the penile shaft.

### Inset and donor closure

- Wrap the flap around the penile shaft for tension-free circumferential coverage.
- Advance the contralateral hemiscrotum and residual scrotal skin for primary donor closure, leaving a lateral scrotal scar.

## Flap Characteristics

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Blood supply | External pudendal artery perforator (specific branch confirmed by individual dissection) |
| Sensory nerve | **Anterior scrotal nerve** |
| Flap thickness | Not reported for the clinical EPAP case; related cadaveric STEPA free-flap mean 1.1 mm<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Pedicle length | Not reported for the clinical EPAP case; related cadaveric STEPA free-flap mean 11 cm (range 10–12 cm)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Pedicle artery / vein diameter | Not reported for the isolated clinical perforator; cadaveric STEPA external pudendal vessels at the femoral origin averaged 2.81 / 4.44 mm<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Composition | Fasciocutaneous (scrotal skin + dartos) |
| Donor closure | Primary |
| Hair-bearing | Yes |

## Advantages

- **Potential for sensation.** The included anterior scrotal nerve and sensation at seven months in the reported patient are encouraging; modality-specific protective sensation and reproducibility remain unmeasured.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Like with like.** Scrotal skin matches penile skin in thinness, elasticity, pliability, and pigmentation. In the STEPA cadaver study, mean flap thickness was 1.1 mm, and the authors described it as the thinnest myocutaneous free flap.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Reduced tethering is plausible.** The reported patient had no pain or stretching at seven months; this does not establish a zero-risk outcome.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Unilateral harvest** preserves the contralateral hemiscrotum and allows primary donor closure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Wider arc of rotation.** The authors attribute this to perforator isolation, which frees the skin paddle from the underlying fascia; no comparative measurement has been reported.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Lateral, more inconspicuous scar** than a midline raphe scar.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Single-stage.** No flap division is required.

## The External Pudendal Flap Family

The EPAP sits within a family of flaps using the same vascular system:

| Flap | Pedicle | Use | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| **STEPA** (Super-Thin EPA, Phoon 2014) | Free flap on EPA | Cadaveric mean thickness 1.1 mm; proposed for hand dorsum, foot, head/neck, joints | Cadaveric study; later selected clinical reports exist, including a 2026 buccal reconstruction with measured 2–3-mm thickness<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| **EPAP hemi-scrotal** (Tsukuura 2025) | Pedicled perforator + anterior scrotal n. | Circumferential penile-shaft resurfacing | Single case report<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **SEPAP** (Kim 2023) | Superficial EPA perforator | Suprapubic / upper scrotum in penoscrotal Paget's reconstruction algorithm | Series<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **DEPAP** (Kim 2025) | Deep EPA perforator from upper medial thigh | Perineal reconstruction after Fournier's; preoperative handheld Doppler mapping; 9-mo stable result in case report | Case report<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Superficial EPA pedicle flap** (Mayer 1991) | SEPA-pedicled suprapubic skin | Vulvar reconstruction; pre-perforator-era predecessor | Series<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Pull-up double-opposing KDPIF on SEPAP** (Lee 2020) | SEPAP "hot spots" | Circumferential penile defect; 10 × 13 cm bilateral keystones; full survival at 6 mo, no ED / shortening | Case report<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |

## Indications

| Indication | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Circumferential penile-shaft skin defect** | The only indication reported for this flap (one case, after penile replantation; Tsukuura 2025).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The rows below are extrapolations from the case and from related external pudendal flaps. |
| **Partial penile skin loss** | When scrotal skin offers the best tissue match. |
| **Post-paraffinoma / foreign-body excision** | E.g., petroleum-jelly injection (Lee 2020 keystone variant).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Penile SCC excision** | Circumferential resurfacing after wide excision. |
| **Penile burns / mechanical trauma** | Penile-skin avulsion or thermal injury. |
| **Penoscrotal Paget's / Fournier's / vulvar reconstruction** | Broader EPA family (SEPAP / DEPAP variants).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |

## Limitations

- **Limited clinical experience.** The flap has been described in a single case report; series and long-term outcomes are lacking.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Hair-bearing.** Scrotal hair on the resurfaced shaft may require depilation.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Pigment and rugation mismatch.** Scrotal skin is darker and more rugated than native penile skin.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Anatomical variability of the EPA system.** The SEPA was duplicated in 46%, a common trunk in 24%, and absent in 8%.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Preoperative handheld Doppler mapping was used in the reported EPAP and DEPAP cases.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Finite flap size.** A single hemiscrotum provides limited tissue; very large defects may require additional flaps or grafts.
- **Ipsilateral testicular position** may be altered by harvest, though primary closure of the donor mitigates this.
