# External Pudendal Artery Perforator (EPAP) Flap

The **external pudendal artery perforator (EPAP) hemi-scrotal flap** is a recently reported local flap for a penile-shaft skin deficit. This page is a limited feasibility and terminology overview, not an operative protocol, flap-selection guide, or assurance of sensory or functional outcome.

## Reported clinical context

The published EPAP report identified in the prior source review is a single case report: a Doppler-planned hemi-scrotal flap was reported for a circumferential skin deficit after penile replantation. The authors described short-term recovery and follow-up findings in that individual case. A single abstract-access case report cannot establish reproducibility, comparative benefit, or a general indication.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Anatomic context

The scrotum has contributions from external-pudendal and perineal vascular systems. A cadaveric microvascular study described lateral territories supplied through the inferior external pudendal system and a central territory associated with perineal branches. This anatomic background does not itself verify the EPAP technique, its perfusion reliability, or patient-level outcomes.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Evidence limits

The full EPAP report, its figures, video, supplementary material, sensory testing details, and long-term outcomes were not recovered in the prior review. No direct comparative study or replication cohort was identified there. Accordingly, this overview intentionally omits fixed flap design, Doppler, dissection, nerve-preservation, transfer, inset, donor-closure, aftercare, complication, and comparative claims.
