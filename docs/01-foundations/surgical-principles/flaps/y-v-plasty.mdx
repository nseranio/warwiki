# Y-V Plasty

**Y-V plasty** is a geometric reconstructive technique in which a **Y-shaped incision is closed as a V**, advancing a triangular flap into a contracted segment to **lengthen the circumference** and relieve stenosis. Unlike most flap operations, the Y-V plasty does not move tissue from a distant donor — it reconfigures local tissue geometry to achieve widening. Its defining genitourinary application is refractory **bladder neck contracture (BNC)** and **vesicourethral anastomotic stenosis** after radical prostatectomy or endoscopic prostate surgery.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Geometric Principle

A Y-shaped incision is made with the two short limbs outlining a triangle on one side of the contracture and the long limb extending into the stenotic segment. The triangular flap is **advanced into the long limb defect**, and the wound is closed as a V — lengthening the perimeter of the previously narrow segment. The principle is identical to that used in limb-contracture release and cleft-palate repair, adapted to the bladder neck.

---

## Primary Application — Refractory Bladder Neck Contracture

BNC after TURP, holmium enucleation, or radical prostatectomy that has failed **≥2 endoscopic treatments** (dilation, transurethral incision, steroid injection, mitomycin C injection) is the textbook indication for Y-V plasty.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Open Y-V plasty

1. Midline lower abdominal incision; retropubic exposure
2. **Y-shaped full-thickness incision** into the anterior bladder wall extending through the contracture into the healthy bladder
3. **Triangular (V-shaped) bladder flap** mobilized and advanced distally into the stenotic segment
4. **Mucosa/submucosa** closed to both sides with consecutive sutures in a V configuration
5. **Seromuscular layer** closed over the mucosal repair to reinforce

### Laparoscopic modified Y-V plasty

Extraperitoneal laparoscopic approach using **barbed suture** — reduces operative time and anastomotic leakage vs. open.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Robotic Y-V plasty (RYVBNR)

Transperitoneal robotic approach. **Firefly (ICG) fluorescence** is used to localize the bladder neck contracture relative to adjacent structures in the scarred field. Median operative time ~240 minutes.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### Endoscopic Y-V plasty (novel transurethral variant)

A transurethral approach using a cystoscopic incision plus intraluminal laparoscopic suturing devices to bring healthy bladder mucosa across the defect **without an abdominal incision**. Reported **89% success after one procedure, 100% after a second procedure.**<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

---

## Outcomes

| Outcome | Range across series |
|---|---|
| **Success (relief of obstruction)** | **90–100%** for refractory BNC after ≥2 failed endoscopic treatments<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Qmax improvement** | 6.7 mL/s → **14.8–26.6 mL/s**<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Postvoid residual** | 223 mL → **12–45 mL**<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Continence** | **87–90%** retained<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Persistent storage symptoms** | **61%** of patients — predicted by baseline BMI and IPSS<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |

---

## Indications

- **Refractory BNC** after ≥2 failed endoscopic treatments (dilation, incision, steroid/mitomycin injection)
- **Vesicourethral anastomotic stenosis** (VUAS) after radical prostatectomy
- **Bladder neck stenosis** after TURP or holmium enucleation
- Can be combined with AUS placement in patients with concurrent incontinence

---

## Contraindications / Caveats

- **Active infection** at the operative site
- **Extensive bladder neck calcification** — may require adjunctive debridement
- **Heavily irradiated field** — consider [omental flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/omental) interposition at the time of repair
- **Patient expectation management:** 61% persistent storage symptoms is not surgical failure — it reflects underlying detrusor pathology<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

---

## Technical Pearls

- **Extend the Y well into healthy bladder** — the V advancement only works if the triangle has perfusion and lax tissue beyond the stenotic segment
- **Full-thickness incision** is required — partial-thickness closure recontracts
- **Robotic ICG fluorescence** substantially shortens the learning curve in the scarred post-prostatectomy field
- **Concurrent AUS** is reasonable in patients with established stress incontinence — staged AUS after Y-V plasty healing (usually 3–6 months) is the alternative
