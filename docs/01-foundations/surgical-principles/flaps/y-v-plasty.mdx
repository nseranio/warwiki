# Y-V Plasty

Y-V plasty is a local-tissue advancement concept used in several reconstructive settings. The precise geometry, tissue lengthening, operative design, and clinical effect vary by anatomy and indication.

## Reported genitourinary uses

For refractory bladder-neck stenosis after transurethral prostate surgery, a 2025 retrospective series reported Y-V plasty in five people with prior failed endoscopic treatments. At median 13-month follow-up, all met the authors' success definition; this small series cannot establish a standard indication, technique, or expected continence outcome.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

An 18-person retrospective series of open Y-V plasty for bladder-neck contracture after at least two failed endoscopic treatments reported no need for further instrumentation over a mean 14.8-month follow-up, while 61% reported persistent storage symptoms. Its cohort and short follow-up limit generalization.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For UPJ obstruction, a 36-person series of laparoscopic anterior Y-V pyeloplasty reported 34 successes among 35 people with follow-up. The authors described the approach as an option for selected small or intrarenal pelvis cases without dorsally crossing vessels; this does not establish comparative superiority or replace procedure-specific assessment.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For post-hypospadias meatal stenosis, a 2025 single-center Y-V meatoplasty series included 57 people and reported two early repeat operations; six people did not retain follow-up beyond one year.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Evidence boundary

The first three sources were reviewed as PubMed records and abstracts. The Y-V meatoplasty abstract and record were reviewed in the prior V-Y advancement batch and are reused here without a new full-source claim. These sources do not establish universal geometric formulas, operative steps, indications, contraindications, comparisons with other reconstruction, robotic or endoscopic technique standards, or routine use across bladder-neck, UPJ, meatal, hypospadias, or non-GU settings.
