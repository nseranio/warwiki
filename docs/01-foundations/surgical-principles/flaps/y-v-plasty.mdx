# Y-V Plasty

**Y-V plasty is a family of advancement repairs** in which a Y-shaped incision and advancement of a triangular tissue segment change the closure configuration toward a V. Running cutaneous Y-V scar release, bladder-wall Y-V reconstruction, Foley pyeloplasty and meatal Y-V repair share a geometric name but differ in tissue planes, vascular pedicles and selection criteria; no single no-undermining or low-necrosis rule covers them all.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In GU practice, Y-V designs have been reported for refractory **bladder neck contracture (BNC)** or **vesicourethral anastomotic stenosis (VUAS)**, ureteropelvic-junction obstruction and post-hypospadias meatal stenosis.

This page is the foundations-level deep dive. The procedure-specific technique pages are listed under [See Also](#see-also).

---

## Geometric Principles

The fundamental geometry of Y-V plasty involves three elements:

1. **The Y-shaped incision** — two limbs diverge from a central point (the upper arms of the Y) and a single stem extends distally. The tissue between the upper limbs forms a triangular (V-shaped) flap.
2. **Advancement mechanism** — the triangular flap **slides forward along its substratum** (without being lifted off its base) into the stem of the Y. This converts the Y into a V configuration, lengthening the tissue along the axis of the original stem.
3. **Pedicle and tissue quality** — the original running cutaneous Y-V design advances tissue on its local base, but operative dissection and vascular risk differ for bladder wall, renal pelvis and meatus. Perfusion cannot be assumed in scarred or irradiated tissue.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### Mathematical analysis

For a multiple Y-V plasty applied to a rectangular scar of length *l* and width *a*, the gain in length simplifies to:

```
Gain  =  (l × x) / a
```

where *x* is the advancement distance of the triangular flap **in that modeled multiple cutaneous Y-V design**. The equation is not a validated length-gain prediction for bladder-neck, UPJ or meatal surgery; angles, available tissue, perfusion and tension still matter to clinical design.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### Y-V vs. V-Y plasty — directional distinction

The names describe opposite incision-to-closure directions, but the clinical operations are not interchangeable:

| Operation | Incision → closure | Effect | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Y-V plasty** | Y → V | Advances tissue across a contracted segment | Selected cutaneous contractures and luminal stenoses |
| **V-Y plasty** | V → Y | Advances a flap into a neighboring defect | Selected cutaneous wounds (vulvar, perineal or scrotal) |

<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Applications in Plastic Surgery

**Burn-scar contracture release** is the classic indication. The "running Y-V plasty" technique uses multiple parallel Y-shaped incisions across a linear scar cord; each triangular flap slides into its respective stem, collectively lengthening the entire contracture without undermining.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Key advantages:

- Limited donor dissection in the reported running cutaneous design; comparative morbidity against Z-plasty is not established for every site<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Applicability to cord-like or linear contractures of the axilla, extremities, and trunk<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- The **opposite running Y-V plasty** variant uses Y-V flaps from both sides of a broad contracture band, particularly useful for axillary contractures<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- The procedure can be repeated when the initial release is insufficient<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

Other plastic-surgery applications include release of nostril stenosis, congenital soft-tissue shortening, nasal-tip / alar resurfacing, and supplemental release after Z-plasty.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Applications in Urology

The same geometric principle — advancing healthy tissue into a stenotic or contracted segment — has been adapted across the GU tract.

### 1. Refractory Bladder Neck Contracture (BNC) and VUAS

Bladder-wall Y-V reconstruction is a reported option for **selected refractory BNC or VUAS** after endoscopic management. The two-or-more failed-treatment threshold described in one 18-patient BNC cohort is that study's enrollment criterion, not a universal definition of refractory disease.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

#### Technique

A Y-shaped incision is made on the anterior bladder wall, with the stem extending through the contracture into healthy urethra. The V-shaped bladder flap is advanced distally to widen the bladder neck, and fibrotic tissue is excised.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

| Approach | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Open Y-V plasty** | Midline lower-abdominal exposure; full-thickness Y incision through the contracture into healthy bladder; V-flap advanced; layered closure |
| **Extraperitoneal laparoscopic modified Y-V** | Barbed-suture approach reported; the cited technical article does not establish a controlled leak-rate or operative-time advantage over open surgery<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Robotic Y-V plasty (RAYV / RYVBNR)** | Transperitoneal robotic approach; **Firefly (ICG) fluorescence** localizes the contracture in the scarred field; median operative time ~240 min<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **Endoscopic Y-V-like mucosal realignment** | A distinct transurethral incision/transverse-mucosal-realignment technique in 19 patients, not a bladder-wall Y-V flap: **89%** anatomic success after one procedure and **100%** after a second at median six-month follow-up<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |

#### Outcomes

| Outcome | Range across series |
|---|---|
| **Open Y-V plasty success** | **100% freedom from further instrumentation** in one 18-patient post-transurethral-prostatectomy BNC series (mean follow-up 14.8 mo); not a cross-etiology durability estimate<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **Robotic Y-V plasty success** | **100%** at median 8 mo in 7 patients (Granieri)<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>; **83.3%** at 24 mo in 30 patients (Abo Youssef RAYV) with IPSS 17 → 6, PVR 90 → 0 mL, Qmax 7.4 → 17 mL/s<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **Continence** | Definitions and baseline function vary; no pooled 87–90% preservation estimate is established by these small, mixed series<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **Persistent storage symptoms** | **61%** in the same 18-patient post-transurethral-prostatectomy cohort; higher BMI and baseline IPSS predicted postoperative OAB-V8 change in its regression model<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |

Persistent storage symptoms can coexist with an anatomically patent outlet; their cause in an individual patient requires assessment rather than being assigned automatically to detrusor pathology.

### 2. Foley Y-V Pyeloplasty for Ureteropelvic Junction Obstruction

The **Foley Y-V pyeloplasty** is a **non-dismembered** technique reported for selected UPJ obstruction:<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

- A Y-shaped incision is made with the stem crossing the stenotic UPJ and the arms extending onto the renal pelvis
- The resulting V-flap of pelvic wall is advanced across the obstruction
- In one randomized 25-versus-25 laparoscopic comparison, reported success was **86% for Y-V versus 95% for Anderson-Hynes** over about 26 months; the difference was not statistically significant and the trial does not prove equivalence across all UPJ anatomy<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- Particularly valuable for patients with a **small or intrarenal pelvis** where dismembered pyeloplasty is technically difficult<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- A modification extending the cranial limbs in an elliptic fashion allows simultaneous reduction of a hydronephrotic pelvis<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- A crossing vessel changes operative planning but is not, by itself, an absolute exclusion: crossing vessels were more common in the Y-V arm of one comparative trial, while the cited 54-patient series selected technique by vessel course and pelvic anatomy<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

See [Pyeloplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/pyeloplasty) for the full technique page.

### 3. Y-V Meatoplasty for Meatal Stenosis

Y-V meatoplasty has been described for **post-hypospadias meatal stenosis**:<sup>[[20]](#ref20)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

- A small inverted V-flap is elevated at the meatal tip
- The stenotic ring is excised
- The flap is advanced into the widened meatus, **interrupting the circumferential scar line**
- A single-center retrospective 57-patient series reported symptom relief in **55/57 (96.5%)** with median 4-year follow-up; six patients were followed for less than one year<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

### 4. Y-V Glanuloplasty in Distal Hypospadias Repair

Y-V glanuloplasty has been used as part of distal hypospadias repair, where a Y-shaped glans incision is converted to a V to accommodate the advanced urethra.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

## Indications Across the GU Tract

- **Selected refractory BNC** after endoscopic treatment; two previous failures was an enrollment threshold in one cohort, not a universal rule
- **VUAS** after radical prostatectomy
- **Bladder-neck stenosis** after TURP or holmium enucleation
- **Selected UPJ obstruction** with anatomy suitable for Foley Y-V pyeloplasty, including small or intrarenal pelvis in reported series
- **Selected post-hypospadias meatal stenosis** (Y-V meatoplasty)
- **Distal hypospadias** as part of glanuloplasty
- Concurrent stress incontinence requires a separate [AUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) assessment; these Y-V series do not establish a routine combined-operation pathway

---

## Contraindications and Caveats

- **Active infection** at the operative site
- **Extensive bladder-neck calcification** — may require adjunctive debridement
- **Heavily irradiated field** — consider [omental flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/omental) interposition at the time of repair
- **Crossing vessels at the UPJ** — assess vessel position and whether transposition or dismemberment is needed; the cited studies do not support a blanket contraindication to Y-V<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- **Patient expectation management** — 61% persistent storage symptoms in one small BNC cohort despite anatomical success; determine their mechanism rather than assuming detrusor pathology<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

---

## Technical Pearls

- **Extend the Y well into healthy tissue** — V-flap advancement only works if the triangle has perfusion and lax tissue beyond the stenotic segment
- **Incision depth is site-specific** — bladder-wall Y-V and superficial cutaneous scar release use different tissue planes; avoid applying a universal full-thickness rule across organs
- **Robotic ICG fluorescence** was used for localization in Granieri's seven-patient series; an effect on the learning curve has not been established<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Concurrent vs. staged AUS** requires assessment of outlet patency and continence; no universal timing or combined-procedure recommendation is established by these Y-V series
- The multiple-cutaneous-Y-V model's *x*-dependent length gain is a teaching aid for that geometry, not an instruction to maximize advancement without regard to blood supply or closure tension<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Summary of Advantages Across Applications

- May preserve local tissue continuity in selected designs
- Provides a useful geometric model for advancement, subject to anatomy and tension
- Avoids graft harvest in selected repairs
- Repeatable when the initial result is insufficient
- Reported through open, laparoscopic and robotic approaches; endoscopic transverse mucosal realignment is a distinct Y-V-like procedure
