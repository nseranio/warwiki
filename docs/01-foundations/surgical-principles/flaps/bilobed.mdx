# Bilobed Flap

A bilobed flap is a local double-transposition flap: one lobe transfers into a defect and a second lobe transfers into the donor site created by the first. It redistributes local-tissue movement across sequential transpositions. This page is a source-bounded design overview; it does not provide patient selection, geometric templates, operative instruction, or outcome expectations.

## Design concept

Bilobed flaps have a shared pivot region and two adjacent lobes. Historical and modern descriptions differ in geometry, but the commonly described modification reduces the overall arc of rotation compared with early designs. The relationship among defect, lobe size, pivot placement, tissue laxity, and adjacent structures determines whether a particular design is suitable.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The technique is best known for selected cutaneous defects, especially in nasal reconstruction. Its applicability outside that setting depends on local vascular anatomy, tissue characteristics, defect geometry, and reconstructive goals.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## GU and perineal context

The term “bilobed” can also describe specialized flap designs used in pelvic, perineal, or genital reconstruction. For example, a small case series described a bilobed gracilis modification for complex perineal defects. That limited evidence does not establish an indication, a comparative advantage, or a reproducible technique for general use.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

Dedicated, current source review is required before using bilobed concepts to guide vulvar, perineal-body, genital, oncologic, or gender-affirming reconstruction.
