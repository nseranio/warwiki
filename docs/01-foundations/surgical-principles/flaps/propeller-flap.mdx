# Propeller Flap

The Tokyo consensus defines a propeller flap as an island flap reaching the recipient site through axial rotation. Its classification includes subcutaneous-pedicled, perforator-pedicled, and supercharged forms; rotation is described from 90 to 180 degrees. This is a terminology and evidence-context overview—not operative, flap-selection, or defect-management guidance.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Reported GU reconstruction contexts

Han and colleagues retrospectively reported 10 consecutive penoscrotal reconstructions with an internal-pudendal-artery-perforator propeller flap from the gluteal fold. This is a small, single-technique series and does not establish universal performance, selection, or aesthetic claims.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Schulster and colleagues reported a single posterior-thigh propeller-flap perineal-urethrostomy revision. It is a case report and should not be read as a standard revision procedure.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For region-specific small-series evidence, see [PMTP Propeller Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/pmtp-propeller), [IPAP Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/ipap), and [Lotus Petal Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/lotus-petal).

## Evidence boundaries

The accessible source package for this recovery consists of the terminology consensus, one 10-patient retrospective penoscrotal series, and one urethrostomy-revision case report. It does not support retaining inherited geometry rules, imaging or dissection requirements, complication or venous-congestion protocols, comparative tables, universal indications, reconstructive algorithms, recommendations, or postoperative protocols.
