# Posteromedial Thigh Perforator (PMTP) Propeller Flap

The **posteromedial thigh perforator (PMTP) propeller flap** is a **perforator-based island flap that rotates up to 180° on a single pedicle** to cover extensive perineal defects. It belongs to the broader family of **propeller flaps** — perforator-based flaps in which an isolated perforator serves as the pivot point, allowing the island of skin and subcutaneous tissue to rotate like a propeller blade to reach a distant defect.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The PMTP is a modern workhorse for **extensive perineal and perineoscrotal reconstruction** where regional fasciocutaneous advancement is insufficient and a free flap is undesired.

---

## Vascular Basis

- **Source vessel:** perforator branch of the **deep femoral artery (profunda femoris)**
- Perforator emerges through the posterior or posteromedial thigh muscles (adductor magnus, semimembranosus) to reach the skin
- **Single dominant perforator** serves as the pivot for the propeller rotation
- Preoperative mapping with **handheld Doppler** or **CT angiography** is recommended to identify the most reliable perforator

---

## The Propeller Flap Concept

| Element | Description |
|---|---|
| **Skin island** | Elliptical or pennant-shaped; raised from the donor region |
| **Pedicle** | Isolated perforator skeletonized to allow full 180° rotation |
| **Rotation** | The flap "blades" rotate around the pedicle as a propeller — the longer "blade" becomes the flap that reaches the defect |
| **Design principle** | Flap width ≥ defect diameter; length tailored to reach |

Advantages over traditional rotation-advancement flaps:

- **No microvascular anastomosis** required (unlike free flap)
- **Similar tissue** (posteromedial thigh matches perineal skin character)
- **Larger arc of rotation** (up to 180°) than conventional rotation flaps (typically 60–90°)
- **Donor site primarily closed** in most cases

---

## GU / Perineal Applications

- **Extensive perineal defects** after Fournier's gangrene debridement
- **Perineoscrotal reconstruction** where regional flaps are insufficient
- **Radiation necrosis** perineal wounds
- **Post-resection defects** from extensive pelvic or vulvar tumor surgery
- **Abdominoperineal resection** wound dehiscence or delayed coverage

Average flap size in the Kwon 2021 series: **256.5 cm² (range 136–400 cm²)** — substantially larger than most regional perineal options.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Harvest

### Preoperative mapping

- **Handheld Doppler** to identify perforators along the posteromedial thigh
- **CT angiography** for complex cases or reoperative fields
- Mark the **dominant perforator location** — this becomes the pivot

### Design

- Flap designed as an **eccentric ellipse** around the perforator
- **Long axis** oriented so the longer "blade" will reach the defect after 180° rotation
- Flap width sized to the defect plus closure margin

### Dissection

1. Incise skin and subcutaneous tissue circumferentially around the designed flap
2. **Identify the perforator** at the pivot point and carefully skeletonize it — preserve a 2–3 cm length of perforator for rotation clearance
3. Elevate the flap in the **suprafascial or subfascial plane** depending on bulk requirements
4. **Rotate 180°** around the perforator — the longer blade swings into the perineum
5. **Inset tension-free** into the perineal defect
6. **Donor site closed primarily** if flap width ≤ ~10 cm; otherwise STSG

---

## Outcomes

**Kwon 2021 series (extensive perineal defects):**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- **100% flap survival**
- **No major complications**
- Average flap size 256.5 cm² (136–400 cm²)
- All donor sites closed primarily
- Patient satisfaction at follow-up (average 22.4 months)

**Scaglioni 2015 case report (perineoscrotal):**<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- Complete survival
- Replaced defect with tissue of similar character (posteromedial thigh skin)
- No microvascular anastomosis required

---

## Advantages

- **180° rotation** — greatest arc of any regional perineal fasciocutaneous option
- **Perforator isolation** allows long pedicle with free geometric positioning
- **Avoids microvascular anastomosis** — shorter operative time and no anticoagulation requirement
- **Similar tissue** (posteromedial thigh matches perineal skin character)
- **Large flap size** — routinely covers defects that would otherwise require free tissue transfer
- **Donor site primarily closed** in most cases

---

## Limitations

- **Perforator dissection learning curve** — dissection of a skeletonized perforator is unforgiving; venous congestion and flap loss with perforator compromise
- **Preoperative mapping essential** — anatomic variability of the posteromedial thigh perforators
- **Donor closure limit** — very wide flaps may require STSG
- **Not compatible with prior posteromedial thigh surgery** — prior hernia surgery, thigh liposuction, or trauma may have compromised perforators

---

## Related Propeller Flaps in GU Reconstruction

The PMTP is the best-characterized propeller flap for perineal work, but several related perforator propeller flaps are described for overlapping indications:

| Flap | Perforator source | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **PMTP (this page)** | Deep femoral a. perforator (profunda femoris) | Extensive perineal defects |
| **DEPAP** (Deep External Pudendal Artery Perforator) | Deep external pudendal a. | Fournier's perineal defects including immunocompromised / oncologic patients |
| **SCIP propeller** | Superficial circumflex iliac a. | Perineoscrotal defects up to 22 × 10 cm; rotation 150–180° |
| **EPAP** (External Pudendal Artery Perforator) | Superficial external pudendal a. | Circumferential penile shaft coverage |

---

## Technical Pearls

- **Skeletonize the perforator conservatively** — leave 2–3 cm of soft tissue cuff around the vessel to preserve venous drainage during rotation
- **Rotate carefully** — torsional perforator kinking is the most common flap-loss mechanism; rotate in stages, checking Doppler between stages
- **Inset under no tension** — the most common early complication is focal necrosis from tight closure
- **Plan the pivot before incision** — Doppler the perforator, mark the rotation arc, confirm the flap will reach
