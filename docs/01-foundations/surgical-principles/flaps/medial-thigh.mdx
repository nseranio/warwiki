# Medial Thigh Fasciocutaneous Flap

The **medial thigh flap** is the **most commonly used regional flap for reconstruction after Fournier's gangrene** and is a workhorse for perineal and scrotal skin coverage.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Based on the **upper medial thigh vascular plexus** (fed by perforators from the obturator, deep external pudendal, and medial femoral circumflex systems), it provides tissue that closely matches perineal and scrotal skin character, within easy pedicled reach of the defect, and with a donor site that primarily closes in most cases.

The medial thigh flap has largely superseded the random-pattern scrotal advancement flap as the first-line regional option for post-Fournier's reconstruction in contemporary practice.

---

## Vascular Anatomy

The upper medial thigh is perfused by a plexus of perforators from multiple named sources:

- **Deep external pudendal artery** (medial branch)
- **Obturator artery** (anterior cutaneous branches)
- **Medial femoral circumflex artery** (cutaneous branches through gracilis)

This **redundant multi-pedicle plexus** accounts for the flap's reliability and tolerance of partial ischemia — even when one contributing vessel is compromised (prior surgery, vasculopathy), the flap usually survives on the remaining contributors.

For perforator-specific variants (DEPAP, PMTP), see [PMTP propeller flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/pmtp-propeller) and the external pudendal artery perforator (EPAP) variant.

---

## Applications in GU Reconstruction

### Primary indication — post-Fournier's gangrene

- **Scrotal and perineal resurfacing** after debridement
- Coverage of **exposed testes, urethra, or deep perineal structures**
- Most commonly used regional flap in Fournier's systematic-review data<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Secondary applications

- **Radical vulvectomy defect coverage** (as a V-Y or rotation advancement variant)
- **Perineal reconstruction** after radiation cystitis necrosis
- **Scrotal reconstruction** after radical penile surgery, Fournier's, or trauma

---

## Harvest

### Design

- Flap outlined **parallel to the natural creases of the upper medial thigh**
- **Length-to-width ratio up to 2:1** when harvested as a random-pattern advancement
- **Higher length** achievable when designed over identified perforators (up to 3:1)

### Dissection

1. Incise skin and subcutaneous tissue down to the adductor fascia
2. Raise the flap in the **fasciocutaneous plane** — above or including the adductor fascia depending on variant
3. Identify and preserve perforators as they enter the undersurface of the flap
4. Mobilize medially/anteriorly toward the defect; tunnel if interposition is needed

### Variants

- **V-Y advancement** — for small defects; the V is outlined on the thigh and closed as a Y after advancement
- **Rotation-advancement** — for larger defects; the flap rotates 45–90° into the perineal defect
- **Island flap** — pedicle is isolated on a single dominant perforator for greater rotational arc (see [PMTP propeller](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/pmtp-propeller) for the 180° variant)

### Closure

- **Donor site** typically closes primarily if flap width ≤ 8–10 cm
- Wider flaps require **STSG** of the donor thigh
- **Tension-free inset** into the perineum is the key endpoint — tension predicts necrosis

## Outcomes

Across Fournier's gangrene reconstruction series:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- **Overall flap loss rate ~1.6%** — the medial thigh flap is the most forgiving regional option
- Partial-thickness necrosis in 5–10%; managed with local wound care
- Donor-site complication (dehiscence, seroma) in 5–15%; usually self-limited
- Functional outcomes: most patients return to ambulation and sexual activity after 6–8 weeks

---

## Advantages

- **Proximity** — in the same operative field as the perineal defect
- **Tissue match** — medial thigh skin is pigmented and pliable, matches perineal skin well
- **Reliable vascular redundancy** — multi-pedicle plexus tolerates partial injury
- **Pedicled** — no microvascular anastomosis required
- **Donor closure usually primary** — minimal additional scarring

---

## Limitations

- **Flap length limited** without perforator isolation (2:1 random-pattern maximum)
- **Obesity** — in heavily adipose thighs, the flap can be too bulky for thin perineal tissue
- **Prior vascular or inguinal surgery** — may have disrupted key perforators
- **Hair-bearing** — may be undesirable if placed adjacent to visible perineal skin
