# Radial Forearm Free Flap (RFFF)

The **radial forearm free flap (RFFF)** is an option for total phallic reconstruction and a selected salvage option for complex urethral defects. Its radial-artery pedicle supplies thin, pliable tissue that can be designed for a neourethra and sensate shaft; hair distribution and eventual sensation vary by patient and design.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Originally described by Yang in 1981 for head-and-neck reconstruction and adapted for penile reconstruction by Chang and Hwang in 1984, it has long-term phalloplasty series. “Gold standard” is a common expert description, not proof of superiority for every patient's goals or donor anatomy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

This page is the foundations-level deep dive on the RFFF as a cross-application reconstructive flap. Site-specific technique pages (phalloplasty, urethroplasty, GAS feminizing or masculinizing) link back here.

---

## Flap Anatomy and Design

The RFFF is a fasciocutaneous free flap based on the radial artery and its venae comitantes, with additional superficial venous drainage via the cephalic vein:<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

| Component | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Arterial pedicle** | Radial artery — large caliber (~2.5 mm), long pedicle (up to 20 cm), consistent anatomy |
| **Venous drainage** | Deep (radial-artery venae comitantes) and superficial (often cephalic) systems. Plan reliable outflow and add another suitable anastomosis when indicated. Fewer than two veins was associated with vascular complications in Falcone's 25-patient series, not a universal threshold: a separate 38-free-flap series had four single-vein anastomoses without vascular events.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[45]](#ref45)</sup> |
| **Sensory innervation** | Lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve (LABCN), medial antebrachial cutaneous nerve (MABCN), and the **posterior antebrachial cutaneous nerve (PABC)** — coapted to recipient nerves (dorsal nerve of clitoris / penis, ilioinguinal) for sensate reconstruction<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Skin** | Thin, pliable skin can support a **tube-within-a-tube** neourethra and shaft; hair-bearing areas need mapping and, when appropriate, hair removal before urethral use. Color match and thickness are patient-dependent.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| **Bone** | A segment of the radius can be included (osteocutaneous variant) for rigidity, though rarely used today given the availability of penile prostheses<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |

## Preoperative Assessment — the Allen Test

Adequate hand perfusion via the ulnar artery must be confirmed before harvest, since the radial artery is sacrificed:<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

| Test | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Clinical Allen Test (CAT)** | Bedside test — compress both arteries, exsanguinate, release ulnar, observe reperfusion. Sufficient (&lt; 6 s), intermediate (6–10 s), or insufficient (&gt; 10 s)<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| **Doppler Allen Test (DAT)** | In an **87-patient oral-cancer reconstruction cohort**, DAT and the intraoperative surgical test agreed while CAT and DAT agreed weakly; Doppler findings changed the planned donor side or flap in 39%. This is that cohort's yield, not the prevalence in all phalloplasty candidates.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| **Practical controversy** | A 237-RFFF retrospective series reported no hand ischemia regardless of clinical test use, but zero events in one cohort does not establish that perfusion assessment can be omitted. An abnormal clinical test may require further evaluation rather than automatic rejection, and ischemia has been reported even after apparently reassuring testing.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |

## Application 1 — Total Phallic Reconstruction (Phalloplasty)

The primary urologic application and the most extensively studied.

### Flap design techniques

- **Chang and Hwang (1984).** Original "tube-within-a-tube" design: a de-epithelialized strip separates inner tube (neourethra, ulnar portion) from outer tube (shaft, radial portion).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- **Gottlieb and Levine (1993).** Modified design with a centrally located neourethra in continuity with a neoglans, eliminating the circumferential meatal suture line that drives meatal stenosis in the Chang-Hwang design.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Spennato 2022.** Direct head-to-head in 45 patients: Chang-Hwang had lower rates of urologic complications (fistulas, stenosis); Gottlieb-Levine had significantly lower rates of partial flap necrosis. Urethral fistulas were the leading cause of revision in both.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

### Combined / modified configurations

- **Modified tube-in-tube with combined flaps (Staud 2021).** RFFF as the inner tube (neourethra) combined with a free ALT flap as the outer tube (shaft) in 21 patients: 100% flap survival at mean 4.4 yr; urethral fistula 38%, stricture 24%.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- **Single-tube phalloplasty without neourethra (Miller 2022).** A two-case technical report for patients who do not seek urethral lengthening, with a cosmetic neomeatal pouch. Avoiding a neourethra changes the complication profile, but this report does not quantify a comparative reduction or prove equivalent aesthetic outcomes.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

### Garaffa 2010 series (UCL, n = 115)

The landmark series:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- **97% flap survival** (3 total losses from venous thrombosis)
- **97% patient satisfaction** with cosmesis and size
- **86% reported phallic sensation**
- Urethral strictures 7.8%; fistulae 17.4%
- After revision surgery, 99% voided from the tip while standing

### Outcomes by indication

#### Gender-affirming surgery (FTM)

RFFF is often favored for phalloplasty when its skin, vascular and nerve properties fit the patient's goals. The evidence comparing donor sites is mostly observational, so preference is not a universal clinical ranking.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

| Study | n | Flap survival | Fistula | Stricture | Satisfaction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Garaffa 2010**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 115 | 97% | 17.4% | 7.8% | 97% |
| **Falcone 2021**<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | 25 | 96% (1 loss) | — | — | — |
| **Spennato 2022**<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> | 45 | 100% | Leading cause of revision | — | — |
| **Staud 2021** (RFFF + ALT)<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> | 21 | 100% | 38% | 24% | — |
| **Ascha 2018**<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> | 149 RFFF vs. 64 ALT | 96.6% vs. 92.2% | Lower with RFFF | Similar | — |

**Netshiongolwe 2025.** Most recent SR comparing RFFF versus pedicled ALT in 769 patients (614 RFFF, 155 pALT):<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- Flap failure: RFFF 1.9% versus pALT 0.6% (p = 0.348)
- Patient satisfaction: RFFF 78% versus pALT 76.2% (NS)
- The abstract reports a shorter operative-time comparison for ALT but gives the RFFF-versus-ALT order as 290 versus 516 minutes, inconsistent with that conclusion; the numerical direction needs full-table verification.
- The authors favor RFFF, while grading comparative evidence low and identifying serious selection bias. The pooled rates do not prove either flap is best for every patient.

Wang 2026 offered a counterpoint in a 57-patient single-center retrospective cohort: RFFF had 80% overall complications versus 60% with ALT and 40.9% with pedicled abdominal flaps (p = 0.023). Urologic complications were 60% in the RFFF group; fistulas were 48%, while the 44% stricture rate was not significantly different across groups (p = 0.24). Technique, staging and patient selection limit causal comparison.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

#### Penile cancer (post-penectomy)

- **Gobbo 2025.** Narrative review of post-penectomy phalloplasty (48 patients across 6 studies). RFFF and ALT are preferred techniques; RFFF offers superior tactile recovery; ALT reduces donor-site morbidity. Urethral stricture or fistula in up to 64.3%, but functional outcomes (standing micturition, sexual activity) are achievable and satisfaction remains high.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
- **Falcone 2016.** 10 patients with traumatic penile amputation (self-amputation, RTA, blast injury, donkey bite, Fournier's). All satisfied with size, cosmesis, and sensation at median 51 months. 6 received IPPs and engaged in penetrative sex; all with complete urethral reconstruction could void and ejaculate from the tip.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

#### Congenital penile inadequacy

- **Spennato 2020.** 23 patients undergoing phalloplasty for non-gender-affirming indications (DSD 34.8%, oncologic 34.8%, automutilation 8.7%, iatrogenic 8.7%, micropenis 8.7%). Total flap necrosis 8.7% (3 of 4 with any necrosis were heavy smokers). Despite high complication rates, satisfying results in experienced centers.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>
- **Massanyi 2013.** 10 exstrophy patients with 100% graft survival; all reported protective and erogenous sensation with ability to achieve orgasm.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>
- **Harris 2021.** Pedicled ALT and RFFF phalloplasty for bladder exstrophy with comparable outcomes.<sup>[[44]](#ref44)</sup>

## Application 2 — Complex Urethral Reconstruction

The RFFF is a salvage option for complex urethral defects when local tissue is unavailable or has failed:

- **Dabernig 2007.** 9 patients (3 post-penectomy, 6 failed GAS urethroplasty): 100% flap survival; 2 stenoses requiring revision (success 7/9 after first operation); no postoperative fistula; satisfactory uroflowmetry. RFFF is a good option "particularly when patients need extensive and long urethral reconstruction".<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>
- **Lee and Khouri 1993.** First description of RFFF for a 23 cm near-total urethral defect after trauma. Single-stage transfer enabled standing urination without strictures and maintained continence.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup>
- **Morrison 1996.** 7 patients with scarred urethras; one with hirsute forearm skin received a prefabricated hairless flap by applying a full-thickness skin graft from a non-hairy site onto the forearm fascia before flap transfer.<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup>
- **Erickson 2006.** RFFF for a posterior urethral stricture with rectourethral fistula after multiple failed repairs; flap anastomosed to the inferior gluteal artery and vein, providing well-vascularized tissue outside the zone of injury.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup>

## Application 3 — Combined Flap Techniques

- **RFFF with dorsalis pedis flap (Ma 2016, n = 14).** RFFF for shaft, dorsalis pedis for glans. No urethral fistula or stenosis; high satisfaction and sensory recovery at 3–6 mo.<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup>
- **RFFF with vascularized appendix (Koshima 1999).** A specialized description using vascularized appendix for neourethra with an osteocutaneous RFFF shaft. Mucosal lining does not establish a low stricture rate across patients.<sup>[[33]](#ref33)</sup>
- **Staged non-microsurgical forearm transfer (Mutaf 2001).** A described reverse-flow island-flap alternative with delayed pedicle division. It is not a free flap or a general substitute for microsurgical planning; assess hand perfusion, reach, staging and team experience for this technique.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

## Sensory Outcomes and Nerve Coaptation

RFFF designs can include cutaneous nerves for coaptation, but the location and quality of eventual sensation vary, and these studies do not establish a uniform sensory advantage over every alternative flap:<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)[[34]](#ref34)</sup>

- LABCN to the dorsal nerve of the clitoris or penis (erogenous)
- MABCN to the ilioinguinal nerve (protective)
- PABC (posterior antebrachial cutaneous): recently proposed as an additional or alternative nerve innervating most of the phallic shaft, where sensation is most desired<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

| Study | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Ma 2011** (definitive innervation study, n = 45, mean 9.1-yr follow-up) | Innervated flaps had significantly better pain, vibratory threshold, temperature, and two-point discrimination in the distal neophallus vs. non-innervated (p &lt; 0.05)<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Küenzlen 2020** (multimodal sensory evaluation, n = 20) | Reported sensation across modalities and orgasm without difficulty in 15 patients at an earlier observation and 20 at a later one. A small observational cohort does not establish a guaranteed recovery trajectory or predict an individual's sexual outcome.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Hu 2024** (prospective) | **75% regained sensation** at average 73 days, with progressive improvement in pressure thresholds<sup>[[34]](#ref34)</sup> |

## Penile Prosthesis Implantation

An optional later stage for patients who desire prosthesis-assisted penetrative function; implantation adds device-specific risks and is not required for every phalloplasty goal:<sup>[[35]](#ref35)[[36]](#ref36)[[37]](#ref37)[[38]](#ref38)[[39]](#ref39)</sup>

- **Three-piece IPPs** are most commonly used; cylinders are wrapped with synthetic material or allografts and bone-anchored to the pubic symphysis.<sup>[[35]](#ref35)</sup>
- **5-yr IPP retention 42–78%.** Lower than in anatomic phallus.<sup>[[35]](#ref35)</sup>
- Cohen 2017: 80% functioning at last follow-up; 70% required reoperation (mean 1.4 revisions).<sup>[[36]](#ref36)</sup>
- Van der Sluis 2019: 32 transgender men; 44% of all implanted prostheses (n = 45) were surgically replaced or removed; uneventful course in only 31.3%.<sup>[[37]](#ref37)</sup>
- Pang 2025 SR (184 AMAB patients post-phalloplasty): complications up to 64.3% (infection ≤ 33.3%, mechanical failure ≤ 28.6%, malposition / migration ≤ 40%); explantation up to 40%; 80–100% satisfied with outcomes.<sup>[[38]](#ref38)</sup>
- Young 2017: prosthesis patients scored higher in orgasmic function, intercourse satisfaction, and overall sexual satisfaction versus phalloplasty-only patients.<sup>[[39]](#ref39)</sup>

## Donor-Site Morbidity

The forearm is the principal disadvantage of the RFFF.

### Motor function and strength

- **Küenzlen 2022.** 20 selected patients: no significant measured grip-strength difference from controls (p = 0.629), and several tested motions were preserved. This small case-control finding does not exclude functional limitation in other patients or with larger donor wounds.<sup>[[40]](#ref40)</sup>
- **Ozturk 2024.** 20 patients with DASH, POSAS, SBSES: minimal-to-no functional impairment on Quick DASH; overall scar satisfaction; worsening DASH sport or music score correlated with wound surface area (p = 0.037).<sup>[[41]](#ref41)</sup>

### Nerve morbidity

- **Peters 2022.** 37 patients: 83.8% had minimal-mild donor-site pain (VAS 0–3); 16.2% had moderate-severe pain with significant QoL impact (sleep interference, sadness, frustration, decreased hopefulness).<sup>[[42]](#ref42)</sup>
- **Küenzlen 2022.** Superficial radial nerve irritation common: hypoesthesia 40%, neuroma 45%; dorsal ulnar nerve injury 0% hypoesthesia.<sup>[[40]](#ref40)</sup>

### Donor-site wound management

Falcone 2023 compared FTSG with Integra plus STSG for donor closure in 34 selected patients:<sup>[[43]](#ref43)</sup>

- The Integra-plus-STSG group had shorter reported healing time (24 versus 30 days), higher complete graft take (93.8% versus 27.8%) and higher cosmetic satisfaction (93.8% versus 66.7%). These retrospective group results do not establish a routine preferred closure for every forearm wound.

A separate 15-patient donor-site comparison (only two STSG-only recipients) found no significant improvement in patient-reported function, patient-reported scar scores or volume deficit with Integra/BTM plus STSG. A blinded clinician score differed across groups, but this very small, nonrandom comparison and the different closure timing prevent a universal matrix recommendation.<sup>[[46]](#ref46)</sup>

## Key Takeaways

The RFFF remains a major phallic-reconstruction option because of thin pliable tissue, a useful pedicle and the opportunity for sensory nerve coaptation. These features do not guarantee the best functional result for every patient. Its principal applications are:

1. **Phalloplasty.** Gender-affirming, post-penectomy, congenital penile inadequacy<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
2. **Complex urethral reconstruction.** Selected salvage option for extensive defects when local tissue is unavailable; no single length threshold defines its use<sup>[[28]](#ref28)[[29]](#ref29)[[30]](#ref30)</sup>
3. **Combined reconstructions.** RFFF neourethra with ALT shaft, or RFFF with dorsalis pedis glans<sup>[[20]](#ref20)[[32]](#ref32)</sup>

The trade-offs include microsurgical complexity, a conspicuous donor site, possible sensory or wound morbidity and urethral complications when lengthening is undertaken. Published fistula and stricture rates vary markedly with technique, selection and follow-up; the Garaffa and Wang rates above cannot be combined into a universal RFFF range or used to rank every alternative. Staging, nerve coaptation and donor closure are individualized rather than proven one-size-fits-all improvements.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[23]](#ref23)[[46]](#ref46)</sup>
