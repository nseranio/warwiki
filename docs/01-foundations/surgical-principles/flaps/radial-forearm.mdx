# Radial Forearm Free Flap (RFFF)

The radial forearm free flap is a fasciocutaneous free-tissue transfer first described in 1981. It is used across reconstructive practice, including phalloplasty and selected complex urethral reconstructions. Its thin, pliable tissue and radial-artery pedicle are useful attributes, but harvest also creates a visible forearm donor site and sacrifices the radial artery.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Scope in GU reconstruction

Published GU reports describe use in phalloplasty and in highly selected long or complex urethral defects. For example, a nine-person 2007 series included three people after penectomy and six after failed prior urethral construction; two developed stenosis requiring revision and no flap failures or postoperative fistulas were reported in that series.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> This is a small, retrospective experience, not a general indication or a comparison with other reconstructive options.

In gender-affirming phalloplasty, a 2025 systematic review comparing RFFF with pedicled anterolateral-thigh flaps included 19 observational studies (769 people). It reported low flap-failure rates in both groups and high reported satisfaction, but rated the evidence low because of observational designs, selection bias, and inconsistent reporting.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The review does not establish a universally preferred flap or a patient-specific treatment recommendation.

## Donor-site evidence boundary

Forearm donor-site wound, functional, sensory, and appearance concerns must be included in preoperative counseling and individualized surgical planning. A 2025 systematic review of full- versus split-thickness graft closure for fasciocutaneous RFFF donor sites found no randomized trials and no conclusive advantage for either graft type for wound, functional, or aesthetic outcomes; certainty was low to very low.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Its studies covered several reconstructive settings, so their findings should not be assumed to determine outcomes after phalloplasty specifically.

## What this page does not establish

This page does not provide harvest geometry, vascular testing thresholds, nerve-coaptation plans, tube-within-a-tube design, urethral reconstruction steps, donor-site dressing protocols, prosthesis implantation protocols, complication rates, or a flap-selection algorithm. Those decisions require procedure-specific assessment, current specialty guidance, and local reconstructive expertise.
