# Surgical Principles

Reconstructive frameworks, universal operative technique, and GU-specific principles. Organized into three groups: **plastic surgery principles** that govern when and why to reconstruct, **general surgical technique** applicable to any discipline, and **GU-specific reconstruction principles** unique to the functional-urology / urogynecology surgeon.

See also: [Surgical Skills](/docs/foundations/surgical-skills) (named-technique repertoire) and [Perioperative Care](/docs/foundations/perioperative-care).

---

The reconstructive-thinking layer. When to reconstruct, how tissue heals, which ladder rung applies, and the tissue-transfer toolkit common to any reconstructive surgeon.

## General Surgical Technique

Universal operative technique applicable to any surgical discipline. Needles, sutures, incisions, closure patterns, bowel anastomosis, and the hand-economy that underpins them all.

<ul className="section-stack">
  <li>
    <a href="/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/exposure" className="section-stack-title section-stack-link">Operative Exposure</a>
    <span className="section-stack-desc">Vaginal, perineal, and abdominal exposure by target — positioning, incisions, tissue-plane dissection, Kocher / Mattox / Cattell–Braasch visceral rotations, transmesenteric approaches, and the zone-based retroperitoneal algorithm in trauma.</span>
  </li>
  <li>
    <a href="/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/incisions-closure" className="section-stack-title section-stack-link">Incisions &amp; Closure</a>
    <span className="section-stack-desc">The incision atlas (midline, Pfannenstiel, Gibson, flank, Chevron, thoracoabdominal, dorsal lumbotomy, perineal), fascial closure, layered vs mass closure, and modern evidence on small-bites fascial technique.</span>
  </li>
  <li>
    <a href="/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/needles" className="section-stack-title section-stack-link">Needles</a>
    <span className="section-stack-desc">Needle anatomy — cutting vs reverse-cutting vs taper vs blunt-taper vs taper-cut tips — and needle-size selection by tissue.</span>
  </li>
  <li>
    <a href="/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/sutures" className="section-stack-title section-stack-link">Sutures</a>
    <span className="section-stack-desc">Materials and selection — absorbable vs non-absorbable, monofilament vs multifilament, strength retention, knot security, and a procedure-by-procedure selection table for GU reconstruction.</span>
  </li>
  <li>
    <a href="/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/barbed-sutures" className="section-stack-title section-stack-link">Barbed Sutures</a>
    <span className="section-stack-desc">Dedicated deep-dive — V-Loc, Quill, Stratafix, MONOFIX biomechanics; the upper-tract pyeloplasty controversy (Liatsikos 83% / Radford 40% failure); overtightening-to-avoid technique; SBO risk from exposed tails; clinical decision framework.</span>
  </li>
  <li>
    <a href="/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/bowel-anastomosis" className="section-stack-title section-stack-link">Bowel Anastomosis</a>
    <span className="section-stack-desc">Hand-sewn and stapled anastomosis configurations (end-to-end, end-to-side, functional end-to-end / GIA-stapled), single- vs double-layer, and the anastomotic-leak-risk factors relevant to urinary diversion with bowel.</span>
  </li>
  <li>
    <a href="/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/surgical-ergonomics" className="section-stack-title section-stack-link">Surgical Ergonomics</a>
    <span className="section-stack-desc">OR-table height, monitor placement, loupes, foot-pedal workflow, and the posture economics that determine whether a 6-hour case leaves the surgeon with a functioning neck and back.</span>
  </li>
</ul>

## GU-Specific Reconstruction Principles

Principles unique to the functional-urology, reconstructive-urology, and urogynecology surgeon. The universal technique above gets applied through these three lenses.

    <span className="section-stack-desc">Overarching framework — tension-free, watertight, well-vascularized, stented where relevant, staged when appropriate — and how these translate across urethra, bladder, ureter, and pelvic floor.</span>
  </li>
  <li>
    <a href="/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/gu-anastomotic-technique" className="section-stack-title section-stack-link">GU Anastomotic Technique</a>

  </li>

  </li>
</ul>
