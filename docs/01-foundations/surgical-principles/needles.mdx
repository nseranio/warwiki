# Needles

Needle selection is inseparable from suture selection and the operative context. Modern surgical needles vary in alloy, surface coating, point geometry, curvature, and attachment to the suture.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> This page provides limited evidence orientation; it is not a manufacturer-specific catalogue, procedural protocol, or tissue-by-tissue selection rule.

## Point geometry and tissue interaction

Needle point geometry affects tissue interaction. A 2025 laboratory study using an artificial skin model found higher tear-through force with the tested taper-point needle than with the tested conventional- and reverse-cutting needles; larger tested suture diameter was also associated with higher tear-through force.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> These results should not be extrapolated to every tissue, needle, suture, or clinical outcome.

## Blunt-tip needles and occupational exposure

A Cochrane review of 10 randomized trials (2,961 surgical staff) found that blunt needles reduced glove perforation and self-reported needlestick injury compared with sharp needles in the operations studied. The trials included abdominal closure, cesarean section, vaginal repair, and hip replacement; higher penetration force was commonly rated more difficult but acceptable.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Whether a blunt-tip needle is appropriate in a specific repair remains an operative and institutional safety decision.

## Dural-puncture needles

For diagnostic or therapeutic dural puncture, a Cochrane review found moderate-quality evidence that atraumatic needles reduce post-dural-puncture headache compared with traumatic needles, without an observed increase in paresthesia or backache in the included studies.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> This evidence concerns dural puncture and does not determine choices for surgical suturing.

## Evidence boundary

The inherited page included manufacturer-code mappings, tissue-specific needle recommendations, curvature and loading instructions, handling protocols, injury-rate claims, biopsy specifications, and physics rules. Those claims require source- and procedure-specific review and are not retained here. This page does not establish a required needle, a safe technique, or a product equivalent.
