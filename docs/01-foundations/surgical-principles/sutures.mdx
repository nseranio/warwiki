# Sutures

Suture selection combines material, structure, absorption profile, diameter, needle, and closure technique. The appropriate choice depends on the tissue, contamination risk, planned healing interval, and the operative context; this page is an evidence orientation, not a procedure-specific protocol or product-selection guide.

## Material, structure, and size

The practical distinctions among absorbable and non-absorbable materials, and between monofilament and multifilament construction, are relevant to handling, tissue interaction, and the period of wound support. A surgical-suture review advises choosing the smallest diameter that accomplishes the repair in order to limit tissue trauma and retained foreign material.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Product-specific strength-retention tables, knot-throw counts, and fixed material-by-operation defaults are intentionally not reproduced here. They require current manufacturer instructions for use, local policy, and case-specific surgical judgment.

## Closure evidence is context-specific

In a Cochrane review of fascial closure after laparotomy, the eligible trials were heterogeneous in patients, incisions, materials, and techniques. The review found a possible reduction in incisional hernia with monofilament versus multifilament sutures, while many other material and technique comparisons did not demonstrate a difference in the assessed outcomes.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> These findings are not a transferable rule for urinary-tract, pelvic, skin, or other reconstructive closures.

## Antibacterial-coated sutures

Evidence should not be translated into a universal routine-use recommendation. A 2022 systematic review and meta-analysis restricted to high-quality randomized trials found no statistically significant surgical-site-infection difference between triclosan-coated and uncoated sutures in the included contaminated and dirty-surgery evidence base.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Applicability to a particular operation, wound class, and institutional protocol should be evaluated locally.

## Barbed sutures

Barbed sutures are a material and closure-method choice rather than a universally interchangeable substitute for conventional sutures. The dedicated [Barbed Sutures](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/barbed-sutures) article contains the bounded discussion of benefits, limitations, and reported-use evidence. A 2015 meta-analysis of urethrovesical anastomosis during robot-assisted radical prostatectomy reported shorter procedural times with unidirectional barbed sutures but no significant differences in the reported leak, catheterization, or continence outcomes; its authors noted limitations in the included evidence.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Evidence boundary

This recovery review read the inherited page and the cited PubMed records/abstracts named below. It did not retrieve or adjudicate full source packages, manufacturer instructions for use, current professional-society guidance, or local protocols. Therefore, it does not establish clinical verification or prescribe a material, size, needle, knot, or closure plan for an individual case.
