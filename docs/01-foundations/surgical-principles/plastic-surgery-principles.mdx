# Plastic Surgery Principles

Plastic and reconstructive surgery addresses defects across varied anatomy and causes. This section connects cross-cutting topics relevant to genitourinary reconstruction; it is not a substitute for procedure-specific planning, specialist assessment, or perioperative decision-making.

## Historical frameworks

Ambroise Paré, Harold Gillies, and D. Ralph Millard are commonly associated with influential historical frameworks for reconstructive surgery. A 2017 editorial revisits those operative principles in their historical context.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> These frameworks are useful for orientation, but individual lists or aphorisms do not establish a contemporary standard of care.

## Contemporary orientation

Reconstructive planning must account for the defect, tissue characteristics, vascularity, donor-site effects, functional aims, prior treatment, and the patient's goals. A review of urogenital flap reconstruction describes the importance of anatomical and functional reconstruction while considering donor-site morbidity and interdisciplinary care.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
