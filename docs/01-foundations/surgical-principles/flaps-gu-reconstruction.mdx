# Flaps in Genitourinary Reconstruction

A flap transfers tissue while preserving a blood supply; a graft is transferred without its original blood supply. Reconstructive choice depends on the defect, recipient tissue, available donor tissue, vascular anatomy, prior treatment, and the operative team's experience. This page is a navigation overview, not a flap-selection algorithm or operative guide.

## Evidence boundary

The inherited page included flap dimensions, vascular-pedicle details, geometric rules, complication estimates, technique steps, outcome comparisons, and condition-specific treatment recommendations. Those claims need source- and patient-specific review and are maintained, where appropriate, on dedicated pages. This overview does not establish a preferred flap, a safe flap dimension, or an indication for a particular operation.

<a id="vascular-basis-of-flap-design"></a>

## Vascular concepts

Perforator anatomy is variable at the individual level. The perforasome literature describes direct and indirect connections between adjacent perforator territories and emphasizes the relevance of vascular anatomy to flap planning.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> A review of common GU flaps notes the continuing role of vascularized tissue in complex urethral reconstruction, particularly after failed repair or in obliterative strictures.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For Fournier's gangrene reconstruction, a 2026 systematic review identified medial thigh, pudendal thigh, anterolateral thigh, and gracilis flaps as commonly reported options. The review describes heterogeneous evidence and limited standardized outcome reporting; it should not be read as a selection algorithm.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
