# Flaps in Genitourinary Reconstruction

A flap is a unit of tissue transferred with its **native blood supply preserved**, distinguishing it from a graft which is transferred without one. Flaps are selected when the recipient site is inadequately vascularized for graft take, when bulk or structural support is required, or when the defect is too large or complex for simpler reconstruction.

This page serves as a reference database for flaps commonly used in genitourinary and pelvic reconstruction.

---

## Flap Classification Overview

Flaps are classified along several intersecting axes:

| Axis | Categories |
|---|---|
| **Blood supply** | Random (subdermal plexus, no named vessel) · Axial (named artery along flap axis) · Perforator (named perforating vessel through muscle/fascia) |
| **Tissue composition** | Cutaneous · Fasciocutaneous · Myocutaneous · Adipofascial · Omental |
| **Movement** | Advancement · Rotation · Transposition · Interpolation |
| **Transfer method** | Pedicled (continuous vascular connection) · Free (microvascular anastomosis at recipient site) |

In GU reconstruction, **fasciocutaneous** and **myocutaneous** flaps predominate. Most pedicled flaps used in perineal and pelvic reconstruction are **axial**, based on named vessels with reliable anatomy.

---

## Geometric Plasty Techniques

Several reconstructive maneuvers rearrange local tissue through defined geometric patterns rather than moving tissue from a distant donor. These are **part of the flap toolkit** and appear repeatedly in GU reconstructive practice:

| Technique | Principle | Primary GU role |
|---|---|---|
| **[Y-V plasty](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/y-v-plasty)** | Y-shaped incision → V-shaped closure; advances triangular flap into contracted segment to **lengthen** perimeter | Refractory bladder neck contracture, VUAS after radical prostatectomy |
| **V-Y advancement** | V-shaped incision → Y-shaped closure; advances triangular flap to **cover** a defect | Vulvar reconstruction (pubolabial, medial thigh, gluteal V-Y); scrotal and perineal coverage |
| **Z-plasty** | Two transposed triangular flaps; lengthens along an axis and breaks scar contractures | Scrotal/penile scar contracture release |
| **Rhomboid (Limberg) flap** | Rhomboid-shaped rotation flap; random vascularization | Small vulvar defects; designed to natural tension lines |
| **Bilobed flap** | Two-lobed transposition flap; distributes tension across two donors | Vulvar / perineal reconstruction (bilobed pudendal artery perforator flap); modified [bilobed gracilis](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/gracilis) for extensive perineal defects |
| **[Propeller flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/pmtp-propeller)** | Perforator-based island that **rotates up to 180°** around a single pedicle | Extensive perineal coverage (PMTP, DEPAP, SCIP propeller, EPAP); avoids free flap |
| **Lotus petal flap** | Multiple petal-shaped fasciocutaneous flaps based on distinct pudendal territories | Vulvovaginal reconstruction after oncologic resection |

**Key insight:** The Y-V plasty is frequently forgotten as a "flap" because the tissue doesn't travel — but it is the operation of choice for refractory bladder neck contracture and deserves the same attention as any distant-tissue flap.

## Flap Detail

### Pedicled Penile / Preputial Skin Flaps

→ Dedicated page: [Pedicled Penile / Preputial Skin Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/penile-preputial)

Local pedicled skin flaps harvested from the penile shaft or prepuce, based on the dartos fascia vascular plexus supplied by branches of the superficial external pudendal artery.

**Key properties:**
- Non-glabrous preputial skin is the preferred donor — closely matches urethral epithelium in surface properties, withstands the wet urinary environment
- Hair-bearing penile shaft skin is **not appropriate** for use within the urethral lumen
- Dartos fascia pedicle provides robust axial blood supply and allows mobilization as an island flap

**GU applications:** Anterior urethral reconstruction (Orandi dorsal onlay, Quartey island preputial flap), hypospadias repair (Duckett transverse preputial island flap), penile shaft resurfacing

**Limitations:** Requires intact, unscarred penile/preputial skin; limited donor availability after circumcision; not available in reoperative or radiation-compromised fields

---

### Radial Forearm Free Flap (RFFF)

→ Dedicated page: [Radial Forearm Free Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/radial-forearm)

A workhorse free flap for complex urethral and penile reconstruction, based on the radial artery with venous drainage via paired venae comitantes and/or the cephalic vein.

**Key properties:**
- Thin, pliable fasciocutaneous tissue — ideal for neotubularization
- Can be made **sensate** by including the lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve (LACN), anastomosed to the dorsal nerve of the penis — critical for erogenous sensation in phalloplasty
- Long pedicle (up to 20 cm), allowing anastomosis to external pudendal or femoral vessels

**GU applications:**
- **Phalloplasty:** The most commonly used flap; the tube-within-tube design creates the neophallus and neourethra simultaneously from a single forearm skin paddle
- **Neourethroplasty:** Long-segment anterior urethral reconstruction when local tissue is unavailable or failed
- **Penile resurfacing:** After oncologic resection

**Limitations:** Visible forearm donor scar (can be covered with STSG); risk of cold intolerance, sensory loss in thumb web space (superficial branch radial nerve); Allen's test must confirm ulnar collateral circulation; thin flap may lack bulk for large perineal defects

---

### Gracilis Flap

→ Dedicated page: [Gracilis Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/gracilis)

A Type II Mathes-Nahai muscle flap (one dominant pedicle, one minor pedicle) based on the **medial circumflex femoral artery**, a branch of the profunda femoris. The dominant pedicle enters the muscle ~8–10 cm from the pubic tubercle.

**Key properties:**
- Can be harvested as muscle-only, myocutaneous, or musculocutaneous with a reliable skin paddle over the proximal medial thigh
- Expendable muscle — loss produces minimal functional deficit
- Can be harvested pedicled (perineal reach) or free (microsurgical transfer with neurotization for dynamic sphincter reconstruction)
- Bilateral gracilis flaps available when single flap is insufficient

**GU applications:**
- **Perineal reconstruction** after APR, pelvic exenteration, Fournier's gangrene
- **Vaginal reconstruction** (neovagina) — bilateral pedicled gracilis myocutaneous flaps (Singapore flap is a fasciocutaneous variant of this concept)
- **Scrotal reconstruction** — myocutaneous or skin paddle variants
- **Sphincter reinnervation** — free gracilis with obturator nerve-to-pudendal nerve coaptation for urinary/fecal sphincter reconstruction

**Limitations:** Skin paddle reliability can be variable (perforators not always robust to distal paddle); short pedicle limits reach in some cases; skin paddle in medial thigh may not match perineal skin color

---

### Anterolateral Thigh (ALT) Flap

→ Dedicated page: [Anterolateral Thigh (ALT) Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/anterolateral-thigh)

A large, versatile flap based on the **descending branch of the lateral circumflex femoral artery** (LCFA), with perforators passing through or around the vastus lateralis muscle to supply the lateral thigh skin.

**Key properties:**
- Can be harvested as perforator-only (fasciocutaneous), myocutaneous (with VL), or chimeric (multiple tissue components on one pedicle)
- Thickness varies markedly with patient habitus — thin in lean patients (ideal for tube construction), bulky in obese patients (useful for dead space obliteration)
- Long pedicle (8–12 cm), large skin paddle (up to 25×15 cm)
- Can be raised as pedicled (reaching perineum via subcutaneous tunnel) or free flap

**GU applications:**
- **Large perineal/vaginal defects** after pelvic exenteration or extensive tumor resection
- **Pelvic floor reconstruction** with bulk
- **Phalloplasty** — used at select centers, particularly when RFFF is unavailable (prior harvesting, inadequate Allen's test)

**Limitations:** Perforator anatomy variable (~87% have reliable musculocutaneous or septocutaneous perforators but mapping preferred); bulky in obese patients when thin flap needed; motor branch to VL must be preserved if VL function is important

---

### VRAM (Vertical Rectus Abdominis Myocutaneous Flap)

→ Dedicated page: [VRAM Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/vram)

A pedicled myocutaneous flap based on the **inferior epigastric artery** (a branch of the external iliac artery), raised on a vertical skin paddle overlying the rectus abdominis muscle.

**Key properties:**
- Large volume of well-vascularized tissue on a reliable, dominant pedicle
- The inferior epigastric pedicle allows the flap to reach the perineum through the pelvis or via subcutaneous tunnel
- The rectus muscle provides bulk for obliterating the dead space left after pelvic exenteration or radical cystectomy
- Prior abdominal surgery or inferior epigastric vessel ligation (after TRAM, DIEP, prior laparotomy) may compromise pedicle

**GU applications:**
- **Pelvic floor and perineal reconstruction** after radical cystectomy, pelvic exenteration, APR
- **Vaginal reconstruction** (neovagina after total pelvic exenteration)
- **Perineal wound** after Fournier's gangrene involving the lower abdominal wall
- **Bladder exstrophy reconstruction**

**Limitations:** Donor site weakness/hernia risk; contraindicated if inferior epigastric vessels ligated (prior TRAM/DIEP, prior iliac vessel surgery); not preferred when abdominal wall integrity is critical (e.g., planned future ostomy)

---

### Omental Flap

→ Dedicated page: [Omental Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/omental)

The greater omentum is mobilized on its gastroepiploic vascular arcade (right or left **gastroepiploic artery**) and transferred to the pelvis or perineum via transabdominal or laparoscopic/robotic approach.

**Key properties:**
- Abundant lymphatic and vascular tissue; promotes neovascularization and immune surveillance
- **Porous** structure allows fluid (hematoma, seroma, urine) to drain through the omentum — prevents accumulation of dead space that would otherwise impair graft take
- Can be harvested pedicled (most common in GU reconstruction) or as a free flap with omental vessels anastomosed microsurgically

**GU applications:**
- **BMG ureteroplasty:** Wrapped around the buccal mucosa graft as a vascularized backing, promoting inosculation and preventing fluid accumulation
- **Urethrovaginal / vesicovaginal fistula:** Interposed between repaired bladder/urethra and vaginal closure as a vascularized barrier
- **Pelvic dead space obliteration:** After exenteration or radical cystoprostatectomy, filling the pelvic basin to prevent fluid collections and bowel prolapse
- **Radiation wounds:** Introduces non-irradiated, well-vascularized tissue into a hypovascular radiation field

**Limitations:** Requires abdominal access (open or laparoscopic); prior abdominal surgery may have shortened or compromised the omental pedicle; omentum may be insufficient (post-omentectomy, prior adhesions)

---

### Peritoneal Flap

→ Dedicated page: [Peritoneal Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/peritoneal)

A thin, highly vascular flap of parietal or visceral peritoneum, harvested robotically or laparoscopically and used as an onlay or wrap in ureteral reconstruction.

**Key properties:**
- Thin, easily mobilized, readily available during any abdominal/pelvic procedure
- Vascular supply derived from underlying mesenteric and peritoneal perforators
- Mesothelial surface may provide a scaffold for ureteral re-epithelialization

**GU applications:**
- **Peritoneal ureteral flap (Nezhat technique):** Tubularized peritoneum used as an onlay or tubular replacement for segment ureteral defects — described for both open and robotic approaches
- **Ureteral wrap:** Pedicled peritoneum wrapped circumferentially around a diseased ureteral segment (ureterolysis for retroperitoneal fibrosis) to prevent re-encasement
- **Mitrofanoff reconstruction adjunct**

**Limitations:** Peritoneal flap ureteroplasty has limited long-term data compared to BMG ureteroplasty; mesothelium may not provide durable epithelialization; not preferred in prior pelvic surgery with extensive adhesions

---

### Vastus Lateralis Flap

→ Dedicated page: [Vastus Lateralis Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/vastus-lateralis)

A pedicled or free myocutaneous/muscle-only flap harvested from the vastus lateralis, based on the same **lateral circumflex femoral artery** as the ALT flap (descending branch).

**Key properties:**
- Large muscle bulk — excellent for obliterating deep pelvic dead space
- More reliable motor nerve anatomy than the ALT in patients with large amounts of subcutaneous fat
- Can be combined with an ALT skin paddle as a chimeric flap on the same LCFA pedicle

**GU applications:**
- **Perineal and gluteal defects** after pressure sore excision, tumor resection
- **Pelvic dead space** obliteration when VRAM is unavailable or prior abdominal surgery precludes its use
- **Salvage reconstruction** when gracilis and ALT territories are compromised

**Limitations:** Functional deficit in quadriceps (knee extension weakness); less commonly used than gracilis or ALT in pure GU reconstruction; requires expertise in thigh dissection

---

### SCIP Flap (Superficial Circumflex Iliac Artery Perforator)

→ Dedicated page: [SCIP Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/scip)

An emerging free perforator flap based on the **superficial circumflex iliac artery (SCIA)**, a branch of the femoral artery, with the donor scar concealed in the natural groin crease.

**Key properties:**
- Extremely low donor site morbidity — no muscle sacrifice, scar hidden in the inguinal fold
- Very thin and pliable tissue, ideal for urethral tube construction
- Short pedicle (3–5 cm) — requires careful recipient vessel selection
- Anatomy can be variable; preoperative mapping with Doppler or CTA is recommended

**GU applications:**
- **Urethral reconstruction:** Thin, pliable skin suitable for tubularization as a neourethra
- **Penile shaft resurfacing**
- **Phalloplasty:** Emerging use at specialized centers as an alternative to RFFF, with comparable tissue characteristics and superior donor site concealment

**Limitations:** Short pedicle demands recipient vessels close to the groin (femoral, external pudendal); steep learning curve for perforator dissection; limited long-term data in GU-specific applications

---

### Blandy Flap

→ Dedicated page: [Blandy Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/blandy)

A pedicled fasciocutaneous flap of penile or scrotal skin raised on the dartos fascia pedicle (perforators from the superficial external pudendal artery and perineal branches of the internal pudendal artery), described by John Paul Blandy for perineal urethral reconstruction and urethrostomy creation.

**Key properties:**
- Local tissue — no distant donor site required
- Dartos fascia provides reliable axial blood supply as an island flap
- Can be configured as a transverse or longitudinal island to reach the perineum

**GU applications:**
- **Perineal urethrostomy** — creating a permanent perineal urinary stoma using a pedicled island of scrotal or perineal skin to achieve a tension-free, well-vascularized stoma
- **Anterior urethral reconstruction** — as an onlay or inlay flap in the perineal or bulbar urethra

**Limitations:** Limited tissue volume; unavailable in circumcised patients with limited preputial/penile skin; not suitable in irradiated or fibrotic genital skin

---

### Martius Flap

→ Dedicated page: [Martius Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/martius)

A pedicled adipofascial flap of the labial fat pad (bulbocavernosus fat pad) based on the **posterior labial artery** (branch of the internal pudendal artery) or the **anterior labial artery** (branch of the external pudendal artery), named after Heinrich Martius who described it in 1928.

**Key properties:**
- Well-vascularized, pliable adipofascial tissue interposed between two repaired tissue planes
- Based posteriorly (posterior labial a.) for maximum reach toward the vagina and bladder neck; based anteriorly (anterior labial a.) for more anterior urethral defects
- Bilateral Martius flaps available when a single flap is insufficient
- Brings in non-scarred, non-irradiated, highly vascularized tissue to a fibrotic or radiated field

**GU applications:**
- **Urethrovaginal fistula repair** — the Martius flap is the standard interposition layer between urethral and vaginal repairs to prevent re-fistulization
- **Vesicovaginal fistula repair** — interposition layer, particularly in complex or recurrent fistulas
- **Female urethral reconstruction** — vascularized backing for urethral onlay or tube reconstruction in female urethroplasty
- **Radiation-associated fistula** — introducing non-irradiated tissue into a chronically hypovascular field

**Limitations:** Applicable only in female patients; labial disfigurement/asymmetry at donor site; bilateral harvest has more significant donor morbidity; limited tissue volume for large defects

## Flap Selection Principles by Indication

### Urethral strictures (anterior urethra)

- **Vascularization is paramount** — all penile flaps depend on dartos vascularity<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Onlay is superior to tubularized** — penile circular fasciocutaneous flap: **13% recurrence (onlay) vs. 58% recurrence (tubularized)** in the McAninch-Morey series<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Failed urethroplasties and obliterative strictures** — vascularized flaps preferred over grafts because the recipient bed is inadequate for graft take
- **Preserve erectile function** — IIEF-5 maintained at 23.5–23.9 in contemporary penile flap series<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### Bladder neck contracture / VUAS

- **≥2 failed endoscopic treatments** define the threshold for open or robotic [Y-V plasty](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/y-v-plasty)
- **Robotic/laparoscopic approaches** offer reduced invasiveness with similar success to open (90–100%)<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Firefly (ICG) fluorescence** is useful for localizing the contracture in the scarred post-prostatectomy field
- **Novel transurethral endoscopic Y-V** variant described with 89% first-pass success<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

### Vesicovaginal and urethrovaginal fistula

- **Simple VVF:** direct repair without flap interposition
- **Complex / radiation-induced VVF:** [Martius flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/martius) interposition is standard; [omental flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/omental) as alternative for transabdominal repairs
- **VVF with vaginal wall deficit:** [labia majora fasciocutaneous flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/labia-majora-fasciocutaneous) — the flap provides both fistula interposition *and* vaginal wall tissue<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Recurrent VVF after failed Martius:** escalate to labia majora fasciocutaneous, gracilis myocutaneous, or peritoneal/omental adjunct
- **Single-layer annular vaginal flap** — modified transvaginal technique offering shorter operative time, less blood loss, and no cystostomy; 82.3% first-time success<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

### Perineal / genital coverage (Fournier's, extensive defects)

- **Exposed vital structures** (testes, urethra, vessels) require **immediate flap coverage** — delay risks desiccation and secondary infection
- **Regional flaps overwhelmingly preferred** — systematic review of Fournier's reconstruction shows **overall flap loss of only 1.6%**<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **[Medial thigh flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/medial-thigh)** is the most commonly used regional option — proximity, tissue match, redundant vascular plexus
- **[Singapore / pudendal thigh flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/singapore-pudendal-thigh)** is the most common posterior-perineal / vaginal reconstruction option
- **Perforator propeller flaps** ([PMTP](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/pmtp-propeller), DEPAP, SCIP, EPAP) increasingly favored for **extensive defects** — large coverage without microvascular anastomosis
- **VRAM / ALT** reserved for defects too large or deep for regional coverage
- **Primary indications for flap coverage** (per Fournier's systematic review): exposed structures 52%, functional restoration 39%, cosmesis 4%<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

### Penile shaft coverage

- **Modified bilateral scrotal flap** ("butterfly" design) — restores penile skin length from bilateral scrotal donors<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **External Pudendal Artery Perforator (EPAP) hemi-scrotal flap** — sensate, thin, pigmented coverage of circumferential penile shaft defects with wider rotation arc than conventional scrotal flaps<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **[SCIP flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/scip)** — thin free flap alternative with inguinal donor concealment

### Vulvovaginal reconstruction (oncologic)

- **Small defects:** rhomboid (Limberg) flap or V-Y advancement
- **Moderate defects:** lotus petal or superficial external pudendal artery flaps based on distinct vascular territories
- **Large defects / full vaginal reconstruction:** bilateral [Singapore / pudendal thigh flaps](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/singapore-pudendal-thigh) or [VRAM](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/vram) or [gracilis myocutaneous](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/gracilis)
- **Compound perineal + vulvar defects:** bilobed pudendal artery perforator flap preserves distinct tissue character of urogenital and anal triangles<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
