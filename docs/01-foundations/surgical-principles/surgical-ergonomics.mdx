# Surgical Ergonomics

Work-related musculoskeletal disorders are an important occupational issue for surgeons and interventionalists. A 2018 systematic review and meta-analysis found high and heterogeneous prevalence estimates across procedural specialties and an unmet need for ergonomics education.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Urologic context

Urology spans open, laparoscopic, robotic-assisted, microscopic, and endoscopic work, each with distinct ergonomic demands. A urology-focused review describes this breadth and the limited strength of the current ergonomics evidence base.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The review's practical suggestions should be adapted to the local environment, operator, procedure, and institutional safety policies rather than used as a universal setup protocol.

## Intraoperative microbreaks

In a multicenter cohort study, surgeons and operating-room staff used guided targeted stretching microbreaks of 1.5–2 minutes at 20–40-minute intervals. The study reported improved postprocedure pain scores without a detected difference in operative duration; it was not a randomized trial and does not establish a required microbreak schedule.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
