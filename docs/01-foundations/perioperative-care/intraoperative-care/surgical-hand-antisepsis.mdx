# Surgical Scrub & Hand Antisepsis

Surgical hand preparation reduces organisms on the hands and forearms before sterile gloves are donned. WHO accepts either **a suitable antimicrobial soap-and-water scrub or a validated surgical alcohol-based hand rub**. Gloves and hand antisepsis are complementary barriers.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Choose a Validated Surgical Method

| Method | Execution | Important qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Antimicrobial soap-and-water scrub** | Use the product's technique and duration, covering hands and forearms; rinse and dry with a sterile towel before gloving. | There is no universal first-case five-minute/subsequent-case three-minute rule. WHO describes usual scrub times of 2–5 minutes while directing users to the manufacturer.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Surgical alcohol-based hand rub** | Start with clean, completely dry hands. Apply sufficient product to keep the specified areas wet for the entire validated application time; allow complete drying before gloving. | Use a product validated for **surgical** preparation. Volume, aliquots and duration depend on the formulation and hand/forearm coverage; neither 4–6 mL per aliquot nor 90 seconds applies to every product.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

Do not routinely perform a medicated surgical scrub immediately followed by an alcohol rub. Residual water can impair the rub's activity; WHO advises against combining the two methods sequentially.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Prepare Hands and Protect Skin

- Remove hand/wrist jewelry; keep natural nails short. Scrubbed personnel should not wear artificial nails, extenders or nail polish. Clean visible subungual debris with a nail cleaner under running water.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Avoid scrub brushes; they are unnecessary for effective preparation and can injure skin. Report dermatitis, damaged skin or suspected product allergy and select a compatible preparation with occupational-health/infection-prevention support.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Wash visibly soiled hands with soap and water. After caring for patients with spore-forming pathogens, follow the facility's hand-hygiene protocol. The limited activity of alcohol against spores does **not** justify removing alcohol hand-rub access from the wider care environment.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Complete drying both **before applying an alcohol rub after washing** and **before putting on sterile gloves**. Check compatibility with gloves and facility-approved skin-care products.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Read the Evidence by Outcome

The Cochrane review included 14 trials: four measured SSI and most measured bacterial colony counts on hands. Formulations, timing and trial quality varied, and the review could not establish that one preparation method reliably prevents more SSIs. Some products lowered bacterial counts more than others, but that surrogate difference is not proof of fewer wound infections.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

A later meta-analysis of seven trials involving 764 healthcare workers likewise principally addressed **microbial counts**, not patient infection outcomes. It should not be cited as proof of clinical equivalence between all scrubs and rubs.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> A well-tolerated, validated alcohol rub can simplify practice and reduce irritation; correct execution and adherence to its instructions remain essential.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
