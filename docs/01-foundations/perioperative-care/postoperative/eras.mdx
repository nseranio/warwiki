# Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS)

Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) is a **bundled, evidence-based perioperative care pathway** that treats surgery as a controllable physiologic insult and layers 15–25 interventions across the pre-, intra-, and postoperative phases to minimize that insult. Originally codified by Henrik Kehlet's Copenhagen group for colorectal surgery in the late 1990s, ERAS was formally extended to urology by the **ERAS® Society guideline for radical cystectomy in 2013**,<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> and has since been adapted and revalidated for radical cystectomy, robotic prostatectomy, partial and radical nephrectomy, and major reconstructive procedures.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Compliance with ≥70% of ERAS elements consistently produces the largest reductions in length of stay and morbidity — individual elements matter less than bundled execution.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For reconstructive urologists, the flagship indication is **open radical cystectomy with urinary diversion** — the GU operation with the most robust ERAS evidence base and the largest absolute benefit.

See also: [Analgesia](/docs/foundations/perioperative-care/anesthesia-pain/analgesia), [Nerve Blocks](/docs/foundations/perioperative-care/anesthesia-pain/nerve-blocks), [Antithrombotic Therapy](/docs/foundations/perioperative-care/postoperative/antithrombotic-therapy), [Constipation](/docs/foundations/perioperative-care/postoperative/constipation), [Frailty](/docs/foundations/perioperative-care/preoperative/frailty).

## Preoperative Elements

### Counseling and Education

Structured preoperative counseling — covering the pathway, expected milestones, diet, mobilization, and discharge criteria — reduces anxiety, shortens LOS, and improves compliance with postoperative tasks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> For cystectomy patients, dedicated **stoma siting and stoma-nurse consultation** is non-negotiable.

### Prehabilitation

Targeted 4–6 week preoperative conditioning improves postoperative function:

- **Exercise** — aerobic + resistance training, 3–5× weekly. Measurable improvements in 6-minute walk distance and postoperative recovery in cystectomy and abdominal surgery populations.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Nutrition** — screen with MUST or NRS-2002; pre-op oral nutritional supplements for malnourished patients (albumin &lt;3.0 g/dL, unintentional 10% weight loss). Immunonutrition (arginine/omega-3/nucleotides) is supported by ERAS guidelines for major oncologic surgery but evidence is heterogeneous.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Smoking cessation** — minimum 4 weeks preoperative reduces pulmonary complications.
- **Alcohol cessation** — minimum 4 weeks preoperative.
- **Anemia correction** — IV iron for iron-deficiency anemia; avoid preoperative transfusion if possible (transfusion is an independent predictor of worse oncologic and complication outcomes in cystectomy).

### Medical Optimization

Standard preoperative evaluation (cardiac risk, diabetes, frailty, steroids, immunosuppression — see linked pages) integrated into the ERAS pathway rather than deferred to a separate preadmission visit.

### Fasting and Carbohydrate Loading

- **Clear fluids permitted up to 2 hours before induction** — not midnight.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Solids up to 6 hours** before induction.
- **Complex carbohydrate drink** (e.g., 400 mL maltodextrin) the evening before and 2 hours before induction — reduces preoperative insulin resistance and postoperative nausea. Safe in non-diabetic patients; data in well-controlled type 2 diabetes are reassuring but cautious individualization is warranted.

### Bowel Preparation

- For **radical cystectomy with bowel urinary diversion**, the ERAS® Society guideline and EAU recommend **no routine mechanical bowel preparation**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Evidence shows no reduction in surgical site infection or anastomotic complications with MBP, and MBP causes fluid/electrolyte disturbance and ileus.
- **Selective oral antibiotic prep** (neomycin + erythromycin or neomycin + metronidazole) with or without limited MBP may reduce SSI in some bowel surgery populations but remains debated for cystectomy.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### VTE Prophylaxis — Initiated Preoperatively

Pharmacologic VTE prophylaxis (LMWH) started **preoperatively** or within 12 hours postoperatively in moderate-to-high risk cases; **extended 4 weeks post-discharge for pelvic cancer surgery**, including cystectomy and open pelvic reconstruction.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> See [Antithrombotic Therapy](/docs/foundations/perioperative-care/postoperative/antithrombotic-therapy).

### Antibiotic Prophylaxis

Single dose of appropriate IV antibiotic within 60 minutes of incision; re-dose for prolonged cases or blood loss &gt;1500 mL.

## Intraoperative Elements

### Minimally Invasive Approach Where Feasible

Laparoscopic, robotic, and single-port approaches reduce ileus, pain, and LOS compared with open — this is the single highest-impact intraoperative ERAS element for procedures where MIS is appropriate.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### Regional Anesthesia and Multimodal Analgesia

Opioid-sparing analgesia is a cornerstone element:

- **Thoracic epidural** (traditional for open cystectomy) or **transversus abdominis plane (TAP) block** / **quadratus lumborum block** / rectus sheath block for MIS and selected open cases.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Multimodal baseline:** scheduled acetaminophen + NSAID or COX-2 inhibitor.
- **Adjuncts:** IV lidocaine infusion, IV ketamine, dexmedetomidine for selected patients.
- **Intrathecal morphine** (0.1–0.2 mg) for RARP and selected cases.

### Goal-Directed Fluid Therapy

**Euvolemia, not supernormal hydration.** Target zero weight gain postoperatively. Excess crystalloid — historically common in long cystectomies — causes bowel wall edema, ileus, and pulmonary overload. Use stroke-volume variation or pulse-pressure variation guidance in high-risk cases; lean vasopressor-forward ("balanced") for hemodynamic support rather than volume-forward.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

### Normothermia

Forced-air warming, warmed IV fluids and insufflation gas; target core temperature &gt;36°C throughout the case. Hypothermia increases wound infection, cardiac events, and coagulopathy.

### No Nasogastric Tube (or Early Removal)

Routine prophylactic NGT placement is **not recommended** for abdominal urologic surgery — it delays return of bowel function and increases pulmonary complications. If placed intraoperatively, remove before emergence from anesthesia.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Short-Acting Anesthetics and PONV Prophylaxis

Dual or triple PONV prophylaxis (dexamethasone + 5-HT3 antagonist ± droperidol or scopolamine patch) based on Apfel score. See [Nausea & Vomiting](/docs/foundations/perioperative-care/anesthesia-pain/nausea-vomiting).

### Drain Minimization

Routine pelvic drains are not required after robotic RP or most reconstructive cases. In cystectomy, selective drain placement — removed early (POD 1–3) when output is low and non-chylous/non-urinous.

## Postoperative Elements

### Early Mobilization

**Out of bed on the day of surgery** (POD 0) — even if only sit-to-stand. Ambulate 2–4× daily from POD 1. Early mobilization is independently associated with reduced ileus, VTE, and LOS.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### Early Oral Nutrition

- **Clear liquids on POD 0** for MIS procedures; within 24 h for open cystectomy.
- **Regular diet as tolerated** once liquids are tolerated — do not wait for flatus.
- Oral nutritional supplements between meals if intake &lt;60% of baseline.

### Early Catheter and Drain Removal

- Urethral catheter removal on POD 1 after RARP and partial nephrectomy when uncomplicated.
- Drains removed by POD 2–3 when output is low.

### Opioid-Sparing Postoperative Analgesia

- Scheduled acetaminophen + NSAID (ketorolac IV transitioning to oral ibuprofen, in patients without contraindications).
- Regional catheter infusion (epidural or TAP) for 24–72 h.
- Oral breakthrough opioids at the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration.
- Gabapentinoids are **not** recommended routinely (2024 evidence shows increased sedation and falls without meaningful opioid-sparing effect in many populations).

### Alvimopan for Open Radical Cystectomy

**Alvimopan** (12 mg PO preop, then BID until return of GI function, max 15 doses) — a peripherally-acting μ-opioid antagonist — **accelerates return of bowel function and reduces LOS by ~1 day** after open radical cystectomy in a randomized trial.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> FDA-approved specifically for this indication. Largely superseded in MIS cystectomy, where baseline ileus rates are lower and the absolute benefit narrows.

### Postoperative Nausea and Vomiting Management

Scheduled antiemetics for the first 24 h in high-Apfel-score patients; avoid reflexive opioid escalation.

### Glycemic Control

Target blood glucose 140–180 mg/dL postoperatively; insulin infusion protocols for values &gt;180 mg/dL. Perioperative hyperglycemia increases SSI risk independent of diabetes status.

### Extended VTE Prophylaxis

LMWH continued for **4 weeks post-discharge** after pelvic oncologic surgery (cystectomy, nephrectomy with regional lymphadenectomy, major pelvic reconstruction).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

### Discharge Criteria

Discharge when the patient:

- Tolerates oral intake (solid food)
- Has adequate pain control on oral analgesia
- Has passed flatus (and, for cystectomy, stool)
- Is mobilizing at preoperative baseline
- Has met procedure-specific milestones (stable diversion function, drain removed, etc.)

## Procedure-Specific ERAS Pathways

### Radical Cystectomy — The Flagship ERAS Procedure

Radical cystectomy with urinary diversion is the urologic operation with the most robust ERAS evidence base. **ERAS pathways reduce LOS by 2–4 days, reduce 30-day complications by ~20–30%, and do not increase readmissions** across multiple cohort and trial comparisons.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[11]](#ref11)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

Core cystectomy-specific elements beyond the general pathway:

- **Alvimopan** for open cystectomy.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **No routine MBP.**
- **Goal-directed fluid therapy** — historic over-resuscitation produced ileus and neobladder edema.
- **Early nasogastric tube removal / avoidance.**
- **Metabolic management of ileal conduit / neobladder** — monitor for hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis; sodium bicarbonate supplementation as indicated.
- **Extended 4-week VTE prophylaxis.**
- **Early stoma education** and stoma-nurse pathway.

A 2020 BJUI Compass narrative review consolidated these elements for the contemporary urology audience,<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> and a 2024 EJSO update extended the evidence to oncologic MIS cystectomy cohorts.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

### Robotic Radical Prostatectomy (RARP)

- Increasingly performed as **same-day discharge** in selected ERAS programs. Reported same-day discharge rates exceed 90% in contemporary series, particularly with the SP transvesical approach.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **No pelvic drain** routinely.
- **Opioid-free or low-opioid** discharge is achievable — 96% discharged without opioid prescription in transvesical SP RP.

### Partial and Radical Nephrectomy

- **Same-day discharge** increasingly common for uncomplicated partial nephrectomy at high-volume ERAS centers, particularly with SP-LAA and SARA approaches.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Minimize intraoperative fluids**, especially during clamped-parenchyma partial nephrectomy.
- **TAP/QL blocks** for postoperative analgesia.

### Major Reconstruction (Augmentation, Posterior Urethroplasty, Continent Diversion)

No formal society guideline yet, but ERAS principles apply — opioid-sparing analgesia, no MBP for routine augmentation if bowel prep is not surgically required, early mobilization, early nutrition, and extended VTE prophylaxis for pelvic procedures.

## Compliance and Outcomes — the Bundle Matters More Than Any Element

The consistent finding across urologic and colorectal ERAS literature is that **compliance with the bundle — not adherence to any single element — predicts outcomes**.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[3]](#ref3)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> Key benchmarks:

- **≥70% element compliance** is associated with significantly reduced LOS and complications vs &lt;70%.
- **Pathway audit and feedback** at the institutional level is an independent predictor of compliance.
- **Dedicated ERAS coordinator** roles correlate with higher compliance and better outcomes.

Aggregate benefits in radical cystectomy ERAS programs:<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[11]](#ref11)[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

- LOS reduction: **2–4 days**
- 30-day complication reduction: **~20–30%**
- No increase in readmission
- No compromise of oncologic outcomes
- Measurable cost savings

## Practical Implementation Checklist

For a reconstructive urology service launching or auditing an ERAS pathway:

1. **Dedicated ERAS coordinator** (nurse, APP, or quality-improvement lead).
2. **Written protocol** covering the pre-, intra-, and postoperative elements above.
3. **Patient-facing pathway document** given at the surgical consent visit.
4. **Anesthesia alignment** — standardized opioid-sparing induction, GDFT, regional block plan.
5. **Prospective audit of compliance** with individual elements, reviewed quarterly.
6. **Feedback loop to surgeons, anesthesia, and nursing** — compliance variances drive pathway updates.
