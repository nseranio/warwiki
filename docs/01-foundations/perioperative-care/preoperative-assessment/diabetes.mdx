# Diabetes — Perioperative Management

Diabetes is present in **~20% of patients undergoing major general surgery**, with another **23–60% harboring prediabetes or undiagnosed diabetes**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> For the reconstructive urologist, two consequences matter above all others: **perioperative hyperglycemia is a risk factor for surgical site infection and other postoperative complications** (including concern for prosthetic implant infection), and **it is associated with longer stay and higher mortality**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> This article covers the 2026 ADA perioperative standards, the evidence behind the glycemic targets, and the specific medication-hold decisions that the urology service has to make the day before surgery.

See also: [Cardiovascular Risk](/docs/foundations/perioperative-care/preoperative-assessment/cardiovascular-risk), [ERAS](/docs/foundations/perioperative-care/perioperative-protocols/eras), [Wound Healing](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/wound-healing).

## Why Glucose Control Matters in GU Reconstruction

- **SSI** — in one prospective series of 100 diabetic patients having elective surgery, a glucose &gt;220 mg/dL on POD 1 predicted later nosocomial infection with **87.5% sensitivity but only 33% specificity**. Infection was 2.7 times as frequent (31.3% vs 11.5%) with such a value, and the relative risk for serious infection was 5.7 once minor urinary infections were excluded.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> This is a single small cohort, so use it as a signal rather than a threshold.
- **Prosthetic infection** — HbA1c tracks penile implant infection risk (details below). Optimize glycemia as one part of infection prevention alongside technique, skin and urine assessment, and antimicrobial planning. No universal HbA1c cancellation threshold is established.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- **Wound healing** — hyperglycemia impairs collagen cross-linking, fibroblast function, and neutrophil chemotaxis.
- **Anastomotic healing** — diabetes and hyperglycemia are thought to impair anastomotic healing (microvascular disease, collagen and leukocyte dysfunction); reconstructive-specific data are mostly observational.
- **Perioperative MACE** — diabetes is one of the six RCRI variables; insulin-dependence specifically scores a point.

A meta-analysis of 15 RCTs (patients with and without diabetes) found that intensive protocols aiming for glucose &lt;150 mg/dL lowered SSI (OR 0.43, 95% CI 0.29–0.64) at the cost of more hypoglycemia (OR 5.55).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> In trials restricted to people with diabetes, the Cochrane review found a smaller, non-significant infection reduction (13.0% vs 18.2%, RR 0.75, 95% CI 0.55–1.04).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> WHO conditionally recommends perioperative glycemic control protocols to reduce SSI.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The practical message is to avoid sustained hyperglycemia, not to chase near-normal values.

## Preoperative Targets (2026 ADA)

### HbA1c / Glucose Management Indicator

- **Target A1c &lt;8% (64 mmol/mol) within 3 months** of elective surgery, with individualized risk-to-benefit assessment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Alternatively, **14-day GMI &lt;8% and/or time-in-range &gt;50%** from CGM data.
- **Do NOT postpone elective surgery on A1c alone** — the ADA explicitly cautions against denying needed surgery based on A1c in isolation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Who Needs Tighter Preoperative Optimization

- **Elective prosthetic urologic surgery** — infection risk rises with HbA1c. In a prospective multicenter series of 902 implants (76% malleable), infection was 1.3% with HbA1c &lt;6.5%, 1.5% at 6.5–7.5%, 6.5% at 7.6–8.5%, 14.7% at 8.6–9.5% and 22.4% above 9.5%; an 8.5% cutoff predicted infection with 80% sensitivity and 65% specificity.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> A 2026 meta-analysis of 11 mostly retrospective studies (10,024 men) found infection of 9.1% in cohorts with mean HbA1c ≥8% versus 3.8% in the rest, and called for better threshold studies.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> Use these data to counsel, to push optimization before elective implantation, and to set a local threshold; the evidence does not establish a single mandatory cancellation value or extend it to other implants such as slings or the AUS.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Major reconstructive operations with bowel use** (cystectomy with diversion, augmentation cystoplasty) — A1c &lt;8% is a reasonable target given the combined infection/anastomotic-leak profile.
- Urgent cancer cases — do not delay; optimize intraoperatively and postoperatively.

## Perioperative Glycemic Targets

| Setting | Target | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| **Critically ill / ICU** | **140–180 mg/dL** once insulin started | <sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Non-critically ill** | **100–180 mg/dL** | <sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Pre-, intra-, and postoperative** | **100–180 mg/dL** | <sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |

**Do not target stricter ranges.** The NICE-SUGAR trial (6,104 ICU patients) showed intensive control (80–110 mg/dL) produced:<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- **No mortality benefit**, and in fact **slightly higher mortality (27.5% vs 25%)** vs conventional control (140–180 mg/dL).
- **Severe hypoglycemia (glucose ≤40 mg/dL) in 6.8% vs 0.5%** (206/3,016 vs 15/3,014).

A 2023 Cochrane review confirmed intensive control gives little or no mortality difference, a possible but uncertain reduction in cardiovascular events, and a **RR 4.73 for severe hypoglycemia**.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The net result: the pendulum has definitively swung away from tight control. In perioperative patients with diabetes, pooled data suggest a moderate target (150–200 mg/dL) lowers mortality and stroke compared with a liberal one (&gt;200 mg/dL), with no added benefit from stricter control (&lt;150 mg/dL).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> **140–180 is the operational target for the sick; 100–180 for the floor.**

## Insulin Therapy

### Regimen Principles<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- **Use insulin for glucose management during the procedure.** Medication holds depend on drug class and timing; individualized GLP-1 decisions are described below.
- **Basal-bolus** (basal + premeal short/rapid-acting) outperforms correction-only (sliding-scale) regimens for inpatient glycemic outcomes — **use basal-bolus when the patient is eating**.
- **Sole use of sliding-scale insulin without basal** is discouraged (ADA 16.10; the Endocrine Society 2022 non-critical-care guideline likewise favors basal or basal-plus-correction regimens).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Continuous IV insulin infusion** is the preferred regimen for critically ill patients.

### Dose Adjustments the Night Before Surgery<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Insulin | Preop adjustment |
|---|---|
| **Long-acting basal** (glargine, detemir, degludec) | **75–80% of usual dose** the evening before |
| **NPH** (intermediate) | **50% of usual dose** |
| **Prandial / rapid-acting** | **Hold** while NPO |

A **25% reduction of the evening-before basal dose** is more likely to achieve perioperative glucose goals with lower hypoglycemia risk than the usual dose. ADA notes the reduction may not suit some people with type 1 diabetes, who need enough basal insulin to avoid ketoacidosis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Adjust pump basal rates similarly when the pump is not in automated mode.

### Morning of Surgery

- **Hold prandial / rapid-acting** insulin.
- **Continue adjusted basal** (75–80% long-acting, 50% NPH).
- Check POC glucose on arrival.
- Use IV insulin for critical illness or a hyperglycemic crisis. Stable ward patients who are NPO usually receive basal plus correction insulin; fasting duration or steroid use alone does not mandate an infusion.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### By Clinical Setting<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

**Type 1 diabetes or insulin-dependent type 2:**

| Eating status | Plan |
|---|---|
| Eating | Continue outpatient basal ± prandial (consider 25–50% dose reduction) + correction |
| NPO | Continue basal alone (hold prandial; consider 20–25% basal reduction) + correction |

**Type 2 diabetes, not on insulin:**

| Eating status | Plan |
|---|---|
| Eating | Stop non-insulin agents; start correction (sliding-scale) if well-controlled |
| NPO | Stop non-insulin agents; start correction |
| **Poorly controlled (glucose ≥180 or A1c &gt;10%)** | Start weight-based basal + prandial + correction |

## Non-Insulin Medications

### Metformin

- **Hold on day of surgery.**
- The 2024 AHA/ACC guideline permits perioperative continuation (Class IIa) — the theoretical lactic-acidosis risk is not well-supported by modern data.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Contrast is a separate decision:** ACR permits metformin continuation for IV iodinated contrast when there is no AKI and eGFR ≥30 mL/min/1.73 m². With AKI, eGFR &lt;30, or relevant arterial catheter studies, withhold at/before the procedure and for 48 h afterward, then reassess renal function.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> US metformin labeling is more restrictive: stop at or before iodinated contrast when eGFR is 30–60, with hepatic impairment, alcoholism or heart failure, or with intra-arterial contrast, and restart after eGFR is re-evaluated at 48 h and found stable.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

### Sulfonylureas (Glipizide, Glyburide, Glimepiride)

- **Hold on the day of surgery** — hypoglycemia risk while NPO.

### SGLT2 Inhibitors (Canagliflozin, Dapagliflozin, Empagliflozin, Ertugliflozin)

- **Hold 3 days before elective surgery** (4 days for ertugliflozin), consistent with ADA (3–4 days), AHA/ACC 2024 (Class I) and US labeling for empagliflozin (at least 3 days) and ertugliflozin (at least 4 days).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)[[9]](#ref9)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- Mechanism for timing: half-lives and the risk window for **euglycemic DKA** — a documented perioperative emergency where DKA occurs with normal or near-normal glucose.
- Restart postoperatively once stable, eating, not acidotic, and not receiving IV dextrose infusion.

### GLP-1 Receptor Agonists (Semaglutide, Liraglutide, Tirzepatide, Dulaglutide)

A clinical hot topic. The 2023 ASA guidance suggested holding weekly GLP-1s a week before surgery and daily agents on the day of surgery because of **delayed gastric emptying and aspiration risk**; the 2024 AHA/ACC guideline describes a hold of up to a week without a formal recommendation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> FDA labeling for all GLP-1 RAs and dual GIP/GLP-1 RAs warns of pulmonary aspiration with general anesthesia or deep sedation, and ADA notes that a one-week hold of a weekly agent may not empty the stomach.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The 2026 ADA now advises a **personalized approach**, in line with the 2024 multisociety guidance (ASA, AGA, ASMBS, ISPCOP, SAGES):<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

- **Low risk of delayed gastric emptying, elective surgery:** the drug may be continued.
- **Active GI symptoms (nausea, vomiting, dyspepsia, abdominal distension):** consider full stomach precautions (RSI) and delay.
- **Preoperative gastric ultrasound** may be considered to quantify gastric contents.
- **A 24-hour liquid nutrition protocol** before the procedure may help, particularly in higher-risk patients.
- **Use alternative glycemic management** (insulin) if the agent is held and worsening glycemia is expected.

See [Perioperative Nutrition](/docs/foundations/perioperative-care/postoperative-management/nutrition#glp-1--gip-medicines-and-surgery) for the aspiration-planning evidence, including the OCULUS trial, and [Cardiovascular Risk](/docs/foundations/perioperative-care/preoperative-assessment/cardiovascular-risk) for the SGLT2 and cardiac-medication rules.

### DPP-4 Inhibitors (Sitagliptin, Linagliptin)

- **Hold on the day of surgery** — ADA advises holding metformin and other oral glucose-lowering agents that day. Selected stable inpatients with mild to moderate hyperglycemia may receive DPP-4 inhibitors outside the procedural setting.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Thiazolidinediones (Pioglitazone)

- **Hold on day of surgery** — fluid retention.

## Glucose Monitoring

- **POC glucose every 2–4 hours** while NPO, intraoperatively, and postoperatively until stable.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **CGM cannot be used alone** for surgical glucose monitoring — confirmatory POC required before insulin dosing.
- Validation benchmarks: CGM within ±20% of POC when glucose ≥70 mg/dL, or ±20 mg/dL when &lt;70.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Insulin Pumps and Automated Insulin Delivery

**Pumps may be continued intraoperatively** if institutional policy and the procedure allow.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- Patient must be able to self-manage (or have a support person present).
- Adequate supplies and trained staff.
- Contraindications: impaired consciousness, critical illness, DKA/HHS, inability to adjust settings.
- If the pump must come off: **transition to IV insulin infusion or basal + correction subcutaneous.**

**Automated insulin delivery (hybrid closed-loop) systems** — may continue if CGM and pump function are preserved; revert to manual mode if CGM fails.

## Special Situations

### Steroid-Induced Hyperglycemia

Perioperative steroids (adrenal-insufficiency stress dosing, dexamethasone antiemesis, chronic prednisone) cause predictable hyperglycemia. With morning prednisone, glucose is near normal or mildly high fasting and climbs through the afternoon to an evening peak, more so in type 2 diabetes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- **Once- or twice-daily prednisone:** give NPH insulin with the steroid dose, because NPH peaks 4–6 h later. A commonly used starting schema is about 0.1 U/kg of NPH per 10 mg of prednisone equivalent, titrated to glucose.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Dexamethasone or continuous/multidose glucocorticoids:** long-acting basal insulin is often needed for fasting glucose, and prandial and correction doses may need to rise by 40–60% or more.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Enteric Feeding

- Start weight-based basal + correction insulin.
- Check glucose every 4–6 h, matched to the insulin used.
- Keep basal insulin (essential in type 1 diabetes even if feeds stop) and cover the nutrition load separately, about 1 U per 10–15 g carbohydrate. For continuous feeds use rapid-acting insulin every 4 h, regular insulin every 6 h or NPH every 8–12 h; bolus feeds need rapid-acting insulin before each feed. If feeds are interrupted, give IV dextrose for the duration of insulin action.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Total Parenteral Nutrition

- **Add regular insulin directly to the TPN bag** — a starting dose of about **1 U per 10 g dextrose**, particularly if &gt;20 U of correction insulin were needed in the prior 24 h; adjust daily.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Add subcutaneous correction insulin for residual glucose excursions.

### Diabetic Ketoacidosis in the Surgical Patient

Can occur postoperatively in T1DM with inadequate basal coverage, or **euglycemic DKA** with SGLT2 inhibitor continuation through surgery.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

**Diagnosis requires diabetes/hyperglycemia plus ketosis and acidosis:** blood β-hydroxybutyrate ≥3 mmol/L (or urine ketones ≥2+) with pH &lt;7.3 and/or bicarbonate &lt;18 mmol/L. Prior diabetes can satisfy the glucose criterion even when glucose is &lt;200 mg/dL.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

**Use the institutional DKA protocol with frequent glucose/electrolyte/ketone reassessment.** Key points from the 2024 international consensus:<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

1. Give isotonic saline or balanced crystalloid, adjusting volume to hemodynamics and cardiac/renal function.
2. Assess potassium **before insulin**. If K &lt;3.5 mmol/L, replace it and defer insulin until K &gt;3.5; add replacement when K falls below 5.0 to target 4–5 mmol/L.
3. IV insulin commonly starts at 0.1 U/kg/h. When glucose falls below 250 mg/dL, add dextrose and reduce to 0.05 U/kg/h while clearing ketosis.
4. Bicarbonate is not routine; consider it for severe acidosis with pH &lt;7.0.
5. Resolution requires ketones &lt;0.6 mmol/L and pH ≥7.3 or bicarbonate ≥18. **Anion-gap closure alone is not a resolution criterion.** Overlap subcutaneous basal insulin with IV insulin for 1–2 h when transitioning.

## GU-Reconstruction–Specific Considerations

### Prosthetic Urology — AUS, IPP, Sling

Glycemic control is one modifiable risk factor alongside surgical technique, skin/urinary infection assessment, device selection and antimicrobial planning. The strongest HbA1c data concern penile implants; optimize glycemia before elective implantation without treating 7–7.5% as a validated target or 8.5% as a mandatory cancellation cutoff for all devices.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup> Choose prophylaxis using the procedure-specific protocol, allergies, renal function and local resistance data; see the [prosthetic infection prevention hub](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/infection-prophylaxis/prosthetic-infection-biofilm).

### Urinary Diversion (Cystectomy + Ileal Conduit / Neobladder)

- Bowel prep day + NPO + insulin-dependent diabetic is a high-hypoglycemia-risk setting — hold oral agents, reduce basal, maintain IV dextrose if needed.
- **Return-of-feeding gap** — preserve basal insulin in type 1 diabetes; stable ward patients generally use subcutaneous basal/correction coverage. Reserve infusion for clinical need rather than an automatic postoperative day count.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Metabolic acidosis from ileal diversion can worsen any underlying diabetic ketogenesis — monitor bicarbonate.

### Neurogenic / Diabetic Bladder Dysfunction

- Diabetes autonomic neuropathy causes detrusor hypocontractility, impaired bladder sensation, and chronic urinary retention — relevant in the preoperative history for any patient presenting with incontinence or voiding dysfunction.
- **Post-obstructive diuresis** after relief of diabetic neurogenic retention can cause profound electrolyte and glycemic shifts.

### Renal Function

Many long-standing diabetics have **diabetic nephropathy**. This affects:

- DOAC dose selection.
- Metformin continuation and contrast loading.
- Gentamicin and aminoglycoside dosing for prosthetic prophylaxis.
- ERAS fluid-balance decisions.

## Summary of Key Recommendations

| Recommendation | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Preoperative A1c &lt;8% target within 3 months of elective surgery (individualize) | ADA 16.14, C |
| Perioperative glucose target **100–180 mg/dL** (floor), **140–180** (ICU) | ADA 16.15, E; ICU target in ADA text |
| Basal-bolus insulin, not sliding-scale alone, in the inpatient | ADA 16.9–16.10, A |
| **Do NOT use tight glycemic control** (80–110) — NICE-SUGAR / Cochrane | A |
| **Hold SGLT2 inhibitors 3–4 days** before surgery | ADA text; AHA/ACC 2024 Class I; FDA labeling |
| **Personalized hold strategy for GLP-1 receptor agonists** | ADA 2026 text; multisociety guidance |
| Hold metformin on the day of surgery (ADA); continuation is reasonable per AHA/ACC | ADA text; ACC Class IIa |
| Reduce basal insulin to **75–80%** (long-acting) or **50%** (NPH) the evening before surgery | ADA text |
| CGM should not be used alone during surgery | ADA text |
| Insulin pumps may continue with institutional support | ADA 16.7, C |
