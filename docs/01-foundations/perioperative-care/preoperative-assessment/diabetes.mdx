# Diabetes — Perioperative Management

Diabetes is present in **~20% of patients undergoing major general surgery**, with another **23–60% harboring prediabetes or undiagnosed diabetes**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> For the reconstructive urologist, two consequences matter above all others: **hyperglycemia is associated with higher SSI risk** (including concern for prosthetic implant infection), and **uncontrolled diabetes independently predicts wound-healing failure, delirium, and prolonged LOS**.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> This article covers the 2026 ADA perioperative standards, the evidence behind the glycemic targets, and the specific medication-hold decisions that the urology service has to make the day before surgery.

See also: [Cardiovascular Risk](/docs/foundations/perioperative-care/preoperative-assessment/cardiovascular-risk), [ERAS](/docs/foundations/perioperative-care/perioperative-protocols/eras), [Wound Healing](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/wound-healing).

## Why Glucose Control Matters in GU Reconstruction

- **SSI** — blood glucose &gt;220 mg/dL on POD 1 predicts postoperative infection with **87.5% sensitivity**; diabetics have 2.7-fold infection rates, 5.7-fold serious-infection rates.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Prosthetic infection** — optimize glycemia as one part of infection prevention. A universal HbA1c cancellation threshold is not established; the proposed 8.5% IPP threshold remains debated and should not be generalized to every GU implant.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Wound healing** — hyperglycemia impairs collagen cross-linking, fibroblast function, and neutrophil chemotaxis.
- **Anastomotic healing** — diabetes is an independent risk factor for vesicourethral anastomotic breakdown and ureteral reimplant stricture.
- **Perioperative MACE** — diabetes is one of the six RCRI variables; insulin-dependence specifically scores a point.

Intensive perioperative glycemic-control protocols reduce SSI by roughly **57%** (OR 0.43) in meta-analysis.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Preoperative Targets (2026 ADA)

### HbA1c / Glucose Management Indicator

- **Target A1c &lt;8% (64 mmol/mol) within 3 months** of elective surgery, with individualized risk-to-benefit assessment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Alternatively, **14-day GMI &lt;8% and/or time-in-range &gt;50%** from CGM data.
- **Do NOT postpone elective surgery on A1c alone** — the ADA explicitly cautions against denying needed surgery based on A1c in isolation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Who Needs Tighter Preoperative Optimization

- **Elective prosthetic urologic surgery** — discuss glycemic optimization, infection risk and any local eligibility threshold individually; do not present an institution-specific HbA1c target as a universal society mandate.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Major reconstructive operations with bowel use** (cystectomy with diversion, augmentation cystoplasty) — A1c &lt;8% is a reasonable target given the combined infection/anastomotic-leak profile.
- Urgent cancer cases — do not delay; optimize intraoperatively and postoperatively.

## Perioperative Glycemic Targets

| Setting | Target | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| **Critically ill / ICU** | **140–180 mg/dL** once insulin started | <sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Non-critically ill** | **100–180 mg/dL** | <sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Pre-, intra-, and postoperative** | **100–180 mg/dL** | <sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |

**Do not target stricter ranges.** The NICE-SUGAR trial (6,104 ICU patients) showed intensive control (80–110 mg/dL) produced:<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- **No mortality benefit**, and in fact **slightly higher mortality (27.5% vs 25%)** vs conventional control.
- **10–15× higher rate of severe hypoglycemia.**

A 2023 Cochrane review confirmed intensive control gives little or no mortality difference, a possible but uncertain reduction in cardiovascular events, and a **RR 4.73 for severe hypoglycemia**.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The net result: the pendulum has definitively swung away from tight control. **140–180 is the operational target for the sick; 100–180 for the floor.**

## Insulin Therapy

### Regimen Principles<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- **Use insulin for glucose management during the procedure.** Medication holds depend on drug class and timing; individualized GLP-1 decisions are described below.
- **Basal-bolus** (basal + premeal short/rapid-acting) outperforms correction-only (sliding-scale) regimens for inpatient glycemic outcomes — **use basal-bolus when the patient is eating**.
- **Sole use of sliding-scale insulin without basal** is discouraged.
- **Continuous IV insulin infusion** is the preferred regimen for critically ill patients.

### Dose Adjustments the Night Before Surgery<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

| Insulin | Preop adjustment |
|---|---|
| **Long-acting basal** (glargine, detemir, degludec) | **75–80% of usual dose** the evening before |
| **NPH** (intermediate) | **50% of usual dose** |
| **Prandial / rapid-acting** | **Hold** while NPO |

**25% basal dose reduction** is associated with achieving perioperative glucose targets with a lower hypoglycemia rate. Individualize in T1DM — some patients require full basal to avoid DKA.

### Morning of Surgery

- **Hold prandial / rapid-acting** insulin.
- **Continue adjusted basal** (75–80% long-acting, 50% NPH).
- Check POC glucose on arrival.
- Use IV insulin for critical illness or a hyperglycemic crisis. Stable ward patients who are NPO usually receive basal plus correction insulin; fasting duration or steroid use alone does not mandate an infusion.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### By Clinical Setting<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

**Type 1 diabetes or insulin-dependent type 2:**

| Eating status | Plan |
|---|---|
| Eating | Continue outpatient basal ± prandial (consider 25–50% dose reduction) + correction |
| NPO | Continue basal alone (hold prandial; consider 20–25% basal reduction) + correction |

**Type 2 diabetes, not on insulin:**

| Eating status | Plan |
|---|---|
| Eating | Stop non-insulin agents; start correction (sliding-scale) if well-controlled |
| NPO | Stop non-insulin agents; start correction |
| **Poorly controlled (glucose ≥180 or A1c &gt;10%)** | Start weight-based basal + prandial + correction |

## Non-Insulin Medications

### Metformin

- **Hold on day of surgery.**
- The 2024 AHA/ACC guideline permits perioperative continuation (Class IIa) — the theoretical lactic-acidosis risk is not well-supported by modern data.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Contrast is a separate decision:** ACR permits metformin continuation for IV iodinated contrast when there is no AKI and eGFR ≥30 mL/min/1.73 m². With AKI, eGFR &lt;30, or relevant arterial catheter studies, withhold at/before the procedure and for 48 h afterward, then reassess renal function. Product labeling/local policies may be more restrictive.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

### Sulfonylureas (Glipizide, Glyburide, Glimepiride)

- **Hold on the day of surgery** — hypoglycemia risk while NPO.

### SGLT2 Inhibitors (Canagliflozin, Dapagliflozin, Empagliflozin, Ertugliflozin)

- **Hold 3 days before elective surgery** (4 days for ertugliflozin).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- Mechanism for timing: half-lives and the risk window for **euglycemic DKA** — a documented perioperative emergency where DKA occurs with normal or near-normal glucose.
- Restart postoperatively once stable, eating, not acidotic, and not receiving IV dextrose infusion.

### GLP-1 Receptor Agonists (Semaglutide, Liraglutide, Tirzepatide, Dulaglutide)

A clinical hot topic. The 2023 ASA guidance suggested holding weekly GLP-1s a week before surgery and daily agents on the day of surgery due to **delayed gastric emptying and aspiration risk**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> The 2026 ADA advises a **personalized approach**:

- **No GI symptoms, low-risk surgery:** may continue.
- **Active GI symptoms (nausea, vomiting, dyspepsia, early satiety):** full stomach precautions (RSI, consider delaying).
- **Preoperative gastric ultrasound** may be considered in high-risk cases.
- **Liquid diet for 24 h prior** is an emerging mitigation strategy, particularly for elective endoscopic procedures and patients who cannot hold the drug.
- **Implement alternative glycemic management** (insulin) if the agent is held and glycemia worsens.

### DPP-4 Inhibitors (Sitagliptin, Linagliptin)

- **Hold on the day of surgery** under the ADA perioperative approach. Selected stable inpatients with mild hyperglycemia may receive DPP-4 inhibitors outside the procedural setting.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Thiazolidinediones (Pioglitazone)

- **Hold on day of surgery** — fluid retention.

## Glucose Monitoring

- **POC glucose every 2–4 hours** while NPO, intraoperatively, and postoperatively until stable.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **CGM cannot be used alone** for surgical glucose monitoring — confirmatory POC required before insulin dosing.
- Validation benchmarks: CGM within ±20% of POC when glucose ≥70 mg/dL, or ±20 mg/dL when &lt;70.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Insulin Pumps and Automated Insulin Delivery

**Pumps may be continued intraoperatively** if institutional policy allows.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- Patient must be able to self-manage (or have a support person present).
- Adequate supplies and trained staff.
- Contraindications: impaired consciousness, critical illness, DKA/HHS, inability to adjust settings.
- If the pump must come off: **transition to IV insulin infusion or basal + correction subcutaneous.**

**Automated insulin delivery (hybrid closed-loop) systems** — may continue if CGM and pump function are preserved; revert to manual mode if CGM fails.

## Special Situations

### Steroid-Induced Hyperglycemia

Perioperative steroids (adrenal-insufficiency stress dosing, dexamethasone antiemesis, chronic prednisone) drive predictable hyperglycemia peaking 4–8 h after dosing.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

- **NPH insulin at the time of the steroid dose: 0.1 U/kg/day per 10 mg prednisone equivalent.**
- Continuous glucocorticoid → continuous insulin; bolus steroid → matched-peak NPH.

### Enteric Feeding

- Start weight-based basal + correction insulin.
- Check glucose **every 6 h.**
- Separate basal requirements from nutrition coverage. Use short/rapid-acting insulin or an appropriate NPH schedule for continuous feeds; bolus feeds usually need rapid-acting coverage. Plan for feed interruption to prevent hypoglycemia.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Total Parenteral Nutrition

- **Add regular insulin directly to the TPN bag** — approximately **1 U per 10 g dextrose**.
- Individualize to carbohydrate delivery and glucose response; do not assume a fixed percentage of total requirements.
- Add subcutaneous correction insulin for residual glucose excursions.

### Diabetic Ketoacidosis in the Surgical Patient

Can occur postoperatively in T1DM with inadequate basal coverage, or **euglycemic DKA** with SGLT2 inhibitor continuation through surgery.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

**Diagnosis requires diabetes/hyperglycemia plus ketosis and acidosis:** blood β-hydroxybutyrate ≥3 mmol/L (or urine ketones ≥2+) with pH &lt;7.3 and/or bicarbonate &lt;18 mmol/L. Prior diabetes can satisfy the glucose criterion even when glucose is &lt;200 mg/dL.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

**Use the institutional DKA protocol with frequent glucose/electrolyte/ketone reassessment.** Key points from the 2024 international consensus:<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

1. Give isotonic saline or balanced crystalloid, adjusting volume to hemodynamics and cardiac/renal function.
2. Assess potassium **before insulin**. If K &lt;3.5 mmol/L, replace it and defer insulin until K &gt;3.5; add replacement when K falls below 5.0 to target 4–5 mmol/L.
3. IV insulin commonly starts at 0.1 U/kg/h. When glucose falls below 250 mg/dL, add dextrose and reduce to 0.05 U/kg/h while clearing ketosis.
4. Bicarbonate is not routine; consider it for severe acidosis with pH &lt;7.0.
5. Resolution requires ketones &lt;0.6 mmol/L and pH ≥7.3 or bicarbonate ≥18. **Anion-gap closure alone is not a resolution criterion.** Overlap subcutaneous basal insulin with IV insulin for 1–2 h when transitioning.

## GU-Reconstruction–Specific Considerations

### Prosthetic Urology — AUS, IPP, Sling

Glycemic control is one modifiable risk factor alongside surgical technique, skin/urinary infection assessment, device selection and antimicrobial planning. Optimize it without claiming a universally validated 7–7.5% target or mandatory 8.5% cancellation cutoff for all implants.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> Choose prophylaxis using the procedure-specific protocol, allergies, renal function and local resistance data; see the [prosthetic infection prevention hub](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/infection-prophylaxis/prosthetic-infection-biofilm).

### Urinary Diversion (Cystectomy + Ileal Conduit / Neobladder)

- Bowel prep day + NPO + insulin-dependent diabetic is a high-hypoglycemia-risk setting — hold oral agents, reduce basal, maintain IV dextrose if needed.
- **Return-of-feeding gap** — preserve basal insulin in type 1 diabetes; stable ward patients generally use subcutaneous basal/correction coverage. Reserve infusion for clinical need rather than an automatic postoperative day count.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Metabolic acidosis from ileal diversion can worsen any underlying diabetic ketogenesis — monitor bicarbonate.

### Neurogenic / Diabetic Bladder Dysfunction

- Diabetes autonomic neuropathy causes detrusor hypocontractility, impaired bladder sensation, and chronic urinary retention — relevant in the preoperative history for any patient presenting with incontinence or voiding dysfunction.
- **Post-obstructive diuresis** after relief of diabetic neurogenic retention can cause profound electrolyte and glycemic shifts.

### Renal Function

Many long-standing diabetics have **diabetic nephropathy**. This affects:

- DOAC dose selection.
- Metformin continuation and contrast loading.
- Gentamicin and aminoglycoside dosing for prosthetic prophylaxis.
- ERAS fluid-balance decisions.

## Summary of Key Recommendations

| Recommendation | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Preoperative A1c &lt;8% target within 3 months of elective surgery (individualize) | ADA C |
| Perioperative glucose target **100–180 mg/dL** (floor), **140–180** (ICU) | ADA A / E |
| Basal-bolus insulin, not sliding-scale alone, in the inpatient | ADA A |
| **Do NOT use tight glycemic control** (80–110) — NICE-SUGAR / Cochrane | A |
| **Hold SGLT2 inhibitors 3–4 days** before surgery | ADA / ACC Class I C-LD |
| **Personalized hold strategy for GLP-1 receptor agonists** | Expert consensus |
| Metformin may be continued or held on day of surgery | ACC Class IIa C-LD |
| Reduce basal insulin to **75–80%** (long-acting) or **50%** (NPH) the evening before surgery | ADA C |
| CGM cannot be used alone during surgery | ADA E |
| Insulin pumps may continue with institutional support | ADA C |
