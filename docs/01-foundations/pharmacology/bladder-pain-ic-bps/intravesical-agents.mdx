# Intravesical Agents for IC/BPS

**Intravesical instillation** is an option for **interstitial cystitis / bladder pain syndrome (IC/BPS)**. AUA 2022 removed the former numbered treatment tiers. **DMSO (dimethyl sulfoxide)** is the only FDA-approved intravesical agent for this indication.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> The 2022 AUA guideline lists DMSO, heparin and/or lidocaine as bladder instillation options (Statement 17, Option; no hierarchy).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Other bladder instillations used or studied off-label with varying evidence include GAG-replenishment products (hyaluronic acid and chondroitin sulfate), oxybutynin and pentosan polysulfate.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Sustained-release systems are under investigation.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[22]](#ref22)</sup> For refractory IC/BPS, the guideline treats onabotulinumtoxinA as an intradetrusor injection (Statement 20, Option).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**CUA 2025:** evidence does not support choosing one intravesical agent or cocktail over all others. Refractory IC/BPS may be treated with botulinum toxin, with very low certainty of benefit.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

For the broader clinical condition and oral pharmacotherapy, see [IC/PBS](/docs/clinical-conditions/03h-pelvic-pain/ic-pbs) and [Oral IC/BPS Agents](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/bladder-pain-ic-bps/oral-ic-bps-agents). For the dedicated botulinum toxin article, see [Botulinum Toxin](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/neuromodulation-adjuncts/botulinum-toxin).

## DMSO (Rimso-50) — the FDA-approved agent

DMSO has been available since the 1970s and remains the reference intravesical therapy.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

### Multiple mechanisms<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- Anti-inflammatory inhibition of inflammatory mediators
- Direct analgesic effect
- Collagen dissolution (may reduce mural fibrosis)
- Detrusor smooth-muscle relaxation
- Mast-cell stabilization (relevant to mast-cell–mediated IC/BPS pathophysiology)
- **Membrane permeation enhancer.** Rapidly absorbed across the bladder wall and enhances absorption of co-administered agents (a safety point for cocktails)

### Dosing protocol (FDA label)<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

- **Formulation:** sterile Rimso-50, 50% w/w DMSO in water; do not interpret the labeled concentration as a 50:50 volume-mixing recipe
- **Dose:** 50 mL by catheter<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Dwell time:** 15 minutes on the label (the AUA suggests 15–20 minutes); longer dwell times produce significant pain<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Frequency:** every 2 weeks until maximum symptomatic relief, then increase the interval as appropriate
- **Pre-treatment:** intraurethral lidocaine jelly; oral analgesics or B&O suppository for bladder spasm
- **Severe cases:** initial 2–3 treatments may need anesthesia (saddle block)

### Efficacy

A 2025 review of 14 studies (554 patients; five RCTs, seven single-arm studies, and two cohorts) found lower symptom and pain scores after DMSO-containing instillations. Its pooled estimates compared follow-up with pretreatment, only four studies used DMSO alone, and follow-up was at most six months.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

A placebo-controlled trial randomized 96 Japanese patients with cystoscopy-defined bladder-centric IC/BPS to 50% DMSO or placebo every two weeks. At week 12, mean ICSI changes were −5.2 versus −3.4 (between-group difference −1.8, 95% CI −3.3 to −0.3).<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup>

A 2023 open-label RCT randomized 83 women; 70 completed six weekly instillations (42 DMSO plus triamcinolone, 28 BTH). At visit 6, among patients with evaluable paired ICSI data, 19/30 (63%) in the DMSO plus triamcinolone arm versus 9/21 (43%) in the BTH arm achieved the prespecified &gt;29.5% symptom-score reduction (p=0.15). Pain scores favored DMSO plus triamcinolone at visits 5 and 6; nocturia improved within that arm, but the between-arm nocturia difference at visit 6 was not significant.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

### Adverse effects<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- **Garlic-like taste** within minutes of instillation, lasting hours
- **Garlic-like body / breath odor** lasting up to 72 hours, a major patient-acceptance barrier
- Transient chemical cystitis discomfort during instillation
- Hypersensitivity, including possible anaphylactoid symptoms (DMSO can liberate histamine)<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- Overall AE rate about 38% in meta-analysis, mostly mild<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

### Monitoring recommended by the FDA label<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

- **Eye evaluation including slit lamp** before treatment and periodically (lens opacities seen in animals at high chronic doses)
- Liver function, renal function, CBC approximately every 6 months

### Cautions<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

The label lists no absolute contraindications, but warns as follows.

- **Urinary tract malignancy.** The label warns that DMSO-induced vasodilation may be harmful
- **Pregnancy.** Insufficient controlled human data; the label calls for use only when potential benefit justifies fetal risk
- **Lactation.** Excretion in human milk is unknown; use caution during nursing.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Children.** Safety and effectiveness are not established.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Drug interactions.** DMSO may enhance absorption of co-administered drugs, including lidocaine.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Lidocaine

**Mechanism:** direct local anesthetic on the urothelium and submucosal afferents.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

**Evidence:** AUA Option, Grade B (Statement 17). In the multicenter placebo-controlled trial of alkalinized lidocaine, 30% versus 10% reported moderate or marked improvement on the global response assessment three days after completing the five-day course (significant), and 24% versus 11.5% did so at ten days (not significant). The guideline judges the relief short-term (less than two weeks), and instillation can itself be painful.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Practical caution:** when combined with DMSO, DMSO enhances lidocaine absorption and can produce systemic toxicity (perioral numbness, tinnitus, dizziness, seizures, cardiac effects). Use a pharmacy-validated regimen accounting for the total local-anesthetic dose, formulation and dwell time; simply shortening dwell does not establish safety. Counsel on toxicity symptoms.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Heparin

**Mechanism:** heparin is a naturally occurring GAG that augments the urothelial mucopolysaccharide layer, inhibits leukocyte function, and scavenges reactive oxygen species , proposed to supplement the urothelial GAG barrier.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

**Evidence (AUA Option, Grade C; Statement 17):** observational data show clinically significant symptom improvement; one small bicarbonate-controlled crossover trial of heparin plus alkalinized lidocaine found greater pain reduction at 12 hours (42% versus 21%); a separate 14-patient pilot used alkalinized lidocaine alone as comparator. Regimens described in the AUA supporting text include 10,000 IU in 10 mL sterile water three times weekly with a 1-hour retention, and 25,000 IU in 5 mL twice weekly, each for three months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Dosing varies by protocol.** Heparin alone and alkalinized-lidocaine combinations have different doses, volumes and dwell times. Use a documented institutional formulation; pharmacy should verify final pH and lidocaine stability because excessive alkalinization can precipitate lidocaine.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup> Selected patients may self-instill after catheterization training and a plan for infection, hematuria or worsening symptoms; this is not a default daily regimen for everyone.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

## Intravesical Cocktails

Cocktails are used in practice, but formulations are not interchangeable. The following are the actual regimens in Moss 2023, shown to make that trial interpretable; neither is established as a universally preferred prescribing protocol.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

| Trial arm | Instilled mixture | Schedule |
|---|---|---|
| **DMSO plus triamcinolone** | DMSO 50 mL plus triamcinolone 10 mg (1 mL of 10 mg/mL) | Weekly for 6 weeks; 15-minute dwell |
| **BTH** | Bupivacaine 0.5%, 30 mL (150 mg); triamcinolone 20 mg (2 mL of 10 mg/mL); heparin 20,000 units (2 mL of 10,000 units/mL) | Weekly for 6 weeks; 15-minute dwell |

For clinical use, verify sterile preparation, compatibility, final concentration, local-anesthetic exposure and contraindications with pharmacy. DMSO increases absorption; bicarbonate also changes lidocaine penetration. A generic mixture assembled from doses in unrelated protocols is not a validated recipe.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## GAG Replenishment Therapy (HA, CS, HA + CS)

GAG-replenishment is built on the model that IC/BPS reflects urothelial GAG-layer disruption that allows urinary solutes to penetrate the wall and trigger inflammation.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

### Hyaluronic acid (HA)

- In a porcine urothelial cell model, HA influenced barrier-related responses through cell interactions rather than directly restoring the luminal GAG layer.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- A meta-analysis published online in 2025 (2026 journal issue) included 30 BPS studies, alongside separate recurrent-UTI and radiation-cystitis studies. It reported symptom improvement, but included uncontrolled studies and limited randomized evidence. The recurrent-infection outcome pertains to the separate recurrent-UTI studies.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Manufacturer-described CYSTISTAT regimen:** 40 mg HA in a 50 mL vial, instilled weekly for 4–12 treatments, then monthly until symptoms resolve.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup> Retain the instillation as long as possible, at least 30 minutes (Cystistat manufacturer leaflet)<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>
- Widely used in Europe (Cystistat, Ialuril); not FDA-approved in the US for IC/BPS
- **CYSTISTAT precautions.** Do not administer with known hypersensitivity; the leaflet does not recommend treatment in children or during pregnancy or lactation.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

### Chondroitin sulfate (CS)

- GAG-layer replenishment with anti-inflammatory effect<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- In a placebo-controlled trial of 98 women, 38.0% receiving 2% CS versus 31.3% receiving vehicle reported moderate or marked improvement at week 11; the difference was not statistically significant.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
- Network safety rankings are based on limited, heterogeneous comparisons.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Trial regimens used a 2% sodium chondroitin sulfate solution: 20 mL weekly for 8 weeks (Nickel 2012) or weekly for 6 weeks against DMSO (Tutolo 2017); product volumes and maintenance schedules vary.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)[[25]](#ref25)</sup>
- In the 36-patient randomized comparison, 16/22 (72.7%) receiving CS versus 2/14 (14%) receiving DMSO reported moderate or marked GRA improvement at 12 weeks; 6/22 (27%) versus 8/14 (57%) withdrew, and the trial stopped early because of DMSO withdrawals.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

### Combined HA + CS (Ialuril)

- Ialuril (HA 1.6% with CS 2.0%, 50 mL) has been given in a prospective series as four weekly instillations, then two every two weeks and two monthly (eight doses), with a 60-minute dwell; this is a trial schedule, and a manufacturer instruction was not located<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>
- A randomized open-label multicenter study (n=110; 1.6% HA with 2.0% CS or 50% DMSO, 13 weekly instillations) reported improvement in both arms, fewer treatment-related AEs with HA/CS (1.35% versus 22.2%) and a greater pain reduction in the per-protocol analysis.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Network rankings have suggested benefit, but are not a reliable treatment hierarchy<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- The study's setting-specific economic analysis did not measure present-day local costs or access<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Botulinum Toxin A (intradetrusor)

The dedicated [Botulinum Toxin](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/neuromodulation-adjuncts/botulinum-toxin) article covers OAB and NDO uses; the IC/BPS-specific notes:

- **AUA position:** intradetrusor onabotulinumtoxinA may be administered after other treatments fail, with the patient willing to accept the possibility of clean intermittent self-catheterization (Statement 20, Option, Grade C)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Mechanism in IC/BPS:** inhibits ACh and ATP release from nerve and urothelium, leading to reduced hypersensitivity, pain signaling, and involuntary contractions<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Efficacy.** A meta-analysis of 12 RCTs found significant improvements in ICSI (SMD −0.302), ICPI (SMD −0.430), and VAS pain (SMD −0.576); in a 19-patient pilot RCT, intratrigonal 100 U versus placebo achieved ≥ 50% pain reduction at week 12 in 60% versus 22%<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- Older network analyses suggested improvement<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Dose:** the 2022 guideline notes that studies now mainly use 100 U; 200 U has also been studied, with more adverse events. Injection sites vary (posterior and lateral walls, or intratrigonal). The pilot RCT used 100 U as 10 trigonal injections of 1 mL each<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **AEs:** dysuria, large PVR, need for CISC, UTI<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Decision point:** shared decision-making weighs short-term efficacy against the meaningful possibility of CISC<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- A randomized trial analyzed 47 women receiving either 100 U onabotulinumtoxinA or six weekly heparin–lidocaine–bicarbonate instillations. At two months, mean ICSI and ICPI scores favored toxin (6.3 versus 9.6; 5.9 versus 8.3), but those score differences did not persist at six to nine months; 2/25 toxin recipients required self-catheterization.<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup>

## Other Intravesical Agents

### Intravesical oxybutynin

- Anticholinergic with local anesthetic properties<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- An older Cochrane review described possible benefit, but the evidence was limited<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- The 36-woman IC/BPS trial used 10 mg oxybutynin in 500 mL saline for staged bladder filling and training; it did not test a 20–30 mL, 30-minute instillation.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- Listed in the 2025 Global Consensus update<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Intravesical pentosan polysulfate (PPS)

- A network meta-analysis of 16 randomized IC/PBS trials, including two PPS comparisons, reported an urgency estimate favoring intravesical PPS over placebo (SMD −0.78, p=0.016), whereas its urinary-frequency estimate favored placebo (SMD +1.91, 95% CI +1.17 to +2.66).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> A 41-woman randomized trial compared intravesical PPS plus oral PPS with intravesical placebo plus oral PPS; median O'Leary-Sant symptom/problem score change at week 12 was −12 versus −5.5 (p = 0.04).<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup>
- Oral PPS is the only FDA-approved oral agent for IC/BPS, but PPS-associated pigmented maculopathy with chronic oral use has reshaped prescribing<sup>[[17]](#ref17)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Bacillus Calmette-Guérin (BCG)

- Immunomodulation based on autoimmune theory<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Mixed evidence; Cochrane found a trend to less pain and fewer general symptoms; network meta-analysis suggested GRA improvement<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **AUA.** BCG should not be offered outside investigational study settings (Statement 24, Standard, Grade B). Two randomized trials showed response rates not significantly different from placebo (21% versus 12% at seven months in the larger trial, n=265); in that trial, 95% of patients in each arm reported at least one adverse event, and life-threatening events including sepsis are described in the bladder-cancer literature<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Vanilloid agonists (resiniferatoxin, capsaicin)

- TRPV1-mediated C-fiber desensitization<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- The largest RCT (n = 163) of intravesical RTX found no improvement in symptoms, pain, urgency, frequency, nocturia, or void volume over 12 weeks; dose-dependent instillation pain<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- A meta-analysis of seven controlled trials enrolled 355 patients with IC or detrusor overactivity; the pooled RTX–saline bladder-pain VAS difference favored RTX by 0.42 points, with an IC subgroup estimate of 0.35 points (95% CI 0–0.70, p=0.05). The mixed-population analysis found no statistically significant difference in frequency, nocturia or incontinence.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- A network meta-analysis paradoxically ranked low-dose RTX favorably, but this likely reflects small-study effects and heterogeneity<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Not recommended for routine clinical use**<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### Intravesical steroids (triamcinolone)

- Local anti-inflammatory; primarily used as a cocktail component (with DMSO or heparin)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Intralesional triamcinolone for a Hunner lesion is distinct from a steroid-containing bladder instillation.** The AUA states that fulguration and/or triamcinolone injection should be performed when Hunner lesions are present (Statement 19, Recommendation, Grade C); this should not be described as a proven head-to-head RCT advantage.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
- Steroid dose depends on the formulation; adding triamcinolone has not been shown to improve every cocktail.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

## Emerging Therapies and Novel Delivery Systems

### Intravesical interferon

A single-center, double-masked 2024 RCT randomized 52 women with IC to intravesical interferon alfa-2b or HA; at six months, pain-score change favored interferon by 1.3 points (95% CI 0.3–2.3, p=0.02). Interferon remains investigational.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

### Sustained-release platforms

Conventional bladder instillations can lose drug exposure through dilution and voiding, prompting study of sustained-release systems.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[21]](#ref21)[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

- **TRG-100.** Sustained-release lidocaine plus oxybutynin studied in an open-label pilot including IC/BPS, OAB and ureteral-stent populations; this is not a confirmatory IC/BPS trial
- **Silk-elastinlike protein polymer (SELP).** Preclinical delivery platform; the reported 24-hour analgesic response was in a mouse model, not clinical evidence of durable patient benefit
- **Intravesical liposomes.** Under investigation for sustained delivery and barrier protection

### Systemic anti-TNF research and intravesical antiviral approaches

A randomized pilot studied subcutaneous certolizumab pegol in women with refractory IC/BPS; intravesical interferon was studied separately. Both remain investigational, and BK-virus phenotyping remains a research proposal.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)[[20]](#ref20)[[33]](#ref33)</sup>

## Comparative Effectiveness Summary

The 2020 Cochrane review found largely low- or very-low-certainty evidence across 81 small, heterogeneous trials.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> Reassess symptoms and harms after an agreed trial, and discontinue ineffective treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Clinical Positioning

- **DMSO** is FDA-approved; its label uses treatment every two weeks until maximum relief, then longer intervals.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Counsel about odor and discomfort; the label does not mandate exactly six treatments<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Heparin–lidocaine cocktails** may be selected according to response, tolerability and a verified local protocol; home use requires appropriate training and follow-up<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **HA / CS.** Some supportive comparative data, with remaining uncertainty; availability and regulatory status vary by product and country, and it is not FDA-approved for IC/BPS<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **OnabotulinumtoxinA** is reserved for refractory disease and only for patients willing to accept CISC; counsel and document<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Resiniferatoxin / capsaicin** are not appropriate for clinical use<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **DMSO enhances absorption** of co-administered drugs ; verify total local-anesthetic exposure and compatibility instead of assuming that a short dwell prevents toxicity<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Hunner lesions** warrant lesion-directed assessment and discussion of fulguration or intralesional triamcinolone; do not default to prolonged nonspecific instillations.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
