# Bladder Pain &amp; IC/BPS Pharmacotherapy

Pharmacotherapy for **interstitial cystitis or bladder pain syndrome (IC/BPS)** is individualized. The AUA 2022 guideline removed numbered treatment tiers: oral drugs and bladder instillations can be combined with appropriate behavioral and physical therapies, while lesion-directed treatment matters for Hunner lesions. Evidence for many drugs remains uncertain.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Oral pentosan polysulfate (PPS) is an AUA 2022 option (Grade B); CUA 2025 conditionally recommends standard care over it (low certainty), whereas EAU 2026 strongly recommends it for primary bladder pain syndrome.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> CUA 2025 cannot identify a preferred intravesical agent or cocktail.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The oral hub explains the PPS benefit–risk discussion and retinal surveillance.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For evidence on gabapentinoids and TCAs in neuropathic pain, and on vaginal diazepam in pelvic-floor myalgia, see [Neuropathic & Pelvic Pain](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/neuropathic-pelvic-pain). For chronic pelvic pain frameworks see [IC/BPS](/docs/clinical-conditions/03h-pelvic-pain/ic-pbs).
