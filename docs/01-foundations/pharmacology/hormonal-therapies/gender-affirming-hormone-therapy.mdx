# Gender-Affirming Hormone Therapy (GAHT)

**Gender-affirming hormone therapy (GAHT)** encompasses **feminizing regimens** (estrogen ± antiandrogens) and **masculinizing regimens** (testosterone) used to align physical characteristics with gender identity. The Endocrine Society 2017, WPATH Standards of Care Version 8 (2022), ACOG 2021, and AAFP 2023 all recognize GAHT as medically necessary care that improves psychological functioning, quality of life, and reduces gender dysphoria.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

This article is the pharmacology reference for the reconstructive urologist and urogynecologist who participates in GAHT-management decisions perioperatively or longitudinally. For surgical detail, see [Gender-Affirming Surgery overview](/docs/surgical-techniques). For related hormonal-therapy topics, see [Testosterone replacement](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/testosterone-replacement), [Vaginal and topical estrogen](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/hormonal-therapies/vaginal-topical-estrogen), and [Androgen adjuncts](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/androgen-adjuncts).

## Feminizing GAHT — estrogen ± antiandrogen

### Estrogen formulations

The primary agent is 17β-estradiol. SOC 8 recommends against ethinyl estradiol (statement 12.13) and suggests not prescribing conjugated estrogens when estradiol is available (12.15). Historical cohorts using older regimens reported high VTE rates, but the approximately 20-fold signal is not a direct modern head-to-head estimate for ethinyl versus transdermal estradiol.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup><sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

| Formulation | Dose | Route | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Oral estradiol** | 2–6 mg/day, individualized gradual titration | PO | Most commonly prescribed; **higher VTE risk** than transdermal<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Transdermal estradiol** | 0.025–0.2 mg/24 h patch, changed every 3–5 days<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[26]](#ref26)</sup> | Transdermal | SOC 8 suggests it for age &gt;45 or a previous VTE (12.14); smokers and BMI &gt;30 are additional reasons to prefer it<sup>[[26]](#ref26)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Estradiol valerate** | 2–10 mg IM weekly or 5–30 mg IM every 2 weeks (Endocrine Society 2017 and SOC 8 list valerate or cypionate together; titrate to avoid excessive peaks) | IM / SC | Peak/trough variation; comparative VTE risk is not established<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[26]](#ref26)</sup> |
| **Estradiol cypionate** | Same regimen as valerate (2–10 mg IM weekly or 5–30 mg every 2 weeks) | IM / SC | Similar to valerate<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[26]](#ref26)</sup> |
| **Ethinyl estradiol** | — | — | Do not use (SOC 8 recommendation 12.13); thrombotic risk is higher and estradiol assays do not monitor it<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[26]](#ref26)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Conjugated estrogens** | — | — | SOC 8 suggests not prescribing them when estradiol is available (12.15); doses cannot be regulated by serum levels<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[26]](#ref26)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |

**Target serum levels** (Endocrine Society):<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- Estradiol 100–200 pg/mL (wider range may be acceptable for individual goals)
- Testosterone &lt;50 ng/dL

### Antiandrogen agents

Physiologic estrogen doses alone are usually insufficient to suppress testosterone into the female range. SOC 8 recommends a testosterone-lowering medication (cyproterone acetate, spironolactone or a GnRH agonist) when the goal is to approximate cisgender female hormone concentrations (12.16), and it does not favor one agent over another.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

| Agent | Dose | Mechanism | Key considerations |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Spironolactone** | 100–300 mg/day (Endocrine Society 2017 and SOC 8 range)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[26]](#ref26)</sup> | AR antagonist; weak T-synthesis inhibitor | Most commonly prescribed testosterone-lowering agent in the US; monitor K⁺ and creatinine (hyperkalemia rare in healthy adults); weak estrogenic activity<sup>[[26]](#ref26)[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Cyproterone acetate** | 25–50 mg/day in Endocrine Society 2017; 10 mg/day in SOC 8, which uses the lowest effective dose<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[26]](#ref26)</sup> | Progestational antiandrogen; suppresses gonadotropins | Used in some non-US settings; **not approved in the US**. SOC 8 notes a meningioma signal with use beyond 2 years and doses above 10 mg/day, and the EMA restricts products of 10 mg/day or more; hepatotoxicity requires attention; hyperprolactinemia does not by itself establish prolactinoma<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup><sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **GnRH agonists** (leuprolide, goserelin) | Product- and indication-specific depot dosing | Suppress LH/FSH → profound T suppression | Very effective suppression; **very expensive** in US; used by specialist teams for puberty suppression; adult depot schedules are not automatically pediatric schedules<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Finasteride** | 1–5 mg/day | 5α-reductase inhibitor (T → DHT block) | Does not lower T. SOC 8 does not recommend routine use in transfeminine people whose T and DHT are already lowered; data are sparse<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[26]](#ref26)</sup> |
| **Progesterone / progestins** | Variable | Gonadotropin suppression | Anecdotal breast-development and mood benefit; **no well-designed studies**; MPA has WHI CV signal<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |

### Timeline of feminizing changes<sup>[[26]](#ref26)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Onset and maximum effect follow SOC 8 Appendix C Table 1; the Endocrine Society 2017 estimates are the same except where noted. The reversibility column is not given by either guideline.

| Change | Onset | Max effect | Reversibility |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Breast growth** | 3–6 mo | 2–5 y (2–3 y in Endocrine Society 2017) | **Irreversible** |
| Decreased sexual desire | 1–3 mo | Unknown (SOC 8) | Reversible |
| Decreased spontaneous erections | 1–3 mo | 3–6 mo | Reversible |
| Skin softening / ↓ oiliness | 3–6 mo | Unknown | Reversible |
| Fat redistribution (female) | 3–6 mo | 2–5 y | Reversible |
| Decreased terminal body / facial hair | 6–12 mo (Endocrine Society 2017) | &gt;3 y (complete removal needs electrolysis or laser) | Reversible |
| Decreased muscle mass / strength | 3–6 mo | 1–2 y | Reversible |
| Testicular-volume reduction | 3–6 mo | Variable in SOC 8; 2–3 y in Endocrine Society 2017 | Recovery and fertility after cessation are variable; not guaranteed |
| Decreased sperm production | Unknown | 2 y in SOC 8; &gt;3 y in Endocrine Society 2017 | Recovery is variable |
| Scalp hair | Variable | — | Familial scalp hair loss may occur if estrogen is stopped (Endocrine Society 2017) |
| **Voice** | — | — | **N/A** — estrogen does not raise pitch; speech therapy required |

## Masculinizing GAHT — testosterone

Regimens mirror the treatment of male hypogonadism and target the normal physiologic male range.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[26]](#ref26)</sup> See also [Testosterone replacement](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/testosterone-replacement) for the underlying pharmacology.

### Testosterone formulations

| Formulation | Dose | Route | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cypionate** | 50–100 mg SC/IM weekly (or 100–200 mg q2 wk) | SC or IM | **US first-line**; SC increasingly preferred (smaller needle, less pain)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Enanthate** | 50–100 mg SC/IM weekly (or 100–200 mg q2 wk) | SC or IM | Interchangeable with cypionate<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Undecanoate** | **US AVEED: 750 mg IM at baseline, 4 weeks, then every 10 weeks**; 1,000 mg regimens belong to other products/jurisdictions | IM only | US REMS; observe 30 minutes after every AVEED injection for POME/anaphylaxis. GAHT use is off-label.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup> |
| **Transdermal gel / patch** | Gel 50–100 mg/d; patch 2.5–7.5 mg/d | Transdermal | More uniform levels; titrate to clinical goals and measured levels; **transfer risk** to contacts<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Pellets** | 150–450 mg SC q3–6 mo | SC implant | Less common; requires minor procedure<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |

**Target serum levels:**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

- Testosterone in the physiologic male range; for injectable esters, measure midway between injections (target 400–700 ng/dL). For undecanoate, measure just before the next injection and adjust the interval if the level is below 400 ng/dL
- Avoid supraphysiologic levels (erythrocytosis, acne, adverse lipid profile)

### Timeline of masculinizing changes<sup>[[26]](#ref26)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Onset and maximum effect follow SOC 8 Appendix C Table 1; Endocrine Society 2017 differences are noted. Neither guideline gives the reversibility column.

| Change | Onset | Max effect | Reversibility |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cessation of menses | 1–6 mo | 1–2 y in SOC 8 | Reversible |
| **Clitoral enlargement** | 1–6 mo | 1–2 y | **Irreversible** |
| **Voice deepening** | 1–6 mo in SOC 8; 6–12 mo in Endocrine Society 2017 | 1–2 y | **Irreversible** |
| **Facial / body hair growth** | 6–12 mo | &gt;5 y in SOC 8; 4–5 y in Endocrine Society 2017 | **Irreversible** |
| Increased muscle mass | 6–12 mo | 2–5 y | Reversible |
| Fat redistribution (male) | 1–6 mo | 2–5 y | Reversible |
| Acne | 1–6 mo | 1–2 y | Reversible |
| Increased libido | Not tabulated | Not tabulated | Reversible |
| **Scalp hair loss (male-pattern)** | 6–12 mo | &gt;5 y in SOC 8 | Irreversible (genetic) |
| Vaginal atrophy | 1–6 mo | 1–2 y | Reversible |

Persistent uterine bleeding requires assessment of adherence, hormone levels, pregnancy risk, and other gynecologic causes. The Endocrine Society 2017 guideline says clinicians may consider adding a progestational agent or endometrial ablation. GnRH analogs or depot medroxyprogesterone can be given to stop menses before testosterone is started. Ablation requires separate fertility and procedural counseling.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Puberty suppression in adolescents

**GnRH agonists** can suppress further pubertal development from Tanner stage 2 in eligible adolescents after specialist assessment and individualized informed consent. Adult GAHT regimens should not be used as pediatric prescribing instructions.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup><sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### Rationale

- Prevents irreversible development of undesired secondary sex characteristics (breast development, voice deepening, facial hair, skeletal changes)
- Extends the diagnostic window for gender-identity exploration
- May relieve distress from unwanted pubertal changes; psychological outcome estimates come mainly from observational studies and do not establish a uniform causal reduction in suicidality
- Improves cosmetic outcomes if the patient later proceeds to GAHT

### Key considerations

- **Suppression of the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis is reversible** after stopping; this does not mean every downstream developmental outcome is known to be fully reversible
- **Bone mineral density** declines during treatment (particularly in AMAB individuals); may improve with subsequent hormones, but recovery is not uniform across sites or populations
- **Fertility implications.** GnRHa inhibits spermatogenesis and ovulation; fertility preservation counseling before initiation
- **Growth.** Height velocity slows during treatment; final height generally within genetic target

### Signal from real-world data

**Nos 2022** (*JAMA Network Open*) included 434 adolescents, 70 of whom received GnRHa. Median time to GAHT was 1.8 versus 1.0 years with versus without GnRHa. The authors concluded that GnRHa use was not associated with increased subsequent GAHT use. This observational association does not establish that blockers cause delay, determine future identity, or eliminate individual uncertainty.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Cardiovascular and thromboembolic risk — the dominant safety concern

### Feminizing GAHT — VTE

Transfeminine persons on estrogen have higher VTE incidence than cisgender women or cisgender men; risk varies by age, formulation, exposure duration, and baseline factors. Much of the historical signal reflects ethinyl estradiol, which is no longer recommended. Transdermal estradiol has a substantially better risk profile.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

**van Zijverden2025 online/2026 print** Dutch cohort (n = 2,714 transfeminine; 23,907 person-years):<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

- **MI:** SIR 0.50 (95% CI 0.32–0.71), lower than general-population men
- **Stroke:** SIR 0.94 (0.72–1.19), similar
- **VTE:** SIR 1.81 (1.33–2.35), higher

### Masculinizing GAHT — CV

Same Dutch cohort (n = 1,617 transmasculine; 13,457 person-years):<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

- **MI:** SIR 4.20 (2.72–6.01), significantly higher than general-population women
- **Stroke:** SIR 1.55 (1.01–2.20), higher
- **VTE:** SIR 1.00 (0.53–1.61), similar

Testosterone is also associated with erythrocytosis (most common AE), adverse lipid profile (↓HDL, ↑LDL), and higher BP.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

Older perioperative standards stopped hormones for about 1–4 weeks before and after genital surgery to limit VTE risk; SOC 8 notes this rested on no direct evidence and that VTE rates were not higher when hormones were continued. Use current multidisciplinary guidance for the individual operation.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

### Risk-mitigation

- **Transdermal estradiol** over oral for age &gt;45, smokers, BMI &gt;30, VTE risk<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Avoid ethinyl estradiol** (SOC 8 recommendation) **and conjugated estrogens** when estradiol is available (SOC 8 suggestion)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[26]](#ref26)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- Smoking cessation strongly encouraged<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Monitor hematocrit or hemoglobin in patients on testosterone (SOC 8 recommendation 12.17). The Endocrine Society 2017 guideline classes hematocrit above 50% as erythrocytosis; neither GAHT guideline sets a stop threshold. The 2018 hypogonadism guideline stops testosterone above 54%.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)[[1]](#ref1)[[31]](#ref31)</sup>
- **Individualize perioperative hormones with the surgeon and prescriber.** SOC 8 does not support a universal interruption (older standards used 1–4 weeks before and after surgery); weigh VTE risks, prophylaxis, and consequences of withdrawal, particularly after gonadectomy, where continuous sex-steroid replacement protects bone and mood. In one single-center series of 183 transfeminine patients having genital surgery, about 75% continued estradiol perioperatively under an individualized risk algorithm (those who held it for 2–6 weeks were older with higher Caprini scores); one VTE occurred and complication rates did not differ.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)[[30]](#ref30)</sup>
- Standard CV risk-factor management (lipids, BP, diabetes screening)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Monitoring protocols

### Feminizing GAHT<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

| Parameter | Frequency | Target / action |
|---|---|---|
| Serum estradiol | q3 mo × 1 y, then 1–2×/y | 100–200 pg/mL |
| Serum testosterone | q3 mo × 1 y, then 1–2×/y | &lt;50 ng/dL |
| **Potassium and creatinine** (if on spironolactone) | q3 mo × 1 y, then annually | Monitor for hyperkalemia and renal function<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup> |
| Prolactin | Endocrine Society 2017: baseline, at least annually during transition, then every 2 years. SOC 8 makes no recommendation for or against; individualize by regimen and symptoms | Pituitary imaging if levels rise persistently despite stable or reduced estrogen<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup> |
| Fasting lipids, glucose / HbA1c | Baseline then periodically | Standard CV-risk assessment |
| LH | Consider at follow-ups | Helps assess gonadal suppression |
| CBC | Baseline then annually | Hemoglobin may decrease |
| BMD | Consider at baseline; age 60 if low-risk or if not adherent to hormones; measure when risk factors exist, specifically after stopping hormones following gonadectomy | Osteoporosis screening |

### Masculinizing GAHT<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

| Parameter | Frequency | Target / action |
|---|---|---|
| Serum testosterone | q3 mo until at goal, then 1–2×/y | 400–700 ng/dL (midway between ester injections; just before the next undecanoate injection) |
| **Hematocrit / hemoglobin** | Baseline, then q3 mo × 1 y, then 1–2×/y | Hct above 50% is erythrocytosis in Endocrine Society 2017; no GAHT-specific stop threshold |
| Fasting lipids | Baseline then periodically | ↓ HDL, ↑ LDL / TG expected |
| Weight, BP | Every visit | Hypertension and weight gain surveillance |
| BMD | If T discontinued, non-compliant, or risk factors | Osteoporosis screening |
| **Cervical screening** (if cervix present) | Per ACOG | T-induced atrophy → **10× higher unsatisfactory Pap rate**; self-collected HPV may be preferable<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |

## Cancer screening — screen organs present, not identity

Screening is based on the anatomy present, regardless of gender identity.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

### Breast

- **Transfeminine.** Breast cancer incidence is lower than cisgender women (approximately 4.1 versus 155 per 100,000 person-years) but higher than cisgender men. **Screening timing varies by guideline and risk:** ACR considers mammography or DBT appropriate to discuss from age 40 after ≥5 years of hormones; use an individualized local screening pathway.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup> Dense breast tissue (60%) may increase false negatives.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Transmasculine.** If breast tissue remains (reduction mammoplasty, not mastectomy), follow cisgender female guidelines. After mastectomy, limited data support routine screening beyond clinical chest exam.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

### Cervical

All individuals with a cervix should follow standard screening guidelines. Transmasculine patients have lower screening rates due to discomfort and structural barriers; testosterone-induced atrophy drives a 10-fold higher rate of unsatisfactory Paps. Self-collected HPV testing may improve adherence.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### Prostate

Transfeminine patients retain their prostate regardless of surgery. PSA is substantially lowered by feminizing GAHT, with a median of 0.02 ng/mL in one series. PSA 1ng/mL is a proposed concern threshold in androgen-suppressed patients, not a prospectively validated universal normal limit; rising PSA warrants attention. Standard age-appropriate shared decision-making applies.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

## Fertility preservation — counsel before initiating

### Transfeminine

- Estrogen with antiandrogens causes increased sperm abnormalities, reduced count and motility, or azoospermia. Spermatogenesis may partially recover after cessation but is not guaranteed<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- **Sperm cryopreservation** should be offered before starting GAHT<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

### Transmasculine

- Testosterone causes ovarian cortical / stromal changes but persistent folliculogenesis is observed in most studies. Successful oocyte stimulation, fertilization, and live birth have been reported both with and without T discontinuation<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **Oocyte or embryo cryopreservation** should be offered before starting GAHT<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- **Testosterone is not reliable contraception.** Pregnancies have occurred during T therapy<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Discontinue T before a planned pregnancy until after delivery and any chest-feeding<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Urologic considerations

### Feminizing GAHT

- Decreased erectile function and testicular atrophy are expected<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Prostate undergoes atrophy over time (diffuse atrophy and basal-cell hyperplasia on histology)<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- After vaginoplasty, the shortened neourethra is generally uneventful, but urinary complications can occur<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

### Masculinizing GAHT

- **Vaginal atrophy** is common: dyspareunia, dryness, and urinary symptoms<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- **da Silva 2024** cross-section (n = 68 transmasculine on T): 94.1% reported at least one pelvic-floor symptom in this selected cross-sectional sample, not a population prevalence or proof of testosterone causation. Storage symptoms were 69.1%, sexual dysfunction 52.9%, anorectal symptoms 45.6%, urinary incontinence substantial<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- After **phalloplasty**, urethral lengthening drives fistulae, strictures, meatal stenosis, and post-voiding dribble; see the reconstructive literature<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

## Bone health

- **Feminizing:** adequate estrogen maintains BMD. The Endocrine Society 2017 guideline recommends BMD measurement when risk factors exist, specifically after stopping hormones following gonadectomy (Strong, recommendation 4.4). Table 15 also lists baseline testing as an option and screening at age 60 if low-risk<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Masculinizing:** adequate testosterone maintains BMD. Screen if T discontinued, non-compliant, or other risk factors<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **GnRHa in adolescents:** BMD Z-scores can decline during suppression; subsequent recovery varies, and long-term skeletal outcomes need continued follow-up<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Drug interactions

| Interaction | Effect | Practical |
|---|---|---|
| **Boosted PI antiretrovirals** | May decrease estradiol and increase T | Adjust monitoring; check levels<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> |
| **NNRTIs** (efavirenz, etravirine, nevirapine) | May decrease both estradiol and T | Same — monitor<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> |
| **Spironolactone + ACE-I / ARB / K⁺ supp / NSAIDs** | Hyperkalemia | Monitor K⁺<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Smoking** | Increases VTE risk synergistically with estrogen | **Strongly encourage cessation**<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Perioperative** | No universal estrogen hold; testosterone usually continues unless a patient-specific concern | Coordinate hormones and VTE prophylaxis<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup> |

## Mental-health outcomes

Multiple longitudinal and cross-sectional studies demonstrate that GAHT is associated with:<sup>[[24]](#ref24)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

- Reduced depression, anxiety, suicidality
- Improved global psychological functioning
- Improved QoL and body satisfaction
- Reduced gender dysphoria

The 2019 *NEJM* review (Safer & Tangpricha) summarized that gender-affirming care, including hormone therapy, is supported by multiple medical societies as medically necessary treatment that improves well-being.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Clinical Positioning

- **Use 17β-estradiol. SOC 8 recommends against ethinyl estradiol and suggests against conjugated estrogens when estradiol is available.** The historical VTE signal largely traces to ethinyl estradiol, and monitoring requires 17β-estradiol assays.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Prefer transdermal estradiol** in patients &gt;45 y or with a previous VTE (SOC 8 suggestion); smoking and BMI &gt;30 are additional reasons to prefer it. Oral is acceptable in low-risk patients, with full informed consent.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)[[5]](#ref5)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Spironolactone is the most commonly prescribed antiandrogen in the US; GnRH agonists are very effective for T suppression**; cyproterone is prescribed most often in the rest of Europe, is not available in the US and carries meningioma and hepatotoxicity flags. SOC 8 does not favor one agent over another.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[26]](#ref26)</sup>
- **Testosterone in transmasculine adults aims for the physiologic male range.** Target 400–700 ng/dL midway between ester injections; hematocrit or hemoglobin is monitored throughout.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[26]](#ref26)</sup>
- **Testosterone is NOT contraception.** Counsel explicitly and provide contraception separately if pregnancy avoidance is desired.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Fertility-preservation counseling must precede GAHT initiation.** Sperm cryopreservation (transfeminine) or oocyte / embryo cryopreservation (transmasculine). See [Androgen adjuncts](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/androgen-adjuncts) for the post-treatment fertility-recovery armamentarium.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **Cancer screening follows anatomy, not identity.** Prostate, cervix, and breast all require organ-based screening. PSA interpretation must account for androgen suppression; no universally validated threshold exists.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Risk differs by event and comparator.** The Dutch observational cohort shows a roughly 4× MI-risk elevation in transmasculine patients (versus cis women) and a roughly 1.8× VTE-risk elevation in transfeminine patients (versus general-population men). These are associations, not causal estimates of GAHT alone. Actively manage traditional CV risk factors alongside GAHT.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Perioperative management requires a joint plan**, including hormone formulation, prior VTE, immobility, prophylaxis, and effects of withdrawal. Routine cessation for every patient is not established.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>
- **Ask about pelvic-floor and genital symptoms** and treat the identified condition. A small selected cross-sectional sample cannot establish nearly universal dysfunction or justify treatment in asymptomatic people.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- **Adolescent treatment needs specialist assessment, fertility counseling, and discussion of uncertainty.** Nos 2022 is an observational treatment-timing study, not proof of causation or complete developmental reversibility.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Monitoring cadence is q3 months in year 1, then 1–2×/year** for both feminizing and masculinizing regimens, with the parameters above.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
