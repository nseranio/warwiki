# Preoperative Hormonal Priming

Perioperative vaginal estrogen can improve vaginal estrogenization and treat coexisting GSM, but IMPROVE did not demonstrate improved native-tissue apical prolapse repair success at 12 months or 3 years. The newer EVA trial found more women reporting 'much' or 'very much' improvement in prolapse symptoms on PGI-I at 12 months (97/106 versus 83/104); anatomical and composite surgical success did not differ significantly.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> Evidence for fewer postoperative UTIs remains uncertain. Pessary studies address a separate, nonsurgical setting.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

For broader context, see [Vaginal and topical estrogen](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/hormonal-therapies/vaginal-topical-estrogen), [Vaginal DHEA](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/hormonal-therapies/vaginal-dhea), [Ospemifene](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/hormonal-therapies/ospemifene), and the clinical [GSM](/docs/special-populations/05e-womens-health/gsm) article.

## Biological rationale

Vaginal tissue is estrogen-responsive; postmenopausal hypoestrogenism thins epithelium, reduces collagen, increases MMP activity, and impairs wound healing, all of which could theoretically compromise the substrate of a surgical repair.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### Histologic evidence — Rahn 2014 biopsy RCT

**Rahn 2014** randomized 30 postmenopausal women with ≥stage 2 POP to 6 weeks of preoperative conjugated estrogen cream (Premarin 0.625 mg/g, 1 g) versus placebo. Full-thickness vaginal-wall biopsies at hysterectomy; the tissue changes below are for drug-adherent participants (8 estrogen, 13 placebo):<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- **Epithelial thickness ↑ 1.8×** (p = 0.002)
- **Muscularis thickness ↑ 2.7×** (p = 0.088)
- **Collagen type Iα1 mRNA ↑ 6.0×** in the muscularis (p &lt;0.05)
- Changes in a pro-anabolic matrix-protein profile

### Animal model — Balgobin 2013

Ovariectomized guinea pigs treated with estradiol showed increased smooth muscle, vaginal-wall thickness, total / cross-linked collagen, and sustained 12-fold elevation of lysyl oxidase mRNA at 21 days post-injury versus near-baseline in estrogen-deprived animals, supporting a short-term animal tissue-remodeling mechanism without establishing stronger repair or durable human outcomes.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

### Wound-healing meta-analysis — Vodegel 2022 (14 studies; 11 in the meta-analysis)

The review combined human and animal evidence; several strength, collagen, and vascular endpoints came from animal models. These surrogate results cannot establish better human prolapse-repair durability:<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

| Parameter | SMD (95% CI) | Direction | Species |
|---|---|---|---|
| Macroscopic wound closure | 1.82 (1.22–2.42) | ↑ | Women and animals |
| Tissue strength | 1.26 (0.53–1.99) | ↑ | Animals |
| Neovascularization | 1.13 (0.67–1.60) | ↑ | Animals |
| Collagen synthesis | 1.08 (0.42–1.74) | ↑ | Animals |
| Microscopic wound closure | 0.98 (0.66–1.29) | ↑ | Animals |
| Granulation tissue | 1.67 (0.54–2.79) | ↑ | Women and animals |
| Inflammatory response | −0.58 (−1.14 to −0.02) | ↓ | Women and animals |
| TGF-β1 levels | −1.68 (−2.52 to −0.83) | ↓ | Animals |

The mechanistic case is strong. The clinical translation is the story of the IMPROVE trial.

## IMPROVE — multicenter randomized trial

The **IMPROVE trial** (Investigation to Minimize Prolapse Recurrence Of the Vagina using Estrogen) is a multicenter trial with longer-term follow-up, published in *JAMA* 2023 with 3-year follow-up in *AJOG* 2024.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### Design

- 3 tertiary US sites (Texas, Alabama, Rhode Island); randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled superiority trial
- 206 enrolled; 199 randomized; 186 underwent surgery: postmenopausal women with bothersome anterior / apical POP (≥ stage 2)
- **Intervention:** 1 g conjugated estrogen cream (0.625 mg/g) or placebo, nightly × 2 weeks, then twice weekly for ≥5 weeks preop, continued twice weekly for 12 months postop<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- All participants: vaginal hysterectomy (if uterus present) and standardized uterosacral or sacrospinous ligament suspension
- **Primary outcome:** composite failure at 12 months (anterior or posterior prolapse beyond the hymen or apical descent greater than one-third vaginal length; subjective bulge symptoms; or retreatment)

### 12-month results<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Outcome | Vaginal estrogen | Placebo | HR (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Composite failure at 12 mo** | **19% model estimate (20 events)** | **9% model estimate (10 events)** | **1.97 (0.92–4.22)** — not significant |
| Failure components | Anatomic recurrence was the most common component, often partial apical descent without prolapse beyond the hymen or symptoms | — | — |
| **Surgeon-assessed tissue quality** at surgery | **Significantly better** | — | p &lt;0.05 |
| Granulation tissue after month 6 (secondary outcome) | 16/90 (18%) | 7/87 (8%) | p = 0.048; no repeat operations |
| Atrophy symptoms at 12 mo (baseline-symptomatic subset, n = 109) | **Significantly better** | — | p &lt;0.05 |

**Trial authors' conclusion:** "Adjunctive perioperative vaginal estrogen application did not improve surgical success rates after native-tissue transvaginal prolapse repair."<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### 3-year follow-up — Rahn 2024<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- Composite failure at 36 mo: model-estimated 32.6% (estrogen) versus 26.8% (placebo); adjusted HR 1.55 (95% CI 0.90–2.66; p = 0.11)<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Per-protocol analysis: similar
- Among 36-month PGI-I respondents, 75/80 (94%) in the estrogen group and 72/76 (95%) in the placebo group reported being "much or very much better"; this included 51 of 55 "surgical failures".<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- POP-Q, PGI-S, PFDI-20, and PFIQ-7 all improved significantly in both arms without between-group differences
- **Substantial crossover at 36 months.** Among 160 participants reporting estrogen use, 40/82 (49%) of the original estrogen group and 47/78 (60%) of the original placebo group were using vaginal estrogen (p = 0.15)<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

In IMPROVE, perioperative vaginal estrogen improved surgeon-rated tissue quality at surgery, but a reduction in composite repair failure was not demonstrated at 12 or 36 months; the estimates remain imprecise.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## EVA — August 2026 multicenter trial

The **EVA trial** randomized 311 women across 22 Dutch hospitals to perioperative estriol cream or placebo before primary native-tissue repair for stage ≥2 POP; the published report describes 293 participants. EVA used estriol cream 1 mg/g, 0.5 g (0.5 mg) intravaginally daily for 2 weeks, then twice weekly; treatment began 4–6 weeks preoperatively, continued through 12 months postoperatively, and paused for the first 2 postoperative weeks.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

At 12 months, 236 participants remained in follow-up, but the primary PGI-I response was available for 210: 97/106 (91.5%) versus 83/104 (79.8%) reported being much or very much improved (OR 2.73, 95% CI 1.18–6.28; p = 0.02). This roughly 12-percentage-point difference was smaller than the trial's prespecified 15-point threshold for clinical importance. Anatomical outcomes, composite surgical success, sexual function and reintervention did not differ significantly. Secondary outcomes favored estrogen for pelvic-floor quality of life (median PFDI-20 17 versus 25; p = 0.03) and a higher proportion reporting no discomfort or pain (EQ-5D-5L 81/105 [77.1%] versus 63/104 [60.6%]; p = 0.04).<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

The finding supports discussing symptom-focused adjunctive use. Substantial missing outcome data, only 12 months of follow-up and differences from IMPROVE in procedures and formulation limit interpretation.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

## Preoperative effect on pelvic-floor symptoms — IMPROVE ancillary

**Rahn 2023 ancillary analysis** of 7 weeks of preoperative vaginal estrogen on pelvic-floor symptoms before surgery (n = 191):<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

| Symptom | Estrogen improved | Placebo improved | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| SUI (bothersome at baseline) | 16/32 (50%) | 9/21 (43%) | 0.78 |
| UUI (bothersome at baseline) | 13/30 (43%) | 11/36 (31%) | 0.41 |
| Urinary frequency (bothersome at baseline) | 18/44 (41%) | 12/47 (26%) | 0.18 |
| Dyspareunia at preoperative assessment (sexually active participants) | 42% had dyspareunia | 48% had dyspareunia | 0.49 |
| Most bothersome atrophy symptom (adherent) | Slightly more | — | 0.19 |
| **Objective atrophy signs (adherent)** | **+1.54** | **+0.69** | **0.01** |

Despite measurable objective improvement in vaginal estrogenization among adherent participants, the clinical-symptom improvement was inconclusive: urinary function, sexual function, dyspareunia, and atrophy symptoms did not reach statistical significance versus placebo.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Other perioperative evidence

Marschalek 2021 (103 participants) reported fewer postoperative complications and less antibiotic use, although the masked surgical tissue assessment did not show a clear treatment difference. These secondary outcomes from one small trial need cautious interpretation.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

The 2018 Haya Cochrane review (search to November 2017) predates IMPROVE and other 2021–2023 trials. Its 2025 update (search to April 2024) included three perioperative vaginal-estrogen trials (409 women); the postoperative-UTI estimate was uncertain (OR 0.67, 95% CI 0.36–1.26; two trials, 289 women; low-certainty evidence).<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> The later Sicilia 2025 systematic review summarizes ten RCTs and concludes that possible tissue and infection benefits remain limited by the evidence.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The 2026 Vacca meta-analysis (9 RCTs, 764 women, searched to December 2024) found improved tissue quality (standardized mean difference 0.73, 95% CI 0.16–1.30; I² 86.7%) but no difference in composite surgical success (RR 0.94, 95% CI 0.83–1.06), with a trend toward lower success in the estrogen groups.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> Its conclusion supports estrogen for tissue optimization and atrophy symptoms, not for improving surgical success.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

## Postoperative UTI reduction — a possible benefit

**Taithongchai 2023 Cochrane review** reported fewer postoperative UTIs with topical estrogen added to surgery, but stressed variation between contributing studies and insufficient evidence for firm overall conclusions.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

**Sicilia 2025 BJOG systematic review** (10 RCTs, n = 709) reported possible benefits but emphasized limited evidence: vaginal estrogen appeared to reduce postoperative UTIs and antibiotic use, while showing no significant impact on surgical failure or POP recurrence.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The Vacca 2026 meta-analysis judged the UTI effect inconclusive (RR 0.75, 95% CI 0.27–2.04; I² 67.2%), with one study showing harm and two showing benefit.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

The Cochrane authors cautioned that contributing studies varied substantially. IMPROVE itself found no statistically significant reduction in participants with UTI (19% versus 25%; p = 0.38).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Routine preoperative estrogen solely as infection prophylaxis is therefore not established.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

Separate evidence in women with recurrent UTI is more supportive: the Chen 2021 meta-analysis showed that vaginal estrogen reduces rUTI generally (RR 0.42). See [Vaginal and topical estrogen](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/hormonal-therapies/vaginal-topical-estrogen) and [Non-antibiotic UTI prevention](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/infection-prophylaxis/non-antibiotic-uti-prevention).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Pessary users — Zhou 2025 BMJ RCT

**Zhou 2025** (420 randomized, 411 included in the modified intention-to-treat analysis; 12 academic centers in China): postmenopausal women with ≥ stage 2 POP successfully fitted with ring pessaries, randomized to vaginal estrogen cream versus placebo for 12 months:<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

- **Pessary continuation with satisfaction at 12 months:** 181/208 (87.0%) (estrogen) versus 176/203 (86.7%) (placebo); risk difference 0.3% (95% CI −6.2 to 6.9; p = 0.92), no difference<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Pessary-related adverse events reduced:**
  - Excessive discharge: 34/208 (16.3%) versus 52/203 (25.6%) (risk difference −9.3 percentage points; 95% CI −17.1 to −1.4)<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
  - Vaginal erosion or ulcer: 4/208 (1.9%) versus 14/203 (6.9%); vaginal bleeding: 3/208 (1.4%) versus 13/203 (6.4%). These were separate secondary outcomes.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

Zhou's secondary adverse-event findings may support individualized estrogen use in pessary users; continuation was unchanged, and perioperative surgery was not studied.

## Midurethral sling outcomes

### Mesh exposure — Cadish 2016 retrospective cohort

**n = 1,544 Kaiser Permanente cohort:** preoperative vaginal estrogen was not associated with reduced mesh exposure after midurethral sling placement (OR 0.79; 95% CI 0.26–2.38; p = 0.67). Age, BMI, menopausal status, smoking, and diabetes were also not associated with exposure risk.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

### QoL and sexual function — Caruso 2020 RCT

**n = 96** postmenopausal women undergoing TOT (80 completed): ultralow-dose vaginal estriol ovules 0.03 mg daily × 16 weeks (before and after surgery) versus no treatment:<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

- Within the estriol group, Vaginal Maturation Index rose from baseline (38.1) to 43.1 pre-op (p = 0.04) and 47.8 post-op (p = 0.001)
- FSFI improved more after TOT with estriol than in controls (25.2 versus 17.2 post-op; p = 0.001)
- Mental-health QoL improved in the estriol group before surgery (p = 0.02); controls improved only after surgery

The EAU 2026 evidence summary rates local estrogen before or after synthetic midurethral sling surgery as ineffective for improving SUI surgical outcomes (level of evidence 2b), while strongly recommending vaginal estrogen for postmenopausal SUI with vulvovaginal atrophy symptoms.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> In Caruso's unblinded TOT trial, 36/48 estriol-treated participants and 44/48 controls completed 8-week follow-up; sexual-function and quality-of-life scores improved, but sling continence and mesh exposure were not trial outcomes.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

## Typical priming protocols

There is no validated universal priming protocol. The following are study regimens, not interchangeable labeled schedules for every product or vaginal operation:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

| Protocol | Formulation | Loading | Maintenance | Total | Context |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **IMPROVE** | CEE cream 0.625 mg/g, 1 g | Nightly × 2 wk | 2×/wk | ≥5 wk preop + 12 mo postop | POP repair (USLS / SSLS)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **EVA 2026** | Estriol cream 1 mg/g, 0.5 g/application | Daily × 2 wk | 2×/wk; planned 2-wk postop pause | 4–6 wk preop + 12 mo postop | Primary native-tissue POP repair<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| **Rahn 2014 biopsy RCT** | CEE cream 0.625 mg/g, 1 g | Nightly × 2 wk | 2×/wk | 6 wk preop (range 4–8) | POP + hysterectomy<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Marschalek 2021**<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | Linoladiol estradiol cream 0.01% (0.10 mg/g) | Daily × 1 wk | Every 48 h × 1 wk, then twice weekly × 4 wk | 6 wk preop in the trial; exceeds EMA's current single-course 4-week maximum for Linoladiol<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> | POP repair |

## Guideline positions

ACOG's POP bulletin describes limited evidence for treating or preventing prolapse with estrogen. The 2023 Cochrane review searched through June 2022 and therefore predates IMPROVE's published surgical results; its uncertainty should be interpreted alongside the later trial and follow-up. The 2025 AUA/SUFU/AUGS GSM guideline recommends offering the option of local low-dose vaginal estrogen to patients with GSM-related vulvovaginal discomfort/irritation, dryness, and/or dyspareunia (Strong Recommendation; Grade C), and recommends it for patients with GSM and recurrent UTI to reduce future UTI risk (Moderate Recommendation; Grade B). Prescribe for an individual GSM or recurrent-UTI indication when appropriate.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)[[15]](#ref15)[[10]](#ref10)[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## What the evidence supports — and what it does not

| Claimed benefit | Evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Improves tissue quality (histology, collagen, VMI) | Small human biopsy RCT plus mixed human/animal meta-analysis | **Supported**<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Improves surgeon-perceived tissue quality | Trial findings differ (IMPROVE, Marschalek) | Improvement in IMPROVE; no clear masked tissue-quality difference in Marschalek<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **Reduces postoperative UTIs** | Earlier review suggested benefit<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>; updated perioperative reviews report imprecise, inconsistent estimates<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[19]](#ref19)</sup> | **Uncertain; no demonstrated reduction** |
| Reduces postoperative complications / antibiotic use | Limited (1 RCT) | Possibly supported<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **Reduces pessary-related erosion / bleeding** | Secondary outcomes in multicenter RCT | **Possible benefit; continuation unchanged**<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Reduces prolapse recurrence | IMPROVE 12- and 36-month estimates were imprecise | **No demonstrated reduction**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Improves surgical success rates | IMPROVE did not demonstrate improved composite success | **No demonstrated benefit**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Improves preoperative urinary symptoms | Inconclusive (IMPROVE ancillary) | **No demonstrated benefit; effect remains uncertain**<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Improves preoperative sexual function | Inconclusive (IMPROVE ancillary) | **No demonstrated benefit; effect remains uncertain**<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Reduces mesh exposure after sling | Retrospective cohort; 37 mesh exposures among 1,544 women | **No association detected; estimate imprecise** (OR 0.79, 95% CI 0.26–2.38)<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |

## Clinical Positioning

- **Treat coexisting GSM or recurrent UTI when indicated.** Routine priming has not been shown to prevent prolapse recurrence; improvement in tissue appearance is a surrogate outcome.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Counsel that improved repair success was not demonstrated**, rather than promising no recurrence or claiming estrogen causes failure. The trial was not designed to prove equivalence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Use formulation-specific dosing.** IMPROVE's regimen is a trial protocol. A 10 µg estradiol tablet generally uses a two-week daily loading period followed by twice-weekly maintenance; see the [prescribing hub](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/hormonal-therapies/vaginal-topical-estrogen).<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- **Pessary findings apply to pessary care.** Zhou's trial found fewer selected adverse events, with no continuation advantage.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **DHEA is not an automatic safe alternative when estrogen is contraindicated.** It is metabolized to estrogens and androgens, and its label warns about breast-cancer history. No established priming benefit justifies routine substitution; see [prasterone](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/hormonal-therapies/vaginal-dhea).
- Continue GSM treatment according to benefit, tolerability, and patient preference; follow current product-specific contraindications and warnings. Reassess treatment response; long-term treatment and follow-up may be required.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
