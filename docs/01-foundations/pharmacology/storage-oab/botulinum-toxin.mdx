# Botulinum Toxin (OnabotulinumtoxinA &amp; Variants)

**Botulinum toxin** is a zinc-metalloprotease neurotoxin from *Clostridium botulinum* that cleaves SNARE proteins to block presynaptic acetylcholine release at the neuromuscular junction and at parasympathetic, sympathetic, and sensory afferent terminals. In urology, the relevant clinical formulation is onabotulinumtoxinA (Botox®, AbbVie / Allergan), FDA-approved for refractory non-neurogenic OAB (2013) and neurogenic detrusor overactivity (NDO, 2011). Other serotypes and formulations (Dysport, Xeomin, MyoBloc, Daxxify) are not FDA-approved for urologic indications, although abobotulinumtoxinA (Dysport) has European and investigational urologic data.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

For the procedural details of intradetrusor injection, including instrument selection, dilution, injection technique, and post-injection care, see [Intradetrusor Botox Injection](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/intradetrusor-botox).

## Mechanism

Botulinum neurotoxin type A heavy chain binds to SV2 receptors on cholinergic presynaptic terminals; the light chain is internalized and cleaves SNAP-25, disabling synaptic-vesicle fusion. The net effect at the detrusor:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- **Motor blockade:** reduced acetylcholine release at detrusor smooth muscle, decreasing involuntary contraction amplitude.
- **Proposed sensory modulation:** reduced release of ATP, substance P, and CGRP and down-regulation of TRPV1 and P2X3 receptors may contribute to decreased afferent signaling.
- Onset 4–7 days; peak effect 2–4 weeks; duration typically 6–9 months in OAB or NDO.

Efferent acetylcholine blockade is established; sensory modulation may contribute to urgency improvement, but the relative contributions of these pathways in OAB and NDO are not established.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Indications

### Refractory Non-Neurogenic OAB

FDA-approved 2013 based on the Nitti and Chapple trials:<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- **Nitti 2013** (n = 557, 100 U versus placebo): daily urinary-incontinence episodes decreased by 2.65 versus 0.87 at 12 weeks; complete continence occurred in 22.9% versus 6.5%.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Chapple 2013** (n = 548): comparable magnitude (-2.95 versus -1.03 daily incontinence episodes at 12 weeks); CIC was started in 6.9% versus 0.7%.
- The 3.5-year open-label extension reported sustained benefit with repeat treatment, a median duration of effect of 7.6 months, and new catheterization in 4.0% after the first treatment (0.6–1.7% after later ones); this is an uncontrolled extension, not a 5-year randomized comparison.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

A double-blind phase 3b trial in solifenacin-naïve patients with OAB and urinary incontinence, after inadequate response to or intolerance of an anticholinergic, randomized patients to onabotulinumtoxinA 100 U, solifenacin 5 mg (escalation to 10 mg allowed at week 6) or placebo. At 12 weeks, daily incontinence episodes fell by 3.19, 2.56 and 1.33, respectively (both active arms p &lt; 0.001 versus placebo), and 33.8%, 24.5% and 11.7% were dry. In a post hoc comparison, the incontinence reduction was greater with onabotulinumtoxinA than with solifenacin (p = 0.022); UTI (25.5%) and urinary retention (6.9%) were more common with onabotulinumtoxinA.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

AUA/SUFU 2024 supports choosing botulinum toxin, sacral neuromodulation, or tibial nerve stimulation through shared decision-making; a fixed sequence of behavioral and drug failures is not required by the guideline. The FDA-labeled OAB indication still specifies inadequate response to, or intolerance of, an anticholinergic medication. Discuss expected benefit, retention or UTI risk, treatment burden, and access rather than promising one universally superior option.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

The guideline adds practical points: measure PVR before injection and use caution when it exceeds about 100-200 mL relative to voided volumes; if the patient fails a β3-agonist or declines oral drugs, it is reasonable to go directly to botulinum toxin without an antimuscarinic trial; offer it cautiously when symptoms are mainly nocturnal (the nocturia benefit is marginal); and counsel that repeat injections are typically needed every 3-12 months. Trigone-sparing and trigone-including techniques are both effective, and the placebo-controlled trials used trigone-sparing injection. Patients with an inadequate response to 100 U and minimal side effects may be offered 200 U, with counseling that retention and UTI risk may rise.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> The EAU 2026 female LUTS guideline recommends 100 U (Strong) for OAB/UUI refractory to conservative or drug treatment, notes that other doses and other botulinum toxin A brands are not licensed for OAB in Europe, and finds no difference between suburothelial and intradetrusor injection (level 1a).<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> For payer coverage criteria, see [Billing & Coding](/docs/resources/hidden-curriculum/billing-coding#coding-and-coverage-notes-20252026).

### Neurogenic Detrusor Overactivity (NDO)

FDA-approved 2011 (spinal cord injury, multiple sclerosis):<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

- **Cruz 2011 and Ginsberg 2012** trials enrolled 691 patients across 200 U, 300 U and placebo arms. At 6 weeks, 200 U reduced incontinence and improved cystometric capacity and detrusor pressure compared with placebo; numerical effects differ by trial and should not be treated as one pooled estimate. A meta-analysis of 14 studies (734 participants; each study included at least 50% with spinal cord injury) found large before-and-after effects on bladder capacity, compliance and catheterization frequency.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- 300 U gave no clear efficacy gain over 200 U in these trials, with more catheterization (Ginsberg 2012: among participants not catheterizing at baseline, clean intermittent catheterization started in 35% at 200 U and 42% at 300 U, versus 10% on placebo); 200 U is the labeled adult NDO dose.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- A randomized post-approval study in noncatheterizing adults with MS found that 100 U improved incontinence versus placebo at week 6; 10/66 (15.2%) versus 2/78 (2.6%) started CIC during the treatment cycle. The 100-U dose is off-label under US adult NDO dosing, and that study did not compare 100 with 200 U directly.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- Patients must be willing and able to perform catheterization if needed. In adult NDO trial participants not using CIC at baseline, 30.6% (33/108) started CIC for retention after 200 U versus 6.7% (7/104) after placebo; median catheterization duration among affected 200-U recipients was 289 days.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- A small uncontrolled before-and-after study of 30 patients with neurogenic detrusor overactivity (14 with spinal cord injury, 15 with multiple sclerosis, 1 with myelitis) receiving 300 U detrusor injections found symptomatic urinary infections (pyelonephritis, orchitis or prostatitis) fell from a mean 1.75 (SD 1.87) per patient in the 6 months before injection to 0.2 (SD 0.41) per patient in the 6 months after (p = 0.003), tracking the degree of urodynamic improvement. Keep this separate from the label's RCT-derived UTI adverse-event rates above: it used a higher, non-labeled dose, mixed etiologies, and no control arm, so it does not establish that treatment prevents infection or outweighs post-treatment UTI risk in an individual patient.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

For patients with high-risk neuro-urological dysfunction, the 2026 EAU Neuro-Urology guideline recommends (Strong) regular upper-tract assessment and urodynamic follow-up; after injection, urodynamics may be needed to monitor the effect on bladder pressure.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

### Other Urologic Applications (Off-Label)

- **External urethral sphincter** injection for **detrusor-sphincter dyssynergia** (DSD) in SCI or MS (pharmacologic sphincterotomy); dose and technique vary by center (commonly about 100 U).
- **Bladder neck** injection for primary bladder neck obstruction in select patients.
- **Interstitial cystitis or bladder pain syndrome:** variable efficacy; in a randomized trial (n = 60) of refractory IC/BPS, 100 U injected submucosally with hydrodistention reduced pain more than saline (VAS -2.6 versus -0.9 at 8 weeks).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Chronic pelvic pain or pelvic floor myofascial pain:** pelvic-floor injection (off-label, evidence limited).
- **Painful bladder after intravesical chemotherapy:** case series.

## Adverse Effects

The adult label reports the following rates; populations and observation windows matter.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

| Outcome | OAB 100 U | Adult NDO 200 U |
|---|---|---|
| UTI within 12 weeks | 18% | 24% |
| Initiation of CIC for retention over the treatment cycle | 6.5% | 30.6% **among patients not using CIC at baseline** |
| Elevated PVR not requiring catheterization within 12 weeks | 3% | Not reported in this comparison |
| Hematuria within 12 weeks | Not listed in the label's 12-week adverse-reaction table | 4% |
| Dysuria within 12 weeks | 9% (placebo 7%) | Not listed in the label's 12-week adverse-reaction table; 4% at any time before re-injection |

**Do not inject intradetrusor Botox during a UTI, or in a patient with urinary retention or PVR >200 mL who is not routinely performing CIC.** In patients not catheterizing, assess PVR within 2 weeks (when retention risk peaks) and periodically as appropriate through 12 weeks; the guideline also advises a PVR, urinalysis and culture if symptoms fail to improve or worsen after injection. Counsel about difficulty voiding and the possible need for catheterization. The boxed warning covers distant toxin spread, including potentially life-threatening swallowing or breathing difficulty.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

Higher baseline PVR was associated with retention in one retrospective practice cohort that included 100- and 200-U injections: 56/160 patients (35%) had retention, including 17/18 (94%) with pre-injection PVR of at least 100 mL. The clinicians used a lower threshold for catheterization than the pivotal trials.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> In the label's OAB 100-U trials, retention occurred in 12.3% of patients with diabetes versus 6.3% without diabetes; among adult NDO 200-U recipients not using CIC at baseline, CIC for retention began during the treatment cycle in 31% (27/86) with multiple sclerosis versus 27% (6/22) with spinal cord injury.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> Among OAB patients after 100-U Botox injection, UTI occurred in 44% of those whose maximum post-treatment PVR was at least 200 mL versus 23% of those whose maximum was below 200 mL.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> Detrusor hypocontractility should be counseled about accordingly. Pregnancy and lactation are not listed label contraindications: human pregnancy data are inadequate and animal studies show possible fetal harm; milk-transfer and infant-effect data are absent. Discuss individual risks and benefits. Concurrent aminoglycosides may potentiate neuromuscular blockade.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

## Re-Treatment &amp; Antibody Formation

- Re-treatment interval often 6–9 months (the AUA/SUFU guideline cites a typical range of 3–12 months); minimum 12 weeks between injections per FDA labeling.
- **Neutralizing-antibody-mediated resistance** is rare for urologic dosing but documented at high cumulative doses; consider it among causes of secondary loss of response in a prior responder.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> In the label's bladder programs, among patients with analyzable specimens, neutralizing antibodies developed in 0 of 954 OAB patients while receiving 100-U doses and in 3 of 260 (1.2%) after subsequently receiving at least one 150-U dose; in adult NDO they developed in 3 of 300 (1.0%) after receiving only 200-U doses and in 5 of 258 (1.9%) after at least one 300-U dose.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> The label notes that these rates depend on the assay.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- Switching serotype A formulations is not a validated standard or a dose-conversion strategy. In a retrospective NDO series after onabotulinumtoxinA failure, 32/57 patients (56%) had clinical and/or urodynamic benefit after switching to abobotulinumtoxinA; this has been described as an off-label option for selected patients.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

## Formulation Comparison

| Formulation | Brand | Serotype | Urology FDA status | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| OnabotulinumtoxinA | **Botox** | A | **Approved** for OAB &amp; NDO | Reference product |
| AbobotulinumtoxinA | Dysport | A | Not approved (urology) | European urologic experience; **no validated direct unit conversion to Botox** |
| IncobotulinumtoxinA | Xeomin | A | Not approved | No complexing proteins; theoretical lower immunogenicity |
| PrabotulinumtoxinA | Jeuveau | A | Not approved | Cosmetic-only US indication |
| DaxibotulinumtoxinA | Daxxify | A | Not approved | Peptide-stabilized; longer cosmetic duration claim |
| RimabotulinumtoxinB | MyoBloc | B | Not approved | Used when A-resistance develops in non-urologic indications |

**Unit potency is not interchangeable across formulations.** Clinical doses cannot be directly converted. Use brand-specific dosing.
