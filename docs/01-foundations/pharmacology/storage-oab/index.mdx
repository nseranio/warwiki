# Storage &amp; Overactive Bladder Pharmacotherapy

Pharmacologic therapy for **overactive bladder (OAB)** and **neurogenic lower urinary tract dysfunction (NLUTD)** works through two complementary mechanisms at the detrusor: blocking cholinergic contraction (antimuscarinics) or stimulating sympathetic relaxation (β3-agonists). A third distinct agent, desmopressin, addresses nocturnal polyuria rather than storage per se.

This subcategory covers the two oral drug classes for storage symptoms, desmopressin for nocturnal polyuria, and intradetrusor botulinum toxin. For the full clinical framework see the [AUA/SUFU OAB guideline](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38651651/), the [OAB clinical-condition article](/docs/clinical-conditions/03a-storage-incontinence/urgency-incontinence-oab) and the [Nocturia](/docs/clinical-conditions/03a-storage-incontinence/nocturia) article.

## Contemporary Treatment Selection (AUA/SUFU 2024)

Offer bladder training and behavioral measures, and choose additional treatment through shared decision-making. Offer an antimuscarinic or a β3-agonist for urgency, frequency and urgency incontinence (Strong Recommendation, Grade A); counsel on the side effects of each and discuss dementia and cognitive risk with patients taking an antimuscarinic. If one drug fails or is not tolerated, a different drug or a different class may be tried, and combination therapy from different classes may be offered after inadequate monotherapy (Conditional Recommendation, Grade B). When medication or behavioral therapy has failed or caused intolerable side effects, sacral neuromodulation, percutaneous tibial nerve stimulation and intradetrusor onabotulinumtoxinA are all offered (Moderate Recommendation, Grade A). The guideline also allows these procedures through shared decision-making without requiring prior behavioral or drug trials (Expert Opinion). Measure post-void residual before botulinum toxin injection, and repeat it if symptoms worsen or fail to improve afterward. Discuss catheterization and UTI risk and treatment burden when choosing among options. See the [AUA/SUFU guideline](https://www.auanet.org/documents/Guidelines/PDF/2024%20Guidelines/OAB%202024%20Unabridgedv2.pdf).

The EAU 2026 non-neurogenic female LUTS guideline is broadly concordant. It offers β3-agonists to women who fail conservative management (Strong), uses long-term anticholinergics with caution in older women and those at risk of cognitive dysfunction (Strong), and offers 100 U onabotulinumtoxinA for refractory OAB/UUI after counseling on limited duration, UTI and possible intermittent catheterization (Strong). It also offers desmopressin for nocturia from nocturnal polyuria in women, with sodium monitoring in older patients and avoidance when baseline sodium is low (Strong). Procedural detail is on the [Intradetrusor Botox Injection](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/intradetrusor-botox) page.
