# Female Testosterone for HSDD — Off-Label Transdermal Therapy

**Transdermal testosterone** is the only evidence-based pharmacotherapy for **postmenopausal hypoactive sexual desire disorder (HSDD)** per the **2019 Global Consensus Position Statement** on the use of testosterone therapy for women. It is **off-label in the United States** — there is no FDA-approved female testosterone formulation — and it is the standard postmenopausal-HSDD option not addressed by the premenopausal-only FDA-approved agents (flibanserin, bremelanotide).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For the male testosterone-replacement framework (TRAVERSE 2023, hypogonadism, prostate-cancer safety) see [Testosterone Replacement](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/testosterone-replacement). For the clinical-conditions framework on female sexual dysfunction see [Sexual Dysfunction (Women's Health)](/docs/special-populations/05e-womens-health/sexual-dysfunction). For the FDA-approved premenopausal options see [Flibanserin](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/flibanserin) and [Bremelanotide](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/bremelanotide).

## Indication

The **only evidence-based indication for testosterone therapy in women is postmenopausal HSDD** — distress about decreased sexual desire that is not better explained by:

- Coexisting medical / psychiatric condition.
- Relationship factors.
- Effects of a medication (especially SSRIs / SNRIs).
- Genitourinary syndrome of menopause (GSM) — which should first be addressed with vaginal estrogen / DHEA / ospemifene.

Per the **2019 Global Consensus Position Statement** (Davis 2019, endorsed by IMS, ESM, ISSM, ISSWSH, NAMS, RANZCOG, FSPS, RCOG, FIGO, AAGL, ACOG-supportive): testosterone therapy in postmenopausal women is **not recommended for any other indication** — including general well-being, cognitive performance, cardiovascular protection, bone density, or musculoskeletal benefit.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Mechanism and rationale

Endogenous testosterone in women is produced by the ovaries and adrenal glands; circulating levels decline gradually with age and decline more abruptly after surgical menopause / bilateral oophorectomy. The **causal contribution of low testosterone to HSDD is unproven** — there is no validated serum-testosterone threshold below which HSDD is more likely, and serum-testosterone measurement is **not recommended** to diagnose HSDD or to select candidates for therapy. The clinical decision is symptom-driven, not biomarker-driven.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Efficacy — Islam 2019 *Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol* meta-analysis

**Islam 2019** — the foundational evidence base. Meta-analysis of **36 RCTs / 8,480 participants** of testosterone in women, mostly postmenopausal:<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- **Satisfying sexual events (SSEs)** — significant increase of approximately **+0.85 / month** over placebo.
- **FSFI** — significant improvement in desire, arousal, orgasm, and pleasure domains.
- **Distress** — significantly reduced.
- Effects on responsiveness, self-image, and reduced concerns about sexual function — all significantly improved.
- **No effect on serious adverse events, lipid profile (with non-oral routes), or anthropometric measures** when delivered transdermally.

Subsequent reviews and the 2025 *Obstet Gynecol* expert update confirm consistent efficacy of transdermal testosterone in postmenopausal HSDD.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Dosing and formulations

| Formulation | Status | Dosing |
|---|---|---|
| **Transdermal testosterone 1% cream / gel (Australian-approved AndroFeme)** | TGA-approved in Australia for postmenopausal HSDD | 0.5 mL daily to thigh / abdomen — delivers ~ 5 mg testosterone |
| **Compounded transdermal testosterone (US off-label)** | Not regulator-approved; compounded | Variable; clinical practice often targets serum total testosterone in the **mid-to-upper female reference range** |
| **Fractionated dose of male transdermal product** | Off-label US practice when compounded preparations are unavailable | A small fraction of a male AndroGel pump or testosterone-cream dose, delivering ~ 5 mg/day |
| **Oral testosterone** | **Not recommended** | Oral undecanoate adversely affects HDL / LDL cholesterol — transdermal route preferred<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Testosterone implants / pellets** | Not recommended | Supraphysiologic peaks; difficult to titrate or remove; associated with virilizing AEs |
| **DHEA (systemic)** | **Not effective; not recommended** for HSDD per ACOG Level A<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | — |

**More than 2 million testosterone prescriptions are written annually for women in the US**, almost all off-label.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Monitoring

Per the Global Consensus and 2025 expert update.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- **Baseline serum total testosterone** to ensure baseline is not already supraphysiologic.
- **Repeat serum total testosterone at 3–6 weeks** after initiation, then every 6 months — confirm levels remain in the **normal premenopausal female reference range**.
- **Clinical assessment for androgen excess** (acne, hirsutism, voice changes, alopecia) at every visit.
- **Trial duration 3–6 months** — discontinue at 6 months if no symptomatic response.
- Avoid supraphysiologic dosing — supratherapeutic levels are associated with virilization and lipid changes.

## Adverse effects

- **Acne** — common, especially with supraphysiologic dosing.
- **Hirsutism** — facial / body hair, particularly with longer treatment duration.
- **Weight gain** — variable.
- **Voice deepening, alopecia, clitoromegaly** — reported with supraphysiologic dosing; may not be reversible; rare at physiologic dosing.
- **Lipid effects** — significant with oral testosterone (HDL ↓, LDL ↑); minimal or absent with transdermal route.
- **Long-term safety** — including breast-cancer and cardiovascular outcomes — has not been established. The Global Consensus explicitly notes that long-term safety data are lacking.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Counseling and prescribing pearls

- **Postmenopausal HSDD only.** No evidence base for premenopausal use; generally avoided.
- **GSM-driven dyspareunia and arousal symptoms should be addressed first** with vaginal estrogen / DHEA / ospemifene before considering systemic testosterone.
- Set realistic expectations: meta-analysis effect size is **+0.85 SSE/month** vs placebo — modest but the strongest effect size of any HSDD pharmacotherapy.
- **Transdermal route only.** Oral testosterone is not recommended.
- **Aim for the upper end of the female reference range, not the male range.**
- Counsel explicitly on the **off-label status** in the US and the **lack of long-term safety data** as part of informed consent.
- Discontinue at 6 months if no symptomatic response.
- Compounded products vary in concentration / quality — when possible, use a regulator-approved formulation (e.g., Australian AndroFeme for women traveling between regulatory jurisdictions, or fractionated doses of FDA-approved male transdermal products with careful monitoring).
