# Bremelanotide (Vyleesi) — Female HSDD

**Bremelanotide (Vyleesi)** is FDA-approved for **acquired, generalized hypoactive sexual desire disorder (HSDD)** in **premenopausal women only** — the second FDA-approved pharmacotherapy for female HSDD (2019). Unlike daily flibanserin, bremelanotide is dosed **on-demand** as a subcutaneous injection ~ 45 minutes before anticipated sexual activity.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For the clinical-conditions framework on female sexual dysfunction see [Sexual Dysfunction (Women's Health)](/docs/special-populations/05e-womens-health/sexual-dysfunction). For the FDA-approved daily alternative see [Flibanserin](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/flibanserin). For postmenopausal HSDD off-label transdermal testosterone see [Female Testosterone for HSDD](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/female-testosterone-hsdd).

---

## Mechanism

**Melanocortin-4 receptor (MC4R) agonist** with additional MC1R activity. Activation of central MC4R receptors is hypothesized to modulate brain pathways involved in sexual desire and arousal. The mechanism is distinct from flibanserin and from hormonal pathways.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Indication

Premenopausal women with **acquired, generalized HSDD** — distress about decreased sexual desire that is **not** due to:

- Coexisting medical or psychiatric condition.
- Problems with the relationship.
- Effects of a medication or other drug substance.

**Not approved for postmenopausal women** and **not approved for men.**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Dosing

- **1.75 mg subcutaneously** in the abdomen or thigh, **on demand** ~ 45 minutes before anticipated sexual activity.
- Maximum **one dose per 24 hours**.
- **No more than 8 doses per month** is recommended.
- Discontinue if no improvement after **8 weeks** of treatment.

## Contraindications

- **Uncontrolled hypertension** or known cardiovascular disease — bremelanotide produces a transient, dose-dependent increase in blood pressure (~ +6 mmHg systolic, +2.8 mmHg diastolic peaking at ~ 2–4 hours, with a corresponding 5 bpm decrease in heart rate).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Drug interactions

- **Oral medications** — bremelanotide may slow gastric emptying and reduce / delay absorption of concomitantly administered oral drugs. Avoid co-administration with oral medications where rapid absorption matters; specifically, **co-administration with naltrexone (oral) may reduce naltrexone exposure** and is not recommended in patients receiving oral naltrexone for alcohol- or opioid-use-disorder treatment.

## Efficacy — RECONNECT phase 3 trials

**RECONNECT** (Kingsberg 2019, n = 1,267) — two identical 24-week phase-3 RCTs — showed statistically significant improvements over placebo:<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- **FSFI-desire domain** — increase of **+0.35** over placebo (p &lt; 0.001).
- **Female Sexual Distress Scale-Desire/Arousal/Orgasm (FSDS-DAO) Item 13** — significant reduction in distress over decreased sexual desire.
- Number of satisfying sexual events (SSEs) — modest, statistically significant improvement.

The **52-week open-label extension** (Simon 2019) showed sustained improvements with no new safety signals.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

A **prespecified subgroup analysis** (Simon 2022) confirmed efficacy was consistent across age, weight, BMI, and baseline testosterone subgroups.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Adverse effects

| Event | Incidence |
|---|---|
| **Nausea** | ~ 40% (most common; particularly with first 1–2 doses; pre-medication with antiemetic may help) |
| **Flushing** | ~ 21% |
| **Headache** | ~ 12% |
| **Injection-site reactions** | ~ 13% |
| **Vomiting, dizziness, nasal congestion** | Less common |
| **Focal hyperpigmentation** | Reported with frequent / high-cumulative dosing — particularly in patients with darker skin |
| **Transient blood-pressure increase** | Dose-dependent; basis for the cardiovascular contraindication |

## Counseling and prescribing pearls

- **On-demand dosing** is the key advantage over daily flibanserin for patients who prefer not to take a chronic medication.
- Counsel that **nausea is the dominant adverse effect** — typically improves with subsequent doses; consider antiemetic pre-medication for the first one or two administrations.
- **Blood-pressure screening** is essential before prescribing; avoid in uncontrolled hypertension.
- Allow **8 weeks** (≥ 8 doses) before judging response.
- **Avoid in patients on oral naltrexone** for alcohol- or opioid-use disorder.
- Discuss focal-hyperpigmentation risk with patients who have darker skin or who anticipate frequent dosing.
