# Flibanserin (Addyi) — Female HSDD

**Flibanserin (Addyi)** is FDA-approved for **acquired, generalized hypoactive sexual desire disorder (HSDD)** in **premenopausal women only** — not for HSDD due to a coexisting medical or psychiatric condition, problems with the relationship, or the effects of a medication or other drug substance. It was the first FDA-approved pharmacotherapy for female HSDD (2015).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For the clinical-conditions framework on female sexual dysfunction see [Sexual Dysfunction (Women's Health)](/docs/special-populations/05e-womens-health/sexual-dysfunction). For the FDA-approved alternative on-demand option see [Bremelanotide](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/bremelanotide). For postmenopausal HSDD off-label transdermal testosterone see [Female Testosterone for HSDD](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/female-testosterone-hsdd).

---

## Mechanism

Multifunctional serotonin agonist / antagonist (MSAA) acting as a **5-HT1A agonist and 5-HT2A antagonist** with weaker affinity at 5-HT2B, 5-HT2C, and dopamine D4 receptors. Net effect — increased dopamine and norepinephrine + decreased serotonin in mesocorticolimbic circuits implicated in sexual desire.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Indication

Premenopausal women with **acquired, generalized HSDD** — distress about decreased sexual desire that is **not** due to:

- Coexisting medical or psychiatric condition.
- Problems with the relationship.
- Effects of a medication or other drug substance.

**Not approved for postmenopausal women** and **not approved for men.**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Dosing

- **100 mg orally once daily at bedtime.**
- Discontinue if no improvement after **8 weeks**.

## Boxed warning — alcohol and severe hypotension / syncope

- **Alcohol must be avoided within 2 hours of dosing.**
- Concurrent alcohol use significantly increases the risk of severe hypotension and syncope.
- Originally launched with a REMS program restricting prescribing and dispensing; **the alcohol-related REMS was modified in 2019** to a contraindication-based labeling rather than mandatory prescriber certification.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Contraindications

- Concomitant alcohol consumption.
- Concomitant **strong or moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors** (e.g., ketoconazole, ritonavir, clarithromycin, fluconazole, diltiazem) — markedly increases flibanserin exposure.
- **Hepatic impairment** of any severity.

## Drug interactions

- CYP3A4 substrate — strong / moderate inhibitors are contraindicated; inducers (rifampin, carbamazepine, phenytoin, St John's wort) reduce efficacy.
- CYP2C19 minor pathway.
- Avoid concomitant CNS depressants (additive hypotension / sedation).

## Efficacy

A meta-analysis of 8 RCTs (n = 5,914) showed an increase of approximately **+0.5 satisfying sexual events (SSEs) per month** over placebo, with statistically significant but modest improvements in FSFI-desire and Female Sexual Distress Scale-Revised scores.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The clinical magnitude is small relative to placebo response and is one of the central debates around flibanserin's risk-benefit profile.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Adverse effects

- **Dizziness** (~ 11%).
- **Somnolence** (~ 11%).
- **Nausea** (~ 10%).
- **Fatigue** (~ 9%).
- **Insomnia, dry mouth, sedation.**
- **Severe hypotension and syncope** (boxed warning) — particularly with alcohol or CYP3A4 inhibitors.

## Counseling and prescribing pearls

- Bedtime dosing minimizes daytime sedation and hypotension exposure.
- Patients must be counseled explicitly on **alcohol avoidance within 2 hours of the dose** (and ideally for the rest of the night).
- Onset of action is gradual — **8 weeks** of daily use before assessing response.
- Set realistic expectations regarding the **modest +0.5 SSE/month effect size** vs placebo.
- Re-screen for medical / psychiatric / relational causes if no response by 8 weeks; discontinue if no benefit.
