# Flibanserin (Addyi) — Female HSDD

**Flibanserin (Addyi)** is FDA-approved for acquired, generalized hypoactive sexual desire disorder (HSDD) in women younger than 65, including postmenopausal women (December 2025 label expansion), characterized by low sexual desire causing marked distress or interpersonal difficulty, not attributable to a coexisting medical or psychiatric condition, relationship problems, or medication effects.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> It was the first FDA-approved pharmacotherapy for female HSDD (2015), after two prior FDA rejections, and its approval was accompanied by significant debate about the medicalization of women's sexuality, whether the modest benefits justified the risks, and concerns about pharmaceutical-industry influence on FDA decision-making.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

For the clinical-conditions framework on female sexual dysfunction see [Sexual Dysfunction (Women's Health)](/docs/special-populations/05e-womens-health/sexual-dysfunction). For the FDA-approved alternative on-demand option see [Bremelanotide](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/bremelanotide). For postmenopausal HSDD off-label transdermal testosterone see [Female Testosterone for HSDD](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/female-testosterone-hsdd).

---

## Mechanism of Action

The precise mechanism of action in HSDD is not fully established. Flibanserin is a 5-HT₁A receptor agonist and 5-HT₂A receptor antagonist, with moderate antagonist activity at 5-HT₂B, 5-HT₂C, and dopamine D₄ receptors.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> It acts postsynaptically, preferentially at prefrontal-cortex pyramidal neurons, resulting in sustained increases in dopamine and norepinephrine and transient decreases in serotonin in select brain regions (prefrontal cortex, nucleus accumbens, hypothalamus), a profile thought to disinhibit pathways involved in sexual desire.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Flibanserin was originally developed as an antidepressant but showed only mild antidepressant activity; it was subsequently repurposed for HSDD.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

---

## Indication

Women younger than 65 with acquired, generalized HSDD — distress about decreased sexual desire that is not due to:

- Coexisting medical or psychiatric condition.
- Problems with the relationship.
- Effects of a medication or other drug substance.

**Not indicated for men or to enhance sexual performance.**<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Dosing and Administration

- **100 mg orally once daily at bedtime.**<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Bedtime dosing required to reduce the risk of hypotension, syncope, and CNS depression.
- Patients should avoid activities requiring full alertness (e.g., driving) for at least **6 hours** after dosing.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Discontinue after 8 weeks** if no improvement in symptoms.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Efficacy

Flibanserin's efficacy is modest. Findings.

- **Jaspers 2016 *JAMA Intern Med*** meta-analysis of 8 RCTs (n = 5,914 women): flibanserin 100 mg increased satisfying sexual events (SSEs) by +0.49 per month over placebo, with small improvements in eDiary desire and FSFI desire-domain scores.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Simon 2019** pooled analysis of the three pivotal trials (VIOLET, DAISY, BEGONIA; n = 2,465 premenopausal women): mean change from baseline 2.1 vs 1.2 SSEs per 28 days (flibanserin vs placebo; post hoc analysis), with statistically significant improvements in desire and reductions in sexual distress.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Kamrul-Hasan 2024 *Medicine*** meta-analysis of 8 RCTs (n = 7,906 women) found flibanserin superior to placebo in both premenopausal (SSE mean difference +0.69 per month) and postmenopausal women, though adverse events were more frequent; the December 2025 label now includes postmenopausal women younger than 65.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup><sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Toledo 2026 *J Minim Invasive Gynecol*** systematic review and meta-analysis (36 studies of desire, arousal and orgasm disorders): flibanserin improved total FSFI and desire and reduced distress; no trial directly compared CBT with drug therapy.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- ACOG PB 213 notes the overall quality of efficacy and safety evidence is "very low," with an average improvement of less than one additional satisfying sexual event per month.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

> **"The efficacy of flibanserin is modest. In a meta-analysis of eight trials including 5,914 participants, flibanserin was shown to have increased the number of satisfying sexual experiences per month by 0.5 but with considerable side effects."** — Davis SR, *Sexual Dysfunction in Women*, *N Engl J Med* 2024<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Boxed Warning and Contraindications

Flibanserin carries a **boxed warning for hypotension and syncope** in specific settings.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Alcohol interaction (boxed warning)

- Taking flibanserin within 2 hours of alcohol consumption increases the risk of **severe hypotension and syncope**.
- Patients should wait **≥ 2 hours after 1–2 standard drinks** before taking the dose, or **skip that evening’s dose if ≥ 3 drinks** were consumed. After dosing, **avoid alcohol until the following day**.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### CYP3A4 inhibitors (contraindicated)

- **Contraindicated with moderate or strong CYP3A4 inhibitors** (e.g., ketoconazole, fluconazole, certain HIV protease inhibitors) — these increase flibanserin concentrations and the risk of severe hypotension / syncope.
- Wait **2 weeks after the last CYP3A4-inhibitor dose** before restarting flibanserin; stop flibanserin **at least 2 days before** starting a moderate/strong CYP3A4 inhibitor.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Multiple concomitant **weak CYP3A4 inhibitors** (e.g., ginkgo, resveratrol, cimetidine, oral contraceptives, fluoxetine) may also increase concentrations.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Hepatic impairment (contraindicated)

- **Contraindicated in hepatic impairment of any degree** — flibanserin exposure increases **4.5-fold**.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse Effects

Most common adverse reactions in the premenopausal label trials (100 mg at bedtime vs placebo). Dizziness, somnolence and nausea each occurred in about 10% or more of treated women.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

| Event | Flibanserin | Placebo |
|---|---|---|
| **Dizziness** | 11.4% | 2.2% |
| **Somnolence** | 11.2% | 2.9% |
| **Nausea** | 10.4% | 3.9% |
| **Fatigue** | 9.2% | 5.5% |
| **Insomnia** | 4.9% | 2.8% |

Somnolence, sedation or fatigue combined occurred in about 21% vs 8% (premenopausal trials) and 10% vs 6% (postmenopausal women younger than 65).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Risk ratio for study discontinuation due to adverse events compared with placebo: 2.19 (95% CI 1.50–3.20).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

Serious adverse events and severe adverse events were comparable between flibanserin and placebo groups across the 8-RCT meta-analysis.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> Hypotension occurred in 0.2% and syncope in 0.4% of flibanserin-treated premenopausal patients even without concomitant risk factors.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Flibanserin does not prolong the QT interval to a clinically relevant extent.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Special Populations

- **Strong CYP2C19 inhibitors** (e.g., proton-pump inhibitors, SSRIs, benzodiazepines, some antifungals) may raise flibanserin exposure; discuss before prescribing.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **CYP2C19 poor metabolizers** (2–15% of Asians, 2–5% of Caucasians / Africans): increased flibanserin exposure; increased monitoring for hypotension is recommended.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Postmenopausal women.** The December 2025 label includes women younger than 65; the postmenopausal efficacy trial lasted 24 weeks.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup><sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Not indicated in men** or to enhance sexual performance.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Safety and effectiveness have not been established in geriatric patients (≥ 65 yr).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## REMS Program and Prescribing Requirements

Both the prescribing physician and the dispensing pharmacist were originally required to complete a Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS) certification. FDA removed the prescriber/pharmacy certification elements on October 9, 2019; the modified REMS retained a Medication Guide. Counseling must follow the current alcohol-timing and interaction instructions above.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Guideline Recommendations

| Guideline | Position |
|---|---|
| **ACOG 2019 (Practice Bulletin 213)**<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | Flibanserin **can be considered** for HSDD in premenopausal women without depression who are appropriately counseled about alcohol risks; evidence quality is "very low" and benefits are minimal. |
| **AAFP 2025 (Dalrymple)**<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | **Psychological interventions** (CBT, mindfulness-based therapy, sex therapy) are recommended **first-line** for disorders of sexual desire and arousal; flibanserin is noted as having modest benefits and being relatively expensive. |
| **Davis 2024 NEJM review**<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | Characterizes flibanserin's efficacy as modest; notes that bremelanotide (Vyleesi) is the alternative FDA-approved pharmacologic option for premenopausal HSDD, administered as a subcutaneous injection on an as-needed basis. |

## Counseling and Prescribing Pearls

- **Bedtime dosing minimizes** daytime sedation and hypotension exposure.
- Patients must be counseled explicitly on alcohol avoidance — wait ≥ 2 hours after 1–2 drinks before the dose, skip the dose if ≥ 3 drinks, avoid alcohol until the following day after dosing.
- Onset of action is gradual — allow 8 weeks of daily use before assessing response.
- Set realistic expectations regarding the modest +0.49 SSE/month effect size vs placebo.
- Re-screen for medical, psychiatric or relational causes if no response by 8 weeks; discontinue if no benefit at 8 weeks per the FDA label.
- Screen for moderate or strong CYP3A4 inhibitors and hepatic impairment at every refill — both are contraindications.
- Discuss first-line psychological interventions (CBT, mindfulness, sex therapy, couples therapy) before or alongside pharmacotherapy.
- Consider [bremelanotide](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/bremelanotide) for patients who prefer on-demand dosing or cannot tolerate daily bedtime sedation, and [transdermal testosterone](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/female-testosterone-hsdd) for postmenopausal patients (off-label).
