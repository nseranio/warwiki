# Sexual Medicine &amp; Andrology Pharmacotherapy

Pharmacotherapy for **erectile dysfunction (ED), Peyronie's disease (PD), priapism, male hypogonadism, and female hypoactive sexual desire disorder (HSDD)**. The toolkit spans oral first-line therapy (PDE5 inhibitors), second-line intracavernosal and intraurethral injectables, intralesional plaque-modifying agents (clostridial collagenase, interferon, verapamil), the testosterone-replacement framework reshaped by the **TRAVERSE 2023 cardiovascular non-inferiority** result, and the female-HSDD agents (flibanserin, bremelanotide, and off-label transdermal testosterone, for which no FDA-approved product exists).

For surgical management of these conditions see the [Sexual Dysfunction Treatment Atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction). For the clinical article on [Peyronie's disease](/docs/clinical-conditions/03g-genital-scrotal/peyronies-disease) see the linked page; male hypogonadism is covered under [Testosterone Replacement](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/testosterone-replacement).

  <li>
    <a href="/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/pde5-inhibitors" className="section-stack-title section-stack-link">PDE5 Inhibitors</a>
    <span className="section-stack-desc">Sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil, avanafil. FDA-approved for ED; tadalafil also for BPH (AUA 2026 recommends 5 mg daily for LUTS/BPH). Label doses, renal and CYP3A4 adjustments, and the nitrate contraindication (Trolle Lagerros 2024 *JACC* mortality data). Post-prostatectomy rehabilitation (Sari Motlagh network meta, REACTT, Jo 2018 early start). Off-label uses with limited evidence: prevention of recurrent ischemic priapism (Burnett 2014 RCT negative; Hou 2021 retrospective; PIN 2025 phase 2), Peyronie's curvature (Spirito 2024 retrospective), adjunct to an alpha-blocker for distal ureteral stones, and transplant tacrolimus interactions.</span>
  </li>
  <li>
    <a href="/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/intracavernosal-injection-agents" className="section-stack-title section-stack-link">Intracavernosal Injection Agents</a>
    <span className="section-stack-desc">An AUA 2018 ED treatment option; oral agents need not fail first. Alprostadil, BiMix / TriMix / QuadMix compounds (none FDA-approved) and aviptadil-phentolamine (Invicorp; not FDA-approved; Al-Mitwalli 2025). Dosing, teaching, fibrosis and priapism risk, the 4-hour emergency rule, and phenylephrine rescue for prolonged erection. Plaque injection for Peyronie's disease is covered on the Peyronie's page.</span>
  </li>
  <li>
    <a href="/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/intraurethral-alprostadil" className="section-stack-title section-stack-link">Intraurethral Alprostadil (MUSE)</a>
    <span className="section-stack-desc">MUSE applicator technique and US label strengths (250, 500 and 1000 micrograms; first dose under supervision). Padma-Nathan 1997 *NEJM* pivotal (home intercourse 64.9% vs 18.6% among clinic responders); clinic series report roughly 35–78% success; Shabsigh 2000 / Shokeir 1999 head-to-head vs ICI (injection more effective, MUSE better accepted); ACTIS band; MUSE plus PDE5 inhibitor for salvage; McCullough 2010 open comparison in post-prostatectomy rehabilitation (no significant difference from sildenafil).</span>
  </li>
  <li>
    <a href="/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/testosterone-replacement" className="section-stack-title section-stack-link">Testosterone Replacement</a>
    <span className="section-stack-desc">**TRAVERSE 2023** finding MACE non-inferiority in its studied population; Snyder 2024 fracture paradox; libido and sexual activity improve, erectile function only slightly; full formulation table including oral undecanoate (Jatenzo / Tlando / Kyzatrex) and nasal Natesto; T4DM vs TRAVERSE glycemic contrast; prostate-cancer safety (TRAVERSE substudy, Baik 2025 Medicare HR 0.84, García-Becerra 2026 41-RCT meta, saturation-model rationale); fertility-preservation alternatives.</span>
  </li>
  <li>
    <a href="/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/androgen-adjuncts" className="section-stack-title section-stack-link">Androgen Adjuncts</a>
    <span className="section-stack-desc">hCG (the agent with a labeled male hypogonadotropic-hypogonadism indication), clomiphene, anastrozole and enclomiphene (the last three not FDA-approved for men). AUA/ASRM 2024 conditional support in men who want fertility preserved. Habous 2018 RCT (similar testosterone rise with clomiphene, hCG or both); Muir 2025 meta-analysis of spermatogenesis induction in gonadotropin deficiency; Helo 2015 clomiphene vs anastrozole RCT; Burnett-Bowie 2009 anastrozole bone-loss signal; combination strategies.</span>
  </li>
  <li>
    <a href="/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/peyronies-disease-agents" className="section-stack-title section-stack-link">Peyronie's Disease Agents</a>
    <span className="section-stack-desc">CCH (Xiaflex; the only FDA-approved drug; label indication, REMS, injection and modeling, boxed warning for corporal rupture; IMPRESS 52-week curvature improvement 33–35% vs 18–22% with placebo; Ziegelmann 2023 incremental benefit by cycle; Goldstein 2020 five-year follow-up); intralesional interferon α-2b (no longer recommended by EAU 2026) and verapamil (AUA 2015 conditional; EAU 2026 finds evidence insufficient; Cochrane very low certainty); **AUA recommends against** vitamin E, tamoxifen, procarbazine, omega-3 and vitamin E plus L-carnitine; PDE5 inhibitors for coexisting ED; ESWT, traction and hyaluronic acid; PRP (Ledesma phase 2, small).</span>
  </li>
  <li>
    <a href="/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/priapism-management" className="section-stack-title section-stack-link">Priapism Management</a>
    <span className="section-stack-desc">Intracavernosal phenylephrine (AUA/SMSNA 2022 and EAU 2026 regimens, with blood-pressure monitoring); methylene blue for injection-induced priapism (Martínez Portillo 2001 series); recurrent ischemic priapism, where no preventive drug is established: daily PDE5 inhibitor (Burnett 2014 RCT negative; PIN 2025 phase 2), ketoconazole with glucocorticoid replacement (Hoeh-Levine series; hepatotoxicity boxed warning), and hormonal suppression with its fertility impact.</span>
  </li>
  <li>
    <a href="/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/flibanserin" className="section-stack-title section-stack-link">Flibanserin (Addyi)</a>
    <span className="section-stack-desc">FDA-approved for **acquired, generalized HSDD in women younger than 65, including postmenopausal women** (label expanded December 2025). Daily 100 mg PO at bedtime; 5-HT1A agonist / 5-HT2A antagonist. Boxed warning: alcohol-related severe hypotension and syncope; contraindicated with moderate or strong CYP3A4 inhibitors and in hepatic impairment. Common adverse reactions in label trials: dizziness (11.4%) and somnolence (11.2%). Modest efficacy: meta-analysis of 8 RCTs / 5,914 women shows about **+0.5 satisfying sexual events per month** over placebo.</span>
  </li>
  <li>
    <a href="/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/bremelanotide" className="section-stack-title section-stack-link">Bremelanotide (Vyleesi)</a>
    <span className="section-stack-desc">FDA-approved for **acquired, generalized HSDD in premenopausal women only** (2019). On-demand 1.75 mg SC ~45 min before activity; melanocortin-4 receptor agonist. **RECONNECT phase 3** n=1,267 (Kingsberg 2019; Simon 2019 / 2022): FSFI-desire +0.35 vs placebo (p&lt;0.001), but satisfying sexual events did not differ from placebo; the 52-week open-label extension raised no new safety signals. Nausea in about 40%; transient BP rise, so avoid in uncontrolled hypertension or known cardiovascular disease.</span>
  </li>
  <li>
    <a href="/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/female-testosterone-hsdd" className="section-stack-title section-stack-link">Female Testosterone for HSDD</a>
    <span className="section-stack-desc">**Postmenopausal HSDD** is the evidence-based indication in the **2019 Global Consensus Position Statement**; ISSWSH also discusses selected late-reproductive-age women with more limited evidence. Off-label in the US (no FDA-approved product for women). Islam 2019 *Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol* meta-analysis of 36 RCTs / 8,480 women: about **+0.85 satisfying sexual events per month** over placebo. Transdermal route only; monitor to avoid exceeding the premenopausal reference range, not to target its upper end; 3–6 month trial; discontinue at 6 months if no response.</span>
  </li>
</ul>
