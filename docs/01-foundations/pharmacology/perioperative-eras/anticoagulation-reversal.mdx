# Anticoagulation Reversal — Drug-Class Hub

Anticoagulation reversal is a rapidly evolving field, and the single most important **recent change** the reconstructive urologist needs to know: **andexanet alfa (Andexxa) has been withdrawn from the US market** after the FDA concluded in December 2025 that its risks (notably excess thromboembolic events) outweigh its benefits; AstraZeneca ended US sales on December 22, 2025.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> As a result, **4-factor PCC is now the primary reversal strategy for apixaban- and rivaroxaban-associated life-threatening bleeding** — update institutional reversal algorithms accordingly. Idarucizumab (Praxbind) remains the standard for dabigatran reversal.

This article is the **drug-class pharmacology hub** — mechanism, dose, evidence, safety for each reversal agent (idarucizumab, 4F-PCC, vitamin K, protamine) along with the post-andexanet landscape and the emerging ciraparantag pipeline. The **clinical workflow** — procedure-specific bleed-vs-thrombosis weighing, DOAC hold timing, bridging, antiplatelet management — lives at [Antithrombotic therapy](/docs/foundations/perioperative-care/perioperative-protocols/antithrombotic-therapy) and should not be re-derived here.

For adjacent topics see [VTE prophylaxis](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/perioperative-eras/vte-prophylaxis) and [Perioperative antibiotic prophylaxis](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/infection-prophylaxis/perioperative-antibiotic-prophylaxis).

## Quick Reference — Matching Reversal Agent to Anticoagulant

| Anticoagulant | First-line reversal | Alternative | Onset | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Warfarin (VKA)** | **4F-PCC + IV vitamin K 5–10 mg** | FFP + IV vitamin K | PCC ~10 min; vit K 4–6 h IV | PCC ~8 h; vit K sustained<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| **Dabigatran** | **Idarucizumab 5 g IV** | aPCC 50 U/kg or 4F-PCC 50 U/kg | &lt; 5 min | 12–24 h<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| **Apixaban / Rivaroxaban / Edoxaban** | **4F-PCC 25–50 U/kg** (andexanet withdrawn in US)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | aPCC 50 U/kg | ~10 min | ~8 h |
| **Fondaparinux** | No specific antidote; specialist-guided off-label rescue for life-threatening bleeding | — | — | — |
| **UFH** | **Protamine 1 mg / 100 U** (max 50 mg) | — | Immediate | ~2 h<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[22]](#ref22)</sup> |
| **LMWH (enoxaparin)** | **Protamine 1 mg / 1 mg** if &lt; 8 h; 0.5 mg / 1 mg if 8–12 h | Specialist-guided rescue if protamine cannot be used | Partial anti-Xa reversal; anti-IIa more completely reversed | Variable<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[29]](#ref29)</sup> |

## 1. Idarucizumab (Praxbind) — The Dabigatran-Specific Standard

Idarucizumab is a **humanized monoclonal antibody Fab fragment** that binds dabigatran and its acylglucuronide metabolites with higher affinity than dabigatran has for thrombin, neutralizing its effect immediately. **No procoagulant effect** on endogenous thrombin potential — the critical safety advantage over andexanet.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

### Dose

- **5 g IV** — **two consecutive 2.5 g / 50 mL vials**, given as sequential infusions or bolus injections
- An **additional 5 g** may be considered if clinically relevant bleeding recurs with elevated coagulation parameters, or before a second urgent procedure; supporting data are limited<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

### Evidence — RE-VERSE AD

**Pollack 2017 NEJM** prospective cohort (n = 503):<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

- Group A: uncontrolled bleeding (n = 301); Group B: urgent procedure (n = 202)
- **Median maximum reversal 100%** (dilute thrombin time or ecarin clotting time) in evaluable patients with prolonged baseline clotting times; complete reversal by dTT in 99%, aPTT 93%, ECT 82% per the label summary<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Median time to cessation of bleeding: 2.5 h**; **periprocedural hemostasis normal in 93.4%**; median time to procedure start 1.6 h
- **90-day thrombotic events: 6.3% in group A and 7.4% in group B**; restart anticoagulation when medically appropriate, with timing driven by bleeding control and thrombotic risk
- No safety signals attributable to idarucizumab

### Guideline positioning

- **2023 ACC/AHA/ACCP/HRS AF** — recommends idarucizumab for life-threatening dabigatran bleeding<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **AHA/ASA ICH 2022** — recommended for dabigatran-associated ICH<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **NCS/SCCM 2016** — strong recommendation for dabigatran-associated ICH if within 3–5 half-lives or renal insufficiency<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Special points

- **Dabigatran can be restarted 24 h after idarucizumab**; resume anticoagulation as soon as medically appropriate<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- Contains **sorbitol 4 g per 5 g dose** — risk of serious reaction in **hereditary fructose intolerance**<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **If unavailable:** aPCC 50 U/kg or 4F-PCC 50 U/kg (conditional, low-quality evidence)<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- Dabigatran is approximately **35% protein bound** (65% unbound). Hemodialysis can remove it, but clinical data are limited and redistribution can occur; discuss with nephrology when needed, especially with renal impairment.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
- **Activated charcoal 50 g** if ingestion within 2–4 h<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Coagulation parameters may re-elevate between 12–24 h due to drug redistribution — clinically significant recurrence was rare in RE-VERSE AD<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

## 2. Four-Factor PCC (Kcentra / Beriplex) — The Versatile Workhorse

4F-PCC contains **coagulation factors II, VII, IX, and X plus proteins C and S** from pooled human plasma. The **most versatile reversal agent** — FDA-approved for VKA reversal and now, post-andexanet withdrawal, the primary option for FXa inhibitor reversal.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

### A. Warfarin (VKA) reversal — INR-based dosing

| Pretreatment INR | Dose (units factor IX / kg) | Max dose |
|---|---|---|
| 2–&lt; 4 | **25 U/kg** | 2,500 units |
| 4–6 | **35 U/kg** | 3,500 units |
| **&gt; 6** | **50 U/kg** | 5,000 units |

Source: product label.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[25]](#ref25)</sup> Dose by actual body weight up to 100 kg; for heavier patients do not exceed the listed maximum. Repeat dosing has not been studied and is not recommended. Measure the INR near the time of dosing. Balfaxar (Octapharma), a second US 4F-PCC, uses the identical INR/weight table but is labeled for VKA reversal before urgent surgery/invasive procedures; it also contains heparin.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>

**Evidence:**

- **INCH RCT** (VKA-associated intracranial hemorrhage, n = 50 treated) — 4F-PCC 30 IU/kg vs FFP 20 mL/kg: **INR ≤ 1.2 within 3 h in 67% vs 9%**; 13 deaths overall (FFP 8, five from hematoma expansion; PCC 5, none from expansion). The trial was terminated early for safety concerns and was too small to show a clinical-outcome benefit; FFP is a second-line option when PCC is unavailable, at the cost of large volume and slower correction<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>
- **Puchstein 2023 meta** — 4F-PCC superior to 3F-PCC for INR normalization (OR 3.50)<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- 4F-PCC contains **~25× the concentration of vitamin K-dependent factors vs plasma** (25 U/mL vs 1 U/mL) → smaller volumes, faster infusion<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

**Must always be co-administered with IV vitamin K (5–10 mg).** PCC provides immediate but temporary factor replacement (~8 h); vitamin K restores endogenous factor production. **Without vitamin K, INR rebounds at 12–24 h** → hematoma re-expansion.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

### B. FXa inhibitor reversal — the primary strategy post-andexanet

- **Fixed 2,000 U** or **weight-based 25–50 U/kg**<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Off-label for FXa inhibitors; supported by the ACC 2020 bleeding pathway and the AF guideline<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Mechanism:** PCC does not reduce anti-FXa activity levels; it overwhelms the anticoagulant effect through factor supplementation — a non-specific "bypass" strategy that restores thrombin generation<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

### C. Dabigatran reversal (off-label) when idarucizumab unavailable

**4F-PCC 50 U/kg** or **aPCC (FEIBA) 50 U/kg** — conditional recommendation, low-quality evidence.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Safety

- **Boxed warning (Kcentra, Balfaxar): arterial and venous thromboembolic complications**, including fatal events. Weigh reversal against the thrombotic risk of the underlying indication, and restart anticoagulation as soon as bleeding risk allows.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)[[28]](#ref28)</sup>
- Thromboembolism risk within 30 d — similar to FFP<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Kcentra contains heparin and is contraindicated in known HIT, DIC, or severe reactions to its components.** Do not substitute another PCC by class name alone: composition and indications differ. Select an alternative with hematology/transfusion medicine.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>
- The labels state PCC may not be suitable after a thromboembolic event within the prior 3 months<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

## 3. Vitamin K (Phytonadione) — Specific VKA Antidote

Restores intrinsic hepatic carboxylation of vitamin K-dependent clotting factors by overcoming warfarin's competitive inhibition.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

### Routes and onset

| Route | Onset | Full correction |
|---|---|---|
| **IV** (urgent) | INR ↓ begins at 2 h | **4–6 h** |
| **Oral** | Slower | **18–24 h** — 5 mg PO ≈ 1 mg IV at 24 h<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |
| **Subcutaneous** | Unpredictable absorption | **Not recommended**<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |

### Dosing by scenario

| Scenario | Dose | Route |
|---|---|---|
| **Life-threatening bleeding** (with PCC) | **5–10 mg** | IV slow (no faster than 1 mg/min) |
| **Urgent surgery within 24 h** | Low dose (commonly 1–2.5 mg; regimens vary) | IV slow |
| **Urgent surgery within 48 h** | Low dose (commonly 2.5 mg) | Oral |
| **INR 4.5–10, no bleeding** | Hold/adjust warfarin; **no routine vitamin K** (individualize for exceptional bleeding risk or procedural urgency) | — |
| **INR >10, no bleeding** | Oral vitamin K; dose and follow-up INR per protocol | Oral |

Sources: ACCP 9th edition (major bleeding: 4F-PCC preferred over plasma, plus vitamin K 5–10 mg slow IV; INR 4.5–10 without bleeding: routine vitamin K not suggested; INR &gt; 10: oral vitamin K suggested), ACC 2020 expert consensus, AHA/ASA 2022. The no-bleeding INR recommendations are distinct from emergency major-bleeding reversal.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup><sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

### Key teaching points

- **Vitamin K alone does not achieve rapid hemostasis** — must be combined with PCC for life-threatening bleeding.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Anaphylactoid reactions** rare (~ 1 in 3,000) when given slowly in ≥ 50 mL IV fluid over 15–30 min.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- Vitamin K **reduces response to subsequent warfarin** — if rapid re-anticoagulation is needed, heparin is usually preferred initially.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

## 4. Andexanet Alfa (Andexxa) — US Market Withdrawal

**Andexxa withdrawn from the US market.** In December 2025 the FDA judged that the risks of andexanet alfa, including increased thromboembolic events, outweigh its benefits, and AstraZeneca ended US sales on December 22, 2025.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> **4F-PCC is now the primary reversal strategy** for FXa inhibitor-associated life-threatening bleeding in the US.

### Mechanism

Modified recombinant inactive form of human factor Xa that **binds and sequesters** apixaban and rivaroxaban. Also **binds and inhibits tissue factor pathway inhibitor (TFPI)**, contributing to the prothrombotic signal. Elimination t½ ~5–7 h.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

### Evidence

**ANNEXA-4 (NEJM 2019, n = 352)** — single-arm cohort:<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- Anti-FXa ↓ **92%** for both apixaban and rivaroxaban; hemostasis at 12 h: **82%**
- **14% died and 10% had thrombotic events at 30 days** — majority in patients not restarted on anticoagulation
- Anti-FXa reduction **did not predict** hemostatic efficacy

**ANNEXA-I (NEJM 2024, n = 530)** — first RCT, andexanet vs usual care (85.5% PCC) in FXa-associated acute ICH:<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

- Hemostatic efficacy: **67.0% vs 53.1%** (adjusted difference 13.4%; p = 0.003)
- **Thrombotic events: 10.3% vs 5.6%** (p = 0.048); **ischemic stroke: 6.5% vs 1.5%**
- **No differences in mRS or 30-day death** — hematoma control did not translate into functional benefit

**Panos 2025 CCM retrospective** (n = 1,096) — andexanet vs PCC: higher hemostatic efficacy (OR 1.60) **but** higher thrombotic events (OR 1.91); in severely neurologically injured (GCS &lt; 8), trend toward *worse* mortality.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

### Key safety liabilities that drove the withdrawal

- **Boxed warning:** arterial and venous thromboembolism, MI, ischemic stroke, cardiac arrest, sudden death<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **10.7% thrombotic event rate** in ANNEXA-4; median time to first event 10 days<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **TFPI inhibition** may create prothrombotic state beyond simple reversal
- Renders patients **unresponsive to UFH** — heparin cannot be used for anticoagulation after andexanet<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Anti-FXa activity re-elevates** after the short half-life wears off<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

### Post-withdrawal implications

1. **4F-PCC (25–50 U/kg or 2,000 U fixed) is now first-line** for apixaban / rivaroxaban / edoxaban-associated life-threatening bleeding
2. **aPCC 50 U/kg** is the alternative
3. Institutional pre-op checklists that list andexanet as backup need updating
4. Keep the companion [Antithrombotic therapy](/docs/foundations/perioperative-care/perioperative-protocols/antithrombotic-therapy) workflow and local formulary algorithm aligned

## 5. Protamine Sulfate — Heparin Reversal

Basic protein derived from salmon sperm; binds heparin via nonspecific electrostatic interaction. **Only clinically available UFH reversal agent.**<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

### UFH reversal

- **1 mg protamine per 100 U of UFH**, counting only heparin still circulating: the requirement falls quickly with time (about half the usual dose if given 30 min after the heparin dose)<sup>[[22]](#ref22)[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
- **Max single dose 50 mg**, by very slow IV injection over 10 min (about 5 mg/min)<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
- Monitor with aPTT or anti-Xa

### LMWH reversal

| Time since LMWH | Protamine dose |
|---|---|
| **Within 8 h** | **1 mg per 1 mg enoxaparin** (or 1 mg per 100 anti-Xa U dalteparin); max 50 mg |
| **8–12 h** | **0.5 mg per 1 mg enoxaparin** |
| **&gt; 12 h** | Protamine may not be required; individualize |
| Persistent bleeding | Repeat **0.5 mg per 1 mg enoxaparin** |

**Protamine fully reverses anti-IIa but only ~60% reverses anti-Xa activity** — a pharmacologic limitation, not a dosing one. The enoxaparin label gives the 1 mg/1 mg (≤ 8 h) and 0.5 mg/1 mg (&gt; 8 h) schedule and a second 0.5 mg/mg dose if the aPTT stays prolonged 2–4 h later.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[29]](#ref29)</sup>

### Fondaparinux

Protamine has **no effect**. Older intracranial-hemorrhage guidance discusses rFVIIa as an off-label rescue option; clinical evidence is limited and thrombotic risk requires specialist assessment.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### FDA boxed warning

**Protamine Boxed Warning.** Severe hypotension, cardiovascular collapse, noncardiogenic pulmonary edema, catastrophic pulmonary vasoconstriction, pulmonary hypertension, anaphylaxis — particularly with high dose, overdose, rapid administration, or repeat exposure.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

### Risk factors for severe protamine reactions

- High dose / rapid administration
- **Prior NPH or protamine-zinc insulin** (common in diabetics) — a frequently missed pre-op screening question
- **Fish allergy**
- **Previous vasectomy or male infertility** (anti-protamine antibodies via autoimmune response)
- Severe LV dysfunction; abnormal pulmonary hemodynamics<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

### The "excess protamine causes bleeding" paradox

**Excess protamine has its own anticoagulant effect and can worsen bleeding.** Dose against the heparin still present, accounting for elapsed time, and administer slowly with coagulation monitoring; the label specifies up to 50 mg over 10 minutes.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

## 6. Ciraparantag (PER977) — Emerging Universal Reversal

Synthetic small molecule designed as a **universal reversal agent** for DOACs (dabigatran, apixaban, rivaroxaban, edoxaban) **and heparin**. **Investigational; not a routinely available reversal treatment.**<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

- Binds anticoagulants through non-covalent hydrogen bonding and charge-charge interactions
- Early data — rapid reversal of whole blood clotting time within 10–30 min of single IV dose
- **Urgency increased** post-andexanet withdrawal — ciraparantag may fill the coverage gap if approved

## 7. Elective Surgery — Interruption, Bridging and Neuraxial Anesthesia

Reversal is for emergencies. For elective reconstructive surgery, plan interruption ahead; the full protocol lives on the [Antithrombotic therapy](/docs/foundations/perioperative-care/perioperative-protocols/antithrombotic-therapy) page.

- **DOACs (PAUSE):** in 3,007 patients with atrial fibrillation on apixaban, dabigatran or rivaroxaban, omitting the DOAC **1 day before a low-bleeding-risk procedure and 2 days before a high-bleeding-risk procedure**, and resuming **1 day and 2–3 days after**, without heparin bridging or drug-level testing, gave 30-day major bleeding of about 1–3% and arterial thromboembolism of 0.2–0.6%.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup> Adjust for renal function per the workflow page.
- **Warfarin bridging (BRIDGE):** in AF, omitting LMWH bridging was noninferior for arterial thromboembolism (0.4% vs 0.3%) and reduced major bleeding (1.3% vs 3.2%).<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup> The 2022 ACCP perioperative guideline strongly recommends against heparin bridging in AF; bridging decisions for mechanical valves and recent VTE are separate.<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup>
- **Neuraxial anesthesia:** the enoxaparin label carries a **boxed warning for spinal/epidural hematoma** (risk rises with indwelling epidural catheters, concurrent NSAIDs, platelet inhibitors or other anticoagulants, and traumatic or repeated punctures), and states that optimal timing between the LMWH dose and neuraxial procedures is not known.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup> Use the ASRA fifth-edition intervals for each agent and dose level (which are deliberately conservative) when timing blocks, catheter removal and DOAC or LMWH resumption.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Practical Pearls

- **Andexanet alfa is withdrawn from the US market.** 4F-PCC 25–50 U/kg (or fixed 2,000 U) is now first-line for FXa-inhibitor-associated life-threatening bleeding. Update institutional pre-op checklists and reversal algorithms.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Idarucizumab is the gold standard for dabigatran reversal** — rapid, complete, durable, no procoagulant effect. 5 g dose (2 × 2.5 g). Expect possible coagulation-parameter rebound at 12–24 h.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Always give IV vitamin K (5–10 mg) with 4F-PCC** for warfarin reversal. Without vitamin K, INR rebounds in 12–24 h.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Protamine overdose causes bleeding** — avoid excess dosing and reassess coagulation rather than repeating a fixed ratio.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
- **Protamine only ~60% reverses LMWH anti-Xa activity** — pharmacologic limitation.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- **Ask about fish allergy, prior NPH insulin, and prior vasectomy** before giving protamine.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
- **Most post-reversal thrombotic events occur in patients not resumed on anticoagulation** — restart as soon as medically safe.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Ciraparantag remains investigational** as a potential broad reversal agent; do not include it as an available rescue drug.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
