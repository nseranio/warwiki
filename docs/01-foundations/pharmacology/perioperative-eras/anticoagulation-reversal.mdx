# Anticoagulation Reversal — Drug-Class Hub

Anticoagulation reversal is a rapidly evolving field, and the single most important recent change is that andexanet alfa (Andexxa) has been withdrawn from the US market after the FDA concluded in December 2025 that its risks (notably excess thromboembolic events) outweigh its benefits; AstraZeneca ended US sales on December 22, 2025.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> As a result, 4-factor PCC is now the primary reversal strategy for apixaban- and rivaroxaban-associated life-threatening bleeding; update institutional reversal algorithms accordingly. Idarucizumab (Praxbind) remains the standard for dabigatran reversal.

This article is the drug-class pharmacology hub: mechanism, dose, evidence and safety for each reversal agent (idarucizumab, 4F-PCC, vitamin K, protamine) along with the post-andexanet landscape and the emerging ciraparantag pipeline. The clinical workflow (procedure-specific bleed-versus-thrombosis weighing, DOAC hold timing, bridging, antiplatelet management) lives at [Antithrombotic therapy](/docs/foundations/perioperative-care/perioperative-protocols/antithrombotic-therapy) and should not be re-derived here.

For adjacent topics see [VTE prophylaxis](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/perioperative-eras/vte-prophylaxis) and [Perioperative antibiotic prophylaxis](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/infection-prophylaxis/perioperative-antibiotic-prophylaxis).

| **Dabigatran** | **Idarucizumab 5 g IV** | If unavailable, aPCC or 4F-PCC 50 U/kg (maximum 4,000 units) | &lt; 5 min | 12–24 h<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |

## 1. Idarucizumab (Praxbind) — The Dabigatran-Specific Standard

Idarucizumab is a humanized monoclonal antibody Fab fragment that binds dabigatran and its acylglucuronide metabolites with higher affinity than dabigatran has for thrombin, neutralizing its effect immediately. Idarucizumab alone showed no procoagulant effect in endogenous-thrombin-potential testing; this surrogate result does not establish lower clinical thrombotic risk than a different reversal agent.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

### Dose

- **5 g IV.** Two consecutive 2.5 g / 50 mL vials, given as sequential infusions or bolus injections<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- An additional 5 g may be considered if clinically relevant bleeding recurs with elevated coagulation parameters, or before a second urgent procedure; supporting data are limited<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

### Evidence — RE-VERSE AD

**Pollack 2017 NEJM** prospective cohort (n = 503):<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

- Group A: uncontrolled bleeding (n = 301); Group B: urgent procedure (n = 202)<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Median maximum reversal 100%** (dilute thrombin time or ecarin clotting time) in evaluable patients with prolonged baseline clotting times; complete reversal by dTT in 99%, aPTT 93%, ECT 82% per the label summary<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- In group A, bleeding cessation was confirmed within 24 h in 134/203 assessable patients; the median was 2.5 h among those 134. In group B, hemostasis was normal in 184/197 (93.4%) patients who underwent a procedure, which started a median of 1.6 h after idarucizumab.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **90-day thrombotic events: 6.3% in group A and 7.4% in group B**; restart anticoagulation when medically appropriate, with timing driven by bleeding control and thrombotic risk<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- No safety signals attributable to idarucizumab

### Guideline positioning

- **2023 ACC/AHA/ACCP/HRS AF.** Recommends idarucizumab for life-threatening dabigatran bleeding<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **AHA/ASA ICH 2022.** Recommended for dabigatran-associated ICH<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **NCS/SCCM 2016.** Strong recommendation for dabigatran-associated ICH if within 3–5 half-lives or renal insufficiency<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Special points

- **Dabigatran can be restarted 24 h after idarucizumab**; resume anticoagulation as soon as medically appropriate<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- Contains sorbitol 4 g per 5 g dose; risk of serious reaction in hereditary fructose intolerance<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **If idarucizumab is unavailable:** aPCC or 4F-PCC 50 U/kg (maximum 4,000 units); evidence for either fallback is limited.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- Dabigatran is approximately 35% protein bound (65% unbound). Hemodialysis can remove it, but clinical data are limited and redistribution can occur; discuss with nephrology when needed, especially with renal impairment.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
- **Activated charcoal 50 g** if ingestion within 2–4 h<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Coagulation parameters may re-elevate between 12–24 h due to drug redistribution; clinically significant recurrence was rare in RE-VERSE AD<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

## 2. Four-Factor PCC (Kcentra / Beriplex) — The Versatile Workhorse

4F-PCC contains coagulation factors II, VII, IX, and X plus proteins C and S from pooled human plasma. 4F-PCC is FDA-approved for urgent VKA reversal in adults with acute major bleeding or a need for urgent surgery or an invasive procedure.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> For life-threatening oral FXa-inhibitor bleeding in patients with AF, ACC/AHA/ACCP/HRS recommends it as one reversal option.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> For spontaneous FXa-inhibitor-associated ICH, AHA/ASA says it may be considered.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> FXa-inhibitor use is off-label, and Andexxa is no longer sold in the US.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### A. Warfarin (VKA) reversal — INR-based dosing

| Pretreatment INR | Dose (units factor IX / kg) | Max dose |
|---|---|---|
| 2–&lt; 4 | **25 U/kg** | 2,500 units<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> |
| 4–6 | **35 U/kg** | 3,500 units<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> |
| **&gt; 6** | **50 U/kg** | 5,000 units<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> |

Source: product label.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[25]](#ref25)</sup> Dose by actual body weight up to 100 kg; for heavier patients do not exceed the listed maximum. Repeat dosing has not been studied and is not recommended. Measure the INR near the time of dosing. Balfaxar (Octapharma), a second US 4F-PCC, uses the identical INR/weight table but is labeled for VKA reversal before urgent surgery/invasive procedures; it also contains heparin.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>

**Evidence:**

- **INCH RCT** (VKA-associated intracranial hemorrhage, n = 50 treated): 4F-PCC 30 IU/kg versus FFP 20 mL/kg: INR ≤ 1.2 within 3 h in 67% versus 9%; 13 deaths overall (FFP 8, five from hematoma expansion; PCC 5, none from expansion). The trial was terminated early for safety concerns and was too small to show a clinical-outcome benefit; FFP is a second-line option when PCC is unavailable, at the cost of large volume and slower correction<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>
- **Puchstein 2023 meta.** 4F-PCC superior to 3F-PCC for INR normalization (OR 3.50)<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- 4F-PCC contains about 25× the concentration of vitamin K-dependent factors versus plasma (25 U/mL versus 1 U/mL), allowing smaller volumes and faster infusion<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

**Co-administer vitamin K with 4F-PCC for urgent VKA reversal; for life-threatening bleeding, use 5–10 mg IV vitamin K.**<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup> PCC provides immediate but temporary factor replacement (about 8 h); vitamin K restores endogenous factor production. Without vitamin K, INR rebounds at 12–24 h, with hematoma re-expansion.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

### B. FXa inhibitor reversal — the primary strategy post-andexanet

- **FXa-inhibitor bleeding:** off-label 4F-PCC has been given as fixed 2,000 U or 25–50 U/kg; the optimal dose is unestablished.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> For FXa-associated intracranial hemorrhage specifically, the 2026 NCS/SCCM update conditionally favors 4F-PCC over andexanet for spontaneous ICH (moderate certainty) and traumatic ICH (very low certainty).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[33]](#ref33)</sup>
- Off-label for FXa inhibitors; supported by the ACC 2020 bleeding pathway and the AF guideline<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Mechanism:** PCC does not reduce anti-FXa activity levels; it overwhelms the anticoagulant effect through factor supplementation, a non-specific "bypass" strategy that restores thrombin generation<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

### C. Dabigatran reversal (off-label) when idarucizumab unavailable

**4F-PCC or aPCC (FEIBA) 50 U/kg, maximum 4,000 units,** may be used when idarucizumab is unavailable. The NCS/SCCM conditional recommendation (low-quality evidence) applies to direct-thrombin-inhibitor-associated intracranial hemorrhage with recent exposure or renal insufficiency.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Safety

- **Boxed warning (Kcentra, Balfaxar): arterial and venous thromboembolic complications**, including fatal events. Weigh reversal against the thrombotic risk of the underlying indication, and restart anticoagulation as soon as bleeding risk allows.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)[[28]](#ref28)</sup>
- Thromboembolism risk within 30 d, similar to FFP<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Kcentra contains heparin and is contraindicated in known HIT, DIC, or severe reactions to its components.** Do not substitute another PCC by class name alone: composition and indications differ. Select an alternative with hematology/transfusion medicine.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>
- The labels state PCC may not be suitable after a thromboembolic event within the prior 3 months<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

## 3. Vitamin K (Phytonadione) — Specific VKA Antidote

Restores intrinsic hepatic carboxylation of vitamin K-dependent clotting factors by overcoming warfarin's competitive inhibition.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

### Routes and onset

| Route | Onset | Full correction |
|---|---|---|
| **IV** (urgent) | INR ↓ begins at 2 h | **4–6 h** |
| **Oral** | Slower | Variable; 5 mg PO and 1 mg IV produce similar INR effects at 24 h<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |
| **Subcutaneous** | Unpredictable absorption | **Not recommended**<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |

### Dosing by scenario

| Scenario | Dose | Route |
|---|---|---|
| **Life-threatening bleeding** (with PCC) | **5–10 mg** | IV slow (no faster than 1 mg/min)<sup>[[26]](#ref26)[[34]](#ref34)</sup> |
| **Urgent VKA reversal when INR reduction is needed in 4–6 h** | Vitamin K **2–10 mg**, according to INR; use PCC when immediate reversal is required | IV slow<sup>[[36]](#ref36)</sup> |
| **Urgent VKA reversal when 18–24 h for INR reduction is acceptable** | Vitamin K **2–10 mg**, according to INR | Oral<sup>[[36]](#ref36)</sup> |
| **INR 4.5–10, no bleeding** | Hold/adjust warfarin; **no routine vitamin K** (individualize for exceptional bleeding risk or procedural urgency) | — |
| **INR >10, no bleeding** | Oral vitamin K; dose and follow-up INR per protocol | Oral |

Sources: ACCP 9th edition (major bleeding: 4F-PCC preferred over plasma, plus vitamin K 5–10 mg slow IV; INR 4.5–10 without bleeding: routine vitamin K not suggested; INR &gt; 10: oral vitamin K suggested), ACC 2020 expert consensus, AHA/ASA 2022.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup> The no-bleeding INR recommendations are distinct from emergency major-bleeding reversal.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup><sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

### Key teaching points

- **Vitamin K alone does not achieve rapid hemostasis.** Must be combined with PCC for life-threatening bleeding.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **IV phytonadione can cause severe, sometimes fatal hypersensitivity even after dilution and slow infusion.** Older guidance estimated anaphylactoid reactions at about 1 in 3,000 IV doses and advised dilution in at least 50 mL over 15–30 min; the current label warns that reactions can occur on first exposure and limits IV administration to no faster than 1 mg/min.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)[[19]](#ref19)[[34]](#ref34)</sup>
- Vitamin K reduces response to subsequent warfarin; if rapid re-anticoagulation is needed, heparin is usually preferred initially.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

## 4. Andexanet Alfa (Andexxa) — US Market Withdrawal

**Andexxa withdrawn from the US market.** In December 2025 the FDA judged that the risks of andexanet alfa, including increased thromboembolic events, outweigh its benefits, and AstraZeneca ended US sales on December 22, 2025.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> 4F-PCC is now the primary reversal strategy for FXa inhibitor-associated life-threatening bleeding in the US.

### Mechanism

Modified recombinant inactive form of human factor Xa that binds and sequesters apixaban and rivaroxaban. Also binds and inhibits tissue factor pathway inhibitor (TFPI), contributing to the prothrombotic signal. Elimination t½ about 5–7 h.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

### Evidence

**ANNEXA-4 (NEJM 2019, n = 352).** Single-arm cohort:<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- Median anti-FXa activity fell 92% after the bolus for both apixaban and rivaroxaban; adjudicated excellent or good hemostasis 12 h after the end of the 2-hour infusion occurred in 204/249 efficacy-evaluable patients (82%).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **At 30 days, 49/352 patients (14%) had died and 34/352 (10%) had a thrombotic event.** Of the 34 thrombotic events, 26 occurred before anticoagulation was restarted.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- Anti-FXa reduction did not predict hemostatic efficacy overall, but was modestly predictive in the intracranial-hemorrhage subgroup.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

**ANNEXA-I (NEJM 2024, n = 530).** First RCT, andexanet versus usual care (85.5% PCC) in FXa-associated acute ICH:<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

- **Composite 12-hour hemostatic efficacy** (hematoma expansion no more than 35%, NIHSS increase &lt; 7, and no rescue therapy between 3 and 12 h) occurred in 150/224 (67.0%) with andexanet versus 121/228 (53.1%) with usual care (adjusted difference 13.4 percentage points; p = 0.003).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Thrombotic events: 10.3% versus 5.6%** (p = 0.048); ischemic stroke: 6.5% versus 1.5%<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **No appreciable difference was detected in mRS or 30-day death.** ANNEXA-I was not powered to establish whether better hematoma control improved functional outcome.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

**Panos 2025 CCM retrospective** (n = 1,096). In the full retrospective analysis, andexanet was associated with higher odds of excellent or good hemostasis (OR 1.60) and in-hospital thrombotic events (OR 1.91) than PCC; a prespecified sensitivity analysis excluding admission GCS &lt; 7 found no hemostatic or thrombotic difference.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

### Key safety liabilities that drove the withdrawal

- **Boxed warning:** arterial and venous thromboembolism, MI, ischemic stroke, cardiac arrest, sudden death<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **In the later ANNEXA-4 label safety subset of apixaban- or rivaroxaban-treated patients, 45/419 (10.7%) had a thrombotic event** (median first event 10 days).<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> In the FDA’s later 471-patient complete-follow-up ANNEXA-I safety analysis, 30-day thromboses were 35/239 (14.6%) with andexanet versus 16/232 (6.9%) with usual care, and thrombosis-related deaths were 6/239 (2.5%) versus 2/232 (0.9%); these denominators differ from the published trial analysis above.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[35]](#ref35)</sup>
- **TFPI inhibition** may create prothrombotic state beyond simple reversal
- **UFH unresponsiveness has occurred after andexanet**, including failure of activated clotting time to prolong and serious thrombosis. The former US label advised against UFH after administration and recommended an alternative anticoagulant when needed.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Anti-FXa activity re-elevates** after the short half-life wears off<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

### Post-withdrawal implications

1. **With andexanet unavailable in the US, off-label 4F-PCC regimens (25–50 U/kg or 2,000 U fixed) are options** for apixaban / rivaroxaban / edoxaban-associated life-threatening bleeding<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
2. **aPCC 50 U/kg** is the alternative<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
3. Institutional pre-op checklists that list andexanet as backup need updating
4. Keep the companion [Antithrombotic therapy](/docs/foundations/perioperative-care/perioperative-protocols/antithrombotic-therapy) workflow and local formulary algorithm aligned

## 5. Protamine Sulfate — Heparin Reversal

Basic protein derived from salmon sperm; binds heparin via nonspecific electrostatic interaction. Only clinically available UFH reversal agent.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

### UFH reversal

- **1 mg protamine per 100 U of UFH**, counting only heparin still circulating: the requirement falls quickly with time (about half the usual dose if given 30 min after the heparin dose)<sup>[[22]](#ref22)[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
- **No more than 50 mg over 10 min**, by very slow IV injection (about 5 mg/min); further dosing depends on the heparin remaining and coagulation results.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
- Monitor with aPTT or anti-Xa

### LMWH reversal

| Time since LMWH | Protamine dose |
|---|---|
| **Within 8 h (enoxaparin)** | **1 mg per 1 mg enoxaparin**; administer no more than 50 mg over 10 min. For therapeutic-dose dalteparin-associated intracranial hemorrhage within 3–5 LMWH half-lives, use 1 mg per 100 anti-Xa U (up to 50 mg).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[23]](#ref23)[[29]](#ref29)</sup> |
| **8–12 h** | **0.5 mg per 1 mg enoxaparin**<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup> |
| **&gt; 12 h** | Protamine may not be required; individualize |
| Repeat dose when indicated | If aPTT remains prolonged 2–4 h after the first infusion, repeat **0.5 mg per 1 mg enoxaparin**; NCS/SCCM also suggests repeat dosing for persistent life-threatening intracranial bleeding or renal insufficiency after therapeutic-dose LMWH.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[29]](#ref29)</sup> |

**Protamine fully reverses anti-IIa but only about 60% reverses anti-Xa activity.** A pharmacologic limitation, not a dosing one. The enoxaparin label gives the 1 mg/1 mg (≤ 8 h) and 0.5 mg/1 mg (&gt; 8 h) schedule and a second 0.5 mg/mg dose if the aPTT stays prolonged 2–4 h later.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[29]](#ref29)</sup>

### Fondaparinux

Protamine has no effect. Older intracranial-hemorrhage guidance discusses rFVIIa as an off-label rescue option; clinical evidence is limited and thrombotic risk requires specialist assessment.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### FDA boxed warning

**Protamine Boxed Warning.** Severe hypotension, cardiovascular collapse, noncardiogenic pulmonary edema, catastrophic pulmonary vasoconstriction, pulmonary hypertension, anaphylaxis — particularly with high dose, overdose, rapid administration, or repeat exposure.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

### Risk factors for severe protamine reactions

- High dose / rapid administration
- **Prior NPH or protamine-zinc insulin** (common in diabetics). A frequently missed pre-op screening question
- **Fish allergy**
- **Previous vasectomy or male infertility** (anti-protamine antibodies via autoimmune response)
- Severe LV dysfunction; abnormal pulmonary hemodynamics<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

### The "excess protamine causes bleeding" paradox

**Excess protamine has its own anticoagulant effect and can worsen bleeding.** Dose against the heparin still present, accounting for elapsed time, and administer slowly with coagulation monitoring; the label specifies up to 50 mg over 10 minutes.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

## 6. Ciraparantag (PER977) — Emerging Universal Reversal

Synthetic small molecule designed as a universal reversal agent for DOACs (dabigatran, apixaban, rivaroxaban, edoxaban) and heparin. Investigational; not a routinely available reversal treatment.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

- Binds anticoagulants through non-covalent hydrogen bonding and charge-charge interactions
- Early data: rapid reversal of whole blood clotting time within 10–30 min of single IV dose
- **Urgency increased** post-andexanet withdrawal; ciraparantag may fill the coverage gap if approved

## 7. Elective Surgery — Interruption, Bridging and Neuraxial Anesthesia

Reversal is for emergencies. For elective reconstructive surgery, plan interruption ahead; the full protocol lives on the [Antithrombotic therapy](/docs/foundations/perioperative-care/perioperative-protocols/antithrombotic-therapy) page.

- **DOACs (PAUSE):** in 3,007 patients with atrial fibrillation on apixaban, dabigatran or rivaroxaban, omitting the DOAC 1 day before a low-bleeding-risk procedure and 2 days before a high-bleeding-risk procedure, and resuming 1 day and 2–3 days after, without heparin bridging or drug-level testing, was associated with 30-day major bleeding of 0.90–1.85% and arterial thromboembolism of 0.16–0.60% across the three drug cohorts; major bleeding was 2.95–2.96% in the high-bleeding-risk apixaban and rivaroxaban subgroups.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup> Adjust for renal function per the workflow page.
- **Warfarin bridging (BRIDGE):** in AF, omitting LMWH bridging was noninferior for arterial thromboembolism (0.4% versus 0.3%) and reduced major bleeding (1.3% versus 3.2%).<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup> The 2022 ACCP perioperative guideline strongly recommends against heparin bridging in AF; bridging decisions for mechanical valves and recent VTE are separate.<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup>
- **Neuraxial anesthesia:** the enoxaparin label carries a boxed warning for spinal/epidural hematoma (risk rises with indwelling epidural catheters, concurrent NSAIDs, platelet inhibitors or other anticoagulants, and traumatic or repeated punctures), and states that optimal timing between the LMWH dose and neuraxial procedures is not known.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup> Use the ASRA fifth-edition intervals for each agent and dose level (which are deliberately conservative) when timing blocks, catheter removal and DOAC or LMWH resumption.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Practical Pearls

- **Andexanet alfa is withdrawn from the US market.** Off-label 4F-PCC regimens of 25–50 U/kg or fixed 2,000 U are options for FXa-inhibitor-associated life-threatening bleeding; select a regimen by bleeding site and local protocol.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Update institutional pre-op checklists and reversal algorithms.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Idarucizumab is the specific agent for urgent dabigatran reversal.** Give 5 g IV (2 × 2.5 g); median maximum dTT/ECT reversal was 100% in patients with prolonged baseline assays. Idarucizumab alone showed no procoagulant effect on endogenous thrombin potential, a surrogate assay. Coagulation parameters can rise again at 12–24 h; reassess if bleeding recurs.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Give vitamin K concurrently with 4F-PCC for urgent warfarin reversal.** For VKA-associated major bleeding, ACCP suggests 5–10 mg by slow IV injection (Grade 2C)<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>; NCCN cancer-associated VTE guidance specifies 10 mg IV for serious or life-threatening bleeding<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>; AHA/ASA guidance specifies 5–10 mg IV for VKA-related intracerebral hemorrhage<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>. In a 17-patient case review cited by the AHA/ASA guideline, INR rebounded 12–24 h later in two patients who received PCC without vitamin K.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Protamine overdose causes bleeding.** Avoid excess dosing and reassess coagulation rather than repeating a fixed ratio.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
- **Protamine only partly reverses LMWH anti-Xa activity.** ASRA states that only anti-IIa activity is completely reversed, anti-Xa activity is not fully neutralized, and both activities may return up to 3 hours after protamine;<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> the figure of about 60% comes from a separate review.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- **Ask about fish allergy, prior NPH insulin, and prior vasectomy** before giving protamine.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
- **Reassess anticoagulation after bleeding control.** In the single-arm ANNEXA-4 cohort, 26/34 thrombotic events preceded any anticoagulant restart; RE-VERSE AD was also an uncontrolled cohort, so these data cannot establish that earlier restart prevents thrombosis. Resume when medically safe after weighing recurrent bleeding and thrombosis.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Ciraparantag remains investigational** as a potential broad reversal agent; do not include it as an available rescue drug.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
