# Alpha Blockers (α1-Adrenergic Receptor Antagonists)

## Clinical role

Alpha-blockers are an option for bothersome moderate-to-severe male LUTS attributed to BPH. They relax smooth muscle in the prostate and bladder neck, often improving symptoms within days, with fuller benefit over several weeks. They do not shrink the prostate or prevent long-term retention or BPH surgery. Choice depends on blood pressure, interacting medicines, ejaculation goals and cataract plans.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

**AUA 2026 recommends offering a uroselective agent: alfuzosin, silodosin or tamsulosin (Strong, Grade A).** Doxazosin and terazosin have more orthostasis; a patient doing well on either need not switch automatically. Changing alpha-blockers for inadequate symptom response is generally unhelpful, but a switch can be considered when benefit is limited by adverse effects.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

At appropriate doses the five commonly used agents have broadly similar symptom efficacy. Controlled studies of alpha-blockers typically show a 30–40% IPSS reduction and a 20–25% increase in peak flow from baseline; placebo groups also improve. The AUA 2026 guideline reports mean IPSS changes from baseline of 3.69–7.06 points across agents.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> Benefit can persist during long-term treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

### Mechanism and receptor subtypes

Alpha-blockers relax α1-receptor-mediated smooth-muscle tone in the prostate, prostatic capsule and bladder neck, lowering the dynamic component of outlet resistance. They have no effect on gland volume.

| Subtype | Main location | Practical consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **α1A** | Prostate stroma, capsule, bladder neck | Target for LUTS; selectivity for it (tamsulosin, silodosin) reduces blood-pressure effects |
| **α1B** | Vascular smooth muscle | Blockade drives orthostatic hypotension (doxazosin, terazosin, alfuzosin block it more) |
| **α1D** | Detrusor, spinal cord, distal ureter | Proposed basis for storage-symptom and distal-ureteral-stone effects |

Alfuzosin is not receptor-subtype selective but concentrates in prostatic tissue, so it behaves as "functionally uroselective."<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Agents, doses and formulation rules

These are adult US BPH-label regimens. Off-label indications below require their own assessment. Do not substitute immediate-release and extended-release doxazosin schedules.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

| Agent | Starting / maintenance regimen | Important limits |
|---|---|---|
| **Tamsulosin** | 0.4 mg once daily, about 30 minutes after the same meal; may increase to 0.8 mg after 2–4 weeks if response is inadequate | Restart at 0.4 mg after interruption for several days. Usual renal adjustment is not required, but end-stage renal disease (CrCl &lt;10 mL/min/1.73 m²) and severe hepatic impairment were not studied. Do not combine with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors. <sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Silodosin** | 8 mg once daily **with a meal**; 4 mg daily if CrCl 30–50 mL/min | Contraindicated at CrCl &lt;30, in severe hepatic impairment and with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Alfuzosin ER** | 10 mg once daily with the same meal; swallow whole | Contraindicated in moderate/severe hepatic impairment and with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors. Use caution at CrCl &lt;30 and with QT prolongation or QT-prolonging drugs.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Doxazosin immediate release** | 1 mg once daily, morning or evening; titrate at 1–2-week intervals to 2, 4 and, if needed, 8 mg daily | Monitor postural BP, particularly after the first dose or increase. Restart the initial regimen if interrupted for several days. <sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Doxazosin extended release (Cardura XL)** | 4 mg once daily with breakfast; may increase to 8 mg after 3–4 weeks | Swallow whole. Start/restart at 4 mg, including when switching from the immediate-release product. <sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **Terazosin** | **1 mg at bedtime initially**, then gradually increase through 2, 5 and usually 10 mg daily according to response | First-dose syncope is a major concern. Restart the initial regimen after interruption for several days. <sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |

Tamsulosin preferentially blocks α1A/α1D receptors; silodosin is highly α1A-selective. Doxazosin and terazosin have greater systemic BP effects. Alfuzosin is not receptor-subtype selective but generally has less BP effect than doxazosin or terazosin. Uroselectivity does not eliminate hypotension or syncope.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### Choosing an agent

Symptom benefit is similar across agents, so selection rests on side effects and comorbidity (AUA 2026 clinical principles).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> In a 2011 randomized trial, 1,228 men entered washout and placebo run-in, and 955 were randomized 2:2:1 to silodosin, tamsulosin or placebo for 12 weeks. Both active groups had similar IPSS improvement greater than placebo; only silodosin significantly reduced nocturia versus placebo.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

| Situation | Reasonable choice | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Younger man concerned about ejaculation or fertility | Alfuzosin | Lowest rate of abnormal ejaculation in comparative trials |
| Older man or cardiac risk, ejaculation less important | Silodosin (AUA's likely first choice), or tamsulosin | Higher α1A selectivity and less orthostatic hypotension than alfuzosin in small comparative trials; silodosin carries the highest ejaculatory cost |
| Hypertension already treated with doxazosin or terazosin | Continue it | Do not add a uroselective agent for another indication; do not switch a patient who is doing well |
| Cataract surgery planned | Defer starting; consider tadalafil 5 mg daily | See the IFIS section below <sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| CrCl 30–50 mL/min | Silodosin 4 mg once daily with a meal, or another agent with caution | Label dose reduction <sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| Hepatic impairment | Alfuzosin is contraindicated in moderate–severe; silodosin in severe | Label contraindications<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |

Alfuzosin, silodosin and tamsulosin start at the listed doses without required titration; doxazosin and terazosin require titration because of first-dose hypotension. EAU 2026 suggests reviewing symptom response and adverse effects four to six weeks after starting an alpha-blocker, then at six months and annually if stable (weak follow-up recommendation).<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup>

## Safety and interactions

- Review orthostasis, falls, volume depletion, antihypertensives and other vasodilators before prescribing. Do not combine two alpha-blockers. Hypersensitivity and organ-function restrictions are product-specific.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- EAU 2026 summarizes observational and pooled data: vasodilating effects are greatest with doxazosin and terazosin, and in men older than 66 years alpha-blocker use was associated with falls (OR 1.14) and fractures (OR 1.16). Long-term use was associated with cardiac failure (HR 1.22), higher with non-selective agents. Alfuzosin, doxazosin and terazosin carried more vascular events than placebo in pooled trials.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **PDE5 inhibitors plus nitrates are contraindicated**, regardless of whether an alpha-blocker is used. An alpha-blocker plus a nitrate also warrants hypotension assessment, but this is distinct from the PDE5–nitrate absolute prohibition.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (ketoconazole, itraconazole, clarithromycin, ritonavir) are contraindicated with alfuzosin and silodosin and should not be used with tamsulosin. The tamsulosin label advises caution with moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors, strong or moderate CYP2D6 inhibitors (for example paroxetine) and known CYP2D6 poor metabolizers; check the actual interacting drug and dose.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- First-dose hypotension: after the first dose or each dose increase of doxazosin, the label advises monitoring BP for at least 6 hours; terazosin is started at 1 mg at bedtime.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> Both carry the same syncope risk again if restarted after several days off. Terazosin is contraindicated with hypersensitivity to terazosin; doxazosin is contraindicated with hypersensitivity to doxazosin, other quinazolines or its components.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- These BPH approvals are for men. Selected female voiding-disorder use is off-label, not a blanket contraindication based on sex; pregnancy and the specific indication require separate assessment.
- Rare priapism has been reported.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> AUA/SMSNA lists doxazosin, prazosin, tamsulosin and terazosin as medications associated with priapism. An erection lasting four hours needs urgent assessment; do not imply a proven racial or sickle-cell-trait risk specific to alpha-blocker exposure.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

### Tadalafil combination: guideline option and US label differ

The AUA 2026 guideline conditionally permits tadalafil 5 mg daily with a uroselective alpha-blocker, with or without ED (Grade C); EAU 2026 gives the combination a weak recommendation and describes modest additional symptom benefit.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[1]](#ref1)</sup> It is an option for selected men and is not an automatic preferred regimen for everyone with BPH and ED.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

The US CIALIS label does not recommend alpha-blocker combination treatment for BPH, citing BP risk and insufficient combination-efficacy evidence in its labeling. For ED use with an alpha-blocker, the label requires stability on the alpha-blocker first and initiation at the lowest recommended tadalafil dose. Document this distinction between the label and the guideline and consider volume status, other BP drugs and symptoms.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

### Cognition and dementia

A Medicare cohort of men aged 65 and older with BPH found more incident dementia with tamsulosin than with no BPH drug (HR 1.17, 95% CI 1.14–1.21) and than with other alpha-blockers, over a median 19.8 months.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> Later work is mixed: a 2024 systematic review of seven studies (three RCTs) found no convincing causal link between α1-blockers, including tamsulosin, and cognitive dysfunction, and a matched NACC cohort of new users (n=916 per arm) found no statistically significant difference in cognitive decline.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)[[23]](#ref23)</sup> Confounding by indication and prodromal cognitive impairment are the usual explanations. A Finnish nationwide case-control study (24,602 cases, 98,397 controls) cited by EAU 2026 found no significant association with dementia.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Do not withhold an alpha-blocker for this reason, but weigh it when choosing an agent in men with established cognitive impairment.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[22]](#ref22)[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

### Ejaculation and sexual counseling

Reduced or absent seminal emission is particularly associated with silodosin and tamsulosin. In their separate US registration trials, abnormal or retrograde ejaculation was reported in 28.1% with silodosin, and 8.4% with tamsulosin 0.4 mg versus 18.1% with 0.8 mg.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> These are different trial populations, not a head-to-head comparison; retrograde ejaculation led to discontinuation in 2.8% of silodosin-treated men.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> In the RCTs reviewed for the AUA guideline, abnormal ejaculation was 3–10% with tamsulosin and silodosin versus 0% with alfuzosin.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> Alfuzosin, doxazosin and terazosin generally have less ejaculatory impact, with different BP tradeoffs.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> In the meta-analysis cited by EAU 2026, ejaculatory dysfunction was more common than with placebo (OR 5.88), especially with silodosin (OR 32.5) and tamsulosin (OR 8.57), and not clearly more common with doxazosin (OR 0.80) or terazosin (OR 1.78).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The abnormal ejaculation is a reduction or absence of seminal fluid rather than true retrograde ejaculation, young age is a risk factor, and greater symptom and flow benefit tracked with more ejaculatory dysfunction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Much of this phenomenon reflects reduced or absent emission rather than semen demonstrably entering the bladder. AUA/SMSNA describes retrograde ejaculation and failure of emission as possible alpha-blocker effects (Statement 11 discussion).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[27]](#ref27)</sup> It is not appropriate to label every event as proven retrograde ejaculation or to promise a precise recovery percentage. Discuss fertility and bothersome symptoms, then consider a monitored dose or agent change. The cited live-imaging experiment used rodent tissue ex vivo; it is mechanistic evidence, not a human clinical outcome study.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Cataract surgery and other perioperative decisions

AUA 2026 asks clinicians to counsel every man with LUTS/BPH who is planning cataract surgery about IFIS (flaccid, billowing iris, progressive miosis despite dilation, and iris prolapse) and to have him tell his ophthalmologist. Tell the ophthalmologist about current and previous alpha-blocker use, particularly tamsulosin.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- **IFIS risk:** Tamsulosin is strongly associated with IFIS; alfuzosin, doxazosin and terazosin are also associated. The silodosin US label warns about IFIS, and a matched cataract cohort recorded IFIS in 16/43 silodosin users (37.2%) versus 12/350 controls (3.4%), with an adjusted OR of 8.47 (95% CI 4.01–17.92).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[28]](#ref28)</sup> These observational data do not establish a cross-drug ranking. Other reported contributors include 5-ARIs, ARBs, benzodiazepines, antipsychotics, antidepressants, diabetes, hypertension, older age and a small pupil.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[10]](#ref10)[[28]](#ref28)</sup>
- **After stopping:** AUA states the risk persists after discontinuation for as long as a year. The tamsulosin label reports most IFIS in men who stopped 2–14 days earlier and a few after 5 weeks to 9 months. The benefit of stopping before surgery has not been established, so do not promise that a washout removes the risk.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Before starting:** if a man has symptomatic LUTS and a cataract, AUA advises cataract surgery before starting an alpha-blocker; daily tadalafil 5 mg is an alternative.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> EAU likewise advises against starting an α1-blocker before scheduled cataract surgery.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Operative response:** the cataract surgeon chooses the technique. The label notes modifications such as iris hooks, iris dilator rings and viscoelastic agents; the urologist's role is to document exposure and notify.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For other surgery, there is no blanket rule to withhold all nonselective alpha-blockers that morning. UKCPA advises continuing doxazosin with BP monitoring; anesthesia and postoperative plans should account for hypotension, oral intake and the original indication. After successful BPH surgery and catheter removal, reassess ongoing need rather than applying a fixed 2–4-week withdrawal schedule.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Other indications and strength of evidence

| Setting | Practical interpretation |
|---|---|
| **Trial without catheter after BPH-related acute retention** | Alpha-blockers improve the chance of successful catheter removal. The 2014 Cochrane review found success in 366/608 (60.2%) versus 185/486 (38.1%), RR 1.55 (95% CI 1.36–1.76; moderate-certainty evidence). Do not rank individual agents by indirect subgroup RRs. AUA 2026 advises a uroselective alpha-blocker for at least three days before the voiding trial; optimal timing and duration are not established. Tell men who pass that they remain at increased risk of recurrent retention.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| **CP/CPPS with voiding symptoms** | AUA 2025 recommends offering an alpha-blocker for CP/CPPS with voiding symptoms (Statement 18; Moderate Recommendation, Grade B). The Cochrane review included 24 alpha-blocker studies (2,061 participants); the short-term symptom analysis of 18 studies/1,524 participants found NIH-CPSI mean difference −5.01 (95% CI −7.41 to −2.61), but certainty was **very low**. Reassess benefit and discontinue ineffective treatment; this is not a universal pain treatment or proof of benefit from indefinite empiric prescribing.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| **NLUTD in spontaneous voiders** | AUA/SUFU 2021 (Statement 34) says clinicians may recommend alpha-blockers to improve voiding parameters in patients who void spontaneously (Conditional Recommendation, Grade C). It does not require proven anatomic obstruction in every case. Alpha-blockers reduce outlet resistance; they do not restore absent detrusor contraction or replace a safe emptying plan. The MS review contained only three studies/50 patients (one randomized trial, two cohorts), so its high response percentages should not be used as a general prognosis.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| **Primary bladder-neck obstruction** | EAU 2026 weakly recommends considering alpha-blockers first-line for young men with PBNO. A 2025 systematic review pooled a 7-point IPSS improvement from baseline at three months; included alpha-blocker studies reported ejaculatory dysfunction in 47–50% and treatment failure in 23–52%. EAU reports discontinuation ranging from 4.7% to 55% across studies. Discuss ejaculation and fertility. For well-informed men unresponsive to alpha-blockers, EAU weakly recommends considering bladder-neck incision or intermittent catheterization; transurethral bladder-neck resection is also described as a surgical procedure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[29]](#ref29)</sup> |
| **Women: functional bladder outlet obstruction, underactive bladder** | Off-label. EAU 2026 gives a weak recommendation to offer uroselective alpha-blockers to women with functional BOO after discussing benefits and adverse events, and to offer them in underactive bladder before more invasive options. Evidence is limited: the only placebo-controlled RCT reporting a urodynamically proven BOO subgroup found no significant between-group benefit after eight weeks in 117 women (58 alfuzosin, 59 placebo); dysfunctional voiding and PBNO were not distinguished.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> |
| **Distal ureteral stone MET** | Off-label. EAU 2026 gives a strong recommendation to offer them as one option for distal stones 5–10 mm, with the main benefit above 5 mm, when observation is otherwise suitable. Use a defined follow-up plan; infection, uncontrolled pain or deteriorating renal function should prompt reassessment for intervention. This limited stone indication is included for mixed-practice context.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |
| **Premature ejaculation after first-line treatment failure** | AUA/SMSNA 2020 permits considering α1-adrenergic antagonists (Statement 11; Expert Opinion). Studies are small and use inconsistent definitions and outcomes; their PE regimens are separate from the BPH-label doses above.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup> |

Alpha-blockers are not antifibrotic treatment for urethral stricture or bladder-neck contracture. After endoscopic scar treatment or reconstruction, continued use should address a separate plausible functional indication or demonstrated symptom benefit; routine prevention of recurrent scar is not established.

## Combining with a 5α-reductase inhibitor

In MTOPS, 3,047 men were followed for a mean of 4.5 years.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Relative reductions in the composite clinical-progression endpoint versus placebo were 39% with doxazosin, 34% with finasteride and 66% with combination therapy.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The composite included a ≥4-point symptom-score increase and less frequent events such as retention; it was not a 66% reduction in every individual complication.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Finasteride and combination therapy, but not doxazosin alone, reduced acute retention and invasive treatment. EAU 2026 strongly recommends the combination for men with moderate-to-severe LUTS and increased progression risk.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Use [5α-reductase inhibitors](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/voiding-outlet/alpha-reductase-inhibitors) for men with demonstrable enlargement and meaningful progression risk, after discussing slower benefit, PSA interpretation and sexual adverse effects. Different guidelines use different enlargement thresholds; an alpha-blocker failure is not required before considering combination treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

### Reassessing long-term tamsulosin — 2026 N-of-1 trial

A single-center, blinded crossover study tested continued benefit among men aged 55–80 who had used tamsulosin for at least 12 months. Of 31 enrolled and 30 attempting the protocol, 11 had minimal or no individual symptom benefit, 11 moderate benefit, four strong benefit, and four could not tolerate the placebo run-in because symptoms worsened. Among the 26 protocol completers, the group mean daily AUASI difference favored tamsulosin by 2.96 points (95% CI 1.54–4.37).<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

This supports individual reassessment of benefit with monitored symptom tracking when considering deprescribing; it does not justify routine withdrawal or establish safety in men at risk of recurrent retention. Men with prior retention, UTI, or obstructive kidney disease were excluded. The small sample and short treatment periods limit generalizability.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
