# Voiding &amp; Outlet Pharmacotherapy

Pharmacotherapy targeting **bladder outlet relaxation, voiding-phase contraction, and outlet tone** — the medical companion to surgical management of BPH/LUTS, stress incontinence, underactive bladder, and detrusor-sphincter dyssynergia. Most agents act at three loci: the **bladder neck and prostatic smooth muscle (α₁ receptors)**, the **detrusor (cholinergic receptors)**, and the **external sphincter and pelvic floor (skeletal muscle)**.

For OAB / storage-phase therapy see [Storage & OAB](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/storage-oab). For pelvic-floor hypertonicity in the context of neuropathic pelvic pain see [Neuropathic & Pelvic Pain](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/neuropathic-pelvic-pain).

<ul className="section-stack">
  <li>
    <a href="/docs/foundations/pharmacology/voiding-outlet/alpha-blockers" className="section-stack-title section-stack-link">α-Blockers</a>
    <span className="section-stack-desc">Tamsulosin, silodosin, alfuzosin, doxazosin, terazosin. Uroselective agents for bothersome BPH/LUTS (symptom relief within days; no effect on gland volume); at least three days before a trial without catheter (TWOC) after acute retention; off-label medical expulsive therapy for distal ureteral stones 5–10 mm; CP/CPPS with voiding symptoms. Counsel every man about intraoperative floppy iris syndrome (IFIS) before cataract surgery; orthostatic hypotension and ejaculatory effects are the other main safety points. The tamsulosin–dementia signal is observational and not established as causal.</span>
  </li>
  <li>
    <a href="/docs/foundations/pharmacology/voiding-outlet/alpha-reductase-inhibitors" className="section-stack-title section-stack-link">5α-Reductase Inhibitors</a>
    <span className="section-stack-desc">Finasteride and dutasteride. BPH with an enlarged prostate: symptom benefit takes 3–6 months, and PLESS showed fewer acute retention episodes and BPH operations; MTOPS/CombAT combination with an α-blocker; PSA roughly halves on treatment and needs drug-specific interpretation; the PCPT / REDUCE high-grade-cancer warning and detection-bias debate; sexual effects and the poorly defined post-finasteride syndrome; 1-mg finasteride for hair loss is a separate product.</span>
  </li>
  <li>
    <a href="/docs/foundations/pharmacology/voiding-outlet/alpha-agonists" className="section-stack-title section-stack-link">α-Agonists</a>
    <span className="section-stack-desc">Midodrine, pseudoephedrine, ephedrine, imipramine (a reuptake blocker grouped with them). Off-label options for retrograde ejaculation or anejaculation; weak SUI evidence (Cochrane; not recommended by AUA/SUFU 2023, with duloxetine the EAU 2026 drug option); midodrine for neurogenic orthostatic hypotension, with a boxed warning for supine hypertension; phenylpropanolamine withdrawn from the US market in 2000; OTC decongestants as a cause of retention. Intracavernosal phenylephrine for ischemic priapism is covered in <a href="/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/priapism-management">Priapism Management</a>.</span>
  </li>
  <li>
    <a href="/docs/foundations/pharmacology/voiding-outlet/cholinergic-agonists" className="section-stack-title section-stack-link">Cholinergic Agonists</a>
    <span className="section-stack-desc">Bethanechol as the canonical agent; labeled for acute postoperative or postpartum non-obstructive retention and neurogenic atony. For underactive detrusor the evidence is low-quality, and EAU 2026 recommends against prescribing parasympathomimetics; reliable bladder emptying remains central. Contraindicated in outlet obstruction or recent bladder or bowel surgery.</span>
  </li>
  <li>
    <a href="/docs/foundations/pharmacology/voiding-outlet/skeletal-muscle-relaxants" className="section-stack-title section-stack-link">Skeletal Muscle Relaxants</a>
    <span className="section-stack-desc">Baclofen (oral and intrathecal) for spasticity and dyssynergia, diazepam (including compounded vaginal), and cyclobenzaprine for pelvic-floor hypertonicity or myofascial pain. Urologic evidence is limited, and agent-specific sedation, withdrawal, retention and anticholinergic effects matter; no relaxant is routinely indicated for postoperative retention.</span>
  </li>
  <li>
    <a href="/docs/foundations/pharmacology/voiding-outlet/dantrolene" className="section-stack-title section-stack-link">Dantrolene</a>
    <span className="section-stack-desc">Direct skeletal-muscle relaxant tried for external-sphincter hypertonicity in spinal cord injury; the urologic role is historical and off-label. Boxed hepatitis warning limits use; intrasphincteric botulinum toxin is an off-label alternative for selected dyssynergia. The <a href="/docs/foundations/pharmacology/voiding-outlet/skeletal-muscle-relaxants">Skeletal Muscle Relaxants</a> page summarizes the dantrolene evidence and label limits.</span>
  </li>
</ul>
