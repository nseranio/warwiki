# 5α-Reductase Inhibitors (5-ARIs)

## Clinical role

Finasteride and dutasteride reduce conversion of testosterone to dihydrotestosterone (DHT). In men with **symptomatic BPH and an enlarged prostate**, they gradually reduce gland volume and lower the risk of acute retention and BPH surgery. They are a long-term option; symptom benefit usually takes 3–6 months, so set that expectation at the start. AUA 2026 suggests a first reassessment at about six months. They do not provide immediate relief of retention or replace evaluation of another cause of LUTS.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

EAU 2026 recommends 5-ARIs for moderate-to-severe LUTS with increased progression risk, using prostate volume &gt;40 mL as an example. AUA 2026 recommends offering a 5-ARI for symptoms when estimated prostate volume is **&gt;30 cc and/or PSA &gt;1.5 ng/mL** (Moderate, Grade A); reducing progression in this enlarged-prostate group is a Clinical Principle. The alpha-blocker/5-ARI combination uses the same enlargement criteria (Moderate, Grade B). These are selection guides rather than proof that every man above one threshold needs treatment. PSA must first be interpreted in its clinical context.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Mechanism and expected effects

5α-reductase converts testosterone to dihydrotestosterone (DHT), which is more potent at the androgen receptor and drives prostatic growth. Blocking it reduces growth signaling, increasing apoptosis and atrophy of prostatic tissue. Two isoenzymes matter: type 2 predominates in prostate tissue, while type 1 is found in skin, scalp and liver. Finasteride inhibits type 2 only; dutasteride inhibits both.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

| Parameter | Finasteride 5 mg | Dutasteride 0.5 mg |
|---|---|---|
| Serum DHT suppression | About 70% | About 94–95% (median, years 1–4) |
| Prostate volume | −17.9% at 4 years (PLESS MRI subset) vs +14.1% with placebo | −24.7% at 12 months and −26.7% at 24 months vs −3.4% and −2.2% with placebo |
| Symptom score | Modest gain over placebo (see PLESS below) | AUA-SI change at 24 months −3.8 vs −1.6 with placebo in men ≥40 cc (difference −2.2) |
| Serum testosterone | Rises about 10–20%, within the physiologic range | Rises about 19–26%, within the physiologic range |

Volume reduction is greater in larger glands. Off-treatment, DHT returns to pretreatment levels in about two weeks after finasteride, and prostate volume that had fallen about 20% after three months of treatment returned close to baseline about three months after stopping. Symptom control therefore lasts only while treatment continues.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The deeper DHT suppression with dutasteride has not translated into differences in volume reduction, symptom score or flow in head-to-head data (see EPICS below).

## Agents and administration

| Agent | Adult BPH regimen | Important formulation and pharmacology points |
|---|---|---|
| **Finasteride (Proscar)** | **5 mg orally once daily**, with or without food | Primarily type 2 inhibition. The 1-mg alopecia product is a separate indication/dose that also lowers PSA (about 40% at 48 weeks per AUA 2026). No renal dose adjustment; use caution with liver dysfunction. |
| **Dutasteride (Avodart)** | **0.5 mg orally once daily**, with or without food | Type 1 and 2 inhibition. Swallow capsules whole; do not open or chew. No renal adjustment. Hepatic impairment has not been studied adequately; exposure may increase. The approximately five-week terminal half-life means effects/exposure do not disappear immediately after stopping. |
| **Dutasteride/tamsulosin (Jalyn)** | **0.5/0.4 mg once daily**, about 30 minutes after the same meal | Swallow whole; the tamsulosin component adds orthostasis, interactions, ejaculation effects and IFIS precautions. See the [alpha-blocker hub](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/voiding-outlet/alpha-blockers). |

Dosing and organ-function information are from the US labels.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Dutasteride lowers circulating DHT more than finasteride, but this is not proof of better clinical outcomes. In **EPICS**, 1,630 men randomized to the two agents had similar prostate-volume, symptom and flow improvements over 12 months; the primary endpoint was prostate volume, not long-term retention or surgery.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

Finasteride has no established clinically important pharmacokinetic interactions in its label. Use caution with **chronic potent CYP3A4 inhibition and dutasteride**; combination products require review of both ingredients. Adding an alpha-blocker can increase adverse effects even when there is no major pharmacokinetic interaction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## What the major BPH trials establish

| Evidence | Population and interpretation |
|---|---|
| **PLESS** | 3,040 men with enlarged prostates randomized to finasteride 5 mg or placebo for four years. Finasteride reduced BPH surgery by 55% (5% vs 10%) and acute urinary retention by 57% (3% vs 7%), with a modest symptom-score gain (mean decrease 3.3 vs 1.3).<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> |
| **MTOPS** | 3,047 men, mean follow-up 4.5 years. Doxazosin, finasteride and combination reduced composite clinical progression by 39%, 34% and 66%, respectively, versus placebo. The composite was largely symptom worsening; only finasteride and combination reduced retention and invasive therapy. Do not read the 66% as a reduction in each complication.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **CombAT** | 4,844 men with IPSS ≥12, prostate volume ≥30 mL and PSA 1.5–10 ng/mL; four years, no placebo arm. Dutasteride/tamsulosin improved symptoms and reduced overall clinical progression more than either alone. For the primary **AUR-or-surgery** endpoint, combination was superior to tamsulosin **but not significantly superior to dutasteride**.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |

Combination therapy can be considered initially in a symptomatic patient with substantial progression risk; failure of alpha-blocker monotherapy is not a prerequisite. Typical candidates have a gland &gt;30 cc and/or PSA &gt;1.5 ng/mL with moderate-to-severe LUTS, or already have symptoms on alpha-blocker monotherapy. The alpha-blocker supplies early relief while the 5-ARI effect builds over months. In CombAT, prostate biopsies obtained for PSA changes did not show a higher rate of high-grade cancer with combination therapy, and in a trial of immediate dutasteride/tamsulosin versus watchful waiting with delayed tamsulosin, withdrawals for erectile and ejaculatory adverse events were higher in the combination group.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

Stopping one component after a period of control is not well studied. In a small RCT of men controlled on combination therapy, IPSS deterioration of at least 2 points at 24 months occurred in 27.8% after alpha-blocker withdrawal and 26.4% after 5-ARI withdrawal, and men with larger prostates more often needed to resume the 5-ARI or proceed to TURP. Daily tadalafil 5 mg plus a 5-ARI is a conditional AUA option for a gland &gt;30 cc and/or PSA &gt;1.5 ng/mL, with or without ED.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Balance expected benefit against sexual effects, treatment burden and the time needed for the 5-ARI to work. Treatment is generally prolonged while useful, but it is not automatically lifelong after effective surgery or a change in goals.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## PSA interpretation: use the drug-specific timeline and the trend

5-ARIs commonly lower PSA by about half, **including when prostate cancer is present**. Record the drug, start date and adherence wherever PSA is interpreted.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

| Product label | Serial measurements | Comparing one value with untreated reference ranges |
|---|---|---|
| **Finasteride 5 mg** | Establish a new baseline after **at least six months**; monitor periodically | After ≥6 months, double the observed value for comparison |
| **Dutasteride 0.5 mg** | Establish a new baseline after **at least three months**; monitor periodically | After ≥3 months, double the observed value for comparison |

Doubling is an **approximate interpretation adjustment**, not the patient's exact untreated or “true” PSA. It does not replace the trend: evaluate a **confirmed increase from the on-treatment nadir**, even if the number falls within an untreated normal range. Review adherence and other explanations; an isolated fluctuation is not automatically cancer. Percent free PSA generally does not require the same adjustment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The 2019 Veterans Affairs cohort of 80,875 men with prostate cancer associated prediagnostic 5-ARI use with longer diagnostic delays and worse cancer-specific outcomes. This observational finding reinforces the need to recognize PSA suppression; it does not establish that the medicine itself caused aggressive cancer or that missed PSA adjustment was the sole cause.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Sexual effects, fertility and breast symptoms

Discuss erectile dysfunction, reduced libido, lower ejaculate volume and breast tenderness/enlargement before treatment; AUA 2026 recommends counseling on side effects and on the effect on prostate cancer diagnosis when prescribing (Moderate, Grade C). Frequency varies by product, dose, follow-up and whether an alpha-blocker is also used. The label rates below are drug-related events against placebo.

| Adverse event | Finasteride 5 mg, year 1 (vs placebo) | Finasteride, years 2–4 (vs placebo) | Dutasteride, months 0–6 (vs placebo) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Erectile dysfunction | 8.1% (3.7%) | 5.1% (5.1%) | 4.7% (1.7%) |
| Decreased libido | 6.4% (3.4%) | 2.6% (2.6%) | 3.0% (1.4%) |
| Ejaculatory disorder or reduced volume | 3.7% volume (0.8%); disorder 0.8% (0.1%) | 1.5% volume (0.5%) | 1.4% (0.5%) |
| Breast enlargement or disorder | 0.5% (0.1%) | 1.8% (1.1%) | 0.5% (0.2%) |

Finasteride figures are from the 4-year placebo-controlled study in the Proscar label; dutasteride figures are from the pooled 24-month trials in the Avodart label. Events cluster in the first year and are near placebo rates by years 2 to 4. In the REDUCE trial gynecomastia was 1.9% with dutasteride versus 1.0% with placebo, and combination with an alpha-blocker adds ejaculatory and erectile effects (MTOPS, CombAT). Most side effects of BPH pharmacotherapy resolve after discontinuation, though some long-term effects are reported and merit shared decision-making.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Fewer *newly reported* sexual adverse events in later trial years does not prove that every existing symptom resolves.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Both labels acknowledge reports of sexual problems persisting after discontinuation, with frequency and the independent drug contribution difficult to determine. AUA 2026 describes post-finasteride syndrome as a poorly defined constellation, drawn mainly from patient reports in men taking finasteride for hair loss and not substantiated as a clinical entity by RCT adverse-event reporting, but notes that the FDA has amended the labels to warn of it.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Take persistent symptoms seriously and assess other contributors rather than promising reversibility or assigning an inevitable permanent syndrome.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Semen changes can matter when fertility is a priority. The dutasteride label describes a small healthy-volunteer study (27 treated, 23 placebo) with reduced total sperm count, volume and motility; mean sperm count had not returned to baseline at 24 weeks after treatment. Average normal-range values do not establish safety for an individual with impaired fertility. Finasteride has postmarketing infertility/poor-semen-quality reports, including improvement after withdrawal. Consider a medication and semen assessment when conception is difficult.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Evaluate new breast lumps, nipple discharge or other concerning changes. Male breast cancer has been reported after marketing; a causal relationship is not established.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Mood and suicidality: current regulatory distinctions

The US **Proscar** label lists depression and suicidal ideation in postmarketing experience. The reviewed US **Avodart** label lists depressed mood; it should not be described as identical wording for both drugs.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

**EMA's 2025 review** confirmed suicidal ideation as an adverse effect of oral finasteride **1 mg and 5 mg**, with unknown frequency and most reports arising from alopecia use. It found no direct link for dutasteride and recommended a precautionary warning because of the shared mechanism.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

The **May 11, 2026 MHRA update** reinforces baseline mood-history assessment and regular review for psychiatric and sexual effects. Its instructions distinguish stopping **finasteride 1 mg** and promptly contacting the prescriber if depression/suicidal thoughts develop from seeking prompt medical advice for **finasteride 5 mg or dutasteride**. Immediate danger or self-harm requires emergency care regardless of drug or dose.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

Large observational cohorts have associated 5-ARI use with depression; suicide estimates have been less consistent. Welk's 2017 study was a **matched cohort**, not a case-control study. The Swedish cohort's early dementia association diminished with longer exposure, raising detection/confounding concerns. Neither observational association nor a proposed neurosteroid mechanism proves a specific cause in an individual patient.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

## Prostate cancer: prevention trials do not remove the label warning

PCPT (finasteride, 18,882 men, seven years) and REDUCE (dutasteride, 8,231 men with PSA 2.5–10 ng/mL and a recent negative biopsy, four years) reduced biopsy-detected prostate cancer overall, mainly lower-grade disease: a 24.8% reduction in prevalence in PCPT (18.4% vs 24.4%) and a 22.8% relative risk reduction in REDUCE (659/3,305 vs 858/3,424 men).<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[21]](#ref21)</sup> Both designs relied on protocol biopsies, and finasteride's PCPT Gleason 7–10 tumors were more frequent (6.4% vs 5.1% of men).<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> **Neither drug is FDA-approved for cancer prevention.** Current US labels retain warnings concerning **Gleason 8–10** cancers: finasteride 1.8% versus placebo 1.1% in PCPT, and dutasteride 1.0% versus 0.5% in REDUCE.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Do not conflate those warnings with every Gleason ≥7 comparison. In REDUCE, overall Gleason 7–10 counts were similar (220 vs 233 tumors), while a Gleason 8–10 imbalance emerged in years 3–4 (12 vs 1 tumors). Smaller prostate volume and improved detection can contribute to the findings, but a blanket statement that bias conclusively explains every high-grade signal goes beyond the labels and evidence.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

Long-term evidence is reassuring about mortality, with uncertainty:

- At median 18.4-year follow-up in PCPT, there were **42 prostate-cancer deaths among 9,423 eligible finasteride-assigned men and 56 among 9,457 placebo-assigned men**, HR 0.75 (95% CI 0.50–1.12). This did not establish a mortality reduction.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- A 2023 meta-analysis of 11 studies and more than 3.2 million men found no statistically significant mortality association, adjusted HR 1.04 (95% CI 0.80–1.35). Mixed study designs and confounding limit causal conclusions; “not significant” is not proof of zero risk.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

Use these findings for balanced BPH counseling alongside careful PSA surveillance, not to promote routine chemoprevention or dismiss the current high-grade warning.

## Other uses and perioperative decisions

- **Prostatic hematuria:** after excluding other causes, a 5-ARI may help refractory bleeding attributed to an enlarged prostate. This AUA 2026 Expert Opinion option does not replace hematuria evaluation or acute clot-retention management; do not impose the BPH symptom-response timeline on bleeding control.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **CP/CPPS:** AUA 2025 permits 5-ARIs when there are coexisting voiding symptoms from BPH or enlargement (expert opinion). They are not general-purpose pelvic-pain treatment.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- **TURP bleeding:** 5-ARI pretreatment lowers prostatic microvascular density and has reduced perioperative blood loss at TURP in RCTs.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Active surveillance for prostate cancer:** AUA 2026 advises counseling that 5-ARI treatment is unlikely to increase cancer progression (Expert Opinion). Expect PSA suppression and interpret the trend accordingly.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Surgery:** UKCPA advises continuing finasteride/dutasteride perioperatively. The old instruction to stop two weeks before prostate surgery to improve bleeding control was unsupported. After effective TURP and successful catheter removal, reassess whether the medication is still needed; no universal 2–4-week restart rule applies.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

## Contraindications and interactions

Both drugs are contraindicated in pregnancy (see below) and in men with hypersensitivity to the drug; the dutasteride label extends this to other 5-ARIs, and lists serious skin reactions and angioedema among reported reactions. Consider other urologic causes before starting, since BPH and prostate cancer can coexist. Dutasteride is metabolized by CYP3A4/5: use caution with potent chronic inhibitors such as ritonavir. Verapamil and diltiazem raise dutasteride exposure, but no dose change is advised. Tamsulosin and terazosin do not change dutasteride steady-state pharmacokinetics.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Pregnancy, handling and donation

Pregnancy exposure can disrupt development of male external genitalia. These BPH products are not indicated for women or children. A person who is or may be pregnant should avoid **crushed/broken finasteride tablets and dutasteride capsules**, especially leaking capsules; wash exposed skin promptly. Intact finasteride coating prevents contact during ordinary handling. Current narrative pregnancy labeling replaces the old letter-category shorthand.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Very small quantities occur in semen; do not turn this into a universal US-label condom requirement for both products. Discuss pregnancy exposure using the applicable product and jurisdiction's labeling. **No blood donation during dutasteride treatment or for at least six months after the last dose**; check the blood service's deferral rules for other products.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Practical points

1. Chart the 5-ARI and start date so every clinician interpreting PSA applies the adjustment. Any confirmed rise above the on-treatment nadir needs evaluation.
2. Set the 3–6-month expectation at the start so a patient does not stop at two months for apparent failure.
3. For a large gland with substantial progression risk, consider combination therapy from the outset instead of sequencing.
4. Choose between finasteride and dutasteride on cost, formulary and patient factors; clinical outcomes are comparable.
5. Screen mood history and counsel on sexual and mood effects before starting; ask about breast symptoms at follow-up.
6. Reinforce handling precautions for pregnant household members.
