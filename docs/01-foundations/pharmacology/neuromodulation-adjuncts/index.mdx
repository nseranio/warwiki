# Neuromodulation Adjuncts

Injectable biologic agents used as neuromodulatory therapy. The category is currently dominated by **botulinum toxin**, a chemodenervating agent. Its established urologic indications are intradetrusor injection for idiopathic OAB and neurogenic detrusor overactivity (NDO). Use in the external sphincter (detrusor-sphincter dyssynergia), bladder neck (primary bladder-neck obstruction), pelvic floor (hypertonicity, CP/CPPS) and the bladder base for IC/BPS (intratrigonal injection) is off-label and rests on smaller studies. This subsection covers the pharmacologic agents themselves; for the device-based neuromodulation procedures, see [sacral neuromodulation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/sacral-neuromodulation), [percutaneous tibial nerve stimulation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/percutaneous-tibial-nerve-stimulation) and [implantable tibial nerve stimulation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/implantable-tibial-nerve-stimulation) in the Treatment Atlas, and the [intradetrusor injection procedure](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/intradetrusor-botox) for the technique.

For CP/CPPS, AUA 2025 found the evidence too weak to recommend botulinum toxin treatment, in an ungraded Future Directions discussion.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
