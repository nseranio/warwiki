# Phytotherapy for LUTS / BPH

Phytotherapy for LUTS/BPH requires product-specific counseling.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[31]](#ref31)[[32]](#ref32)</sup> EAU 2026 weakly recommends offering hexane-extracted *Serenoa repens* to men with LUTS who want to avoid adverse effects, particularly sexual effects.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> It strongly recommends counseling that the benefit may be modest.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> The AUA 2026 LUTS/BPH guideline makes no recommendation for or against herbal products.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

This article covers the four agents that dominate the BPH/LUTS phytotherapy literature: saw palmetto (*Serenoa repens*), β-sitosterol, pumpkin seed (*Cucurbita pepo*), and Pygeum africanum, with the evidence with priority given to placebo-controlled studies and current guidance. Cernilton (rye-grass pollen extract) is noted at the end of the agent sections. For medical therapy, see the α-blocker and 5-ARI articles; the EAU recommends 5-ARIs for men with moderate-to-severe LUTS and increased progression risk (for example, prostate volume &gt;40 mL; Strong).<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> For surgical options, see [bladder outlet obstruction](/docs/clinical-conditions/03b-voiding-outlet/bladder-outlet-obstruction).

---

## Why Phytotherapy Lives in "Legacy &amp; Low-Evidence"

- **Limited evidence and product-specific guidance.** Established LUTS drugs and progression-directed 5-ARI therapy have a different evidence base. Arnold et al. discuss 5-ARIs for uncontrolled LUTS with prostate volume at least 30 mL.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> EAU 2026 strongly recommends a 5-ARI for men with moderate-to-severe LUTS and increased progression risk (for example, prostate volume &gt;40 mL), without requiring an α-blocker trial.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
- **Heterogeneous products.** The hexanic lipidosterolic extract of *Serenoa repens* (Permixon) behaves differently from ethanolic extracts; non-glucosidic β-sitosterol differs from the β-D-glucoside form; pumpkin-seed "extract" is not a single entity.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> EAU 2026 lists the EMA herbal committee (HMPC) monographs: only hexane-extracted *Serenoa repens* has well-established use for symptomatic BPH, whereas ethanol-extracted saw palmetto, pumpkin seed, *Prunus africana* bark, nettle root and willow herb are traditional use only.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> The EAU panel notes that batches from one producer can differ in active content.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
- **Signal-to-noise problem.** The best-designed, large-scale placebo-controlled RCTs (CAMUS 2011, STEP 2006) are negative for saw palmetto; positive meta-analyses rely heavily on older, smaller trials and observational data with heterogeneous extracts.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Trial tolerability is generally favorable**. Check the exact ingredients, concurrent drugs and adverse effects.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[16]](#ref16)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

## 1. Saw Palmetto (*Serenoa repens*)

The most widely used BPH phytotherapy worldwide. Mechanistically attractive (proposed 5α-reductase inhibition, anti-inflammatory, anti-androgenic, and antiproliferative effects), but none conclusively proven.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

**The two evidence worlds:**

| Camp | Key data | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| **Negative** | **CAMUS (JAMA 2011)** — n = 369, dose escalation 320 → 640 → 960 mg vs placebo × 72 wk. **No dose of saw palmetto reduced LUTS more than placebo** for AUASI, Qmax, PVR, QoL, or nocturia.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Cochrane 2023 — high-certainty evidence of statistically better urologic scores but **differences of no clinical importance**.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | High-certainty overall finding of little/no clinically important benefit |
| **Positive studies, differing preparations/designs** | **Vela-Navarrete 2018 meta-analysis** — 27 studies / N = 5,800 across all arms (n = 3,926 received hexanic extract [Permixon]) — versus placebo, nocturia −0.64 voids/night (95% CI −0.98 to −0.31) and Qmax +2.75 mL/s (95% CI 0.57–4.93); IPSS improvement similar to α-blockers.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> **Ye 2019 Chinese multicenter placebo-controlled RCT** — 354 randomized; IPSS, Qmax, QoL and sexual-function changes favored extract over placebo at 24 weeks.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | Vela combined randomized and observational studies; in Ye's placebo-controlled trial, IPSS and Qmax changes favored the tested extract at 24 weeks.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |

Preparation, study design and bias may contribute to different findings.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> Generic "saw palmetto" supplements are not equivalent to Permixon hexanic extract.

**Safety of hexanic extract (Permixon):** generally well tolerated; gastrointestinal disorders had a mean incidence of 3.8%. Studies found no clinically relevant PSA effect (important for cancer screening) or relevant adverse effect on sexual function, a tolerability advantage over 5-ARIs.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## 2. β-Sitosterol

β-Sitosterol is a plant phytosterol reported in saw palmetto, Pygeum bark and pumpkin seeds.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> Phytosterols recur across BPH phytotherapy preparations.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

**Mechanism (modern preclinical work):**

- **APOPROSTAT Forte formulation.** In isolated human prostate tissue, this β-sitosterol-rich preparation inhibited adrenergic, non-adrenergic, and neurogenic contractions by 63–71%. The study also used the whole formulation for its stromal-cell assay; the active constituent was not identified.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Stromal cell proliferation** inhibited up to 67% at 72 h<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Weak 5α-reductase type 2 inhibition.** IC₅₀ 3.24 μM, approximately 660-fold weaker than dutasteride (IC₅₀ 4.88 nM)<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

**Evidence:**

- **Cochrane (Wilt 2000)** (4 RCTs / n = 519): IPSS −4.9 (95% CI −6.3 to −3.5; 2 trials), Qmax +3.91 mL/s (95% CI 0.91–6.90; 4 trials), PVR −28.6 mL (95% CI −41.4 to −15.8; 4 trials).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> No effect on prostate size.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Berges 1995 Lancet RCT:** In 200 randomized patients, Harzol (a mixed-phytosterol preparation reported as 20 mg β-sitosterol three times daily) reduced the modified Boyarsky score by 6.7 points versus 2.1 with placebo at six months. Among patients with six-month data, IPSS fell by 7.4 versus 2.1 points; mean Qmax rose from 9.9 to 15.2 mL/s and mean PVR fell from 65.8 to 30.4 mL in the treated group.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

**Preparation caveat:** Three of four trials used nonglucosidic preparations; the sole β-D-glucoside-only product (WA184) showed no improvement in urinary flow measures.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> Without label transparency, the product risk is real.

**Limitations:** short trial durations (4–26 weeks), no long-term progression data, no data on whether it prevents BPH complications.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## 3. Pumpkin Seed (*Cucurbita pepo*)

Evidence is preparation-specific. The GRANU trial randomized 1,431 men to whole pumpkin seed, pumpkin-seed extract or placebo for one year. The primary responder outcome was a ≥5-point IPSS decrease. Extract did not differ from placebo; in the partially blinded whole-seed arm, 275/470 (58.5%) met this outcome versus 223/467 (47.8%) with placebo in the intention-to-treat, last-observation-carried-forward analysis. The whole-seed comparison was descriptive because confirmatory testing stopped after the negative extract comparison.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)[[15]](#ref15)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

**Mechanism:**

- Inhibits hyperplastic prostate-cell growth approximately 40–50% in vitro<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- The tested hydroethanolic pumpkin-seed extract and cucurbitin did not activate androgen, estrogen or progesterone receptors in yeast transactivation assays.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- Phytosterol (including β-sitosterol) content may contribute the same mechanisms seen above<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

**Other, uncontrolled evidence:**

- **Theil 2022** noninterventional cohort: 130 men enrolled for up to 24 months, with 83 analyzed at 12 months. Mean IPSS change at 12 months was −4.7 points (95% CI −5.4 to −3.9); 62/83 (74.7%) had a ≥3-point reduction. IPSS-QoL "mostly satisfied" rose from 9/83 (11%) to 51/83 (61%) in that analyzed group. Sexual-function scores did not worsen in this uncontrolled cohort.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Leibbrand 2019** single-arm pilot, n = 60 enrolled (n = 56 per-protocol analysis): at 12 weeks, mean IPSS fell 30.1% from baseline; mean PVR fell from 83.7 to 63.1 mL.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

## 4. Pygeum africanum (African Plum Tree Bark)

Used in Europe for BPH since 1969. Older reviews report a short-term signal.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

**Mechanism:**

- **Anti-inflammatory.** Decreased leukotrienes and 5-LOX metabolites<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- **Antifibroblast** in the prostate<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- **Antiandrogenic.** N-butylbenzenesulfonamide (NBBS) from dichloromethane extract acts as an androgen-receptor antagonist<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- **Modulates bladder contractility and restores prostate-epithelium secretory activity**<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

**Evidence:**

- **Wilt 2002 Cochrane review (subsequently withdrawn)** included 18 RCTs (n = 1,562; mean duration 64 days). Six trials (n = 474) yielded a combined symptom/flow effect size of −0.8 SD (95% CI −1.4 to −0.3), not an IPSS change. In five trials (n = 430), physician-rated overall improvement was 65% versus 30% (RR 2.1, 95% CI 1.4–3.1). The nocturia difference versus placebo was −0.91 voids/night (−19%; 95% CI −1.95 to +0.14; three trials, n = 325), which did not establish a nocturia benefit. Versus placebo, Qmax was 2.5 mL/s higher (+23%; four trials, n = 363) and PVR was 13 mL lower (−24%; two trials, n = 264).<sup>[[19]](#ref19)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- **Ishani 2000 meta-analysis.** Ishani 2000 and the later 2002 Cochrane review each reported 18 RCTs (n = 1,562); dropout was 13% with Pygeum and 11% with placebo.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

**Limitations:** short mean duration of approximately 64 days, inconsistent reporting of means and SDs limits meta-analysis precision, no long-term or disease-progression data.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

---

**Cernilton** (*Secale cereale* pollen extract): a Cochrane review of four small trials (n = 444, 12–24 weeks) found modest improvement in self-rated symptoms and nocturia, with no improvement in flow, residual volume or prostate size, and rare, mild adverse events. Preparation quality was unknown and the comparators were unproven.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup> The 2000 review was withdrawn in 2011 because the authors did not update it.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup> It has no guideline recommendation.

## How to Counsel a Patient Who Asks About Phytotherapy

**Scenario 1.** Patient already taking it and LUTS improved:

- Confirm what they are taking.
- Interpret PSA in the clinical context.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Studied Serenoa preparations have not shown the predictable PSA reduction expected with 5-ARIs.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Discuss observed benefit, cost, product uncertainty and adverse effects.

**Scenario 2.** Patient asking whether to try phytotherapy instead of an α-blocker or 5-ARI:

- Explain the limited evidence and the specific EAU option above. If a patient chooses a trial, agree on symptom reassessment and stopping criteria; do not substitute it for indicated treatment of obstruction, retention or renal risk.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
- **For progression prevention in symptomatic BPH with demonstrable enlargement,** assess prostate size, PSA when clinically available, and progression risk for 5-ARI treatment. Phytotherapy has not established prevention of retention or need for BPH surgery.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

**Scenario 3.** Patient failing phytotherapy:

- Reassess as with any failing medical therapy: symptom severity, PVR, consider pressure-flow testing when DU versus BOO remains uncertain and the result would guide treatment, particularly before invasive treatment<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup>, prostate size on imaging, and escalate to guideline-directed α-blocker ± 5-ARI ± surgery.

## Practical Pearls and Perioperative Review

- **Document the actual product and ingredients.**
- **Avoid unsupported efficacy rankings.** Older positive meta-analyses, mechanistic experiments and modern placebo-controlled trials answer different questions.
- **Progression prevention is unproven.** Phytotherapy has not established prevention of retention or need for BPH surgery.
- Review supplements with anesthesia before surgery and use the local discontinuation plan. Saw palmetto is generally considered to have a low likelihood of clinically important drug interactions,<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup> but a case report described severe intraoperative hemorrhage in a saw-palmetto user with prolonged bleeding time that normalized after stopping it.<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup> Ask about use before prostate, perineal or prosthetic surgery, especially with anticoagulants or antiplatelets.
- **Supplement quality varies.** US herbal products are marketed as dietary supplements without premarket proof of efficacy or standardized content.
