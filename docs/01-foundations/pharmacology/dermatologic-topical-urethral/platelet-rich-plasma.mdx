# Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP)

**Platelet-rich plasma (PRP)** is an autologous blood-derived biologic studied across urologic and urogynecologic conditions. These applications remain investigational. Two 2026 IC/BPS meta-analyses report symptom improvement, predominantly from uncontrolled studies; benefit over placebo or standard care remains unestablished.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> PRP has also been studied as an adjunct to internal urethrotomy, for female SUI and male post-prostatectomy incontinence, erectile dysfunction, Peyronie's disease, genital lichen sclerosus, and hypospadias-repair complication prevention. The dominant cross-cutting limitation across urology is the lack of standardization in preparation protocols, concentrations, activation methods, and dosing.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

This article is the class-level hub across urologic PRP applications. For narrower deep-dives, see [Antimitotics & antifibrotics](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/dermatologic-topical-urethral/antimitotics-antifibrotics) (PRP as a stricture adjunct among other antifibrotics), [Peyronie's disease agents](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/peyronies-disease-agents) (PRP as one of several intralesional options), and [Intravesical IC/BPS agents](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/bladder-pain-ic-bps) (the broader instillation armamentarium).

## Mechanism and preparation

PRP is produced by centrifugation of autologous whole blood to concentrate platelets above baseline; urologic protocols have typically targeted about 2.5–5× peripheral-blood concentration.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[9]](#ref9)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> Concentration depends on the separation system and spin protocol.<sup>[[33]](#ref33)</sup> After activation (thrombin, calcium chloride, or collagen), platelet α-granules release a growth-factor cascade:<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

| Factor | Role |
|---|---|
| **PDGF** | Fibroblast recruitment and proliferation |
| **TGF-β** | ECM synthesis and remodeling (can also drive fibrosis — see caveat below) |
| **VEGF** | Angiogenesis and revascularization |
| **IGF-1, EGF, bFGF, HGF** | Cell proliferation, differentiation, tissue regeneration |

Proposed regenerative mechanisms include revascularization, connective-tissue remodeling and effects on progenitor cells.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Autologous sourcing reduces donor-related concerns but does not eliminate contamination, injection injury or reactions to additives. Infection control remains necessary.<sup>[[34]](#ref34)</sup>

**Regulatory status.** In the United States, FDA-cleared platelet and plasma separators in the bone-graft-handling category prepare PRP for mixing with autograft or allograft bone.<sup>[[51]](#ref51)</sup> That device clearance does not establish an indication for injection into the bladder, urethra, corpora or genital skin.<sup>[[37]](#ref37)[[51]](#ref51)</sup>

### Classification and standardization problem

Classification systems include Dohan Ehrenfest (P-PRP, L-PRP, P-PRF, L-PRF based on leukocyte and fibrin content) and the PAW, DEPA, and MARSPILL systems.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Across urologic PRP literature, there is no standard for:<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- Single versus double spin; target platelet concentration
- Leukocyte content (leukocyte-rich versus leukocyte-poor)
- Activation method
- Injection volume, number of sessions, and session interval
- Outcome measures and follow-up

This is the single biggest obstacle to cross-study comparison and clinical adoption.

## IC/BPS — investigational bladder-wall injections

Two 2026 meta-analyses define the current evidence.

### Meta-analyses

- **Ali 2026** (8 studies): intravesical PRP improved VAS pain (pooled SMD −0.78; 95% CI −1.15 to −0.41; p &lt; 0.001), urinary frequency, O'Leary-Sant ICSI and ICPI scores, functional bladder capacity and uroflowmetry, with within-cohort improvements reported through six months, without a placebo-controlled durability estimate.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Simões 2026.** Confirmed the signal but noted all included studies were quasi-experimental, uncontrolled or non-randomized; overall evidence level low. Pooled GRA ≥ 2 success was 48.3% in five uncontrolled studies of 128 women, assessed 1–3 months after treatment.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Key prospective data

| Study | n | Protocol | Finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Jiang 2020** | 40 refractory IC/BPS | 4 monthly intravesical injections (10 mL, 2.5× concentration) | GRA ≥ 2 in **28/40 (70%) at 1 mo** and **27/40 (67.5%) at 3 mo** after the 4th injection; urinary biomarkers showed ↓ NGF, MMP-13, VEGF with ↑ PDGF-AB<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **Xu 2026** | 102 non-ulcerative primary BPS | Novel **2-phase protocol** — weekly × 3 wk, then monthly × 3 mo; apheresis-derived PRP | Higher cystoscopic hydrodistention grades (II–III) showed greater and more durable improvement than Grade I; **cystoscopic grade was the only independent predictor of response**<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Jiang 2022** (dose-finding) | 63 randomized to single high-dose subgroups (60 analyzed); compared with 55 earlier patients (52 analyzed) | Single high-dose (100 mL blood) with saline or plasma over 20 or 40 sites, vs a previous cohort given 4 monthly low-dose (50 mL blood) injections | At 6 mo after the first injection (6 mo after single high-dose versus 3 mo after fourth low-dose), ICSI and ICPI improvements were smaller after single high-dose than after 4 low-dose injections (a comparison with an earlier cohort, without a sham arm); vehicle and injection-site number had no effect<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |

### Practical takeaway

Four monthly PRP bladder-wall injections are a studied IC/BPS protocol, not an established standard; the 2025 German S2k guideline makes no recommendation for PRP alone because data are insufficient and protocols and preparations vary.<sup>[[54]](#ref54)</sup> Hydrodistention grade and biomarkers are exploratory correlates; uncontrolled studies cannot distinguish treatment benefit from placebo, regression to the mean or co-interventions. See [Intravesical IC/BPS agents](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/bladder-pain-ic-bps) for the broader instillation framework including triamcinolone for Hunner lesions.

## Urethral stricture — adjunct to internal urethrotomy

Rezaei 2019 supplies the randomized recurrence result covered in [Antimitotics & antifibrotics](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/dermatologic-topical-urethral/antimitotics-antifibrotics). A later randomized study of strictures under 2 cm (Choudhury 2026; 32 per arm) reported mean six-month Qmax of 17.34 versus 11.39 mL/s after urethrotomy with versus without PRP; it did not establish lower recurrence.<sup>[[42]](#ref42)</sup> Points repeated here for reference:

- **Rezaei 2019 RCT** (n = 87, bulbar strictures) of IU plus submucosal PRP versus IU plus saline: 12-mo recurrence 9.09% versus 26.82% (p = 0.032); 24-mo recurrence 9/41 (21.95%) versus 18/41 (43.90%) among patients assessed.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> The indexed abstract prints p = 0.34 while EAU reports p = 0.034; this discrepancy precludes asserting that efficacy disappeared at two years.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[38]](#ref38)</sup> EAU 2026 describes primary bulbar strictures under 1.5 cm and reports grade-1 complications in 70% with PRP and 79% with saline.<sup>[[38]](#ref38)</sup> It gives a Weak recommendation to use intralesional injections after DVIU only within a clinical trial.<sup>[[38]](#ref38)</sup> The AUA 2023 stricture guideline does not include PRP among its recommendations.<sup>[[55]](#ref55)</sup>
- **Aydın 2020** rat urethral-trauma model: PRP reduced mucosal inflammation, spongiofibrosis, and edema<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Tavukcu 2018.** PRP normalized the collagen I:III ratio toward uninjured controls in a rat urethral-injury model<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Gul 2016 hypothesis.** Gul proposed TGF-β1-antibody-modified PRP to reduce urethral fibrosis and recurrence after urethrotomy; this has not been tested in a urethral cohort<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

PRP contains the profibrotic mediator TGF-β1, although a small rat urethral-injury study found a collagen ratio closer to uninjured controls after PRP.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> The balance of these effects in human urethral tissue remains unknown; TGF-β1-neutralized PRP remains a proposed, untested clinical adjunct.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

## Stress urinary incontinence

### Female SUI

**Utama 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis** included eight studies (three RCTs and five uncontrolled before/after studies), including a 62-woman cohort treated with PRP plus fractional CO₂ laser; it did not include the Grigoriadis 2024 saline-controlled RCT, so its pooled estimates do not synthesize all available randomized evidence.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[40]](#ref40)[[53]](#ref53)</sup> It reported short-term reductions in UDI-6 and ICIQ-SF scores, but its pooled ALPP estimate came from two small uncontrolled cohorts.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> Reported outcomes include:

- **Abdominal leak-point pressure** increased at three months in a pooled analysis of 26 women and 35 mixed-sex patients, including 21 after prostatectomy (MD +51.07 cmH₂O; 95% CI 36.21–65.93; p &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[52]](#ref52)</sup>
- Significant reduction in 1-hour pad weight
- Significant improvement in patient-reported symptoms

| Study | n | Key finding |
|---|---|---|
| **Saraluck 2024 RCT** (single-blind assessment) | 64 randomized (32/arm); 60 analyzed (31 vs 29) | A-PRP (2 injections) + PFMT vs PFMT alone — A-PRP arm had **about 8 g greater reduction in 1-h pad weight at 5 mo**; symptom improvement sustained at 2 and 5 mo; no AEs reported<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> |
| **Grigoriadis 2024 saline-controlled RCT** | 50 (25 vs 25) | Two periurethral PRP injections versus saline: subjective cure 32% versus 4% during six-month follow-up; one-hour pad loss also favored PRP at 6 mo.<sup>[[40]](#ref40)</sup> |
| **Ashton 2024 saline-controlled RCT** | 50 (25 vs 25) | One 5-mL anterior vaginal-wall injection versus saline: no significant difference in 6-mo composite success (negative cough stress test and PGI-I much or very much better).<sup>[[41]](#ref41)</sup> |
| **Chiang & Kuo 2022** (uncontrolled) | 26 ISD | 4 monthly urethral-sphincter injections — GRA ≥ 2 **50%**; complete dryness **46.2% at 3 mo / 26.9% at 12 mo**; ALPP **109.9 → 155.6 cmH₂O** at 3 mo after treatment (p &lt; 0.001; table value differs from the abstract)<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| **Long 2021** (pilot, uncontrolled) | 20 | Anterior vaginal wall / mid-urethral A-PRP injection — significant symptom improvement at 1 and 6 mo; trend toward better outcomes in younger patients (p = 0.07)<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |

### Male post-prostatectomy incontinence

**Yang 2026**, a later uncontrolled same-center series, reported GRA ≥ 2 in 29/71 (40.8%) three months after four monthly injections; cohort overlap with the 2021 study is unclear.<sup>[[46]](#ref46)</sup> **Lee 2021** prospective, uncontrolled (n = 28 PPI, 4 monthly urethral-sphincter injections):<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

- **Complete continence (pad-free): 21.4%**
- **GRA ≥ 2 success rate: 71.4%**
- GRA ≥ 1 clinical improvement: 92.9%
- VAS SUI from 6.5 to 3.5 (p &lt; 0.001)

## Erectile dysfunction — conflicting RCTs

The **AUA 2018 ED guideline** states that PRP should be considered experimental and not offered outside an IRB-approved research protocol (Expert Opinion); it also treats stem-cell and shockwave therapy as investigational.<sup>[[39]](#ref39)</sup> ED trials since then have conflicting results. Masterson 2023 is not the only double-blind placebo-controlled RCT: Poulios 2021 randomized 60 men to 10 mL PRP or placebo (two sessions) and reported a 6-month IIEF-EF MCID in 20/29 (69%) versus 7/26 (27%) assessed men (single center, one separation system).<sup>[[33]](#ref33)</sup> EAU still restricts intracavernosal PRP to clinical trials.<sup>[[32]](#ref32)[[33]](#ref33)</sup>

### Masterson 2023 (negative RCT)<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

Double-blind placebo-controlled (n = 61 mild-to-moderate ED, 2 intracavernosal PRP versus placebo):

- **No difference** in one-month IIEF-EF MCID achievement among assessed men (14/24 [58.3%] PRP versus 15/28 [53.6%] placebo; p = 0.730)<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- No difference in IIEF-EF scores at any timepoint
- No change in penile Doppler parameters
- No major adverse events occurred in this 61-man trial, and PRP did not outperform placebo on the prespecified one-month endpoint<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

### Shaher 2023 (positive RCT)<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

Double-blind randomized trial (109 allocated; 100 analyzed, 50 per arm; three PRP or saline injections 15 days apart): IIEF-EF, SEP Q2/Q3, and duplex parameters favored PRP at 1, 3, and 6 months; MCID was 76% versus 18% at 1 month and 70% versus 16% at 6 months.

### Du 2024 meta-analysis<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

Du pooled 11 controlled studies (n = 991, including one retrospective) and single-arm cohorts (n = 377). Ten controlled studies (n = 932) contributed to the IIEF SMD +0.59 (95% CI 0.34–0.84), and six (n = 503) to the MCID RR 1.94 (95% CI 1.33–2.83). The synthesis mixed PRP monotherapy with PRP plus shockwave therapy and reported heterogeneous results; the authors called for higher-quality trials.

**Practical interpretation:** differences in preparation, eligibility, co-treatment and analysis limit comparison. Randomized-trial-only reviews published in 2026 give differing pooled estimates: Jacob found no consistent, clinically meaningful IIEF benefit for PRP monotherapy versus placebo; de Amorim found no overall IIEF advantage for PRP/PRFM at one, three or six months but a six-month MCID signal from three trials; Savira found an IIEF signal at six months or later in six PRP/PRFM trials<sup>[[43]](#ref43)[[44]](#ref44)[[45]](#ref45)</sup> See [PDE5 inhibitors](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/pde5-inhibitors) for the established pharmacologic first-line.<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup>

## Peyronie's disease — early signal

**Ledesma 2024 Phase 2 RCT** randomized 41 men to PRP–placebo or placebo–PRP sequences; no adverse events were reported among the 28 included in the preliminary analysis. **PRP–placebo sequence:** median curvature changed from 40° at baseline to 25° at six months after crossover (within-sequence p = 0.047). **Placebo-PRP group:** curvature to 32.5° (p = 0.490); PDQ reduction significant (p = 0.020); a delayed effect was hypothesized.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

**Asmundo 2024 systematic review.** PRP studies for PD generally showed small-to-moderate benefits with mild transient side effects; protocol variation, small samples, and short follow-up limit interpretation.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> EAU 2026 gives a Strong recommendation to counsel patients that intralesional PRP evidence for reducing pain or curvature remains limited.<sup>[[50]](#ref50)</sup>

See [Peyronie's disease agents](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/peyronies-disease-agents) for the Dachille 2025 and Zugail 2024 PRP data alongside CCH, IFN, and verapamil.

## Vulvar lichen sclerosus

**PRP remains investigational for genital LS.** The controlled evidence should take precedence over satisfaction rates from uncontrolled series.

- **Goldstein 2019 placebo-controlled RCT:** 30 women randomized 2:1 to PRP or saline; 29 completed treatment. After two injections six weeks apart, histological inflammation improved in 5/19 PRP recipients and 5/10 controls, with no between-group difference (*p* = 0.542). Patient symptom scores also showed no advantage (*p* = 0.654). Bruising was the only reported adverse effect, but the small trial cannot establish uncommon-harm safety.<sup>[[35]](#ref35)</sup>
- **El Attar 2026 review:** one RCT alongside predominantly uncontrolled reports.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>
- **Boero 2024 and Medina Garrido 2023:** uncontrolled studies in refractory vulvar LS reported symptom improvement; the Boero pilot's 100% satisfaction result is not a comparative efficacy estimate.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)[[27]](#ref27)</sup>
- **Tedesco 2020:** exploratory disease-stage findings with adipose stromal vascular fraction plus PRP.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>
- **Sun 2025:** platelet-rich fibrin (PRF) mechanistic findings are not interchangeable with clinical PRP efficacy or evidence of cancer prevention.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup>

The German S3 guideline gives PRP in women and men with genital LS an open recommendation (no recommendation for or against), and a strong recommendation against PRP in girls and boys.<sup>[[36]](#ref36)</sup> See [clinical LS](/docs/clinical-conditions/03g-genital-scrotal/lichen-sclerosus#laser-and-prp-symptoms-are-not-the-same-as-disease-control) for the laser comparisons and [topical corticosteroids](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/dermatologic-topical-urethral/high-potency-topical-corticosteroids) for first-line treatment. Neither symptom improvement nor an autologous source removes the need for inflammation control and surveillance.

## Hypospadias repair — complication prevention

**Eryilmaz 2020** compared PRP graft use during primary mid-penile TIP repair in 40 children (20 per group) and reported lower early and long-term fistula, stenosis, and infection rates; its abstract gives no effect sizes, follow-up duration, or randomization method.<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup> In a 2026 RCT of 160 selected distal repairs with healthy wide urethral plates, adding a PRP layer to a dartos flap reduced fistulae at six months (3/80 versus 12/80) and wound infections (2/80 versus 10/80).<sup>[[47]](#ref47)</sup> A separate 2026 RCT of 50 distal/mid-penile repairs found 8/25 versus 12/25 fistulae at three months (p = 0.25); a 2026 meta-analysis of seven controlled studies (562 children; five RCTs) pooled different autologous platelet products, limiting PRP-specific inference.<sup>[[48]](#ref48)[[49]](#ref49)</sup>

## One cautionary preclinical signal — renal ischemia-reperfusion

**Martín-Solé 2016** compared subcapsular PRP with saline in 30 monorrenal rats during 45 minutes of renal ischemia. At day seven, renal-artery maximum velocity was lower with PRP (73.08 versus 87.1 cm/s; p = 0.045) and histologic injury was greater (p &lt; 0.0001); three of 15 PRP-treated rats died before day seven versus none of 15 controls (survival p = 0.073).<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup>

## Safety — limited detection of uncommon harms

| Domain | Signal |
|---|---|
| Reported AEs | Dysuria, hematuria, micturition pain, and UTI have been reported; small trials cannot define uncommon infection risk or show that every event resolves without treatment<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[20]](#ref20)[[21]](#ref21)</sup> |
| Serious AEs | Small cohorts and incomplete reporting cannot exclude uncommon or delayed harm |
| Infection / reactions | Autologous sourcing does not replace sterile processing; additives and procedure-related harms must be considered<sup>[[34]](#ref34)</sup> |
| Preparation standardization | Poor across studies — the main source of heterogeneity<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| Renal IR cautionary signal | Preclinical (Martín-Solé 2016): subcapsular PRP in a 30-rat renal ischemia–reperfusion study was associated with lower day-seven renal-artery maximum velocity and greater histologic injury versus saline<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup> |

## Clinical Positioning

- **Keep PRP within research-oriented care rather than a routine treatment step.** In ED, current EAU guidance explicitly limits intracavernosal PRP to clinical trials.<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup>
- **PRP bladder-wall injection has no established standard IC/BPS regimen.** Before/after improvements do not place it alongside guideline-supported DMSO, heparin or lidocaine instillation or in a numbered treatment sequence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Do not position PRP routinely before AUS, instead of a sling, or as a substitute for urethral reconstruction.** The cited SUI and PPI and stricture studies are too small or uncontrolled to establish those treatment sequences.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- **Peyronie and genital LS applications remain preliminary, and PRP in hypospadias repair remains investigational.**<sup>[[24]](#ref24)[[25]](#ref25)[[30]](#ref30)[[47]](#ref47)[[48]](#ref48)[[49]](#ref49)</sup>
- Record preparation, additives, dose, injection sites and outcomes in any protocol; counsel about uncertain benefit and procedure-related risks.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Autologous does not mean infection-proof.** CDC documented probable HIV transmission associated with unsafe PRP cosmetic procedures; this is not a urologic risk estimate, but demonstrates why handling and sterility matter.<sup>[[34]](#ref34)</sup>
- The renal ischemia-reperfusion harm signal is preclinical and does not justify empiric renal-parenchymal treatment.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup>
