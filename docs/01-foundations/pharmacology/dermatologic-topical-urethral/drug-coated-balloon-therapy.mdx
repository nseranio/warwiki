# Drug-Coated Balloon Therapy — Optilume

**Optilume® (Urotronic, Inc.)** is a **paclitaxel-coated drug-coated balloon (DCB)** — the **FDA-approved antimitotic device** for urethral stricture disease. It combines mechanical urethral dilation with localized paclitaxel delivery to inhibit fibrosis and reduce stricture recurrence. The US label covers symptomatic anterior urethral strictures ≤3 cm in adult men. The **AUA Urethral Stricture Disease Guideline Amendment 2023** permits dilation or DVIU combined with a DCB for **recurrent bulbar urethral strictures &lt;3 cm**, and the **EAU 2026 guideline** recommends it for short bulbar strictures that have recurred after at least two endoscopic treatments in men unfit for or declining urethroplasty; both selections are narrower than the FDA label.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[18]](#ref18)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

This article is the **device-level deep-dive**: mechanism and preclinical tissue pharmacology, the complete **ROBUST trial program** (I / III with 5-year and 3-year extensions), procedural technique, off-label use patterns from the Sugrue 2026 international survey, and device-specific safety including the Gao 2025 pudendal-artery case. For the **class-level comparison** against MMC, HA/CMC, PRP, and other adjuncts, see [Antimitotics & antifibrotics](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/dermatologic-topical-urethral/antimitotics-antifibrotics). For the clinical condition, see [Urethral stricture](/docs/clinical-conditions/03b-voiding-outlet/urethral-stricture).

## Device description and mechanism

The Optilume DCB is a urethral dilation balloon coated with **paclitaxel** — a microtubule-stabilizing taxane that produces **antiproliferative and antifibrotic effects** by preventing cell division, inhibiting fibroblast proliferation, and reducing collagen deposition.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### Preclinical tissue pharmacology

- **Will 2011** — in canine ureteral smooth-muscle cells (in vitro), paclitaxel 10 and 100 nM significantly inhibited proliferation and 100 nM reduced type III collagen production, **without cytotoxicity** at concentrations up to 100 nM<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Barbalias 2018** rabbit urethral model — paclitaxel distributes to **all urethral layers** (urothelial, submucosal, and smooth-muscle) immediately after DCB inflation; drug and mild inflammation persist at 24 and 48 hours<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Liourdi 2015** porcine ureteral model — paclitaxel penetrates through urothelium to submucosa and muscle by 12–24 hours, with **reduced inflammation** compared to conventional balloon dilation<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

The **dual mechanism** — immediate mechanical dilation for symptom relief combined with sustained local drug delivery to prevent fibrotic recurrence — is what distinguishes the DCB from plain balloon dilation or DVIU.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Available sizes

The US catheter is a 0.038-in over-the-wire balloon in **18, 24, 30 and 36 Fr** (6–12 mm) diameters, each in 30 mm and 50 mm working lengths. Rated burst pressure is 12 atm for 18 and 24 Fr, 10 atm for 30 Fr and 8 atm for 36 Fr. For bulbar strictures the IFU advises a diameter slightly larger than the distal healthy urethra, not more than 30% oversized, with a working length that covers the whole stricture; most bulbar ROBUST III cases used 30 Fr. The 36 Fr balloon does not pass through a flexible cystoscope and is placed side by side with the wire. In ROBUST I, 5-year functional success was higher with the 30 Fr than the 24 Fr balloon (77% vs 38%, as summarized by the EAU), and the 24 Fr/3 cm balloon was associated with more recurrence in real-world data (see below).<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[19]](#ref19)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## ROBUST I — single-arm long-term durability

| Timepoint | n evaluable | Functional success | Freedom from reintervention | IPSS (baseline → f/u) | Qmax change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 y | 46 | 70% | — | 25.2 → 6.9 (p &lt; 0.0001) | Significant ↑<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| 3 y | 43 | 67% | 77% | 25.2 → 5.5 (p &lt; 0.0001) | Significant ↑<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| **5 y** | 43 in functional-success analysis; 31 completed follow-up | **58%** | **71.7% (KM)** | 25.2 → 7.2 (p &lt; 0.001) | **5.0 → 19.9 mL/s** (p &lt; 0.01)<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |

**Cohort profile:** men with recurrent bulbar strictures ≤2 cm (mean 1.7 prior dilations; 43% with &gt;1 prior dilation). At 5 years, Optilume maintained **71.7% freedom from repeat intervention**. **Mean erectile-function scores did not deteriorate** and **no serious treatment-related AEs** were observed across the entire 5-year study period.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## ROBUST III — pivotal multicenter RCT

The pivotal multicenter single-blind RCT that supported FDA approval.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

### Design

- **127 patients** at 22 sites; recurrent anterior strictures ≤3 cm, ≤12F, ≥2 prior endoscopic treatments, IPSS ≥11, Qmax &lt;15 mL/s (average 3.6 prior treatments and 1.7 cm length)
- Randomized to Optilume DCB vs standard endoscopic treatment (DVIU or dilation)

### Outcomes

| Endpoint | Optilume DCB | Control | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Anatomical success at 6 mo (≥14F)** | **75%** | 27% | &lt;0.001 |
| **Freedom from reintervention at 1 y** | **83.2%** | 21.7% | &lt;0.001 |
| **Freedom from reintervention at 3 y** | **71%** | — | — |

### 3-year extension (Srikanth 2025)<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

- DCB arm maintained **71% freedom from reintervention** at 3 years. The extension does not provide a contemporaneous randomized three-year control comparison.
- Clinically significant subgroups (≥5 prior dilations, strictures ≥2 cm) showed no statistically detected differences; these exploratory subgroups do not establish equivalent efficacy
- Crossover cohort (control patients who failed standard treatment and opted for DCB) produced a Kaplan-Meier curve **closely resembling the original DCB cohort**; the crossover analysis is not a new randomized comparison

## AUA guideline positioning

**AUA 2023 Statement 11b** allows dilation or DVIU combined with a drug-coated balloon for recurrent bulbar strictures &lt;3 cm (conditional recommendation; evidence grade B). The panel restricted the recommendation to recurrent bulbar disease because ROBUST III was not powered for the penile subgroup (about 10% of participants), and it does not recommend repeat DCB use because efficacy of repeat treatment has not been established.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**EAU 2026** gives a strong recommendation to offer a DCB for a short (&lt;3 cm) bulbar stricture recurring after at least two endoscopic treatments in patients unfit for or unwilling to undergo urethroplasty. Use beyond that population (penile, membranous, bladder-neck, VUAS, longer, treatment-naive or post-urethroplasty strictures) is described as too limited for a recommendation and is advised within a trial or registry.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

**FDA labeling is a separate question:** the US indication is symptomatic anterior strictures ≤3 cm in adult men, without a recurrent-only or bulbar-only restriction. Penile or primary anterior treatment meeting those criteria is within the anatomical label but has less established evidence than recurrent bulbar disease. Posterior/BNC/VUAS treatment is off-label.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

## Procedural technique

Use the current regional manufacturer IFU for the exact catheter. Trial protocols and surveys describe practice; they do not supersede the device instructions.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| Anesthesia | General (58% in the Sugrue 2026 survey) or local / sedation |
| Cystoscopy | Confirm stricture location and length |
| **Pre-dilation** | Uncoated balloon or sequential dilators; all Optilume patients in ROBUST III were pre-dilated, and published series commonly dilate to ~20F (the IFU does not set a size) |
| **Optilume inflation** | Choose balloon diameter/length for the stricture and adjacent urethra (sizing above); leave the balloon uninflated across the stricture for ≥60 seconds to hydrate the coating, then inflate to the rated burst pressure for ≥5 minutes without exceeding it. Deflate fully before withdrawal; do not manipulate it inflated |
| **Post-procedure catheter** | **12–14F Foley for at least 2 days**, or longer standard of care per US IFU |
| **Follow-up** | Uroflowmetry, IPSS, cystoscopy per institutional protocol |

### Practice-variation signal — Sugrue 2026

International survey of **102 Optilume users** revealed significant practice variation in catheter duration, perioperative antibiotic use, and postprocedure contraception advice — reflecting the early-adoption phase of this technology.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

## Use beyond the best-supported trial population

The Sugrue 2026 survey (n = 102) documents use beyond the AUA guideline population. The AUA issues recommendations, not regulatory approvals; the label distinction below follows the US IFU:<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

| Use pattern | % of surveyed urologists |
|---|---|
| **Penile urethral strictures** (anterior label if other criteria met; limited subgroup evidence) | 65% |
| **Primary / first-line treatment** (not excluded by anterior label; evidence gap) | 64% |
| **Bladder-neck stenoses** (off-label) | 65% |

Higher-volume users (≥10 / y) were more likely to perform DCB under flexible cystoscopy (OR 5.14), for bladder-neck strictures (OR 4.66), and for recurrences (OR 6.92).<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

### Posterior urethral stenosis (BNC / VUAS)

**Berg 2025** retrospective comparative study (n = 141) — DCB dilation significantly improved **recurrence-free survival** vs standard endoscopic treatment (p = 0.013); treatment modality confirmed as significant predictor on multivariate analysis (HR 0.40; 95% CI 0.19–0.87; p = 0.021). Qmax improved from 9 → 22 mL/s (p = 0.001).<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

### Membranous / sphincteric strictures

**Jelisejevas 2025** retrospective series (n = 53 — 35 membranous strictures, 18 VUAS extending into the sphincter; median 2.5 prior interventions, median length 3 cm; dilation to 20 Fr, then a 30 Fr DCB at 10 bar for 10 minutes) — **66.6% (membranous) and 65.6% (VUAS) were free from recurrence and reintervention** at median 13.3-month follow-up, with **no de novo incontinence**, an important finding given the proximity to the external urethral sphincter. Two men with pre-existing post-prostatectomy incontinence remained incontinent.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

### Penile urethral strictures

**Salem 2025** small multicenter series (n = 8) — median IPSS **25.5 → 5.0** (p &lt; 0.01); very small uncontrolled series, insufficient to establish routine penile-stricture efficacy.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

### Real-world 319-patient TURNS experience

**Patel 2026** (260 anterior strictures, 59 posterior stenoses; median follow-up only 5.7 months) — 1-year functional recurrence-free survival **78.4%** for anterior strictures and **75.8%** for posterior stenoses. Independent predictors of recurrence of any kind:<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- **Strictures after hypospadias repair** — HR **5.21** (95% CI 1.06–25.6; p = 0.042)
- **24F/3 cm DCB use** — HR **3.64** (95% CI 1.10–12.09; p = 0.035)

Anatomical recurrence-free survival was lower than functional (66.4% anterior / 59.4% posterior) — some patients tolerate mild anatomical recurrence without functional impairment.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## FIRST-CARE — the pending first-line RCT

**FIRST-CARE** (NCT06827210) — multicenter, single-blind RCT enrolling **140 treatment-naïve patients** with single bulbar urethral strictures ≤3 cm to evaluate Optilume DCB as **first-line treatment vs standard endoscopic management**. Primary endpoint: freedom from repeat intervention at 12 months. This trial directly addresses the first-line-use evidence gap; the protocol does not yet establish a first-line benefit.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

## Safety profile

| Concern | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Common AEs** | UTI, post-procedural hematuria, dysuria — generally mild and self-limited; hematuria and dysuria were more frequent than with standard treatment in ROBUST III (about 11% vs 2%)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Serious treatment-related AEs** | **None** reported in ROBUST I through 5 years; rare in ROBUST III<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Erectile function** | No deterioration detected in measured group scores through reported follow-up; not a guarantee for an individual<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Continence** | **No de novo incontinence** in the Jelisejevas 2025 sphincteric-stricture series<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Rare vascular injury** | One case report (Gao 2025) of recurrent massive urethral bleeding requiring **selective embolization of the internal pudendal artery** after Optilume use in a post-urethroplasty recurrence<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| **Systemic paclitaxel concerns** | Paclitaxel is delivered locally, and no serious treatment-related adverse events were reported in ROBUST I through 5 years. It is a genotoxic aneugen and was detectable in semen in 60% of men at 1 month, 39% at 3 months and 8% at 6 months (IFU); the effect on sperm and spermatogenesis is unknown<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| **Contraception — required counseling** | Abstain from sex or use condoms for 30 days. Men with partners of childbearing potential should use highly effective contraception and avoid fathering a child for at least 6 months; paclitaxel can persist in semen.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |
| **Contraindications / infection** | US contraindications include paclitaxel-related hypersensitivity and urologic implants such as penile implants or AUS. Resolve active UTI before treatment.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |

## Limitations and unanswered questions

Several key evidence gaps remain:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

- **Durability in complex or longer strictures (&gt;3 cm)** is uncertain
- **Repeated DCB use** has not been validated; the AUA does not recommend it and the IFU notes that repeat treatment of the same stricture increases paclitaxel exposure with unknown risk
- **Post-urethroplasty recurrence** — the role of DCB as salvage therapy requires further investigation (the Gao 2025 bleeding case occurred in this setting)
- **Penile urethral strictures** — the ROBUST III trial was not powered for this subgroup; the anterior label does not establish efficacy for this subgroup
- **First-line use** — awaiting FIRST-CARE results
- **Cost-effectiveness** — described as "cost-effective" in reviews, but formal health-economic comparisons vs urethroplasty and repeated DVIU are limited
- **Long-term oncologic safety** — theoretical concern regarding paclitaxel's effects on urethral tissue over decades; no malignant transformation observed to date

## Clinical Positioning

- **Use the recurrent bulbar evidence to guide selection**, and explain alternatives including urethroplasty. Regulatory anatomy and guideline selection are distinct, as detailed above.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **ROBUST III supports benefit over repeat standard endoscopy** in its selected recurrent-stricture population. The three-year extension and ROBUST I five-year single-arm results support durability but do not compare DCB with urethroplasty.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Do not choose a smaller balloon to avoid the 24F association.** Observational device-size associations may reflect anatomy and selection; use the IFU sizing method. Hypospadias-associated recurrence estimates are imprecise and warrant individualized counseling.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **Posterior/BNC/VUAS use remains off-label with mainly retrospective evidence.** Absence of new incontinence in a small series does not establish safety equivalence or a routine alternative to reconstruction.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Repeat DCB and routine first-line treatment remain evidence gaps.** FIRST-CARE is a protocol, not a positive trial result.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- Follow the device precautions and contraception intervals above. Discuss bleeding, infection and recurrence; the isolated vascular-injury report cannot estimate their incidence.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
