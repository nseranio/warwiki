# HPV / Condyloma Topical Agents

Genital and perianal **condylomata acuminata** (anogenital warts) are caused by low-risk HPV genotypes — predominantly HPV 6 and 11. Reconstructive urologists and urogynecologists encounter them as incidental findings during preoperative genital examination, in the context of immunosuppression (transplant, HIV, biologics), and as comorbid lesions in patients undergoing penile, vulvar, or perianal reconstruction. Patient-applied and provider-administered treatments are both initial options; choose by site, burden and preference. No single treatment is superior for all patients.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> For PeIN / VIN / SCC see the [glans resurfacing](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/glans-resurfacing) and [vulvar primary closure](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar-primary-closure) pages.

This page addresses benign warts. Suspicious lesions require diagnostic assessment; wart treatments should not be assumed appropriate for PeIN, VIN or cancer.

## Agent Overview

| Agent | Class | Mechanism | FDA status | Patient- or clinician-applied |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Imiquimod 5% / 3.75%** | Toll-like receptor 7 agonist (immunomodulator) | Induces local IFN-α, TNF-α, IL-6, IL-8 → CD8 T-cell viral clearance | FDA-approved (Aldara 5%: initial US approval 1997; Zyclara 3.75%: external-genital-wart indication added 2011)<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup> | Patient-applied |
| **Sinecatechins 15%** (Veregen) | Green-tea catechin extract (epigallocatechin gallate dominant) | Clinical wart-clearance mechanism unknown; in vitro antioxidant activity has uncertain clinical significance<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | FDA-approved (2006) | Patient-applied |
| **Podofilox 0.5% (US)** | Plant-derived antimitotic | Microtubule polymerization arrest → keratinocyte necrosis | US Condylox 0.5% gel; other regional products differ<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | Patient-applied |
| **Podophyllin resin 10–25%** | Crude podophyllum extract | Antimitotic (variable concentration) | Clinician-applied (in-office)<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | Clinician-applied |

| **5-Fluorouracil 5%** | Pyrimidine antimetabolite | Thymidylate synthase inhibition | Off-label for condylomata; FDA-approved for actinic keratosis / superficial BCC<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | Clinician- or patient-applied |
| **Intralesional interferon-α** | Recombinant cytokine | Antiviral / immunomodulatory | Historically FDA-approved as INTRON A for selected adults with external genital/perianal condylomata; CDC lists intralesional interferon as an alternative regimen<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | Clinician-applied |

## Imiquimod (Aldara / Zyclara)

**Mechanism**: TLR7 agonist activating dendritic and plasmacytoid cells, leading to a local type I interferon and cytokine response and then cytotoxic-T-cell-mediated HPV clearance. Does not directly destroy keratinocytes; efficacy depends on a functional local immune response.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

**Regimens**:

- **5% cream**: applied 3 nights per week (eg Mon / Wed / Fri) for up to 16 weeks; left on 6–10 hours, then washed off.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **3.75% cream**: applied nightly for up to 8 weeks, then washed off after about 8 hours.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

**Efficacy**:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- 5% cream three times weekly for up to 16 weeks cleared all baseline warts in 50% versus 11% with vehicle (Edwards RCT, n = 311); 13% of cleared patients had a recurrence within 12 weeks.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Cochrane review: superior to placebo for complete clearance (RR 4.0), with very low-quality evidence; effects versus podophyllotoxin and versus provider-applied ablation were imprecise.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- CDC 2021 finds no treatment clearly superior; warts commonly recur, especially in the first 3 months.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

**Adverse effects**: local erythema, erosion, burning, pruritus (very common; counsel patients in advance); pigmentary change in dark skin; rare systemic flu-like reaction.

**Reconstructive-urology relevance:** coordinate lesion treatment and wound healing before elective genital reconstruction; do not apply wart medication to a fresh operative wound without a specific specialist plan.

## Sinecatechins (Veregen 15%)

**Mechanism**: Veregen is a green-tea catechin extract dominated by epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> Its mode of wart clearance is unknown; in vitro antioxidant activity has unknown clinical significance.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

**Regimen**: 0.5 cm ribbon to each wart three times daily until clearance, up to 16 weeks.

**Efficacy**:<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

- Complete clearance 57.2% versus 33.7% vehicle with the 15% ointment over up to 16 weeks (Tatti RCT, n = 502); among patients with complete clearance in the 15% group, recurrence of any wart was 6.5% during the following 12 weeks.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- Sinecatechins and imiquimod were assessed in separate trials; cross-trial clearance and recurrence percentages do not establish superiority over imiquimod.

**Adverse effects**: local erythema and burning are common; comparative tolerability depends on regimen and patient. Veregen is a standardized sinecatechins ointment evaluated in randomized trials; generic green-tea products should not be assumed equivalent.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

**Practice notes:** US labeling covers immunocompetent adults ≥18. Avoid sexual contact while ointment is present; it may weaken condoms or diaphragms. Do not use on internal mucosa, open wounds or in immunocompromised patients without recognizing that safety and effectiveness are unestablished.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Podofilox / podophyllotoxin (US Condylox 0.5%)

**Mechanism**: pure podophyllotoxin (the active component of podophyllin resin) that binds tubulin, arrests mitosis at metaphase, induces keratinocyte necrosis.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

**Regimen**: applied to each wart twice daily for 3 consecutive days, then 4-day off period; repeat for up to 4 cycles. For US 0.5% gel, limit to ≤10 cm² of wart tissue and ≤0.5 g/day; reassess if no improvement after four weeks.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

**Efficacy**: The US Condylox 0.5% gel label reports complete patient-level wart clearance at four weeks in 38.4% and 25.6% of evaluable gel-treated patients in two trials. The first enrolled 326 patients and reported efficacy data for 260, including 176 given gel; the second reported 108 evaluable patients across gel and solution groups.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> In a separate randomized 0.5% podofilox trial, recurrence affected 34% of previously resolved warts, a wart-level endpoint.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

**Advantages**: patient-applied, predictable mechanism, low cost.

**Adverse effects**: local erosion, burning, pain (common and dose-dependent). Avoid in pregnancy (CDC 2021); the label permits use only if the benefit justifies the fetal risk.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Podophyllin Resin 10–25% (Clinician-Applied)

Crude alcohol extract of *Podophyllum peltatum* containing variable concentrations of podophyllotoxin and other lignans. CDC no longer recommends it routinely because of systemic-toxicity concerns. If a clinician uses it, limit each session to &lt;0.5 mL or &lt;10 cm² of warts, avoid open or friable tissue, allow it to air-dry, and wash it off after 1–4 hours.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- **Largely supplanted** by purified podophyllotoxin (Condylox); podophyllin resin has variable potency and can be systemically absorbed with large-area application; it should not be used in pregnancy.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Still used in some international settings where Condylox is unavailable.

## Trichloroacetic Acid (TCA) 80–90%

Caustic acid applied in-office by clinician; chemically coagulates protein in wart tissue.

- **Useful for small numbers of lesions, pregnancy** (no teratogenicity), and selected vaginal, cervical, or intra-anal sites under appropriate specialist care. Exophytic cervical warts require biopsy evaluation to exclude HSIL before treatment.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- TCA can be repeated weekly if needed; BASHH 2024 notes that multiple sessions are usually required for clearance.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- Adverse effects: localized burning, ulceration if over-applied. Neutralize excess acid with sodium bicarbonate, liquid soap or talc.

## 5-Fluorouracil 5% Cream (Off-Label for Condylomata)

**Mechanism**: thymidylate synthase inhibition leading to DNA replication arrest in proliferating keratinocytes (the same mechanism that makes 5-FU useful for PeIN, VIN, actinic keratosis, and intraurethral condylomata).

- Intraurethral 5-FU is a specialist off-label approach with limited evidence; it is not a standard CDC regimen. The US Mylan 5% cream label advises against mucosal application because of inflammation and ulceration and says the cream should not be used in DPD deficiency.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- Topical 5-FU is not a CDC-recommended regimen for external condylomata and can cause local reactions; specialist oncologic use follows diagnosis-specific guidance rather than this benign-wart framework.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- Pregnancy contraindication.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

## Special Situations

### Pregnancy

- Clinician-administered TCA, cryotherapy or surgical removal may be considered with obstetric assessment.
- Avoid podofilox, podophyllin and sinecatechins in pregnancy; imiquimod should also be avoided pending more data. This differs from a formal imiquimod contraindication. 5-FU is contraindicated in pregnancy.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup><sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

### Immunosuppression (Transplant, HIV)

- Clearance rates reduced, recurrence rates higher across all topical agents.
- Biopsy suspicious lesions. HIV does not itself mandate a different treatment algorithm; combination-treatment evidence is limited.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Cidofovir** topical (off-label) has been used for refractory immunocompromised condyloma but is not first-line. The only FDA-labeled indication for cidofovir is intravenous treatment of CMV retinitis in patients with AIDS; the label carries a boxed warning for renal impairment, neutropenia and animal carcinogenicity, teratogenicity and hypospermia. Topical HPV use is a compounded, off-label preparation.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

### Intraurethral &amp; Meatal Lesions

- External-skin agents (imiquimod cream, sinecatechins, podophyllotoxin) should not be applied inside the urethra.
- CDC recommends cryotherapy or surgical removal for urethral-meatal warts.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- For deep intraurethral disease, cystoscopic laser fulguration is one specialist option. BASHH 2024 recommends surgical excision, laser, or electrosurgery for urethral warts (1A) and suggests cryotherapy for distal lesions whose base is clearly visible on meatal eversion (2C).<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- Examine the urethral meatus in patients with genital warts using good light; meatal eversion and a meatoscope or otoscope can clarify lesion extent.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> Urethrocystoscopy may be used when intraurethral or bladder warts are suspected, but high-level evidence for invasive localization is lacking.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> Glans involvement alone does not establish a need for cystoscopy.
- Surgical smoke from electrosurgical and laser fulguration devices, including this cystoscopic laser fulguration, can contain viral material and is discussed as a potential transmission risk for HPV, HIV, and hepatitis B; it also contains carcinogenic chemicals and particulate matter, and standard surgical masks alone do not adequately filter it. Because HPV is the organism being ablated in intraurethral condyloma fulguration, smoke evacuation at the source and adequate mask filtration are a procedure-specific occupational-safety measure, not only a generic operating-room practice.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

### HPV Vaccination as Prevention

- **9-valent HPV vaccine (Gardasil 9)** covers HPV 6 and 11 (about 90% of anogenital warts) plus seven oncogenic types (16, 18, 31, 33, 45, 52, 58).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- ACIP recommends routine catch-up vaccination through age 26 and shared clinical decision-making for ages 27–45.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- Vaccination prevents infection with HPV types to which a person has not yet been exposed; it does not treat existing warts, and trials have not demonstrated a reduction in recurrence after wart treatment.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

## Agent Selection Summary

| Scenario | Preferred topical |
|---|---|
| Few external lesions, immunocompetent | Imiquimod, podofilox 0.5% (within the gel limits of ≤10 cm² and ≤0.5 g/day), or sinecatechins; choose by site and preference.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Many / refractory external lesions | Reassess diagnosis and response; for large, extensive, or recalcitrant lesions, consider cryotherapy, excision, electrosurgery, laser, or TCA according to site and burden.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| Pregnancy | **TCA** or surgical removal |
| Meatal lesions | Cryotherapy or surgical removal |
| Intraurethral lesions | Specialist endoscopic assessment / ablation; drug instillation is not routine |
| Immunosuppressed patient | Site-directed treatment, biopsy suspicious lesions; avoid assuming sinecatechins suitability |
| Preoperative clearance before genital reconstruction | Imiquimod or excisional removal; coordinate timing with planned operation |
