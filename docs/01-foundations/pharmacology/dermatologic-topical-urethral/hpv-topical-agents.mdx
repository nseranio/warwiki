# HPV / Condyloma Topical Agents

Genital and perianal **condylomata acuminata** (anogenital warts) are caused by low-risk HPV genotypes — predominantly HPV 6 and 11. Reconstructive urologists and urogynecologists encounter them as incidental findings during preoperative genital examination, in the context of immunosuppression (transplant, HIV, biologics), and as comorbid lesions in patients undergoing penile, vulvar, or perianal reconstruction. Patient-applied and provider-administered treatments are initial options selected by site, burden and preference; CDC 2021 also considers observation acceptable for selected patients because warts may resolve spontaneously within a year.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> No single treatment is superior for all patients.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> For PeIN / VIN / SCC see the [glans resurfacing](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/glans-resurfacing) and [vulvar primary closure](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar-primary-closure) pages.

This page addresses benign warts. Evaluate for PeIN, VIN or cancer before treating a suspicious lesion as a wart: biopsy lesions that are pigmented, indurated, fixed, bleeding or ulcerated, and consider biopsy when diagnosis is uncertain or lesions fail to respond or worsen during treatment, particularly with immunocompromise.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Imiquimod (Aldara / Zyclara)

**Mechanism**: TLR7 agonist activating dendritic and plasmacytoid cells, leading to a local type I interferon and cytokine response and then cytotoxic-T-cell-mediated HPV clearance. Does not directly destroy keratinocytes; efficacy depends on a functional local immune response.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

**Regimens**:

- **5% cream**: applied 3 nights per week (eg Mon / Wed / Fri) for up to 16 weeks; left on 6–10 hours, then washed off.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> BASHH 2024 suggests considering another treatment if wart volume falls by &lt;50% after 8 weeks (2B).<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **3.75% cream**: applied nightly for up to 8 weeks, then washed off after about 8 hours.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

**Efficacy**:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- 5% cream three times weekly for up to 16 weeks cleared all baseline warts in 50% versus 11% with vehicle (Edwards RCT, n = 311); 13% of cleared patients had a recurrence within 12 weeks.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Cochrane review: superior to placebo for complete clearance (RR 4.0), with very low-quality evidence; effects versus podophyllotoxin and versus provider-applied ablation were imprecise.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- CDC 2021 finds no treatment clearly superior; treatment removes visible warts without curing HPV infection, and recurrence is common, especially during the first 3 months.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

**Adverse effects**: Counsel patients about common local erythema, erosion, burning and pruritus. Severe vulvar swelling can cause urinary retention and warrants interrupting or stopping treatment; pigmentary change and flu-like systemic symptoms may occur.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> BASHH 2024 advises caution in solid organ transplant recipients and patients with autoimmune conditions because imiquimod can exacerbate inflammatory skin disease.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

**Reconstructive-urology relevance:** coordinate lesion treatment and wound healing before elective genital reconstruction; do not apply wart medication to a fresh operative wound without a specific specialist plan. Imiquimod cream may weaken condoms or vaginal diaphragms while present.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Sinecatechins (Veregen 15%)

**Mechanism**: Veregen is a green-tea catechin extract dominated by epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> Its mode of wart clearance is unknown; in vitro antioxidant activity has unknown clinical significance.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

**Regimen**: 0.5 cm ribbon to each wart three times daily until clearance, up to 16 weeks. For the UK 10% sinecatechins formulation, BASHH 2024 suggests considering another treatment if wart volume falls by &lt;50% after 8 weeks (2B).<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

**Efficacy**:<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

- In Tatti’s 502-adult RCT, 15% sinecatechins cleared all baseline and newly occurring external warts in 57.2% versus 33.7% with vehicle after up to 16 weeks; 6.5% of complete responders in the 15% group had a wart recur during the following 12 weeks.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- Sinecatechins and imiquimod were assessed in separate trials.

**Adverse effects**: local erythema and burning are common; comparative tolerability depends on regimen and patient. Veregen is a standardized sinecatechins ointment evaluated in randomized trials; generic green-tea products should not be assumed equivalent.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

**Practice notes:** US labeling covers immunocompetent adults ≥18. Avoid sexual contact while ointment is present; it may weaken condoms or diaphragms. Do not apply to internal mucosa or open wounds. CDC does not recommend sinecatechins for patients with HIV, other immunocompromising conditions or genital herpes because safety and efficacy have not been evaluated.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Podofilox / podophyllotoxin (US Condylox 0.5%)

**Mechanism**: pure podophyllotoxin (the active component of podophyllin resin) that binds tubulin, arrests mitosis at metaphase, induces keratinocyte necrosis.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

**Regimen**: applied to each wart twice daily for 3 consecutive days, then 4-day off period; repeat for up to 4 cycles. For US 0.5% gel, limit to ≤10 cm² of wart tissue and ≤0.5 g/day; reassess if no improvement after four weeks.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

**Efficacy**: The US Condylox 0.5% gel label reports complete patient-level wart clearance at four weeks in 38.4% and 25.6% of evaluable gel-treated patients in two trials. The first enrolled 326 patients and reported efficacy data for 260, including 176 given gel; the second reported 108 evaluable patients across gel and solution groups.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> In a separate randomized 0.5% podofilox trial, recurrence affected 34% of previously resolved warts, a wart-level endpoint.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

**Advantages**: patient-applied, predictable mechanism, low cost.

**Adverse effects**: local erosion, burning, pain (common and dose-dependent). Avoid in pregnancy (CDC 2021); the label permits use only if the benefit justifies the fetal risk.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Podophyllin Resin 10–25% (Clinician-Applied)

Crude alcohol extract of *Podophyllum peltatum* containing variable concentrations of podophyllotoxin and other lignans. CDC no longer recommends it routinely because of systemic-toxicity concerns. If a clinician uses it, limit each session to &lt;0.5 mL or &lt;10 cm² of warts, avoid open or friable tissue, allow it to air-dry, and wash it off after 1–4 hours.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- **Largely supplanted** by purified podophyllotoxin (Condylox); podophyllin resin has variable potency and can be systemically absorbed with large-area application; it should not be used in pregnancy.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Still used in some international settings where Condylox is unavailable.

## Trichloroacetic Acid (TCA) 80–90%

Caustic acid applied in-office by clinician; chemically coagulates protein in wart tissue.

- **Useful for small numbers of lesions** and selected vaginal, cervical, or intra-anal sites under appropriate specialist care. Exophytic cervical warts require biopsy evaluation to exclude HSIL before treatment.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Patients with external anal or perianal warts may also have intra-anal lesions; CDC notes that digital examination or anoscopy may help assess the anal canal.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> In pregnancy, UK BASHH 2024 permits clinician-applied TCA (1C) but notes that supporting clinical studies are lacking; WHO 2024 lists TCA as contraindicated and favors cryotherapy when treatment is needed.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- TCA can be repeated weekly if needed; BASHH 2024 notes that multiple sessions are usually required and suggests considering another treatment if wart clearance is &lt;50% after four applications (1C).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- Adverse effects: localized burning, ulceration if over-applied. Neutralize excess acid with sodium bicarbonate, liquid soap or talc.

## 5-Fluorouracil 5% Cream (Off-Label for Condylomata)

**Mechanism**: thymidylate synthase inhibition leading to DNA replication arrest in proliferating keratinocytes (the same mechanism that makes 5-FU useful for PeIN, VIN, actinic keratosis, and intraurethral condylomata).

- Intraurethral 5-FU is an off-label specialist approach described in a case report of one man with refractory meatal intraurethral warts; CDC recommends cryotherapy or surgical removal for urethral-meatal warts.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The US Mylan 5% cream label advises against mucosal application because of inflammation and ulceration and says the cream should not be used in DPD deficiency.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- Topical 5-FU is not a CDC-recommended regimen for external condylomata and can cause local reactions; specialist oncologic use follows diagnosis-specific guidance rather than this benign-wart framework.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- The US 5% cream label contraindicates use in women who are or may become pregnant during treatment.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> BASHH 2024 advises against use while attempting to father a child and advises effective contraception for women of childbearing potential during treatment and for 7 months afterward.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

## Special Situations

### Pregnancy

- For uncomplicated external warts in pregnancy, treatment can be deferred when acceptable; when removal is indicated, consider cryotherapy or surgical removal with obstetric assessment. UK BASHH 2024 also permits clinician-applied TCA (1C), whereas WHO 2024 lists it as contraindicated and identifies cryotherapy as the safest option when treatment is necessary.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[14]](#ref14)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- Avoid podofilox, podophyllin and sinecatechins in pregnancy; imiquimod should also be avoided pending more data. This differs from a formal imiquimod contraindication. 5-FU is contraindicated in pregnancy.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup><sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- During breastfeeding, BASHH 2024 permits cryotherapy, laser, electrosurgery, excision or TCA (1C); no self-applied topical wart agent is licensed for this indication in the UK.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

### Immunosuppression (Transplant, HIV)

- In patients with HIV or other immunosuppression, warts may respond less well to treatment and recur more often.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Biopsy suspicious lesions. HIV does not itself mandate a different treatment algorithm; combination-treatment evidence is limited.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Cidofovir** topical (off-label) has been used for refractory immunocompromised condyloma but is not first-line. The only FDA-labeled indication for cidofovir is intravenous treatment of CMV retinitis in patients with AIDS; the label carries a boxed warning for renal impairment, neutropenia and animal carcinogenicity, teratogenicity and hypospermia. Topical HPV use is a compounded, off-label preparation.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

### Intraurethral &amp; Meatal Lesions

- External-skin agents (imiquimod cream, sinecatechins, podophyllotoxin) should not be applied inside the urethra.
- CDC recommends cryotherapy or surgical removal for urethral-meatal warts.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- For deep intraurethral disease, cystoscopic laser fulguration is one specialist option. Urethral condyloma treatment carries a stricture risk, and the urethra should be reassessed for recurrence at 3–4 months.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> BASHH 2024 recommends surgical excision, laser, or electrosurgery for urethral warts (1A) and suggests cryotherapy for distal lesions whose base is clearly visible on meatal eversion (2C).<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- Examine the urethral meatus in patients with genital warts using good light; meatal eversion and a meatoscope or otoscope can clarify lesion extent.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> Urethrocystoscopy may be used when intraurethral or bladder warts are suspected, but high-level evidence for invasive localization is lacking.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> Glans involvement alone does not establish a need for cystoscopy.
- Surgical smoke from electrosurgical and laser procedures can contain cellular material and particulates, poses potential exposure to HPV, HIV and hepatitis B, and has shown cytotoxic, genotoxic and mutagenic effects; standard surgical masks alone do not adequately filter it.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> A smoke evacuator near the source is an occupational safety measure.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> For laser or electrosurgical ablation of anogenital warts, BASHH 2024 recommends exhaust ventilation and PPE including an N95 or FFP2/3 particulate respirator (1A).<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

### HPV Vaccination as Prevention

- **9-valent HPV vaccine (Gardasil 9)** covers HPV 6 and 11 (about 90% of anogenital warts) plus seven oncogenic types (16, 18, 31, 33, 45, 52, 58).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- ACIP recommends routine catch-up vaccination through age 26 and shared clinical decision-making for ages 27–45.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- Vaccination prevents infection with HPV types to which a person has not yet been exposed; it does not treat existing warts, and trials have not demonstrated a reduction in recurrence after wart treatment.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

## Agent Selection Summary

| Scenario | Preferred topical |
|---|---|
| Few external lesions, immunocompetent | Imiquimod, podofilox 0.5% (within the gel limits of ≤10 cm² and ≤0.5 g/day), or sinecatechins; choose by site and preference.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Many / refractory external lesions | Reassess diagnosis and response; choose treatment by site and burden. Cryotherapy and TCA remain options, while CDC notes that large or extensive warts may benefit most from surgical therapy; BASHH 2024 recommends prioritizing laser, electrosurgery or excision for recalcitrant or persistently recurrent warts (1A).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| Pregnancy | If treatment is indicated, cryotherapy or surgical removal with obstetric assessment; TCA is permitted by UK BASHH (1C) but contraindicated by WHO.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |
| Meatal lesions | Cryotherapy or surgical removal |
| Intraurethral lesions | Specialist endoscopic assessment / ablation; drug instillation is not routine |
| Immunosuppressed patient | Site-directed treatment, biopsy suspicious lesions; avoid assuming sinecatechins suitability |
| Preoperative clearance before genital reconstruction | Imiquimod or excisional removal; coordinate timing with planned operation |
