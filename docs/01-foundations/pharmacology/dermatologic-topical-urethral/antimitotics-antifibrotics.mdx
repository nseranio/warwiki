# Antimitotics and Antifibrotics

**Antimitotic and antifibrotic adjuncts** to endoscopic urethral stricture management target the pathologic wound-healing cascade of fibroblast proliferation, collagen deposition, and spongiofibrosis that drives recurrence after internal urethrotomy (IU) and dilation. **Mitomycin C (MMC)** has a favorable pooled recurrence signal. **Paclitaxel-coated balloon catheters (Optilume)** have FDA approval for adult men with obstructive urinary symptoms associated with anterior urethral strictures ≤3 cm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[37]](#ref37)</sup>

This article is the hub for MMC, paclitaxel DCBs, and related antimitotic / antifibrotic adjuncts (HA/CMC, PRP, tamoxifen, and preclinical agents). For intralesional corticosteroid adjuncts (triamcinolone), which are mechanistically distinct and covered separately, see [Intralesional corticosteroids](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/dermatologic-topical-urethral/intralesional-corticosteroids). For PRP-specific detail beyond this article, see [Platelet-rich plasma](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/dermatologic-topical-urethral/platelet-rich-plasma). For the clinical condition, see [Urethral stricture](/docs/clinical-conditions/03b-voiding-outlet/urethral-stricture).

## Why adjuncts are needed

Internal urethrotomy and dilation are commonly performed, but outcomes depend on stricture selection. A 2016 review reported 0–9% long-term success across endoscopic series, including 9% after initial DVIU at 1–3 years.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> EAU 2026 reports up to 77% five-year stricture-free survival for selected primary, single, short (&lt;2 cm), nonobliterative bulbar strictures and weakly recommends DVIU or dilation for that group.<sup>[[36]](#ref36)</sup> Recurrence is driven by pathologic wound healing: excessive fibroblast proliferation, collagen deposition, and spongiofibrosis.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The AUA Urethral Stricture Disease Guideline Amendment 2023 states that clinicians may endoscopically inject pharmacologic agents at the time of direct visual internal urethrotomy (DVIU) to reduce recurrence risk, though the evidence remains limited.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

**Pang 2021 European Urology systematic review and meta-analysis** identified 26 studies of 13 adjuncts; its meta-analysis of 13 RCTs (859 men) found lower recurrence with an adjunct versus none (OR 0.37; 95% CI 0.27–0.50; p &lt; 0.001), although most studies had high risk of bias. Agent-specific signals:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Agent | Intralesional OR | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| **Mitomycin C** | **0.23** (0.11–0.48) | Lowest intralesional versus-control OR reported in Pang<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Paclitaxel DCB | Not intralesional; not pooled in Pang’s RCT meta-analysis | ROBUST III RCT reported separately; FDA-approved<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[18]](#ref18)[[37]](#ref37)</sup> |
| Corticosteroids | Modest benefit | See [Intralesional corticosteroids](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/dermatologic-topical-urethral/intralesional-corticosteroids) |
| Captopril | Signal present; 61% urethral pruritus at instillation | Tolerability problem |

## Mitomycin C — the best-studied antifibrotic adjunct

### Mechanism

MMC is an alkylating agent derived from *Streptomyces caespitosus* that cross-links DNA, inducing fibroblast apoptosis and inhibiting fibroblast proliferation and collagen synthesis.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> In a small rat urethral-injury study, MMC reduced urothelial proliferation and DNA-damage markers while increasing DNA-repair markers; the authors proposed that this might permit re-epithelialization. MMC is a cytotoxic DNA-crosslinking drug; extravasation and tissue injury remain clinically important.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Urethral stricture evidence

| Study | n | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| **Mazdak 2007 RCT** | 40 | Recurrence at 6 mo **10% MMC vs 50% control** (p = 0.006) — the anchor RCT<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| **Irdam 2019 meta** | 311 reported (4 studies; randomization methods incompletely reported) | Random-effects RR **0.41** (95% CI 0.25–0.68; p &lt; 0.001) for recurrence<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **Farrell 2017** | 44 recurrent bulbar / bulbomembranous (all prior failures) | At median 25.8 mo, **33/44 (75%)** needed no further surgery after one DVIU + MMC (non-radiation 21/26, 80.8%; radiation 12/18, 66.7%). Four elected urethroplasty after recurrence; the other seven underwent repeat DVIU + MMC, leaving **37/40 (92.5%)** without further surgery in the endoscopic pathway<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Farrell 2015** | 37 recurrent stricture / BNC | **75.7%** no further surgery at 23 mo; non-RIS 84.6% vs RIS 54.5%<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |

### Bladder-neck contracture and VUAS evidence

| Study | Population | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| **Vanni 2011** | 18 recurrent BNC | 72% patent after 1 procedure; **89% after 2** at median 12 mo follow-up<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Rozanski 2021 multi-institutional** | 86 recurrent BNC / VUAS | **65% after 1 procedure, 83% after 2, and 90% cumulative after allowing 3 or more** at median 21.1 mo; overall success non-radiated 94% vs radiated 76% (p = 0.04)<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **Sourial 2017** | 29 recurrent post-RP VUAS | **79% patent at 12 mo** after single MMC + dilation; 86% with salvage injection; 20/29 (69%) opted for anti-incontinence surgery, and all 20 were cured or improved at median 58 mo<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| **TURNS — Redshaw 2015 (cautionary)** | Multicenter BNC cohort; 55/66 analyzed after ≥3 mo cystoscopic follow-up | **32/55 (58%) resolution** after 1 procedure at median 9.2 mo — lower than single-center series; **4/55 (7%) MMC-related serious adverse events** (3 needing or planning cystectomy)<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |

The AUA 2023 guideline specifically notes "conflicting data about the utility of Mitomycin-C for the treatment of recurrent vesicourethral stenosis, with further study necessary to validate its use."<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### Historical study protocols — not standardized prescribing

These protocols describe published studies, not interchangeable or recommended regimens. EAU 2026 gives a weak recommendation to use intralesional injections only within clinical trials because drug, dose, volume and technique remain uncertain.<sup>[[36]](#ref36)</sup> For posterior stenosis, MMC injection has been associated with necrosis and fistula; EAU strongly recommends against deep 6- and 12-o’clock DVIU incisions for VUAS or radiation-induced BMS and against endoluminal treatment of completely obliterated VUAS, BMS, or BNS.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[36]](#ref36)</sup>

| Protocol | Dose / concentration | Injection sites | Post-procedure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Farrell / Levine** | 10 mL of **0.4 mg/mL (4 mg total)** in 0.2–0.4 mL aliquots | At each cold-knife incision site (12, 3, 9 o'clock) | CIC × 1 month<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Vanni / Buckley** | 0.3–0.4 mg/mL | Tri- or quadrant cold-knife incisions | Variable<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Klein / Rusilko** | **2 mg total in 5 mL** sterile water | 5 and 7 o'clock incision sites | Foley catheter<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| **Sourial** | MMC at 3, 6, 9 o'clock | Followed by dilation to 26F | Cystoscopic follow-up<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| **Mazdak original RCT** | **0.1 mg submucosal** | At urethrotomy site | Standard<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |

### Safety

- Farrell’s 44-patient 2017 series (median 25.8 months) reported no long-term complications attributable to MMC.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> The earlier 37-patient series (median 23 months) reported no significant morbidity.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> Rozanski’s 86-patient series (median 21.1 months) reported no long-term complications.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **TURNS BNC series.** Four of 55 analyzed patients (7%) had MMC-related grade 3 events after bladder-neck incision: two cases of osteitis pubis, one bladder-neck necrosis, and one rectourethral fistula; three needed or were planning cystectomy.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[36]](#ref36)</sup>
- **Carcinogenicity concern.** Mitomycin cross-links DNA, and its intravenous-product label reports increased tumor incidence in rats and mice; malignant transformation risk after local urethral injection is unknown.<sup>[[40]](#ref40)</sup>
- FAERS pharmacovigilance identified a bladder-perforation reporting signal for mitomycin; these reports cannot establish event frequency or the risk from urethral injection specifically<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

## Paclitaxel drug-coated balloons (Optilume)

### Mechanism

Paclitaxel stabilizes microtubules, leading to cell-cycle arrest, inhibited fibroblast proliferation and reduced collagen deposition. The Optilume DCB combines mechanical dilation with local paclitaxel delivery to a dilated stricture segment, preventing the usual post-dilation restenosis cascade.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

### FDA status and guideline positioning

- **FDA-approved for anterior urethral strictures** (adult men; PMA P210020). The label contraindicates use in patients with hypersensitivity to paclitaxel or structurally related compounds, or a urologic implant such as a penile implant or artificial urinary sphincter; an active UTI must resolve before treatment. Men should abstain from sex or use condoms for 30 days; those with partners of childbearing potential should use highly effective contraception and avoid fathering a child for at least six months<sup>[[37]](#ref37)</sup>
- **Label technique:** place a 0.038-inch guidewire across the stricture, hold the uninflated balloon in position at least 60 seconds to hydrate the coating, then inflate to the size-specific rated burst pressure (8 to 12 atm across the 18 to 36 Fr balloons) for at least 5 minutes, never exceeding that pressure; fully deflate the balloon before withdrawal.<sup>[[37]](#ref37)</sup>
- **AUA 2023 guideline amendment**: surgeons *may* offer dilation or DVIU combined with a DCB for recurrent bulbar urethral strictures &lt;3 cm (conditional recommendation, Grade B evidence)<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **EAU 2026**: a *strong* recommendation to offer a paclitaxel DCB for a short (&lt;3 cm) bulbar stricture recurring after at least two endoscopic treatments in patients unfit for or declining urethroplasty. Evidence for other sites (penile, membranous, bladder neck, VUAS), longer strictures, first-time strictures or prior urethroplasty is too limited for recommendations, and paclitaxel is detectable in semen for up to six months<sup>[[36]](#ref36)</sup>

### Key evidence

| Trial | n / Design | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| **ROBUST III RCT** (Elliott 2022) | 127 recurrent anterior strictures &lt;3 cm | **1-year freedom from reintervention 83.2% DCB vs 21.7% control** (p &lt; 0.0001); **6-month anatomic success (16 Fr scope or 14 Fr catheter passes) 74.6% vs 26.8%, complete-case analysis**<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| **ROBUST III 3-year** (Srikanth 2025) | DCB arm of the same cohort; 56 in the three-year IPSS analysis | At 3 years, **71.9% Kaplan–Meier freedom from reintervention** and **27/56 (48%)** with at least 30% IPSS improvement without reintervention; no contemporaneous three-year randomized control<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> |
| **TURNS real-world** (Patel 2025) | 319 analyzed (260 anterior, 59 posterior; patients without follow-up or maintained on intermittent catheterization excluded); median follow-up 5.7 mo | At 1 y, **functional** recurrence-free survival was **78.4% anterior / 75.8% posterior**, but **anatomical** recurrence-free survival was **66.4% / 59.4%**, respectively (Kaplan–Meier); hypospadias repair (HR 5.21) and a 24F/3 cm DCB (HR 3.64) predicted recurrence of any kind<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> |
| **FIRST-CARE RCT** (protocol 2026) | Ongoing — treatment-naïve bulbar strictures ≤3 cm | Primary endpoint: freedom from reintervention at 12 months — tests Optilume as **first-line** rather than recurrent-stricture therapy<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> |

For device-specific technical detail, see [Drug-coated balloon therapy](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/dermatologic-topical-urethral/drug-coated-balloon-therapy).

## Hyaluronic acid / carboxymethylcellulose (HA/CMC)

**Mechanism:** biophysical barrier gel that separates injured tissue surfaces, reduces adhesion formation, and modulates the wound-healing environment. Not a direct pharmacologic antifibrotic.

### Evidence

- **Chung 2013a multicenter RCT** (n = 120 enrolled, 101 analyzed; post-EIU): recurrence at 24 wk 9.4% HA/CMC versus 22.9% control (p = 0.029); at 1 mo, lower VAS pain scores (0.67 versus 3.60; p &lt; 0.001)<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
- **Chung 2013b multicenter RCT** (n = 180 enrolled, 161 analyzed; post-TURP): stricture incidence 1.3% (1/80) versus 8.6% (7/81) control; lower pain<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
- **Kim 2025** NIUS-delivered HA versus conventional HA injection in 192 men after TURBT (multicenter RCT): higher day-1 patient satisfaction and less pain at placement; at 90 days, grade 1 cystoscopic narrowing was 3.9% versus 27.1%, but grade 2 narrowing requiring dilation was 1.3% versus 1.4% (45 missing 90-day surveillance)<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

Systematic reviews classify HA/CMC alongside MMC as the best-supported adjuncts, with low to intermediate risk of bias.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Platelet-rich plasma (PRP)

**Mechanism:** Autologous PRP contains concentrated growth factors (PDGF, TGF-β, VEGF) proposed to support organized tissue healing. In rat urethral-injury models, PRP reduced mucosal inflammation, edema and spongiofibrosis and brought the collagen I:III ratio toward uninjured controls; these tissue findings have not been established in human urethral strictures.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

### Evidence

- **Rezaei 2019 RCT** (87 men with symptomatic bulbar strictures randomized) of IU plus submucosal PRP versus IU plus saline: among assessed men, 12-mo recurrence was 9.09% versus 26.82% (p = 0.032); at 24 mo, recurrence was 9/41 (21.95%) versus 18/41 (43.90%). The two-year significance is unresolved; the indexed abstract prints p = 0.34, whereas EAU reports p = 0.034<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>
- Animal studies confirm reduced mucosal inflammation, spongiofibrosis, and edema; normalized collagen-I:III ratio<sup>[[26]](#ref26)[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

See [Platelet-rich plasma](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/dermatologic-topical-urethral/platelet-rich-plasma) for the broader PRP / PRF platform.

## Oral systemic adjuncts

- **Tamoxifen.** SERM with antifibrotic effects in a rat renal-fibrosis model (reduced TGF-β1 and collagen expression); its urethral mechanism and estrogen-receptor dependence remain uncertain.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup> Included in the Pang 2021 meta (n = 30) as an adjunct to IU; limited data. AEs observed in the small stricture series: mild gynecomastia (6.7%) and GI effects (6.7%); systemic tamoxifen also carries thromboembolism risk, unquantified for short stricture regimens<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[38]](#ref38)</sup>
- **Deflazacort.** Oral corticosteroid; n = 36 in the meta-analysis; very limited evidence<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Combination regimens

Reported in the Pang 2021 systematic review:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- **Triamcinolone plus MMC plus hyaluronidase** (n = 103; optical internal urethrotomy cohort): combines anti-inflammatory, antifibrotic, and tissue-penetration mechanisms<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Triamcinolone plus MMC plus hyaluronidase plus N-acetyl cysteine** (n = 50; holmium laser urethrotomy cohort): adds antioxidant and mucolytic properties<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Head-to-head comparisons between combination and single-agent regimens are lacking. The review's randomized meta-analysis did not include these combinations, so it cannot establish their relative effect.

## Corticosteroids — pointer only

Triamcinolone and other corticosteroid adjuncts are covered fully in [Intralesional corticosteroids](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/dermatologic-topical-urethral/intralesional-corticosteroids). Summary pointers relevant here:

- **Zhang 2014** 8-RCT meta: steroids prolong time to recurrence (10.14 versus 5.07 months) but effect wanes over time<sup>[[35]](#ref35)</sup>
- Pang 2021 reported separate versus-control estimates for MMC and steroid regimens; it did not compare them directly<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
-Topical corticosteroids used with self-dilatation or for LS are distinct from intralesional post-DVIU injections.<sup>[[36]](#ref36)</sup>

## Clinical Positioning

- **Do not treat a favorable MMC meta-analysis as a routine-use recommendation.** EAU advises intralesional post-DVIU agents within clinical trials and specifically cautions against posterior MMC outside trials. Small uncontrolled BNC or VUAS series cannot establish a standard dose or net benefit.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[36]](#ref36)</sup>
- **TURNS reported serious tissue injury in 4/55 analyzed bladder-neck contracture patients (7%; 55/66 treated had ≥3 months of cystoscopic follow-up).** Three required or were planning cystectomy or diversion. Radiation and extravasation concerns deserve explicit consent, as does the EAU-listed risk of de novo or worsened incontinence after endoluminal treatment of VUAS or bladder-neck stenosis.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[36]](#ref36)</sup>
- **DCB evidence is stronger for a defined recurrent bulbar population.** AUA 2023 advises offering urethroplasty instead of repeated endoscopic treatment after failed dilation or DVIU for recurrent anterior strictures (Moderate Recommendation; Grade C); it also says surgeons may use dilation or DVIU with a DCB for recurrent bulbar strictures &lt;3 cm (Conditional Recommendation; Grade B). ROBUST III compared DCB with standard endoscopy, not urethroplasty. The broader FDA anterior-stricture label is not the same as the AUA selection recommendation. See the device companion for exact labeling, contraindications and contraception.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[18]](#ref18)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- **HA/CMC and PRP remain investigational stricture adjuncts**, rather than established substitutes when MMC cannot be used. Two small randomized PRP studies with different endpoints: Rezaei reported 12-month recurrence, whereas Choudhury measured six-month flow rather than recurrence.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)[[24]](#ref24)[[28]](#ref28)[[39]](#ref39)</sup>
- **FIRST-CARE is ongoing evidence development**.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- The captopril tolerability signal concerns an intraluminal adjunct study; it is not a reason to discontinue indicated oral ACE-inhibitor therapy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
