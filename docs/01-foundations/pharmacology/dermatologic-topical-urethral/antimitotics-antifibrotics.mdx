# Antimitotics and Antifibrotics

**Antimitotic and antifibrotic adjuncts** to endoscopic urethral stricture management target the pathologic wound-healing cascade — fibroblast proliferation, collagen deposition, and spongiofibrosis — that drives recurrence after internal urethrotomy (IU) and dilation. **Mitomycin C (MMC)** has the strongest evidence base across all adjuncts. **Paclitaxel-coated balloon catheters (Optilume)** are the only FDA-approved antimitotic device for anterior urethral strictures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

This article is the hub for **MMC, paclitaxel DCBs, and related antimitotic / antifibrotic adjuncts** (HA/CMC, PRP, tamoxifen, and preclinical agents). For **intralesional corticosteroid adjuncts** (triamcinolone) — mechanistically distinct and covered separately — see [Intralesional corticosteroids](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/dermatologic-topical-urethral/intralesional-corticosteroids). For PRP-specific detail beyond this article, see [Platelet-rich plasma](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/dermatologic-topical-urethral/platelet-rich-plasma). For the clinical condition, see [Urethral stricture](/docs/clinical-conditions/03b-voiding-outlet/urethral-stricture).

---

## Why adjuncts are needed

Internal urethrotomy and dilation are the most commonly performed procedures for urethral stricture disease but carry **long-term success rates of only 0–9%** for recurrent strictures.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Recurrence is driven by pathologic wound healing — excessive fibroblast proliferation, collagen deposition, and spongiofibrosis.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The **AUA Urethral Stricture Disease Guideline Amendment 2023** states that clinicians may endoscopically inject pharmacologic agents at the time of direct visual internal urethrotomy (DVIU) to reduce recurrence risk, though the evidence remains limited.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

**Pang 2021 European Urology systematic review + meta-analysis** (26 studies, 13 adjuncts) — **any adjunct reduced recurrence** (OR 0.37; 95% CI 0.27–0.50; p &lt; 0.001). Agent-specific signals:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Agent | Intralesional OR | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| **Mitomycin C** | **0.23** (0.11–0.48) | **Strongest signal of any adjunct** |
| Paclitaxel DCB | OR with separate RCT data (ROBUST III) | FDA-approved |
| Corticosteroids | Modest benefit | See [Intralesional corticosteroids](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/dermatologic-topical-urethral/intralesional-corticosteroids) |
| Captopril | Signal present, 61% pruritus | Tolerability problem |

## Mitomycin C — the best-studied antifibrotic adjunct

### Mechanism

MMC is an alkylating agent derived from *Streptomyces caespitosus* that **cross-links DNA**, inducing **fibroblast apoptosis** and inhibiting fibroblast proliferation and collagen synthesis.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Rat urethral-stricture models show MMC decreases cell proliferation and DNA damage in the fibrotic compartment while permitting healthy re-epithelialization — it is a **selective antifibrotic**, not a general cytotoxin.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Urethral stricture evidence

| Study | n | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| **Mazdak 2007 RCT** | 40 | Recurrence at 6 mo **10% MMC vs 50% control** (p = 0.006) — the anchor RCT<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| **Irdam 2019 meta** | 231 (3 RCTs) | Fixed RR **0.32** (95% CI 0.19–0.54; p &lt; 0.001) for recurrence<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **Farrell 2017** | 44 recurrent bulbar / bulbomembranous (all prior failures) | **75%** no further intervention after single DVIU + MMC; **92.5%** after a second procedure; non-radiation 80.8% vs radiation 66.7%<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Farrell 2015** | 37 recurrent stricture / BNC | **75.7%** no further surgery at 23 mo; non-RIS 84.6% vs RIS 54.5%<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |

### Bladder-neck contracture and VUAS evidence

| Study | Population | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| **Vanni 2011** | 18 recurrent BNC | 72% patent after 1 procedure; **89% after 2** at 12 mo<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Rozanski 2021 multi-institutional** | 86 recurrent BNC / VUAS | **65% after 1 procedure, 90% overall** after repeat; non-radiated 94% vs radiated 76% (p = 0.04)<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **Sourial 2017** | 29 recurrent post-RP VUAS | **79% patent at 12 mo** after single MMC + dilation; 86% with salvage injection; 69% went on to successful anti-incontinence surgery<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| **TURNS — Redshaw 2015 (cautionary)** | Multicenter BNC cohort | **58% success** after 1 procedure — lower than single-center series; **7% serious adverse-event rate** (4 patients; 3 requiring / planning cystectomy)<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |

The **AUA 2023 guideline** specifically notes "conflicting data about the utility of Mitomycin-C for the treatment of recurrent vesicourethral stenosis, with further study necessary to validate its use."<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### Technique and dosing

| Protocol | Dose / concentration | Injection sites | Post-procedure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Farrell / Levine** | 10 mL of **0.4 mg/mL (4 mg total)** in 0.2–0.4 mL aliquots | At each cold-knife incision site (12, 3, 9 o'clock) | CIC × 1 month<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Vanni / Buckley** | 0.3–0.4 mg/mL | Tri- or quadrant cold-knife incisions | Variable<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Klein / Rusilko** | **2 mg total in 5 mL** sterile water | 5 and 7 o'clock incision sites | Foley catheter<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| **Sourial** | MMC at 3, 6, 9 o'clock | Followed by dilation to 26F | Cystoscopic follow-up<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| **Mazdak original RCT** | **0.1 mg submucosal** | At urethrotomy site | Standard<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |

### Safety

- Most series report **no long-term complications attributable to MMC**<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **TURNS BNC series** reported the key cautionary signal: **7% serious AE rate** including 3 cystectomies<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Theoretical long-term concern** for malignant transformation given DNA-damaging mechanism — not observed in clinical follow-up to date<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- FAERS pharmacovigilance identified **bladder perforation** as a rare but serious signal — primarily reflecting intravesical instillation for bladder cancer rather than stricture injection<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

## Paclitaxel drug-coated balloons (Optilume)

### Mechanism

Paclitaxel stabilizes microtubules → cell-cycle arrest → inhibited fibroblast proliferation and reduced collagen deposition. The Optilume DCB combines **mechanical dilation with local paclitaxel delivery** to a dilated stricture segment, preventing the usual post-dilation restenosis cascade.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

### FDA status and guideline positioning

- **FDA-approved for anterior urethral strictures**
- **AUA 2023 guideline amendment** recommends restricting DCBs to **recurrent bulbar urethral strictures &lt;3 cm** in patients who have failed at least one prior DVIU / dilation<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### Key evidence

| Trial | n / Design | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| **ROBUST III RCT** (Elliott 2022) | 127 recurrent anterior strictures &lt;3 cm | **1-year freedom from reintervention 75% DCB vs 27% control** (p &lt; 0.001); anatomic success 75% vs 27%<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| **ROBUST III 3-year** (Srikanth 2025) | Same cohort | DCB arm maintained **71% freedom from reintervention at 3 years** — nearly equal to 2-y data, **3× control at 1 y**; clinically significant subgroups (≥5 prior dilations, strictures ≥2 cm) showed no differential outcome; AEs rare and self-limited<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> |
| **TURNS real-world** (Patel 2025) | 319 patients | 1-y recurrence-free survival **78.4% anterior** / **75.8% posterior**; strictures after hypospadias repair (HR 5.21) and 24F/3 cm DCB (HR 3.64) were recurrence predictors<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> |
| **FIRST-CARE RCT** (protocol 2026) | Ongoing — treatment-naïve bulbar strictures ≤3 cm | Primary endpoint: freedom from reintervention at 12 months — tests Optilume as **first-line** rather than recurrent-stricture therapy<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> |

For device-specific technical detail, see [Drug-coated balloon therapy](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/dermatologic-topical-urethral/drug-coated-balloon-therapy).

## Hyaluronic acid / carboxymethylcellulose (HA/CMC)

**Mechanism:** biophysical barrier gel — separates injured tissue surfaces, reduces adhesion formation, and modulates the wound-healing environment. Not a direct pharmacologic antifibrotic.

### Evidence

- **Chung 2013a multicenter RCT** (n = 120, post-EIU) — recurrence at 24 wk **9.4% HA/CMC vs 22.9% control** (p = 0.029); lower VAS pain scores (0.67 vs 3.60; p &lt; 0.05)<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
- **Chung 2013b multicenter RCT** (n = 180, post-TURP) — stricture incidence **1.3% (1/80) vs 8.6% (7/81)** control; lower pain<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
- **Kim 2025** novel drug-injectable urethral catheter (NIUS) for HA delivery (n = 192 multicenter RCT) — improved patient satisfaction, less pain, fewer strictures vs conventional HA injection<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

Systematic reviews classify HA/CMC alongside MMC as the best-supported adjuncts, with low to intermediate risk of bias.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Platelet-rich plasma (PRP)

**Mechanism:** autologous concentrated growth factors (PDGF, TGF-β, VEGF) promote organized tissue healing, normalize the collagen-I-to-collagen-III ratio (a marker of pathologic fibrosis), and reduce mucosal inflammation and spongiofibrosis.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

### Evidence

- **Rezaei 2019 RCT** (n = 87) — IU + submucosal PRP vs IU + saline: 12-mo recurrence **9.09% vs 26.82%** (p = 0.032); at 24 mo 21.95% vs 43.90% (p = 0.34, not significant); PRP also decreased stricture length in recurrence cases<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>
- Animal studies confirm reduced mucosal inflammation, spongiofibrosis, and edema; normalized collagen-I:III ratio<sup>[[26]](#ref26)[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

See [Platelet-rich plasma](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/dermatologic-topical-urethral/platelet-rich-plasma) for the broader PRP / PRF platform.

## Oral systemic adjuncts

- **Tamoxifen** — SERM with ER-independent antifibrotic effects (TGF-β1 and collagen-synthesis inhibition).<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup> Included in the Pang 2021 meta (n = 30) as an adjunct to IU; limited data. AEs: mild gynecomastia (6.7%), GI effects (6.7%)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Deflazacort** — oral corticosteroid; n = 36 in the meta-analysis; very limited evidence<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Preclinical and early-clinical agents

| Agent | Evidence | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Captopril** (intraluminal ACE inhibitor) | n = 37 clinical — **61% pruritus** limits tolerability<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Clinical but poorly tolerated |
| **Bevacizumab** (anti-VEGF) | Rabbit model: inhibits fibrosis comparably to MMC<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup> | Preclinical |
| **5-Fluorouracil** | Rabbit model: comparable antifibrotic to MMC<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup> | Preclinical |
| **Pirfenidone** (nanoparticle-loaded catheter) | Rabbit model: reduced fibrosis; TGF-β1 inhibition<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup> | Preclinical delivery platform |
| **Wnt/β-catenin inhibitors** (ICG-001, PRI-724) | Rat model: reduced collagen I and α-SMA<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup> | Preclinical |
| **Glycyrrhizic-acid biogel scaffold** | Rabbit model: M2 macrophage polarization, reduced pro-inflammatory cytokines<sup>[[33]](#ref33)</sup> | Preclinical |
| **Protein-nanofilm drug-eluting catheters** | ECM-homeostasis modulation; reduced collagen + enhanced MMP-1 degradation<sup>[[34]](#ref34)</sup> | Preclinical drug-delivery platform |

## Combination regimens

Included in the Pang 2021 meta-analysis:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- **Steroid + MMC + hyaluronidase** (n = 103) — combines anti-inflammatory, antifibrotic, and tissue-penetration mechanisms
- **Triamcinolone + MMC + N-acetyl cysteine** (n = 50) — adds antioxidant / mucolytic properties

Head-to-head comparisons between combination and single-agent regimens are lacking. The meta-analysis did not demonstrate superiority of combinations over the best single agents.

## Corticosteroids — pointer only

Triamcinolone and other corticosteroid adjuncts are covered fully in [Intralesional corticosteroids](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/dermatologic-topical-urethral/intralesional-corticosteroids). Summary pointers relevant here:

- **Zhang 2014** 8-RCT meta — steroids **prolong time to recurrence** (10.14 vs 5.07 months) but effect wanes over time<sup>[[35]](#ref35)</sup>
- In the **Pang 2021** network, MMC outperforms steroids on recurrence OR<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Practical:** reserve steroids when MMC is unavailable, contraindicated, or for patients already on long-term topical / intralesional steroid protocols for coexistent LS

## Clinical Positioning

- **Mitomycin C is the best-evidenced injectable antifibrotic adjunct** — Pang 2021 OR 0.23 and the Mazdak / Irdam RCT evidence put it at the top of the injectable hierarchy. The Farrell and Rozanski series support routine use for recurrent strictures, BNC, and VUAS.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **The TURNS cautionary data in BNC are load-bearing.** 7% SAE rate including 3 cystectomies in the Redshaw series should temper enthusiasm and drive careful patient counseling and selection.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Paclitaxel DCB (Optilume) is now the FDA-approved antimitotic option** — ROBUST III and the 3-year extension show durable 71% freedom from reintervention in recurrent bulbar strictures &lt;3 cm. Per AUA 2023, restrict to patients with prior DVIU / dilation failure and strictures &lt;3 cm.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[18]](#ref18)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- **FIRST-CARE will define whether DCB moves to first-line** for treatment-naïve bulbar strictures; results expected to reshape the algorithm.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- **HA/CMC is the best-supported non-pharmacologic adjunct** — two multicenter RCTs with significant recurrence and pain reduction. Reasonable alternative in centers where MMC is unavailable or high-risk patients where TURNS-type SAEs are a concern.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
- **PRP is promising with a single RCT** — 12-month benefit, 24-month signal attenuates. Reasonable in trial settings or as an autologous alternative in MMC-contraindicated patients.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>
- **Do not use oral captopril as an adjunct** — 61% pruritus incidence is unacceptable.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Corticosteroid adjuncts prolong time to recurrence but do not reduce ultimate recurrence rate** — default to MMC when available; see [Intralesional corticosteroids](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/dermatologic-topical-urethral/intralesional-corticosteroids) for detailed framework.<sup>[[35]](#ref35)</sup>
- **For radiated VUAS, expect reduced MMC efficacy** — Rozanski 2021 non-radiated 94% vs radiated 76%; counsel patients accordingly.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Document MMC protocol carefully** — dosing ranges from 0.1 mg total (Mazdak) to 4 mg total (Farrell); the most widely adopted Farrell protocol uses 10 mL of 0.4 mg/mL with clean intermittent catheterization × 1 month.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
