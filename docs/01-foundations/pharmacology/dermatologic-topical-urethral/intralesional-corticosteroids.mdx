# Intralesional Corticosteroids

**Intralesional corticosteroids** (primarily triamcinolone acetonide) are used across urology as anti-inflammatory and anti-fibrotic adjuncts rather than stand-alone therapies. The most guideline-supported indications are submucosal injection for Hunner lesions in IC/BPS (AUA Grade C); stricture or BNC injections have more uncertain, largely off-label evidence. Other applications include genital lichen sclerosus / lichen planus, BXO at preputioplasty, vesicourethral anastomotic stenosis (VUAS) after RP, and ureteroenteric anastomotic strictures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For related agents, see [High-potency topical corticosteroids](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/dermatologic-topical-urethral/high-potency-topical-corticosteroids), [Topical calcineurin inhibitors](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/dermatologic-topical-urethral/topical-calcineurin-inhibitors), [Antimitotics / antifibrotics](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/dermatologic-topical-urethral/antimitotics-antifibrotics) (mitomycin C), [Platelet-rich plasma](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/dermatologic-topical-urethral/platelet-rich-plasma), and [Intravesical IC/BPS agents](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/bladder-pain-ic-bps).

## Mechanism in urologic fibrotic and inflammatory disease

Triamcinolone acetonide acts through:<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- **Anti-inflammatory.** Phospholipase-A2 inhibition leading to reduced prostaglandins and leukotrienes; decreased capillary permeability and leukocyte migration
- **Anti-fibrotic.** Suppression of fibroblast proliferation and collagen synthesis; reduced MMP-3 and TGF-β expression in treated bladder-neck tissue<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Local immunomodulation** with minimal systemic exposure when properly dosed

Published urologic protocols often use triamcinolone acetonide suspension. **Formulations and routes are not interchangeable:** the cited US Kenalog-40/80 label is for intramuscular or intra-articular use, not intradermal injection; these urologic injections require a deliberately selected off-label protocol and pharmacy verification.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## IC/BPS — Hunner lesions (AUA-recommended)

**AUA IC/BPS guideline 2022 (Recommendation; Grade C):** if Hunner lesions are present, fulguration with electrocautery and/or injection of triamcinolone should be performed.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### Technique

Endoscopic submucosal injection of triamcinolone directly into and around Hunner lesions under cystoscopic guidance.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Efficacy

| Study | n | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| **Funaro 2018** | 36 | Pain score **8.3 → 3.8** at about 1 month (p &lt; 0.001); 72% needed only one set of injections, and the mean interval to repeat injection in the rest was about 345 days (retrospective)<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **Mateu 2017** | 20 | VAS **8 → 2.5** (p &lt; 0.001) after flexible-cystoscope injection; retrospective, median follow-up 7 months; 7 of 10 patients followed at least 8 months needed retreatment<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |

Triamcinolone injection is one of the few IC/BPS therapies producing months-long improvement after a single procedure, though periodic retreatment is typically necessary.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> See [Intravesical IC/BPS agents](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/bladder-pain-ic-bps) for the broader instillation and injection framework.

## Urethral stricture — adjunct to internal urethrotomy

Intralesional corticosteroid is one of the most-studied adjuncts to internal urethrotomy (IU) / urethral dilation.

### Meta-analytic evidence

- **Pang 2021** systematic review and meta-analysis (26 studies): any adjunct to minimally invasive stricture treatment lowered recurrence (OR 0.37; 95% CI 0.27–0.50; p &lt; 0.001). Mitomycin C had the strongest signal (intralesional OR 0.23; 95% CI 0.11–0.48); indirect pooled comparisons do not establish a preferred injectable<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Zhang 2014 meta-analysis** (8 RCTs, n = 203): local steroids plus IU prolonged time to recurrence (mean 10.14 versus 5.07 months; p &lt; 0.001), but did not significantly change the overall recurrence rate or the success of second urethrotomy<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Soliman 2022 meta-analysis** (7 RCTs, n = 365; injected or topical urethral steroid after DVIU): lower recurrence (RR 0.67; 95% CI 0.49–0.90) and longer time to recurrence (HR 0.58; 95% CI 0.39–0.85), with no significant Qmax gain. Certainty was downgraded for small trials and unclear bias<sup>[[35]](#ref35)</sup>
- **Jacobs 2021 systematic review:** the positive clinical effect of steroids appears to decrease with longer follow-up, suggesting a time-limited anti-fibrotic benefit<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **AUA 2023 amendment:** surgeons may inject pharmacologic agents into the stricture at DVIU; steroid studies showed a consistently lower recurrence rate that did not reach statistical significance, with short follow-up. This is a permissive statement, not a recommendation for a specific agent<sup>[[36]](#ref36)</sup>

### Technique

Triamcinolone acetonide 40 mg/mL injected submucosally at the stricture site at 4–8 points via cystoscopic injection needle, either at the time of urethrotomy or as a postoperative series.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> The original Sharpe-Finney 1976 series (n = 96) established the technique and noted particular utility for distal or meatal strictures and post-RP anastomotic strictures.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

**EAU 2026:** the summary of evidence rates injections after DVIU as possibly improving short-term stricture-free rates (LE 1a) but notes that drug, dose, volume and technique are uncertain and use is off-label. The recommendation (weak) is to use intralesional injections only within clinical trials. **Agent comparison:** current EAU guidance limits intralesional post-DVIU injections to clinical trials; there is no established default MMC-versus-steroid sequence.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup> See [Antimitotics / antifibrotics](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/dermatologic-topical-urethral/antimitotics-antifibrotics).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

**Intraurethral steroid with self-dilatation:** a separate strategy from injection. In an EAU panel meta-analysis of five RCTs (n = 250), adding topical triamcinolone ointment to ISD after DVIU raised stabilisation at short-term follow-up (77% versus 64%; OR 0.67; 95% CI 0.45–0.99) with no extra complications reported, though bias risk was unclear to high. EAU 2026 gives a weak recommendation to use intraurethral corticosteroids in addition to ISD.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)[[37]](#ref37)</sup>

## Bladder-neck contracture and post-RP VUAS

BNC after TURP or open prostatectomy and VUAS after radical prostatectomy both carry high recurrence rates after endoscopic treatment alone. Triamcinolone has been studied as an adjunct, but uncontrolled outcomes cannot establish its incremental benefit.

### Post-TURP BNC

- **Zhang 2021.** 28 patients with highly recurrent BNC (mean recurrence interval 2.2 months) treated with transurethral resection plus intra- and postoperative triamcinolone injections (40 mg/mL at 8 points, repeated q4 wk × 3): 92.9% success at median 2.8-year follow-up<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Sun 2022.** 180-W GreenLight laser vaporization with repeated triamcinolone injections (n = 46): zero recurrence during follow-up; significant improvement in Qmax, IPSS, QoL, PVR. Immunohistochemistry showed reduced collagen I, MMP-3, and TGF-β in treated tissue<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- **Palminteri–Ferrari 2024 BNC series.** An uncontrolled bladder-neck-contracture technique report, not a general VUAS protocol. Do not extrapolate quadrant incisions to post-RP or radiated stenosis; deep 6/12-o’clock cuts risk rectal injury or urosymphyseal fistula.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[31]](#ref31)</sup>

### Post-RP VUAS

- **Eltahawy 2008.** Holmium laser incision at 3 and 9 o'clock with triamcinolone at incision sites: 19 patients with patency (reported as 83%; the abstract enrolled 24 and has a denominator inconsistency) at mean 24-mo follow-up<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Kravchick 2013.** TRUS-guided injection of long-acting steroids into the scar area after dilation had the highest efficiency quotient among treatment modalities, with lower retreatment rates and no incontinence versus cold-knife urethrotomy or TUR<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

## Peyronie's disease — not recommended

Intralesional corticosteroids have a long history but limited supporting evidence and are not recommended by current guidelines as a preferred intralesional agent.

- **AUA PD guideline 2015.** Intralesional corticosteroids (dexamethasone, betamethasone with hyaluronidase and lidocaine) have been studied but have not consistently shown efficacy in placebo-controlled trials<sup>[[17]](#ref17)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Rosenberg 2023 Cochrane review.** Very low certainty evidence for intralesional betamethasone versus saline; uncertain effects on curvature (RR 0.75; 95% CI 0.20–2.79; single small study, n = 30)<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **Manfredi 2025 SR** on acute-phase PD injection therapy: corticosteroids showed variable efficacy with favorable safety, but overall evidence quality is low<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- **Ure 2021** non-randomized methylprednisolone 40 mg weekly × 8 weeks (n = 48 acute-phase): significant reductions in plaque size (13.6 to 10.8 mm; p = 0.025) and PDQ scores; no AEs<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

### Why not preferred

- Risk of tunical atrophy and local tissue thinning with repeated injection
- Potential worsening of erectile function
- Other agents have differing guideline support: CCH has randomized evidence, whereas EAU finds insufficient support for intralesional verapamil. There is no uniform all-society endorsement of every injection.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[21]](#ref21)[[22]](#ref22)[[33]](#ref33)</sup>

See [Peyronie's disease agents](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/peyronies-disease-agents) for the broader PD intralesional comparison.

## Genital lichen sclerosus and lichen planus

**Intralesional corticosteroids are a selected specialist option** for adult vulvar LS that remains active despite appropriate topical therapy. The German S3 guideline gives an open recommendation, not evidence of proven comparative benefit. Biopsy a resistant hyperkeratotic plaque to exclude neoplasia before injection. Choose concentration, volume, depth and interval for the particular lesion and formulation; there is no universal safe 40-mg vulvar ceiling.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[23]](#ref23)[[34]](#ref34)</sup>

### Intraurethral clobetasol for penile / urethral LS

- **Potts 2016.** Intraurethral clobetasol applied with a catheter or dilator: 89% success in a selected observational series at mean 24.8-month follow-up. Most responders continued treatment; avoidance of escalation during follow-up is not a permanent cure or proof that medication reverses established fibrosis.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)[[34]](#ref34)</sup>
- **Hayden 2020.** Topical and intraurethral clobetasol improved AUASS from 12 to 8 and QoL bother from 4 to 2; 85.7% avoided surgery<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

### BXO in children — Wilkinson 2012

An observational comparison reported 81% fully retractile foreskin after preputioplasty plus intralesional triamcinolone, and meatal stenosis rates of 6% versus 19% in the circumcision group at approximately 14 months. Treatment selection and the combined intervention limit causal interpretation; this does not establish superiority over circumcision.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup> See [High-potency topical corticosteroids](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/dermatologic-topical-urethral/high-potency-topical-corticosteroids) for the first-line topical framework in LS.

## Ureteroenteric anastomotic strictures

Triamcinolone has been incorporated into endoscopic management of benign UEAS after urinary diversion.

- **Katims 2021.** 24 UEAS treated with laser incision plus triamcinolone plus balloon dilation to 24F plus temporary stenting: 83.3% success at median 30-month follow-up<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>
- **Meretyk 1992.** Early endoureterotomy experience suggested triamcinolone into the stricture bed favorably influenced subsequent ureteral patency<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>

---

## Idiopathic bulbar urethritis

**Ashraf 2017 pilot** (n = 14 boys with idiopathic bulbar urethritis: hematuria with or without dysuria, without infection): cystoscopy-guided instillation of 40 mg triamcinolone with 85.7% complete or partial resolution, though 50% required at least one additional treatment.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup>

## Published dosing examples — not a universal protocol

Concentration alone is not a dose. Total exposure, dilution, route, excipients and site-specific technique require verification; the studies below are heterogeneous and do not establish routine scheduling.

| Indication | Typical dose | Technique | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hunner lesions (IC/BPS)** | Variable low-dose submucosal | Endoscopic submucosal injection into / around lesions | Single session; retreat PRN (repeat interval varies from about 4 to 12 months in these series)<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| **Urethral stricture** | 40 mg/mL at **4–8 points** | Submucosal injection at urethrotomy site | At IU ± postoperative series<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Bladder-neck contracture** | 80 mg (2 mL of 40 mg/mL) at 8 points | Cystoscopic injection at incision sites | Intraop + q4 wk × 3 postop<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **VUAS post-RP** | 40 mg at incision sites | Transurethral or TRUS-guided | At incision; repeat PRN<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| **Vulvar LS** | Individualized concentration and total volume | Selected refractory plaques after excluding neoplasia | Specialist-directed; no universal vulvar dose ceiling<sup>[[23]](#ref23)[[34]](#ref34)</sup> |
| **BXO — pediatric** | Intralesional at preputioplasty | Injection into BXO-affected tissue | At time of preputioplasty<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup> |
| **Ureteroenteric stricture** | Into stricture bed | Antegrade / ureteroscopic injection | At laser incision<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup> |

## Safety

Generally well tolerated when dosed appropriately; complications are mild and localized in most series.

| Concern | Details |
|---|---|
| **UTI** | 2.9–14% with urethral steroid injection (pooled from small, high-risk-of-bias studies)<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| Bleeding | 8.8%<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| Extravasation | 5.8%<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **Iatrogenic Cushing's syndrome** | Two pediatric cases reported after intralesional triamcinolone for urethral strictures — an old case report does not establish a safe alternative formulation, dose or interval for children<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup> |
| Tissue atrophy | Recognized local corticosteroid risk; especially relevant with repeated injection and thin genital skin<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Systemic steroid effects | Impaired wound healing, hyperglycemia, adrenal suppression with repeated high doses, immunosuppression<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |

## Clinical Positioning

- **For Hunner lesions, triamcinolone injection may be used with or without fulguration.** The AUA recommendation does not require the combination.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Stricture adjuncts are not a routine MMC-first sequence.** EAU limits intralesional post-DVIU agents to clinical trials. Posterior BNC and VUAS series do not prove which injection or schedule is best.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup>
- **Do not generalize study injection or incision recipes across anatomy.** Current label formulation restrictions and posterior injury risks matter even where selected off-label use is supported.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[31]](#ref31)</sup>
- **Intralesional corticosteroids are not established Peyronie therapy.** See the dedicated agent page for the different levels of support for CCH, interferon and verapamil.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)[[18]](#ref18)[[21]](#ref21)[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- **For refractory genital LS, exclude neoplasia before injection** and use specialist site-specific dosing rather than treating 40 mg as a universal safe vulvar ceiling.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
- **Pediatric BXO preputioplasty evidence is limited.** EAU recommends circumcision for BXO or treatment-refractory phimosis; a small foreskin-preservation series does not establish superiority.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[26]](#ref26)[[32]](#ref32)</sup>
- **Cushing syndrome, adrenal suppression and tissue atrophy remain possible.** Small-study adverse-event percentages are not safety ceilings, and an old pediatric report is not a modern dosing standard.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[30]](#ref30)</sup>
