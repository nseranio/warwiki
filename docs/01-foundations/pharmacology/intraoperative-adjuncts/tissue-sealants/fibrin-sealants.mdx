# Fibrin Sealants — Tisseel, Evicel, Artiss

**Fibrin sealants** are two-component biologic adhesives that reproduce the **final common pathway of coagulation at the application site** — delivering concentrated **fibrinogen** and **thrombin** simultaneously through a dual-syringe applicator. When the two components meet, thrombin immediately cleaves fibrinogen to form a **fibrin clot** that bonds tissue, seals bleeding surfaces, and reinforces suture lines. The clot is broken down during healing; absorption depends on formulation, amount and application site.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Product indications differ: **Tisseel** is a surgical hemostatic adjunct and has a specific sealing indication for colonic anastomoses after temporary colostomy reversal. **Artiss** is an adhesive for burn skin grafts and facial-rhytidectomy flaps, and is **not indicated for hemostasis**. These labels do not establish routine prevention of GU anastomotic leakage.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

|---|---|---|---|
| **Tisseel** | Baxter | Rapid; allow at least 2 minutes before disturbing | Thrombin 500 IU/mL (label range 400–625); synthetic aprotinin; frozen prefilled syringe or lyophilized kit; hemostasis adjunct plus colonic-anastomosis sealing |
| **Evicel** | Ethicon (Omrix) | Rapid | Human fibrinogen with human thrombin 800–1,200 IU/mL; no aprotinin; hemostasis adjunct; spray with pressurized CO<sub>2</sub> only |
| **Artiss** | Baxter | **Up to ~60 seconds** (slow-cure) | Low thrombin concentration (~4 IU/mL; label range 2.5–6.5); skin-graft adherence in burns and facial-rhytidectomy flap adhesion; not for hemostasis |
| **Vistaseal** | Ethicon-distributed (manufactured by Grifols) | Rapid | Fibrinogen ~80 mg/mL; hemostasis adjunct for mild to moderate bleeding; supplied with a dual applicator |
| **Raplixa** | Historical powder product | N/A | Dry formulation; confirm current local availability rather than assuming routine stock |

Specifications are from the manufacturers' US labeling.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Evicel has no aprotinin and its label authorizes CO<sub>2</sub> spray only; the Evicel document on the FDA site is an older revision, so confirm against the current insert.

## Mechanism

1. **Component A (fibrinogen ± aprotinin):** pooled human fibrinogen, roughly 67–106 mg/mL in Tisseel and Artiss, with synthetic aprotinin (2,250–3,750 KIU/mL)
2. **Component B:** human thrombin with calcium chloride; the listed human fibrin sealants should not be conflated with stand-alone bovine topical thrombin
3. When mixed at the tissue surface:
   - **Thrombin cleaves fibrinogen** → fibrin monomer
   - **Fibrin monomers polymerize** into a stable clot, which strengthens over roughly the first two hours (Artiss label)
   - **Calcium** in the thrombin solution supports the reaction
   - **Aprotinin (in Tisseel and Artiss)** slows plasmin-mediated fibrinolysis, extending clot durability

### Cure time by product

- **Standard (Tisseel, Evicel, Vistaseal):** high thrombin concentration; sets within seconds. Tisseel labeling asks for at least 2 minutes after application to reach adequate polymerization.
- **Slow-cure (Artiss):** low thrombin; initial polymerization takes up to 60 seconds, which allows tissue repositioning / flap adhesion before setting.

## GU Reconstruction Applications and Evidence Limits

Urinary-tract suture-line reinforcement, buccal graft fixation and pelvic reconstruction uses have been reported. They require a procedure-specific rationale and are not substitutes for watertight suturing, vascularized graft contact, tension control or appropriate drainage. A generic claim that glue improves inosculation or routinely reduces urinary leaks is not established by the sources cited here.

**TachoSil already carries fibrinogen and thrombin.** Its tunical sealing technique is distinct from adding liquid fibrin sealant beneath a graft; do not assume a separate glue layer is necessary. See the [patch companion](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/intraoperative-adjuncts/tissue-sealants/tachosil) and the operative Peyronie's pages for the described methods.

For skin-graft or flap adherence, Artiss provides up to 60 seconds of positioning time, followed by at least three minutes of gentle compression; the clot reaches final strength in about 2 hours. Its labeled applications should not be generalized to every phalloplasty flap or to hemostasis.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

The 2025 publication of an IPP infection-prevention bundle reported 0/103 infections versus 4/115 historical controls at 12 months, with Evicel applied to corporotomy suture lines and the pump pocket. The bundle changed skin preparation (chlorhexidine-alcohol versus povidone-iodine), sealant use and drain practice simultaneously, and the controls were historical. It does not isolate a benefit from fibrin sealant or establish a zero-infection guarantee.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Application and Safety

Preparation time, thawing, warming, storage after preparation and applicator compatibility are **product-specific**. Follow the kit in use; there is no universal preparation time or shelf life. Examples from labeling: frozen Tisseel and Artiss syringes must be fully thawed and warmed to 33–37 °C, never microwaved, and not refrigerated or re-frozen after thawing; Evicel components thaw in a refrigerator, at room temperature or at 37 °C and are used within 24 hours (room temperature) or 30 days (refrigerated) of thawing.

- Prepare the visible surface and apply a thin, even layer. Excessive clot thickness can delay wound healing; a routine second thick coat or interposition beneath a graft is not inherently beneficial.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Gas embolism is a potentially fatal application risk.** Use only the approved regulator/applicator combination, gas, pressure and working distance. **EASYSPRAY must not be used in enclosed body cavities** for Tisseel or Artiss. For open surgery the Tisseel/Artiss label specifies EASYSPRAY at 10–15 cm and 1.5–2.0 bar (21.8–29.0 psi); for minimally invasive surgery it specifies the DUPLOSPRAY MIS regulator with CO<sub>2</sub> only, 2–5 cm (3 cm recommended) and 1.18–1.50 bar. Evicel is sprayed with pressurized CO<sub>2</sub> only. Spray only on visible sites, monitor blood pressure, oxygen saturation and end-tidal CO<sub>2</sub> during spraying, and do not spray when the required distance cannot be assured. Do not use air or gas to dry the field.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Never inject into the circulation or into highly vascularized tissue; intravascular application can cause thromboembolic events. Tisseel is also contraindicated for severe or brisk arterial/venous bleeding and for aprotinin hypersensitivity.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Artiss contains aprotinin too. Anaphylaxis can occur with first or repeat exposure; document prior use and component allergies.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- All of these products contain human plasma-derived proteins. Confirm the individual patient's acceptance of these components; donor screening and viral inactivation reduce but do not eliminate infectious-agent risk (vCJD theoretically; parvovirus B19 is hard to inactivate and matters most in pregnancy, immunocompromise and increased erythropoiesis).
- Alcohol, iodine and heavy-metal solutions can denature the proteins: rinse and dry the site if the field was prepared with them.
- Costs and routine stock depend on the institution. Do not open expensive kits automatically for every reconstructive case without a planned indication.

## Fibrin Sealant vs. Collagen-Fibrinogen Patch vs. Hemostatic Matrix

| Agent | Role |
|---|---|
| **Fibrin sealant (this page)** | Liquid two-component products with distinct hemostatic, sealing or adhesive indications |
| **[TachoSil / collagen-fibrinogen patch](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/intraoperative-adjuncts/tissue-sealants/tachosil)** | Solid-phase fibrin sealant on an absorbable carrier — used for selected focal hemostatic applications; carrier and indication vary |
| **[FloSeal](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/intraoperative-adjuncts/hemostatic-agents/floseal)** | Thrombin + gelatin matrix for selected bleeding sites within its instructions |
| **[Cyanoacrylates](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/intraoperative-adjuncts/tissue-sealants/cyanoacrylates)** | Skin-closure products have specific external-skin restrictions; other formulations are distinct |
