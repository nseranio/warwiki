# TachoSil and Evarrest — Fibrin Sealant Patches

These patches combine human fibrinogen and thrombin with an absorbable carrier, but their carriers and indications differ. The fibrin layer adheres to the application surface; this does not establish them as interchangeable structural grafts.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Composition and US Indications

| Product | Carrier and identification | US labeled role |
|---|---|---|
| **TachoSil** | Equine collagen sponge; yellow active side, off-white inactive side; Corza Medical | Adjunct to hemostasis with manual compression in adults and children aged at least one month in cardiovascular and hepatic surgery when suture, ligature or cautery is ineffective or impractical. Use elsewhere, including urologic surgery, is off-label in the US |
| **Evarrest** | **Oxidized regenerated cellulose plus polyglactin 910 fibers**, not collagen; powdery active side and embossed inactive side; Ethicon | Adjunct to surgical hemostasis with manual compression in adults when conventional techniques are ineffective or impractical; pediatric safety and effectiveness are not established |

Both contain human plasma-derived proteins. Evarrest's noncollagen carrier does not make the entire product synthetic or free of blood-derived components. TachoSil contains approximately 5.5 mg/cm² fibrinogen and 2 units/cm² thrombin; these strengths should not be copied to Evarrest.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

The EU authorization is broader: supportive treatment in surgery to improve hemostasis, promote tissue sealing and provide suture support in vascular surgery where standard techniques are insufficient (adults and children from 1 month), plus dural sealing in adult neurosurgery.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> Tunical coverage and urinary-tract sealing must be considered separately from these US hemostatic indications. Neither product replaces mechanical repair of major arterial or venous defects.

## GU Reconstruction Applications

### 1. Peyronie's disease — tunical defect sealing

TachoSil tunical coverage is outside its US hemostasis indications.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> In men with stable Peyronie's disease and preserved erectile function, fibrin-coated collagen fleece has been used to seal a concave-side tunical defect without suture fixation after partial plaque excision.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> After IPP placement for complex Peyronie's disease, collagen fleece has been used over a relaxing tunical incision for residual curvature; a separate retrospective implant cohort used Evarrest and other hemostatic patches.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- **Hatzichristodoulou sealing technique:** 319 of 367 operated patients returned for long-term assessment; 93.7% immediate and 91.2% long-term complete straightness at median 47.2 months. Erectile function worsened in 15.7%. This cohort does not establish superiority over other grafts
- **PICS technique (Penile Implant with Collagen Sealing).** TachoSil is applied over the tunical defect after IPP insertion in severe Peyronie's; 84% complete straightening in a retrospective multicenter series of 37 patients. Early complications occurred in 24%; one acute implant infection required explantation
- **Operative limitation.** In tunical lengthening for Peyronie's disease, the EAU guideline notes that residual deformity cannot be assessed perioperatively after TachoSil fleece grafting.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- See [Peyronie's — plaque incision/grafting](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease/plaque-incision-grafting) and [Peyronie's — prosthesis with straightening](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease/prosthesis-with-straightening)

### 2. Partial nephrectomy and other GU sealing uses

In laparoscopic partial nephrectomy for pT1aN0M0 renal-cell carcinoma, a single-institution retrospective comparison (n=113) found post-operative pseudoaneurysm requiring embolization in 11/81 conventional-suture patients versus 0/32 patients whose renorrhaphy bound a TachoSil patch into the closure. This is an off-label technique from a non-randomized series, not a demonstrated reduction in leak or bleeding.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

Parenchymal or collecting-system reinforcement and urinary-diversion applications have been described, but a routine reduction in urinary leakage cannot be inferred from hemostatic indications. In particular, Farrell 2019 studied Peyronie's surgery with an implant, not partial nephrectomy: 18 patients received mixed hemostatic patches (10 Evarrest, 6 Nu-Knit and 2 TachoSil), compared retrospectively with 15 pericardium recipients. Median follow-up was 6.6 months for the mixed-patch group versus 34.6 months for the pericardium group.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> This is neither a TachoSil-specific randomized comparison nor evidence of kidney collecting-system benefit.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

For the operative choice between tunical grafting and implant-associated straightening, use the procedure pages linked below; do not extrapolate a small patch series into a universal first-line material.

## Efficacy Evidence

The US label rests on randomized comparisons against standard hemostatic care. In cardiovascular surgery, 44/59 TachoSil patients (75%) versus 20/60 patients (33%) treated with hemostatic fleece without active coagulation agents achieved hemostasis within three minutes. In hepatic surgery, 92/114 (81%) versus 55/110 (50%) achieved the same endpoint against oxidized-cellulose fleece.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> A systematic review also reports randomized data in liver, thoracic and urologic surgery.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The US label trials address cardiovascular and hepatic hemostasis; the review also includes thoracic and urologic trials, which do not establish efficacy for GU reconstructive sealing.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Application and Safety

**Follow the actual product instructions; do not use a generic patch recipe.** The US labels cap TachoSil use at 10 patches of 9.5 × 4.8 cm or 14 patches of 4.8 × 4.8 cm, and Evarrest use at eight patches of 5.1 × 10.2 cm.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- **TachoSil:** remove contaminating fluids and disinfectants. It may be applied wet or dry. If premoistening is chosen, use 0.9% saline and apply immediately; an oozing surface needs no premoistening. Apply the yellow side with at least three minutes of gentle pressure using moistened gloves or a pad. Choose a patch that extends 1–2 cm beyond the wound margin and overlap multiple patches by at least 1 cm. Alcohol, iodine and heavy-metal residues denature the proteins, so irrigate them away first.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Evarrest:** keep the patch dry before application, place the active surface on the visible bleeding site with the recommended overlap, and apply continuous manual compression for three minutes. Its label limits minimally invasive use when manual compression is difficult; trocar delivery is not automatically appropriate.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **TachoSil:** Leave an adherent patch in place; if placement is unsatisfactory or bleeding persists, repeat application without removing the applied patch. Remove unattached pieces to limit dislodgement; the August 2025 US label strengthened this precaution.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> **Evarrest:** Remove and replace a poorly positioned, folded or incompletely adherent patch; if bleeding reflects insufficient coverage, add an overlapping patch.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Do not apply intravascularly or substitute for repair of major arterial or venous bleeding. Avoid use in infected or contaminated sites according to labeling. Do not pack confined spaces; compression and unwanted adhesions, including bowel obstruction, are recognized risks.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Check component allergy: TachoSil is contraindicated with horse-protein or human-blood-product hypersensitivity; Evarrest is contraindicated with anaphylactic or severe systemic reactions to human blood products. Both retain a residual infectious-agent transmission risk despite processing.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

A patch may provide local hemostasis or coverage in a selected operation. It does not itself prove improved graft inosculation, preservation of penile length, or prevention of urinary leakage.
