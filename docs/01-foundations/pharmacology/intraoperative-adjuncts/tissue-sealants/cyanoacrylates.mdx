# Cyanoacrylate Tissue Adhesives — Dermabond, Histoacryl

**Cyanoacrylate adhesives** are liquid monomers that polymerize on contact with tissue moisture into a rigid adhesive film. Their primary role in reconstructive urology is topical skin closure, the final layer over a subcuticular suture, but they also have niche applications in mucosal adhesion, vessel embolization (interventional radiology), and experimental GU-tract applications.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Chemistry

| Monomer | Products (examples) | Clinical use |
|---|---|---|
| **n-butyl cyanoacrylate** | Histoacryl (B. Braun); Indermil; LiquiBand Surgical (Advanced Medical Solutions) | Topical skin closure of minimum-tension wounds (US labeling for Histoacryl); stiffer, faster-setting film. Skin-closure products are not interchangeable with embolization or endoscopic products |
| **2-octyl cyanoacrylate** | Dermabond (Ethicon); SurgiSeal (Adhezion); LiquiBand Exceed (Advanced Medical Solutions) | **Topical skin closure standard**; more flexible polymer |
| **n-butyl + octyl blend** | Indermil flexifuze | Flexible skin application |
| **n-butyl + methacryloxysulfolane** | Glubran 2 (GEM) | CE-marked in Europe for external and internal (surgical, endoscopic, endovascular) use; not a skin-closure product and not FDA-cleared for those uses |

### Polymerization

- Liquid monomer applied to tissue
- Moisture from tissue surface triggers anionic polymerization
- Setting and apposition times depend on the product; full polymerization of Dermabond Advanced occurs when the film is no longer tacky
- **Exothermic reaction.** Minor warmth during set
- Polymer film sloughs off the skin over 5–10 days as epithelium turns over

## GU Applications

### 1. Topical skin closure

Use a skin adhesive on clean, easily approximated skin edges after the required deeper closure. Port sites and abdominal incisions are common examples. Do not generalize this to all penile, vulvar or perineal incisions: Dermabond Advanced must not be placed on mucosa, across mucocutaneous junctions, or on skin regularly exposed to body fluids; repeated moisture and friction also limit use. Select the actual skin site and product accordingly.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The polymerized film requires no routine removal. Dermabond Advanced instructions permit gentle showering as directed by the surgeon, but only transient wetting: no scrubbing, soaking or swimming while the film remains. This is not an immediate waterproof guarantee for every adhesive or operation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

### 2. Off-label and investigational uses

- **Sutureless circumcision:** in a non-randomized pediatric series, 2-octyl cyanoacrylate approximation of the skin edges shortened operative time (18.4 versus 28.6 min) with complication rates not significantly different from suture closure.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> The closure line here abuts the mucocutaneous junction and body fluids, which lies outside the Dermabond Advanced label. Treat this as an individual-surgeon technique.
- **Mucosal, fistula-tract and lymphocele applications:** n-butyl products (Histoacryl, Glubran 2) are used endoscopically for gastric varices and in interventional radiology. Reports in fistula-tract and lymphocele sealing are case-level and off-label; the skin-adhesive labels prohibit internal application.
- **Graft or flap fixation:** not a labeled use of any skin adhesive. Subcutaneous or mucosal placement risks a foreign-body reaction and thermal injury.

### 3. Interventional radiology (not surgical)

- **N-butyl cyanoacrylate** is used by IR for vessel embolization (for example AVMs; embolization products are separate from skin-closure products)
- Not applied intraoperatively by the reconstructive surgeon but worth knowing in overlap cases

## Application Technique (Topical Skin)

1. Complete the necessary deep closure and remove skin tension; an adhesive does not replace required deep dermal stitches.
2. Cleanse the wound, achieve hemostasis, dry the skin and approximate the edges.
3. Follow the exact applicator instructions. Dermabond Advanced uses one continuous layer, not a routine second coat; hold the edges together for about 60 seconds after application. Other formulations have different layer instructions.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
4. Keep adhesive on the surface rather than inside the wound. Avoid excess applicator pressure, runoff and pooling.
5. Wait for full polymerization before applying a protective dry dressing. Do not apply ointments or liquid medications over the film, which can weaken adhesion.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Contraindications and Cautions

- **Skin-adhesive indications are product-specific.** Do not use Dermabond Advanced on mucosa, mucocutaneous junctions, regularly wet skin, or within the wound or body cavity.
- **Infection, gangrene and decubitus wounds:** do not seal these with Dermabond Advanced. The Histoacryl label additionally excludes patients with systemic infection, uncontrolled diabetes or conditions impairing wound healing, and high-tension wounds.
- **Below the skin surface and internal tissue:** the polymer is not absorbed and causes a foreign-body reaction. Dermabond Advanced must not be used subcutaneously, and the Histoacryl label prohibits application to internal organs, vessels, nerve tissue, mucosa, mucocutaneous junctions or dense hair. Internal use of a cyanoacrylate is off-label in the US, apart from products specifically cleared for it.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Dense hair:** avoid on skin with dense natural hair (Dermabond Advanced label).
- **Allergy:** check for cyanoacrylate, formaldehyde and benzalkonium chloride hypersensitivity for Dermabond Advanced.
- **Heat and wet preparations:** polymerization releases heat and is accelerated by water or alcohol-containing fluid; both labels warn against wet wounds. Allow preparation solutions to dry, apply a thin single film (heavy application can cause thermal tissue injury and delayed healing) and avoid pooled adhesive; the label does not establish spontaneous ignition as the routine mechanism.
- **Eye protection:** prevent runoff toward the eye; eyelids have been sealed shut, occasionally requiring surgical release. If contact occurs, flush with saline or water, apply ophthalmic ointment to loosen the bond, and obtain ophthalmology review. Do not force the eye open.
- **Tension, moisture and friction:** inadequate support or repeated wetting can cause dehiscence. Dermabond Advanced is not a substitute for a suitable deeper closure.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Choosing a Closure Method

Adhesives can reduce closure time and avoid suture removal in appropriate wounds. The choice depends on tension, location, contamination, skin condition and the particular product. The evidence does not justify calling adhesive strips universally cosmetically inferior or ranking infection risk across all three closure methods.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
