# PEG-Based Sealants — CoSeal and Related Products

**PEG-containing sealants are not one interchangeable product class.** Their chemistry, approved tissues, swelling, application and contraindications differ. CoSeal forms a synthetic mechanical barrier; products combining PEG with a biologic protein have different source and allergy considerations.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Product Distinctions

| Product | Relevant distinction (manufacturer labeling) |
|---|---|
| **CoSeal** (Baxter) | Two synthetic PEGs; US indication is adjunctive hemostasis by mechanically sealing leakage in vascular reconstructions; no known contraindications listed<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **DuraSeal** (Integra) | PEG hydrogel for cranial dural repair, adjunct to sutured repair with autologous duraplasty; swelling up to 50% linear; not for confined bony structures with nerves; absorbed in about 4–8 weeks<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Progel Pleural Air Leak Sealant** (BD) | PEG plus **human serum albumin**; visceral pleura after lung resection only; contraindicated with HSA or component allergy or insufficient renal capacity to clear the PEG load<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Progel Platinum** (BD) | PEG plus **recombinant human albumin** (rice-derived); same pleural indication; rice/rHA allergy is a contraindication<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |

CoSeal contains no thrombin. Its hemostatic effect is mechanical sealing, and it does not replace sutures, staples or vascular repair.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Chemistry, approved tissue and precautions differ between products, so do not transfer one label to another. Progel is restricted to visceral pleura and is not a GU sealant.

## Composition and Mechanism

- CoSeal is supplied as two PEG powders and two buffer solutions. Syringe-to-syringe mixing gives two dissolved PEGs that are co-extruded through the applicator.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- The mixed PEGs form a hydrogel that adheres to tissue and synthetic graft and bonds to itself. The FDA summary reports gel formation within seconds and resorption over several weeks (in vitro gel time under 3 seconds; resorbed in under 30 days).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- DuraSeal degrades by hydrolysis of ester bonds; the degradation products are excreted. Its FDA summary reports up to 200% volumetric swelling.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Implications for GU Reconstruction

Approvals for vascular, lung or dural sealing do not establish prevention of urinary leaks. Urinary-tract anastomotic or suture-line reinforcement (ureteroenteric anastomosis, cystotomy, fistula repair) is off-label, and controlled urologic data are lacking. Use it only with a procedure-specific rationale, as an adjunct to a sound, tension-free tissue repair. Swelling is the main concern near the ureter, urethra and pelvic nerves and in the presacral space, so keep the layer thin and avoid a compressive mass.

For patients who decline blood or animal-derived components, confirm the exact product composition and the individual patient's choices. CoSeal is fully synthetic, but Progel contains albumin, and a religious identity does not establish acceptance of every product.

## CoSeal Handling and Safety (manufacturer IFU)

- Aspirate excess blood and blot or air-dry the surface first.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Spray a **thin, continuous layer**, about 1 mm (1 mL per 10 cm²). Hold the spray tip about 10 cm from the site and overlap passes slightly. Use the minimum needed to seal.
- CoSeal **swells up to four times its volume within 24 hours**, with further swelling as the gel resorbs. Consider the maximum swell volume and its effect on compression-sensitive structures; use a thin layer in compression-sensitive cavities.
- Wait at least **60 seconds** before restoring circulation, irrigating, blotting or touching the sealant. If it stays watery and does not gel in about 30 seconds, flush with saline and aspirate.
- Do not inject into vessels. Do not use as an adhesive, even temporarily, for any object; do not cover devices that will need removal; do not lay objects on it until it is non-tacky.
- Pressurized-gas spray systems carry risks of air embolism, tissue rupture and gas entrapment; follow the applicator instructions.
- Safety and performance are not established in children or pregnant women. Experience is limited to 2–16 mL per patient; safety above 16 mL has not been evaluated. Animal testing showed mild skin sensitization.
- The IFU lists no known contraindications and no drug or blood-product interaction.
- Efficacy: in the pivotal randomized trial (n=148, vascular graft suture lines) sealing within 10 minutes was 81% with CoSeal versus 78% with gelatin sponge/thrombin, with more immediate sealing (32% vs 16%).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Practical Selection

Choose a sealant for a defined tissue and purpose, using its current instructions and the evidence for the actual operation. Synthetic composition does not by itself prove superior hemostasis, urinary-leak prevention or allergy safety.
