# Gelfoam and Surgiflo — Gelatin-Based Hemostatic Matrices

**Gelatin-based hemostatic matrices** are absorbable porcine-gelatin products that provide a physical scaffold and absorb blood (the Gelfoam sponge absorbs up to 45× its weight of whole blood). Platelet-mediated clotting has been proposed for Gelfoam, but its mode of action is not fully understood. Available as sponges (Gelfoam) for sheet application and as flowable gel (Surgiflo) for irregular cavities and minimally invasive delivery.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Gelatin matrices are frequently combined with topical thrombin to convert a passive matrix into an active hemostatic agent.

---

## Mechanism

- **Porcine-gelatin foam** provides a physical scaffold; the Gelfoam sponge absorbs up to 45× its weight of whole blood, and its capacity rises with sponge size<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Platelet contribution** has been proposed: platelets contacting the sponge’s interstices may release thromboplastin; the label says the effect appears more physical than an alteration of coagulation<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Mechanical tamponade** of the bleeding surface
- **Absorbed in 4–6 weeks** when placed in soft tissue (amount, blood saturation and site all affect this); on nasal, rectal or vaginal mucosa it liquefies within 2–5 days<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>. Spongostan (sponge and powder) and Surgiflo are both labeled as absorbed in about 4–6 weeks<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Can be saturated with topical thrombin before application to add direct coagulation-cascade activation

## GU Applications

### Gelfoam sponge
- **Exposed bone bleeding.** Apply a minimal piece to the pubic bone (including after pubectomy for UPF), iliac bone, or sacrum; avoid packing bony cavities or foramina, and remove excess after hemostasis.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Deep pelvic dissection.** For pelvic sidewall or presacral venous-plexus bleeding, apply the sponge to the visualized bleeding surface when feasible; do not place it inside a vessel, and avoid positioning that creates negative peripheral venous pressure.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Retroperitoneal or renal bed.** After partial nephrectomy
- **Donor bed** after flap harvest (gracilis, ALT)

### Gelfoam + topical thrombin (slurry)
- Gelfoam sponge or powder saturated with reconstituted topical thrombin produces an active hemostatic matrix
- Often chosen when a passive matrix alone is unlikely to be enough, such as diffuse oozing in an anticoagulated patient. Assess and treat clinically important systemic coagulopathy as indicated: the label does not recommend gelatin sponge as primary treatment of a coagulation disorder, and combined use with thrombin has not been tested in controlled trials<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Used by some surgeons as a cautery-free option near the neurovascular bundle during RARP (operative technique; not a labeled use)

### Surgiflo (flowable)
- **Irregular cavity bleeding.** A syringe delivers flowable gel into spaces not accessible to sponge application
- **Laparoscopic or robotic application.** Delivered through trocar-compatible applicator
- **Not for pumping arterial bleeding**, where blood has pooled, or where the bleeding point is submerged; it does not act as a tampon or plug<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Partial nephrectomy.** Apply to residual bleeding in the renal parenchymal defect after tumor excision when conventional control is ineffective or impractical; do not leave the matrix in the renal pelvis or ureters. A 2026 systematic review of topical agents added to renorrhaphy found no demonstrated benefit for routine adjunctive use, with insufficient evidence on selective rescue use.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Technique Pearls

- **Visualize the bleeding site**, then use the minimum amount needed. The sponge may be applied dry or wet; a first piece usually controls bleeding, and if not, repeat with a fresh piece<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Wet application:** immerse in sterile saline or thrombin, squeeze out air bubbles between gloved fingers, and confirm it re-expands before use. Reconstitute thrombin as its own label directs<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- The sponge may be left in place, and the wound closed over it, when necessary. Left on mucosa, it liquefies on its own<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Hold moderate pressure until hemostasis; moisten an adherent overlying pledget with saline before removal to avoid disrupting the clot.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Do not over-pack; remove excess. Remove from confined bony or nerve spaces after hemostasis whenever possible because expansion can cause neural injury.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Avoid placing in infected fields** and use caution in contaminated ones: gelatin can form a nidus for infection and abscess. Do not saturate it with antibiotic solution or dust it with antibiotic powder. If infection or abscess develops around it, re-operation may be needed to remove it<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

- **Persistent foreign body** for 4–6 weeks. Residual material can look like a pseudotumor or pseudoabscess on follow-up imaging and may prompt unnecessary reintervention<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Collecting system:** the Surgiflo label states it should not be left in the renal pelvis or ureters, because of potential foci for calculus formation; the Gelfoam sponge label has no equivalent statement, but the same caution is reasonable for any retained gelatin<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Swelling:** the sponge re-expands on absorbing fluid and can compress adjacent structures. Avoid packing bony cavities or foramina, and remove excess after hemostasis<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>. Surgiflo incorporated in a clot may swell up to 20% on contact with more fluid<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Bleeding it is meant for:** the label covers capillary, venous and arteriolar bleeding when ligature or other conventional control is ineffective or impractical. It does not substitute for ligating or suturing a named vessel<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Gelfoam sponge contraindications (label):** closure of skin incisions (it interposes between skin edges), intravascular placement (embolization risk) and known porcine-collagen allergy. Anaphylaxis has been reported.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Surgiflo kits with human thrombin:** EVITHROM is contraindicated in patients with a history of anaphylactic or severe systemic reaction to EVITHROM or human blood products; as a human plasma-derived product, it may transmit infectious agents despite risk-reduction measures.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Other label cautions:** not for postpartum bleeding or menorrhagia; not with autologous blood salvage circuits; not with methylmethacrylate cement. Avoid patient positioning that produces negative peripheral venous pressure, which has been linked to intravascular migration<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Porcine origin.** Not suitable for patients with strict dietary or religious objections to porcine products

## Gelfoam vs. Surgicel vs. Arista vs. FloSeal

| Feature | Gelfoam | Surgicel | Arista AH | FloSeal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Source** | Porcine gelatin | Plant cellulose | Plant starch | Bovine gelatin + thrombin (current US Recothrom formulation available) |
| **Mechanism** | Passive + platelet contact | Passive + acidic pH | Passive + water absorption | Active (thrombin) |
| **Form** | Sponge / powder / flowable | Fabric / fibril / powder | Powder | Flowable gel |
| **Resorption** | 4–6 weeks | Not fixed by label (amount, saturation, tissue bed); about 7–14 days commonly quoted | 24–48 hours | 6–8 weeks |
| **Coagulopathy considerations** | Native clotting needed; thrombin may assist | Native clotting needed | Native clotting needed | Thrombin may assist; not systemic reversal |
| **Cost** | Low | Low–moderate | Moderate | High |
