# Hemostatic Agents

Topical hemostatic agents applied to the surgical field to **control bleeding that cannot be addressed by sutures, cautery, or simple pressure** — typically diffuse oozing from raw surfaces, capillary bleeding, or venous bleeding on tissue that won't tolerate cautery. They are divided by mechanism into **passive matrices** (mechanically enhance clot formation) and **active agents** (direct biochemical effect on the coagulation cascade).

---

## Mechanistic Classification

### Passive hemostatic matrices

Porous or fibrous matrices that **physically contact blood and platelets**, promoting fibrin deposition and mechanical clot formation. Depend on the patient's platelets and clotting substrate to differing degrees; they do not correct systemic coagulopathy.

- **[Oxidized regenerated cellulose](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/intraoperative-adjuncts/hemostatic-agents/surgicel)** (Surgicel) — plant-cellulose-derived; contact activation of platelets
- **[Microporous polysaccharide hemospheres](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/intraoperative-adjuncts/hemostatic-agents/arista)** (Arista AH) — plant-starch-derived; absorbs water rapidly to concentrate clotting factors
- **[Gelatin matrices](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/intraoperative-adjuncts/hemostatic-agents/gelfoam)** (Gelfoam sponge; Surgiflo flowable) — porcine gelatin; mechanical tamponade + platelet activation. Gelfoam and Surgiflo can be saturated with topical thrombin, which converts them into a thrombin-augmented matrix.

### Active hemostatic agents

Contain **thrombin or other active coagulation factors** that directly catalyze fibrin formation. May assist local clot formation in some coagulation defects, but thrombin alone still requires fibrinogen and is not a substitute for systemic reversal.

- **[Topical thrombin](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/intraoperative-adjuncts/hemostatic-agents/topical-thrombin)** (Evithrom, Recothrom) — bovine-, pooled-human-, or recombinant-sourced thrombin
- **[FloSeal](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/intraoperative-adjuncts/hemostatic-agents/floseal)** — flowable gelatin matrix combined with thrombin; supplied with human or recombinant (Recothrom) thrombin depending on kit (passive + active hybrid)

### Agent comparison

| Agent | Composition | Mechanism | Absorption (per child pages/labeling) | Key warnings |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Surgicel** (ORC) | Plant cellulose | Passive; acidic pH activates platelets | Not fixed by label (amount, saturation, tissue bed); about 7–14 days commonly quoted | Inactivates thrombin (do not combine); remove near spinal cord, optic nerve/chiasm and other vulnerable structures; not intravascular<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Arista AH** (MPH) | Plant starch | Passive; absorbs water, concentrates clotting factors | 24–48 h (amylase-degraded; longer in neonates and infants) | Label indication: any surgical field except neurological and ophthalmic; embolization/death if placed in a vessel; swells on contact, so remove excess; do not leave in renal pelvis or ureters<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Gelfoam / Surgiflo** | Porcine gelatin | Passive tamponade + platelet contact; active if thrombin-soaked | 4–6 weeks | Swelling in confined spaces; avoid infected fields; Surgiflo not for pumping arterial bleeding and not to be left in the renal pelvis or ureters<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| **FloSeal** | Bovine gelatin granules + thrombin (human or recombinant Recothrom, by kit) | Hybrid passive + active | 6–8 weeks | About 10–20% swelling (about 20% in the human-thrombin IFU); bovine allergy; hamster-protein hypersensitivity (Recothrom kit); human-plasma infectious-agent precautions (human-thrombin kit); not intravascular; not for infection<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Topical thrombin** | Bovine, pooled human or recombinant | Active; fibrinogen to fibrin | Not applicable (liquid) | Never intravascular; requires local fibrinogen; hypersensitivity |

Passive agents depend on the patient's own platelets and fibrinogen. Thrombin-containing agents act locally but are not a substitute for correcting systemic coagulopathy.

### Combination / advanced sealing products

Discussed under [Tissue Sealants & Adhesives](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/intraoperative-adjuncts/tissue-sealants):

- Fibrin sealants (Tisseel, Evicel) — fibrinogen + thrombin
- Collagen-fibrinogen patches (TachoSil, Evarrest) — solid-phase fibrin sealant

## Selection by Clinical Situation

| Situation | Possible adjunct after source control |
|---|---|
| **Broad raw surface, diffuse oozing** (renal bed or pelvic sidewall; avoid intraluminal placement) | FloSeal |
| **Venous bleeding on a pelvic sidewall** | Surgicel or FloSeal |
| **Partial nephrectomy renal bed** | Renorrhaphy and suture control first; an adjunct for residual ooze is reasonable, but routine use has not lowered blood loss, transfusion or bleeding complications in an RCT or meta-analysis<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Bone bleeding (pubic bone, sacrum)** | Bone wax (outside this section) or Gelfoam |
| **Bleeding around a nerve where cautery is contraindicated** | A carefully selected adjunct with a removal plan; swelling can injure nerves |
| **Diffuse capillary ooze from flap donor bed** | Arista AH or Surgicel (in one small RCT of Arista at ALT donor sites, drain output, seroma and hematoma did not differ from controls)<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **Bleeding in a patient on anticoagulation** | FloSeal or a thrombin-augmented gelatin matrix, since passive matrices depend on native clotting; treat as a local adjunct alongside systemic correction |
| **Peritoneum or serosa with persistent ooze** | Thin Surgicel layer + gentle pressure |
| **Urinary tract / mucosa** | Avoid persistent foreign-body matrices; use active agents sparingly; many surgeons avoid cellulose and gelatin in direct urinary contact due to stone-nidus concern; the Arista label warns against leaving it in the renal pelvis or ureters |

## Key Pitfalls

### Urinary tract contact

- **Gelatin matrices (Surgiflo, FloSeal) and Arista carry labeled warnings against leaving them in the renal pelvis or ureters** because of potential foci for calculus formation. Surgicel labeling instead warns that dislodged fragments can plug the urethra, ureter or a catheter
- In partial nephrectomy, place hemostatic agents on the **parenchymal renal bed** rather than within the calyceal reconstruction

### Nerve compression

- **Gelatin matrices, FloSeal and Arista swell** as they absorb fluid, and cellulose products carry labeled removal requirements. Packed against a nerve (e.g., lumbosacral trunk during pelvic dissection) they can cause focal compression injury.
- Follow product-specific removal requirements near confined or vulnerable structures such as bone foramina, the spinal cord and the optic nerve; do not simply pack around a nerve
- **Do not combine Surgicel with topical thrombin.** Its acidic environment inactivates thrombin.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### Mass-effect / foreign body reaction

- Large volumes of Surgicel or gelatin matrices left in the body can produce **organized granuloma** mimicking tumor on surveillance imaging
- Document the location and volume in the operative note

### Thromboembolic risk from intravascular migration

- **Thrombin-containing products must never enter a vessel.** Labeling warns of extensive, potentially fatal intravascular clotting. Arista and Surgicel labels likewise warn against intravascular placement (embolization).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
