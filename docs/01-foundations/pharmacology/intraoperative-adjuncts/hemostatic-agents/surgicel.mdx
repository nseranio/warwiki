# Surgicel — Oxidized Regenerated Cellulose

**Surgicel** is a family of sterile, absorbable hemostats made from oxidized regenerated plant cellulose (ORC), supplied as knitted fabric, coherent Fibrillar fibers, structured nonwoven fabric, or powder. Its products are labeled as adjuncts for capillary, venous, and small-arterial bleeding when ligation or conventional control is impractical or ineffective.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> It works through an acidic blood-cellulose clot and mechanical tamponade, and is one of the oldest and most widely used passive hemostatic agents in surgery (Ethicon / Johnson & Johnson).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Mechanism

- Cellulose fibers are chemically oxidized to introduce carboxylic acid groups, producing a low-pH, locally acidic microenvironment when hydrated
- The acidic pH promotes a gel-like, brown blood-cellulose coagulum as the matrix absorbs blood (the low pH also destroys thrombin; see below)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Mechanical tamponade** provides a secondary hemostatic effect
- The low pH is also bactericidal in vitro, including against MRSA and VRE; this is not a substitute for systemic prophylaxis or treatment of infection<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- The label gives no fixed absorption time: it states absorption depends on the amount used, degree of blood saturation and the tissue bed (and may be prevented in chemically cauterized areas).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Ethicon’s family comparison table lists 7–14 days for Original, Fibrillar, SNoW, Nu-Knit and Powder.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

Surgicel is a passive matrix. It enhances local clot formation but contains no thrombin or other active coagulation factors, so efficacy depends on the patient's native clotting capacity. It is not for large-artery bleeding, non-hemorrhagic serous oozing, or use as an adhesion barrier.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Product Forms

| Form | Texture | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Surgicel Original** | Knitted fabric (pad) | Sheet placement on bleeding surface |
| **Surgicel Fibrillar** | Fluffy cotton-like | Conforming to irregular bleeding surfaces; remove material used for temporary packing after hemostasis |
| **Surgicel SNoW** | Structured non-woven fabric | Endoscopic and laparoscopic use; the label says it may be more convenient than other forms |
| **Surgicel Nu-Knit** | Knitted fabric | Sheet placement; can be cut to size for endoscopic use |
| **Surgicel Powder** | Fine ORC powder; for laparoscopic or other endoscopic use, use only the dedicated endoscopic applicator (fits a 5 mm or larger trocar) | Diffuse oozing: apply dry, use minimal amounts, then irrigate and aspirate excess without disturbing the clot. It can scatter or migrate, and dislodged powder can plug the urethra, ureter, catheter or drain. Do not place it in an open vessel, use it intraluminally, or use it with autologous blood-salvage circuits.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

The manufacturer lists Original, Fibrillar, Nu-Knit and SNoW as cuttable for endoscopic use.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Handling and removal instructions differ by form; read the package insert for the form in hand.

## GU Applications

- **Partial nephrectomy renal bed.** Placement after sliding-clip renorrhaphy for diffuse capillary oozing; keep it out of the collecting system, where fragments can plug the ureter or a catheter (the label reports a blocked ureter after kidney resection)
- **Pelvic sidewall.** After lymphadenectomy for residual oozing
- **Bladder-neck dissection.** Controlling diffuse oozing after radical prostatectomy or bladder-neck reconstruction
- **Urethroplasty donor bed.** Control of bed bleeding at graft harvest sites (buccal mucosa, penile skin)
- **Retropubic space.** After closure of large pelvic dissections
- **Neurovascular-bundle protection during RARP.** Some surgeons place a thin strip over the NVB for hemostasis without cautery-related thermal injury (operative technique, not a labeled use)
- **Scrotal or perineal wounds.** Avoid closing material into an infected or contaminated wound; this is not routine packing after Fournier debridement.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Technique Pearls

- Pre-cut the sheet to the size of the bleeding area; oversize increases residual foreign-body volume
- Apply dry; the hemostatic effect is greater dry, so do not moisten with water or saline; do not pre-treat the site with silver nitrate or other escharotics (they can prevent absorption)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Hold firmly in place until bleeding stops** (commonly a few minutes), then avoid wiping or rinsing it off
- Use the minimum and remove excess before closure; retained bulk can encapsulate and mimic artifacts or masses on imaging; in urologic surgery use minimal amounts and avoid plugging the urethra, ureter or catheter<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Do not apply too tightly as a vascular wrap; stenosis has been reported<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Not a substitute for a suture or clip; control arterial sources first; it is not indicated for large-artery bleeding<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Limitations and Cautions

Do not combine Surgicel with topical thrombin: the low pH destroys thrombin activity and the manufacturer states hemostasis is not enhanced. Do not impregnate it with antibiotics or other agents. Use only the minimum needed; product forms have different handling and removal instructions.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- **Not for packing or wadding** unless it will be removed once hemostasis is achieved; not for implantation in bone defects<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Contaminated or infected fields.** Closing it inside a contaminated wound can cause complications; it is bactericidal in vitro but does not replace drainage or antimicrobials<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Urinary tract.** Fragments can plug the urethra, ureter or catheter; the label reports difficulty passing urine after prostatectomy and one blocked ureter after kidney resection that required postoperative catheterization. Avoid placing it within the collecting system. (The stone-nidus warning appears in the gelatin and Arista labels, not in Surgicel's.)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Foreign-body reaction and imaging mimicry.** Retained Surgicel can encapsulate and mimic tumor recurrence or an abscess on later imaging (reported in case series and case reports); document placement in the operative note<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Heparinized or coagulopathic patients.** As a passive matrix it depends on native clotting; consider an active (thrombin-containing) agent instead, and never add thrombin to Surgicel
- Remove after hemostasis in bony confines or near structures threatened by swelling; the IFU requires removal near foramina in bone, the spinal cord, optic nerve or chiasm and tubular structures that swelling could constrict. Dislodgement and migration have been reported.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Not for non-hemorrhagic serous oozing.** Serum and other non-blood fluids do not produce a satisfactory hemostatic effect<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Not ideal for high-flow bleeding.** Control the source first

## Surgicel vs. Alternatives

| Agent | Mechanism | Best for vs. Surgicel |
|---|---|---|
| **Surgicel** | Passive ORC matrix; appears to aid clot formation physically as it swells into a gelatinous mass when saturated with blood<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | Broad / sheet-like raw surfaces; no coagulopathy |
| **[Gelfoam](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/intraoperative-adjuncts/hemostatic-agents/gelfoam)** | Passive matrix, neutral pH | Bone bleeding; can combine with thrombin |
| **[Arista AH](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/intraoperative-adjuncts/hemostatic-agents/arista)** | Passive, absorbs water → concentrates clotting factors | Diffuse oozing; rapid absorption but excess must still be removed |
| **[FloSeal](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/intraoperative-adjuncts/hemostatic-agents/floseal)** | Active — contains thrombin | Heparinized patient with heavy local bleeding (adjunctive hemostasis)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **[Topical thrombin](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/intraoperative-adjuncts/hemostatic-agents/topical-thrombin)** | Active — pure thrombin | Combined with Gelfoam for targeted activity |
