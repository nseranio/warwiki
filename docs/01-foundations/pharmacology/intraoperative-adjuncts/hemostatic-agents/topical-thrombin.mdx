# Topical Thrombin

**Topical thrombin** is a purified active coagulation factor that converts local fibrinogen to fibrin at a bleeding surface; it bypasses upstream coagulation steps but still requires fibrinogen for clot formation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Available in bovine, pooled-human plasma-derived, and recombinant human sources, topical thrombin is used alone (spray or painted onto a surface), combined with gelatin matrix (Gelfoam and thrombin slurry, or pre-formulated FloSeal), or as a component of fibrin sealants (Tisseel, Evicel).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Sources

| Source | Product names | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Bovine** | Thrombin-JMI | Boxed warning: fatal severe bleeding or thrombosis from antibodies to bovine thrombin or factor V; no re-exposure if antibodies known or suspected<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Human plasma pooled** | Evithrom | Derived from screened human plasma; carries a theoretical infectious-agent risk; contraindicated after anaphylaxis to human blood products<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Recombinant human** | Recothrom | Recombinant CHO-cell production; not human-plasma-derived; hamster/component allergy precautions apply |

Recothrom avoids a human plasma donor supply, but it is produced in a modified hamster-cell line and may contain hamster or snake proteins. Check the local formulary and product-specific allergy precautions.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In a mixed-surgery trial with baseline and day-29 samples, 3/198 (1.5%) Recothrom recipients developed specific anti-product antibodies, while 43/200 (21.5%) bovine-thrombin recipients were antibody-positive after treatment. None of the anti-Recothrom antibodies neutralized native human thrombin; the bovine-product antibodies were not tested for this endpoint.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Mechanism

- Thrombin cleaves fibrinogen directly to fibrin monomer, which polymerizes into insoluble fibrin strands
- Bypasses the entire intrinsic and extrinsic cascade and may assist local hemostasis despite some upstream factor deficiencies, but requires fibrinogen and is not systemic anticoagulant reversal
- Concentration is product-specific. Recothrom and Evithrom are reconstituted to 1,000 units/mL. Thrombin-JMI is labeled at 1,000–2,000 IU/mL (or dry powder on oozing surfaces), with about 100 IU/mL used for plastic surgery and skin grafting<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- In mixed-specialty trials using gelatin sponge, hemostasis at one selected bleeding site by 10 minutes occurred in 95% of both recombinant (N=198) and bovine (N=203) efficacy-evaluable groups, and in 149/153 (97.4%) human versus 148/152 (97.4%) bovine recipients in a separate trial. At 3 minutes in the latter trial, rates were 112/153 (73.2%) and 110/152 (72.4%). These are local hemostasis endpoints, not GU-specific outcomes.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Delivery Modes

### 1. Thrombin alone (spray or painted)

- Spray applicator or saturated pledget
- Painted onto a bleeding surface with a blunt catheter or syringe
- **Limited standalone use.** Better coupled with a matrix to localize and prolong contact

### 2. Thrombin-soaked Gelfoam

The classical combination:

- Reconstitute thrombin per the product label (usually 1,000 units/mL)
- Saturate a Gelfoam sponge in the thrombin solution, kneading out air; Evithrom trials used up to 10 mL per site
- Apply the soaked sponge to the bleeding surface
- Provides the mechanical tamponade of Gelfoam and the active biochemistry of thrombin
- Do not add topical thrombin to oxidized regenerated cellulose (Surgicel): its low pH destroys thrombin activity.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### 3. Gelatin–thrombin matrix (FloSeal)

- See [FloSeal](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/intraoperative-adjuncts/hemostatic-agents/floseal)
- Reconstitute the kit's thrombin and mix it with gelatin granules before applying the flowable matrix.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### 4. Fibrin sealant component

- See [Fibrin sealants (Tisseel, Evicel)](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/intraoperative-adjuncts/tissue-sealants/fibrin-sealants)
- Thrombin and fibrinogen delivered as a dual-syringe spray that forms fibrin instantly at the application site

## GU Applications

- **Neurovascular bundle protection during RARP.** Thrombin-soaked Gelfoam strip placed along the NVB for hemostasis without cautery
- **Partial nephrectomy renal bed.** Thrombin-soaked Gelfoam or FloSeal
- **Post-prostatectomy pelvic sidewall.** Thrombin-soaked pledgets for diffuse oozing
- **Bladder neck reconstruction.** After Y-V plasty or complex repair
- **Bone bleeding** (pubic bone, sacrum). For exposed marrow oozing, apply the minimum amount of thrombin-soaked Gelfoam to the bleeding surface (operative use, not a bone-specific labeled indication); avoid packing or wadding it into a bony cavity and remove excess once hemostasis is achieved because swelling can compress adjacent tissue.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Contraindications and Cautions

- **Never inject intravascularly.** All three labels say topical use only. Entry into the circulation causes extensive intravascular clotting and can be fatal; fatal thrombosis after inadvertent intravenous injection of bovine thrombin has been reported<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Not for brisk arterial bleeding.** All three labels contraindicate use for massive or brisk arterial bleeding; thrombin is for oozing from accessible capillaries and small venules when suture, ligature or cautery is ineffective or impractical<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Bovine thrombin antibodies.** In one mixed-surgery trial, the THROMBIN-JMI label reports 18.4% seroconversion; 43/200 (21.5%) were antibody-positive after treatment. Antibodies to bovine thrombin or factor V (which can cross-react with human factor V) can cause fatal bleeding or thrombosis. Check coagulation studies if unexplained bleeding follows, and do not re-expose. Recombinant or human thrombin reduces this specific concern but does not eliminate hypersensitivity or other adverse events<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Pediatric use.** Recothrom is labeled from age 1 month; Thrombin-JMI safety and effectiveness in children are not established<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Anaphylaxis.** Possible with any thrombin product. Recothrom is contraindicated with known hamster-protein or component hypersensitivity; Thrombin-JMI with hypersensitivity to bovine material; Evithrom with prior anaphylaxis to human blood products.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Fibrinogen deficiency.** Thrombin needs local fibrinogen to form fibrin. A fibrin sealant supplies fibrinogen at the treated surface but does not correct systemic hypofibrinogenemia; assess a significant systemic deficit separately and consider fibrinogen replacement when indicated.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Key Pearl — When to Reach for Thrombin

**Topical thrombin may be an adjunct for oozing (not brisk arterial) bleeding when:**

- The patient is heparinized and passive matrices are inadequate
- Accessible local oozing persists in a patient with cirrhosis, DIC, factor deficiency, or DOAC exposure; use topical thrombin as a local adjunct when standard hemostasis is ineffective or impractical while clinically important systemic coagulopathy or anticoagulant effect is assessed and managed.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- Bleeding is from a raw surface that cannot be cauterized (nerve, major vessel adventitia, bone)
- Gelfoam alone is failing. Saturate it with thrombin to upgrade to an active agent
