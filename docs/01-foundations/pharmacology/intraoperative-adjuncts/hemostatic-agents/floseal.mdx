# FloSeal — Flowable Gelatin-Thrombin Hemostatic Matrix

**FloSeal** is a **flowable hemostatic matrix** that combines **bovine-derived cross-linked gelatin granules** with a **thrombin component** to deliver a hybrid passive-plus-active hemostatic in a single syringe-based product. It is an adjunct for **diffuse oozing on irregular surfaces** — the renal bed after partial nephrectomy, the pelvic sidewall after lymphadenectomy, the retropubic space after complex reconstruction — particularly in patients with impaired clotting capacity (heparinization, coagulopathy).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Composition and Mechanism

| Component | Role |
|---|---|
| **Gelatin matrix (bovine)** | Passive mechanical scaffold; contact-activates platelets; swells to fill irregular cavities |
| **Thrombin** (current US product includes recombinant Recothrom; older kits used plasma-derived human thrombin) | Converts fibrinogen → fibrin directly; bypasses the intrinsic/extrinsic coagulation cascade |
| **Combined effect** | Passive matrix + active thrombin = local clot formation that still requires available fibrinogen; **not treatment for systemic coagulopathy** |

### Mechanism sequence

1. Syringe delivers **flowable gel** onto the bleeding surface
2. **Gelatin granules swell** (about 10–20%, maximal within roughly 10 minutes) as they absorb blood and tissue fluid
3. **Thrombin converts local fibrinogen → fibrin** immediately, catalyzing clot formation
4. **Mechanical tamponade** of the bleeding surface as the gel conforms to irregular anatomy
5. Matrix **resorbs over 6–8 weeks**

## GU Reconstruction Applications

### 1. Partial nephrectomy renal bed

The highest-yield application:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- Combined with **sliding-clip renorrhaphy** (suture hemostasis of the parenchymal bed)
- FloSeal fills the tumor excision defect, controlling diffuse parenchymal oozing after tumor removal
- May assist focal oozing control; routine reductions in warm ischemia time or transfusion are not established by the limited GU evidence cited here
- Used in **both open and robotic partial nephrectomy** — the syringe tip is trocar-compatible

### 2. Pelvic sidewall / lymphadenectomy

- After pelvic lymph node dissection during radical cystectomy, prostatectomy, or deep endometriosis resection
- Controls capillary and small-venous oozing without cautery near iliac vessels or obturator nerve

### 3. Retropubic space

- After complex bladder-neck reconstruction (Y-V plasty, complex pelvic fistula repair)
- After radical prostatectomy when anterior urethral / puboprostatic ligament dissection has created a raw surface

### 4. Deep pelvic dissection

- Presacral venous plexus bleeding — the label warns against inadvertent intravascular uptake when large veins or venous plexi are open, and negative venous pressure (for example from patient positioning) can draw material into the circulation; apply only to the surface, without pressure into open veins
- Pelvic sidewall after extensive dissection
- Post-exenteration bleeding

### 5. High-risk patient populations

- **Heparinized patients** — topical thrombin may assist local control, but anticoagulant effects are not universally bypassed
- **Coagulopathic patients** — local adjunct only; correct clinically important systemic coagulopathy in parallel
- **Radiated tissue** — where tissue quality is poor and standard suturing is unreliable

## Preparation and Application

### Preparation and application

Follow the **specific kit IFU**: current US FloSeal uses Recothrom (recombinant topical thrombin), while older product instructions describe plasma-derived human thrombin. Reconstitution and mixing instructions must match the supplied kit.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Apply directly to the source of bleeding, then remove material not incorporated in the clot with gentle irrigation. Allow the mixed product to stand about 30 seconds before use, do not cut the applicator tip, and do not clear the tip with air. Do not inject or compress into vessels or tissue, apply while a vessel is clamped or bypassed (no active flow), use prophylactically on a nonbleeding surface, close skin incisions with it, or use it in infection (caution in contaminated fields). It is not a substitute for ligature or suture hemostasis, and it is not treatment for a primary coagulation disorder. Consider the matrix's expansion near confined structures and nerves.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### Volume

- Kits are supplied in **5 mL and 10 mL** sizes<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- The volume needed depends on the size of the defect or bleeding surface; a moderate renal bed or sidewall is usually covered by a fraction of a kit, and large irregular cavities may need a full 10 mL kit or repeat application (surgeon judgment, not a labeled dose)

## Advantages

- **Active + passive combined** — the thrombin component works locally, so the matrix can still form clot in anticoagulated patients; it does not correct systemic coagulopathy
- **Conforms to irregular surfaces** — flowable delivery reaches crevices inaccessible to sheet products
- **Trocar-compatible** — direct laparoscopic / robotic application
- **Excellent for parenchymal surfaces** (renal bed, liver in GU-adjacent trauma)

---

## Limitations

- **Cost** — FloSeal is substantially more expensive than Surgicel or Arista (~$200–500/kit)
- **Swelling** — about 10–20% expansion after application; can compress nerves or narrow spaces (use sparingly and irrigate meticulously near foramina, the spinal canal, or pelvic sidewall nerves)
- **Bovine gelatin origin** — rare anaphylaxis reported, including a prolonged life-threatening reaction to the gelatin component on a partial-nephrectomy renal bed<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>; contraindicated in patients with known bovine allergy; Recothrom also has hamster-protein and component hypersensitivity contraindications
- **Not for intravascular use** — injection or compression into a vessel, or use with no active flow, can cause extensive intravascular clotting, thromboembolism and death
- **Preparation time** — reconstitution takes a few minutes; not instantly available if unanticipated bleeding
- **Urinary-tract collecting-system contact** — as with other hemostatic matrices, avoid direct contact with the urinary collecting system (stone nidus concern); the label states it should not be left in the renal pelvis or ureters

## Evidence Base

FloSeal's pivotal trial was in mixed surgical specialties against a gelatin sponge plus thrombin control; the manufacturer states that safety and effectiveness in urologic procedures have not been established through randomized clinical studies.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Urologic use rests on surgeon series and reviews.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

In partial nephrectomy, a 2026 randomized trial of 178 analyzed robotic cases found no difference in hemoglobin drop (1.7 vs 1.8 g/dL), estimated blood loss or major bleeding between routine hemostatic-agent use and none, and a 2026 meta-analysis of 15 studies (2 RCTs, 13 observational) found no demonstrated benefit of adjunctive agents over renorrhaphy alone, with low-certainty evidence.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Use FloSeal selectively for diffuse oozing that suture and energy control leave behind, rather than routinely. Other specialties' transfusion or reconstruction data do not transfer directly.
