# Hydrodissection

**Hydrodissection**, the submucosal injection of fluid into the vaginal wall to hydraulically separate the vaginal epithelium from the underlying fibromuscular layer, is a commonly used adjunct in vaginal prolapse surgery, with variation in technique and use.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> It serves two purposes at once: it creates a defined dissection plane (between the epithelium and the pubocervical connective tissue anteriorly and the rectovaginal septum posteriorly), and, when a vasoconstrictor is added, it provides tamponade and vasoconstriction that reduce intraoperative blood loss. The 2025 Cochrane review found limited perioperative evidence and did not establish clinically meaningful benefit for most interventions. Small hydrodissection studies do not establish improved repair success.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

This page covers both halves of the topic the bedside decision actually involves: the agent (what to put in the syringe) and the technique (where and how to inject it).

---

## Principle and Purpose

The injected fluid balloons the submucosa and visually delineates the cleavage plane, so dissection follows hydraulics rather than blind sharp cutting. Adding a vasoconstrictor compresses and constricts the submucosal vessels along that plane. The two goals are partly independent: plain saline can separate the plane; vasoconstrictors may modestly reduce blood loss but introduce systemic cardiovascular risk. Neither approach guarantees a correct plane or improved long-term repair.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Technique by Procedure

### Anterior colporrhaphy

After an Allis clamp is placed on the anterior vaginal wall, solution is injected submucosally along the planned incision and laterally into the vesicovaginal space until the tissue balloons and the plane between epithelium and pubocervical connective tissue is visible. A midline vertical incision is carried through the distended epithelium, then dissection is limited to the extent needed for the planned repair. A routine midline colporrhaphy does not require dissection to the arcus tendineus fascia pelvis.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> A randomized trial (46 patients) found hydrodissection produced significantly less bleeding (ΔHb 0.66 versus 1.21 g/dL, p = 0.05) without degrading the plane; histology showed no difference in connective tissue left at the surgical margin.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Posterior colporrhaphy

Subepithelial infiltration can facilitate separation of vaginal epithelium from the supporting fibromuscular layer while protecting the rectum. Routine posterior colporrhaphy does not require full-thickness posterior-wall dissection or opening the pararectal space to the sacrospinous ligament; that exposure belongs to separately indicated procedures.

### Vaginal hysterectomy

A pericervical or intracervical circumferential injection before the initial circumferential incision distends the vesicovaginal and rectovaginal planes and eases entry into the anterior and posterior cul-de-sacs. A hysterectomy meta-analysis of 7 RCTs (455 patients; five vaginal and two abdominal trials) found vasopressin cut estimated blood loss by a mean of 119.85 mL (95% CI −177.55 to −62.14; six trials reporting this outcome).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> An RCT of 8 units intracervical vasopressin reduced blood loss to about 145 versus 266 mL (p = 0.022) but raised mean blood pressure more at 5 minutes and increased postoperative morphine use.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### Colpocleisis (LeFort and total)

In Raju and colleagues’ LeFort colpocleisis technique, lidocaine with epinephrine hydrodissection separates the epithelium from the underlying fibromuscular layer before anterior and posterior rectangular patches are excised and plicated.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> In Linder and colleagues’ total colpocleisis technique, the same injectate precedes circumferential, quadrant-based epithelial removal; serial purse-string sutures then reduce the prolapse.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### Historical vaginal mesh procedures

Published transvaginal POP-mesh techniques describe normal saline infiltration before the vaginal-wall incision in transobturator or transgluteal and single-incision systems; the described dissection depends on the device. These historical techniques do not establish a current US indication for transvaginal POP mesh.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Injectate Agents and Dilutions

| Agent | Typical dilution / dose | Effect on blood loss | Key consideration |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Normal saline alone** | Volume tailored to tissue and procedure | Small-trial blood-loss signal | Baseline; plane separation without vasoconstriction<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Epinephrine-containing injectate** | **1:200,000** (5 µg/mL) was used with bupivacaine in one vaginal-hysterectomy trial | Direct vaginal evidence comparing epinephrine alone with saline is limited | Account for cardiovascular effects and total dose<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[20]](#ref20)</sup> |
| **Ornipressin (Por-8)** | 5 IU in 100 mL saline (0.05 IU/mL); ~80 mL/compartment | Median 35 vs 81 mL saline (p = 0.03) | No measured BP/pulse difference in one small trial; rare-event safety not established<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Vasopressin** | Off-label local injection. Vasostrict is supplied as a 20 units/mL concentrate for dilution and as premixed IV products at 0.2–1 units/mL; its label gives no local-infiltration preparation. Published local concentrations are roughly 0.05–0.3 units/mL (8 units total in one vaginal hysterectomy RCT).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[14]](#ref14)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | ~120 mL mean reduction in a mixed hysterectomy meta-analysis | Serious CV events can occur even with dilute solutions<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Lidocaine + epinephrine** | Standard local-anesthetic concentrations | Analgesia + vasoconstriction | Used in some colpocleisis techniques<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>; calculate cumulative lidocaine exposure and LAST risk from all local anesthetics. For otherwise healthy adults, the Xylocaine with epinephrine label recommends no more than 7 mg/kg lidocaine and generally no more than 500 mg; use a lower patient- or procedure-specific limit when applicable.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |

A review included 9 RCTs (903 participants); its vasoconstrictor meta-analysis of six RCTs (491 participants) found vasoconstrictive agents (vasopressin or ornipressin) reduced blood loss by an overall about 70 mL (95% CI −125 to −14) versus placebo or saline, with substantial heterogeneity in dose and technique.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> For POP surgery specifically, the 2025 Cochrane review pooled two RCTs (146 women) and estimated 28.46 mL less blood loss with vasoconstrictors than with saline (95% CI 22.47–34.45 mL less; moderate-certainty evidence), a difference the authors judged unlikely to be clinically relevant.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Vasopressin

The most studied agent, given by intracervical or pericervical injection. A meta-analysis of 7 hysterectomy RCTs (455 patients overall; 6 RCTs contributing blood-loss data) showed a mean blood-loss reduction of 119.85 mL; a double-blind RCT (117 women) confirmed lower EBL (312 versus 446 mL, p = 0.006) and a smaller hemoglobin drop with no statistically detected increase in pelvic infection (1.6% versus 7.3%).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> It has the most vaginal-surgery trial evidence among these agents, and serious cardiovascular events after local injection have been reported; comparative cardiovascular risk remains unknown.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

### Ornipressin (Por-8)

A synthetic vasopressin analogue with a more selective V1 profile. The only dedicated vaginal-prolapse RCT (80 randomized; 76 analyzed, 5 IU/100 mL, 80 mL per compartment) cut median blood loss from 81 to 35 mL (p = 0.03) with no significant difference in median pre/postoperative blood pressure or pulse; this does not establish superior cardiovascular safety or exclude rare severe events. Availability is jurisdiction-specific.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

### Epinephrine (adrenaline)

Epinephrine 1:200,000 has been studied as part of a bupivacaine injectate for vaginal hysterectomy. A 2025 multicentre randomized trial recruited 156 women to receive bupivacaine plus epinephrine or saline; the combination produced 110 mL less estimated blood loss (p &lt; 0.001) and smaller increases in same-day and next-day pain scores, with no detected difference in hemoglobin or postoperative analgesia.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> Because bupivacaine was also present, the trial does not isolate epinephrine’s effect.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> An older randomized study of vaginal or laparoscopically assisted vaginal hysterectomy found less bleeding with local epinephrine than terlipressin, but more hypertensive reactions and mild arrhythmias.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> In a 60-patient laparoscopic myomectomy RCT, dilute vasopressin and epinephrine had similar blood loss; transient BP and HR increases occurred with epinephrine in 13% versus 0% with vasopressin (p = 0.112).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> The lidocaine-with-epinephrine label (1:200,000 = 5 µg/mL) advises cautious, carefully circumscribed quantities in areas supplied by end arteries or with compromised blood supply, where ischemic injury or necrosis may result.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> Do not extrapolate vaginal infiltration practice to penile blocks, digits, compromised flaps or other end-artery fields; use the relevant procedure and local-anesthetic safety guidance.

### Head-to-head summary

| | Vasopressin | Ornipressin | Epinephrine 1:200,000 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CV observations** | Bradycardia, ischemia and cardiac arrest reported | No significant median pre/postoperative BP or pulse difference; mean intraoperative systolic and diastolic BP was higher with ornipressin in the 76 analyzed women<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[22]](#ref22)</sup> | BP/HR changes and arrhythmia risk depend on exposure |
| **Rare-event certainty** | Serious case reports establish need for precautions | Trial too small to establish absence of serious harms | Standard dilution does not eliminate systemic risk |
| **Availability** | Wide (some countries restrict) | Not in US | US-labeled injectable formulations<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |
| **Evidence in vaginal surgery** | Multiple vaginal RCTs; one mixed-route hysterectomy meta-analysis and one vaginal-surgery meta-analysis pooling vasopressin/ornipressin<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> | Single RCT (80 randomized; 76 analyzed)<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | Vaginal hysterectomy RCTs (see the cited [Epinephrine section](#epinephrine-adrenaline))<sup>[[20]](#ref20)[[21]](#ref21)</sup> |

The evidence does not establish a best safety-to-efficacy agent. Choose according to procedure, cardiovascular risk, availability and the agreed anesthesia and pharmacy protocol; modest blood-loss changes must be weighed against rare serious harms.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Cardiovascular Safety — Vasopressin

The dominant safety concern is vasopressin. Serious events (bradycardia, myocardial ischemia, and cardiac arrest) have been reported even at low concentrations, with cardiac arrest occurring within about 2 minutes of injection in one case report and immediately after injection of 11 units (0.2 units/mL) in another, thought to follow inadvertent intravascular injection or rapid absorption; reported laparoscopic or robotic cases do not establish a comparative risk estimate by surgical approach.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> Practical precautions:<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

- **Aspirate and inject incrementally**; a negative aspiration does not exclude intravascular injection or rapid absorption.
- Use the lowest effective exposure in a defined local protocol. The label indication for vasopressin is IV use in vasodilatory shock, so local infiltration is off-label; a 0.2 units/mL, 11-unit injection caused arrest in a healthy patient, so neither that concentration nor any published total dose is a validated safety threshold.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- Tell the anesthesia team before injecting; have atropine and resuscitation equipment ready.
- Extra caution with cardiovascular or coronary disease. ACOG hysteroscopy guidance discusses cautious vasoconstrictor use in that setting; it does not set colporrhaphy dosing.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

## Does Hydrodissection Harm the Repair Plane?

A recurring worry is that ballooning the submucosa "floats" the fibromuscular layer ("pubocervical fascia," not a true anatomic fascia) off where the surgeon needs it. The small Schwarzman RCT measured a histological surrogate: no difference in loose connective tissue at the surgical margin was detected with versus without hydrodissection (13.6% versus 27.3%, p = 0.46); long-term anatomical and functional outcomes were not assessed.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The practical caveat is volume: over-injection, especially in atrophic postmenopausal tissue, can distort planes and make the correct layer *harder* to find.

---

## Adjacent Considerations

- **Vaginal microcirculation.** In an exploratory study of 10 women undergoing native-tissue anterior or posterior colporrhaphy with saline and 1:200,000 epinephrine infiltration, incident dark-field imaging found lower surgical-site microvascular flow 1 day after surgery and flow comparable to baseline at 6 weeks. Mesh exposure and prolapse recurrence were not measured; the authors proposed studying links between vascular changes and long-term outcomes or vaginal implants in future work.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Practice variation.** A Dutch survey of 133 gynecologists found wide variation in whether and how hydrodissection is used during anterior colporrhaphy, supporting the need to describe the actual technique rather than label it universally standardized.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

---

## Practical Pearls

- **Saline alone is an option.** It can facilitate dissection; adding a vasoconstrictor is an individualized decision.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Incremental injection, limited exposure, communication with anesthesia** whenever vasopressin is used.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Inject to balloon, not to flood.** Enough to define the plane; excess distorts it, worst in atrophic tissue.
- **Match agent to procedure and patient.** Local analgesia, fluid volume and vasoconstriction are separate choices. Do not use the same recipe for every vaginal repair.
