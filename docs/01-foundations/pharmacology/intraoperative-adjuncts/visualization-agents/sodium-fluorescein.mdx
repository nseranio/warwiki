# Sodium Fluorescein

**Sodium fluorescein** is a fluorescent xanthene dye that emits a yellow-green color under blue (490 nm) or ultraviolet light, visible even at very low concentrations in urine. In GU practice it has a niche role as an IV ureteral-identification agent and as a dye for cystoscopic confirmation of ureteral efflux, filling the gap left by indigo carmine shortages and serving as a low-cost alternative to NIR fluorophores in centers without NIR imaging.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Pharmacology

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| **Class** | Xanthene fluorescent dye |
| **Color** | Yellow-green (visible), intensifies under blue/UV light |
| **Route** | IV (studied for ureteral efflux); intravesical use is not standardized |
| **Renal excretion** | Rapid renal excretion → yellow efflux seen at the ureteral orifice about 3–10 minutes after IV dose (median 5 min, range 3–9, in a small pelvic-reconstruction series)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Duration** | Urine stays discolored for 24–36 hours; skin discoloration fades in 6–12 hours<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **FDA approval** | FLUORESCITE 10% (500 mg/5 mL vial): diagnostic fluorescein angiography or angioscopy of the retina and iris vasculature; **GU use is off-label**<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |

---

## GU Applications

### 1. IV ureteral identification during MIS / open pelvic surgery

- **25–100 mg IV (0.25–1 mL of the 10% solution)** produces a bright yellow ureteral jet within minutes, visible with the standard white-light cystoscope
- Particularly useful when indigo carmine is unavailable and NIR imaging is not in place
- Doyle et al. 2015 described 0.25–1 mL of 10% (25–100 mg) IV; Cohen 2017 used 0.5 mL (50 mg) and Grimes 2017 used 25 mg. These are off-label regimens, well below the labeled 500 mg ophthalmic dose.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Comparative data:** fluorescein efflux appeared in a median of 5 minutes versus 70 minutes for oral phenazopyridine (n = 12).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> In the Grimes RCT (n = 130; oral phenazopyridine, IV fluorescein, mannitol distention, saline distention), surgeons rated mannitol bladder distention most satisfactory (median VAS 0 versus 20 for fluorescein; lower is better), with no difference in cystoscopy time or adverse events.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Fluorescein is therefore a reasonable IV option, not the only or best-rated one.

### 2. Intravesical applications

Intravesical use is off-label and lacks a standardized regimen established by the cited IV-efflux studies. Do not transfer an IV or ophthalmic preparation into the bladder without an appropriate local protocol and pharmacy review.

### 3. Perfusion / angiography (limited GU use)

- Widely used in ophthalmology for retinal angiography
- Occasional intraoperative use for flap perfusion assessment in plastic surgery
- Rare in GU reconstruction (ICG preferred for perfusion)

---

## Dosing

- **IV ureteral identification:** 25–100 mg (0.25–1 mL of 10%) IV, through a well-functioning line; published series report doses of 25, 50 and 25–100 mg
- **Intravesical leak testing:** use a locally reviewed protocol; the IV studies do not establish a bladder dose.
- Verify the supplied concentration: FLUORESCITE 10% is 100 mg/mL (500 mg/5 mL vial). The labeled adult angiography dose is 500 mg IV (pediatric 7.7 mg/kg, maximum 500 mg), injected rapidly into an antecubital vein; GU studies used far less.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Adverse Events

- **Skin yellowing.** Transient; the label reports fading in about 6–12 hours. Transient scleral and palmar yellowing occurred in the Doyle series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Yellow urine.** Expected, lasting 24–36 hours per the label<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Nausea, vomiting, GI upset.** Listed as common adverse reactions at the labeled dose; less well characterized at GU doses
- **Hypersensitivity, including fatal anaphylaxis.** Known fluorescein or component hypersensitivity is a contraindication; reactions are unpredictable and more frequent after prior adverse reactions, and the label calls for an emergency tray. Anaphylactic shock has been reported after a low-dose IV injection for cystoscopy (incidence 0.05–1.0% cited across fluorescein uses), so review allergy history first.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Extravasation** can cause severe tissue damage; confirm a functioning IV.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Fluorescent or colorimetric assays may be affected by the dye; notify the laboratory of recent administration if samples are drawn or urine is tested in the following day

## Sodium Fluorescein vs. Indigo Carmine vs. Methylene Blue

| Feature | Sodium Fluorescein | Indigo Carmine | Methylene Blue |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Color** | Yellow-green | Blue-violet | Deep blue |
| **Visible in urine** | Yes, in white light; enhanced under UV/blue | Yes | Yes |
| **Best use** | Alternative to indigo carmine when unavailable | Cystoscopic ureteral-efflux aid | Fistula / orifice / anastomotic leak |
| **Availability** | Consistent | BLUDIGO marketed; check local supply | Consistent |
| **Cost** | Low | Low | Very low |
| **Need for special imaging** | Enhanced by blue filter (optional) | None | None |
