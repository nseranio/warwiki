# Methylene Blue

**Methylene blue** is a visible blue dye and an active drug. The US **PROVAYBLUE** indication is **acquired methemoglobinemia in adults and children**; fistula localization, urinary leak testing and other surgical dye uses are **off-label**. Its redox and monoamine-oxidase effects mean that dye use still requires medication and contraindication review.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Formulation and pharmacology

PROVAYBLUE contains **5 mg/mL**; other products may have different concentrations. Verify the actual vial rather than assuming every injectable product is 1% (10 mg/mL). Methylene blue appears in urine, although urinary color and the timing of efflux can vary. Its visible-light role differs from fluorescence imaging with a compatible camera; an ICG-oriented NIR system is not automatically suitable.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgical applications

### Fistula localization

Dilute intravesical methylene blue can help identify communication between the bladder and vagina. In a double-dye examination, oral phenazopyridine colors upper-tract urine orange while the bladder contains blue solution. **Blue vaginal staining supports a bladder communication; orange leakage without blue suggests a ureteral source.** Mixed colors, urethral leakage, contamination, intermittent leakage and inadequate filling can complicate interpretation. Swab position alone does not define the entire anatomy, and a negative test does not exclude a fistula. Follow the [clinical fistula evaluation](/docs/clinical-conditions/03f-fistulas/in-females/vesicovaginal) pathway and obtain anatomical confirmation as indicated.

### Urinary anastomosis and bladder leak testing

Gentle filling with saline or a locally specified dilute dye can identify visible extravasation. Select volume and pressure for the repair; a universal 200–300 mL fill is inappropriate for every reconstructed bladder or urethra. Neither a negative leak test nor a visible ureteral jet excludes later ischemic failure.

### Ureteral visualization

IV methylene blue has been studied as an off-label aid, including fluorescence imaging in digestive surgery. The WSES guideline describes limited clinical evidence; use depends on the intended imaging method, patient safety and local protocol.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Concentrated intravesical dye can obscure the field and is not a universal way to “darken the mucosa” to find orifices. Do not use an unverified intraventricular or intrathecal dye technique.

## Labeled methemoglobinemia treatment

For **PROVAYBLUE**, the usual label regimen is **1 mg/kg IV over 5–30 minutes**. One additional **1 mg/kg** dose may be given after one hour if methemoglobin remains above 30% or symptoms persist. Persistent disease after two doses requires an alternative approach and expert toxicology/hematology support. For **eGFR 15–59 mL/min/1.73 m²**, the label recommends **a single 1 mg/kg dose**; do not automatically repeat. If dilution is needed, use the label's **5% dextrose** instructions; sodium chloride can reduce solubility. These IV treatment instructions are separate from off-label bladder instillation protocols.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Contraindications and important precautions

- **G6PD deficiency is a contraindication**, as are severe hypersensitivity reactions to methylene blue or another thiazine dye. Do not portray an unvalidated low-dose dye regimen as an established exception.
- **Boxed warning: serotonin syndrome**, potentially fatal, with serotonergic drugs and opioids. Review SSRIs, SNRIs, MAO inhibitors and other interacting drugs with anesthesia/pharmacy. Avoid concomitant use; urgent unavoidable treatment requires the label's monitoring precautions. The label advises avoiding serotonergic drugs for **72 hours after the last dose**. Do not assume local instillation or a small dose is interaction-free.
- **Hemolysis** may occur, sometimes delayed; efficacy can also be limited in some causes of methemoglobinemia.
- Pregnancy requires an individualized benefit/risk assessment; the label includes pediatric treatment rather than a universal “never IV in neonates” rule. Breastfeeding interruption for up to **8 days** is advised after treatment.
- Renal impairment increases exposure, and hepatic impairment may prolong toxicity and interaction risk.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Monitoring and practical points

Methylene blue may cause an artifactual **decrease**, not increase, in pulse-oximeter saturation. Inform anesthesia, assess for true hypoxemia and use an appropriate alternative measurement when needed. Do not dismiss a low reading solely because dye was given. Urine color and some laboratory assays can also be affected. Record the product, dose, concentration and route; use a reviewed local dilution protocol for surgical applications.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
