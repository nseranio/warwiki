# Contigen — Glutaraldehyde Cross-Linked Bovine Collagen (GAX-Collagen)

**Contigen** (C.R. Bard, Covington, GA) was the **first FDA-approved injectable urethral bulking agent** (October **1993**) — glutaraldehyde cross-linked bovine dermal collagen (**GAX-collagen**) for female SUI. It served as the **gold-standard comparator** in virtually every subsequent bulking-agent pivotal trial until the manufacturer **voluntarily discontinued production in 2011** as non-immunogenic alternatives entered the market.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Composition &amp; Material Properties

- **Active**: highly purified bovine dermal collagen cross-linked with glutaraldehyde.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Composition**: ≥ 95% **type I**, 1–5% **type III** collagen.
- **Carrier**: phosphate-buffered physiological saline.
- **Biodegradable**: implant gradually resorbed and replaced by host tissue.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Xenogeneic / immunogenic**: skin testing **mandatory** before injection.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Glutaraldehyde cross-linking** improved durability and reduced (but did not eliminate) immune-type reactions.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Mechanism

Periurethral / transurethral submucosal injection at the bladder neck and proximal urethra creates immediate bulk that improves coaptation — urodynamically increasing opening pressure without changing urethral closure pressure.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

Remodeling:<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

- Host fibroblasts invade the bovine collagen implant.
- New **human type I and III collagen** is laid down, replacing the implant.
- **Minimal localized inflammatory reaction** without granuloma formation (unlike PTFE).
- Implant does not hinder subsequent reimplantation surgery.

The biodegradation is also the fundamental limitation — as the implant is resorbed, the bulking effect diminishes and incontinence recurs.

## Skin-Testing Requirement

Unique among bulking agents:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

- **Positive skin test in ~3–3.8%** of candidates → ineligible.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **~28%** of treated patients developed anti-bovine type I collagen antibodies (predominantly IgG; IgA ~40%; no IgE).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Delayed hypersensitivity** in up to **2.5%** despite negative skin test — could cause prolonged urinary retention.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **HLA-DR2/DR4** phenotype associated with positive skin-test responses.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Dermatomyositis / polymyositis**: historical cohort (~345,000 bovine-collagen implant recipients) found statistically increased incidence (SIR 18.8; 95% CI 8.1–37.0).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

The 4-week wait between skin test and treatment was a significant logistical disadvantage compared to non-immunogenic alternatives.

## Injection Technique

Transurethral or periurethral under cystoscopic guidance:<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- Local anesthesia; office / outpatient ~30 minutes.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Volume**: mean **6.2–6.6 mL per course** — the highest of any bulking agent.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- **Sessions**: typically **2–3 in women, 3–5 in men**; cumulative collagen often substantial (mean 11.9 mL in women achieving continence; up to 28.3 mL in men).<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[18]](#ref18)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

## Indications

**FDA-approved**: female SUI due to intrinsic sphincter deficiency (ISD).<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

**Off-label use**: female SUI with urethral hypermobility, male post-prostatectomy SUI, pediatric VUR.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[20]](#ref20)[[21]](#ref21)[[22]](#ref22)[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

## Clinical Efficacy

### Female SUI — ISD (Primary Indication)

| Study | n | Follow-up | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Smith 1997 | 96 | 14 mo median | **67% continence** (38.3% dry, 28.7% socially continent); 82.9% maintained at 1 yr; mean 2.1 procedures, 11.9 mL.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| Richardson 1995 | 42 | 46 mo mean | 83% cured/improved; median 2 treatments; mean 28.3 cc total.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |
| Cross 1998 | 139 | 18 mo median | 74% substantially improved; 72% continent after ≤ 2 injections.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> |
| Gorton 1999 | 61 | **&gt; 5 yr** | Only **26% subjectively improved**; only 1 completely dry; MUCP &gt; 20 cmH₂O predicted success.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> |
| Chrouser 2004 | 43 | 62 mo median | Only **5% effective** at last follow-up; 9% at 36 mo.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup> |

### Urethral Hypermobility

Bent 2001 (n = 90 with hypermobility): **33% dry + 33% improved** at 12 mo (ITT 21% + 21%); probability of maintaining initial improvement for 12 mo only **44%**.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

### Head-to-Head RCTs — Contigen as the Gold-Standard Comparator

| Comparator | Study | n | Follow-up | Finding |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Macroplastique | **Ghoniem 2009** | 247 | 12 mo | Macroplastique **superior**: dry 36.9% vs 24.8% (p &lt; 0.05); ≥ 1 Stamey 61.5% vs 48%.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup> |
| Durasphere | Lightner 2001 | 355 | 12 mo | Equivalent: 80.3% vs 69.1% ≥ 1 Stamey (NS); Durasphere less volume.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup> |
| Bulkamid | Sokol 2014 | 345 | 12 mo | **Non-inferior** PAHG: 53.2% vs 55.4% ≥ 50% leak reduction; 47.2% vs 50% zero SUI.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup> |
| Coaptite | Mayer 2007 | 296 | 12 mo | Equivalent: 63.4% vs 57.0% ≥ 1 Stamey (NS); CaHA less volume.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |

The Ghoniem 2009 Macroplastique RCT was the **only** trial to demonstrate **statistical superiority** of a comparator over Contigen.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

### Male Post-Prostatectomy SUI

Modest, non-durable:<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[18]](#ref18)[[30]](#ref30)[[31]](#ref31)</sup>

- Westney 2005 (n = 322): 17% complete continence; mean duration of response **6.3 months**; mean 4.37 injections.
- Smith 1998 (n = 62): 38.7% social continence, 8.1% dry; median 4 injections.
- Faerber 1997 (n = 68): 10% cured, 10% greatly improved, 67% minimal/no improvement.
- Post-TURP responded significantly better than post-RP (62.5% vs 35.2%); bladder neck contracture predicted failure.

Authors' conclusion: collagen is a "good option for **short-term therapy**" in post-prostatectomy SUI.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

### Pediatric VUR

Cure 62.7–75% after single injection; 77–89.7% after 2 injections.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)[[7]](#ref7)[[32]](#ref32)</sup> Reunanen 1995: 93.9% at 1 mo declining to 81.8% at 4 yr for simple ureters, only **21.4% at 4 yr** for duplex.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> Superseded by Deflux for this indication.

## Safety Profile

Overall complication rate ~**20%** (Stothers cohort, n = 337):<sup>[[33]](#ref33)</sup>

- **De novo urgency with incontinence**: **12.6%** — most frequent and serious; often **irreversible**.
- Hematuria ~5% (transient); UTI common in long-term FU; transient retention ~1.9%.
- **Delayed hypersensitivity** up to 2.5%; **antibody formation** 28%.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Dermatomyositis / polymyositis** signal (SIR 18.8).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- Rare: sterile abscess at the injection site (requiring drainage); pulmonary embolism (described for periurethral autologous fat, listed as a known periurethral-bulking complication); osteitis pubis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[34]](#ref34)</sup>

## Long-Term Durability — The Critical Limitation

Consistently poor:<sup>[[25]](#ref25)[[26]](#ref26)[[18]](#ref18)[[35]](#ref35)</sup>

- Gorton 1999: **26% improvement at &gt; 5 yr**; 1/53 completely dry.
- Chrouser 2004: **5% effective at 62 mo**.
- Westney 2005 (men): mean response only **6.3 months**.
- *Nature Reviews Urology* 2020 conclusion: "positive outcomes are poorly sustained for more than 6 months."

Biodegradability is the cause — as bovine collagen is resorbed and replaced, bulking diminishes, necessitating repeat injections (2–5 sessions, large cumulative volumes).

| Volume / course | **6.2–6.6 mL** (highest) | ~2–4 mL | ~5–6.3 mL | ~4.0 mL | ~4.8 mL |

## Discontinuation &amp; Legacy

Voluntarily discontinued by C.R. Bard in **2011** — not a safety recall, but declining commercial viability:<sup>[[35]](#ref35)[[13]](#ref13)[[2]](#ref2)[[36]](#ref36)</sup>

- Skin-testing burden and 3–4% exclusion rate.
- Poor long-term durability vs newer agents.
- Large volumes and multiple sessions required.
- Non-immunogenic alternatives (Durasphere 1999, Coaptite 2005, Macroplastique 2006, Bulkamid 2020) eliminated the skin-test requirement.
- BSE-era concerns about bovine-derived products.<sup>[[37]](#ref37)</sup>

Place in history:

- **First FDA-approved urethral bulking agent** — established the minimally invasive injection paradigm.
- **Gold-standard comparator** in virtually every subsequent pivotal trial.
- Its limitations — biodegradability, immunogenicity, poor durability — directly informed the design of every successor.

## Reconstructive-Urology Relevance

Contemporary relevance is forensic / clinical:

- **Adult patients with prior Contigen** may present years later with **recurrent SUI** as the implant is resorbed — typically managed today with reinjection ([Bulkamid](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/bulking-agents/bulkamid)) or progression to midurethral sling.
- Historical Contigen exposure is **not a contraindication** to subsequent surgery — the implant does not hinder reimplantation or sling placement.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
