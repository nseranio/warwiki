# Historical Bulking Agents

**Historical bulking agents** are older or experimental urologic injectables that are no longer in routine use. They matter because patients treated with them years ago still present with late effects. Each article separates historical efficacy reports from safety findings and current options.

| Material | Main reason to revisit the history |
|---|---|
| [Teflon (PTFE / Polytef)](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/bulking-agents/teflon) | Persistent particles, migration and delayed foreign-body masses after incontinence or reflux treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Injected for incontinence from the 1960s and for reflux (STING) from the 1980s. |
| [Contigen (GAX-collagen)](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/bulking-agents/contigen) | Prior bovine-collagen injections, declining continence benefit and agent-specific reactions. FDA approved in 1993 and withdrawn in 2011.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| [Autologous Fat](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/bulking-agents/autologous-fat) | Failed historical urethral-bulking approach with a fatal embolism in its placebo-controlled trial; distinguish it from cell-based research.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| [Autologous Chondrocytes](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/bulking-agents/autologous-chondrocytes) | Experimental cultured-cartilage implants, their durability and late imaging findings.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |

## Evaluation After Prior Injection

Presentations to expect include the following.

- **Calcified subureteral mounds** (chondrocyte and Deflux implants) that mimic ureterovesical-junction stones on imaging.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Recurrent stress incontinence** after periurethral fat or collagen injection.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **PTFE deposits, PTFE-laden pelvic lymph nodes or a late teflonoma** after prior PTFE injection.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

Document the agent, injection site, timing, repeat treatments and subsequent operations when possible. Current symptoms may reflect recurrent incontinence or reflux, obstruction, inflammation, infection, retained material or an unrelated problem. A visible implant does not by itself establish its function or explain every symptom.

Prior injection alone does not settle eligibility for further reconstruction. Assess tissue quality, obstruction, infection and the planned operation; symptomatic masses or fibrosis can alter the approach. Use the relevant device and clinical pathway rather than an old injection recipe.

See also: [Bulkamid](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/bulking-agents/bulkamid), [Macroplastique](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/bulking-agents/macroplastique), [Deflux](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/bulking-agents/deflux), [Incontinence Procedures](/docs/surgical-techniques/incontinence).
