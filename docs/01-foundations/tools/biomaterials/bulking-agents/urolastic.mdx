# Urolastic — In-Situ Polymerizing Silicone Bulking Implant

**Urolastic** (PDMS-U) is a nonabsorbable silicone gel that polymerizes after periurethral injection to form a solid elastomeric implant.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> It differs from [Macroplastique](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/bulking-agents/macroplastique), whose silicone macroparticles are formed before injection. Observational studies report improvement in stress urinary incontinence (SUI), but also retention, pain, exposure, erosion and excision of implanted material.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Material and Mechanism

The material is injected as a viscous liquid and cross-links within the periurethral tissue. The resulting mass adds bulk that augments urethral coaptation. Because it is a continuous mass rather than discrete particles, it was designed to conform to tissue contours and to limit particle migration. The material is nondegradable, but clinical series report exposure, erosion and material within the bladder, so permanence of the implant does not mean permanent continence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Injection Technique

Published series describe periurethral injection under local anesthesia according to the manufacturer's instructions, without routine cystoscopy. The sites are 10, 2, 4 and 8 o'clock, with 0.5 to 1.25 mL per site.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Injection was performed by physicians certified in the technique.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Reinjection was needed in 16.7% to 35% of patients, with a pooled rate of 20% (95% CI 15% to 24%).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Confirm site, volume and technique against the current manufacturer's instructions for use.

## Indications Studied

Urolastic has been studied in women who are poor candidates for a midurethral sling (MUS). Kowalik and colleagues treated 20 such women, whose reasons were recurrent SUI, prior gynecologic oncologic surgery, neurologic disease with voiding problems, maximal flow below 15 mL/s, or a contraindication to general or regional anesthesia. Post-void residual above 150 mL was the most common adverse event (40%) but persisted in one patient, who preferred catheter management.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> These are study selection criteria. They are not a list of approved indications.

## Efficacy

Report the endpoint and denominator with every rate. Success, dryness and satisfaction are different outcomes.

| Study | Design and population | Findings |
|---|---|---|
| Futyma 2015 | Uncontrolled multicenter study, 105 women, including 91 with recurrent SUI | At 12 months, objective success was reported in 59.3% of the recurrent-SUI group. Ten of 14 women with primary SUI (71.4%) improved.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Kowalik 2018 | Prospective study, 20 women not optimal for MUS; 6 months | Subjective improvement 90%, subjective cure 56%, objective cure 65%.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Futyma 2016 | 66 women with recurrent SUI; 24 months | Objective success was 32.7% and complete dryness 22.4% among those evaluated. Intention-to-treat estimates were 24.2% and 16.7%.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| Capobianco 2018 | Systematic review of five studies; follow-up 6 to 24 months | Pooled objective success was 57% (95% CI 38% to 75%; *I²* = 82.3%), with a range of 32.7% to 67.0%. Subjective improvement exceeded 80% in two cohorts.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Casteleijn 2020 | Cross-sectional study; 110 participants, 87 revisited; median follow-up 25 months | Satisfaction 51%, subjective cure 46% and objective cure 47%. Objective cure worsened with time after treatment.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

The Capobianco estimate is heterogeneous and short-term. It does not establish a durable cure rate or equivalence to another agent.

### Comparison With Midurethral Sling

A prospective two-arm cohort treated 131 women with Urolastic and 153 with MUS. At one year, subjective cure was 101/112 (90%) with MUS versus 40/87 (46%) with Urolastic (adjusted OR 4.9). Objective cure was 98/109 (90%) versus 58/92 (63%) (adjusted OR 5.4). Treatment was not randomized.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

Mean total cost was lower with Urolastic (€3,567 versus €6,688). At a willingness-to-pay of €25,000, MUS had an 84% chance of being cost-effective using the disease-specific quality-of-life measure, and Urolastic a 99% chance using EQ-5D-5L. The economic result therefore depended on the measure chosen.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Safety and Removal

Complication and reintervention rates are the main concern with this agent.

- **2020 cross-sectional cohort.** Retention occurred in 22%, pain in 15% and dyspareunia in 15%. Exposure occurred in 7% and erosion in 5%. Reinjection was performed in 6% and excision of the bulk material in 18%. Most complications were mild and transient, but excision was indicated for severe or persistent pain, exposure or erosion.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **2016 recurrent-SUI series.** Complications occurred in 17/66 (25.8%). An oval-shaped mass of material was found inside the bladder in 3/66 (4.5%). The mechanism (migration, erosion or misplacement) was not established.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **2021 systematic review.** Erosion was reported with Macroplastique, Coaptite and Urolastic, at a rate as high as 24.6% in one Urolastic study. No erosion or migration was reported with Bulkamid. The review found no direct comparisons of agents, so these figures come from different, largely noncomparative series.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

### Learning Curve

A secondary analysis of 203 procedures by nine physicians used predefined acceptable failure rates for overall complications, retention and excision. Five physicians performed at least 20 procedures. Two reached the competence threshold, one at procedure 20 and one at procedure 40. Procedure number was not significantly associated with complications. Technique varied between physicians, and retrospective underreporting is possible.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Guideline Position

The AUA/SUFU 2023 female SUI guideline does not mention Urolastic. It states that bulking agents are viable treatments, that retreatment is common, and that data are inadequate to recommend one injectable agent over another. Calcium hydroxylapatite, polydimethylsiloxane and polyacrylamide hydrogel have persistence data at 73.2, 83 and 96 months, respectively. Erosion was not present with polyacrylamide hydrogel in the studies cited.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Regulatory Status

The manufacturer's distributor lists Urolastic in Europe.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Confirm CE certification, national authorization and current instructions for use before considering the product. Historical publications do not establish present availability.

## Prior Urolastic Implants

In a patient with prior Urolastic, assess leakage, emptying, pain, hematuria, infection and exposure. Excision may be needed for severe or persistent pain, exposure or erosion.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
