# Autologous Fat Injection — Historical Bulking Agent

**Periurethral injection of unprocessed or minimally processed autologous fat should not be used as routine treatment for stress urinary incontinence (SUI).** The placebo-controlled trial found no demonstrated benefit over saline and included a fatal pulmonary fat embolism. Its relevance today is understanding prior treatments and distinguishing them from investigational cell-based therapies.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Why the Approach Was Studied

Fat could be harvested from the patient's own subcutaneous tissue and used to increase periurethral bulk. Autologous origin reduces concern about an immune response to foreign donor tissue, but it does not eliminate infection, necrosis, inflammation, embolism or harvest-site harm. The procedure also has costs even when no commercial implant is purchased.

Early uncontrolled series appeared encouraging (table below). The saline arm of the later randomized trial improved at a similar rate, which suggests that placebo response contributed to those results.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

## Technique as Reported

In the early series, fat was harvested from the abdominal wall by liposuction and injected periurethrally under local anesthesia. Injections were repeated at 1- to 3-month intervals in patients who remained incontinent. Su and colleagues injected a mean of 14.8 ± 4.8 cc per patient. The randomized trial allowed up to three injections of the assigned agent.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> These are historical protocols, not current recommendations.

## Uncontrolled Series

| Study | Population and follow-up | Findings |
|---|---|---|
| Santarosa and Blaivas 1994 | 15 women and 6 men with post-prostatectomy incontinence; 12 to 30 months (mean 18) | 10/12 women with ISD improved at one month after the first injection, and 7/9 who continued had lasting improvement after 1 to 4 injections (mean 2.4). Only 1 man, and none of the women with urethral hypermobility, had lasting improvement.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Su 1998 | 26 women with recurrent SUI; at least 12 months | 13 (50%) were dry and 4 (15.4%) improved and satisfied. Six had minor immediate complications (23%). Minimal urethral resistance rose from 0.122 to 0.205 (*p* = 0.023) in the successful group only.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| Dmochowski and Appell 2000 | Review | Fat was initially effective in more than 50% of women, but resorption and fibrous replacement compromised graft stability.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |

None of these series had a placebo group. Post-prostatectomy incontinence responded poorly in the only male data here: 1 of 6 men improved.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## The Placebo-Controlled Trial

Lee and colleagues randomized 68 women, 35 to fat and 33 to saline. Complete three-month results were available for 27 and 29, respectively. The study stopped early after a treatment-related death.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

| Outcome | Fat | Saline | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Patient-reported cure or improvement at three months | 6/27 (22.2%) | 6/29 (20.7%) | No demonstrated benefit; not a 12-month cure rate. |
| Recorded complications per injection | 29/91 (32%) | 11/98 (11%) | Injection-level events across repeated treatments, not unique-patient risk or a patient-level number needed to harm. |
| Urinary retention | Six patients | None | Clinically important harm in a small trial. |
| Fatal pulmonary fat embolism | One patient, three days after her second injection | None reported | A treatment-related death prompted termination. |

The Cochrane review's RR 0.98 referred to failure to improve, not the proportion cured. The trial's small size and early termination limit precision, but the absence of demonstrated benefit together with a fatal complication supports abandoning this technique for routine urethral bulking. Cochrane concluded that the single trial of autologous fat is a reminder that periurethral injection can occasionally cause serious side effects.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The review recorded misplacement during 17/91 fat injections versus 6/98 saline injections, including intraurethral, intravaginal or intravesical placement. These are procedure counts. The review assigned two liposuction-site infections to patients in the saline group.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The 2017 Cochrane article incorporated a trial search ending in November 2010. It remains relevant to this historical technique, but its publication year should not be mistaken for contemporary surveillance of every bulking product.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Embolism and Local Tissue Response

A separate case report described pulmonary embolism after periurethral fat injection; the patient needed ventilatory support for a short time and survived without long-term consequences. The proposed mechanism is direct intravascular injection into the periurethral venous plexus. The two reports establish a serious hazard, not an embolism incidence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

Fat entering the circulation can embolize. Autologous tissue can migrate or cause inflammatory injury. Animal graft experiments explain why the effect fades. In mice, nearly all adipocytes in a graft die within a week except in a superficial surviving zone. A regenerating zone forms new adipocytes, and a central necrotizing zone fails to regenerate and heals by cicatrization.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Grafted fat in athymic mice was gradually replaced by fibrosis, inflammation and vacuolar tissue.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> In a rabbit-ear model, resorption at 24 weeks was 49.4% for fat alone and 17.4% with stromal vascular fraction plus platelet-rich fibrin.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> These are animal data at non-urethral sites. They do not give a human periurethral resorption rate.

Historical studies of ordinary fat injection for male incontinence or VUR were small and unfavorable. In the VUR series of 12 transplant candidates with 17 ureters, only one ureter stopped refluxing. These observations do not support another current urologic indication.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Cell-Based Research Is a Different Intervention

Processed adipose-derived regenerative cells, stromal vascular fraction and cultured muscle-derived cells are heterogeneous investigational products. Some protocols combine cells with adipose tissue or a scaffold. They cannot be grouped together as either the old fat-bulking technique or proven functional sphincter regeneration.

- **ADRESU.** This multicenter single-arm study enrolled 45 men with persistent mild-to-moderate post-prostatectomy SUI. The reported proportion with more than 50% reduction in pad-test leakage at 52 weeks or last assessment within that period was 37.2%. No serious events were attributed to the regenerative cells. Without a control group, this does not establish comparative efficacy or exclude rare harm.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Longer adipose-cell follow-up.** A 13-man series (mean follow-up 69 months, range 55 to 72) reported mean leakage changing from 260.7 to 152.7 g/day overall. The larger quoted reduction, 281.5 to 119.0 g/day, applied only to the ten responders. The other three men did not improve at one year or at the final assessment.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Early phase I and pilot reports.** Six men with post-prostatectomy incontinence received adipose-derived regenerative cells mixed with adipose tissue and improved on 24-hour pad testing during 12 weeks of follow-up; mean maximum urethral closing pressure rose from 44.0 to 63.5 cm H2O.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> In a 10-woman pilot of suburethral stromal vascular fraction with platelet-rich fibrin, the cough stress test was negative in 8, 6 and 4 women at 3, 6 and 9 months. Subjective improvement was reported by 9 women at 3 months and 7 at 9 months, with no adverse events.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Larger muscle-cell trial.** Iltamiocel is not a fat product. A 2024 placebo-controlled study randomized 297 women and did not meet its primary endpoint: at least 50% reduction in stress-leak episodes occurred in 52% versus 53.6% at 12 months. A favorable prior-surgery subgroup warrants further study rather than a general claim that cell therapy regenerates a functional sphincter.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

The EAU 2026 non-neurogenic female LUTS guideline advises caution against autologous fat because of the risk of fatal embolism.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> AUA/SUFU 2023 limits stem-cell therapy for female SUI to investigational protocols. Device clearance for a cell-processing system, autologous origin, or encouraging uncontrolled outcomes does not establish approval or efficacy of a particular SUI treatment.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Fat Injection at Other Sites

Autologous fat injection remains in use for vocal-fold augmentation. Fat there has pliability and vibratory characteristics close to those of vocal-fold tissue. A systematic review of 13 studies (472 patients) found improved maximum phonation time and Voice Handicap Index at 12 months or longer, with heterogeneous techniques and no control groups.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> A prospective series of 22 patients reported a mean Voice Handicap Index of 73.45 before and 44.88 at 36 months, with CT evidence of persisting adipose tissue.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> A retrospective comparison of 72 lipoinjections and 100 type I thyroplasties found similar reoperation rates and improvement at 6 and 12 months.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> A smaller retrospective series (28 patients) found that only medialization laryngoplasty kept a significant improvement over follow-up.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> A meta-analysis of unilateral vocal-fold paralysis reported benefit that could last more than 12 months.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> These results come from a different recipient site and endpoint and do not support repeating the urethral technique.

## Evaluating a Previously Treated Patient

Clarify the original agent and procedure: ordinary fat, a processed cellular preparation and a commercial bulking implant have different evidence and complication profiles. Reassess current incontinence type, emptying, prior operations and tissue condition. Resorption or fibrosis may contribute to recurrence, but should not be assumed to explain every new symptom.

Subsequent management follows current [incontinence pathways](/docs/surgical-techniques/incontinence), with treatment selected for the present findings and patient preferences. Recurrent SUI after periurethral fat is managed like other recurrent SUI, with a sling or a current bulking agent such as [Bulkamid](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/bulking-agents/bulkamid) after evaluation. AUA/SUFU 2023 lists bulking agents as an option for women with insufficient improvement after a prior anti-incontinence procedure.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
