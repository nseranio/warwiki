# Teflon — Polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE / Polytef)

**Polytef paste (PTFE / Teflon)** was the **first injectable bulking agent ever used in urology**, pioneered by **Victor Politano in 1964** for female urinary incontinence and adapted by **Prem Puri in 1981** for endoscopic correction of vesicoureteral reflux (the original **STING** procedure).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> It was **never FDA-approved** for urologic use and has been **largely abandoned worldwide** after the discovery of distant particle migration to the lungs, brain, lymph nodes, kidneys, and spleen.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Composition &amp; Material Properties

- **Chemical**: polytetrafluoroethylene — (C₂F₄)ₙ; produced by pyrolysis of Teflon.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Formulation**: PTFE particles in **glycerine + polysorbate** carrier.
- **Particle size**: heterogeneous, **3–100 μm** — critically, a substantial fraction is **&lt; 50 μm and phagocytosable** by macrophages.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Nonbiodegradable**; non-immunogenic (no skin test required).
- **Hydrophobic**: serum proteins bind instantly and are denatured at the particle surface, amplifying foreign-body response — especially for smaller particles.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

The wide particle size distribution — including a phagocytosable fraction — is the design flaw that distinguishes PTFE from all later-generation bulking agents (Macroplastique &gt; 100 μm, Durasphere 212–500 μm, Coaptite 75–125 μm).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Historical Development

- **1964** — Victor Politano (Miami): first use of an injectable bulking agent for female SUI. 128-patient series accumulated 1964–1991.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **1974** — first formal publication of periurethral PTFE for SUI.
- **1981** — Prem Puri (Dublin) adapts the technique for VUR: the **STING** (Subureteral Transurethral INjection) procedure — the acronym persists today even though the material has been replaced.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **1984** — **Malizia JAMA** documents distant particle migration in animals.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **1984–2003** — continued European/non-US use despite migration concerns; Puri's group accumulates &gt; 12,000 ureters.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Late 1990s–2000s** — progressively replaced by Deflux (FDA 2001) and Contigen/Macroplastique/Bulkamid for SUI; clinical use largely abandoned.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Mechanism

**Permanent, nonbiodegradable bulking** at the injection site. After injection a **foreign-body giant-cell granulomatous reaction** develops around the PTFE particles; the paste undergoes diffuse interstitial splitting with multinucleated giant cells, macrophages, and increased collagen deposition.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Injection Technique

### For Female SUI<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

- Local or brief GA/spinal; outpatient ~30 min.
- Periurethral or transurethral under cystoscopic guidance.
- 18-G needle or 14F angiocatheter.
- **Large volumes**: 5–20 cc per session; cumulative 16–85 mL across multiple injections.
- Lotenfoe modified technique (paste heating + 3–5 d suprapubic catheter) improved cure 11% → 39%.

### For VUR — STING<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

- GA in children; ~15 min day-case.
- **Very small volumes** (&lt; 1 mL).
- Needle at 6 o'clock below the ureteral orifice; "volcano-mound" elevation.

## Clinical Efficacy

### Female SUI

| Study | n | Follow-up | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lopez / Politano 1993 | 128 (1964–1991) | 31 mo mean | **73% improved, 54.3% totally dry**.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Herschorn / Glazer 2000 | 46 | 15.9 mo | 30.4% dry, 41.3% improved; **probability of remaining dry 90% at 1 yr → 60% at 2 yr**.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Lotenfoe 1993 | 21 (type III) | 11.4 mo | Standard 11% / modified 39% cure.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| Kiilholma 1993 | 22 | **5 yr** | Only **18% cure**.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |

### Male Post-Prostatectomy

Notably poor:<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

- Kabalin 1994 (n = 13): only 23% improved; none dry; 16–23 cc per session.
- Stanisic 1991 (n = 20): 35% long-term improvement; only 20% would recommend.

### Pediatric VUR — The Largest Bulking-Agent Experience

| Study | n (ureters) | Follow-up | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Puri / Granata 1998** multicenter survey | **12,251** (8,332 children, 41 centers) | 1–13 yr | **75.3% resolved after 1 injection**; 12% after 2; 4.5% failure → reimplantation; **0.33% VUJ obstruction**; 2.8% recurrence.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| Chertin 2002 long-term | 393 (258 children) | **11–17 yr** | 76.8% after 1 injection; **95% remain reflux-free** at 11–17 yr; 5% recurrence (mostly low grade).<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| Chertin 2003 high-grade | 717 (526 children, grade IV–V) | 11.6 yr median | 58% after 1; **84% after 1–3 injections**; 1.2% recurrence.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| Puri 1995 infants | 75 (52 infants) | 1–9 yr | 65% after 1; **93% remain reflux-free**.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| Yücel 2007 | 48 (38 patients) | 12.5 yr mean | **73% remain reflux-free; 27% recurrence** (median 2 yr; mostly grade IV–V).<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |

## The Migration Problem — The Defining Safety Concern

### Malizia 1984 (JAMA)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Pivotal animal study after periurethral PTFE injection in dogs and monkeys:

- **50–70 d**: particles in **pelvic lymph nodes 6/7, lungs 4/7**.
- **10.5 mo**: particles in **pelvic lymph nodes, lungs, and brain 7/7; kidneys 4/7; spleen 2/7**.
- X-ray microanalysis confirmed PTFE.
- Polytef granulomas at injection sites and at some distant migration sites.
- Conclusion: until long-term human effects are known, **polytef paste should not be used in children or young adults with normal life expectancy**.

### Confirmatory Studies

- **Aaronson 1993**: PTFE migration to **lungs and brain within 2 weeks** in animals; brain particles up to **15 μm** — pulmonary bed an "inefficient filter." Stated the data are a **contraindication to use in children**.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- **Vandenbossche 1993**: PTFE particles in rabbit lungs after bladder submucosal injection.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- **Claes 1989**: First **human** case of clinically significant **pulmonary migration** after periurethral PTFE.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- **Aragona 1997**: **Giant granuloma + pelvic lymph node adenopathy** with heavy multinucleated foreign-body reaction in all 3 children who underwent open surgery after failed STING.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

The mechanism — phagocytosable small particles entering lymphatic and venous channels — directly drove the engineering of all subsequent bulking agents above the 80–100 μm phagocytosis threshold.

## Local Complications — "Teflonoma"

Foreign-body granuloma surrounding PTFE particles:<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[24]](#ref24)[[25]](#ref25)[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

- **Periurethral teflonoma** with **bladder outlet obstruction** — McKinney case **9 years** after injection; obstructing mass was "almost totally composed of foreign-body giant-cell response to PTFE."<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Complete urinary obstruction** from granulomatous reaction (Boykin).<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
- Periurethral granuloma with urethral wall prolapse, periurethral abscess, urethral diverticulum, Teflon cyst, perineal migration with pain, periurethral fistula.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[25]](#ref25)</sup>
- **VUJ obstruction in 0.33%** (41/12,251 ureters) requiring reimplantation.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

### Laryngeal Teflonomas (ENT)

PTFE was also widely used for vocal fold medialization with parallel complications:<sup>[[26]](#ref26)[[27]](#ref27)[[28]](#ref28)[[29]](#ref29)[[30]](#ref30)</sup>

- Wenig 1990: 8 cases of laryngeal/neck teflonomas **clinically simulating malignancy**.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>
- Giant Teflon granuloma → airway obstruction and persistent dysphonia.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>
- Varvares: dysphonia from overinjection, malposition, or the proliferative granulomatous response — **deficits persist despite corrective procedures**.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup>
- Nakayama 1993: review of 28 failures concluded "the success and safety of this treatment have been overstated."<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup>

## Long-Term Durability

**VUR**: 95% reflux-free at 11–17 years in Puri's series<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>; 27% recurrence at 12.5 years in Yücel — higher in grade IV–V.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> Oberritter: **disappearance of the Teflon deposit on ultrasound** was tightly associated with VUR recurrence.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup>

**SUI**: poor — 18% cure at 5 yr (Kiilholma); 90% → 60% dry probability over 1 → 2 yr (Herschorn).<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Regulatory Status

- **Never FDA-approved** for urologic use.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[32]](#ref32)</sup>
- Used extensively in Europe and elsewhere without formal approval in many jurisdictions.
- FDA refusal based on Malizia migration data and subsequent reports.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- PTFE is approved for unrelated applications (e.g., ePTFE vascular grafts) — the injectable particulate paste was never approved for soft-tissue augmentation.
- Clinical use largely abandoned worldwide.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Legacy &amp; Historical Significance

PTFE holds an irreplaceable position in the history of bulking agents:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- **First injectable urologic bulking agent** (1964) — created the field.
- **First agent used for endoscopic VUR correction** (1981) — the STING procedure remains the basis of all endoscopic VUR treatment.
- **Largest VUR experience** of any agent (&gt; 12,000 ureters).
- **Longest follow-up** (17 years).
- **Drove the engineering of all later bulking agents** above the 80–100 μm phagocytosis threshold.
- The **term "STING"** persists in nomenclature today.

## Reconstructive-Urology Relevance

Contemporary relevance is **largely forensic**: adult patients with prior pediatric STING may present with:

- Imaging abnormalities (PTFE deposits, calcified pelvic lymph nodes) of unclear significance.
- Late teflonoma with bladder outlet obstruction (case reports up to 9 years out).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- Recurrent VUR requiring definitive reimplantation.
