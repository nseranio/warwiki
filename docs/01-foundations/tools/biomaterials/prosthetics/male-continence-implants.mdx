# Male Continence Implant Devices (Sling Family)

**Male suburethral sling implants** are fixed-tension or adjustable devices built from polypropylene mesh, silicone cushions and pads, and transobturator or retropubic anchoring. They differ in material, anchoring route and adjustment mechanism. For patient selection, comparative outcomes and implantation, see [Male Urethral Slings](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/male-urethral-slings). The [AUS](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/prosthetics/artificial-urinary-sphincter) and [ProACT balloons](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/prosthetics/proact-balloons) have separate device pages.

The AUA/GURS/SUFU guideline supports discussing male slings for mild-to-moderate SUI and advises against their routine use for severe SUI. For SUI after radiotherapy it favors AUS over slings or adjustable balloons. Statements 20, 21 and 24 are Moderate Recommendations (Grade B or C). None compares the individual fixed and adjustable implants below.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Device Family Overview

| Device | Components and principal action | Postoperative adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| **AdVance / AdVance XP** — Boston Scientific | Polypropylene mesh with two transobturator arms; designed to reposition the bulbar urethra | Fixed after implantation |
| **Virtue** — Coloplast | Four-arm polypropylene sling; two transobturator and two prepubic arms provide elevation and compression | Fixed after implantation |
| **ATOMS** — A.M.I. | Fillable silicone cushion, polypropylene transobturator anchoring and scrotal access port | Clinician adjusts cushion volume through the port |
| **Argus / Argus T** — Promedon | Silicone foam pad, segmented silicone arms and retaining washers; retropubic and transobturator variants | Washer position can be changed, including after implantation, through a procedure to access the hardware |
| **Remeex MRS** — Neomedic | Polypropylene tape connected by traction threads to a suprapubic mechanical regulator (*varitensor*) | Clinician reconnects an external manipulator when later readjustment is needed |

These are passive continence devices; adjustment is a clinician procedure, and the patient does not operate a pump to void. Adjustment is not noninvasive in every device, and adjustable devices still carry revision risk.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup><sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup><sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup><sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup><sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Component and Handling Distinctions

### AdVance and AdVance XP

The XP IFU describes permanent monofilament polypropylene mesh, tissue anchors molded into its arms, integrated tensioning fibers, and removable sheaths and liners. The anchors are molded into the arms rather than harpoon-tipped, and the sling has no reservoir or adjustment port. Anchors were introduced to limit the early sling migration seen with the original AdVance.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Excessive tension can still cause obstruction, retention, pain, erosion and tissue injury. The manufacturer's MR Safe designation applies to the specified sling implant, not to other sling systems that contain metal components. The [AdVance procedure page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/advance-sling) covers selection and implantation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Virtue

Virtue combines transobturator and prepubic arms in a four-arm knitted monofilament polypropylene implant. Its compression and elevation mechanism differs from a two-arm repositioning sling. In the multinational trial, the first (unfixed) cohort had subjective and objective success of 41.9% at 12 months, and the cohort with a fixation technique had 70.9% and 79.2% with a median pad-weight reduction of 88.3%; there was no prolonged retention. Neither FDA clearance nor this uncontrolled trial shows superiority over other implants. See [Virtue Sling](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/virtue-sling).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup><sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### ATOMS

A fillable silicone cushion supports and compresses the bulbospongiosus region. Polypropylene arms provide transobturator fixation, while a scrotal port permits clinician adjustment using the specified noncoring needle. Port construction has changed across generations; identify the implanted model before planning adjustment. Fluid, needle, volume and timing limits come from the IFU for the implanted model. In a 99-man multicenter series (mean follow-up 17.8 months), a mean of 3.8 adjustments were needed, success was 92% (63% dry, 29% improved), and four men (4%) had port-site wound infection requiring explantation. See [ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup><sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

### Argus

Argus uses a silicone foam pad and segmented silicone arms held by washers; the manufacturer describes a mesh-free implant. Classic Argus follows a retropubic route, whereas Argus T uses transobturator fixation. The two routes do not end in the same rectus-fascia plane.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

Postoperative adjustment is possible, but requires access to the washers rather than injection through a scrotal port. In an older retrospective series of 101 men (mean follow-up 2.1 years), 39 (38.6%) had adjustment, 79.2% were dry by pad test, and 16 (15.8%) had the sling removed for erosion or infection, six of them among the first 22 placements. These are early-experience results, not the outlook for a current candidate. See [Argus Sling](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/argus-sling).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### Remeex MRS

The suprapubic regulator changes tension on the suburethral tape. An external manipulator is connected for clinician adjustment and subsequently removed; later adjustment requires reconnection through a procedure. It is not a permanent transcutaneous handle, so readjustment needs skin access. In a 51-man European series (mean follow-up 32 months), 44 needed a second regulation under local anesthesia, 64.7% were cured and 19.6% improved. See [Remeex MRS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/remeex-mrs).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup><sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Mechanism and Materials

| Device | Acts on | Principal mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| AdVance / AdVance XP | Bulbar urethra | Proximal repositioning |
| Virtue | Bulbar urethra | Compression and repositioning (quadratic) |
| ATOMS | Bulbar urethra | Adjustable cushion compression |
| Argus | Bulbar urethra | Static foam-pad compression |
| Remeex | Bulbar urethra (suburethral tape) | Adjustable tape tension via suprapubic regulator |
| ProACT | Bladder neck | Bilateral periurethral balloon compression |
| AMS 800 AUS | Bulbar urethra | Circumferential cuff occlusion |

The main distinctions are compression versus repositioning, and adjustable versus fixed. Polypropylene mesh is used in AdVance, Virtue, the ATOMS anchoring mesh and the Remeex tape. Silicone is used in the ATOMS cushion, the Argus pad and columns, and the ProACT balloons. A titanium port is used in ATOMS and ProACT.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup><sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup><sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup><sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup><sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Regulatory and Material Considerations

AdVance XP and Virtue have FDA 510(k) clearances as Class II male incontinence meshes. This differs from premarket approval of hydraulic continence devices. The cited XP record identifies a US predicate cleared in 2018. The 2021 XP and 2023 Virtue submissions concern particular device changes, not comparative efficacy trials.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup><sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Availability, authorization and instructions vary by country and model. ATOMS, Argus and Remeex are CE-marked and used widely in Europe and Latin America, but current US authorization is not established here. The legacy bone-anchored InVance sling was withdrawn, and I-STOP TOMS is a regional legacy device. A CE mark or an overseas series does not establish US marketing authorization.

Polypropylene is also used in female midurethral slings, but indications, regulatory classifications and restrictions differ. See the [polypropylene mesh page](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/synthetic-meshes/polypropylene) and each implant's own labeling.

## Device-Level Failure Modes

Counsel about infection, pain, retention or obstruction, erosion or extrusion, migration, recurrent incontinence and possible revision or explantation. Port and cushion systems add leakage and port-related problems; mechanical regulators and washers add hardware-access and adjustment issues. Mesh devices carry erosion and extrusion risk, and ATOMS and ProACT share port-pocket infection. Argus washers can slip and the Remeex varitensor can fail mechanically. Trocar passage can injure the bladder or urethra. Device-specific incidence and treatment are on the linked procedure pages.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup><sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup><sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup><sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
