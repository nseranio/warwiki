# Malleable (Semirigid) Penile Prosthesis

A **malleable (semirigid) penile prosthesis** is a pair of implanted rods with a bendable core. It gives support without an inflatable pump or separate reservoir, and the penis is bent upward for intercourse and downward for concealment. It is an option for erectile dysfunction when other treatments are unsuitable or unsuccessful, or when an informed patient prefers definitive prosthetic treatment. Selection depends on anatomy, tissue health, dexterity, expectations, access and cost.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The three-piece [inflatable prosthesis](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/prosthetics/inflatable-penile-prosthesis) is the more common device in the United States. The malleable device is often chosen for poor manual dexterity, infection salvage, ischemic priapism after prolonged (over 48 hours) episodes, gender-affirming phalloplasty, hostile pelvis and cost-limited settings. Outside the United States it is implanted more often than the inflatable device because reimbursement is lacking and it costs less.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup><sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup><sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

For implantation and salvage, see the [Penile Implants atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/penile-implants/).

## History and Evolution

Intracorporeal penile supports began with rib cartilage in a reconstructed phallus after traumatic amputation (Bogaraz, 1935–1936), followed by acrylic and polyethylene devices.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup><sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In 1975, Small and Carrion reported 31 patients with a paired silicone-rod prosthesis, which began the modern era of penile prostheses.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The malleable design then evolved through the current Genesis and Spectra devices, and newer models now include Rigi10, TUBE and the Zephyr ZSI 100 and 100 FTM (designed for phalloplasty).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup><sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

## Construction and Current Models

Malleable designs evolved from early fixed supports and silicone devices to several different flexible cores. Tactra and the older Spectra are distinct designs with different construction and outcomes, and current malleable devices do not share one material or bending mechanism.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup><sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

| Device | Verified construction and size distinctions | Practical boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **Tactra — Boston Scientific** | Nitinol core with dual-layer silicone; diameters 9.5, 11 and 13 mm | Not the articulating-segment Spectra, and not described as Parylene coated. |
| **Genesis — Coloplast** | Silver-wire coil and hydrophilic surface; diameters 9.5, 11 and 13 mm | Follow the product-specific preparation instructions; the surface is hydrophilic. |
| **Rigi10 — Rigicon** | Stainless-steel core, titanium end caps and HydroShield hydrophilic coating; 9, 10, 11, 12, 13 and 14 mm | The manufacturer excludes the 14 mm model from US availability. Size is chosen from corporal anatomy. |
| **TUBE — Promedon** | Silver-wire core, PTFE layer and silicone construction | A separate product from the Shah implant, not a Nitinol device. |

The table reflects current manufacturer descriptions, not comparative trials. Confirm the actual model, local authorization, available sizes and supplied instructions before selection. Devices designed for a neophallus have different anchoring and tissue-support requirements from paired implantation into native corpora.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup><sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup><sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup><sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup><sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## What the Patient Trades Off

Malleable rods stay semirigid and are positioned for intercourse or concealment. They avoid pump cycling, but the patient must still position the implant, so hand function is still assessed. Persistent firmness and concealability are the main counseling points. Malleable implants give no girth or length expansion and look less natural than an inflatable device.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

There are no hydraulic components to leak, but mechanical failure still occurs, as do rod or shell damage, migration, erosion, pain, infection and revision. The device is not a lifetime implant. Omitting the reservoir removes that placement step but not the risk of urethral, corporal, vascular or soft-tissue injury.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup><sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Candidate Selection and Safety

- **ED and patient preference:** discuss malleable, two-piece and three-piece devices in terms of the patient's priorities. Concealability is a preference to weigh and is not a contraindication to malleable implantation.
- **Limited dexterity or difficult reservoir placement:** malleable implantation may simplify operation or avoid a separate reservoir. A two-piece inflatable implant may also be relevant; previous pelvic surgery does not dictate the device.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Infection or tissue compromise:** resolve active infection and assess tissue quality before routine implantation. Salvage of an infected device is a separate operation (see below) and does not make primary implantation through infection safe.
- **Device-specific exclusions:** Tactra safety information contraindicates use where compromised tissue would make constant pressure harmful, in patients requiring repeated endoscopic procedures beyond routine cystoscopy, and outside its stated corporal-length range. Other models have their own exclusions.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Tactra's proximal nonmalleable section must remain proximal to the penoscrotal junction. Follow its marked proximal trimming instructions and avoid bending beyond the natural U shape. These instructions apply to Tactra only; other rod designs have their own trimming and rear-tip extender instructions.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Preparation depends on the surface and exact model. Hydrophilic devices have model-specific wetting or dipping instructions, and no single antibiotic mixture applies to all. The rods are trimmed to the measured corporal length. The approach is penoscrotal, infrapubic or subcoronal. For broader infection-prevention evidence, see the [IPP hub](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/prosthetics/inflatable-penile-prosthesis#infection-prevention).

## Outcomes and Durability

### Malleable Versus Inflatable Devices

A 2026 global EHR analysis of 29,385 men (18,749 inflatable, 10,636 semirigid) reported similar five-year revision or removal for inflatable and semirigid implants: 13.9% versus 13.7% (p = 0.77). Mechanical breakdown was less frequent with semirigid devices (6.7% versus 9.1%), device displacement was less frequent (1.6% versus 2.3%), and explantation was more frequent (8.4% versus 6.5%). Infection did not differ. These are observational, code-based outcomes with unmeasured differences in selection and follow-up. Mechanical failure is reduced but not eliminated with malleable devices.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

Comparative satisfaction studies used different questionnaires, devices and populations. In a study of couples (mean follow-up 68 months), satisfaction was significantly higher with three-piece inflatable than malleable implants (p &lt; 0.001), with no difference by brand; 27 malleable recipients (7.7%) converted to a three-piece device for dissatisfaction, versus 1 (0.2%) of inflatable recipients converted to malleable. Revision for corporal perforation was most frequent with malleable devices (2.6%) and revision for malfunction with three-piece devices (5.5%).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Within malleable devices, Genesis and Spectra had similar EDITS satisfaction (77.1% and 75.6%, p = 0.50, n = 60), and in 46 men with Spectra (mean follow-up 3.2 years) IIEF-5 rose from 5.86 to 22.5 and EDITS was 71.1.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup><sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> Ask about concealment, rigidity, comfort and ease of use rather than quoting one satisfaction percentage.

### Model-Specific Reports

The Rigi10 report included 605 first-time patients (46 surgeons, 15 countries), with revision or explantation in 6 (0.99%), infection in 2 (0.33%), erosion in 1, and an estimated 99.2% freedom from revision at three years. Mean follow-up was about 22 months. It used manufacturer patient-information forms and had no comparator, so it is an early device-surveillance estimate.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

### Length Expectations

In Habous's 133 patients (88 malleable, 45 inflatable), erect length increased by 0.22 cm and girth by 0.7 cm with malleable devices, and by 0.62 cm and 1.7 cm with inflatable devices (p &lt; 0.05), measured against preimplant stretched length and flaccid girth. Perceived length over years was not assessed. Fibrosis, prior surgery, anatomy and baseline expectations limit length, and neither device guarantees length restoration. Malleable rods are also used to conserve length in severe corporal fibrosis, because the solid rod maintains corporal space.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup><sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Infection Salvage

The malleable implant salvage technique removes infected hardware, performs the salvage procedure and replaces it with a malleable implant, either definitively or with possible later conversion. In Gross's 58-patient retrospective series, 54 remained infection-free; 17 of those 54 later converted to an inflatable implant, at a mean of 6.7 months. This selected experience is not a general success probability for every infected implant. In the same series 37 kept the malleable implant, and four had persistent infection and underwent explantation.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

A 2026 Veterans Affairs analysis of 76 salvage procedures at 30 centers found reinfection in 29.5%, higher in diabetic men (45.5% vs 20.5%, p = 0.04); among men without reinfection, 70% kept the malleable implant and 28% converted electively to an inflatable device. Outcomes vary by setting and patient selection. The malleable implant also maintains corporal space during healing and may allow a later inflatable device with longer, wider cylinders. The appropriate procedure depends on infection severity, tissue viability, comorbidity, prior surgery and the patient's goals.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

## Ischemic Priapism

Prosthesis discussion is appropriate after prolonged ischemia or refractory treatment, when the chance of useful erectile recovery is poor. Early implantation in the first days can limit fibrosis and shortening, and a malleable device may later be exchanged for an inflatable one, at the cost of another operation. In one series of 50 men receiving immediate implants for acute ischemic priapism (43 malleable, 7 inflatable), 96% were satisfied and none reported shortening, with infection in 6%.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> EAU 2026 recommends delaying implantation after a shunt to reduce infection and erosion risk. Timing decisions belong in the [Priapism pathway](/docs/special-populations/05a-trauma-emergencies/priapism), not a device-selection shortcut.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

## Use After Phalloplasty

A neophallus lacks normal corporal containment; fixation, sensation and soft-tissue coverage change the risk profile. Native-penis ED results do not apply to this setting. A 2024 meta-analysis of nine studies estimated complication rates of 37% for malleable and 38% for inflatable devices, with wide confidence intervals (18–62% and 21–59%) and no significant difference. The leading malleable complications were dislocation (14.9%), infection (11.2%), dysfunction (9.1%) and extrusion (7.6%). Explantation was 13% for malleable and 19% for inflatable devices (not significant).<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

Sun's single-center series implanted 40 infrapubic malleable devices (mean follow-up 34.9 months in the 30 followed); revision was needed in 12 of 30 (40%), with no erosion or flap loss. Pigot's early ZSI 100 FTM series of 25 patients (mean follow-up 6.3 months) had explantation for complications in 8 (32%). In a 2025 cohort of 50 men receiving 85 ZSI FTM implants (66 hydraulic, 19 malleable), 3-year explantation-free survival was 39% for hydraulic and 31% for malleable devices (p = 0.64), and secondary implantation carried a higher complication risk (OR 3.5).<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> In a 2026 retrospective comparison of 32 patients, 74% of ZSI FtM 100 devices were complication free at 24 months versus 12% of conventional models (p &lt; 0.001), although ZSI FtM 100 devices had more breakage.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> The 2026 international protocol is expert consensus. See the [Gender-Affirming Reconstruction atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery).<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup><sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup><sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Peyronie's Disease

A malleable prosthesis can be combined with plaque incision, multiple corporeal incisions and collagen-fleece grafting for complex Peyronie's disease with ED. In a series of 43 men with either implant type, mean lengthening was 2.5 cm and 89.7% would recommend the surgery. Operative time was 71.6 minutes for the malleable and 86.7 minutes for the inflatable procedure.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

## Imaging and Follow-up

MRI eligibility is model-specific and conditional. The August 2026 Coloplast memo lists different conditions for Genesis and current Titan models, and the newer Titan parameters do not apply to Genesis or older implants. MRI staff check the implant's field, gradient, RF and scanning-duration conditions.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

Provide the implant identification record and arrange ongoing device review. New pain, skin change, drainage, exposure, urinary difficulty, loss of support or difficulty positioning the device merits assessment. Persistent firmness is expected; progressive pressure injury or erosion is not.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup><sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
