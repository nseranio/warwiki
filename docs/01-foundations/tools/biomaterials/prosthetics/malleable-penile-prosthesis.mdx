# Malleable (Semirigid) Penile Prosthesis (MPP)

The **malleable penile prosthesis** is the **oldest and simplest form of penile implant** — paired silicone rods with an internal metallic wire core. The wire provides sufficient axial rigidity for penetration while allowing the penis to be bent downward for concealment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Although the three-piece [IPP](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/prosthetics/inflatable-penile-prosthesis) is the gold standard in advanced economies, MPP retains important and **expanding** clinical roles in **salvage surgery, refractory priapism, limited-dexterity patients, gender-affirming phalloplasty, and resource-limited settings**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## History &amp; Evolution

The concept of an intracorporeal penile support dates back &gt; 500 years.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- **1936** — Nikolaj Bogaraz: first penile prosthesis (autologous rib cartilage graft) in a reconstructed neophallus after traumatic amputation.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **1940s–1950s** — extracavernosal acrylic stents (Goodwin 1952); intracavernosal polyethylene rods (Loeffler, Sayegh).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **1966** — Beheri: first large series of intracavernosal polyethylene prostheses.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **1973–1975** — **Small &amp; Carrion** silicone prosthesis — paired silicone rods via perineal approach (31-patient series 1975 established the modern era).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **1975–1980s** — Finney hinged prosthesis (Flexirod) for concealability; Jonas / Jacobi silver-wire-core silicone for true malleability.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **1983** — **AMS 600** introduced braided stainless-steel wire core within silicone — prototype for all modern MPPs.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **1990s–2000s** — AMS Spectra (articulating segments) and Coloplast Genesis (PTFE-coated silver wire).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **2019** — Rigicon Rigi10: six widths, hydrophilic coating.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **2010s–present** — transgender-specific devices (Zephyr ZSI 100 FTM) and the TUBE / Shah prosthesis.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

All modern MPPs share **paired, trimmable silicone rods with a metallic wire core**. Rods are sized intraoperatively by trimming the proximal end to corporal length.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Design Principles

- **Permanent semirigid state** — the penis is always partially rigid; bent upward for intercourse, downward against the body for concealment.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Axial rigidity** from the wire core — sufficient for penetration.
- **Lateral flexibility** for concealment — articulating-segment designs (Tactra / Spectra) outperform pure wire-core designs.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **No moving parts** — no hydraulics, pump, reservoir, or tubing → mechanical failure is essentially eliminated.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Cannot produce a fully erect penis** — no girth or length expansion; less natural-appearing than an IPP.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Indications

Primary scenarios where MPP simplicity offers advantages over IPP:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

- **Poor manual dexterity** — arthritis, Parkinson's, stroke, SCI, advanced age unable to operate a scrotal pump.
- **Infection salvage (MIST — Malleable Implant Salvage Technique)** — replacement of an infected IPP with a malleable as either definitive or "corporal space-maintaining" placeholder for later IPP conversion.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Refractory ischemic priapism** — early insertion (&lt; 48–72 h) prevents corporal fibrosis and shortening; later exchangeable for IPP.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Gender-affirming phalloplasty** — simpler implantation in the neophallus; comparable complication rates to IPP.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Resource-limited settings** — substantially cheaper; outside the US, MPPs are more commonly implanted than IPPs.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Hostile pelvis** — prior pelvic surgery, radiation, mesh hernia repair where reservoir placement is unsafe.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Penile length conservation** — increasingly used for length preservation in severe corporal fibrosis or prior shortening.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Relative contraindications** (favor IPP): patients prioritizing natural appearance and concealability; patients with adequate dexterity and no specific MPP indication.

## Surgical Technique

Technically simpler and faster than IPP:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

**Approach**: penoscrotal (most common), infrapubic, or subcoronal.

**Steps**:

1. Bilateral corporotomies (longitudinal tunica albuginea incisions).
2. Sequential corporal dilation — proximally to the crura, distally to the glans.
3. Measurement of corporal length (proximal + distal).
4. Trim rods to measured length.
5. Insert paired rods into the corpora.
6. Close corporotomies and wound.

**Advantages over IPP**: no reservoir → no bladder / bowel / vascular risk; no pump → no scrotal complications; no tubing → no tubing failures; **shorter OR time (~72 vs ~87 min for IPP with PD grafting)**; possible under local / regional anesthesia in select patients.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

**Infection prevention**: same no-touch and antibiotic-irrigation principles as IPP. Rigi10 / ZSI hydrophilic coatings allow surgeon-applied antibiotic dipping; Tactra has parylene coating; Genesis has silicone coating.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Outcomes &amp; Satisfaction

- **Patient satisfaction 75–96%** across series / questionnaires.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Partner satisfaction 65–85%**.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- AMS Spectra (Akdemir): **96.2% patient, 84.6% partner** satisfaction at 3.2 yr mean; IIEF-5 5.86 → 22.5; EDITS 71.1.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- Genesis vs Spectra: no significant difference (EDITS 77.1% vs 75.6%, p = 0.50).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- Middle East cross-sectional (100 patients, 36% MPP / 64% hydraulic): **no significant difference in EDITS**.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

**vs IPP couples' satisfaction:** significantly higher with three-piece IPP than MPP (p &lt; 0.001); **7.7% MPP → IPP conversion** due to dissatisfaction vs only **0.2% IPP → MPP**.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

### Penile Length

Habous prospective (n = 133): implant surgery **does not decrease penile length**. Both groups gained slightly — MPP +0.22 cm length / +0.7 cm girth; IPP +0.62 cm / +1.7 cm.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> MPP is increasingly used specifically for **length conservation** in corporal fibrosis — the solid rod maintains corporal space and prevents further contracture.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

## Device Survival &amp; Mechanical Reliability

Superior to IPP due to absence of hydraulic components:<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

- **Rigi10**: 3-yr implant survival from revision **99.2%** (605 patients, 46 surgeons, 15 countries); only 0.99% required revision / explantation.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Real-world EHR (n = 29,385)**: 5-yr revision / removal rates similar between IPP (13.9%) and MPP (13.7%, p = 0.77). But **mechanical breakdown significantly lower** with MPP (6.7% vs 9.1%, p ≤ 0.0001), and **explantation higher** with MPP (8.4% vs 6.5%, p &lt; 0.05).<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
- **Highest revision-by-corporal-perforation** rate is with MPP (2.6%); **highest revision-by-mechanical-malfunction** rate is with IPP (5.5%).<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

## Complications

Cocci 2025 SR (n = 92,777) data across all prosthesis types:<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

| Complication | MPP | IPP | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Infection | 0.33–5% | 0.46–5% | Similar rates |
| Erosion | Higher | Lower | Constant pressure on tissues; more common distally |
| Corporal perforation | **2.6%** | Lower | Most common MPP revision indication |
| Mechanical failure | **Very rare** | 5.5–15%+ | Major MPP advantage |
| Concealability issues | Common | Rare | Constant semirigid state hard to hide |
| Displacement / dislocation (5 yr) | 1.6% | 2.3% | Lower with MPP |

## Special Populations

### Infection Salvage (MIST)

Replacing an infected IPP with a malleable during Mulcahy salvage offers:<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[14]](#ref14)[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

- Gross multicenter original series (n = 58): **93% infection-free**; **31% later converted to IPP** at 6.7 mo mean, often achieving **longer / wider cylinders** than the original infected IPP because the malleable preserved corporal space.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- 2026 **VA national analysis** (76 patients, 30 centers): **29.5% reinfection rate**, significantly higher in diabetics (45.5% vs 20.5%, p = 0.04). Among those without reinfection: **70% retained the MPP** definitively; **28% converted to IPP**.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>
- Functions as a **corporal space maintainer** — prevents fibrosis and contracture while infection resolves, easing future IPP placement.

### Ischemic Priapism

MPP insertion within **48–72 h of refractory priapism** prevents corporal fibrosis and penile shortening. Can later be exchanged for an IPP with potentially longer / wider cylinders.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

### Gender-Affirming Phalloplasty

MPPs are widely used in the neophallus due to simpler implantation:<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)[[10]](#ref10)[[26]](#ref26)[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

- **Fraiman meta-analysis**: overall complication **37% MPP vs 38% IPP** (NS). Top MPP complications: dislocation 14.9%, infection 11.2%, dysfunction 9.1%, extrusion 7.6%. Explantation **13% MPP vs 19% IPP** (NS).<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Sun 2023 infrapubic technique** (n = 40): 40% required revision; no erosions or flap loss.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **ZSI 100 FTM** (Pigot 2020): 32% explantation early on, but **better 24-mo complication-free survival (74%)** vs conventional MPPs (12%, p &lt; 0.05).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Levy 2025 ZSI FTM cohort** (hydraulic + malleable): 3-yr explantation-free survival poor at 31–39%, no significant malleable vs hydraulic difference. **Secondary implantations: 3.5× complication risk**.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>
- **Levy 2026 Delphi consensus** established the first standardized phalloplasty implant protocol.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>

See [Penile Implant After Phalloplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/penile-implant-after-phalloplasty).

### Peyronie's Disease

MPPs can be combined with plaque incision + grafting for complex PD + ED. Fernández-Pascual series (n = 43, MPP or IPP with multiple corporeal incisions + collagen fleece): mean penile lengthening **2.5 cm**; 89.7% would recommend; MPP operative time shorter (**71.6 vs 86.7 min**).<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

## Malleable vs Inflatable — Comprehensive Comparison

| Feature | Malleable (MPP) | Three-Piece Inflatable (IPP) |
|---|---|---|
| Erection quality | Semirigid; no girth expansion | Near-physiologic; girth + length expansion |
| Concealability | Difficult (always semirigid) | Excellent (fully deflatable) |
| Couples' satisfaction | 75–77% EDITS | 77–93% EDITS; significantly higher |
| Mechanical reliability | Excellent (no hydraulics) | 5.5–15% failure at 5–11 yr |
| Operative time | Shorter (~72 min) | Longer (~87 min) |
| Surgical complexity | Simple (no reservoir / pump) | Complex (reservoir + pump + tubing) |
| Cost | Significantly lower | Higher |
| Infection | Similar (~0.3–5%) | Similar (~0.5–5%) |
| Corporal perforation | **Higher (2.6%)** | Lower |
| Revision / removal (5 yr) | 13.7% | 13.9% |
| Penile length change | +0.22 cm | +0.62 cm |
| Dexterity requirement | None | Requires pump operation |
| Reservoir complications | **None** | Herniation, erosion, vascular |
