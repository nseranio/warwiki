# Inflatable Penile Prosthesis

An **inflatable penile prosthesis** (IPP) is an implanted hydraulic device that gives patient-controlled rigidity for erectile dysfunction. It is used when other treatments are unsuitable or have failed, or when an informed patient prefers definitive prosthetic treatment. It requires surgery, realistic expectations and the ability to operate the device. Trying every nonsurgical option first is not an absolute prerequisite.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For surgical selection, operative approaches, reservoir placement and salvage, see the [Penile Implants atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/penile-implants/). For patients who may be better served by persistent firmness and simpler operation, see [Malleable Penile Prosthesis](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/prosthetics/malleable-penile-prosthesis).

## History and Evolution

F. Brantley Scott published the first inflatable penile prosthesis in 1973.<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup><sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup> The design changes since then, in the order they were introduced:<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup><sup>[[33]](#ref33)</sup>

- The first implant was a three-piece device: intracavernosal cylinders, a scrotal pump and a retropubic reservoir. Self-contained two-piece devices (cylinders and pump, no separate reservoir) followed.
- Kink-resistant tubing, changes to the cylinder fabric weave and shear-resistant coatings, rear tip extenders and lockout valves to prevent autoinflation were added.
- Antibiotic or antimicrobial surface treatments were introduced: InhibiZone (rifampin and minocycline, AMS) and the Coloplast hydrophilic coating, which the surgeon soaks in antibiotic.
- Steve Wilson described ectopic (submuscular) reservoir placement in 2002. It avoids the space of Retzius and a second incision, and lockout valves and flat reservoirs made it practical.<sup>[[33]](#ref33)</sup>

## Components and Current Models

A three-piece implant contains paired corporal cylinders, a scrotal pump and a separate fluid reservoir. Inflation and deflation transfer fluid between these components. A two-piece design has fluid storage within the cylinders and therefore avoids a separate abdominal reservoir. Neither configuration eliminates infection, mechanical failure or revision.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup><sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

| Platform | Current device distinctions | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| **AMS 700 — Boston Scientific** | CX/CXR provide girth expansion; LGX adds length expansion. TENACIO and earlier MS pumps have distinct instructions. Specified components have rifampin/minocycline InhibiZone treatment. | Length expansion does not reliably restore perceived penile length. Identify the actual pump and cylinder model. |
| **Titan / Titan Prime — Coloplast** | Bioflex cylinders and hydrophilic surface; pump/model variants differ. Titan Prime introduces the Press pump and revised components. | FDA approval and product availability are separate questions. Manufacturer bench data are not long-term clinical outcomes. |
| **Infla10 — Rigicon** | X provides girth expansion; AX provides length and girth expansion. HydroShield hydrophilic coating includes components, connectors and rear-tip extenders. | The manufacturer's current page states that it is **not available for sale in the US**. |
| **AMS Ambicor — Boston Scientific** | Prefilled, preconnected two-piece device; fluid reservoirs are in the proximal cylinders. | It has no separate abdominal reservoir. Deflation involves bending the cylinders as taught for this model, not pressing a three-piece deflation button. |

Match the actual implant, supplied instructions and local authorization. No high-quality evidence establishes one brand as best for every patient. In a three-cadaver study, all implants performed similarly at maximum inflation, and the circumferentially expanding devices had greater rigidity in load testing; the study did not test clinical satisfaction or durability.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup><sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup><sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup><sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup><sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

FDA approved supplement P000006/S072 on June 2, 2026. ## Device Safety Notices

:::warning[Match the exact model and lot]

**AMS 700 with TENACIO:** FDA Class II recall event 97603, initiated September 11, 2025, concerns specified devices with potential inflation or deflation problems from a nonconforming component. Check the exact catalog number or UPN, UDI and lot in the linked records. Affected unused inventory is not used and is returned through the recall process. The notice states that no action is required for an implanted affected device that is functioning properly, and inflation or deflation difficulties warrant evaluation. The recall does not cover every TENACIO implant and does not call for routine explantation.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

An earlier Titan Touch recall, event 91800, concerns specified lots with a pump-wall thickness issue that could lead to premature failure. Its affected-inventory instructions also require exact model and lot matching. Not every Titan is affected; consult the current record and manufacturer action notice.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Indications and Selection

The IPP is used after failure of, intolerance to or contraindication to PDE5 inhibitors, vacuum devices and intracavernosal injections. Common indications are:

- erectile dysfunction with Peyronie's disease;
- erectile dysfunction after radical prostatectomy that has not responded to rehabilitation;
- ischemic priapism, either early for refractory priapism or late after corporal fibrosis;
- corporal fibrosis from prior priapism, infection, trauma or explantation;
- gender-affirming phalloplasty;
- patient preference for definitive surgical treatment.

Preoperative assessment covers HbA1c, active urinary infection, hand function and key pinch strength for pump operation, the partner's sexual function, and consent about irreversibility, possible shortening, mechanical failure and infection.

Consider the patient's erectile goals, hand function, cognition, tissue quality and ability to obtain follow-up. A clinic demonstration can identify practical problems with the chosen pump. The Ambicor label still requires manual dexterity and strength, so a two-piece device is not automatically suitable for a patient who cannot operate a three-piece pump.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Counseling covers irreversible corporal changes, perceived shortening, altered rigidity or sensation, persistent pain, infection, erosion and future surgery. An implant is not a lifetime device and does not restore libido, fertility or an absent orgasmic response. Urination, sensation and ejaculation can be affected by surgery or its complications.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup><sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Active infection and device-specific material or medication hypersensitivity need review. AMS 700 InhibiZone components have additional contraindications including the listed antibiotic allergies and systemic lupus erythematosus. Diabetes and other comorbidities are optimized before surgery; no single HbA1c value is a validated infection-safe threshold.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup><sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Prior pelvic surgery or hernia mesh changes reservoir planning but does not automatically prohibit a three-piece device. The space of Retzius reservoir carries risk of bladder, iliac vessel and bowel injury. High submuscular (HSM) placement is increasingly used: in a CT comparison, HSM reservoirs (n = 107) were about five times farther from the bladder (10 vs 49 mm) and iliac vessels (5 vs 23 mm) than space of Retzius reservoirs (n = 32), with less mass effect on the bladder (9% vs 72%) and iliac vessels (4% vs 34%).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> In one surgeon's 733 procedures, revision for a reservoir-related complication was needed in 10 of 430 HSM and 6 of 131 space of Retzius cases (p = 0.22), mostly herniation; after the "five-step" HSM technique was adopted, 3 of 297 cases (1.0%) needed revision, all for herniation.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> Ectopic placement (lateral retroperitoneal, intraperitoneal or epigastric extraperitoneal) has its own risks of injury, malposition, herniation and discomfort. See [Reservoir Placement](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/penile-implants/reservoir-placement).

The incision is penoscrotal, infrapubic or subcoronal. A critical review found no difference between penoscrotal and infrapubic approaches in implant size, achieved length, satisfaction, infection or urethral injury. Dorsal nerve injury risk appears greater with the infrapubic approach.<sup>[[34]](#ref34)</sup>

## Device Handling

- **AMS 700 InhibiZone:** the treated system can include cylinders, pump and reservoir. The TENACIO IFU prohibits soaking treated components; a brief rinse or dip is distinct from soaking. This is different from the AMS 800 AUS, whose PRB is not treated.
- **Hydrophilic devices:** follow the model's specified preparation. Another brand's instructions do not transfer.
- **Cycling:** pump controls differ. Autoinflation and unintended deflation remain possible despite design features intended to reduce them.
- **After implantation:** intracavernosal injection therapy can damage the prosthesis and should not be continued. Preserve the device identification and component records.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup><sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup><sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For patient teaching and troubleshooting, use [Device Operation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/penile-implants#device-operation) with the actual manufacturer's instructions.

## Infection Prevention

Infection prevention combines patient assessment, skin preparation, antimicrobial selection, appropriate device handling and operative technique. No single coating or irrigation solution eliminates infection.

| Evidence | What it establishes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| **Eid 2012**, one surgeon, 2,347 IPPs | Infection was 5.3% with 132 noncoated implants, 2% with 704 coated implants (2003–2005) and **0.46%** (7 of 1,511) after the "no touch" technique was added (2006–2010) | Randomized separation of the coating and technique effects; the rate is one surgeon's |
| **Bole 2023**, 1,071 procedures | Infection was 2.6% overall and 0.9% (8 of 919) after 5% povidone-iodine irrigation was replaced with vancomycin-gentamicin; median time to symptoms was 36 days; 19 of 21 positive cultures (90.5%) grew organisms of high virulence | A randomized comparison of irrigants; “virulent” does not mean Gram-negative or fungal |
| **Chawareb 2025**, 5,261 patients at 16 centers | 102 infections (**1.9%**); diabetes (OR 1.68) and prior IPP infection (OR 4.67) were independent risk factors, and perioperative IV antifungal use was associated with lower infection odds (**OR 0.22**). Prolonged IV and postoperative oral antibiotics were not associated with protection | That every candidate needs antifungal prophylaxis, or a universal antibiotic regimen |

These findings support review of local protocols and stewardship, with allergy, renal function, organism history and local susceptibility considered. Prolonged postoperative antibiotics have not been shown to protect. See the [operative infection page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/penile-implants/infection) for the procedure-specific discussion.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup><sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup><sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup><sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In a meta-analysis of 14 studies (9,910 first implants), infection was 2.32% with noncoated and 0.89% with coated devices (p &lt; 0.01), roughly a 50% reduction.<sup>[[35]](#ref35)</sup> The "no touch" technique includes plastic draping of the skin edges to prevent skin-to-implant contact, glove changes before handling the device, and minimal device exposure time.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

In a 2025 database analysis of 18,475 men, infection was 3.1% at a median follow-up of 3 years, with 2.5% in the first 6 months. Type 2 diabetes with comorbidities (HR 1.56), spinal cord injury (HR 2.81), obesity (HR 1.74) and concomitant circumcision (HR 2.01) were associated with infection.<sup>[[36]](#ref36)</sup>

## Outcomes and Durability

### Satisfaction and Function

Many recipients report substantial improvement, but response rates, questionnaires and follow-up affect the estimate. In Luna's cohort of 542 first implants, 322 answered the questionnaire (93.1% positive responses) and 109 attended clinical reassessment. Of those 109, 60.5% complained of a decrease in penile size, although objective length was greater in 61.4% and girth in 49.4%, and shortening was measured in 11% and 3.7%.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> In a comparison of AMS 700CX and Coloplast Titan at a mean follow-up of about 58 months, EDITS scores were 77.2 and 77.5 (p = 0.956).<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> At 20 years in a series of 51 men, 41% were still using the original device and quality-of-life scores remained high in those men.<sup>[[37]](#ref37)</sup>

### Long-Term Survival

Miller's 2022 systematic review included 12 studies and 20,161 recipients. Estimated implant survival was 87.2% at five years, 76.8% at ten, 63.7% at fifteen and 52.9% at twenty years. The pooled estimates combine different implant eras and cohorts. Five-year survival was 90.6% in newer studies and 82.1% in older ones (p = 0.01), and the authors estimate a median device survival of about 20 years.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

The 2025 EAU-panel systematic review included 151 studies and 92,777 patients. Reported infection rates ranged from 0.03% to 14.3%, with most studies below 5%, and erosion from 0.02% to 32.5%. Follow-up and methods differed substantially, and mechanical failure accumulates over time.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

### Reading Mechanical-Failure Reports

Studies of revised implants describe why devices were revised, not the probability that an implanted device fails. In Griggs's 72 mechanical revisions (63 Boston Scientific, 9 Coloplast), Boston Scientific failures were cylinder rupture in 41.3%, pump in 33.3%, tubing fracture in 11.1%, cylinder aneurysm in 9.5% and reservoir in 4.8%; Coloplast failures were tubing fracture in 7 of 9. Median time to mechanical failure was 48 months (Boston Scientific) and 41 months (Coloplast). In Smelser's 68 revisions (46 Boston Scientific, 22 Coloplast), Coloplast devices failed at the tubing in 19 of 22 (83%), Boston Scientific devices had no predominant site, and time to failure did not differ (p = 0.096). These cohorts cannot rank brand lifespans. New pain, loss of rigidity, difficulty cycling, erosion or infection needs assessment for the specific cause.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup><sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

## Special Clinical Settings

**Peyronie's disease:** concomitant ED and residual deformity influence device selection and any additional straightening. In a multicenter series of 499 men (mean preoperative curvature 39.4°), 17.6% needed IPP placement alone. Manual modeling, with or without the scratch technique, was used in 74.7% (median correction 26°), tunica plication in 4.8% (40°) and grafting in 2% (55°, in men with median preoperative curvature of 60°).<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> Adjunctive maneuvers for residual curvature are reviewed in reference 23. In a 37-man series of collagen-fleece grafting (PICS), 31 (84%) had a straight penis after implantation, and the scratch technique with postoperative vacuum therapy reduced residual curvature from 14–22° to 8–9° at 24 weeks in a separate series.<sup>[[38]](#ref38)</sup><sup>[[39]](#ref39)</sup> See [Prosthesis With Straightening](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease/prosthesis-with-straightening).<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

**Ischemic priapism:** delayed presentation and refractory ischemia justify early prosthesis discussion. Evidence is predominantly retrospective. One series of 50 men who received immediate implants for acute ischemic priapism (43 malleable, 7 inflatable) reported 96% satisfaction and no penile shortening, with infection in 6%.<sup>[[40]](#ref40)</sup> The EAU guideline advises delaying implantation after a shunt to reduce infection and erosion. Delayed implantation after established fibrosis may need cavernotomes, narrow-base cylinders and corporal reconstruction with grafting. Device choice and timing depend on viable tissue, prior treatment and the patient's goals. See [Priapism](/docs/special-populations/05a-trauma-emergencies/priapism).<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

**After phalloplasty:** the absence of native erectile tissue changes anchoring and complication risks. Cylinders are wrapped (Dacron, Gore-Tex or allograft) and fixed to the pubic bone. In a systematic review of 792 prostheses, 36.2% of patients had a complication, 60.0% still had the original implant at a mean of 3.0 years, and 83.9% achieved penetration.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup> In 247 men, 5-year device survival was 78%, infection 8.5% and revision 43%; 88% were satisfied but 77% used the device for intercourse.<sup>[[41]](#ref41)</sup> Five-year retention in flap phalloplasty has been reported at 42–78%, lower than in native anatomy.<sup>[[42]](#ref42)</sup> The 2026 international protocol is expert consensus.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> See [Penile Implant After Phalloplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/penile-implant-after-phalloplasty).

**Combined AUS and IPP:** a single-institution series found no increase in device infection, erosion or malfunction with combined or staged implantation, although operative time was longer (218 vs 146 minutes for IPP alone).<sup>[[43]](#ref43)</sup> A claims-based analysis found no difference in 30- or 90-day complications or long-term device survival.<sup>[[44]](#ref44)</sup> A database study found a higher likelihood of IPP reoperation with dual implantation at 1 year (OR 2.08) and 3 years (OR 2.60), with no effect on AUS reoperation.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup> See [AUS device hub](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/prosthetics/artificial-urinary-sphincter).<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

## MRI, Activation and Follow-up

These devices are MR Conditional, not universally MRI-safe. TENACIO, Ambicor, current Titan/Prime and older implants have different specified conditions, and MRI staff check the implant's gradient, RF, SAR and duration limits, not field strength alone. Newer Titan conditions do not apply to Genesis or older models.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup><sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup><sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup>

Activation and intercourse depend on wound healing, comfort, device operation and the surgeon's instructions. The cited AMS labels describe initial cycling around 3–6 weeks and assessment for intercourse around 4–6 weeks; these are not universal schedules for complicated salvage or priapism. Arrange ongoing, usually annual, device review and earlier assessment for new pain, redness, drainage, urinary problems or impaired function.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup><sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
