# Medtronic InterStim — Sacral Neuromodulation

The **Medtronic InterStim** is the **pioneering and most extensively studied sacral neuromodulation (SNM) device** — FDA-approved since **1997** for urinary dysfunction and **2011** for fecal incontinence. **&gt; 350,000 implants worldwide** with &gt; 25 years of clinical data.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## History &amp; Evolution

NIH-funded research at UCSF in the 1970s (Schmidt and Tanagho) demonstrated that stimulation of the **S3 sacral nerve root** modulates bladder and sphincter function through afferent pathways.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- **1981** — first chronic sacral nerve stimulation implant in a human.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Early 1990s** — initial trials required open surgery and general anesthesia.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **1997** — FDA approval for **urgency urinary incontinence (UUI)**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **1999** — FDA approval expanded to **urgency-frequency** and **non-obstructive urinary retention**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **~2002** — **tined lead** (Model 3889): self-anchoring percutaneous lead enabling local-anesthesia placement. Test-phase success rate **~60% → ~80%**.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **2006** — **InterStim II**: smaller non-rechargeable IPG.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **2011** — FDA approval for **fecal incontinence**.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **2019–2020** — **InterStim Micro**: miniaturized rechargeable IPG with **full-body 1.5T and 3T MRI conditional** labeling.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

References: <sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[7]](#ref7)[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

**Battery life note.** The InterStim II median lifespan (5.9 yr) is significantly shorter than the original InterStim (7.3 yr, p &lt; 0.05).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

**MRI compatibility.** Non-rechargeable InterStim devices are FDA-labeled only for **head-only 1.5T MRI**. Real-world Swiss data (191 MRIs, 92% at 1.5T, 58% non-head) with the device off: only 2 (1%) minor AEs (transient pain / heat).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> InterStim Micro's full-body conditional labeling at both 1.5T and 3T is a major advance.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Mechanism

Continuous low-amplitude pulses to the **S3 root** via a quadripolar tined lead through the S3 foramen:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

- **Somatic-afferent activation** — inhibits parasympathetic motor neurons in the sacral spinal cord, rebalancing excitatory and inhibitory reflexes for bladder, bowel, and pelvic floor.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Supraspinal effects** — fMRI shows modulation of anterior cingulate, mPFC, periaqueductal gray, and limbic system.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Sympathetic-parasympathetic rebalancing.**
- **Neural plasticity** at peripheral, spinal, and supraspinal levels.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Reversible** — effect ceases when stimulation is off; off-device outcomes return to baseline.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- Typical parameters: **14 Hz, pulse width 210 μs**, sub-sensory / sensory threshold amplitude.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## FDA-Approved Indications

1. **Urgency urinary incontinence** (1997)
2. **Urinary urgency-frequency** (1999)
3. **Non-obstructive urinary retention** (1999)
4. **Fecal incontinence** (2011)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

**Off-label use with emerging evidence:**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[18]](#ref18)[[19]](#ref19)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

- **Interstitial cystitis / bladder pain syndrome** — meta-analysis (n = 583): 84% success with significant ↓ pelvic pain / frequency / nocturia.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- **Neurogenic bladder** — meta-analysis (n = 573) with significant ↑ in storage parameters; pooled permanent SNM success **84.2%**.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- **Chronic constipation** — limited evidence (Cochrane).<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
- **Chronic pelvic pain, sexual dysfunction** — emerging.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

## Surgical Technique

### Testing Phase Options<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)[[24]](#ref24)[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

- **PNE** (percutaneous nerve evaluation) — office; temporary wire near S3 for **3–7 days**. Success ~46–60%.
- **Stage I tined lead** (staged) — permanent tined lead in OR under fluoro; external stimulator **≥ 14 days**. Success **~80%**. **44% of PNE failures respond** to tined-lead testing.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[25]](#ref25)</sup>
- **Single-stage implantation** — both lead + IPG in one procedure. Cost-minimization analysis: single-stage is cost-effective when success rate exceeds **61–65%** (which contemporary rates do).<sup>[[26]](#ref26)[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

### Implantation Steps<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

1. Prone position; fluoro identification of S3 foramen.
2. Foramen needle confirmed by **bellows response** (pelvic-floor / anal-sphincter contraction) and great-toe plantar flexion.
3. Tined lead advanced through the needle alongside S3.
4. Lead connected to IPG in a subcutaneous upper-buttock pocket.
5. Device programmed and activated.

**Staged implants** have a **17% lower** likelihood of device-related reoperations vs PNE/full-implant in Medicare data.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>

## Clinical Outcomes by Indication

### OAB / UUI — InSite Trial (n = 340)

5-yr outcomes:<sup>[[29]](#ref29)[[30]](#ref30)</sup>

- **5-yr therapeutic success 67%** (modified completers) to **82%** (completers).
- Mean ↓ **2.0 leaks/day** (UUI), **5.4 voids/day** (urgency-frequency).
- Significant improvement across all ICIQ-OABqol measures.

Worldwide prospective (n = 152, 17 centers) 5-yr success: **68% UI / 56% urgency-frequency / 71% retention**.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup>

### Non-Obstructive Urinary Retention

- Mean catheterization volume **380 → 109 mL**; catheterizations **5.3 → 1.9/day** at 5 yr.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup>
- **5-yr success 71%** — highest among urinary indications.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup>
- Long-term single-center: **87.5% success** for idiopathic retention.<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup>

### Fecal Incontinence

- **Pivotal North American multicenter (n = 133)**: **86% therapeutic success** at 3 yr; **40% complete continence**; FI episodes 9.4 → 1.7/week at 12 mo.<sup>[[33]](#ref33)</sup>
- **10-yr French multicenter (n = 360)**: ITT favorable outcome **45%**; Kaplan-Meier estimated favorable outcome **64% at 10 yr**.<sup>[[34]](#ref34)</sup>
- ASCRS recommends SNM **first-line surgical option** for FI failing conservative treatment, **irrespective of anal sphincter integrity**.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## ROSETTA — SNM vs OnabotulinumtoxinA

386 women, 9 US centers, refractory UUI:<sup>[[35]](#ref35)[[36]](#ref36)</sup>

**6 months:**

- OnaBT greater mean ↓ UUI episodes (3.9 vs 3.3/day; MD 0.63, p = 0.01).
- OnaBT higher satisfaction and endorsement.
- No differences in convenience, AE perception, or treatment preference.

**24 months:**

- **No difference** in mean UUI reduction (−3.88 vs −3.50/day, p = 0.15).
- No differences in UUI resolution, ≥ 75% / ≥ 50% reduction rates.
- OnaBT maintained higher satisfaction / endorsement.
- **Recurrent UTI significantly higher with OnaBT (24% vs 10%, p &lt; 0.01)**.

**Cost-effectiveness**: SNM was **not cost-effective** vs OnaBT at 2 yr ($35,680 vs $7,460) or 5 yr ($36,550 vs $12,020); no QALY difference.<sup>[[37]](#ref37)</sup>

**AUA/SUFU OAB guidelines** recommend both as options for inadequate response to pharmacotherapy, guided by shared decision-making.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

## Complications &amp; Reintervention

| Complication | Rate |
|---|---|
| Implant site pain | 15–28% |
| Undesirable stimulation change | 12–22% |
| Lead migration | 3–12% |
| Infection | 2–10% |
| Paresthesias | 15% |
| Therapeutic ineffectiveness | 13% |

| Study | n | Follow-up | Revision rate | Explantation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Medicare women ≥ 65<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup> | 32,454 | 8 yr | **43% cumulative** (9.4% at 1 yr, 20% at 3 yr) | Included |
| Single-center 14-yr<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup> | 96 | 50.7 mo median | **39%** | **20.8%** |
| Multicenter<sup>[[38]](#ref38)</sup> | 219 | 164 d median | **16%** | Included |
| FI systematic review<sup>[[39]](#ref39)</sup> | 3,770 | ≥ 36 mo | **35.2%** | **19.7%** |
| French FI 10-yr<sup>[[34]](#ref34)</sup> | 360 | 10 yr | **64.7%** | **26.1%** |

**Risk factors for revision / explantation**:<sup>[[40]](#ref40)[[41]](#ref41)[[42]](#ref42)</sup>

- Age &lt; 55, female sex.
- BMI change between implantation and revision.
- History of trauma, pain-clinic enrollment.
- Longer follow-up (cumulative revision rises).
- **Antimicrobial envelope** did **not** reduce complication rates.<sup>[[40]](#ref40)</sup>
- The tined-lead technique reduced revision rates from **50% → 31%** vs the older lead.<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup>

## Rechargeable vs Non-Rechargeable — A Critical Real-World Finding

InterStim Micro promised longer life and full-body MRI. Real-world data raise concerns:<sup>[[43]](#ref43)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

- Comparative study: rechargeable devices had **significantly lower revision-free survival** than non-rechargeable for OAB. By ~1,600 days, revision-free survival ~**64% rechargeable vs 82% non-rechargeable**. Over a third of rechargeable-group revisions were for **difficulty charging and connecting**.<sup>[[43]](#ref43)</sup>
- Single-center FI study (n = 37, InterStim Micro): **62% expressed regret**, **96% reported charging challenges**, **86% connectivity issues**; modest continence improvement below expectations. **Non-rechargeable systems with extended battery life may be the preferable first-line option**.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Neurogenic Bladder

- Meta-analysis (n = 573): significant improvement in voiding frequency, leakage, nocturia, compliance, max detrusor pressure.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- Pooled test-stimulation success **66.2%**; permanent SNM success **84.2%**.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- AUA/SUFU NLUTD: Conditional Recommendation (Grade C) in select patients with SUI and acceptable storage.<sup>[[44]](#ref44)</sup>
- **Progressive neurological disease** is generally a contraindication.<sup>[[45]](#ref45)</sup>
- Early bilateral SNM during spinal shock may prevent subsequent neurogenic detrusor overactivity (long-term data pending).<sup>[[45]](#ref45)</sup>

## Guideline Recommendations

| Society | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **AUA/SUFU (OAB 2024)**<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> | Offer SNM for inadequate response to pharmacotherapy; may offer without prior medication failure |
| **ASCRS (FI 2023)**<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | First-line surgical option after failed conservative treatment, **with or without sphincter defects** |
| **ACOG (FI 2019)**<sup>[[46]](#ref46)</sup> | Consider for women with FI failing conservative treatment, irrespective of sphincter integrity |
| **ACG (Anorectal 2021)**<sup>[[33]](#ref33)</sup> | Recommended for FI refractory to medical therapy |
| **AUA/SUFU (NLUTD 2021)**<sup>[[44]](#ref44)</sup> | Conditional recommendation in select neurogenic patients |
| **EAU (Female LUTS)**<sup>[[47]](#ref47)</sup> | SNM and OnaBT both second-line options; choice guided by individual factors |

## Competitive Landscape

- **Axonics** (now Boston Scientific) — rechargeable SNM with full-body MRI compatibility, manufacturer-reported **≥ 15-yr** battery life. ARTISAN-SNM pivotal: **90% responder rate at 6 mo**.<sup>[[48]](#ref48)[[49]](#ref49)</sup>
- **Medtronic InterStim X** — non-rechargeable extended-battery generation positioned against Axonics' recharge-free era.
- **BetterStim** (China) — comparable efficacy to InterStim for OAB.<sup>[[50]](#ref50)</sup>
