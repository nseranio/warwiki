# Revi — Implantable Tibial Neuromodulation

**Revi (BlueWind Medical)** uses a battery-free implant near the tibial nerve, powered during treatment by an external rechargeable wearable. FDA granted De Novo classification on August 16, 2023 for urgency incontinence alone or with urinary urgency. The indication does not explicitly require prior conservative-treatment failure; however, the pivotal study largely enrolled previously treated patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## History

The first-generation device, marketed as RENOVA iStim, was a battery-free implant powered by an external unit. In the first-in-human series of 15 patients (13 women, 2 men; 5 previously treated with PTNS), median operative time was 34 minutes. At 3 months, daily frequency fell from 11.8 to 8.1 voids, severe urgency episodes from 6.5 to 2.0 per day and severe incontinence episodes from 2.8 to 0.3 per day. Three patients received prolonged antibiotics, and one device was explanted for pain and swelling suspicious for infection with negative tissue cultures.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> Three-year follow-up included 20 of the 34 patients originally implanted; overall treatment success was 75% at 36 months in both per-protocol (16) and intention-to-treat (20) analyses, and no technical failures were reported.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> The current Revi System was evaluated in the OASIS pivotal study, which was published under the earlier RENOVA name.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Components and Current Models

The system includes the implant, rechargeable wearable, clinician programmer and data-transfer hub. The implant is placed subfascially near the posterior tibial neurovascular bundle and secured to fascia. Patients wear the external battery-operated unit during prescribed home sessions after healing, and it powers the implant during each session. The lower-leg approach avoids an abdominal or buttock incision. The absence of an implanted battery avoids internal battery-depletion replacement, but does not eliminate charging of the wearable, device failure, explantation or other repeat procedures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Because percutaneous and implantable tibial stimulation target the same nerve, a 2024 narrative review argued that randomized data for percutaneous stimulation inform implantable devices. That argument supports plausibility. It does not replace device-specific outcomes.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

| Update | What changed |
|---|---|
| December 12, 2025 (K252391) | Updated wearable with a replaceable rechargeable battery, revised controls and associated software |
| July 28, 2026 (K253651) | Modified implant materials/manufacturing and 15-year labeled implant life, versus 10 years for its predicate |

The manufacturer calls the newer implant Revi Extend and announced a limited US launch planned for Q4 2026. The 15-year specification is supported by device testing, including active aging; it is not 15 years of observed clinical success and should not be applied automatically to older implants. External equipment has its own service and replacement requirements.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup><sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## OASIS Evidence — Keep the Denominators Visible

OASIS was prospective, multicenter, open-label and single arm: 282 people screened, 151 women implanted. Most had failed conservative therapy; stable OAB medication was permitted. A response meant at least 50% reduction in UUI episodes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

| Analysis | Result | Important boundary |
|---|---|---|
| FDA intention-to-treat analysis | 76.4% at six months; 78.4% at 12 months | Missing-data handling included; 12-month efficacy analysis was posthoc |
| Two-year report | 79% of 97 completers responded | Selected follow-up cohort |
| Three-year report | 71/90 (79%) responded; 25/90 dry on a three-day diary | 90 provided diaries from 151 originally implanted; 119 entered the extension |

At three years, 83/87 questionnaire respondents were satisfied. These data support persistent benefit among many participants remaining in follow-up, but loss to follow-up, lack of blinding and absence of a comparator limit interpretation. Different diary lengths and changing populations also complicate cross-time comparisons. In the 12-month report, 82% of the 139 participants with completed diaries were responders, 50% were dry on at least three consecutive diary days and 93.5% reported improvement.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup><sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup><sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

The three-year report observed no device- or procedure-related serious adverse events, but recorded wound and stimulation-related problems and explantations. “No serious events observed” is not a guarantee of zero risk, and cannot establish superiority to an SNM cohort followed for a different period.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

A completers quality-of-life analysis through 24 months reported clinically significant (10 points or more) improvements in all OAB-q domains and the HRQL score. At 24 months, 96.8% reported treatment benefit, 96.7% were satisfied and 100% were willing to continue. Among the 20 participants (20.6%) who did not meet the responder threshold, 83.3% still reported benefit and 66.7% were satisfied. This is a completers analysis in a manufacturer-sponsored study.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

## Safety and Practical Selection

Assess ability to use the wearable independently or with assistance, lower-leg skin and circulation, prior local surgery or trauma, other implants and pregnancy. Current manufacturer contraindications include obstructive BPH or other lower-tract obstruction, unsuitable local tissue or vascular conditions and specified nearby metal implants. Safety and effectiveness are not established in all neurological populations. Consult the complete current model-specific instructions.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Revi is MR Conditional with restrictions. The implant's 1.5T/3T conditions include scanner, anatomical landmark, energy and scan-time limits; external components are MR Unsafe. Diathermy and other energy procedures need separate review. Battery-free design does not make the implant immune to heating or interference.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Position Relative to PTNS and SNM

The 2024 AUA/SUFU guideline permits individualized minimally invasive treatment without mandatory preceding trials, but its unabridged Statement 25 specifically names percutaneous tibial stimulation. The guideline's implantable-device section gives no specific recommendation because direct comparative evidence was lacking. It should not be cited as Grade A support for Revi itself.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

A 2025 meta-analysis pooled separate SNM and implantable-tibial cohorts; it was not a head-to-head comparison. Weighted UUI responder rates were 71.8% for SNM and 71.3% for implantable tibial neuromodulation. Similar pooled response estimates do not establish equivalent benefit, fewer revisions or lower lifetime cost.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

Compared with SNM, Revi offers a lower-leg implant, and the implantable tibial studies pooled in the meta-analysis did not use a trial phase.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Its US indication is not limited to patients who have failed conservative therapy. Its limitations are follow-up of 3 years versus more than 20 years of SNM experience, a UUI-only indication with no data for non-obstructive retention or fecal incontinence, the need to wear and charge an external ankle unit, and the absence of a head-to-head trial. The evidence base is predominantly female.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup><sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup><sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

| Implantable tibial system | Power and use | Pivotal design |
|---|---|---|
| Revi (BlueWind) | Battery-free implant powered by an external wearable used at home; patient-controlled | Single arm, 151 women; ITT responder rate 76.4% at 6 months<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| [eCoin](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/neuromodulation-devices/ecoin) (Valencia, Boston Scientific) | Non-rechargeable implanted battery; automatic programmed stimulation | Single arm, 132 in the ITT cohort; 68% responders at 48 weeks |
| Medtronic implantable tibial system | Leadless, rechargeable | TITAN 2 single arm, 126 implanted; 59% responders at 6 months |

Response definitions, analysis populations and time points differ between these pivotal studies, and no head-to-head comparison exists, so the percentages are not directly comparable.
