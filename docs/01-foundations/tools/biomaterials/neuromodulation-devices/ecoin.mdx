# eCoin — Implantable Tibial Nerve Stimulator

**eCoin** (Valencia Technologies, now part of Boston Scientific) is a leadless tibial neurostimulator powered by a non-rechargeable battery. US labeling covers urgency urinary incontinence (UUI) after intolerance of or inadequate response to conservative treatments, or after a successful percutaneous tibial nerve stimulation (PTNS) trial.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Design and Patient Tasks

The nickel-sized, coin-shaped device is implanted subcutaneously above the lower-leg fascia, over the posterior tibial nerve. The feasibility study described a 20-minute procedure under local anesthesia.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup><sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> It supplies programmed, automatic, low-duty-cycle intermittent stimulation rather than continuous therapy. Treatment begins after wound healing, and the feasibility protocol activated the implant at about four weeks. Clinicians can adjust stimulation amplitude.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup><sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup><sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The patient does not wear an external power unit or attend recurring treatment sessions, but the system still involves patient-operated equipment. The patient controller magnet stops unwanted stimulation, and the patient must be able to use it as instructed. Battery life depends on settings, and depletion requires device replacement. Wound follow-up, programming and eventual replacement are part of counseling. The implant is not maintenance-free.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Mechanism

eCoin delivers tibial nerve stimulation. Tibial afferents share sacral spinal segments with the bladder and pelvic-floor innervation, and afferent modulation is the proposed basis for reduced urgency and detrusor overactivity. The practical difference from office-based PTNS is automatic delivery without the weekly office sessions used for induction and the maintenance sessions that follow.

## Clinical Evidence

The published studies were open-label and single arm.

| Study | Population and result | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Feasibility trial | 46 participants; at 3 months, 32 of 46 (69.6%) had at least 50% UUI reduction (median daily episodes 4.2 to 1.7), with more than 20% dry at 3 and 6 months | 98% women, mean age 63.4 years; no sham or active comparator |
| Feasibility, 12 months | 65% responders and 26% completely continent | Same 46-participant cohort |
| Pivotal trial | 137 enrolled, 133 implanted, 132 in the intention-to-treat cohort; 68% achieved at least 50% UUI reduction at 48 weeks (95% CI 60–76%) | 98% women, mean age 63.9 years; no sham or active comparator |
| Two-year extension | 72 completers; 78% responders (95% CI 67–87%), 48% with at least 75% reduction and 22% dry at 96 weeks; 91.3% did not require additional OAB medications | Attrition changes the denominator, so the higher rate does not show that efficacy increased over time |

The pivotal report found device-related adverse events in 16% through 52 weeks. The extension reported no serious or unanticipated events during that period, which does not erase earlier wound or device risks. One serious wound-care event requiring intravenous antibiotics occurred in the feasibility trial. Most adverse events relate to wound healing at the implantation site.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup><sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup><sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup><sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup><sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## MRI and Other Safety Requirements

The manufacturer labels eCoin MR Conditional under specified 1.5 T and 3 T conditions, with the implant at least 20 cm from the edge of the scanner bore. The lower leg containing the implant must not enter the scanner. The patient magnet is MR Unsafe. MRI review is model-specific, and an ankle implant does not permit every scan.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Other issues include poor local skin or vascular health, unhealed wounds, inability to operate the controller magnet, diathermy and possible interaction with other implanted electronics. The manufacturer provides specific electrosurgical precautions. Cardiac-device interactions require coordination, and the pivotal study excluded pacemakers and ICDs. Lower-leg activity restrictions apply during healing.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Position Among Options

A patient may prefer automatic intermittent stimulation over repeated office visits or a wearable used for each treatment. That convenience is weighed against finite battery life, surgery, MRI restrictions and the limited comparative evidence. Claims of less retention than medication or better safety than SNM require direct evidence, and single-arm trials do not establish them. Published follow-up extends to two years, so longer durability is unknown.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

The Medtronic implantable tibial neuromodulation system is a rechargeable leadless alternative. In the TITAN 2 single-arm pivotal study of 126 implanted participants, 59% had at least 50% UUI reduction at 6 months (95% CI 49–67%) and 61% at 12 months; 20% had adverse device effects through 12 months. No head-to-head data exist, and the two trials' definitions and populations differ.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

For other choices, see [PTNS systems](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/neuromodulation-devices/ptns-systems), [Revi](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/neuromodulation-devices/revi-system), [Altaviva](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/neuromodulation-devices/altaviva) and [InterStim](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/neuromodulation-devices/medtronic-interstim).
