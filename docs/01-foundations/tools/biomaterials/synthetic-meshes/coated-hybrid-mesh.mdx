# Coated / Hybrid Mesh

**Hybrid mesh** describes a construction, not a single material or clinical indication. A device may combine permanent PP with an absorbable polymer, reinforce an extracellular matrix with permanent or resorbable fibers, or combine multiple degradable components. Coating, partial absorption and complete absorption are not interchangeable.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The aims include easier handling, altered tissue interaction and appropriate load transfer. Permanent PP provides lasting reinforcement but sustains a foreign-body response, while biologic and fully absorbable scaffolds integrate well and lack durable strength. Hybrid constructs are designed so that a biologic or absorbable component offsets the negative attributes of the permanent prosthetic, which keeps the mechanical strength.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> These are not class-wide guarantees of less inflammation, pain, infection or mesh exposure.

## Partially Absorbable Constructs

Partially absorbable meshes combine a permanent, lower-weight PP knit with absorbable fibers that degrade over weeks to months.

- **Vypro and Vypro II** (Ethicon) add polyglactin 910 multifilaments to a low-weight PP knit. The absorbable component stiffens the mesh for handling. In a rat abdominal-wall model, the polyglactin multifilaments reduced macrophages and granulocytes and limited scar to the perifilamentary region.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> A meta-analysis of 10 randomized hernia trials (2,027 patients) found no significant difference from PP in recurrence, pain, urinary tract infection or seroma, and less foreign-body sensation (OR 0.58, 95% CI 0.42 to 0.80).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **A lightweight PP with a poliglecaprone component** (Ultrapro type) was the mesh in the lightweight arm of the sacrocolpopexy study below.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

The form of the absorbable component matters. In the rat model, coating the PP with polyglactin favored a connective-tissue capsule around the whole mesh and appeared to hinder incorporation, while adding polyglactin filaments appeared favorable.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Know What Remains Implanted

| Construction | Example and distinction |
|---|---|
| **Permanent plus absorbable synthetic** | PP/polyglactin or PP/poliglecaprone constructs leave permanent PP after the absorbable component is lost. A lighter residual implant still has permanent-mesh risks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Biologic matrix plus permanent polymer** | OviTex LPR's July 2024 IFU describes ovine extracellular matrix with monofilament PP. Its soft-tissue indications include hernia and abdominal-wall defects; the IFU does not establish superior pelvic-floor outcomes.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Biologic matrix plus resorbable polymer** | OviTex PRS Short-Term Resorbable contains ovine matrix and PGA. Its March 2022 IFU excludes high-load applications such as bridging hernias. A brand-family name does not identify polymer permanence or load-bearing suitability.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Coated / tissue-engineered scaffold** | Surface layers may alter handling, adhesion or biologic response; experimental scaffolds may contain no permanent backbone. Clinical benefit and safe tissue contact require evidence for the exact construction. |

Both cited OviTex IFUs contraindicate use with known sensitivity to sheep-derived material. The LPR document also advises against use with known PP sensitivity. Complications can include infection, pain, adhesions, fistula, recurrence and wound failure; adding an ECM layer does not eliminate them.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

**A coating is not automatically an improvement.** In an older rat abdominal-wall experiment, adding polyglactin fibers and coating the entire PP surface produced different tissue responses; the coating promoted encapsulation. This is evidence that construction matters, not a human ranking of all coated devices.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Partially Absorbable Mesh in Vaginal POP Repair

Two retrospective series and one randomized trial associate partially absorbable mesh with less exposure at some time points. The randomized comparison with native tissue found no significant anatomical or composite benefit, and none of the studies shows a consistent reduction in exposure or a superior replacement for native tissue.

| Study | Design and useful finding | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| **Farthmann 2013** | Randomized 200 women to conventional PP or partially absorbable mesh. Exposure with PP versus partially absorbable mesh was 11.3% vs 3.2% at 3 months (P = 0.0492), 6.6% vs 6.3% at 12 months and 7.5% vs 3.4% at 36 months. The authors described a trend favoring the partially absorbable product. Recurrent prolapse was more frequent with the partially absorbable mesh (P &gt; 0.05).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | A time-point rate is not cumulative lifetime exposure. The 12- and 36-month comparisons did not establish a safety advantage. |
| **Lensen 2013** | Retrospective analysis of prospectively collected data on 569 women (347 non-absorbable, 222 partially absorbable mesh), compared two trocar-guided kits. At 12 months, exposure was 12% vs 5%, and reoperation in untreated compartments was 5% vs 1%. Failure rates were similar.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | Two different kits and a nonrandom comparison, so mesh type is confounded with the kit. |
| **Quemener 2014** | Retrospective series of 250 women with partially absorbable mesh, median follow-up 20 months. Reintervention 8% (mesh exposure 2%, recurrent prolapse 1.2%, urinary complications including de novo SUI 4.8%).<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | The authors concluded that partially absorbable mesh did not appear to give an advantage in reintervention rates over non-absorbable mesh. |
| **Cho 2018** | Retrospective single-center cohort, non-absorbable vs partially absorbable mesh for anterior and apical prolapse. Objective outcomes were better with partially absorbable mesh at 1 and 2 years, and exposure was similar in the two groups. Recurrence was higher with partially absorbable mesh (not significant).<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> | Nonrandom, single center. The abstract reports better objective outcomes and higher recurrence together. |
| **Steures 2019** | Randomized 163 women to partially absorbable mesh or native-tissue repair. At 24 months, anatomical success was 32/71 versus 22/69 (RR 1.4, 95% CI 0.92–2.2) and composite success 88% versus 73% (RR 1.1, 95% CI 0.93–1.4); no significant anatomical or composite benefit was shown. Four exposures were reported (6%).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | Treatment crossover and follow-up denominators matter. No significant difference does not prove equivalence. |
| **Page 2023: abdominal sacrocolpopexy** | Two consecutive single-center cohorts (median follow-up about 8 years) compared heavier-weight PP (more than 44 g/m², n = 101) with lightweight PP (28 g/m²) containing a resorbable poliglecaprone component (n = 238). Graft-related complications occurred in 7.3% (13/178) versus 22.8% (23/101) (HR 3.3, 95% CI 1.6 to 7.1), and reintervention for them in 2.1% versus 18.8%. Patient-reported improvement (PGIC 4 or higher) and anatomical failure did not differ.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | An abdominal study cannot establish safety for transvaginal placement. Weight and the absorbable component differ together, so the study cannot isolate the effect of either. Ethicon supported the audit. |

FDA's transvaginal POP restrictions are not bypassed by describing a device as hybrid or biologic. Keep this indication separate from abdominal prolapse repair and SUI slings.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Reinforced Tissue Matrices

Reinforced tissue matrices embed a polymer in decellularized extracellular matrix. The matrix is intended to recruit host cells and attenuate the foreign-body response while the polymer offloads abdominal pressure. OviTex (TELA Bio) is the best-known example, and the LPR version combines ovine matrix with monofilament PP.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The published data are from hernia and abdominal-wall repair, including a review by authors affiliated with the manufacturer, and pelvic-floor outcome data are lacking.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> No head-to-head trials against standard synthetic mesh exist in urogynecologic indications.

## Tissue-Engineered Constructs

Nanofiber coatings, drug-eluting layers and cell-seeded scaffolds remain active research areas. All of the following are preclinical unless stated.

- **PLCL and fibrinogen co-electrospun mesh.** In a canine abdominal-defect model, the mesh had similar host responses to PP with better early vascularization and tissue organization. In a human comparison for anterior pelvic floor reconstruction, Aa-point improvement at 6 months was greater than with PP (P &lt; 0.005), with follow-up limited to 6 months.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Polyurethane and gelatin nanofiber-coated PP.** No mesh exposure was observed in the treated rat prolapse model, with higher collagen content and fewer macrophages. This did not demonstrate elimination of exposure in women.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **PCL mesh with PLGA nanofibers.** 3D-printed PCL mesh had strength comparable to commercial PP meshes and survived 10,000 fatigue cycles. Metronidazole, lidocaine and estradiol released for 4, 25 and 30 days in vitro, and CTGF for more than 30 days. The mesh lost mechanical strength over time in animals as the polymer degraded.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- **Polystyrene and gelatin core-sheath nanofibers.** The gelatin sheath provides a hydrophilic surface over a non-degradable core, and the implant switched to an accommodation immune phenotype by 2 weeks in vivo.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **PCL mesh with CTGF, PEG-fibrinogen and mesenchymal stem cells.** In elderly rats at about 53 weeks, the mesh showed support, biocompatibility and no mesh-related complications in an abdominal repair model.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- **Chitosan, PLA and chitosan trilayer for urethral reconstruction.** In vitro, the scaffold had higher elastic modulus and tensile strength than PLA or acellular dermis, with smooth muscle cell growth and greater angiogenic potential in a chicken embryo assay. It has not been used in clinical urethral reconstruction.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Collagen and PLAC hybrid for bladder regeneration.** Human bladder smooth muscle and urothelial cells proliferated on the hybrid in vitro, and the hybrid produced less inflammation than PLAC mesh alone after subcutaneous implantation in nude mice.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

Animal histology, antimicrobial release and in-vitro strength should not be converted into a clinical recommendation without appropriate human evidence, regulatory status and durable patient outcomes. Hernia studies likewise cannot establish vaginal safety or continence efficacy. Reviews describe drug-eluting layers, 3D-printed patient-specific scaffolds and cell-seeded constructs as directions for development.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

## Current Use

- **Midurethral slings.** Hybrid slings are not in widespread clinical use. See [Polypropylene Mesh](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/synthetic-meshes/polypropylene) for sling material.
- **Sacrocolpopexy.** Lightweight PP is the usual material. Hybrid constructs have not been widely adopted.
- **Transvaginal POP repair.** Mesh products for vaginal POP have been withdrawn in many countries, and hybrid mesh did not change that regulatory position.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
- **Urologic reconstruction.** Tissue-engineered hybrid scaffolds with biologic components or cells are the most active research area, and all remain investigational.

## Practical Use

Before choosing a hybrid device, identify its exact components, indication and jurisdiction; determine whether reinforcement remains permanent; and check tissue-contact, load, allergy and handling restrictions in that version's IFU. For pelvic-floor decisions, use the condition and procedure evidence rather than the material's marketing category.
