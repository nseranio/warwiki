# Absorbable Synthetic Mesh

**Absorbable synthetic meshes** are polymers that degrade by hydrolysis in vivo. They provide temporary reinforcement while host tissue infiltrates and remodels, with the aim of leaving no permanent foreign body and so avoiding long-term erosion, chronic pain and late infection.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> Polymer loss, declining mesh strength and the strength of the repaired tissue are different measurements. Complete resorption does not guarantee freedom from recurrence, scarring, pain, infection, erosion or fistula.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Fully absorbable mesh should not be substituted for a permanent sling or prolapse implant merely because both are called surgical mesh.

## Materials and Support Duration

| Material / example | What the evidence or product information establishes |
|---|---|
| **Polyglactin 910 / rapidly absorbable polymers** | Provide short-lived reinforcement. In a rat abdominal-wall model, polyglactin 910 (Vicryl) lost 50% of its mechanical stability within 3 weeks. It produced a pronounced early inflammatory reaction and more connective tissue, followed by loss of stability and dilatation of the repair. The authors linked this to the frequent incisional hernias with short-term absorbable meshes. It does not establish that every absorbable reconstruction fails.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Polylactide (PLA)** | The long-term absorbable polylactide mesh in the same rat model kept more than 50% of its mechanical stability for over 1 year, with less inflammation and connective tissue than polyglactin.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **P4HB / Phasix** | Fully resorbable monofilament mesh; manufacturer information describes degradation over approximately 12–18 months. This is not a promise of unchanged tensile strength throughout that interval.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **PGA:TMC / GORE BIO-A** | A scaffold containing 67% polyglycolic acid and 33% trimethylene carbonate, with an approximately 6–7-month resorption period. It is not intended where permanent mesh support is required.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **TIGR Matrix and other dual-fiber constructs** | A review of preclinical abdominal-wall studies reported only partial remodeling with a persistent foreign-body reaction after 1 year for TIGR Matrix, and good remodeling of GORE BIO-A at 6 to 12 months. Chemistry and architecture differ substantially, so use the exact product's strength-retention and resorption data rather than a class-wide support duration.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| **Partially absorbable PP constructs** | The absorbable component disappears but PP remains permanently. See [Coated / Hybrid Mesh](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/synthetic-meshes/coated-hybrid-mesh). |

The desired gradual transfer of load to healing tissue is a design goal, not proof of durable pelvic support. Rat and rabbit comparisons of Phasix and BIO-A measured inflammation, repair thickness, strength and bacterial colonization under experimental conditions. In rats, BIO-A resorbed faster, with a greater inflammatory response at 4 weeks and a thinner repair site than Phasix at 16 and 24 weeks. In rabbits inoculated with MRSA, Phasix had lower abscess scores and bacterial colonization and greater strength than BIO-A. The authors, who were consultants to the Phasix manufacturer, attributed this to monofilament macroporous versus multifilament microporous construction. These findings do not demonstrate lower clinical pelvic infection rates.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Product Safety Boundaries

:::warning[Check the exact product and jurisdiction]
Phasix should not be used where permanent wound or organ support from mesh is required. Its US safety information prohibits direct contact with bowel or viscera, advises avoidance with known tetracycline or kanamycin allergy, and notes that unresolved infection may require removal. The manufacturer's CE-marked product information explicitly contraindicates POP repair and SUI treatment. This jurisdiction-specific wording should not be erased by a generic soft-tissue indication.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
:::

GORE BIO-A is indicated for reinforcement of soft tissue, including hernia-related applications; its product information contraindicates use requiring permanent support and reconstruction of cardiovascular defects. Resorbability is not an infection-proofing or urinary-contact claim.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

A product cleared for abdominal-wall reinforcement has not thereby established benefit for a vaginal prolapse repair, midurethral sling, bladder patch or urethral replacement. FDA's 2019 transvaginal POP order was not limited to permanent PP: the affected products included Xenform, a biologic matrix.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Pelvic Organ Prolapse Evidence

The 2024 Cochrane review did not establish a benefit of absorbable mesh over native-tissue vaginal repair. Evidence for individual outcomes came from a small trial (54–66 assessed women) with wide confidence intervals; lack of a statistically detectable difference is not proof of equivalent durability. The review does not support routine absorbable-mesh augmentation for vaginal POP.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

A 2024 sheep study implanted P4HB or PP in the posterior vaginal wall. Exposures at 12 months occurred in 3/8 P4HB and 4/8 PP explants; at 24 months they occurred in 0/8 and 4/8, respectively. P4HB had fully resorbed with maintained explant stiffness at the later endpoint, and P4HB explants had higher collagen content and less myofibroblast differentiation than PP at 24 months. The study was funded in part by the P4HB manufacturer. These separate animal groups do not show that early human exposures resolve, or establish a safe clinical replacement for PP.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Urinary Exposure: Keep Materials and Models Separate

Urine can alter material performance, but findings depend on the polymer, construction, pH, organism and test conditions. A suture immersion experiment found that PDS lost all strength after 3 days in sterile urine, and PGA lost 64% after 10 days. In *Proteus*-infected urine, both lost all strength after 1 day. Catgut kept its strength.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> A different experiment using polyglactin 910 mesh found that at day 21 breaking strength was below 10% of control in serum and culture media and 12% to 24% of control in urine, and below 5% in all solutions by day 38. Degradation was prolonged in low-pH and infected urine, and minimal at 38 days in infected urine at pH 5.0.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> The first study tested sutures and the second tested mesh, so neither justifies a universal claim that every absorbable mesh or PDS suture loses all strength after three days in any urinary reconstruction. The findings do mean that the mechanical window of a short-term absorbable in urine is unpredictable, and a short-term absorbable should not be the sole load-bearing element in genitourinary reconstruction.

Do not prescribe urinary acidification, alkalinization or tolerated infection to manipulate mesh degradation on the basis of these bench findings. For an anastomosis, choose a clinically appropriate suture and technique; for an implant exposed to urine, establish product-specific compatibility and the reconstructive plan.

## Investigational GU Reconstruction

Cell-seeded polymer scaffolds for bladder reconstruction and bioengineered urethral replacements remain research approaches.

- **Bladder augmentation scaffolds.** In a canine augmentation cystoplasty model, a PLGA mesh seeded with autologous urothelial and smooth muscle cells restored baseline urodynamics as early as 4 months, and the unseeded scaffold did not return to baseline by 9 months. The difference was associated with cell seeding. This is not evidence that an off-the-shelf absorbable hernia mesh can replace bowel augmentation or reconstruct a human bladder wall.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **PLGA composites.** PLGA mesh coated with fibrin or collagen was compatible with differentiated adipose-derived stem cells in vitro, and PLGA-fibrin gave better leak and porosity properties. Tensile properties were measured on the bench only.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Renal hemostasis.** In a rabbit partial nephrectomy model, PGA mesh stopped bleeding immediately. The fibers were digested between 2 weeks and 2 months and the mesh was completely resorbed by 3 months, leaving a new fibrous capsule with no renal reaction and no difference from an omental fat graft.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Biodegradable urologic stents.** Self-reinforced 80L/20G PLGA stents degraded faster in artificial urine than in phosphate buffer, and the in vitro degradation rate coincided with a clinical test in the authors' report.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

When mesh avoidance is a priority, discuss established native-tissue operations or [autologous fascia](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/autologous-tissue/rectus-fascia) as appropriate to the indication. Material innovation should be evaluated against meaningful symptom, recurrence, reoperation and long-term safety outcomes.

## Advantages and Limitations

The theoretical advantages are no permanent foreign body and organized collagen remodeling rather than dense scar, particularly with P4HB in the sheep model.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> Monofilament macroporous designs showed lower bacterial colonization in the rabbit model.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

The limitations are these:

- Recurrence risk if tissue regeneration lags resorption. Short-term absorbable meshes used as an abdominal inlay have been reported to result in large incisional hernias in almost all cases, which the authors of the rat study linked to the loss of stability at 2 to 3 weeks.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Unpredictable degradation in urine.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- No support for transvaginal POP repair in the 2024 Cochrane review.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Clinical evidence in pelvic floor surgery is limited, and no study reviewed here compares absorbable with permanent synthetic mesh in these indications.

## Current Use

In pelvic floor reconstruction, absorbable mesh has largely been supplanted by native-tissue repair when mesh avoidance is the goal, by permanent PP mesh where augmentation is indicated and approved, and by [autologous rectus fascia](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/autologous-tissue/rectus-fascia) when permanent biologic support is preferred. P4HB scaffolds and partially absorbable constructs are candidates for a future clinical role, and cell-seeded PLGA constructs are the main investigational route for bladder regeneration.
