# Tutoplast-Processed Allografts

**Tutoplast** is a tissue-processing method, not a single graft. This page concerns donated human pericardium, fascia lata and dermis. Tissue source, processing and operation matter: outcomes with bovine pericardium, autologous fascia, freeze-dried cadaveric fascia and solvent-dehydrated Tutoplast fascia cannot be combined as if they describe one implant.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Coloplast currently lists Tutoplast pericardium, Suspend fascia lata and Axis dermis. Its February 2026 Axis/Suspend safety statement includes supplemental suburethral support for SUI and pelvic-floor fascial reinforcement for POP. These are 361 human cell and tissue products restricted to homologous use. Availability and this regulatory category do not establish superiority over another operation or material.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Related materials: [Autologous Fascia Lata](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/autologous-tissue/fascia-lata), [Bovine-Derived Grafts](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/biological-grafts/bovine-dermal), and [Human Acellular Dermal Matrix](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/biological-grafts/human-acellular-dermal-matrix).

## Preparation and safety

The currently linked Tutoplast pericardium IFU, DOC 5052 R3 dated June 29, 2018, describes solvent dehydration and terminal low-dose gamma irradiation to a sterility assurance level of 10⁻⁶. It does not provide a universal irradiation dose for all Tutoplast products.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For that pericardium product, soak in room-temperature sterile saline for up to 30 minutes to improve handling. Use it under minor to moderate tension, place stitches 2–3 mm from the edge, and select long-lasting material if using absorbable sutures. It is for one patient on one occasion, without resterilization. Inspect packaging, expiration and labeling, and complete the tissue-utilization record for traceability. The two-minute soak advertised for Axis/Suspend is not the preparation instruction for every Tutoplast graft.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Processing and donor screening reduce infectious risk but do not eliminate it. The label warns about potential disease transmission, localized immunological reactions, trace processing chemicals, active infection and poor perfusion. Host response determines incorporation. Do not promise a DNA-free implant, zero prion risk, no inflammatory reaction or assured replacement by functional native tissue.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

In a rat model of tunical substitution (20 animals, 4 months), Tutoplast pericardium did not change erectile function on cavernosal-nerve stimulation. Histology showed mild to moderate fibrosis around the patch. The authors concluded that the graft was strong enough to withstand intracorporeal pressure and called for long-term human follow-up.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Peyronie's grafting

Pericardium is an available option for tunical reconstruction in appropriately selected patients, but current EAU guidance identifies no best graft material. An off-the-shelf graft avoids tissue harvest. It does not remove the operation's risks of erectile dysfunction, sensory change, recurrent curvature or shortening. Baseline erectile function, deformity and the extent of tunical incision or excision guide counseling.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### Pericardium series

All the series below are retrospective and use different definitions of success. They describe outcomes in selected men and do not compare graft materials directly, except where stated.

| Series | n and follow-up | Reported outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Hellstrom and Reddy 2000 | 11 men; mean 14 months | Curvature resolved in all 11 (3 also had a penile prosthesis placed for erectile dysfunction). No rejection or infection. One urethro-corporal communication led to explantation of a single cylinder.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Chun 2001 (dermal vs pericardial, modified Horton-Devine) | 27 men (18 dermal, 9 pericardial); mean 10 months | Results were comparable between grafts. Of the 24 reached by telephone, 22 reported successful coitus and 15 needed assistance for a rigid erection. Eight reported minimal recurrent curvature.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Levine and Estrada 2003 | 40 men; mean 22 months | Straightening in 39/40 (98%); coitus in 38 (95%); full unaided erections in 28 (70%). Twelve men (30%) had erectile dysfunction needing pharmacological assistance. No major or graft-related complications.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Usta 2003 | 19 men grafted with Tutoplast pericardium (61 men in the whole cohort); mean 21.9 months | Complete straightening in 15/19 (78.9%). Residual curvature above 30° in 3 (15.7%).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |

Taylor and Levine reported 81 men who had partial plaque excision and Tutoplast pericardial grafting, followed for an average of 58 months (range 6–185). At survey, 91% reported residual curvature below 30°, which is not necessarily a completely straight penis. Rigidity was unchanged or better in 68%. Rigidity was adequate for coitus in 79%, with or without a PDE5 inhibitor, and 75% were satisfied. Objective flaccid stretched length changed by an average of 0.2 cm (range −1.5 to 2.0). Treatment was assigned by the authors' algorithm, not randomized against plication or another graft.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

### Dermal, pericardial and SIS grafts compared

Kovac and Brock retrospectively reviewed 36 men grafted between 1999 and 2005 (mean follow-up 673 days) and found the following self-reported outcomes.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

| Graft | Curvature resolved | Length maintained | Rigidity maintained |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tutoplast pericardium | 100% | 23% | 39% |
| Dermal graft | 60% | 30% | 60% |
| Stratasis (porcine SIS) | 76.9% | 54% | 77% |

The authors reported less loss of length and rigidity, and a significant IIEF-5 improvement, with Stratasis. The groups were small and unmatched, and the results are self-reported.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

### Fascia lata

For Tutoplast fascia lata, a separate 14-man series reported 11 completely straight penises, one new case of erectile dysfunction and four men reporting shortening greater than 1 cm at a mean 31 months. Thirteen of 14 were satisfied. This cannot establish lower erectile-dysfunction risk than pericardium from a different cohort.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

### Comparative reviews

A 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis of grafts in Peyronie's surgery without a prosthesis included mostly single-arm studies at high risk of bias. Buccal mucosa grafts had the highest straightening rates and the least de novo erectile dysfunction. TachoSil performed best when preoperative curvature was taken into account. Tutoplast grafts had a higher incidence of postoperative erectile dysfunction. The authors state that randomized comparisons are still needed to determine graft superiority.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

Small retrospective comparisons and pooled single-arm series differ in baseline erectile function, curvature, surgical technique and follow-up. They do not justify a ranking of buccal mucosa, TachoSil, SIS and pericardium. They also do not show why one material has higher erectile-dysfunction rates. The mild to moderate perigraft fibrosis seen in the rat model is one candidate mechanism, and it has not been shown in men.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> See the [Peyronie's Disease surgical atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease).

## Corporoplasty with a penile prosthesis

Palese and Burnett described Tutoplast pericardium as a corporoplasty patch during complex prosthesis surgery. The situations they listed were penile fibrosis, prosthesis erosion and a severely damaged or deficient corporal body. They noted that infections associated with synthetic graft materials limit their use in these repairs.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

In Farrell 2019, 15 men receiving pericardium were compared retrospectively with 18 receiving hemostatic patches: ten Evarrest, six Nu-Knit and only two TachoSil. Median follow-up was 34.6 months for pericardium versus 6.6 months for patches. Operative time was shorter in the pooled patch group (122 vs 166 minutes, p = 0.01). Residual curvature above 20° occurred in 2/15 with pericardium and 3/18 with patches. Penetrative intercourse was reported by 14/15 and 17/18. No material-attributable complications or prosthesis herniations occurred in either group.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

This was not a TachoSil randomized trial, and its small groups with unequal surveillance do not establish equivalent durability or lower infection risk. Defect closure, infection management and prosthesis selection remain operation-specific decisions.

## Pubovaginal slings

Allograft fascia avoids a harvest incision, but continence durability has been inconsistent. Results also depend on the particular tissue-bank preparation. Freeze-dried fascia studies are not automatically Tutoplast studies.

### Early series

| Series | Design | Reported outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Wright 1998 | 59 allograft vs 33 autograft slings for intrinsic sphincter deficiency; mean 11.5 months | Similar improvement on the SEAPI score. No infection or erosion. Operative time and hospital stay were shorter with allograft.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| Brown and Govier 2000 | 121 cadaveric vs 46 autologous fascia lata slings; 12 vs 44 months | Of 104 cadaveric-sling responders, 85% reported cure of stress incontinence. Follow-up was much shorter in the allograft group.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> |
| Amundsen 2000 | 104 freeze-dried, nonirradiated allograft slings from one tissue bank; mean 19.4 months | Daily pad use fell from 4.6 to 1.1. Urge incontinence resolved in 41% of those who had it.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| Flynn and Yap 2002 | 63 allograft vs 71 autograft slings, single surgeon; minimum 2 years (mean 29 vs 44 months) | Cure of stress and urge incontinence in 45/63 vs 55/71 (p = 0.42). Recurrent SUI in 8 vs 7 (p = 0.58). Less postoperative pain and disability with allograft.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |

### Later and comparative series

| Series | Design | Reported outcome |
|---|---|---|
| FitzGerald 1999 | 12 women reoperated after failure of irradiated, freeze-dried donor fascia lata grafts; graft retrieved in 7 | Histology showed disorganized remodeling and degeneration in most retrieved grafts, and an immune reaction in some. The authors advised against using this tissue in urogynecologic procedures.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> |
| Soergel 2001 | 12 fascia lata allograft vs 33 rectus fascia slings for intrinsic sphincter deficiency | Success in 33.3% vs 78.8% (p = 0.006). The abstract does not identify the processing method.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> |
| McBride 2005 | Suspend Tutoplast fascia lata (32) vs autologous fascia lata (39), reviewed at 2 years or more | Only 47 of 71 women were assessed. Urodynamic stress incontinence was found in 41.7% of assessed allograft patients vs none of the autograft patients (p = 0.007). Subjective outcomes did not differ.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> |
| Howden 2006 | 303 women, 153 autologous and 150 cadaveric fascia, observational | Regular leakage 39.6% vs 28.3% (p = 0.04) and reoperation for SUI 12.7% vs 3.3% (p = 0.003), cadaveric vs autologous. Adjusted for follow-up, reoperation was 4 vs 1 per 100 women-years.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> |

The McBride 41.7% estimate should not be applied to all 32 originally treated women. Howden is a broader cadaveric-fascia comparison and not a randomized estimate for every current Suspend product. Together with FitzGerald and Soergel, these series support caution about the durability of cadaveric fascia in slings.

The AUA/SUFU 2023 guideline panel notes that efforts to substitute porcine dermis or cadaveric fascia for autologous fascia have shown inferior results.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> The AUA/SUFU surgical options for an index patient include synthetic midurethral sling, autologous fascia pubovaginal sling, Burch colposuspension and bulking agents. Cadaveric graft availability should not be interpreted as guideline endorsement of equivalent long-term continence.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

## Prolapse support

Keep abdominal sacrocolpopexy evidence separate from vaginal graft augmentation. In Loffeld 2009, a retrospective comparison of laparoscopic sacrocolpopexy with Tutoplast fascia lata (19) or Prolene mesh (20) at a mean 45 months, the relative risk of reintervention for recurrent prolapse was 2.9 with Tutoplast (95% CI 0.9–9.5). Women in the Prolene group were more satisfied.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

The Culligan 2005 randomized study enrolled 100 women with posthysterectomy vault prolapse and compared cadaveric fascia lata with polypropylene mesh. Four assigned fascia received polypropylene because of a supply shortage, and the published outcome groups followed the received material. Among 89 one-year attendees, objective success was 41/45 with mesh and 30/44 with fascia (p = 0.007). Failure was any postoperative POP-Q stage 2 or greater. Failures were anterior or posterior, with no apical point-C failures. Subjective outcomes were not reported in that paper, and the trial was too small to establish comparative safety.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

At five years, only 58 women returned, 29 per group. Objective success was 27/29 with mesh and 18/29 with fascia (p = 0.02). Clinical success, defined with symptoms, was 28/29 and 26/29 without a statistically significant difference (p = 0.61). Attrition and the different outcome definitions matter.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

These findings support concern about anatomical durability of cadaveric fascia in sacrocolpopexy. They do not prove that all human allografts are ineffective, that synthetic mesh is risk-free, or that the same estimates apply to vaginal prolapse repair. Current Axis/Suspend labeling and individualized procedural evidence should be assessed separately.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Venous reconstruction

Bovine pericardium, which is a different product from Tutoplast human pericardium, was used as a patch or tubularized graft for complex urologic venous reconstruction in 7 patients (IVC and iliac patches, left renal vein replacement, renal autotransplantation). No venous thrombosis was seen at a mean 14.8 months.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup> See [Bovine-Derived Grafts](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/biological-grafts/bovine-dermal) for bovine materials.

## Positioning

**Reasons authors have given for choosing cadaveric pericardium or fascia** include avoiding a second incision and its morbidity, minimal preparation, pliability, and shorter operative time in sling series.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)[[16]](#ref16)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

**Limits reported in the series above:**

- Sling and sacrocolpopexy durability was inferior to autologous fascia and polypropylene mesh.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)[[26]](#ref26)[[27]](#ref27)</sup>
- Postoperative erectile dysfunction was more frequent with Tutoplast than with buccal mucosa in the 2024 meta-analysis, and Kovac reported less length and rigidity preservation than with SIS.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- Availability depends on the manufacturer's current listing, which should be checked before planning a case.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

| Use | Evidence in this page | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Peyronie's grafting, pericardium | Retrospective series; curvature resolved in 79–100% | Erectile dysfunction risk needs counseling; buccal mucosa and TachoSil ranked ahead in the 2024 meta-analysis |
| Peyronie's grafting, fascia lata | One 14-man series | Shortening above 1 cm in 4 of 14 |
| Corporoplasty with prosthesis | Small comparative series | Outcomes similar to hemostatic patches; longer operative time |
| Pubovaginal sling, cadaveric fascia | Retrospective series and one large cohort | More SUI recurrence and reoperation than autologous fascia |
| Sacrocolpopexy, cadaveric fascia lata | Randomized trial to 5 years | Lower objective anatomic success than polypropylene mesh |
