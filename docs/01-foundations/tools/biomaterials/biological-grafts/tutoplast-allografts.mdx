# Tutoplast-Processed Allografts (Cadaveric Pericardium &amp; Fascia)

**Tutoplast** is a proprietary tissue-sterilization process (originally **Tutogen Medical**, now **RTI Surgical / Surgalign**) that prepares human cadaveric and bovine tissues for surgical implantation. The process combines **osmotic-, oxidative-, and alkaline-treatment steps**, **solvent dehydration** (acetone), and **low-dose gamma irradiation (17.8 kGy)** to inactivate viruses, prions, and bacteria while preserving the native collagen ultrastructure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The resulting graft is dry, room-temperature-stable, off-the-shelf, and rehydrates in saline in 1–2 minutes.

In reconstructive urology and urogynecology, three Tutoplast-processed products are clinically relevant:

- **Tutoplast pericardium** — human cadaveric pericardium for **Peyronie's plaque-incision-and-grafting**.
- **Tutopatch / Tutomesh** — bovine pericardium variants (covered in [Bovine-Derived Grafts](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/biological-grafts/bovine-dermal)).
- **Tutoplast fascia lata** — human cadaveric fascia lata for **pubovaginal slings** and **Peyronie's grafting**, used when autologous fascia harvest is undesirable.

For the autologous counterpart see [Autologous Fascia Lata](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/autologous-tissue/fascia-lata).

## Material Science &amp; Processing

The Tutoplast sequence:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

1. **Osmotic treatment** (alternating hypertonic / hypotonic baths) — disrupts cell membranes, removes cellular debris.
2. **Oxidative treatment** (hydrogen peroxide) — destroys residual antigenic proteins.
3. **Alkaline treatment** (sodium hydroxide) — inactivates prions; validated against TSE / vCJD risk.
4. **Solvent dehydration** (acetone) — preserves collagen triple-helix integrity while permitting room-temperature storage.
5. **Double-sterile packaging** + terminal **low-dose gamma irradiation** (~ 17.8 kGy).

Histologic analysis after Tutoplast processing shows preserved collagen architecture with **no residual donor cells, DNA, or HLA antigens** — meeting AATB and FDA HCT/P standards.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Mechanical testing confirms tensile strength comparable to native tissue, with rehydrated grafts handling and suturing like autologous fascia.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The acetone-dehydration step distinguishes Tutoplast from freeze-dried or solvent-detergent allografts (eg AlloDerm); the trade-off is excellent shelf stability against limited host-cell remodeling (the graft acts more as a **biologic scaffold for fibrous incorporation** than as a tissue-regeneration template).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Tutoplast Pericardium — Peyronie's Disease

Tutoplast pericardium has the **longest off-the-shelf track record** of any cadaveric graft for Peyronie's plaque-incision and grafting (PIG).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

**Key series:**

| Study | n | Follow-up | Outcomes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Egydio 2002<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | 33 | 6–24 mo | Complete straightening 91%; preserved erections 88%; **geometric incision technique** anchored on this graft |
| Usta 2004<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | 40 | Mean 30 mo | Curvature correction 87.5%; de novo ED 17.5%; **higher ED than vein graft series** |
| Hatzichristou 2002<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | 28 | Mean 21 mo | Complete straightening 79%; satisfaction 75% |
| Egydio 2008<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | 100 | Mean 47 mo | Straightening 95%; preserved erection 89%; durable at 4 yr |
| Pathak 2020<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> | 28 | 12 mo | Straightening 86%; mean curvature 65° → 7°; 21% de novo ED |

**Comparative context** (Natsos 2024 systematic review of grafts for Peyronie's):<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

- **Buccal mucosa graft (BMG)** — highest straightening rate, lowest de novo ED.
- **TachoSil collagen fleece** — best overall when preoperative curvature severity is weighted in.
- **Tutoplast cadaveric pericardium** — durable straightening but **higher incidence of postoperative ED** than BMG or saphenous vein.
- No randomized trial has definitively established graft superiority; selection is surgeon-driven.

The **ED signal** with Tutoplast pericardium is the principal limitation. Proposed mechanisms include cavernosal venous-leak from the relatively non-elastic graft, perigraft fibrosis with veno-occlusive dysfunction, and patient-selection bias (Tutoplast is often chosen for the most severe curvatures requiring the largest grafts).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Tutoplast Fascia Lata — Pubovaginal Slings &amp; Peyronie's

### Female SUI — Pubovaginal Sling

**Cadaveric fascia lata** (Tutoplast-processed and freeze-dried variants) was widely used in the late 1990s and early 2000s as an **off-the-shelf alternative to autologous rectus fascia or fascia lata** when patients declined the donor-site incision.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

**Critical historical signal — high failure rates with freeze-dried preparations:**

| Study | n | Material | Outcomes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fitzgerald 1999<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> | 35 | Freeze-dried cadaveric fascia | **20% graft disintegration** at re-exploration; **37% objective failure** at 12 mo |
| Brown 2000<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> | 19 | Cadaveric fascia | High failure at 12 mo prompted abandonment in some centers |
| Soergel 2001<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> | — | Tutoplast solvent-dehydrated | Lower disintegration than freeze-dried; outcomes closer to autologous |

The **Fitzgerald signal** drove a generation-wide retreat from cadaveric slings; many centers reverted to autologous fascia. Subsequent work distinguished **solvent-dehydrated Tutoplast preparations** (more durable) from **freeze-dried cadaveric fascia** (the failure-prone preparation), but the synthetic mid-urethral mesh era subsequently displaced cadaveric slings in mainstream practice.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

### Peyronie's Disease — Tutoplast Fascia Lata Grafting

Kalsi 2006<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> reported the largest dedicated Tutoplast-fascia-lata Peyronie's series — n = 14, plaque-incision-and-grafting:

- Complete straightening 11/14 (**79%**)
- Patient satisfaction 13/14 (93%)
- De novo ED in 1 patient (7%)
- No graft retraction, infection, or rejection

Lower ED rate than reported with Tutoplast pericardium in the same era — though small numbers limit conclusions.

## Indications &amp; Positioning

**When Tutoplast-processed grafts make sense:**

- Patient declines an autologous donor-site incision (cosmetic concern, prior abdominal surgery, BMI).
- Re-do Peyronie's grafting where autologous tissue is exhausted.
- Long graft requirement (eg circumferential or bilateral plaque) where autologous yield is insufficient.
- Off-the-shelf availability needed (no preoperative harvest planning).

**When to choose otherwise:**

- **BMG remains the contemporary graft of choice** for Peyronie's where straightening and ED-preservation are jointly prioritized.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Autologous rectus fascia** remains the gold-standard PVS material for female SUI; cadaveric slings are not first-line.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- For male SUI / continence devices, synthetic slings (AdVance) and the AMS 800 AUS are the modern mainstays — cadaveric fascia has no current male-SUI role.

## Safety &amp; Regulatory Status

- **Disease transmission**: no confirmed cases of viral or prion transmission attributable to Tutoplast processing in any indication; AATB tissue-banking standards apply.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Immunogenicity**: clinical antigenic reactions are exceedingly rare; preserved collagen scaffold integrates as a fibrous template.
- **Manufacturer transitions**: Tutogen → RTI Biologics → RTI Surgical → **Surgalign Holdings** (2020); product availability has varied by region. Several Tutoplast-branded products were temporarily off-market during corporate restructuring.
