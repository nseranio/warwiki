# Bovine-Derived Grafts (Pericardium &amp; Dermis)

Bovine pericardium and bovine dermis are different xenograft materials. Processing, crosslinking, thickness, preservation and the intended implantation site matter more than the animal source alone. Clinical experience with one preparation does not establish the performance or labeling of another.

:::warning[Check the product and intended use]
The reviewed Peri-Guard instructions describe a glutaraldehyde-crosslinked bovine pericardial patch for specified cardiovascular and thoracic repairs; they do not list Peyronie's grafting, urethroplasty or bladder augmentation as indications. The published urologic uses are outside those labeled indications. The US FDA's April 2019 stop-sale order for transvaginal POP devices included Xenform, a bovine-derived graft, as well as synthetic products.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
:::

## Material and handling

The reviewed Peri-Guard IFU describes tissue stored wet in a water/propylene-oxide solution, with glutaraldehyde processing and subsequent sterilization. It requires immersion and agitation in 500 mL of sterile normal saline for at least three minutes before use. This is a product-specific instruction, not a generic preparation method for all bovine grafts. Bovine-material hypersensitivity is a contraindication; reuse and resterilization are prohibited. Fibrosis, inflammation and calcification remain possible despite processing.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Products named in the urologic literature include Peri-Guard and Veritas (bovine pericardium; Synovis/Baxter) and Xenform (non-crosslinked bovine dermis; Boston Scientific). Peri-Guard is described as glutaraldehyde-fixed and Xenform as non-crosslinked.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Glutaraldehyde fixation delays immune rejection but compromises regenerative potential, whereas unfixed scaffolds that keep native matrix architecture allow host-cell repopulation. In a rabbit implantation study, both SDS decellularization and glutaraldehyde fixation disrupted matrix architecture and provoked foreign-body response with fibrotic encapsulation, and an experimental amidosulfobetaine-14 (ASB-14) process that removed hydrophilic and lipophilic antigens avoided it. That process is investigational.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> Fixed grafts are generally chosen for load-bearing use and unfixed grafts for remodeling, but that is a design expectation, not a demonstrated clinical difference.

Crosslinking changes durability and remodeling, but neither “crosslinked” nor “non-crosslinked” guarantees incorporation, freedom from infection or preservation of organ function. Use the actual package IFU and confirm its indication, preparation and fixation requirements. Historical papers may describe preparations differently from currently distributed products.

## Peyronie's disease

Bovine pericardium is one of several materials used in tunical lengthening procedures. Current EAU guidance does not identify one graft material as best. Patient selection, baseline erectile function, the deformity and the operative technique determine the tradeoff between straightening and risks such as erectile dysfunction, recurrent curvature, sensory change and shortening. See the [Peyronie's surgical atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease) for procedure selection.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

In a rat model of tunica albuginea substitution, cadaveric pericardium, dermis, vein and Gore-Tex all preserved erectile function on nerve stimulation. Fibrosis was minimal with dermal and vein grafts, moderate to severe with Gore-Tex, and moderate at 4 months but minimal at 6 months with pericardium. These are cadaveric human tissues in rats, not bovine grafts.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

Clinical cohorts illustrate the limits of material-specific claims:

| Source | What was observed | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| **Romero Otero et al.**, prospective single-surgeon series: 43 treated, 41 followed; mean 14 months | Complete straightening in **33/41 (80.5%)**; 31/41 reported intercourse without further ED treatment; 35/41 would choose surgery again. One glans ischemic event, one edema event and two sensory-loss events occurred. | The cohort was highly selected: age under 55, good baseline erectile function and limited vascular risk. Plaque excision, occasional plication and postoperative rehabilitation accompanied the graft. It does not show universal erectile preservation, superior material performance or a significant length gain.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Caraceni et al.**, Xenform series: 28 treated, **16 assessed at one year or longer** | Complete straightening in 75% of those assessed; ED in 25% and reduced glans sensitivity in 43.8%; one hematoma required surgical revision. | The outcomes are not based on all 28 treated patients. A small uncontrolled study cannot establish low complication risk or equivalence to other grafts.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |

A 21-man retrospective series used an H-shaped incision with a bovine pericardial graft for curvature above 60° in men without preoperative erectile dysfunction. At 1 year, curvature fell from a median 70° to 5° (p &lt; 0.001), penile Doppler resistive index and erection hardness score did not change, and patient satisfaction was high. Satisfaction correlated with the size of the intraoperative defect and inversely with residual curvature.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> A retrospective comparison of 18 bovine pericardium and 15 testicular tunica vaginalis grafts (33 men, 30 months) found similar curvature correction, IIEF-5 and length change.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> A 2024 systematic review found that buccal mucosa had the highest straightening rates and the least de novo erectile dysfunction, that TachoSil performed best when preoperative curvature was considered, and that most studies were single-arm with high risk of bias. Randomized comparisons are needed.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

Uncontrolled pooled success rates should not be used to rank bovine pericardium above or below oral mucosa, porcine matrices or collagen patches: populations, operations, outcome definitions and follow-up differ.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Pelvic organ prolapse and continence surgery

**Transvaginal POP repair requires separate regulatory and clinical interpretation.** Xenform is not a currently authorized US transvaginal POP option. The FDA's subsequent review of its completed postmarket study continued to find an unfavorable benefit–risk profile for transvaginal POP use.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- A 94-patient randomized anterior-repair trial found no statistically significant improvement from bovine collagen reinforcement; 72 patients were assessed at one year and 59 at two years. This was not proof of equal efficacy or safety.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- The 2022 Xenform study was nonrandomized, comparing 228 graft patients with 485 native-tissue repairs using propensity adjustment. At 36 months, reported composite success was 83.6% versus 80.5%. It met its specified noninferiority margins, but this does not demonstrate superiority or erase the FDA's regulatory conclusion. Serious adverse events were reported in 12/228 versus 13/485, and exposure in 2/228 graft patients.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- The six-year PROSPECT follow-up found no symptom benefit from adding biological graft to primary vaginal wall repair. It studied a group of biological materials, not a randomized comparison proving the performance of a particular bovine brand; 65% returned six-year questionnaires and there was no examination at that follow-up. The 2023 analysis included 1,348 women.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

- The 2024 Cochrane review of transvaginal repair (51 trials, 7,846 women) found no difference between biological graft and native tissue repair in awareness of prolapse, repeat surgery or recurrence on examination. It concluded that data do not support biological grafts for transvaginal prolapse repair.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> A 2024 narrative review found modest evidence for anterior-wall augmentation with biologic grafts and called for trials in recurrent prolapse.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

For sacrocolpopexy, published bovine-specific data are sparse. In the Deprest series of porcine SIS and dermis xenografts (50) vs polypropylene (100), xenografts had more vault failures (21% vs 3%) and all reoperations for prolapse.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> That series is not bovine.

These vaginal-repair findings do not determine the results of abdominal sacrocolpopexy or a continence sling. Likewise, studies of porcine dermis/SIS slings or human cadaveric pericardium should not be presented as bovine-graft evidence. See [Porcine Acellular Collagen Matrix](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/biological-grafts/porcine-acellular-collagen) and [Autologous Rectus Fascia](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/autologous-tissue/rectus-fascia) for those separate materials.

## Urethral and bladder reconstruction

Current evidence does not establish bovine matrices as a routine substitute for oral mucosa in urethroplasty or for bowel in augmentation cystoplasty. The AUA 2023 guideline states, as Expert Opinion, that surgeons should not perform substitution urethroplasty with allograft, xenograft or synthetic materials except under experimental protocols.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> EAU guidance favors oral mucosa when available and advises against cell-free tissue-engineered grafts in extensive spongiofibrosis, after failed urethroplasty or for strictures longer than 4 cm; this is a weak recommendation, not a bovine-product license or a guarantee below that length.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

Animal incorporation or histology is not equivalent to durable human urinary function. For example, one canine urethral-patch experiment reported fistulas in 24/30 animals. That result concerns its specific experimental preparation and operation; it does not estimate human risk for every bovine material.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> Bladder studies are animal experiments and case reports, and the results depend on preparation:

| Study | Model | Material | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kambic 1992 | 10 dogs, 50% partial cystectomy | Biodegradable acetylated bovine pericardium | Adequate capacity on urodynamics for up to 36 months. The inner surface was epithelialized, but the bladder apex lacked a smooth-muscle layer. The graft acted as an epithelial template while it was gradually resorbed.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> |
| Portis 2000 | 31 minipigs, laparoscopic augmentation | Bovine pericardium (6) vs porcine acellular matrix, human placental membrane and porcine SIS | All bovine pericardium grafts failed to incorporate. The other three materials stayed in place with mucosal coverage and a larger bladder capacity at 12 weeks.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| Chee 2015 | Two cases in previously irradiated pelvises | Bovine pericardium in one, ileum in the other | Bladder defects were closed successfully in both.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |

Small bladder-repair reports and regenerative experiments remain insufficient to support routine bovine-graft augmentation.
