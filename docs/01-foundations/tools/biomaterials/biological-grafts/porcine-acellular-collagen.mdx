# Porcine Acellular Collagen Matrix

Porcine acellular matrices are a family of tissue-derived scaffolds, including dermis, small intestinal submucosa (SIS) and urinary bladder matrix (UBM). Tissue source, crosslinking, decellularization and sterilization affect handling and remodeling; these products are not interchangeable. Avoiding tissue harvest is a practical advantage, but neither an animal origin nor decellularization establishes durable support, freedom from inflammation or suitability for a particular urinary reconstruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Products named in the literature include Surgisis and Stratasis (Cook porcine SIS), Pelvicol and PelviSoft (Bard porcine dermis, no longer listed as current), ACell urinary bladder matrix, and InteXen (porcine dermal collagen, historical). Availability changes, so check the current listing before planning a case.

This overview covers material selection and evidence boundaries. See [Porcine SIS](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/biological-grafts/porcine-sis) for that specific material and [Decellularized ECM](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/tissue-engineering/decellularized-ecm) for scaffold research.

## Remodeling and product selection

The intended response is host-cell infiltration and replacement of the scaffold. Clinical remodeling can be incomplete or accompanied by fibrosis, inflammation, infection and loss of mechanical support. A pathology series of patients requiring reintervention after xenograft sacrocolpopexy found different degradation patterns for porcine dermis and SIS. Dermal failures showed local degradation with minimal foreign-body reaction, whereas dermal erosions and early SIS infections showed inflammatory infiltrates. This selected failure series cannot estimate complication incidence, but it contradicts a guarantee of no foreign-body response.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Use the exact product's current instructions for indication, animal-tissue hypersensitivity precautions, preparation and fixation. Historical Pelvicol or PelviSoft outcomes do not establish the availability or performance of a currently supplied graft. The route and reconstructive purpose also matter: evidence from a sling, vaginal-wall repair, sacrocolpopexy or urethral onlay cannot be transferred automatically to another use.

## Urethral and bladder reconstruction

In rabbits, an onlay of acellular matrix from porcine bladder submucosa showed host-cell infiltration and angiogenesis by 2 weeks, a confluent transitional-cell layer, unorganized muscle bundles at 2 months and organized bundles at 6 months, with no strictures on urethrography.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> Mantovani reported the first clinical porcine SIS urethroplasty, with satisfactory urodynamic and subjective outcome at 16 months.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> SIS series in men are summarized on the [Porcine SIS](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/biological-grafts/porcine-sis) page.

The AUA 2023 urethral-stricture guideline states, as Expert Opinion, that surgeons should not perform substitution urethroplasty with allograft, xenograft or synthetic materials except under experimental protocols.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> The EAU 2026 urethral-stricture guideline gives a weak recommendation to avoid cell-free tissue-engineered grafts with extensive spongiofibrosis, a previous failed urethroplasty or a stricture longer than 4 cm. This boundary is not proof that every shorter defect is suitable. The guideline favors buccal or lingual mucosa when a graft is needed and available, and strongly advises against tubularized grafts in a single-stage urethroplasty.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Small salvage reports show feasibility, with important co-interventions. A pediatric report included ten fistula repairs and two redo urethroplasties in 12 boys, with the matrix used as a splint over the repair. All were reported free of fistula recurrence at a median 2.5 years, and one child developed a wound infection that needed debridement. Two female UBM reconstructions also used labial fat-pad transposition and a biologic pubovaginal sling. Neither report establishes equivalence to vascularized tissue or attributes continence to the scaffold alone.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

For bladder augmentation, SIS produced regeneration in rat and canine models, and clinical translation has been inconsistent (see [Porcine SIS](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/biological-grafts/porcine-sis) for the human series).<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> Regenerative scaffolds remain a research field. A 2025 TissueSpan report described animal development and initiation of a first human trial, without reporting human outcomes. The 2025 UROGRAFT study investigated a bladder-matrix/collagen/cellulose composite preclinically. These studies do not establish a clinically validated replacement for conventional urethral reconstruction or enterocystoplasty. Animal defect lengths and failure percentages should not become clinical selection thresholds.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Stress urinary incontinence

Porcine dermis and SIS slings gave good short-term results and inferior long-term results. The series below use different materials and definitions of success.

| Series | Material and design | Reported outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Barrington 2002 | Pelvicol porcine dermis, 40 women; mean 12 months | Cure 85%, a further 10% improved.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| Giri 2006 | Cross-linked porcine dermis (51) vs rectus fascia (50), nonrandomized; 36 months or more | Patient-perceived success (cured or improved) in 54% vs 80.4% (p = 0.009), among 94 with complete data. Failures in the porcine group appeared by 24 months. The authors concluded that porcine dermis should not substitute for rectus fascia.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> |
| Broussard 2013 | Porcine dermis bladder-neck sling, 70 women; mean 62.1 months | SUI cure 42.9% and global cure 11.4%. Thirty of 40 women with recurrence had it within 12 months, and 28.6% had further anti-incontinence surgery.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |

SIS sling series (Rutner 2003, Siracusano 2011) are on the [Porcine SIS](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/biological-grafts/porcine-sis) page. The AUA/SUFU 2023 panel notes that efforts to substitute porcine dermis or cadaveric fascia for autologous fascia have shown inferior results.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

The long-term randomized comparison concerns Pelvicol porcine dermis, not every porcine scaffold. In the follow-up of a four-centre randomized trial, 162 of 201 women were assessed at a median of ten years. Patient-reported dry-or-improved rates were 58% with Pelvicol, 75.4% with autologous fascia and 73% with TVT; complete dryness was 15.7%, 50.8% and 31.7%, respectively. Complete dryness favored autologous fascia over Pelvicol. These separate endpoints and follow-up losses matter when counseling about durability.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

A short uncontrolled series with a high initial cure rate cannot establish long-term equivalence to an [autologous fascial sling material](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/autologous-tissue/rectus-fascia) or [polypropylene sling](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/synthetic-meshes/polypropylene).

## Pelvic organ prolapse

| Setting | Evidence and implication |
|---|---|
| **Primary transvaginal anterior/posterior repair** | The large multicentre PROSPECT trial found no clinically important symptom or functional-cure advantage from biological xenograft augmentation at six years. About 65% returned six-year questionnaires, and no examination was performed at that time. The result concerns primary vaginal-wall repair; it does not evaluate abdominal sacrocolpopexy or all salvage reconstructions. The 2024 Cochrane review (51 trials) likewise found no difference between biological graft and native tissue repair in awareness of prolapse, repeat surgery or recurrence on examination.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[21]](#ref21)</sup> |
| **Laparoscopic sacrocolpopexy** | Culligan's single-centre RCT randomized women to PelviSoft crosslinked porcine dermis or polypropylene; 57 and 58 were analyzed at 12 months. Objective cure was 46/57 (80.7%) versus 50/58 (86.2%). No significant difference was detected, but the trial was designed to detect a large, 23-percentage-point difference. Clinical cure (84.2% vs 89.7%) also did not differ. It was not an equivalence or noninferiority trial and cannot establish comparable long-term durability or rare-event safety.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> In the earlier Deprest series (50 xenograft, of which 29 were porcine dermis, vs 100 polypropylene sacrocolpopexies, mean 33 months), vault failure was 21% with xenografts vs 3% with polypropylene, and all reoperations for prolapse were in the xenograft group.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> In the pathology series of failures, porcine dermis was usually locally degraded but still recognizable years later, with minimal foreign-body reaction. SIS had been replaced by connective tissue, and erosions and early infections showed inflammatory infiltrates.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

**US regulatory scope:** the FDA's 2019 stop-sale order for devices intended for transvaginal POP repair included Xenform, a bovine-derived product, as well as synthetic products. It was not confined to synthetic materials. This action should not be generalized to all midurethral slings, abdominal sacrocolpopexy mesh or every biological graft use.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Practical selection questions

- What exact tissue, product generation and current labeled use are being considered?
- Does the evidence match the operation, route, fixation and required duration of support?
- Is the recipient tissue suitable for incorporation, and what alternatives have better evidence?
- Are the patient-relevant outcome, follow-up losses and potential need for reoperation clear?
