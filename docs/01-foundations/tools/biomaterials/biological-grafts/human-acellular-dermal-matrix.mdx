# Human Acellular Dermal Matrix

Human acellular dermal matrix (hADM) is donated human dermis processed to remove cells while retaining a collagen-rich scaffold. Early AlloDerm burn experience demonstrated host-cell infiltration and neovascularization beneath a skin autograft; it does not establish equivalent outcomes in the urethra, tunica albuginea or pelvic floor.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Identify the actual product and intended use.** Human dermis, human pericardium, porcine dermis and porcine small-intestinal submucosa are different materials. Processing, preservation, thickness and labeling also vary between products. See [Tutoplast allografts](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/biological-grafts/tutoplast-allografts), [porcine matrices](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/biological-grafts/porcine-acellular-collagen) and [bovine grafts](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/biological-grafts/bovine-dermal).

Human dermal matrices named in the urologic and reconstructive literature include AlloDerm, FlexHD, Belladerm, Repriza, AlloMax, DermaMatrix and Epiflex (DIZG, Germany). Processing, preservation and sterilization differ between them, so each product's own instructions apply.

## AlloDerm SELECT: labeling and preparation

The US May 2025 instructions describe a human tissue product regulated under 21 CFR Part 1271, intended for repair or replacement of inadequate integument and other homologous uses. This classification does not establish an FDA-approved indication for every reported urologic application. It is not a dural substitute.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

AlloDerm SELECT is supplied hydrated and terminally sterilized by **electron-beam irradiation**, with a stated sterility-assurance level of 10⁻³. Despite the ready-to-use designation, soak it for **at least two minutes** in room-temperature sterile saline or lactated Ringer's solution. The instructions allow up to four hours in that sterile solution before implantation. Place the dermal side against the most vascular tissue; use the label's orientation method. These instructions should not be transferred to older freeze-dried AlloDerm or another brand.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Do not use in a patient sensitive to **polysorbate 20 or an antibiotic listed on the package**. Do not use dry tissue, compromised packaging, an unacceptable temperature indicator or expired material. Do not reuse or resterilize; retain donor/product traceability.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The manufacturer's current safety information identifies infection, seroma, dehiscence, immune reactions and graft failure as possible complications. Donor screening and processing reduce but cannot eliminate transmission risk. Poor perfusion, radiation and impaired healing require particular assessment; an acellular graft does not supply its own blood flow or substitute for infection control.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Clinical role in reconstructive urology

### Urethral reconstruction

The AUA 2023 guideline states, as Expert Opinion, that surgeons should not perform substitution urethroplasty with allograft, xenograft or synthetic materials except under experimental protocols.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> EAU guidance favors available buccal or lingual mucosa when a urethral graft is needed. Evidence for cell-free matrices remains heterogeneous. Its weak recommendation advises against these matrices with extensive spongiofibrosis, previous failed urethroplasty or a stricture longer than 4 cm. That restriction is not proof that shorter strictures are routinely suitable, and evidence for one acellular material cannot be assigned to another brand.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

In a retrospective series of 49 men with anterior urethral stricture repaired with allogeneic acellular dermal matrix (two centers, 2015 to 2019), mean maximum flow rose from 1.3 mL/s to 14.6 mL/s at 12 months. Two patients had infection, 7 developed non-infective restricture at 6 to 10 months and 1 developed a urinary fistula. The EAU guideline lists this series (85.7% patency at 15 months) among cell-free graft series with varying patency and success definitions.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

See [urethral reconstruction principles](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/urethral-reconstruction-principles) for selection and operative planning.

### Peyronie's disease and corporal reconstruction

There is no proven best graft for Peyronie's surgery. Selection depends on deformity, erectile function, tissue characteristics and the operation; grafting can cause erectile dysfunction, recurrent curvature or shortening. Current EAU guidance does not establish AlloDerm as the preferred material, or buccal mucosa as superior for preserving erections.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

Adamakis and colleagues reported the first use of an acellular human dermis graft (Epiflex, DIZG; 20 × 40 mm) for cavernosal defects after plaque incision. Five men with stable deformity and IIEF-5 above 20 were followed for 6 months. Two also had a small plication. All resumed sexual activity at 1 month, with no deformity, infection or de novo erectile dysfunction.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> A 2021 review of acellular dermal matrices in genitourinary reconstruction concluded that human ADM is a valuable option for tunica albuginea replacement, with caution when it is used to support free grafts because of graft take. It also notes that biomechanical comparisons between graft types are limited.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

A pericardial or small-intestinal-submucosa series is not an hADM series. Specialized corporal or genital reconstruction requires evidence and labeling specific to the proposed material and use. See [plaque incision and grafting](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease/plaque-incision-grafting) for the procedural discussion.

### Phalloplasty and penile reconstruction

In congenital aphallia, a modified scrotal-flap phalloplasty with an AlloDerm patch added girth and support to the phallus, with a satisfactory cosmetic result and no documented complication over 12 months.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> This is a single description. The 2021 review lists neophallus construction, phalloplasty for trauma, penile augmentation and Peyronie's grafting as reported genitourinary uses.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

### Abdominal wall and parastomal reconstruction

A 2011 systematic review of parastomal hernia repair with biologic grafts found four retrospective studies (57 patients), with recurrence in 15.7% (95% CI 7.8 to 25.9) and wound-related complications in 26.2%. No mortality or graft infection was reported. Because synthetic mesh had similar recurrence and complication rates in this setting, the authors concluded that the evidence does not support biologic grafts.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> Small series describe hADM repair in patients with inflammatory bowel disease: 11 patients had 3 recurrences at a mean 8.7 months,<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> and 13 patients had 2 asymptomatic recurrences at about 290 days, with seroma and incisional separation in 15.4% each.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> A 2013 review of biological prostheses for incisional hernia (60 studies, mostly retrospective) reported at least one complication in 87% of repairs, wound infection in 16.9%, seroma in 12.0% and recurrence of 15.2% at a mean 13.6 months. It found insufficient high-quality evidence for their value.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

**Contamination alone does not establish biological-mesh superiority.** In a 253-patient randomized trial of elective single-stage class II–III ventral hernia repair, all repairs had retromuscular mesh and fascial closure. Two-year recurrence was 26/127 with non-crosslinked porcine dermis versus 7/126 with polypropylene, without a demonstrated difference in surgical-site occurrences requiring intervention. This was not an AlloDerm trial, and its results do not establish outcomes for bridging, other planes or urinary-diversion-specific hernias.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

Use the [mesh material hub](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/synthetic-meshes/polypropylene) and the relevant reconstruction page to distinguish repair technique from material choice.

### Cosmetic penile girth

EAU guidance recommends against grafts for penile girth enhancement because they remain experimental. Cosmetic wrapping should not be presented as an established use of hADM or supported by unrelated reconstructive series.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> See [dermal-fat grafts and AlloDerm wraps](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/dermal-fat-grafts-alloderm-wraps).
