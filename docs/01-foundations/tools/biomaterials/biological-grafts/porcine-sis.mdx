# Porcine Small Intestinal Submucosa (SIS)

**Porcine small intestinal submucosa** is a processed extracellular-matrix graft. Commercial products (Cook Surgisis and Stratasis) and experimental preparations differ in processing, layer number, dimensions and intended use. A biological scaffold does not guarantee functional tissue regeneration, freedom from inflammation, or durable support. The product and operation are selected individually, and SIS sheets are not interchangeable.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For the broader family, see [Porcine Acellular Collagen Matrix](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/biological-grafts/porcine-acellular-collagen).

:::warning[Two different meanings of SIS]
In the AUA/SUFU female stress-incontinence guideline, SIS means single-incision sling, a synthetic midurethral-sling category. Its success rates and recommendations are not evidence for small intestinal submucosa. Keep this graft material separate from those devices when reading guideline tables.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
:::

## Product handling and safety

Use the package-specific instructions and local authorization. The available Cook Biodesign Surgisis urethral sling IFU, FP0007-3C, describes pubourethral support for stress incontinence associated with urethral hypermobility or intrinsic sphincter deficiency. That intended use does not establish an indication for urethral substitution, bladder augmentation, penile grafting or fistula repair. This is a versioned document, so the instructions supplied with the actual implant apply.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The same IFU contraindicates use in patients with known sensitivity to porcine material and warns of infection, acute or chronic inflammation, and allergic reaction. It specifies single use without resterilization. For that sling, rehydration is at least 10 minutes in at least 50 mL of room-temperature sterile saline or lactated Ringer's solution. All layers require fixation, and suture spacing greater than 2 mm was not evaluated. Excessive tension can cause urinary retention. These details apply only to that sling. Another SIS configuration has its own instructions.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

There is no established universal resorption timetable, and no clinical rule that a particular ply count eliminates fibrosis, aneurysmal change or failure. Experimental cell infiltration, angiogenesis and muscle-marker expression are surrogate findings. They do not establish a functioning human urinary reservoir or a durable urethral lumen.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

In the first human histology, biopsies from 3 reoperated sling patients (from a series of 15, at a mean 12.7 months) showed only focal residues of SIS and no foreign-body or chronic inflammatory reaction.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Intense local inflammatory reactions have also been reported (see the sling section), so a benign biopsy in three women does not predict the response in every patient.

## Urethral reconstruction

**Oral mucosa is the preferred graft when available.** The EAU 2026 urethral-stricture guideline advises against cell-free tissue-engineered grafts in extensive spongiofibrosis, after failed urethroplasty, or for strictures longer than 4 cm. It grades that recommendation weak because the evidence is limited, and it strongly advises against a tubularized graft in a single-stage repair.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> The AUA 2023 guideline states, as Expert Opinion, that surgeons should not perform substitution urethroplasty with allograft, xenograft or synthetic materials except under experimental protocols. It notes that non-autologous grafts may be considered in a man who has failed a prior urethroplasty and has no tissue available for reoperative substitution.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

The published SIS series are small, retrospective and use different definitions of success.

| Series | Design | Reported outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Mantovani 2003 | First clinical use of porcine SIS for urethroplasty; 16 months | Satisfactory urodynamic and subjective outcome reported.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Palminteri 2007 | 20 men, dorsal inlay or ventral onlay of SIS; mean 21 months | Success (no further procedure) in 17/20 (85%). The three failures were penile or penile-bulbar repairs with a mean stricture length of 5.7 cm. SIS was not fully replaced by urothelium at 3 months.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| Fiala 2007 | 50 men, four-layer SIS onlay; mean 31.2 months | Excellent findings in 40 (80%). All 10 recurrences appeared within 6 months; strictures were bulbar (1/10 failed), bulbopenile (5/31) and penile (4/9).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| Xu 2013 | 28 men, four-layer SIS; mean stricture length 4.6 cm; mean 24.8 months | Two narrowings (7.1%). Biopsies at 18 to 42 weeks showed squamous epithelium over the graft.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| Palminteri 2012 | 25 men, bulbar strictures; mean 71 months | 19/25 (76%) successful. Failure was 14% for strictures under 4 cm and 100% for strictures over 4 cm. The authors concluded that SIS is not a first choice but an option when buccal mucosa is refused or unsuitable.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Palminteri 2024 | Propensity-matched 25 SIS vs 25 buccal mucosa bulbar repairs, from a database of 1,132 operations | Estimated success at 13 years was 68% with SIS and 83.4% with buccal mucosa (failure meant any later treatment). Follow-up was longer in the SIS group. SIS graft, prior urethrotomy, longer stricture and lower early Qmax predicted failure. Estimated survival fell below 80% for SIS strictures of 3 cm or longer.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |

SIS may therefore be discussed as a selected alternative when an oral graft cannot be used, in a short bulbar stricture without prior urethrotomy, with explicit counseling about uncertain durability. Several of these reports come from the same institution and should not be counted as independent confirmations. Operative selection belongs in [Urethral Reconstruction Principles](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/urethral-reconstruction-principles).

## Peyronie's disease and congenital curvature

SIS is one of several grafts reported after tunical incision or limited excision. Current EAU guidance does not establish a best graft material. Baseline erectile function, deformity, defect size and the operation itself influence outcomes. Grafting can cause erectile dysfunction, altered sensation, shortening or recurrent curvature even if the material remodels.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

| Series | Design | Reported outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Knoll 2007 | 162 men, curvature 60° or more, Surgisis ES after plaque incision; mean 38 months | Correction in 148/162 (91%). 79% fully potent and 21% needing assistance. The author reported no intraoperative shortening, infection or rejection.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| Sayedahmed 2017 | 43 men, two centers, prospective; mean curvature 73.8°; mean 33 months | Complete straightening in 74.4%; satisfactory intercourse in 88.4%, including men using erectile aids.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| Valente 2017 | 28 men; mean 18 months | Self-reported complete resolution of curvature in 82.1%. Shortening was perceived by 71.4% but objectively present in 4. Erectile function was fully preserved in 64.2%. One infected hematoma required drainage.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> |
| Rosenhammer 2019 | Matched-pair SIS vs collagen fleece, long-term | Both grafts gave good long-term straightening, but relapse of curvature occurred only after SIS. Postoperative shortening was more frequent with SIS (28% vs 5%, p = 0.007) and operating time was longer (104 vs 80 minutes). Baseline IIEF-5 (16 vs 19) and mean follow-up (31 vs 39 months) differed between groups, so this does not prove graft superiority.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| John 2006 | 4 men, single surgeon; 4 months | Recurrent curvature in 3 of 4, two within 4 months. The authors advised against widespread use.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |

**Corporal grafting in hypospadias.** Reports in boys with severe chordee are small and mixed. Weiser 2003 (9 boys, single-layer SIS) reported durable chordee correction at 16 to 21 months in the 8 who completed staged repair.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> Elmore 2007 (28 boys; four-ply in 21 and single-ply in 7) found a straight phallus without fibrosis in all 26 who completed stage 2 and no complication attributable to the graft, apart from one possible perimeatal granuloma.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> Hayn 2009 (15 boys, single and four-layer) reported no chordee recurrence up to 75 months.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> Soergel 2003 (12 boys, four-ply) reported complications in 33%, 2 of them major and needing surgical correction, and the authors stopped using SIS for corporal grafting.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> In Leslie 2008 (71 boys: dermal 29, tunica vaginalis 21, SIS 20), both SIS-related complications occurred with four-ply grafts and none with single-ply.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> This does not establish a universal "single-layer safe, multilayer unsafe" rule, because Elmore's largely four-ply series had no graft complication. Adult Peyronie's results should not be transferred to hypospadias reconstruction.

See the [Peyronie's Disease surgical atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease) for operative decisions.

## Bladder and ureteral substitution

**SIS augmentation is not an established replacement for enterocystoplasty.** In the first exploratory clinical series (8 patients with neurogenic bladder), maximum capacity rose from 170 mL to 385 mL and compliance from 5.9 to 36.3 mL/cmH2O at 12 months, with no metabolic consequences or calculi.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> In the long-term follow-up of 15 neurogenic-bladder patients, only nine had the expected sustained benefit at a mean 6.3 years. Two failed immediately and four lost capacity over time. Complications included reflux in five, a bladder stone in one and perforation in one. The authors specifically advised against substituting SIS for enterocystoplasty, particularly with severe upper-tract deterioration or bladder fibrosis.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup> Procyanidin-crosslinked SIS improved mechanical strength, reduced calcification and promoted smooth-muscle regrowth in a rabbit bladder-defect model.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

Ureteral experiments were done in animals. An SIS patch bridging a partial ureteral defect gave patent ureters with epithelial regeneration at 7 to 9 weeks in pigs.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)[[29]](#ref29)</sup> Tubularized SIS failed in every animal reported: complete obstruction at 12 weeks in the pigs (Sofer) and dogs (El-Assmy) that reached that time point, and severe hydroureteronephrosis or renal atrophy with complete obstruction in all seven tubularized pigs given a collagen inhibitor or none (Duchene).<sup>[[30]](#ref30)[[31]](#ref31)[[32]](#ref32)</sup> These studies do not establish that a free SIS tube, or an SIS onlay in a diseased human ureter, will regenerate a durable conduit. Keep investigational scaffolds distinct from established reconstructive options and from bowel-based reservoir surgery.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Fistula and mesh-perforation repair

SIS interposition has been reported in selected salvage repairs, but it supplies a scaffold rather than a vascularized flap. In 23 complicated vesicovaginal fistula repairs (9 after failed repair, 4 with heavy scarring, the rest fistulas of 1.5 cm or more), all 7 vaginal repairs and 14 of 16 abdominal repairs were dry through six months, with no flap comparator.<sup>[[33]](#ref33)</sup> A separate retrospective series of 38 women undergoing removal of perforating tape or vaginal mesh (26 urethral, 8 bladder, 4 both) reported closure of all urinary-tract defects, but six postoperative complications occurred and 14 women later underwent stress-incontinence surgery at a mean follow-up of 37.2 months. The authors proposed SIS as an alternative to a Martius fat-pad flap.<sup>[[34]](#ref34)</sup>

These reports support feasibility in selected hands. They do not establish equivalence to Martius or omental interposition, nor do they prove that a scaffold compensates for ischemia, radiation injury or incomplete removal of problematic foreign material.

## Continence and prolapse support

### Pubovaginal slings

The AUA/SUFU 2023 guideline panel notes that efforts to substitute porcine dermis or cadaveric fascia for autologous fascia have shown inferior results, and it considers autologous fascia a viable sling option.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The SIS sling series have variable definitions and follow-up.

| Series | Design | Reported outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Rutner 2003 | 152 consecutive women, SIS sling anchored with bone screws; 4-year series | 142 (93.4%) relieved of SUI. Seven had unsatisfactory continence, five within 3 months. No sling infection, erosion or rejection.<sup>[[35]](#ref35)</sup> |
| Siracusano 2011 | 48 women; median 76 months | Cured 69%, improved 12%, failed or unchanged 19%. No erosion or retention. The authors concluded that SIS cannot offer a durable option compared with current minimally invasive techniques.<sup>[[36]](#ref36)</sup> |
| John 2008 | 16 women, 4-ply Stratasis or 8-ply Stratasis-TF | 5 (31.3%) had intense suprapubic pain. One needed drainage of mons pubis induration, one had vaginal inflammation with graft expulsion, and others had rectus-sheath inflammation on CT that resolved conservatively.<sup>[[37]](#ref37)</sup> |

The John series is small and selected and is not an incidence estimate, but it contradicts a claim that SIS cannot provoke inflammation.

### Prolapse repair

For primary transvaginal repair, the PROSPECT randomized program (1,348 women followed to 6 years) found no benefit from augmenting native-tissue repair with a biological xenograft or a polypropylene inlay. Six-year follow-up used questionnaires, with about 65% response and no examination. This informs biological augmentation in that route. It is not a brand-specific SIS trial or evidence about sacrocolpopexy, fistula repair or urethroplasty.<sup>[[38]](#ref38)</sup> The 2024 Cochrane review of transvaginal repair (51 trials, 7,846 women) found no difference between biological graft and native tissue repair in awareness of prolapse, repeat surgery or recurrence on examination, and concluded that data do not support biological grafts in transvaginal prolapse repair.<sup>[[39]](#ref39)</sup>

SIS-specific vaginal series:

- Alexandridis 2021 (155 procedures with SIS, retrospective). Anatomical recurrence (POP-Q stage 2 or more) at 3 months was 22.6% in the anterior compartment, 4.8% posterior and none in the middle compartment. Complications occurred in 56%, mainly retention (19%) and pain (12%). The authors judged that the recurrence rate does not suggest a clear benefit.<sup>[[40]](#ref40)</sup>
- Cao 2016 (40 women). At 12 months the objective recurrence rate was 40.0% and the subjective rate 7.5%. No erosion was identified. Quality-of-life scores improved.<sup>[[41]](#ref41)</sup>

For sacrocolpopexy, Deprest 2009 compared 50 laparoscopic xenograft cases (21 SIS, 29 porcine dermal collagen) with 100 polypropylene controls at a mean of about 33 months. Overall anatomical failure was similar (49% vs 34%, p = 0.053), but vault failure (21% vs 3%) and posterior-compartment failure (36% vs 19%) were more frequent with xenografts. All 6 reoperations for prolapse were in the xenograft group. Graft-related complications were equally frequent (11%) and functional outcome did not differ.<sup>[[42]](#ref42)</sup> In a pathology series of 13 women reoperated after xenograft sacrocolpopexy, the two SIS grafts removed for failure had been replaced entirely by connective tissue. The two early SIS infections showed massive polymorphonuclear infiltration.<sup>[[43]](#ref43)</sup>

Use the operation-specific guideline and the actual implant's authorization. A favorable short series, donor-site avoidance or absence of permanent synthetic mesh is not sufficient reason to promise better continence, lower infection risk or more durable prolapse repair.
