# Metal &amp; Long-Term Ureteral Stents

**Metallic and long-term ureteral stents** are used for chronic ureteral obstruction (CUO) from malignant or benign causes. Polymer double-J stents usually need exchange every 3 to 6 months because of encrustation and loss of patency, and metallic devices aim to reduce the number of drainage procedures in selected patients. They do not guarantee patency, eliminate follow-up or replace reconstruction when a durable repair is appropriate. The permitted indication, insertion method and maximum dwell time belong to the specific device and local labeling; "metallic" does not mean that every stent may remain for 12 months or longer.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

## Choosing Long-Term Drainage

Consider the cause and length of obstruction, renal function, bladder drainage, prior radiation or reconstruction, infection and stone burden, prognosis, anesthetic burden and the patient's goals. Compare a metallic stent with polymer stenting, nephrostomy and definitive repair. In malignant obstruction, the value of drainage depends on symptoms, treatment plans and expected benefit. An exchange-saving device is useful only if it continues to drain.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

**Indications reported in the series below:**

- Malignant extrinsic obstruction in which polymer stents fail or need frequent exchange.
- Benign CUO, including retroperitoneal fibrosis, radiation strictures, postsurgical strictures and ureteropelvic junction obstruction in patients who are not reconstruction candidates.
- Recurrent obstruction despite polymer exchanges.
- Patient preference to reduce the number of procedures.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

**Situations in which failure or harm has been reported:**

- **Urolithiasis.** Benson and colleagues concluded that metal stents are effective in the absence of urolithiasis.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Bulky pelvic or intravesical disease.** Three of 17 Resonance stents failed from the outset in patients with bulky pelvic malignancy and high intravesical pressure.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Prostate cancer invading the bladder.** Resonance failure risk was higher in this group (HR 6.50, 95% CI 1.45–29.20, p = 0.015; 25 patients). Symptomatic subcapsular hematomas occurred in 3 patients.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- **Prior pelvic radiation.** Covered-stent patency was lower after radiation (see Allium below).<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
- **Long dwell of a covered Uventa stent.** Placement of 24 months or more was associated with major complications on univariate analysis (OR 20.4).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

Published cost estimates depend on local prices, anesthesia and exchange schedules. In a retrospective Singapore series of 247 stents in 63 patients (45 metallic), mean dwell was 228.6 versus 146.1 days (p &lt; 0.001), or 1.4 versus 6.3 stent changes per year, and cost per dwell day did not differ (SGD 7.82 vs 8.23, p = 0.888).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> In 13 patients with benign obstruction, the calculated 12-month cost was $11,183 with a metallic stent and $23,999 with polymer stents (43% lower).<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> These retrospective comparisons support the possibility of fewer procedures. They do not establish a universal annual saving or a fixed number of exchanges for every metal or polymer product.

## Device Families

| Device | Design and practical distinction | Evidence or labeling boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **Resonance** | Full-length coiled metal double-pigtail stent; delivered through its dedicated sheath | Current Cook IFU: temporary drainage of **extrinsic ureteral obstruction in adults**, with a **12-month maximum indwell**; earlier intervention may be needed.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Memokath-051** | Segmental thermo-expandable nickel-titanium spiral | NICE recommends it as an option for selected adults, with regular position monitoring and prospective outcome collection. The applicable IFU gives deployment and removal temperatures and dwell limits.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Allium URS** | Segmental self-expanding nitinol stent with a proprietary polymer coating | Current manufacturer description lists 24–30 Fr expanded diameters and a **10 Fr delivery system**. It states that a curative effect is **not CE-approved**; verify the local device indication and IFU.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Uventa** | Double-layered PTFE membrane-covered self-expanding segmental metallic stent | Published long-term series include serious fistulas, perforation and obstruction. Available sizes, approved use and surveillance requirements need product-specific confirmation.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Detour / extra-anatomic nephrovesical bypass** | A subcutaneous kidney-to-bladder bypass rather than a stent passing through the ureter | A salvage option in selected patients when conventional drainage or reconstruction is unsuitable; infection, leakage, dislodgement and revision remain risks.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |

### Resonance: Label and Handling

The alloy is nickel-cobalt-chromium-molybdenum, not a self-expanding nitinol mesh. The coiled stent has no central lumen; urine drains through and around the coils. In an animal experiment, flow continued around a tightly tied constricting suture, but a small animal compression experiment cannot establish that obstruction is impossible in patients.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

The closed-ended stent is inserted through the supplied delivery sheath after removal of the initial catheter and wire, according to the IFU. An ordinary over-the-wire polymer-stent insertion sequence does not apply to the metal coil. Avoid excessive force, bending or stretching, and check final position and function.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Technical success was 100% in prospective and retrospective series, with antegrade and retrograde insertion both used.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

The IFU lists no known contraindications but warns about possible nickel-allergy reactions. It calls for regular follow-up and closer monitoring in patients prone to calcium encrustation. Twelve months is the maximum permitted indwell, not a promise of one year of patency. MRI is conditional, with scanner, gradient, SAR and scan-time limits specified in the IFU.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Reported outcomes.**

- Khoo and colleagues reported a median functional survival of 11.7 months and failure in 11 of 58 insertion episodes (19%), with no failure for benign indications.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- In malignant extrinsic obstruction, 12 of 37 stents (35%) failed in one series, and patency was 86% at 6 months and 60% at 1 year in another (52 patients, 66 ureters).<sup>[[17]](#ref17)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- In patients whose polymer stents had failed, Resonance stents lasted a mean of 4 months longer than the polymer stents they replaced (50 stents, 42 patients, p &lt; 0.0001).<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- A prospective series of 50 patients reported patency of 100% in malignant and 44% in benign obstruction at mean 8.5 months, encrustation in 12 of 54 stents, and demanding exchange in 9 cases.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- A systematic review of metallic stents in malignant obstruction found the lowest migration rate with Resonance (1%).<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

Reports of failure with bulky bladder disease, cancer progression or complex benign strictures should inform selection and follow-up. They are not manufacturer contraindications, and they do not show that a whole cancer category must never receive the device.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### Memokath: Current NICE Position

NICE's 2022 recommendations, now numbered HTG651, support Memokath-051 as an option for adults with malignant obstruction and anticipated medium- or long-term survival; benign obstruction when reconstruction is unsuitable or declined; or obstruction when repeated double-J stenting is unsuitable, declined or particularly risky. NICE also requests prospective data collection and regular monitoring for migration.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The device is a segmental nickel-titanium spiral that is placed in its narrow form and expanded with warm irrigation to anchor above the stricture; cold irrigation collapses it for removal. Temperatures and sizes follow the IFU.

**Reported outcomes.**

- In a three-device comparison, median functional survival was 5.5 months and 31 of 48 insertion episodes (64.6%) failed.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- An independent review of 100 patients with 162 stents found a 72% mean 5-year stent complication rate: migration 46%, blockage 34%, nonfunctioning kidney 8% and urosepsis needing intravenous antibiotics 8%. Median time to first complication was 12.5 months.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- In 21 patients, ureteric stents functioned a mean of 16.1 months, and transplant ureters a mean of 24.3 months.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

The evidence remains largely observational, with substantial reintervention in longer follow-up. NICE's 2018 medical-technology evaluation described Memokath-051 as likely to be cost saving in patients not indicated for reconstructive surgery who are expected to need a stent for at least 30 months, on the basis of an economic model. That assumption is not an approved dwell-time limit.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

### Allium: Drainage Is Distinct From Cure

A covered segmental stent can maintain a lumen while it is present; durable patency after removal is a different outcome. A 2026 retrospective study of balloon dilation plus Allium reported success after removal (no reintervention for at least 12 months) in 64 of 101 patients at median 22.5 months, with no randomized reconstruction comparator. Success was 74.1% for strictures of 2 cm or less and 48.8% for longer strictures; the 92.9% success in the 2 cm or shorter group with 12 to 24 months of stent retention applies to that subgroup only.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

The manufacturer's current page distinguishes its drainage indication from an unapproved curative effect. Its linked full IFU must be checked for the actual product before use; a website statement about possible multiyear use does not authorize a universal dwell or removal protocol.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

**Reported outcomes.**

- A prospective multicenter cohort of 321 patients (366 renal ureter units) with covered metal stents (a category that includes Allium) reported 3-year primary patency of 74.0% and secondary patency of 90.4%. After radiation-induced strictures the rates were 54.7% and 77.4%. Complications occurred in 26.0% of units: migration 7.7%, encrustation 8.5%, end-stent restenosis 6.6%, infection 1.9% and fistula 1.4%. Quality of life improved at 12 and 36 months.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
- A separate retrospective cohort of 30 patients with 40 Allium stents reported migration in 11 (27.5%) and encrustation in 8 (20%) at median 29 months. Lower ipsilateral GFR predicted migration.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- A meta-analysis of 31 studies (1,660 cases) pooled primary success for segmental metal stents at 73% under 1 year, 66% at 1 to 2 years and 39% beyond 2 years, with substantial heterogeneity. Allium reached 81% at 1 to 2 years and 65% beyond 2 years, against 51% and 23% for Uventa and 53% and 23% for Memokath. Covered stents outperformed uncovered stents at 1 to 2 years (75% vs 53%, p = 0.01).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

The meta-analysis, the 2026 curative-intent study and the multicenter cohort share authors, so they are not fully independent confirmations of one another.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

### Uventa

Uventa is a double-layered PTFE membrane-covered self-expandable segmental metallic stent.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- In 54 patients (71 ureter units) with malignant obstruction, overall success was 81.7% and primary success 64.8% at median 308 days. Complications included persistent flank pain (15.5%), lower urinary tract symptoms (7.0%) and migration (2.8%).<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>
- In a small comparison (17 Uventa, 10 Memokath), clinical success was 82.4% versus 42.9% (p = 0.031).<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>
- Longer follow-up is less favorable. In 44 patients (50 units) at median 30.9 months, primary success was 30.0% and 14 units (28.0%) had major complications (Clavien-Dindo IIIb or higher): ureteroarterial fistula 6.0%, ureteroenteric fistula 6.0%, ureterovaginal fistula 2.0%, perforation 2.0% and complete obstruction 10.0%. Associated factors on univariate analysis were female sex, cervical cancer, stricture length of 6 cm or more and placement of 24 months or more.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

| Comparison | Finding | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| Mean dwell and exchanges | 228.6 vs 146.1 days (p &lt; 0.001); 1.4 vs 6.3 changes per year | Retrospective, mixed etiologies, 247 stents<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |

| Yearly cost | $11,183 vs $23,999 | 13 patients, benign obstruction, calculated 12-month cost<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |

## Follow-Up and Failure

Before discharge, record the device, side, size, placement date, indication, planned review and exchange or removal deadline, plus the responsible service. Monitor symptoms, renal function and upper-tract drainage as appropriate. Encrustation, tissue reaction, migration, infection and progression of the underlying obstruction can require intervention before the label's maximum duration.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Fever with obstruction, new severe flank pain, deteriorating renal function or substantial hematuria requires assessment. Recurrent or brisk hematuria in a patient with chronic ureteral instrumentation, pelvic radiation or vascular surgery warrants consideration of a [vascular-urinary fistula](/docs/clinical-conditions/03f-fistulas/all-patients/vascular-urinary) rather than automatic attribution to stent irritation. See also [Ureterocolonic Fistula](/docs/clinical-conditions/03f-fistulas/all-patients/ureterocolonic).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Extra-Anatomic Bypass (Detour and Subcutaneous Nephrovesical Stents)

The Detour stent is an expanded-PTFE and silicone tube placed percutaneously in the kidney, tunneled subcutaneously and sutured into the bladder. It has been described as a salvage option for complete obstruction when conventional stenting has failed, open surgery has failed or is not feasible, and long-term nephrostomy is undesirable.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

- In 13 patients receiving 20 stents (median follow-up 12 months), four patients required revision for leakage, two had recurrent UTI, and one died after dislodgement with intra-abdominal sepsis. All stents continued to drain after revision or treatment. Seven patients died of progressive malignancy with a functioning stent.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Earlier series described a subcutaneous stent made of two double-J stents joined by a connector (8 patients, 10 stents, 6 weeks to 18 months) and, in 24 patients with pelvic metastases (30 stents, mean follow-up 10.6 months), a reduction in serum creatinine from 256 to 124 μmol/L and an increase in quality-of-life score from 3.4 to 7.6 (p &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[28]](#ref28)[[29]](#ref29)</sup>

These outcomes support careful salvage counseling, not a permanent maintenance-free solution.

## Emerging Technologies

- **Biodegradable stents.** A polyurethane and magnesium-alloy stent degraded completely within 4 weeks in pigs, a biodegradable mesh stent degraded over 3 to 5 months in animals with less infection and encrustation than a double-J stent, and a third-generation biodegradable stent (Uriprene) degraded within 4 weeks in 9 of 10 pigs. These are animal studies.<sup>[[33]](#ref33)[[32]](#ref32)[[37]](#ref37)</sup>
- **Drug-eluting and surface-modified stents.** Reviews describe antimicrobial, antispasmodic, anti-encrustation and antitumor drug delivery from biodegradable or coated stents.<sup>[[34]](#ref34)[[35]](#ref35)[[36]](#ref36)</sup>
- The stated aim of biodegradable designs is to avoid the forgotten-stent problem.<sup>[[37]](#ref37)</sup>
