# Double-J (DJ) Ureteral Stent

The **double-J (DJ) ureteral stent** is the workhorse internal drainage device of urology — a soft polymer tube with **curled retaining coils** at each end ("pigtails" in the renal pelvis and bladder) that holds the stent in place while draining urine from kidney to bladder. The pigtail-on-pigtail design was popularized by **Finney in 1978**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> and rapidly displaced earlier straight and single-J ureteral catheters.

## Design

- **Polymer tube** — polyurethane, silicone, or hybrid copolymer; 4.7–8 Fr typical.
- **Graduated stiffness** — stiffer at the coils for retention, softer in the shaft for comfort.
- **Multiple side holes** along the shaft — backup drainage paths if a hole occludes with debris.
- **Graduated markings** for length assessment during placement.
- **Lengths**: 22, 24, 26, 28, 30 cm are standard; 16–18 cm pediatric and up to 32 cm for unusually long ureters.

### Material Properties

| Material | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **Polyurethane** (e.g., Percuflex) | Tunable stiffness; excellent fluoroscopic visibility; lower friction with hydrophilic coatings | Stiffer → more stent symptoms; encrusts more rapidly |
| **Silicone** | Softer; lower encrustation rate; better tolerated long-term | Higher friction; lower radial strength; harder to push past tight strictures |
| **Hybrid / dual durometer** (e.g., Polaris, Contour) | Firm coils for retention + soft shaft for comfort | Cost |

Stent biocompatibility — particularly resistance to **encrustation and biofilm** — is the dominant determinant of long-term performance.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Common Products

- **Percuflex** (Boston Scientific) — classic polyurethane workhorse.
- **Polaris / Polaris Ultra** (Boston Scientific) — dual-durometer; softer mid-shaft; hydrophilic coating.
- **Contour** (Boston Scientific) — hybrid firmness.
- **Sof-Flex / BioSoft Duo** — softer variants prioritizing patient comfort.
- **Universa** (Cook) — commonly used tapered-end stent.
- **Inlay Optima** (Bard) — hydrophilic-coated polyurethane.

## Sizing

- **Diameter**: 4.7–6 Fr is standard for adults; 7–8 Fr for known stricture, tumor compression, or anticipated heavy encrustation.
- **Length** — three common approaches:
  1. **Patient height-based formula**: 22 cm for &lt; 5'10", 24 cm for 5'10"–6'4", 26 cm for &gt; 6'4" (approximate).
  2. **Direct intraoperative measurement** with a calibrated ureteric catheter — most accurate.
  3. **CT / ureteroscopic visual estimate**.
- Length mismatch is a common cause of irritative symptoms (too-long stent crossing midline) or migration (too-short stent).

## Indications

### Short-Term (days–weeks)

- Post-ureteroscopy / stone extraction.
- Post-ureteral reconstruction (ureteroureterostomy, ureteroneocystostomy, Boari flap, psoas hitch).
- Post-pyeloplasty.
- Post-renal transplantation — ureteral anastomosis protection.
- Obstruction from stone, clot, or transient injury.
- Decompression before definitive stone treatment (sepsis, refractory pain).

### Intermediate (weeks–months)

- Ureteral stricture awaiting definitive reconstruction.
- Tumor-related obstruction awaiting definitive management.
- Extrinsic compression awaiting resolution (retroperitoneal fibrosis, pelvic malignancy).
- Urine leak awaiting tract closure (urinoma, post-traumatic).

### Long-Term

- Chronic obstruction in patients unfit for definitive surgery.
- Malignant ureteral obstruction when permanent diversion is not feasible — increasingly managed with [metal / long-term ureteral stents](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/ureteral-stents/metal-long-term-stents).

## Placement Routes

- **Retrograde** (cystoscopic) — most common; over a guidewire through a ureteric catheter.
- **Antegrade** (percutaneous) — through an existing nephrostomy tract; useful for impassable distal obstruction.
- **Open / laparoscopic / robotic** — placed at the time of reconstruction across an anastomosis.

## Post-URS Stent Debate

Routine stenting after uncomplicated ureteroscopy is no longer universal. The **AUA / Endourological Society Surgical Management of Stones Guideline (Assimos 2016)** notes that stenting may be omitted in **selected** uncomplicated URS cases (Grade C), and the EAU Urolithiasis Guidelines reach the same conclusion.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Stent omission reduces stent-related symptoms but may increase post-op pain visits — patient selection is critical.

## Stent-Related Symptoms (USSQ)

Stent symptoms affect **~60–80%** of patients to some degree.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> The **Ureteral Stent Symptom Questionnaire (USSQ)** developed by Joshi 2003 is the validated instrument:<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- **Urinary symptoms** — frequency, urgency, hematuria, dysuria.
- **Pain** — flank, suprapubic, with voiding (the classic "loin-to-groin / loin-with-voiding" pain from reflux up the stent).
- **General health, work performance, sexual matters, additional problems**.

### Pharmacologic Management

- **α-Blockers (tamsulosin, alfuzosin, silodosin)** — Lamb 2011 meta-analysis demonstrated significant benefit on USSQ urinary symptom, pain, and general health domains.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> First-line for symptomatic patients.
- **Antimuscarinics** (solifenacin, tolterodine) — particularly for urgency / frequency; modest benefit alone, additive with α-blockers.
- **β3 agonists** (mirabegron) — emerging data, comparable to antimuscarinics with better tolerability.
- **Combination α-blocker + antimuscarinic** outperforms either alone.
- Anti-inflammatories (NSAIDs, intravesical lidocaine) — adjunctive only.

### Design Modifications to Reduce Symptoms

- **Softer materials and dual-durometer designs** reduce symptom burden.
- **Loop-tail / tail stents** (open-ended distal "tail" rather than coil) reduce trigonal irritation and dyspareunia.
- **Drug-eluting stents** — Mendez-Probst RCT of triclosan-eluting stent reduced stent symptoms and bacteriuria; not widely adopted.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Complications

### Encrustation

Surface deposition of struvite, calcium phosphate, or calcium oxalate. Risk increases sharply with dwell time:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

- 1.6% at &lt; 6 wk → 27% at 6–12 wk → **75% at &gt; 12 wk** in classic data.
- **Forgotten stent** (retained beyond intended dwell): registries report median delay of 12–24 months from intended exchange; advanced cases require staged combined endourological extraction (PCNL + URS + cystolitholapaxy) — the "FECal" approach (Forgotten / Encrusted / Calcified) of Acosta-Miranda.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- Functional kidney loss occurs in a minority of severely forgotten stents.

**Prevention**: documented exchange schedule (every 3–6 months for polyurethane), stent registries / EMR-based recall systems, and patient education at placement.

### Biofilm and Infection

- **Biofilm formation is universal** after a few weeks of indwelling.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Asymptomatic bacteriuria is common and does **not** require treatment.
- Frank UTI or pyelonephritis in a minority — manage with culture-directed antibiotics; consider stent exchange in recurrent / refractory cases.

### Migration

- **Proximal migration** (into the renal pelvis) — often requires URS retrieval.
- **Distal migration / expulsion** — usually from too-short stent or wrong-side placement.

### Fracture

Rare but well-described, especially with prolonged dwell beyond 6–12 months in polyurethane stents.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

### Stent Symptom Syndrome

The constellation of trigonal irritation, reflux-mediated flank pain with voiding, and lower urinary tract symptoms documented above — the single most common reason for early stent removal request.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Dwell Time &amp; Exchange Schedule

| Stent type | Recommended max dwell |
|---|---|
| Standard polyurethane | **3–6 months** |
| Silicone / hybrid softened shaft | Up to 6–12 months in select applications |
| Antimicrobial / coated | As per manufacturer (typically 3–6 months) |
| Metal stents (e.g., Resonance) | 12+ months (see dedicated page) |

Exchange schedule should be agreed at placement and tracked in a stent registry or EMR-based reminder.

## Emerging Designs

- **Biodegradable stents** (e.g., Uriprene, PLGA-based) — eliminate retrieval; clinical data still maturing.
- **Drug-eluting** (triclosan, ketorolac, dexamethasone) — designed to reduce symptoms and biofilm.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Anti-encrustation coatings** (heparin, diamond-like carbon, zwitterionic).
- **Magnetic stents** with thread-pull bladder extraction — avoids cystoscopy for removal in select patients.
- **Drug-coated balloons** for ureteral stricture management have begun to displace some chronic-stenting indications.

## Reconstructive-Urology Relevance

The DJ stent is integral to reconstructive practice as an intraoperative scaffold for:

- Ureteroneocystostomy / Boari flap / psoas hitch — 4–6 wk dwell typical.
- Pyeloplasty — 4–6 wk dwell.
- Renal transplant ureteral anastomosis — 4–6 wk dwell; reduces leak and obstruction.
- Buccal mucosa graft ureteroplasty and appendiceal interposition — longer dwell (6–8+ wk).
- After ureteral injury repair during pelvic / colorectal / gynecologic surgery.
