# Autologous Fascia Lata

**Fascia lata** is the deep fascia of the thigh. It receives contributions posteriorly from the gluteal aponeurotic fascia and laterally from the iliotibial band (ITB), its lateral thickening. The ITB runs from the tensor fasciae latae and gluteus maximus proximally to Gerdy's tubercle distally.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> Over the vastus lateralis the ITB is more compliant distally than proximally, and its stiffness changes with hip and knee position.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> The donor site is not functionally expendable, and harvest planning accounts for this anatomy.

Autologous fascia lata can supply a sling or a larger reconstructive graft without using synthetic mesh. It is distinct from processed cadaveric fascia lata, which has different preparation, tissue-transmission and durability considerations. See [Tutoplast allografts](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/biological-grafts/tutoplast-allografts) for donor-derived products.

## Choosing the Donor Site

Fascia lata is an alternative to [rectus fascia](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/autologous-tissue/rectus-fascia), particularly when the abdominal sheath is scarred, previously harvested, involved in hernia repair or unsuitable for the required dimensions. It offers another donor site and has not been shown to be a superior graft. Commonly cited reasons to choose it:

- a longer or wider graft is needed;
- prior abdominal surgery or hernia repair compromises the rectus sheath;
- rectus exposure is difficult, for example in an obese patient;
- the operation is vaginal only, without abdominal access.

Mobility, thigh symptoms, prior surgery and the patient's priorities regarding scars and recovery also enter the choice.

Laboratory strength does not establish clinical equivalence. The often-cited Lemer study compared autologous rectus fascia with processed cadaveric fascia lata and dermis; it was not a direct test proving that fresh autologous fascia lata and rectus fascia have identical strength or clinical outcomes.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Direct donor-site comparisons are limited. A 2024 registry included 41,533 sling operations but only 81 fascia-lata and 160 rectus-fascia cases. Its 30-day adverse-event association weakened after excluding UTIs; prior continence surgery, postoperative retention and long-term durability were not captured. These data do not establish that one donor tissue is inherently safer or equally effective.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

## Harvest: Match the Technique to the Graft

| Requirement | Planning considerations |
|---|---|
| **Narrow sling strip** | Direct harvest through a lateral thigh incision, or a dedicated fascial stripper (Crawford type) through a small incision. In one 19-woman midurethral series, stripper harvest was associated with minimal postoperative morbidity.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Mean graft size was 10 × 2 cm for pubovaginal sling and 13 × 4 cm for sacrocolpopexy in a 201-woman harvest series.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Short grafts suspended on sutures and long self-supporting strips are different configurations; their dimensions and tensioning are not interchangeable. |
| **Broad prolapse graft** | Direct lateral-thigh exposure permits a wider sheet. Patel's technique used a 4–5 × 15–18 cm graft through a 3–4 inch incision; these are reported technique dimensions, not a mandatory harvest for every patient. |
| **Donor-site closure** | Hemostasis, closure and management of the fascial defect are planned for its size and location. The fascial defect is closed primarily where feasible. A smaller skin incision does not exclude hematoma, nerve symptoms, contour change or functional complaints. |

The [PVS procedure](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/autologous-pubovaginal-sling) covers passage and tensioning. The [sacrocolpopexy procedure](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrocolpopexy) covers apical suspension and fixation.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Donor-Site Morbidity

Common symptoms and uncommon interventions both belong in counseling. A 2026 series of 201 women reported no intraoperative harvest complication, but one postoperative thigh hematoma required transfusion and drainage, and two patients underwent donor-site hernia repair. At six weeks, 64 women (32%) reported a thigh issue. At a median of 12 months (range 3–67), 28 women (17%) reported any thigh issue on direct questioning: cosmetic complaints in 8%, non-bothersome paresthesia in 4% and discomfort in 5%. No functional deficit was reported, and complaints decreased over time.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> An earlier series of 71 patients found immediate complications in 1% hematoma, 3% seroma and 7% cellulitis; among 55 questionnaire responders at a mean of 25 months, 40% reported mild leg symptoms, 5% clinically significant symptoms and 13% dissatisfaction, mostly from cosmesis or leg discomfort.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

A functional deficit was not reported in that cohort, but this is not a guarantee. In Johnson's 2024 survey, only 29/72 eligible patients responded; seven reported leg discomfort and three reported weakness. Most respondents liked the cosmetic result, but nonresponse limits generalization. These are patient-reported symptoms rather than a standardized postoperative strength examination.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

Baseline leg symptoms are recorded, and patients are told that persistent pain, swelling, weakness or a thigh bulge may need evaluation or treatment. Autologous grafting avoids mesh-specific exposure but adds donor-site morbidity and does not eliminate recipient-site wound or voiding complications.

## Female SUI

Autologous fascia lata and rectus fascia are established PVS materials within the AUA/SUFU surgical options. Counseling covers postoperative retention, catheterization, urgency, recurrent SUI and harvest complications.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

- **Latini's retrospective survey.** 100 contactable women at mean 4.4 years; 85% reported dry or improved, and 77% were satisfied. This is not an 85% complete-dry rate or a randomized comparison with rectus fascia.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Brown and Govier.** In a single-center comparison of cadaveric (121 women) and autologous fascia lata (46 women) pubovaginal slings, 30 of the 46 autologous-group women (65%) responded to the questionnaire at a mean of 44 months. Among responders, 90% reported cure of stress incontinence and 90% were satisfied; the cadaveric group had shorter follow-up (12 months). Responder-only data from consecutive, nonrandomized cohorts cannot support a comparative claim.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- **Shaw's midurethral series.** 19 women, comprising 16 fascia-lata and three rectus-fascia grafts, at median nine months. Four had postoperative voiding dysfunction and three underwent sling loosening. The combined outcome cannot be assigned entirely to fascia lata, and a small-incision harvest does not establish an obstruction-free sling.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

The 17-year follow-up of a randomized comparison of standard and short (sling-on-string) autologous fascial slings included 107 patients. It found no significant difference between the two methods in IIQ-7, UDI-6, leakage or urgency, improved bladder function from baseline, low reoperation rates and 67.2% satisfaction.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> The abstract does not state the donor tissue, so the results are not attributed to fascia lata here. The broader randomized comparisons and distinctions between dryness, improvement and satisfaction are summarized on the [rectus-fascia page](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/autologous-tissue/rectus-fascia).

## Sacrocolpopexy

Autologous fascia lata is an option for selected patients wishing to avoid synthetic mesh, and long-term comparative durability is uncertain. In Australia, where no approved prolapse mesh was available, fascia lata has been offered as the alternative graft.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> Material availability varies by product and jurisdiction. The graft is configured as a Y and attached to the vaginal cuff and sacral promontory with nonabsorbable sutures.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

| Study | What was observed | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|
| **Patel 2022** | 34 open/robotic operations; no symptomatic apical failures at mean 13 months. Thigh bulges, seroma and paresthesia occurred. | Small uncontrolled series; symptom-defined failure and variable 2–42 month follow-up. |
| **Bock 2021** | Prospective nonrandomized comparison: 19 fascia-lata and 45 mesh cases; one apical failure in the fascia group and one mesh exposure requiring excision in the mesh group. | Mean follow-up approximately 12 months; no detected difference does not prove equivalence. Harvest complications included a Clavien IIIb event. |
| **Vereeck 2026** | 101 of 124 included patients had at least six-month follow-up; median 12 months. **86/101 (85.1%)** reported much or very much improvement; three underwent repeat prolapse surgery and two underwent thigh reoperation. | Single-arm cohort. PGI-I improvement is not an anatomic cure rate, and these outcomes do not establish superiority to mesh or rectus fascia. |

These studies support feasibility and informed choice.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> A 2024 systematic review reported success of 83–100% for fascia lata (median follow-up 12–52 months) and 87–100% for rectus fascia, and concluded that fascia lata allows a wider graft but has more harvest-site complications.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> These ranges mix endpoints and follow-up durations. A 2026 scoping review of 11 studies (311 women, one randomized trial and ten single-arm series) reported success of 75–100% and de novo stress incontinence of 8.3–32.5% where reported. It identified no direct comparative trial against mesh and no long-term durability data.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

## Penile and Other Reconstruction

**Peyronie grafting.** Selection depends on stable disease, deformity, penile length and erectile function. Current EAU guidance does not identify one graft material as superior. A 12-patient autologous fascia-lata series (curvature above 35 degrees) reported straightening and normal erections without complications at a mean of 10 months; it cannot establish protection from erectile dysfunction, shortening or recurrent curvature.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> The Kalsi series used Tutoplast processed allograft, not the patient's own fascia lata: in 14 patients at a mean of 31 months, the penis was completely straight in 11, 13 were satisfied, one developed de novo erectile dysfunction and four reported shortening of more than 1 cm.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

**Other penile reconstruction.** Burnett described four cases in which autologous fascia lata was used for corporoplasty after multiple failed prosthesis operations, prosthesis fixation after phalloplasty, elongation after partial penile loss from cancer surgery, and tunica albuginea grafting in Peyronie disease. All were successful in that report.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

**Neurogenic outlet reconstruction.** Kakizaki's 13 patients (10 male, 3 female; 11 with neurogenic bladder) ranged from age three to 72; eight received rectus fascia and five fascia lata, and nine also underwent augmentation. Nine were continent at mean 36 months. This is neither a pediatric-only series nor an isolated comparison of graft materials. Safe storage pressures, upper-tract protection and a workable emptying plan are prerequisites for an outlet procedure.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> In a nonrandomized comparison without augmentation in children with neurogenic incontinence, 46% of 35 with a bladder neck sling alone and 82% of 17 with a Leadbetter-Mitchell bladder neck procedure plus fascial sling required no pads (p = 0.02); follow-up was 28 and 13 months, and the graft source is not specified in the abstract.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

**Recurrent urethral fistula.** An eight-child post-hypospadias series (2 × 2 cm graft placed between urethra and skin) reported no recurrence at mean 11 months after free fascia-lata interposition. That short uncontrolled experience does not establish equivalence to a vascularized flap or a universal 100% closure rate.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
