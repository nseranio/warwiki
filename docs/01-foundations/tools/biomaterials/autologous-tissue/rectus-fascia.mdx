# Autologous Rectus Fascia

**Rectus fascia** is the patient's own abdominal-wall fascia, usually harvested from the anterior sheath for a pubovaginal sling. It is used for female stress urinary incontinence (SUI). Other applications, including prolapse and penile reconstruction, have a smaller and less comparative evidence base. A free fascial graft is distinct from a vascularized rectus muscle or myofascial flap.

The [autologous pubovaginal sling procedure](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/autologous-pubovaginal-sling) page covers placement and tensioning. This page covers the graft, harvest trade-offs and material-specific evidence.

## Benefits and Risks

Autologous fascia avoids implantation of a synthetic sling mesh and donor-tissue transmission concerns. Inflammation, infection, pain, graft failure and urinary-tract injury can still occur. Permanent fixation sutures may still be used, and postoperative obstruction or recurrent incontinence can require further treatment.

Autologous fascia is commonly chosen when the patient declines synthetic mesh, has a high risk of mesh erosion (immunocompromise, chronic steroid use, prior radiation), has had a prior mesh-sling failure or mesh complication or a failed Burch colposuspension, or has intrinsic sphincter deficiency, neurogenic bladder-neck incompetence or severe SUI.

The AUA/SUFU guideline includes autologous fascia PVS among options for index SUI patients. It advises against synthetic MUS during concomitant urethral diverticulectomy, urethrovaginal fistula repair or urethral mesh excision; if concurrent continence surgery is appropriate, autologous fascia is preferred over other biological substitutes. A remote healed urethral operation is a different situation. Poor urethral mobility and compromised tissue require individualized choices; radiation or steroid exposure also affects autologous healing.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The FDA's 2019 transvaginal POP mesh action should not be described as a ban on all sling or sacrocolpopexy mesh. Route and indication matter when discussing material options.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Harvest Planning

Prior abdominal scars, hernia repair, mesh, fascial quality and potential future flap needs are reviewed before harvest. A low transverse (Pfannenstiel) incision exposes the anterior sheath; dimensions depend on the operation and available tissue. Published sling techniques describe approximately 10 cm of fascia, and a graft of 10–12 × 2 cm is commonly quoted; neither figure fits every abdominal wall.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Nonabsorbable stay sutures are commonly tied at each end of the strip. For cystocele repair, Cormio used a trapezoidal graft (6 cm major base, 4 cm minor base, 5 cm height) suspended on four sutures.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

In 12 female anatomical donors, Cardenas-Trowers et al. modeled a site 5.4 cm above the pubic symphysis, where the graft's inferior edge could be 9.4 cm long across the midline. The 9.4 cm measurement is transverse length, not a second vertical landmark. Their closer-to-pubis estimate relied on extrapolation. These cadaveric averages do not guarantee nerve preservation in an individual patient and were not a hernia-prevention trial.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Viable fascial edges are preserved and the donor defect is closed with an appropriate abdominal-wall technique, commonly a running nonabsorbable or slowly absorbable suture. Counseling covers hematoma, wound infection/dehiscence, pain, sensory change and incisional hernia. [Fascia lata](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/autologous-tissue/fascia-lata) is an alternative when the abdominal donor site is unsuitable; it substitutes thigh morbidity for abdominal harvest morbidity.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Female SUI: Evidence That Helps Counseling

| Evidence | Result and interpretation |
|---|---|
| **SISTEr randomized trial: 655 women** | At 24 months, autologous rectus PVS had greater SUI-specific success than Burch colposuspension: **66% versus 49%**. The stricter overall continence outcome was **47% versus 38%**. Outcome assessment was available for 520 women; UTI, voiding difficulty and postoperative urgency incontinence were more frequent after the sling.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Khan long-term randomized-cohort follow-up: 162/201 respondents** | At median 10 years, **dry or improved** was 75.4% after autologous fascia versus 73% after TVT; **completely dry** was 50.8% versus 31.7%. No further operation for persistent SUI was reported in the autologous arm. This does not mean no reoperations or complications of any kind.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **E-SISTEr observational extension: 482 enrolled** | Continence declined over time, while most respondents remained satisfied. The five-year cumulative composite continence estimate was 30.8% after sling versus 24.1% after Burch; these estimates retain prior failure and do not represent cross-sectional dryness. At the five-year visit, satisfaction was 83% (148/179) versus 73% (126/172) among respondents. The endpoint differed from the original trial, and women with persistent incontinence were more likely to enroll. These are not interchangeable “cure rates.”<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **2020 Cochrane traditional-sling review** | 34 trials/3,244 women, with the search run to February 2017. Versus open colposuspension, traditional slings probably left more women continent at one to five years (69% vs 59%; moderate-quality evidence) and required repeat continence surgery less often (RR 0.15; high-quality evidence). Versus mid-urethral slings, traditional slings were probably no better and may be less effective at one to five years (67% vs 74%; OR 0.67, 95% CI 0.44 to 1.02), and mid-urethral slings may have fewer perioperative complications. Autologous, donor-derived and synthetic materials are pooled, so the findings are not an autologous-rectus-only estimate.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| **Athanasopoulos retrospective series: 264 women** | Mean follow-up 27.8 months. 224 (85%) were successfully treated and satisfied, including 200 (75.8% of all patients) totally dry. The complication rate was 29.2%, and postoperative urgency was the most common problem (18.5%). Retrospective design and pad-based, satisfaction-linked success define these figures.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |
| **2024 randomized-trial review** | No statistically clear cure difference was detected between autologous fascia and synthetic midurethral slings. Operative time, hospital stay and de novo urgency favored synthetic slings; reported long-term complications favored fascia. The review pooled 10 RCTs in its meta-analysis; de novo urgency was more frequent with autologous slings (RR 2.84, 95% CI 1.13 to 7.10). Heterogeneous procedures and outcome definitions prevent a blanket claim of equal efficacy or universally lower morbidity.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| **Zargham prospective cohort: 125 women** | Classic rectus PVS (52) versus a polyvinylidene fluoride tape sling (73) at a mean of 38 and 41 months. Success was 94% and 93%. Operating time was longer with PVS (91.1 vs 32.4 minutes), and the overall complication rate was 47.2% vs 25% (p = 0.049). The comparator was a single tape type in a nonrandomized cohort.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> |

### Placement and postoperative emptying

Classic PVS supports the bladder neck/proximal urethra; short midurethral fascial slings are modifications, not identical operations. Cystoscopy checks lower-tract integrity after retropubic passage. It does not replace deliberate sling tensioning or a postoperative voiding assessment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

Osman's retrospective midurethral "sling on a string" series included 106 patients with mean nine-month follow-up. A short fascial strip is suspended on sutures and placed loosely at the midurethra, with more tension only in patients with intrinsic sphincter deficiency. In primary SUI (46.2%), subjective cure was 87.8%, de novo storage symptoms 8.2% and intermittent self-catheterization beyond two weeks 2.0%. After prior surgery, the figures were 72.0%, 14.0% and 10.5%, so the primary-SUI results do not apply to every salvage case.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> A laparoscopic midurethral technique has been described in 10 patients, all reporting cure at 12 months.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

An autologous transobturator sling was compared with TVT-O in a randomized trial (dryness 92.4% [49/53] vs 94.6% [53/56] at 24 months; p = 0.47).<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> A 2024 review of 11 studies (323 patients) reported a pooled 12-month success of 96.8% in single-arm series and similar success and complication rates to synthetic transobturator slings, with longer operative time. The authors describe the data as limited and without long-term follow-up.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Prolapse Reconstruction

Rectus fascia can be used in selected [sacrocolpopexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrocolpopexy) or vaginal reconstructive techniques when the patient wishes to avoid synthetic mesh. This does not establish that it has the same durability as mesh, or that it is preferred for every patient with a mesh-risk factor.

In Wang's single-center 132-patient cohort, median follow-up was 2.2 years. Five-year survival estimates were 28.3% composite failure, 6.8% anatomic failure and 13.0% retreatment, with wide confidence intervals. Quoting only anatomic failure would conceal symptomatic and retreatment outcomes.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

Cormio's 30-patient pubovaginal cystocele-sling series reported no anterior recurrence at mean 62.6 months, with one apical/posterior recurrence and one superficial donor-wound dehiscence. This specialized uncontrolled series is not a universal 100% prolapse-success benchmark.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Other Reconstructive Uses

| Application | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|
| **Peyronie/tunical grafting** | A cadaveric comparison and 12-patient series using the **dorsal rectus sheath** found similar histological architecture to tunica and straightening in 10 patients; six had minor complications. Histological resemblance does not establish an ideal graft or protection from erectile dysfunction.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **Corporal coverage during penile prosthesis placement** | Pathak's 15-patient fibrosis series reported functioning implants without graft-related infection/erosion at mean 18 months. It establishes feasibility in selected reconstructions, not universal success or absence of future prosthesis complications.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| **Neurogenic bladder-neck sling/cinch** | Increasing outlet resistance requires assessment of bladder storage pressures, upper tracts and a catheterization plan. Bauer reported that 8 of 11 girls were dry at 3 to 24 months after a rectus fascial sling.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> In Bugg's 15 children undergoing concurrent augmentation, nine were dry by the study definition; the authors subsequently changed their technique because results did not meet expectations.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| **Vascularized myofascial wrap** | A pedicled rectus muscle/sheath construct is a flap with its own blood supply and different harvest burden; outcomes cannot be pooled with free fascia grafts.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| **Rectus abdominis muscle flap at bladder neck closure** | In a six-patient retrospective series, a rectus abdominis muscle flap was interposed between the bladder neck and urethral stump when omentum was unavailable. All six were continent at a mean of 45.5 months without related fistula. This is a muscle flap, not a free fascial graft.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> |
| **Recurrent metoidioplasty fistula** | Johnsen reported no recurrence in **three patients** at median seven months. Free fascia separates suture lines but does not provide the vascular supply of a pedicled interposition flap.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> |

The tissue source, graft dimensions, fixation material, donor-site closure and any associated reconstructive procedure are documented in the operative note.
