## Organizing Framework

Biomaterials in reconstructive urology span ten functional categories — plus two additional categories included because they enter the clinic even though they are not reconstructive biomaterials *per se*:

**Permanent implanted materials:**

1. **Synthetic meshes** — permanent polypropylene dominates; absorbable and hybrid variants exist
2. **Urethral bulking agents** — injectable soft-tissue fillers for SUI
3. **Biological grafts** — xenogenic decellularized collagen matrices (largely fading from prolapse practice)
4. **Autologous tissue** — the patient's own fascia and bowel; the enduring gold standards
5. **Prosthetics** — implanted mechanical devices (IPP, AUS, malleable prosthesis)
6. **Neuromodulation devices** — sacral neuromodulation (InterStim, Axonics) and tibial nerve stimulation (PTNS systems; implantable eCoin, Revi, Altaviva)

**Temporary / indwelling devices:**

7. **Ureteral stents &amp; upper-tract drainage** — double-J stents, nephrostomy, nephroureteral, and long-term metal stents
8. **Urinary catheters** — Foley, Council tip, coudé, suprapubic, self-retaining, intermittent
9. **Surgical drains** — Jackson-Pratt, Blake, Penrose

**Investigational and adjunct:**

10. **Tissue engineering scaffolds** — investigational constructs for bladder, urethra, ureter
11. **Adjunct / specialty** — devices encountered in GU practice that don't fit elsewhere (SpaceOAR, UroLift, Himplant)
12. **Non-medical foreign bodies** — penile pearls and free silicone, which reconstructive urologists see as complications rather than implanted materials

See also: [Grafts in GU Reconstruction](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts-gu-reconstruction), [Flaps in GU Reconstruction](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps-gu-reconstruction), [Reconstructive Ladder](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/reconstructive-ladder).
