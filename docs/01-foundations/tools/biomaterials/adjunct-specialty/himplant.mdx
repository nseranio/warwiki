# Himplant / Penuma — Subcutaneous Silicone Sleeve

**Penuma and Himplant** name a family of preformed silicone sleeves placed around the penile shaft, outside the corpora cavernosa. Penuma is the original device, and Himplant is described by its developer as a later iteration with a refined size matrix. Their cosmetic role differs from that of intracorporal prostheses, which provide erectile rigidity. Device generations and operative approaches have changed, so an older series does not establish outcomes for every current configuration.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For the operative evidence and complications, see [Penuma & Himplant](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/penuma).

## Material and Regulatory Context

The May 2022 FDA **510(k)** clearance (K220760, "Pre-Formed Penile Silicone Block") describes medical-grade silicone with an embedded polyester mesh, in one durometer ("Soft") and three sizes. Size L is 12 cm long, 0.5 cm thick and 2 cm high. Size XL is 15 cm, 0.8 cm and 3 cm. Size XXL is 18 cm, 1.1 cm and 3.5 cm. The surgeon may trim the block to fit the patient. The indication is augmentation, reconstructive and cosmetic surgery, and the summary states that augmentation use is for aesthetic purposes. The clearance rested on substantial equivalence to a predicate (K181387) and cited a retrospective series of 400 patients. It is distinct from a demonstration of long-term benefit and is not a clearance to treat erectile dysfunction. Verify the exact contemporary product rather than assuming that every Himplant has the same size matrix as the cleared model.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

As described on the atlas page, the sleeve is crescent-shaped, with the ventral (urethral) side open. It lies in the subcutaneous plane between dartos and Buck fascia, external to the tunica albuginea, and does not enter the corpora cavernosa. It enlarges the flaccid penis and does not treat erectile dysfunction.

A solid silicone sleeve is also different from [free liquid-silicone injection](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/foreign-bodies/free-silicone). A ventral opening or placement external to the tunica does not guarantee preservation of urethral, skin, sensory or erectile function.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Why Reconstructive Surgeons See These Devices

The devices are cosmetic, but patients with a sleeve in place are referred to reconstructive urologists for complications. These include extrusion or erosion through the skin, infection and abscess, migration or asymmetry, and distal flaring that produces a visible coronal ridge. Vascular or sensory compromise of shaft skin and dissatisfaction that leads to removal also occur. A referral-center series of 11 patients reported sexually disabling deformity, severe shortening and sexual dysfunction after genital-enlargement procedures that included subcutaneous implants. It also included non-Penuma devices and injections, so it cannot give an incidence.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## SMSNA Position and Counseling

The **2024 SMSNA position statement** recommends silicone-sleeve implantation only under **IRB oversight with long-term safety endpoints and no cost to the patient**, by experienced surgeons after discussion of potentially severe complications. This is an expert-consensus recommendation reflecting limited and potentially biased evidence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Infection, erosion, extrusion, migration, distal flaring, deformity and dissatisfaction may lead to revision or removal. Published referral series document persistent shortening, curvature, sensory symptoms and erectile dysfunction after explantation. These selected complication series cannot estimate the overall event rate, but they refute a promise that removal reliably restores the preoperative appearance or function.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Removal and Subsequent Reconstruction

**Surgical removal is possible; complete reversibility is not assured.** Scarring, tissue adherence, infection and skin compromise determine the operative plan and whether staged management is required. Rehabilitation protocols should not be presented as guarantees of restored length or function. The same distinction between explantation and full reversibility also applies to corporal penile prostheses.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
