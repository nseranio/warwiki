# SpaceOAR Hydrogel

**SpaceOAR** creates temporary separation between the prostate and anterior rectal wall to reduce rectal radiation exposure during prostate radiotherapy. **SpaceOAR Vue** is an iodinated PEG formulation designed for radiographic visibility, including CT. Its CT appearance differs from that of the original noniodinated hydrogel.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The February 2026 FDA 510(k) clearance of Vue (K260119) covers limited component changes with indications for use identical to the earlier device. It does not establish a clinical-outcome advantage over that device.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Persistence and Imaging

Manufacturer safety information describes spacing for approximately 12 weeks and absorption around six months, while explicitly noting that complete absorption may take longer. A residual collection is therefore not automatically abnormal at six months. The product, placement date, symptoms and imaging findings are reviewed together.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Evidence

A phase III trial randomized 222 men receiving conventionally fractionated image-guided IMRT. Patients were randomized 2:1 to spacer or control and received 79.2 Gy in 1.8-Gy fractions. At a median follow-up of three years, rectal toxicity and bowel quality of life favored the spacer.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> These results apply to that regimen. A 2025 systematic review of 30 prospective studies reported improved long-term bowel quality of life with implantable spacers and no grade 4 or 5 gastrointestinal toxicity in clinical trials, and it noted that not every patient benefits equally.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Placement Injury and Reconstructive Relevance

The spacer is placed transperineally under ultrasound guidance. It can cause serious injury if injected into the rectal wall, another unintended tissue plane or a blood vessel. The manufacturer's 2022 safety update directs the operator to maintain needle-tip visualization, aspirate before injection, stop if blood is aspirated, and stop immediately if hydrogel is not seen entering the intended perirectal space. Continued injection into an uncertain plane can cause serious harm, including embolism.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

Reported risks include infection or abscess, rectal ulceration, perforation, fistula, necrosis and embolism. Prior pelvic radiotherapy may increase fistula risk. The manufacturer states that patients with PEG sensitivity, and for Vue sensitivity to its iodine-containing material, have not been studied.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Spacer history is relevant when evaluating pain, bleeding, infection, new urinary or rectal symptoms or a suspected post-radiation fistula. See [Radiation Tissue Effects](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/radiation-tissue-effects) and [Rectovesical Fistula](/docs/clinical-conditions/03f-fistulas/in-males/rectovesical).
