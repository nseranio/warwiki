# Rezūm — Water-Vapor Thermal Therapy

**Rezūm** treats BPH-related obstruction by delivering water vapor into prostate tissue in controlled **nine-second doses**. Condensation transfers heat and causes tissue ablation; this is a thermal treatment, not guaranteed preservation of adjacent mucosa, sphincter or ejaculatory structures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For selection and procedure details, see [Rezūm — Water Vapor Therapy](/docs/surgical-techniques/bph-male-luts/rezum-water-vapor-therapy).

## Components and Labeled Use

The system has a **reusable generator** and a **single-use delivery-device kit** with a retractable needle, tubing, syringe, spike adaptor and sterile-water vial. The checked US directions specify a compatible **4 mm, 30-degree, 30 cm cystoscopic lens**; a generic 0-degree scope is not interchangeable. The number and position of injections depend on the anatomy and instructions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The manufacturer-posted US directions indicate treatment in **men aged 50 or older with 30–80 cm³ prostates**, including central-zone and/or median-lobe hyperplasia. The presence of a median lobe is also not a blanket contraindication to every other minimally invasive BPH device. Trial and guideline selection remain separate from device labeling.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Safety

The checked instructions contraindicate treatment with an **artificial urinary sphincter, penile prosthesis or active UTI**. Prior pelvic radiation is an evidence gap in this label. Exclude other causes of obstruction, including urethral stricture, before attributing symptoms to BPH.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Hot vapor during priming can burn tissue. Do not move the delivery device with the needle deployed or withdraw it before confirming complete needle retraction; follow the instructed manual-retraction procedure if required. Early irritative symptoms, bleeding and retention may require catheterization and reassessment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Clinical Evidence

The pivotal trial randomized **197 men** to treatment or sham. Treated participants had sustained symptom improvement at five years, with an approximately **48% IPSS reduction** and **4.4% surgical retreatment**. Sham crossover was offered after three months, so the five-year results are not a five-year randomized sham comparison. Reported sexual-function preservation is reassuring, but does not guarantee no erectile or ejaculatory symptoms.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup><sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Persistent obstruction, infection, retention or suspected urethral/bladder-neck injury require evaluation rather than automatic repeat ablation. See [TURP](/docs/surgical-techniques/bph-male-luts/turp) and [HoLEP](/docs/surgical-techniques/bph-male-luts/prostate-enucleation-holep) for subsequent outlet-procedure planning.
