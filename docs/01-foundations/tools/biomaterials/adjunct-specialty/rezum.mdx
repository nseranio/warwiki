# Rezūm — Water-Vapor Thermal Therapy

**Rezūm** (Boston Scientific) treats BPH-related obstruction by using radiofrequency current to generate water vapor, which is injected into the transition zone and median lobe in controlled nine-second doses. Condensation transfers stored heat to the tissue, and the denatured cells are absorbed by the body. This is a thermal treatment, and preservation of adjacent mucosa, sphincter or ejaculatory structures is not guaranteed.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For selection and procedure details, see [Rezūm — Water Vapor Therapy](/docs/surgical-techniques/bph-male-luts/rezum-water-vapor-therapy).

## Components and Labeled Use

The system has a reusable generator and a single-use delivery-device kit with a retractable needle, tubing, syringe, spike adaptor and sterile-water vial. The checked US directions specify a compatible 4 mm, 30-degree, 30 cm cystoscopic lens; a generic 0-degree scope is not interchangeable. The number and position of injections depend on the anatomy and instructions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The manufacturer-posted US directions indicate treatment in men aged 50 or older with 30–80 cm³ prostates, including central-zone and/or median-lobe hyperplasia. The 2026 AUA guideline states that clinicians should offer water vapor thermal therapy to patients with prostates of 30 to 80 cc (Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level Grade B). Trial and guideline selection remain separate from device labeling.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

The manufacturer's directions give the following starting numbers. The treatment length is the distance from the bladder neck to the verumontanum, estimated by TRUS or cystoscopy. One treatment is a single nine-second vapor delivery. Treatments are placed about 1 cm apart, beginning about 1 cm from the bladder neck and always proximal to the verumontanum. A pre-treatment vapor cycle is run before the device enters the patient.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Distance from bladder neck to verumontanum | Treatments per lateral lobe |
|---|---|
| Less than 2.0 cm | 1 to 2 |
| 2.0 to 3.0 cm | 2 to 3 |
| More than 3.0 cm | 3 to 4 |

A median lobe smaller than 2 cm receives one treatment and a larger one receives two or more. An enlarged central zone with a prostatic urethral angle of at least 35 degrees on sagittal TRUS receives one treatment if under 2 cm and two if larger. One delivery device supports at most 15 full treatments. Exceeding the recommended number may cause prolonged irritative symptoms or catheterization.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Safety

The checked instructions contraindicate treatment with an artificial urinary sphincter, penile prosthesis or active UTI. Prior pelvic radiation is an evidence gap in this label. Exclude other causes of obstruction, including urethral stricture, before attributing symptoms to BPH.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Hot vapor during priming can burn tissue. Do not move the delivery device with the needle deployed or withdraw it before confirming complete needle retraction; follow the instructed manual-retraction procedure if required. Early irritative symptoms, bleeding and retention may require catheterization and reassessment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Clinical Evidence

The pivotal trial randomized 197 men to treatment or sham. Treated participants had sustained symptom improvement at five years, with an approximately 48% IPSS reduction and 4.4% surgical retreatment. Sham crossover was offered after three months, so the five-year results are not a five-year randomized sham comparison. At three months the IPSS fell by 11.2 points in the treatment group and 4.3 points in the control group (p &lt; 0.0001), with 136 men treated and 61 in the sham group. At five years the treatment group also had a 44% higher peak flow rate. No sustained de novo erectile dysfunction was reported. This is reassuring, but does not guarantee no erectile or ejaculatory symptoms.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup><sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The label's summary of the trial reports that 90.4% of treated men were catheterized before discharge, for a mean of 3.4 days (control 19.7%, 0.9 days).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Persistent obstruction, infection, retention or suspected urethral or bladder-neck injury require evaluation rather than automatic repeat ablation. See [TURP](/docs/surgical-techniques/bph-male-luts/turp) and [HoLEP](/docs/surgical-techniques/bph-male-luts/prostate-enucleation-holep) for subsequent outlet-procedure planning.
