# Rezūm — Water-Vapor Thermal Therapy

**Rezūm** (Boston Scientific) treats BPH-related obstruction by using radiofrequency current to generate water vapor, which is injected into the transition zone and median lobe in controlled nine-second doses. Condensation transfers stored heat to the tissue, and the denatured cells are absorbed by the body. This is a thermal treatment, and preservation of adjacent mucosa, sphincter or ejaculatory structures is not guaranteed.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For selection and procedure details, see [Rezūm — Water Vapor Therapy](/docs/surgical-techniques/bph-male-luts/rezum-water-vapor-therapy).

## Components and Labeled Use

The system has a reusable generator and a single-use delivery-device kit with a retractable needle, tubing, syringe, spike adaptor and sterile-water vial. The vial's exterior is not sterile and stays outside the sterile field.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The checked US directions and the 2021 Canada edition specify a compatible 4 mm, 30-degree, 30 cm rigid cystoscopic lens (Storz, InnoView or Richard Wolf); a generic 0-degree scope is not interchangeable, and other lenses may impair performance.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Saline should be at room temperature, because cold saline may reduce effectiveness. The number and position of injections depend on the anatomy and instructions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

FDA clearance K250584 expanded the US indication to men aged 50 or older with 30–150 cm³ prostates, including central-zone and/or median-lobe hyperplasia; the linked 2019 US directions retain the earlier 30–80 cm³ range.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> The 2026 AUA guideline says clinicians should offer water-vapor thermal therapy as an option for patients with 30–80 cc prostates (Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The 2026 EAU guideline lists the procedure among techniques under investigation and gives no recommendation for it.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Trial and guideline selection remain separate from device labeling.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The pivotal trial’s five-year outcomes apply to 30–80 cm³ glands; a 2024 review of 471 men with glands at least 80 cm³ had median follow-up of six months.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

The manufacturer's directions give the following starting numbers. The treatment length is the distance from the bladder neck to the verumontanum, estimated by TRUS or cystoscopy. One treatment is a single nine-second vapor delivery. Treatments are placed about 1 cm apart, beginning about 1 cm from the bladder neck and always proximal to the verumontanum. A pre-treatment vapor cycle is run before the device enters the patient.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Distance from bladder neck to verumontanum | Treatments per lateral lobe |
|---|---|
| Less than 2.0 cm | 1 to 2 |
| 2.0 to 3.0 cm | 2 to 3 |
| More than 3.0 cm | 3 to 4 |

For a median lobe judged to need treatment, the manufacturer's directions specify one injection when it is less than 2 cm and two or more when it is greater than 2 cm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> An enlarged central zone with a prostatic urethral angle of at least 35 degrees on sagittal TRUS receives one treatment if under 2 cm and two if larger. One delivery device supports at most 15 full treatments. Exceeding the recommended number may cause prolonged irritative symptoms or catheterization.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Safety

The checked instructions contraindicate treatment with an artificial urinary sphincter, penile prosthesis or active UTI. Prior pelvic radiation is an evidence gap in this label, and the system was not studied in men with prostate cancer. Allow tissue to heal (for example 30 days) after prostate biopsy or prior prostate surgery.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Exclude other causes of obstruction, including urethral stricture, before attributing symptoms to BPH.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

During priming, the delivery-device tip is pointed away from the patient and personnel because hot vapor can burn skin. Excessive flush-button pressure can deploy the needle unintentionally. The needle is not directed downward toward the rectum, and a full vapor cycle begins only after its black depth marker is no longer visible through the scope.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Do not move the delivery device with the needle deployed or withdraw it before confirming complete needle retraction; follow the instructed manual-retraction procedure if required. Early irritative symptoms, bleeding and retention may require catheterization and reassessment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Clinical Evidence

A 2026 pragmatic randomized trial compared Rezūm with an alpha-blocker plus a 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor in 151 sexually active men. At one year, mean IPSS improvement was 10.8 versus 6.2 points; treatment-related adverse events occurred in 40% versus 28%, including serious events in 12% versus 1.3%. Superiority for preservation of sexual function was not demonstrated after multiple imputation.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

The pivotal trial randomized 197 men to treatment or sham. Among 77 original-treatment participants analyzed at five years (of 136 randomized and 135 treated), mean IPSS was about 48% below baseline. Six of 135 treated men (4.4%) had surgical retreatment, and an additional 11.1% received BPH medication for retreatment.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Sham crossover was offered after three months, so the five-year results are not a five-year randomized sham comparison. At three months, IPSS fell by 11.2 points in the treatment arm and 4.3 points in the sham arm (p &lt; 0.0001); the trial enrolled men with 30–80 cm³ prostates and assigned 136 to Rezūm (135 treated) and 61 to sham.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> At five years, peak flow rate in the original treatment group was 49% higher than its own baseline in the paper’s detailed results (the abstract reports 44%).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> No sustained de novo erectile dysfunction was reported. This is reassuring, but does not guarantee no erectile or ejaculatory symptoms.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup><sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The 2019 US label reports catheterization before discharge in 122/135 treated men (90.4%) and 12/61 sham controls (19.7%). For the label’s catheter-duration samples (N=123 and N=12), mean durations were 3.4 and 0.9 days, respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The 2019 US label's four-year adverse-event table pools treatment and crossover participants. It reports procedure- or device-related dysuria 17%, gross hematuria 12%, decreased ejaculatory volume 5%, retention 5%, anejaculation 3% and worsening erectile dysfunction 2%; the anejaculation and worsening-ED events had not resolved by the February 2019 report. Rectal wall injury and fistula were not reported in these trial cohorts, but remain listed as potential adverse events.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

Persistent obstruction, infection, retention or suspected urethral or bladder-neck injury require evaluation rather than automatic repeat ablation. See [TURP](/docs/surgical-techniques/bph-male-luts/turp) and [HoLEP](/docs/surgical-techniques/bph-male-luts/prostate-enucleation-holep) for subsequent outlet-procedure planning.
