# vNOTES and Robotic vNOTES

**Vaginal natural orifice transluminal endoscopic surgery (vNOTES)** uses a posterior or anterior colpotomy as the working access for endoscopic pelvic and abdominal procedures, eliminating abdominal incisions. **Robotic vNOTES (RvNOTES)** docks a robotic platform — most commonly the [da Vinci SP](/docs/foundations/tools/technology/robotics/single-port) — through the vaginal port, adding wristed instruments, 3D visualization, and tremor elimination to the narrow vaginal corridor.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For the urologic reconstructive analog applying this access route to ureteral reimplantation, see [Transvaginal Ureteral Reimplantation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/transvaginal-ureteral-reimplant).

---

## Concept and Rationale

Traditional vaginal surgery has the shortest hospital stay and fastest recovery for hysterectomy but is limited by restricted visualization and confined working space.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Conventional vNOTES adds endoscopic optics through a colpotomy but still uses rigid, non-articulating instruments.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Robotic vNOTES retains the scarless access while restoring the dexterity of articulating instruments, addressing the principal limitations of both predecessor approaches.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Robotic Platforms

| Platform | Use case |
|---|---|
| **da Vinci SP (Single Port)** | Most widely reported platform for RvNOTES. Single-arm design docks easily through a vaginal gel port (e.g., Gelpoint V-Path); a metal cannula carries three articulating instruments and a flexible camera. Multi-arm robots struggle with arm-angle constraints in the narrow vaginal space; the SP avoids this.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| **Hominis Surgical System** | Purpose-built for RvNOTES. Two-center prospective study of 30 hysterectomies: **zero conversions**, median OR 57 min, median EBL 50 mL, postop pain VAS 3/10.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |

---

## Surgical Steps

1. Lithotomy under general anesthesia.
2. Posterior or anterior **colpotomy**.
3. **Gel port** (e.g., Gelpoint V-Path) inserted through the colpotomy.
4. Pneumoperitoneum established.
5. Robotic system **docked** through the vaginal port.
6. Procedure performed using single-incision robotic technique.
7. Specimen extracted transvaginally (intact or morcellated).
8. Colpotomy closed with absorbable suture.

The entire surgery is performed **"upside down"** relative to the standard transabdominal laparoscopic perspective — the surgeon must remap the locations of the uterine artery, ureter, and rectum.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Indications and Procedures

| Procedure | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Hysterectomy** (benign and malignant) | Most commonly reported.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[9]](#ref9)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| **Myomectomy** | Demonstrated for fibroids up to 8 cm; precise multilayer suturing enabled by robotic articulation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Endometriosis resection** | Including DIE involving parametrium and bowel.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Sacrocolpopexy** | Pelvic-organ-prolapse repair.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Adnexal surgery** | Ovarian cystectomy, detorsion, salpingectomy.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **Sentinel lymph node biopsy** | Endometrial-cancer staging — SLN detection rate 96–97%, comparable to transabdominal robotic surgery.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| **Ureteral reimplantation (urologic application)** | Early experience with single-port robotic transvaginal retroperitoneal access — see [Transvaginal Ureteral Reimplantation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/transvaginal-ureteral-reimplant). |

## Patient Selection

- Adequate vaginal space for port placement.
- BMI, uterine weight, prior vaginal delivery, and history of abdominal surgery predict OR time and approach selection.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- May be **particularly advantageous** in morbid obesity (BMI ≥40) where abdominal access is challenging.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- For myomectomy, ideal candidates have a single large posterior fibroid with desire for fertility preservation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Nulliparity or very narrow introitus may limit feasibility.

---

## Outcomes

- **Operative time:** RvNOTES hysterectomy 57–102 min depending on complexity. Propensity-matched comparison vs conventional vNOTES showed no significant difference (75.4 vs 83.8 min, p = 0.05).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[7]](#ref7)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Blood loss:** Consistently low, typically 30–100 mL.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Conversion:** Very low to zero in reported series.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Hospital stay:** Same-day discharge feasible for many procedures; median 1–3 days.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Postoperative pain:** Minimal (VAS 1–5 in first 24 h).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Complications:** Vaginal cuff cellulitis, UTI, intraoperative hematuria — 3.8% in vNOTES vs 0.5% in LAVH in one large comparison.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

A 773-patient retrospective comparison found vNOTES had shorter OR time and lower blood loss vs LAVH; robotic assistance was associated with slightly longer OR time vs conventional laparoscopy.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

---

## Advantages

- **No abdominal incisions** — true scarless surgery.
- **Wristed instruments** overcome the limited range of motion of conventional vNOTES, enabling precise suturing and multilayer closure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **3D visualization** improves tissue discrimination in confined space.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Improved ergonomics** — reduced surgeon musculoskeletal strain.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Faster recovery** vs abdominal approaches.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Expands the indications for vaginal surgery to large uteri, endometriosis, and obesity.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

---

## Limitations

- **Cost** — robotic surgery remains the most expensive technique. Average total cost for robotic hysterectomy ~€6,528 vs €4,401 for abdominal hysterectomy in one European analysis.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- **No haptic feedback**.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Nonstandardized training and credentialing**.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Limited high-quality evidence** — most data are retrospective single-center single-surgeon; RCTs lacking.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **"Upside-down" orientation** requires significant spatial adaptation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Vaginal-space constraints** in some patients.

---

## Learning Curve

For surgeons experienced in laparoscopic single-site and abdominal robotic surgery, proficiency in RvNOTES is reached after approximately **10 cases**, with 10–20 port placements / docking procedures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> ACOG notes that for general robotic surgery, OR-time efficiency is typically attained after **20–30 cases**, with continuous improvement through the first 100.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Training should include didactics, simulation, hands-on docking, and proctored cases — Fundamentals of Robotic Surgery and Robotic Training Network are standardized curricula.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Guideline Perspectives

- **ACOG Committee Opinion No. 810 (2020).** Vaginal surgery remains the approach of choice when feasible for hysterectomy; robotic surgery provides an alternative MIS tool with perioperative outcomes equivalent to conventional laparoscopy but improved outcomes vs laparotomy.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **RCOG Scientific Impact Paper No. 71 (2022).** Robotic surgery may be particularly beneficial for complex gynecologic surgery and patients with BMI ≥30 or pulmonary comorbidities.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

Both organizations emphasize that well-designed studies are needed to determine which patients benefit most.
