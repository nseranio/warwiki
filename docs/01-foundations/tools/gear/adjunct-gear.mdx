# Adjunct Gear

The gear that's easy to underinvest in but meaningfully affects the working day.

---

## Smoke Evacuators

**Surgical smoke is not benign.** Electrocautery, laser, and ultrasonic dissectors aerosolize viable viral particles (HPV documented), cellular debris, carbonized organic matter, and volatile chemicals. Cumulative occupational exposure over a career has measurable effects on OR personnel pulmonary function.

Options:

- **Integrated pencil** (Bovie with built-in suction) — low friction, always on
- **Standalone wall or floor unit** (Stryker Neptune, Buffalo Filter) — higher capacity, used for laparoscopic pneumo filter and laser plume evacuation
- **Laser-specific evacuator** — high-flow, required for laser procedures

The **AORN guidelines** formally recommend smoke evacuation for any procedure generating surgical smoke. Many states now have laws mandating it in licensed ORs.

## Cooling Vests

For long hot cases (5+ hour perineal urethroplasty, phalloplasty, multi-stage complex reconstruction):

- **Phase-change cooling packs** — cold-pack vests that absorb body heat for 2–3 hours before needing swap-out
- **Evaporative vests** — lighter, use water evaporation; less effective in humid climates
- Worn under the gown; a swap-out between stages is logistically straightforward

**Not universally adopted**, but surgeons who use them report meaningful fatigue reduction at hour 5+.

## Voice Communicators &amp; Headsets

- **Vocera** (hospital-specific wireless badge) — common in teaching institutions
- **Traditional pager** + bedside phone — still standard in many settings
- **Bluetooth headsets** under the surgical cap — useful for console calls and intraoperative consultation, though institutional policy varies
- **Hands-free foot pedals** — not gear per se, but consider the ergonomic impact of pedal arrays under the foot during long cases

---

## Adjustable OR Bed / Positioning

Not personal gear, but closely related: the **bed height, tilt, and articulation** at the start of every case determines your working posture for the next several hours. Take the 30 seconds to re-set the bed when you switch position or when ergonomics degrade — don't operate through discomfort.

---

## Where to Buy &amp; Practical Notes

- **Loupes and headlights:** always demo on the floor at a conference (AUA, SUFU, SESAUA) before buying. Vendors run fitting sessions.
- **Prescription eyewear:** get an updated eye exam first; spec loupes and radiation glasses to the current prescription.
- **Lead:** measure your torso in scrub-ready clothing for apron sizing; fit is the dominant variable.
- **Institutional allowance:** many programs offer a **professional-development budget** that covers gear purchases. Ask early in training or when starting at a new institution.
- **Resale market** for loupes is active; used-but-excellent TTLs can be bought for ~50% of new price if the previous owner's prescription aligns.
