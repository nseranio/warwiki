# Ergonomics &amp; Longevity

Comfortable equipment helps, but preventing occupational strain also requires a suitable operating setup, breaks and access to assessment when symptoms develop. No shoe, stool or stocking brand has been shown here to guarantee a longer surgical career. See [Surgical Ergonomics](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/surgical-ergonomics) for the broader workflow.

## Footwear and Standing Surfaces

Choose well-fitting, supportive shoes with grip appropriate to the floor and protection/cleanability that meets local theatre requirements. Trial them during standing and pedal use; excessive softness or instability may be uncomfortable even when walking feels good. Insoles and anti-fatigue mats can help some workers who stand for long periods, but are not a substitute for movement and an adjustable workstation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Use theatre-approved mats that can be cleaned and positioned without obstructing pedals, equipment or the walking route. Replace footwear according to wear, grip, fit and symptoms; there is no universal six-month replacement rule for surgeons.

## Seating and Console Setup

Choose a stable, adjustable seat that allows comfortable reach to the work and pedals. Set the table, viewer, armrests and seat together so the shoulders can relax and the hands remain near elbow level. Change sustained postures and plan short pauses when the operative stage permits. The **ACS 2025 recommendations** provide illustrated setups for open, laparoscopic and robotic work; specific gear should fit the operator and task.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

A saddle or active stool may suit an individual; neither is universally preferable, and a robotic console does not necessarily come with a dedicated manufacturer-supplied chair. Trial the actual seat-console combination before buying.

## Compression Hosiery

Graduated compression may help selected people with venous symptoms, but the pressure, length and fit should match the clinical indication. A generic “15–20 mmHg for every surgeon, thigh-high is better” prescription is not established. Therapeutic hosiery and hospital anti-embolism stockings serve different settings. Suspected significant arterial disease requires assessment before compression.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For prescribed hosiery, avoid folds and constricting rolls, follow its care instructions and arrange review when fit or symptoms change.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
