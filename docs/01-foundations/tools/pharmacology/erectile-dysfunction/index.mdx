# Erectile Dysfunction Pharmacotherapy

Systemic and local pharmacologic therapies for ED — organized by the AUA ED guideline framework from **first-line oral agents** through **intraurethral and intracavernosal options**. The surgical options (vacuum erection device, penile implant) are covered under [Sexual Dysfunction treatment articles](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction).

---

## Drug Classes

### PDE5 Inhibitors (Oral)

**First-line ED pharmacotherapy.** Potentiate NO-mediated cGMP signaling in cavernosal smooth muscle.

| Agent | Onset | Duration | Distinguishing feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sildenafil** (Viagra) | 30–60 min | 4–6 h | First-in-class; food-sensitive absorption |
| **Tadalafil** (Cialis) | 30 min | **36 h** ("weekend pill") | Longest duration; daily 5 mg approved for BPH/LUTS + ED |
| **Vardenafil** (Levitra, Staxyn) | 30–60 min | 4–5 h | Fast onset ODT formulation |
| **Avanafil** (Stendra) | **15 min** | 4–6 h | Fastest onset; newest FDA-approved |

See [PDE5 inhibitors clinical article](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/pde5-inhibitors) for treatment-pathway context.

**Key contraindications:**

- **Nitrates / nitric oxide donors** — absolute contraindication (severe hypotension)
- **Unstable angina, recent MI / stroke**, **severe hypotension**, **NAION history**
- **α-blockers** — relative; start PDE5i at lowest dose; avoid tamsulosin/silodosin co-administration

### Intracavernosal Injection (ICI)

**Second-line** when oral therapy fails. Direct cavernosal vasodilation; success rate &gt;80%.

| Regimen | Composition | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Alprostadil (Caverject, Edex)** | PGE1 | FDA-approved monotherapy; pain in ~30%; priapism risk ~1% |
| **Bimix** | Papaverine + phentolamine | Compounded; second-line when alprostadil fails or causes pain |
| **Trimix** | Papaverine + phentolamine + alprostadil | Compounded; synergistic; most common ICI regimen in specialty practice |
| **Quadmix** | Trimix + atropine | Added for refractory cases |

See [Intracavernosal injections clinical article](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/intracavernosal-injections) for dosing, titration, and self-injection training.

### Intraurethral Alprostadil (MUSE)

Transurethral alprostadil suppository — PGE1 delivered into the urethral lumen. Less effective than ICI but **no needle required**. See [MUSE / intraurethral therapy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/muse-intraurethral).

### Testosterone Replacement Therapy (TRT)

Hypogonadal men with symptomatic ED may benefit from TRT **in addition to, not instead of, PDE5i**. Diagnosis requires two morning total testosterone levels &lt;300 ng/dL with appropriate symptoms.

**Formulations:** IM (cypionate/enanthate), transdermal gel (AndroGel, Testim, Axiron), subcutaneous pellets (Testopel), subcutaneous auto-injector (Xyosted), nasal gel (Natesto), buccal (Striant), oral undecanoate (Jatenzo, Kyzatrex).

**Monitoring:** serum testosterone, CBC (erythrocytosis), PSA, liver function.

### Emerging / Adjunct Agents

- **Daily low-dose PDE5i** (sildenafil 25 mg daily, tadalafil 5 mg daily) — for endothelial rehabilitation in post-prostatectomy ED
- **Yohimbine** — α2-antagonist; limited evidence; largely supplanted by PDE5i
- **L-arginine, L-citrulline** — over-the-counter precursors; modest evidence
- **Experimental intracavernosal agents** — ROK inhibitors, nitric oxide donors, stem cell therapies (investigational)
