# Desmopressin

**Desmopressin (DDAVP, dDAVP)** is a synthetic analogue of **arginine vasopressin (AVP)** with selective activity at the **V2 receptor** in the renal collecting duct. By increasing water reabsorption and thereby reducing overnight urine output, it addresses the subset of nocturia driven by **nocturnal polyuria** rather than primary storage dysfunction or BPH. FDA-approved in the United States as **NOCDURNA** (sublingual) specifically for nocturnal polyuria in adults.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Mechanism

- Binds **V2 receptors** on principal cells of the renal collecting duct
- **Aquaporin-2 insertion** into the apical membrane → water reabsorption
- **Reduces overnight urine volume** by ~30–50%
- **Minimal V1 (vascular) activity** — does not produce the vasoconstriction / hypertension associated with native AVP

---

## FDA-Approved Indications

- **Nocturnal polyuria** in adults with nocturia (NOCDURNA)
- **Primary nocturnal enuresis** in children
- **Diabetes insipidus** (central / cranial)
- **Von Willebrand disease** (type 1) — different formulation, different clinical use

---

## Dosing — Sex-Specific Considerations

**Key safety principle:** women have higher serum desmopressin concentrations and greater hyponatremia risk at equivalent doses — so **dosing is sex-stratified**:

| Population | NOCDURNA sublingual | Oral (DDAVP) |
|---|---|---|
| **Women** | **27.7 mcg** (0.83 mcg) SL qhs | 0.1–0.2 mg PO qhs |
| **Men** | **55.3 mcg** (1.66 mcg) SL qhs | 0.2–0.4 mg PO qhs |
| **Elderly (&gt;65)** | Lower dose, more monitoring | Lower dose, more monitoring |

---

## Clinical Efficacy

- **Reduction in nocturia episodes** by ~1 per night vs. baseline in pivotal trials
- **Reduction in overnight urine volume** by ~30%
- **Improvement in time to first nocturnal void** (delayed by &gt;30 minutes)
- Benefit sustained over 12+ months with continued therapy

---

## Hyponatremia — The Defining Safety Concern

The dose-limiting adverse effect. Reported rates vary by population and monitoring intensity:

- **Baseline serum sodium &gt;135 mEq/L required** before initiation
- **Check sodium at day 4–8**, at 1 month, then periodically
- **Risk factors:** age &gt;65, female sex, concomitant thiazide / SSRI / SNRI, low baseline sodium
- **Contraindicated** in history of hyponatremia, SIADH, significant CHF, significant renal impairment (CrCl &lt;50), glucocorticoid deficiency

**Signs to recognize:** headache, nausea, lethargy, confusion, seizure. The **elderly are the highest-risk population** — this is the single largest use-limiting factor in practice.

---

## Contraindications

- **Hyponatremia** or history of hyponatremia
- **Severe renal impairment** (CrCl &lt;50 mL/min)
- **SIADH**
- **Polydipsia**
- **NYHA class II–IV heart failure**
- **Loop diuretic use**
- **Systemic or inhaled glucocorticoid use** (increases hyponatremia risk)

---

## Clinical Positioning

**Nocturnal polyuria is a specific phenotype** — define it with a **bladder diary** demonstrating &gt;33% of 24-hr urine output overnight. Many patients with "nocturia" do not have true nocturnal polyuria and are better served with other pharmacotherapy (antimuscarinic, β3-agonist, α-blocker for BPH).

- **First-line behavioral measures:** fluid restriction after 6 PM, leg elevation, compression stockings in patients with dependent edema
- **Desmopressin: add** when behavioral measures are inadequate and bladder diary confirms nocturnal polyuria
- **Monitor sodium rigorously** — this is not a set-and-forget medication
