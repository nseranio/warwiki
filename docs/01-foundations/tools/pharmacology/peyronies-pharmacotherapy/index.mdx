# Peyronie's Disease Pharmacotherapy

Medical therapy for Peyronie's disease divides into **intralesional injection** (the only FDA-approved pathway), **oral agents** (historical, weak evidence), and **adjunctive mechanical therapies** (penile traction, VED, ESWT — covered in the Peyronie's clinical article).

The 2015 AUA Peyronie's guideline and 2023 Cochrane review converge on the conclusion that **no oral monotherapy has robust evidence of efficacy** and that **only intralesional collagenase (CCH/Xiaflex) is FDA-approved** for Peyronie's-specific pharmacotherapy.

---

## Intralesional Therapy

### Collagenase clostridium histolyticum (CCH / Xiaflex)

**The only FDA-approved pharmacotherapy for Peyronie's disease.** Cleaves collagen in the tunical plaque, producing plaque remodeling.

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Indication** | Stable PD with palpable plaque and curvature 30°–90° |
| **Protocol** | Up to 8 injections × 10,000 U over 24 weeks, in 4 cycles of 2 injections 24–72 hours apart |
| **Modeling** | Clinician modeling after each cycle (48–72 hr post-injection); home modeling 3×/day between cycles |
| **Efficacy** | Mean curvature reduction ~17° (CCH) vs. ~9° (placebo) — net benefit ~8° (IMPRESS I & II) |
| **Adverse events** | Ecchymosis, swelling, pain, hematoma; **corporal rupture / fracture ~0.5%** |
| **Contraindications** | Ventral curvature, plaque at the base, inability to perform home modeling, unstable disease |

See [Peyronie's disease — non-surgical therapies](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease#non-surgical-management) for clinical-pathway context.

### Interferon α-2b

Modest evidence for curvature reduction (~12–14°) and pain reduction. Dose: 5 million units bi-weekly × 6 doses. Not FDA-approved for Peyronie's specifically but used off-label.

### Verapamil

Calcium channel blocker; some evidence for plaque softening and pain in uncontrolled series; lacks strong RCT support. Occasionally used but not recommended as primary therapy.

### Other intralesional agents

Limited evidence; **not standard of care:**

- Hyaluronic acid
- Botulinum toxin
- Nicardipine
- Corticosteroids (contraindicated by AUA guideline — tissue atrophy concern)

---

## Oral Agents — Why the Evidence Is Poor

No oral monotherapy has robust evidence of efficacy. Agents historically used — with the AUA and Cochrane conclusions:

| Agent | Class | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Vitamin E** | Antioxidant | Widely used; no demonstrable benefit over placebo |
| **Pentoxifylline** | Phosphodiesterase inhibitor | Mechanistic rationale; no RCT benefit |
| **Colchicine** | Anti-fibrotic | Limited evidence; GI side effects |
| **Tamoxifen** | SERM | Historical; no evidence of benefit |
| **Potassium paraaminobenzoate (Potaba)** | Antifibrotic | Grade 3 evidence; not widely recommended |
| **L-carnitine** | Antioxidant | Small studies; unclear efficacy |

**AUA 2015 / EAU 2025 / BJU 2026 guideline-of-guidelines consensus:** oral monotherapy is **not evidence-based** and should not be recommended as primary Peyronie's treatment.

---

## Adjunctive Mechanical Therapies

Not pharmacotherapy per se, but often combined with intralesional agents:

- **Penile traction therapy (RestoreX)** — 2–8 hours daily; modest curvature reduction + length preservation; particularly valuable in acute phase and as adjunct to CCH
- **Vacuum erection device (VED)** — used adjunctively
- **Extracorporeal shock wave therapy (ESWT)** — evidence for **pain reduction** only, not curvature

See [Peyronie's disease — non-surgical therapies](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease#non-surgical-management).
