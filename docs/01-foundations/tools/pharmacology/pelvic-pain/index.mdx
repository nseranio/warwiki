# Pelvic Pain &amp; Interstitial Cystitis / Bladder Pain Syndrome

Systemic and intravesical pharmacotherapy for **interstitial cystitis / bladder pain syndrome (IC/BPS)**, **chronic pelvic pain syndrome (CPPS / CP/CPPS)**, **provoked vestibulodynia**, and **neurogenic pelvic pain**. These syndromes overlap mechanistically (neurogenic inflammation, central sensitization, pelvic floor dysfunction) and share a pharmacologic toolkit.

For the clinical framework see the [AUA IC/BPS guideline](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35536143/) and the [AUA CP/CPPS guideline](https://www.auanet.org/).

---

## Drug Classes

### Tricyclic Antidepressants (TCAs)

**Amitriptyline** is the workhorse. Mechanisms include **anticholinergic**, **serotonergic**, **α-adrenergic**, **sodium-channel**, and **antihistamine** effects — multifactorial analgesic and bladder-stabilizing activity at doses much lower than depression therapy.

| Agent | Typical pelvic-pain dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Amitriptyline** | Start 10 mg qhs; titrate to 25–75 mg qhs | Workhorse; anticholinergic side effects limit tolerability |
| **Nortriptyline** | 10–50 mg qhs | Metabolite of amitriptyline; less anticholinergic |
| **Imipramine** | 10–50 mg qhs | Alternative when amitriptyline intolerant |

**Key considerations:**

- **Start low, titrate slowly** — nocturnal sedation the limiting factor
- **Anticholinergic burden** — concerns overlap with the OAB antimuscarinic dementia discussion
- **QT prolongation** — caution in cardiac patients; check baseline ECG in vulnerable populations
- **Drug interactions** — SSRIs, tramadol, linezolid (serotonin syndrome)

### Gabapentinoids

**Gabapentin** and **pregabalin** — α2δ calcium channel modulators — for **neuropathic pelvic pain**, particularly after pelvic radiation, pudendal neuralgia, and vulvodynia.

| Agent | Pelvic-pain dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Gabapentin** | Start 300 mg qhs; titrate to 900–3600 mg/day divided | Inexpensive; renally cleared — adjust for CKD |
| **Pregabalin** | Start 75 mg BID; titrate to 150–600 mg/day | Higher bioavailability than gabapentin; faster titration |

**Side effects:** sedation, dizziness, peripheral edema, weight gain. Abuse potential (especially pregabalin — schedule V).

### Pentosan Polysulfate Sodium (PPS / Elmiron)

Oral glycosaminoglycan — theoretically replenishes the damaged bladder urothelial GAG layer in IC/BPS.

- **Dose:** 100 mg PO TID (300 mg total daily)
- **Onset:** 3–6 months to evaluate response
- **Key safety concern:** **pigmentary maculopathy** — long-term exposure (years) linked to retinal deposits and vision changes; **baseline and annual ophthalmologic screening** recommended
- **Efficacy:** modest; AUA guideline downgraded PPS to second-line after maculopathy signal emerged

### Intravesical Therapies

Direct bladder instillation of medications — bypass systemic effects, deliver concentrated therapy to the affected organ.

| Agent | Dose / regimen | Indication |
|---|---|---|
| **DMSO** (dimethyl sulfoxide) | 50 mL of 50% solution weekly × 6, then maintenance | FDA-approved for IC/BPS; garlic-like body odor |
| **Heparin** | 10,000–40,000 U in 10 mL saline | GAG-layer replacement; often combined with lidocaine |
| **Lidocaine** | 1–5% buffered with sodium bicarbonate | Pain control; alkalinization improves mucosal absorption |
| **Bupivacaine** | 0.25% 10 mL | Longer-duration anesthesia; combined protocols |
| **"Rescue cocktail"** | Lidocaine + heparin + bicarbonate | Workhorse flare treatment in IC/BPS |
| **Intravesical BCG, chondroitin sulfate, hyaluronic acid** | Various | Less-common alternatives; international availability |

### Muscle Relaxants (for pelvic-floor hypertonicity)

Pelvic floor hypertonicity / levator ani syndrome often coexists with IC/BPS and CPPS.

- **Cyclobenzaprine** 5–10 mg qhs or TID
- **Diazepam** — oral or as a **vaginal suppository** (2–10 mg) for pelvic-floor muscle spasticity
- **Baclofen** — for refractory pelvic floor spasticity

### Other Systemic Agents

- **Hydroxyzine** — H1 antagonist with mast-cell stabilization effect; classical IC/BPS adjunct (25–75 mg qhs)
- **Cimetidine** — H2 antagonist; limited evidence; occasional use
- **Amitriptyline + hydroxyzine + PPS "triple therapy"** — an older IC/BPS regimen now less common with evolving guidelines
- **Low-dose naltrexone (LDN)** — off-label; limited evidence; emerging interest for chronic pain syndromes
- **Phenazopyridine** — symptomatic urinary tract analgesic; short-term use only

## Treatment Framework (AUA IC/BPS Guideline)

The AUA framework is stepped:

1. **First-line:** education, behavioral modification, stress management
2. **Second-line:** physical therapy (pelvic-floor), oral amitriptyline / hydroxyzine / PPS, intravesical DMSO / heparin / lidocaine
3. **Third-line:** cystoscopy with hydrodistention ± fulguration of Hunner lesions; intravesical triamcinolone for Hunner
4. **Fourth-line:** neuromodulation (SNM, PTNS)
5. **Fifth-line:** intradetrusor botulinum toxin, cyclosporine A
6. **Sixth-line:** surgical — urinary diversion ± cystectomy (rare, end-stage)
