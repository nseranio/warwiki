# Voiding &amp; BPH Pharmacotherapy

Pharmacologic therapy for **benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH)**, **voiding lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS)**, and the common comorbid OAB-with-BPH phenotype in men. Three drug classes carry the evidence weight (α-blockers, 5α-reductase inhibitors, PDE5 for BPH), with **anticholinergics** and **β3-agonists** added for concurrent storage symptoms.

For the clinical framework see the [AUA BPH guideline amendment 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37914154/).

---

## Drug Classes

### α1-Adrenergic Receptor Blockers

Smooth muscle relaxation of the **bladder neck, prostate, and prostatic urethra** via α1 blockade — the first-line symptomatic BPH treatment.

| Agent | Selectivity | Dose | Distinguishing feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tamsulosin** | α1A-selective | 0.4 mg daily (0.8 if needed) | Workhorse; minimal BP effect; **retrograde ejaculation** ~10% |
| **Silodosin** | α1A-highly selective | 8 mg daily | Most selective; highest ejaculatory disorder rate (~28%) but best BPH symptomatic response; minimal orthostasis |
| **Alfuzosin** | Non-selective | 10 mg ER daily | Preferred for patients concerned about sexual side effects |
| **Doxazosin** | Non-selective | 1–8 mg daily (titrate) | Older; significant orthostasis; cardiovascular indication (HTN) |
| **Terazosin** | Non-selective | 1–10 mg daily (titrate) | Older; similar to doxazosin |

**Key use cases:**

- **LUTS/BPH** (all agents FDA-approved)
- **Medical expulsive therapy for ureteral stones** (tamsulosin most studied — outside WARWIKI's primary focus but noted)
- **Post-catheterization/post-PVP trial-of-void**

### 5α-Reductase Inhibitors (5-ARIs)

Inhibit conversion of testosterone → dihydrotestosterone (DHT), shrinking the prostate **~20–30% over 6–12 months**. Benefit only in patients with **significantly enlarged prostate (&gt;30–40 g)**.

| Agent | Enzyme | Dose | Distinguishing feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Finasteride** | Type 2 5-ARI | 5 mg daily | Older; reduces prostate volume and prevents acute urinary retention; reduces PSA by ~50% |
| **Dutasteride** | Type 1 + 2 | 0.5 mg daily | Dual-enzyme inhibition; slightly greater DHT suppression; similar clinical efficacy |

**Key considerations:**

- **Slow onset** (6–12 months for full effect)
- **Reduces PSA by ~50%** — multiply measured PSA by 2 when screening for cancer
- **Sexual side effects:** decreased libido 6–10%, ED 5–8%, ejaculatory dysfunction 2–3%
- **Post-finasteride syndrome** — persistent sexual/neurocognitive symptoms after discontinuation; controversial but meaningful patient concern
- **Reduces acute urinary retention and need for BPH surgery** in large prostates (MTOPS, CombAT trials)

### PDE5 Inhibitors for BPH

**Tadalafil 5 mg PO daily** is FDA-approved for BPH/LUTS — with or without concurrent ED. Acts on **cGMP → smooth muscle relaxation** in prostate, bladder neck, and urethra. Modest symptomatic improvement (IPSS reduction 2–3 points) but meaningful for men with both ED and BPH.

### Combination Therapy

- **α-blocker + 5-ARI** (e.g., tamsulosin + dutasteride, available as Jalyn) — superior to either alone in men with large prostates (CombAT trial)
- **α-blocker + antimuscarinic** — for men with BPH + storage symptoms (Pang 2021 Cochrane)
- **α-blocker + β3-agonist (vibegron)** — FDA-approved specifically for this population per 2024 COURAGE expansion

### Anticholinergics and β3-Agonists for Storage Symptoms

See [Storage & OAB](/docs/foundations/tools/pharmacology/storage-oab) — both classes are used in men with BPH + storage symptoms, with **vibegron specifically FDA-approved** for this phenotype after the 2024 COURAGE trial.
