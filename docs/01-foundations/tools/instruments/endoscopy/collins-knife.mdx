# Collins Knife

The Collins (also spelled Collings) knife is a hook-shaped electrosurgical electrode used through a resectoscope for endoscopic incision. It is discussed in transurethral incision procedures and in management of selected bladder-neck or vesicourethral stenoses.

## Evidence orientation

Choice of electrode, energy platform, incision pattern, and treatment sequence is procedure-specific. In a retrospective series of 123 patients with bladder-neck contracture, balloon dilation followed by bilateral Collins-knife incision was associated with reported patency in 82.1% after one and 94.3% after two procedures; this does not establish comparative superiority or a universal technique.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The AUA urethral-stricture guideline states that dilation, bladder-neck incision, or transurethral resection may be performed for bladder-neck contracture after endoscopic prostate procedures, with repeat endoscopic treatment sometimes needed.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For transurethral incision of the prostate, a systematic review of randomized trials reported similar symptom improvement to TURP at 12 months in patients with relatively small prostates, with limited evidence on longer-term comparative effectiveness.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
