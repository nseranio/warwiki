# Flexible Cystoscope

A flexible cystoscope is a steerable endoscope used chiefly for diagnostic lower-urinary-tract evaluation and selected office procedures. Scope generation, channel size, deflection, image quality, reprocessing, and available accessories vary by device and institution.

## Evidence orientation

Flexible cystoscopy and rigid cystoscopy have different capabilities and patient-experience profiles; neither should be treated as a universal substitute for the other. A 2021 systematic review and meta-analysis found high heterogeneity and did not demonstrate a statistically significant overall difference in pain perception between flexible and rigid office cystoscopy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For comfort during flexible cystoscopy, a 2019 systematic review and meta-analysis of 12 randomized trials reported a small statistically significant pain reduction with intraurethral lidocaine versus plain lubricant in men; evidence for other interventions was insufficient.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Single-use flexible cystoscopes may be reasonable alternatives or adjuncts in some settings. A 101-patient randomized noninferiority study found the evaluated single-use device noninferior for completion, image quality, light quality, and maneuverability; a 2024 systematic review of urologic single-use endoscopes found no difference in the reported infection or overall complication rates, while noting that cost and environmental effects depend on local case volume and reprocessing.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

The 2019 Cochrane review of antimicrobial prophylaxis for cystoscopy found low-certainty and context-dependent evidence; prophylaxis policy should follow current local and specialty guidance rather than this equipment overview.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
