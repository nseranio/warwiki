# Flexible Ureteroscope

A flexible ureteroscope is a deflectable upper-tract endoscope that can provide access beyond the proximal ureter to the renal pelvis and calyces. Its use, accessories, and performance depend on the platform, anatomy, indication, and operator experience.

## Evidence orientation

Flexible ureteroscopy is used in upper-tract diagnostic and therapeutic workflows, including selected stone and urothelial-carcinoma settings. The relevant disease-specific pages and current guidelines, rather than this equipment overview, should determine patient selection and treatment strategy.

Single-use and reusable ureteroscopes both remain in use. A 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis compared reported outcomes, but the retrieved record does not establish that one platform should be preferred for every patient or institution.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Ureteral access sheaths may alter irrigation and intrapelvic pressure. A systematic review found the available evidence low quality and described uncertainty about several patient-important outcomes, including stone-free rate, ureteral strictures, and cost-effectiveness.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The large prospective CROES ureteroscopy study reported global stone-treatment outcomes and complications across diverse practices. Its observational results are useful context but are not a device-selection rule or a substitute for case-specific planning.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
