# Crile Clamp

The **Crile** is a ratcheted, ring-handled hemostat with **transverse serrations along the full jaw**. It is used for selected hemostasis and tissue control before ligation or cauterization. Straight and curved patterns exist; Sklar's 17-3162 is a curved 6¼-inch (159-mm) example.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Crile versus Kelly

| Feature | Crile | [Kelly](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/clamps/kelly) |
|---|---|---|
| **Usual serration length** | Full jaw | Distal half of the jaw |
| **Curvature** | Straight and curved patterns | Straight and curved patterns |
| **Closure** | Ring handles and ratchet | Ring handles and ratchet |
| **Selection** | Match actual jaw size and access to the task | Match actual jaw size and access to the task |

These are recognition features, not a reliable force or tissue-injury scale.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Full-length serrations do not prove uniform pressure across the jaw, and a finer-looking clamp is not automatically safer around nerves or anastomotic tissue.

## Reconstructive use

A Crile can control an identified bleeding point or selected tissue before definitive hemostasis. Adequate exposure is essential: avoid sweeping a blind bite into adjacent viable structures. Use a purpose-designed vascular-control strategy when a vessel must remain patent; a general hemostat is not interchangeable with an atraumatic vascular clamp.

For blunt dissection, use only a clearly identified plane with appropriate exposure. Jaw size and curvature do not justify forceful spreading near the urethra, vas, rectum or a flap pedicle. Select tissue-preserving handling for bowel, ureter, grafts and repair edges.

## Handling and care

- Inspect tip alignment, the box lock and ratchet before use; withdraw a damaged instrument from service.
- Use the closure required for the specific tissue and task. No ratchet notch is a universal safe-pressure setting.
- Reassess if the tissue slips or control is inadequate. Repeatedly crushing a wider bite can capture or injure neighboring structures.
- Keep serrated jaws off the working length of fine sutures. A separate tail used for identification is a different task from grasping a load-bearing strand.
- Do not routinely use a metal hemostat to occlude catheters, drains or implant tubing; use the device's specified method.
- Follow the instrument's instructions for cleaning, inspection and sterilization. The cited reusable model is supplied nonsterile.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
