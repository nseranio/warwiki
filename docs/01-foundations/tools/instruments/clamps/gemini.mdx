# Gemini Dissecting Forceps

Gemini denotes a slender dissecting-forceps pattern in the right-angle/curved-clamp family. Teleflex describes it as a more delicate modification of Mixter, with **acutely curved, serrated jaws**. A catalog name does not establish a precise 90° bend, a universal size or proven tissue-safety superiority.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Match the Model to the Field

The current Teleflex family lists **18.5, 23 and 28.5 cm** lengths. Gemini instruments are therefore not invariably short or limited to 14–20 cm. Check the catalog number, jaw profile, tip width, working length and handling of the actual instrument.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Feature | Practical consequence |
|---|---|
| Slender curved jaws | May fit a prepared passage that is too narrow for a heavier clamp; this does not justify forcing an unexposed plane. |
| Serrated grasping surface | Can hold a tie or loop but can also injure delicate tissue. “Fine” is not equivalent to atraumatic. |
| Long variants | Provide reach; they do not guarantee the same tactile control as a shorter instrument. |
| Ratcheted handle | Allows locking, but the target structure does not necessarily need to be compressed. |

## Use in Reconstruction

Use a suitable Gemini to pass a ligature or loose identification loop around a structure that has been exposed and separated safely. Follow the [Mixter pass-around principles](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/clamps/mixter#controlled-pass-around-technique): identify the target, follow the tip, avoid blind spreading, and confirm what the loop surrounds before applying tension.

Do not select this general instrument merely because the operation involves small anatomy. Nerves, urethral plates, lymphatics and microvascular anastomoses require handling and instruments suited to those tissues. A generic Gemini recommendation cannot substitute for the dedicated procedure's technique.

## Inspection and Selection

Check tip alignment, serrations, hinge and locking action. Do not force a resistant plane or use delicate jaws to lever dense tissue. If exposure or reach is inadequate, improve the exposure or change instruments. Follow the particular model's cleaning and sterilization instructions.

No comparative clinical evidence cited here establishes Gemini as the least traumatic member of the family. Select by anatomy and intended maneuver, with the actual model's design in view.
