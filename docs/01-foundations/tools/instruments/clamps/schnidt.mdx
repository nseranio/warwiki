# Schnidt (Tonsil) Forceps

**A curved, ring-handled hemostatic-forceps family used in tonsil surgery and other confined operative fields.** The curve, serration extent and handle configuration vary. The name does not imply a uniformly broad, heavy jaw or a curve sharper than every Kelly or Crile.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Recognition and variants

| Manufacturer example | Described geometry |
|---|---|
| **Pilling 182390** | Half-curved, 19 cm; **half serrations** |
| **Weck 075116** | Slightly curved, 18.5 cm |
| **Weck 075120 / 075122** | Lightly / strongly curved, 19 cm; serrated, 2 mm tip |
| **Anthony Products 17-21-50E / 17-21-55E** | Light / full curve, 19.05 cm; transverse serrations, box joint, ratchet and one open ring |

Confirm the actual pattern on the tray. Neither full-jaw serrations nor an open ring is a universal Schnidt feature. Related tonsil-forceps names should not be treated as interchangeable specifications.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Use in pelvic reconstruction

A suitable long curved hemostat can reach an **exposed, identified bleeding point** in a perineal or pelvic field. The required jaw opening, tissue purchase and working angle determine whether the stocked Schnidt is appropriate. The catalog's ENT use does not establish a specific indication for urethroplasty, fistula repair or hysterectomy.

For controlled passage around a dissected structure, compare the actual geometry with a [Mixter](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/clamps/mixter) or [Gemini](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/clamps/gemini). For hysterectomy pedicles, see the dedicated [Heaney clamp](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/clamps/heaney) page. A tonsil hemostat is not automatically the safer alternative because a pedicle appears small.

**Separate bleeding-point control from preservation of vascular flow.** A ratcheted hemostat should not replace an appropriate temporary vascular clamp on a vessel required for perfusion or anastomosis.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> In particular, a functioning omental or gracilis flap pedicle is not a routine grasping or clamping target.

## Handling

- **Improve exposure before closing.** See the tip and intended tissue; exclude the ureter, bowel, nerve and viable vascular pedicle from the bite.
- **Use the needed purchase and compression.** Long jaws do not establish uniform pressure. There is no universal safe “first or second ratchet” setting.
- **Avoid forceful blind passage or spreading.** A curve improves access only when it matches an identified plane.
- **Plan definitive hemostasis before division.** The choice and position of controlling clamps, ligatures or sutures depend on the operation. There is no mandatory Schnidt-plus-Kelly sequence.
- **Inspect and process the actual model.** Check alignment, joint and ratchet, and follow its cleaning, sterilization and maintenance instructions.
