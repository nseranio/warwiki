# Wangensteen Tissue Forceps

**Wangensteen-pattern thumb forceps** are available in different lengths and jaw surfaces; the name does not establish a universal 3×3-tooth tip. For example, B. Braun model BD173R is a 230-mm, 6-mm-wide, cross-serrated Wangensteen dressing forceps. Select the actual model for the tissue and exposure rather than inferring a measured trauma advantage from the name.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Design

- **Length**: model-specific; the verified B. Braun BD173R is 23 cm (9 in).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Profile**: moderately wide flat thumb-grip handle tapering to a square tip.
- **Tips.** Verify the exact jaw; BD173R has cross-serrated, not documented 3×3-toothed, 6-mm-wide jaws.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Mechanism**: spring-action; no ratchet.
- **Material**: surgical-grade stainless steel.

## Jaw Pattern and Handling

Do not assume three teeth on each jaw or a fixed position between Adson and Bonney. A broad serrated tip can aid purchase, but grip and trauma depend on tissue, force and model:

- **Cross-serrated models** hold by contact and friction without a prominent 1×2 tooth, but can still compress or shear tissue.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Toothed models, if selected,** need their own catalog specification and careful tissue matching; no 3×3 Bonney/Adson comparison is established by the cited references.
- Brown and colleagues tested fenestrated laparoscopic grasper jaws, not Wangensteen thumb forceps, so that study does not demonstrate a Wangensteen injury reduction.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Reconstructive-Urology and Urogyn Uses

- **Abdominal-wall fascial closure.** Moderate-toughness rectus fascia and external-oblique aponeurosis during midline / Pfannenstiel / Gibson closure after open BNR, augmentation, diversion, AUS pump-pouch incisions, and open ureteral reimplant.
- **Peritoneal handling and entry.** A suitable broad model may help tent a sturdy edge, with care to avoid tearing fragile peritoneum.
- **Skin-and-subcutaneous closure.** Used when 1×2 Adson teeth are slipping: thicker abdominal-wall skin in larger patients, scarred reoperative skin, dermal edges of inguinal and suprapubic incisions.
- **Open prolapse and pelvic-reconstruction work.** Broad-ligament peritoneum, vaginal-cuff peritoneum at high McCall culdoplasty, peritoneal flaps for sacrocolpopexy interposition.
- **Open fistula repair.** Peritoneal-interposition flap mobilization (omental harvest peritoneum, peritoneal flap for VVF / RVF repair).
- **Tissue retraction during dissection.** Secure purchase on cut fascial edges to present them for layered closure.

Avoid using a broad Wangensteen-pattern tip for delicate vessel, ureteral mucosa, bowel serosa or microsurgical layers when finer control is needed; choose the actual jaw and forceps for tissue quality rather than treating another named pattern as automatically safe.

## Technique

- **Grip**: pencil grip, same as any thumb forceps.
- **Match to layer.** Inspect the jaw surface and width; a broad cross-serrated pattern may suit sturdy edges, but tooth count and tissue effect are model-specific.
- **Brief, focal grasp** at cut edges; release as soon as the suture has been thrown.

## Historical Context

The pattern is named for Owen H. Wangensteen (1898–1981), a University of Minnesota surgeon and surgical educator. His work on continuous suction and the management of intestinal obstruction is historically important, but those treatment outcomes are separate from the performance of eponymous forceps.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The forceps name identifies a family, not a fixed jaw geometry.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
