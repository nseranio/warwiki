# Wangensteen Tissue Forceps

**Wangensteen forceps** are spring-action thumb forceps.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> For example, B. Braun model BD173R is a 230-mm, 6-mm-wide, cross-serrated Wangensteen dressing forceps. Select the actual model for the tissue and exposure rather than inferring a measured trauma advantage from the name.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Design

- **Length**: model-specific; the verified B. Braun BD173R is 23 cm (9 in).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Profile**: moderately wide flat thumb-grip handle; the tip outline is model-specific, with rounded tips on Sklar 36-1310.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Tips.** Verify the exact jaw; BD173R has cross-serrated, not documented 3×3-toothed, 6-mm-wide jaws.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Mechanism**: spring-action; no ratchet.
- **Material**: model-specific; most are stainless steel, while the B. Braun BD173R DUROGRIP has tungsten-carbide inserts.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Jaw Pattern and Handling

A broad serrated tip can aid purchase, but grip and trauma depend on tissue, force and model:

- **Cross-serrated models** hold by contact and friction without a prominent 1×2 tooth, but can still compress or shear tissue.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Jaw selection.** Check the selected model’s serration, tooth count and width against the tissue edge and exposure.

## Reconstructive-Urology and Urogyn Uses

- **Abdominal-wall fascial closure.** Moderate-toughness rectus fascia and external-oblique aponeurosis during midline, Pfannenstiel or Gibson closure after open bladder-neck reconstruction, augmentation, diversion or ureteral reimplantation.
- **Peritoneal handling and entry.** A suitable broad model may help tent a sturdy edge, with care to avoid tearing fragile peritoneum.
- **Skin-and-subcutaneous closure.** A Wangensteen model may be used for thicker abdominal-wall skin, scarred reoperative skin, and dermal edges of inguinal or suprapubic incisions when its jaws fit the tissue and exposure.
- **Open prolapse and pelvic-reconstruction work.** Broad-ligament peritoneum, vaginal-cuff peritoneum at high McCall culdoplasty, and peritoneum for mesh coverage during open sacrocolpopexy.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Open fistula repair.** Interposition-flap mobilization: omental or pelvic-peritoneal flaps for VVF repair,<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> and omental flaps for selected transabdominal RVF repair.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Tissue retraction during dissection.** Secure purchase on cut fascial edges to present them for layered closure.

Avoid using a broad Wangensteen-pattern tip for delicate vessel, ureteral mucosa, bowel serosa or microsurgical layers when finer control is needed; choose the actual jaw and forceps for tissue quality rather than treating another named pattern as automatically safe.

## Technique

- **Grip**: use a controlled pencil grip with the selected Wangensteen model and adjust thumb-and-finger pressure to the tissue.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Match to layer.** Inspect the jaw surface and width; a broad cross-serrated pattern may suit sturdy edges, but tooth count and tissue effect are model-specific.
- **Brief, focal grasp** at cut edges; release as soon as the suture has been thrown.

## Historical Context

The pattern is named for Owen H. Wangensteen (1898–1981), a University of Minnesota surgeon and surgical educator. His work on continuous suction and the management of intestinal obstruction is historically important, but those treatment outcomes are separate from the performance of eponymous forceps.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The forceps name identifies a family, not a fixed jaw geometry.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
