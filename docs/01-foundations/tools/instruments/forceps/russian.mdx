# Russian Tissue Forceps

**Non-locking thumb forceps** with broad, cupped tips and radial serrations. The Sklar 19-2280 model is an 8-inch reusable thumb forceps described for thicker tissue and wound closure; that catalog description does not establish a lower clinical injury rate than toothed or DeBakey forceps.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

The cited cupped model uses radial serrations rather than a 1×2 tooth interlock. This distinguishes its grip mechanism but does not make it harmless under squeeze or traction.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Design

- **Tip**: wide, slightly concave disc with concentric fine serrations on the grasping surface.
- **Shaft**: heavier than Adson, lighter than Bonney.
- **Sizes**: catalog model 19-2280 is 8 inches (203 mm); other models require their own catalog check.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Material**: surgical-grade stainless steel; reusable autoclavable and single-use disposable versions.

## Mechanism

The cupped serrated platform provides a broad contact area and grip without a prominent penetrating 1×2 tooth. Cheng and Hannaford modeled laparoscopic jaws against liver tissue, not Russian forceps on human bowel, ureter or fascia; their result cannot quantify a Russian-versus-DeBakey clinical trauma advantage.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Compression and shear remain possible.

| Grasping surface | Radial serrations on cupped tip | Longitudinal fine serrations | 1×2 interlocking teeth | Model-specific prominent teeth | Smooth, toothed or fine serrations by model |
| Tissue trauma | Compression/shear possible | Compression/shear possible | Tooth puncture possible | Tooth puncture possible | Compression/shear or tooth puncture possible |
| Grip strength | Depends on tissue and pressure | Depends on tissue and pressure | Depends on tissue and pressure | Depends on tissue and pressure | Depends on tissue and pressure |
| Best for | General handling, tissue eversion | Vascular, delicate tissue | Skin, fascia | Heavy fascia, skin closure | Microsurgery |
| Typical urologic use | Bladder, ureter, fascia retrieval, flap handling | Vascular pedicles, renal hilum | Skin closure | Fascial closure | Vasal / nerve anastomosis |

## Key Uses in Reconstructive Urology

- **Ureteral surgery.** Selected gentle outer-wall handling during reconstruction; the tip does not guarantee freedom from crush trauma.
- **Bladder surgery.** Cystotomy closure, augmentation cystoplasty, diverticulectomy; useful for everting the bladder edge during running closure.
- **Fascial harvest.** Retrieving the rectus or fascia lata strip during pubovaginal sling harvest.
- **Reconstructive flaps.** Perineal and gluteal flap handling where broad grasp matters more than fine precision.
- **Tunica albuginea.** Gentle handling during Peyronie's plaque incision / grafting and penile-prosthesis implantation.
- **Renal pelvis / adjacent tissue.** Selected broad handling when exposure permits; avoid assuming a cupped tip is safe on renal parenchyma.
- **Suturing assistance.** The broad flat tip is well-suited for everting tissue edges during interrupted or running closure, a workflow advantage prized in urologic and GI anastomoses.

## Limitations

- **Not for very delicate tissue.** Microsurgery, vasal anastomosis, and nerve repair require finer Gerald or jeweler's forceps.
- **Not for the heaviest fascia.** Abdominal-wall fascial closure benefits more from Bonney teeth.
- **Wide tip can obscure the field** in confined spaces or microsurgical work.
- **Excessive squeezing still causes crush injury.** The broad serrated surface lessens but does not eliminate compressive trauma; force-limited grasper designs are under investigation for laparoscopic and robotic platforms.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Practical Tips

- Pencil grip for precision tissue handling; palm grip for heavier-tissue work.
- Use the broad tip to gently evert tissue edges toward the surgeon during suture placement. This is the design's signature workflow advantage.
- Use only the pressure needed for controlled purchase; serrations do not eliminate compression or tissue injury.
- Pair with a Mayo-Hegar or Castroviejo driver, Russian in the non-dominant hand for tissue manipulation and eversion.

## Historical Context

Russian-pattern forceps are a longstanding named member of the broader spring-forceps family discussed in instrument histories. The name alone does not establish a particular national-design lineage or a fixed jaw specification. The cupped serrated tip remains a practical option for selected tissue handling, with grip and injury dependent on the actual model and force.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
