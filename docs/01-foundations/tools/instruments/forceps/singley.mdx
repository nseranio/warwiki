# Singley (Singley-Tuttle) Tissue Forceps

**Spring-action thumb forceps with fenestrated jaws**, available in several lengths for open tissue handling. An Ambler-labeled Singley (Tuttle) model has serrated fenestrated jaws at 7 inches, while a Teleflex Tuttle (Singley) catalog item is 13 inches; check the actual model. Fenestration may help purchase but does **not** prove less bowel crush than toothed or fine-serrated forceps in patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Often written interchangeably as **Singley** or **Singley-Tuttle** — both names refer to the same fenestrated thumb forceps.

## Design

- **Length**: model-specific; the directly checked Ambler and Teleflex listings span 7 and 13 inches.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Profile**: spring-action thumb forceps; no ring handles, no ratchet.
- **Tips**: fenestrated, serrated jaws in the Ambler listing; alignment and specific window shape should be checked on the chosen instrument.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Material**: surgical-grade stainless steel.

## The Fenestrated-Tip Mechanism

Fenestration changes how tissue contacts the jaws but still requires controlled compression and traction. A separate laboratory study found better retention from fenestrated **laparoscopic parallel-occlusion** jaws; it did not test Singley thumb forceps or demonstrate bowel safety in patients.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> In practice:

- A fenestrated jaw may improve purchase in a suitable tissue, but the amount of squeeze and slip depends on tissue, window geometry and force.
- No prominent interlocking tooth avoids that particular puncture mechanism; a window edge can still mark or tear serosa.
- A clinical trauma ranking against DeBakey, smooth or toothed tips has not been established by the cited sources.

This makes the Singley one **available option** for brief, controlled handling of suitable bowel or peritoneal tissue during open reconstruction, not a required tray instrument.

## Reconstructive-Urology Uses

- **Open urinary diversion**: ileal conduit, orthotopic neobladder (Studer, Hautmann), continent cutaneous reservoir (Indiana, Miami, Kock) — bowel-segment isolation, mesenteric windowing, antimesenteric opening, detubularization handling.
- **Augmentation cystoplasty**: ileal / ileocecal / sigmoid patch mobilization and U-shape reconfiguration.
- **Mitrofanoff and Monti channels**: selected appendiceal or bowel handling while avoiding prolonged compression of the channel wall.
- **Open ureteral reconstruction**: ureteral mobilization, tension-free reimplant setup, ureteroureterostomy, ileal-ureter interposition.
- **Peritoneal entry** in selected open cases: tent and inspect the peritoneum before controlled incision, using suitable forceps and confirming that bowel is not incorporated.
- **Open prolapse / fistula repair**: peritoneal apex handling during high McCall / sacrocolpopexy peritoneal-flap closure, vesicovaginal-fistula peritoneal-interposition flap mobilization.
- **Packing and drape control**: secondary use as a long thumb forceps for sponges and packing in deep fields.

## Technique

- **Grip**: pencil grip, same as any thumb forceps.
- **Workflow**: held in the non-dominant hand to tent / present tissue while the dominant hand cuts, dissects, or sutures.
- **Peritoneal entry**: pick up the peritoneum bilaterally with two Singleys, lift to a tent, palpate for trapped bowel, and incise between the forceps with Metzenbaum — a textbook open-laparotomy maneuver.
- **Bowel handling**: handle the selected outer tissue briefly with minimal force and release once positioned; avoid repeated forceful regripping at the same spot. Fenestration does not prevent serosal injury.

## Distinctions from Adjacent Forceps

| Forceps | Tip design | Best layer |
|---|---|---|
| **Singley / Singley-Tuttle** | Fenestrated oval window | Peritoneum, bowel serosa |
| [**DeBakey**](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/forceps/debakey) | Parallel-ridge platform | Vessels, ureter, bowel where DeBakey already in hand |
| [**Russian**](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/forceps/russian) | Radially serrated cupped tips on a verified model | Selected robust-tissue broad grip |
| [**Adson**](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/forceps/adson) (toothed) | Fine 1×2 teeth | Skin, dartos |
| [**Bonney**](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/forceps/bonney) | Heavy 1×2 teeth | Rectus fascia, dense scar |
| [**Babcock** clamp](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/clamps/babcock) | Fenestrated *ring-handled* clamp with ratchet | Sustained bowel / appendix grip when a thumb forceps would tire the hand |

The Singley is a **fenestrated, hand-held, non-ratcheting** option between fine thumb forceps and sustained ring-handled clamps; “atraumatic” does not mean bowel can be held without crush.

## Naming

Catalogs use both **Singley (Tuttle)** and **Tuttle (Singley)** for fenestrated thumb-forceps models; choose by the specified catalog item rather than assuming identical dimensions or jaws.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
