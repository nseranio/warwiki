# Drum Dermatome — Padgett-Hood

The **Padgett-Hood dermatome** is a **drum-type skin-grafting instrument** developed in the late 1930s by Earl Padgett and George Hood. Its adhesive drum and adjustable blade enabled a large split-thickness skin graft (STSG) sheet of planned thickness, following earlier guarded hand knives such as the [Humby](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/graft-harvest/humby-dermatome). Published applications include graft harvest from an **excised gluteal hidradenitis specimen**; other references on this page concern the wider Padgett family and do not always identify a drum model.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Historical Context

- **Earl Calvin Padgett** (University of Kansas Medical Center plastic surgery chair, 1893–1946) imagined the device with help from **George J. Hood** (engineer at the University of Kansas).
- Hood applied for the first patent in **1938**; Padgett displayed a working model at a medical meeting in **1939**.
- Based on the **adhesion-traction principle** — the drum holds the skin in place by adhesive contact while a calibrated blade shaves the graft.
- The **Hood modification** (Hood Laboratories) became the standard commercially available version, hence the combined name **Padgett-Hood**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- A founding member of the American Board of Plastic Surgery called it **"the greatest contribution in many decades to the technique of skin grafting."**

## Design

- **Half-cylindrical metal drum** that rotates as it is advanced across the donor site
- **Dermatome adhesive cement** applied to the drum surface adheres the skin to the drum during rotation — provides the **counter-traction necessary for a clean even cut**<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Calibrated blade** mounted alongside the drum with an **adjustable gap** between blade and drum surface
- **Reported setting:** Kuo's six-patient Padgett-Hood series used **0.012–0.020 inches**; Tang's **0.024–0.026-inch** Padgett report did not specify the drum model. Neither range is a universal device specification.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Manual operation** — no power source

## Mechanism

The surgeon rolls the adhesive drum across a **manufacturer-prepared** donor surface so the skin is held against the drum while the blade cuts the graft. The adhesive/skin preparation must follow the device-specific instructions; do not transfer a powered-dermatome lubrication rule to an adhesive drum. Brody 1979 described device care, but its full technique text has not been verified in this batch.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Reconstructive Use Cases — Modern Niche Applications

The following reports illustrate uses of the Padgett family. Only the reused-skin report explicitly identifies **Padgett-Hood drum** use; other device-specific translations need confirmation.

### 1. Reused-Skin-Graft Technique (Donor-Site-Sparing)

**Kuo & Ohara 2003** described the Padgett-Hood for a **"reused skin graft" technique in six patients** with chronic gluteal/perianal hidradenitis suppurativa:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- After wide en-bloc excision of affected skin
- The **Padgett-Hood harvested STSGs from the excised skin specimen itself** (set at 12–20/1,000")
- Grafts then meshed 1.5× and applied to the surgical defect
- **No separate donor site needed**
- **No reported recurrence** during 8–36 mo follow-up in this small uncontrolled series; one patient had a 3 × 4 cm graft-necrosis/infection area

The adhesive-drum mechanic allowed harvest from the excised specimen in this report. It does **not** establish that another device cannot do so or that every excised hidradenitis specimen is suitable for reuse.

**GU relevance:** gluteal/perianal experience may prompt case-specific consideration near GU reconstruction, but reuse of diseased excised tissue requires separate histologic, infection and surgical assessment; this six-patient series does not validate scrotal or penile application.

### 2. Very Thick STSGs for Mobile / Joint Surfaces

**Tang 2010** described a **Padgett dermatome** for **very thick STSGs (0.024–0.026")** on mobile burned-limb surfaces. The abstract does **not specify that the model was Padgett-Hood drum** or establish a comparative contracture benefit.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

Extrapolation to genital/perineal reconstruction remains unverified.

### 3. Dermal Grafts

**Schaffer 1976** described a dermatome technique for obtaining dermal grafts; device model and comparative results require the full paper before being attributed specifically to Padgett-Hood.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

### 4. Radial Forearm Free-Flap Donor-Site Coverage

**Jaquet 2012** used a **Padgett dermatome** for **0.35–0.45 mm STSGs** from the thigh to cover RFFF donor sites in head-and-neck reconstruction. The original methods do **not identify the drum model**, and this case-control study compared donor-site closure methods, not dermatome models or phalloplasty outcomes.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### 5. De-Epithelialization of Buried Flaps

Published breast-reconstruction experience with a [Padgett electric dermatome](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/graft-harvest/padgett-dermatome) should **not be treated as a Padgett-Hood drum or phalloplasty protocol**. Drum-specific free-flap de-epithelialization evidence has not been verified here.

## Advantages

- **Excellent graft thickness uniformity** — the fixed blade-to-drum gap is highly consistent, superior to freehand
- **Thick graft capability requires model confirmation** — Tang's 0.024–0.026" report names Padgett but not the drum model<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Harvesting from excised tissue** — reported in six selected gluteal/perianal hidradenitis patients using this drum<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **No power source** required — fully manual
- **Large continuous sheet** the size of the drum surface

## Limitations

- **Adhesive cement requirement** — proper application is essential and adds setup complexity<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Technically demanding** — drum pressure, rotation speed, adhesive application all require experience
- **Slower** than powered dermatomes for large-area routine harvest
- **Maintenance burden** — careful care of the drum and blade assembly required to maintain performance<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Largely supplanted** for routine harvesting by powered oscillating-blade dermatomes ([Zimmer](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/graft-harvest/zimmer-air-dermatome), [Padgett electric](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/graft-harvest/padgett-dermatome))

## Padgett-Hood vs Other Drum Dermatomes

| Feature | **Padgett-Hood** | **Reese** | **Original Padgett (1939)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Drum type** | Half-cylindrical | Full-cylindrical with tape squares | Half-cylindrical |
| **Adhesive system** | Dermatome cement on drum | Adhesive tape squares on drum | Dermatome cement on drum |
| **Graft shape** | Large sheet (drum-sized) | Multiple small square grafts | Large sheet |
| **Thickness calibration** | Adjustable blade-drum gap | Adjustable blade-drum gap | Adjustable blade-drum gap |
| **Manufacturer** | **Hood Laboratories** | Various | Original prototype |
| **Current availability** | Verify supplier and model-specific labeling; not established by these papers | Verify supplier | Historical |

## Padgett-Hood vs Modern Powered Dermatomes

| Feature | **Padgett-Hood (drum)** | **[Zimmer Air](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/graft-harvest/zimmer-air-dermatome) / [Padgett Electric](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/graft-harvest/padgett-dermatome)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Mechanism** | Adhesive drum + calibrated blade | Oscillating blade with calibrated lever |
| **Power source** | Manual | Air or electric |
| **Setup time** | Higher (adhesive application) | Lower |
| **Routine large-area harvest** | Slower | **Faster — current standard** |
| **Reused-graft technique** (excised tissue) | Reported in a small selected series<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | Device-specific feasibility not established by Kuo |
| **Very thick STSG (up to 0.026")** | Padgett model not specified in Tang<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | Check device-specific range and indication |
| **Continuous sheet length** | Limited by drum dimension | Limited only by donor area |
