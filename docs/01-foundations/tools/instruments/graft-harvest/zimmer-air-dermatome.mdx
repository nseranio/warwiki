# Zimmer Air Dermatome

The Zimmer Air Dermatome (Zimmer Biomet) is a pneumatic oscillating-blade device for split-thickness skin graft (STSG) harvest, powered by regulated medical-grade compressed air or nitrogen. Zimmer also markets other dermatome models, but their controls and instructions are not interchangeable. In a US and Canada burn-unit director survey of dermatome-induced lacerations, 71.6% of reported events involved a Zimmer-branded dermatome and 73.0% involved an air-powered dermatome; neither percentage measures device use or market share.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

:::warning[Serial-specific field action]
Zimmer Biomet's 2025–26 field safety action ZFA-2025-00141 concerns specified Zimmer Air Dermatome and Dermatome AN serial numbers with a potentially misaligned thickness-control bar and cuts deeper than intended. Check the facility's device serial number against the manufacturer's affected-product list and follow its phase-specific notice and recall-team or representative instructions. The phase-2 hospital instructions call for immediately locating and quarantining affected inventory; the surgeon section separately describes transitional use until a device-specific removal request. This is not a recall of every Zimmer dermatome.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
:::

## Design and Components

- **Oscillating disposable blade** that shears a uniform layer of skin
- **Width plates.** 1", 2", 3", 4" in listed kits, with an optional 1.5" plate<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Thickness adjustment lever.** Nominal range 0–0.030" in 0.002" increments for the Air model; dial setting is not a direct measurement of harvested tissue thickness<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Trigger / handswitch** activation when compressed gas is connected
- **Hose** to medical compressed-gas source (air or nitrogen)

## Typical Thickness Settings

| Setting | Graft type | Common use |
|---|---|---|
| **0.008–0.012"** | Thin split-thickness | Large-TBSA burns |
| **0.012–0.018"** | Intermediate split-thickness | An option for genital / perineal coverage, selected for the recipient and donor sites |
| **0.018–0.025"** | Thick split-thickness | Selected sites; deeper donor wound |

A commonly used practical range is 0.010–0.016" across multiple surgical series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Accuracy and Calibration

The actual graft thickness frequently deviates from the dial setting, a caveat that applies to every Zimmer operator:

- **McBride 2017 pediatric n = 140** at a 0.007" dial setting: median actual thickness 6.94/1000", IQR 5.05–9.28/1000", no preoperative predictors of deviation.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Individual dermatomes calibrate differently.** Apertures ranged from 5.0 to 7.8/1000" across six tested devices.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Operator pressure, donor-skin turgor, blade sharpness** all contribute.

**Scalpel dermatome test** (Dargan 2025): passage of a #15 scalpel blade was studied as a check of blade-aperture behavior on six devices. Reliability was ICC 0.89 within an observer but only 0.52 between observers. Actual harvested graft thickness was not tested, so this is not a validated tissue-thickness calibration or substitute for manufacturer servicing.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

**Practical implication**: follow the specific manufacturer's pre-use and servicing instructions and assess the graft; neither the dial nor an aperture check alone guarantees harvested thickness.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Reconstructive / Urogyn Uses

- **Scrotal reconstruction after Fournier's gangrene.** An established GU indication
- **Post-debridement coverage.** Hidradenitis suppurativa, necrotizing soft-tissue infection, buried-penis reconstruction
- **Penile shaft resurfacing.** Post-free-silicone-explant, chronic penile dermatitis, after extensive lichen-sclerosus excision
- **Perineal reconstruction** after radical debridement
- **Phalloplasty donor-site coverage.** Radial forearm, ALT, MLD harvest sites
- **STSG-lined neovagina.** An alternative to penile-inversion or intestinal vaginoplasty in GAS
- **Coverage over tissue expanders** in staged penile reconstruction

## Dermatome-Induced Lacerations — Rare but Recognized

In a survey with 56 burn-unit director responses (42% response rate), Egro 2020 reported 133 lacerations over five years and estimated approximately 1.3 per 1,000 cases. The following are shares or attributed factors among reported laceration events, not comparative device or setting risks; exposure denominators were not supplied.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Reported event characteristic | Share of lacerations |
|---|---|
| **Air dermatome** | 73.0% |
| **4" guard** | 63.5% |
| **Thickness 0.010–0.015"** | 78.4% |
| **Angulation 30–45°** | 47.3% |
| **Excessive pressure** (most-cited cause) | 25.0% |
| **Patient factors** | 18.4% |

Lacerations typically extended to subcutaneous tissue (70.3%) but rarely caused neurovascular injury (86.5% no NV injury). The dermatome was most often set up by a scrub tech or nurse (48.6%); harvest was performed by residents (39.2%) or attendings (35.1%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Excessive pressure was the most frequently attributed cause in this survey. The data cannot determine whether a 4" guard, a particular setting or angle increases risk without knowing how often each was used.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Technique Pearls

- **Donor-site tumescent infiltration** with saline ± dilute epinephrine creates a firm flat surface and reduces bleeding.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Mineral oil or lubricant** on the donor skin reduces friction; smoother harvest.
- **Angulation.** 30–45° between the dermatome and the skin surface, consistent throughout the pass.
- **Steady, even downward pressure.** Excessive pressure was the most commonly attributed cause among reported lacerations in Egro 2020<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>; too little pressure can cause skip areas.
- **Controlled forward speed.** Too fast skips, too slow yields uneven thickness.
- **Assistant counter-traction** ahead of and behind the dermatome maintains a flat taut surface.
- **Pre-use blade and depth checks.** Follow the device instructions, confirm blade seating and dial setting, and verify recall and servicing status; the scalpel-blade study did not validate harvested thickness.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Choose a width plate appropriate to the defect**; the survey does not establish width-specific comparative risk.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Zimmer Electric vs Air

Zimmer's electric dermatome is a separate model with a different power supply; check its own instructions. Powered dermatomes have a longer historical lineage.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Choice of device depends on equipment, service status and operator training. Egro's injury-event survey cannot establish which power source predominates in all burn-center procedures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Trade-offs vs Adjacent Dermatomes

| Device | Power source | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **Zimmer Air** | Regulated medical-grade nitrogen / air | Requires compatible gas supply and hose; check serial-specific field action |
| **Zimmer Electric** | Wall outlet | Same blade mechanism; no compressed-gas dependency |
| **[Padgett](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/graft-harvest/padgett-dermatome)** | Model-specific electrical supply | No compressed-gas requirement; flap de-epithelialization has been described |
| **[Humby](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/graft-harvest/humby-dermatome)** | Manual | Cheap, portable; depends entirely on surgeon skill for uniformity |
| **[Goulian / Weck](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/graft-harvest/goulian-dermatome)** | Manual | Small handheld; narrow grafts with integrated depth guard |
