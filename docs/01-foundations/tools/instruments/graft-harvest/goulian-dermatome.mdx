# Goulian / Weck Dermatome

The **Goulian/Weck knife** is a handheld manual skin-grafting and tangential-excision instrument with a replaceable blade and interchangeable fixed-gauge guards. Its relatively narrow head can be useful on small or contoured areas where a wider harvester is awkward; the cited evidence does not establish that it is clinically superior for genital sites or quantify its use across burn centers.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Design

- **Reusable handle** accepting disposable Weck razor blades, which are thin, flat, and single-use
- **Interchangeable fixed-gauge guards** rather than one continuously adjustable guard; the selected guard's marked depth is nominal, not a measurement of the harvested tissue<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Narrow head**; listed Goulian guards are 2 3/8 inches (6 cm), so exact working width depends on the assembled product<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Pen-grip** or short-handle ergonomics for fine control in small / curved / irregular anatomic areas (fingers, hands, face, ears, interdigital spaces)

## Mechanism

Surgeon-driven manual draw, like the [Humby](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/graft-harvest/humby-dermatome), with no oscillating or motorized component. The selected guard helps limit cut depth, but actual tissue thickness remains operator- and tissue-dependent.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Reconstructive / Urogyn Uses

The Goulian's niche is precision and small-area work where powered dermatomes are oversized:

- **Small-area STSG harvest** for discrete genital / perineal defects
- **Penile-shaft resurfacing** after limited-area excision (LS, lichen planus, post-trauma skin loss)
- **Selected small genital defects** only when the operative plan, tissue and device geometry support it; no Goulian-specific glans or pediatric outcome series was verified
- **Small hidradenitis-suppurativa excision wounds** in the groin / perineum
- **Scrotal touch-up grafts** during Fournier's reconstruction when only a small additional area needs coverage
- **Manual-technique training** where an appropriate device and supervision are available

## Dual Role — Tangential Excision + Small STSG Harvest

The Goulian's two distinct surgical roles:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### 1. Tangential Excision (Burn Wound Debridement)

Hunt 1979 described Humby or Goulian-Weck tangential excision followed by immediate mesh autografting for deep dermal hand burns. The series reported 100% graft take in all but four hands, but outcomes were not separated by knife; they cannot be attributed specifically to Goulian/Weck.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### 2. Small STSG Harvest

The interchangeable guarded knife can be considered for selected small grafts and contoured areas. Hunt's hand-burn series describes wound excision, not a controlled comparison of Goulian graft harvest at scalp, glans or genital donor sites.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Advantages

- **Compact and maneuverable.** Narrow blade allows precision in small, curved, or irregular areas
- **Selectable nominal depth** by changing fixed-gauge guards<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Disposable Weck blades.** Consistently sharp edge, no sharpening needed
- **Low cost and portable.** No power source
- **Dual-purpose.** Tangential excision and small-graft harvest in the same operative setting<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Simple setup / teaching tool**

## Limitations

- **Limited graft width.** Impractical for harvesting large grafts (e.g., extensive Fournier's coverage)
- **"Shelving" failure mode.** Jeffery 2007 noted the variance between blade shape and certain body contours leads to uneven depth of excision that conflicts with the uniform thickness needed for STSGs or dermal substitutes (e.g., Integra), producing excess tissue at wound peripheries or a "stuck-on" appearance<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Excess tissue removal.** As with all conventional tangential excision, the amount removed often exceeds the necrotic tissue, sacrificing viable dermis<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Operator-dependent.** Graft thickness and excision depth depend on surgeon pressure, angle, and draw speed
- **Some surgeons never adapt** to the Weck-grip ergonomics; preference is real

## Current Role

The Goulian/Weck remains a standard burn-surgery tangential-excision instrument and a precision small-area STSG tool. The Cochrane review (Wormald 2020) classifies it alongside Watson, Humby, Brathwaite, and Cobbett knives as static metallic freehand blades for tangential excision.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Hydrosurgical and conventional knife debridement are distinct approaches. The cited 2020 Cochrane review found one small pediatric RCT with very-low-certainty evidence and could not establish a general healing, infection, operative-time or scar advantage. It does not rank the Goulian against hydrosurgery for GU wounds.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Broader debridement reviews do not supply a device-specific GU comparison.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Technique Pearls

- **Tumescent infiltration** of the donor site (saline ± dilute epinephrine) creates a firm flat surface and reduces bleeding
- **Mineral oil / lubricant** on the donor skin reduces friction
- **Hold like a pen.** Fine motor control, not a power-tool grip
- **Slow steady draw** at a consistent angle
- **Skin counter-traction** by an assistant to keep the donor surface taut and flat
- **Confirm the selected guard, blade and assembly** under the specific instrument's instructions; the guard's nominal gauge is not a tissue-thickness measurement<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
