# Poole (Pool) Suction

**Poole suction** is a large-volume operative suction pattern used for pooled blood and irrigant in abdominal or pelvic fields. A perforated outer sleeve distributes aspiration over many openings rather than concentrating it at one exposed cannula tip. This reduces, but does not eliminate, tissue grab.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Design

- **Inner suction cannula** within a removable perforated outer sleeve on common models. CONMED's single-use Poole has a movable or removable sleeve and describes three positions from sump to focused suction; its sleeve has 132 eyes. These are model-specific details, not a specification for every Poole.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Distributed openings** allow pooled fluid to reach the cannula while lowering the tendency to draw mobile tissue onto a single tip. Omentum, bowel, grafts and suture lines can still be captured, especially if the sleeve is retracted or the suction source is excessive.
- **Reusable variants exist.** The ICRC/IFRC catalog lists a metal, double-tube Poole with a screw-off outer sleeve for abdominal surgery. Reprocessing must follow the exact manufacturer's validated instructions.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Key Uses

- Large-volume fluid evacuation (blood, ascites, irrigation) during abdominal cases
- Evacuation during intraperitoneal spillage
- General abdominal / pelvic irrigation when the surgeon can keep the openings visible and away from delicate tissue

## Contrast with Yankauer

The [Yankauer](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/suction/yankauer) offers a more focused open-field tip; a Poole's perforated sleeve is useful for larger fluid pools. Neither design makes suction against delicate tissue safe. Remove or retract a sleeve for pinpoint suction only when the exact device permits it and direct visualization can protect nearby structures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
