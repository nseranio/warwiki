# Toomey Syringe

The **Toomey syringe** (catheter-tip syringe) is the manual bladder-irrigation syringe: a wide-barrel irrigation and aspiration syringe available as a 60 mL tapered-tip model that seats in a catheter funnel, including a Foley, Coudé, or three-way catheter, and as a 70 mL model supplied with catheter and Luer adapters<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>. It is the standard bedside, OR back-table, and inpatient-ward instrument for manual bladder washout (MBW), tissue or clot evacuation through a catheter, and intravesical medication instillation.

## Design

- **60 mL barrel** (50 / 70 mL variants exist; 60 mL is the predominant clinical size).
- **Tapered smooth catheter tip** slips directly into the catheter funnel with a friction seal; no Luer adapter required.
- **Wide internal bore** accepts small clot fragments, mucus, and tissue chips on aspiration without clogging.
- **Flanged plunger** supports one-handed thumb-and-finger irrigation-aspiration cycles.
- **Disposable polypropylene**; reusable autoclavable glass variants are historical.

## Reconstructive / Functional Urology Uses

### Manual Bladder Washout for Clot Retention

The dominant use. The Toomey is the irrigation arm of the **CATCH-22 protocol** (Clarebrough 2018), a structured manual-bladder-washout pathway for inpatient clot retention. In this prospective service study, referring teams inserted 16–20 Fr catheters and washed out a mean 145 mL, and nearly all needed upsizing. The urology service used a mean 5,392 mL and evacuated a mean 617 mL of clot. The authors recommend a catheter of at least 22 Fr and washing out until no more clot returns plus 1 L (the "clot + 1 L" rule). This is a descriptive protocol, not a randomized comparison.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Technique: use a 50–60 mL syringe for repeated normal-saline irrigation and suction until no further clot returns; the CATCH-22 authors recommend a further 1 L of washout after the last clot.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Forceful but controlled; over-pressurization in a small-capacity, trabeculated or neurogenic bladder risks bladder perforation, and under-volume irrigation just moves clot around.

The companion catheter physics (lumen geometry, eyelet pattern, three-way versus two-way) are addressed on the [Three-Way Catheter](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/urinary-catheters/three-way-catheter) page.

### Post-Procedure Bladder Irrigation

After [TURP](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/endoscopy/resectoscope) / [HoLEP](/docs/surgical-techniques/bph-male-luts/prostate-enucleation-holep) chip evacuation by [Ellik](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/suction/ellik-evacuator), after open bladder closure, and after urethroplasty or fistula-repair cases with intravesical work, some surgeons finish with a gentle Toomey washout through the catheter to clear residual blood, chip remnants and debris. Whether to wash out, and how forcefully, depends on the operation and the surgeon's orders; avoid it over a fresh bladder closure or anastomosis.

### Intravesical Medication Instillation

The Toomey is the standard instillation device for non-oncologic bladder agents in the reconstructive / functional urology space:

- **Povidone-iodine bladder irrigation** for recurrent UTI prevention in NLUTD patients on CIC. In a prospective before-and-after study of 119 patients with NLUTD on CIC and recurrent UTIs, daily intravesical povidone-iodine was followed by large reductions in symptomatic UTIs, ED visits and hospitalizations (IRR 0.008 for symptomatic UTI). It was uncontrolled and single-center, so it does not establish efficacy.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **DMSO, heparin or lidocaine instillations** used for [IC / BPS](/docs/clinical-conditions) symptom relief.
- **Intravesical antibiotic instillation** in selected NLUTD or catheterized patients, per institutional protocol (nonstandard).
- **Bladder priming for urodynamics or VCUG** when a gravity drip is not set up; barrel as funnel with the plunger removed.

(Intravesical chemotherapy and BCG instillation for bladder cancer is the oncology use case, outside WARWIKI scope.)

### Catheter Patency / Troubleshooting

The Toomey is the first-line bedside tool for the "is this Foley actually obstructed?" question. A gentle 30–60 mL instill / aspirate cycle helps separate a clot-occluded catheter (return clears with washout) from mechanical obstruction (no return and no restored flow). Confirm position and drainage clinically, and by ultrasound if needed.

### Bedside / OR-Adjacent Adjunct Uses

- **Wound and graft irrigation.** Buccal-graft bed, donor-site irrigation, perineal-wound copious-irrigation pre-closure.
- **Donor-site rinse during STSG** harvest with the irrigant of choice.
- **Bedside cystoscopy bladder fill** when the dedicated tubing is unavailable; barrel-as-funnel.

## When Toomey Fails — Adjuncts for Tenacious Clot Retention

The Toomey is rate-limited by the operator's hand and the catheter calibre. When standard MBW does not clear the clot:

| Adjunct | Mechanism | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct sheath-to-wall-suction (up to 250 mmHg, as reported)** via cystoscopy | Central suction through a 25 Fr cystoscope or 26 Fr resectoscope sheath in the reported series | Goel 2011 case series of 15 adults who had failed Ellik evacuation<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| **Chymotrypsin + sodium bicarbonate intravesical irrigation** | Enzymatic clot dissolution | Bo 2014 series: 19 of 22 men treated successfully<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Hydrogen peroxide bladder irrigation** | Oxidative clot dissolution | Xu 2020 retrospective series: clot cleared in 27 of 31<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **OR cystoscopy with [Ellik](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/suction/ellik-evacuator) evacuation** | Larger-bore sheath, mechanical chip retrieval | Standard escalation when bedside MBW fails |
| **Open / robotic cystotomy with clot evacuation** | Direct visualization | Last-line salvage; reserved for surgical bladder or persistent uncontrollable retention |

## Toomey vs Standard Syringes vs Bulb Syringe

| Device | Capacity | Catheter interface | Best fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Toomey syringe** | **60 mL** (50 / 70 variants) | **Tapered open tip → catheter funnel** | Bladder irrigation, MBW, intravesical instillation |
| **Luer-tip 60 mL syringe** | 60 mL | Luer-lock → adapter required | General irrigation; not catheter-funnel compatible |
| **Asepto bulb syringe** | ~ 60–120 mL | Open tapered tip | Wound irrigation, cavity rinsing; less controlled vacuum on aspiration |
| **Ellik evacuator** | Continuous-cycle bulb | Sheath-mounted | Chip / clot evacuation **through resectoscope sheath** during transurethral cases |

## Catheter Pairing for Maximum Flow

Effective MBW depends as much on the catheter as the syringe. Bench studies show that catheter design changes washout efficiency. In a gelatin-clot bladder model with 22 Fr catheters, a 6-hole catheter cleared more simulated clot than a Malecot design (77.0% residual clot with the Malecot versus 60.4% with the 6-hole catheter after five cycles).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> In a mechanical model, catheters with a larger total hole area, and an open end with a side hole near the tip, needed less force to clear pseudo-clot.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> A flow-meter study of 16–26 Fr three-way catheters found that Bardex 22 and 24 Fr outperformed Dover through the irrigation port and during continuous irrigation, while manual irrigation through the drainage port was similar.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> These are laboratory findings. See the [Three-Way Catheter](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/urinary-catheters/three-way-catheter) page for the catheter side of the pairing.

CATCH-22 recommends at least a 22 Fr catheter for clot retention; the referring teams' 16–20 Fr catheters nearly all needed upsizing.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

- **Operator-power-limited.** The hand-driven pressure ceiling is far below wall-suction or true cystoscopic evacuation.
- **Trabeculated, small-capacity or NLUTD bladder.** Over-vigorous instillation risks bladder perforation; consider smaller instilled volumes and frequent aspiration in this group.
- **Recent bladder anastomosis or fresh cystotomy closure.** Relatively contraindicated for forceful MBW until surgeon-set day-X; gentle low-volume irrigation only.
- **Friction-seal slip.** The tapered tip occasionally backs out under pressure; brace the catheter funnel with the non-dominant hand.

## Historical Context — David Toomey

The catheter-tip syringe carries the name of its designer, Toomey, and remains the standard hand syringe for catheter irrigation.
