# Yankauer Suction

The **Yankauer suction instrument** is a widely used, relatively broad-bore operative suction pattern for clearing fluid and debris from an open field. Bulb-tip, open-tip, rigid, flexible, vented and non-vented models exist; the term does not specify one tip geometry or control scheme.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Use a finer [Frazier](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/suction/frazier) tip when the surgical corridor or tissue demands more precise aspiration.

## Design

- **Tip and shaft vary.** CONMED currently lists sterile single-use rigid open-tip and bulb-tip models, with or without a control vent; other catalogs include flexible and reusable metal patterns.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Bulb-tip side eyes** are intended to reduce tissue grab, not eliminate it. Open-tip versions reach tighter spaces but offer less shielding of the central opening.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Tubing, vent and on/off switch** depend on model. Confirm fit and the manufacturer's instructions before connecting to the suction source.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Reconstructive / Functional Urology Uses

The Yankauer is a common open-field suction option across the WARWIKI operative landscape:

- **Open urethroplasty and perineal reconstruction.** Bulbar exposure with perineal Bookwalter, posterior anastomotic urethroplasty, perineal urethrostomy, RUF repair
- **Open and laparoscopic abdominal reconstruction.** Cystectomy and diversion, augmentation cystoplasty, Mitrofanoff, fistula repair, Boari flap, ureteroureterostomy
- **Vaginal reconstructive cases.** VVF repair, anterior / posterior colporrhaphy, sacrocolpopexy, SSLF
- **GAS / cosmetic genital surgery.** Vaginoplasty, phalloplasty, scrotoplasty
- **Donor-site management.** STSG / FTSG / BMG donor sites

For finer suction near the operative target (urethra, ureter, NVB, microsurgical anastomosis), switch to a [Frazier](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/suction/frazier) tip; for high-volume abdominal evacuation, switch to a [Poole](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/suction/poole) multi-hole rigid sucker.

## Performance — What the Data Show

These are airway-suction bench studies, not clinical trials of pelvic surgery or bladder clot evacuation. Finke's technical simulation compared a specific 18 Ch Yankauer, 14 Ch soft catheter and 28 Ch DuCanto at one vacuum setting using water and xanthan-thickened fluid. Flow ranked DuCanto, then Yankauer, then the 14 Ch soft catheter across the four tested fluid consistencies. Device diameter and test conditions limit transfer to other models and operative sites.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Suction device | Performance characteristic | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Yankauer** | Intermediate flow in Finke's three-device, four-fluid technical simulation | Finke 2022<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Commercial large-bore catheter** | Faster than the tested Yankauer in Finke's simulation and Nikolla's separate laboratory comparison | Finke 2022; Nikolla 2021<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **ETT + meconium aspirator** | Investigated as a makeshift airway-suction comparator, not a validated urologic instrument | Kei 2017<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |

Viscous or particulate material can reduce flow or obstruct a tip; larger-bore designs may behave differently. These airway-fluid simulations do not justify direct intravesical use of a Yankauer. For catheter washout and endoscopic bladder-content evacuation, see the separate [Toomey syringe](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/suction/toomey-syringe) and [Ellik evacuator](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/suction/ellik-evacuator) workflows.

## Limitations

- **Clogs on thick or particulate material.** Organized clot, dense pus, tissue debris
- **Rigid design.** Single-axis suction is good for the broad operative field but not for narrow deep-pelvic corridors (use Frazier)
- **Control depends on model.** Vented, non-vented and switch-controlled versions differ; no tip should be held against delicate tissue

## Yankauer vs Adjacent Suction Devices

| Device | Bore / tip | Best fit | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Yankauer** | Bulb or open tip; control varies | General open-field operative suction | Can clog on viscous/particulate matter or grab tissue |
| **[Poole (Pool) suction](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/suction/poole)** | Wide-bore multi-hole rigid | High-volume abdominal evacuation | Bulky |
| **[Frazier suction](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/suction/frazier)** | Fine tapered with thumb-vent | Delicate reconstructive fields, NVB, microsurgical | Low volumetric capacity |
| **[Toomey syringe](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/suction/toomey-syringe)** | Manual irrigation-aspiration via catheter | Bladder MBW, catheter-coupled irrigation | Operator-power ceiling |
| **[Ellik evacuator](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/suction/ellik-evacuator)** | Manual bulb via compatible endoscopic sheath | Operative bladder tissue / clot evacuation | Material must pass through sheath; pressure is not universal |

## Bottom Line

The Yankauer is a practical general open-field suction choice. Select a tip, vent, tubing and regulated suction source for the actual field; use a fine tip for delicate dissection and an appropriate large-volume device for fluid pools. Evidence from airway bench tests is not a device-superiority claim for reconstructive surgery.

See also: [Poole Suction](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/suction/poole), [Frazier Suction](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/suction/frazier), [Ellik Evacuator](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/suction/ellik-evacuator), [Toomey Syringe](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/suction/toomey-syringe).
