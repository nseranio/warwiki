# Yankauer Suction

The **Yankauer suction catheter** is a **rigid, wide-bore oropharyngeal / open-field suction device** — the default suction instrument across surgery, emergency, and critical-care environments. Named for **Sidney Yankauer (1872–1932)**, the American otolaryngologist who designed it for oral and pharyngeal surgery, the design has persisted for over a century because the rigid, bulb-tipped, wide-bore mechanic is hard to improve on for the bulk of clinical suctioning needs.

## Design

- **Rigid plastic** (or historical metal) tube
- **Bulb-shaped distal tip** with a **large central opening** and one or more smaller side holes — the bulb shape minimizes mucosal trauma while preserving suction
- **Wide bore** — high volumetric flow rate
- **Standard wall-suction tubing connection** (or portable unit)
- Most disposable variants include a **thumb-controlled vent port** for intermittent suction regulation
- **Single-use disposable** is standard to reduce cross-contamination risk

## Reconstructive / Functional Urology Uses

The Yankauer is the **default open-field suction** across the WARWIKI operative landscape:

- **Open urethroplasty and perineal reconstruction** — bulbar exposure with perineal Bookwalter, posterior anastomotic urethroplasty, perineal urethrostomy, RUF repair
- **Open and laparoscopic abdominal reconstruction** — cystectomy + diversion, augmentation cystoplasty, Mitrofanoff, fistula repair, Boari flap, ureteroureterostomy
- **Vaginal reconstructive cases** — VVF repair, anterior / posterior colporrhaphy, sacrocolpopexy, SSLF
- **GAS / cosmetic genital surgery** — vaginoplasty, phalloplasty, scrotoplasty
- **Donor-site management** — STSG / FTSG / BMG donor sites
- **Airway management during induction / extubation** — the original use case, still standard

For finer suction near the operative target (urethra, ureter, NVB, microsurgical anastomosis), switch to a [Frazier](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/suction/frazier) tip; for high-volume abdominal evacuation, switch to a [Poole](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/suction/poole) multi-hole rigid sucker.

## Performance — What the Data Show

Simulation studies consistently show the Yankauer **outperforms standard soft suction catheters** (e.g., 14 Fr) across fluids of varying viscosity, with **~ 50–100% higher suction rates** for water and thin fluids.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

| Suction device | Performance characteristic | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Yankauer** | Default; 50–100% faster than 14 Fr soft suction for water / thin fluids | Finke 2022; Nikolla 2021<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **DuCanto large-bore catheter** | **~ 20–30% faster than Yankauer across all viscosities** | Finke 2022; Nikolla 2021<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **ETT + meconium aspirator (ETTMA)** | Suctioned **90 mL** of blended food (emesis surrogate) before clogging; **Yankauer clogged immediately** in the same study | Kei 2017<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |

The **viscosity-clogging failure mode** is the most important practical limitation: the Yankauer **clogs on thick particulate material** (emesis with food particles, semi-organized clot, dense secretions). For high-volume rapid bladder-clot evacuation through a catheter, see the [Toomey syringe](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/suction/toomey-syringe) and [Ellik evacuator](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/suction/ellik-evacuator) workflows.

## Practical Considerations

- **Wall-suction pressure**: typically **80–150 mmHg** for oropharyngeal suctioning; field suction in open reconstructive cases is similar.
- **Not for tracheal / endotracheal use** — the rigid design risks mucosal trauma in the trachea; flexible suction catheters are used for ET suctioning.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Disposable single-use** is the modern standard for infection control.
- **Ventilated-patient oral care** — oropharyngeal Yankauer suctioning reduces microaspiration and the associated ventilator-associated-pneumonia risk in the ICU population.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Bougie-conduit adjunct** — case reports describe using the Yankauer as a conduit for a bougie to facilitate intubation in patients with distorted oropharyngeal anatomy.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Limitations

- **Clogs on thick / particulate material** — emesis, organized clot, dense pus.
- **Rigid design** precludes tracheal or transurethral use.
- **Single-axis suction** — the curved tip is good for the oropharynx and broad operative field but not for narrow deep-pelvic corridors (use Frazier).
- **No vacuum control without the thumb-vent variant** — constant suction can avulse delicate tissue if held against it.

## Yankauer vs Adjacent Suction Devices

| Device | Bore / tip | Best fit | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Yankauer** | Wide-bore bulb tip | Default open-field, oropharyngeal | Clogs on thick particulate; rigid |
| **[Poole (Pool) suction](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/suction/poole)** | Wide-bore multi-hole rigid | High-volume abdominal evacuation | Bulky |
| **[Frazier suction](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/suction/frazier)** | Fine tapered with thumb-vent | Delicate reconstructive fields, NVB, microsurgical | Low volumetric capacity |
| **DuCanto large-bore catheter** | Even wider bore than Yankauer | Airway emergencies with vomitus / particulate | Less commonly stocked |
| **[Toomey syringe](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/suction/toomey-syringe)** | Manual irrigation-aspiration via catheter | Bladder MBW, catheter-coupled irrigation | Operator-power ceiling |
| **[Ellik evacuator](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/suction/ellik-evacuator)** | Manual bulb + gravity trap via cystoscope sheath | Bladder chip / clot evacuation during TUR | ~ 80–100 mmHg ceiling |

## Bottom Line

The Yankauer remains the **default oropharyngeal / open-field suction device** across reconstructive urology and urogynecology — simple, available, adequately performing for the vast majority of suctioning needs. **Awareness of its viscosity-clogging failure mode** matters for the operator: in the airway emergency or the high-volume blood / clot field, switch to a larger-bore alternative (DuCanto or ETT + meconium aspirator) or escalate to a catheter-coupled approach.
