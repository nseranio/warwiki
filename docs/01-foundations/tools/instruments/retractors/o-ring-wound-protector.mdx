# O-Ring / Plastic Sheath Wound Retractor-Protectors

**Disposable wound protector-retractors** combine circumferential wound-edge retraction with a plastic sheath between the incision and operative field. The Alexis O Wound Protector-Retractor (Applied Medical) is one dual-ring example; Mobius and O Trac have also appeared in clinical studies. These devices can protect and retract an incision but do not replace directed deep-organ retraction when that is required.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[12]](#ref12)[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

## Design

**Single- and dual-ring configurations have been studied; the effect estimates differ by device class and operation.**<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

### Dual-ring (O-ring)

- **Inner ring** is inserted through the incision and seated against the internal surface.
- **Outer ring** remains external.
- **Plastic sheath** connects the rings and lines the wound circumference.
- Retraction is generated by flipping or rolling the outer ring until the desired radial exposure is achieved. The current setup guide requires checks for bowel or tissue entrapment before and after tensioning.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- Ring flexibility, sheath material, conductivity and available sizes are product-specific. Applied Medical currently catalogs Alexis O sizes by incision range rather than a universal XXS-to-XL scheme.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

### Single-ring

- A single ring sits intra-abdominally and connects to a protective drape. Older meta-analytic subgroup estimates favored dual-ring devices, but this was not a direct head-to-head comparison and the dual-ring evidence was lower quality.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Mechanism of SSI Reduction

The plastic sheath is intended to separate the incision from the operative field. Proposed mechanisms include:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

- **Physical barrier.** Blocks endogenous (enteric or peritoneal contamination) and exogenous pathogens from embedding in skin, fat, fascia, and peritoneum. A bacteriologic study of 272 patients found no positive cultures from the incision margin when a wound protector was used, even when cultures from the abdominal-cavity side of the device were positive.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- Distributed radial retraction rather than a few focal blade contacts.
- **Reduced direct contact between the incision and operative field** while the sheath is correctly seated.

Avoid claiming that the device is pressure-free, preserves perfusion, or eliminates nerve injury. Deep handheld or frame-mounted retractors may still be used, and positioning-related neuropathy remains possible.

## Insertion Technique

1. Make the incision and carry it through the abdominal wall layers.
2. **Compress (fold) the inner ring** and insert it through the incision into the abdominal cavity.
3. Allow the inner ring to open and seat against the internal surface; confirm that bowel or other tissue is not trapped.
4. **Flip or roll the outer ring** on the external surface until the desired retraction is achieved.
5. Adjust retraction by adding or removing rolls.
6. At case end, release the outer ring and remove the inner ring according to the product instructions.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

A 4-handed roll-down technique is described for transoral applications.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Reconstructive-Urology and Urogyn Uses

In reconstructive urology and pelvic surgery, a wound protector-retractor may be considered when a compatible abdominal or extraction incision would benefit from circumferential protection and exposure. No urology-specific comparative trial was verified for this page, so these are workflow examples rather than outcome-backed indications:

- **Open urinary diversion, radical cystectomy or continent cutaneous pouch.** Bowel anastomosis through a transversely-protected sheath, with the device replacing a [Balfour](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/balfour) for wound retraction at the time the bowel is open.
- **Augmentation cystoplasty and bowel-segment harvest.** Protects the incision during enterotomy and reservoir construction.
- **Open neobladder or Indiana pouch creation.** Particularly through Pfannenstiel; an [Alexis-versus-Collins-style](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/collins) decision in non-obese patients.
- **Selected prosthetic or reconstructive access incisions** when the device fits; the reviewed GI/colorectal evidence does not prove lower AUS or IPP infection rates.
- **Open sacrocolpopexy, urethral diverticulectomy, complex VVF, RVF or vaginoplasty.** When a transverse lower-abdominal access is used.
- **Specimen extraction in laparoscopic or robotic RU.** Vaginal or Pfannenstiel mini-laparotomy specimen extraction during robotic cystectomy, partial nephrectomy, or sacrocolpopexy revision.
- **Pediatric abdominal urology** when an appropriately sized device is labeled and available; the WHO guideline did not identify pediatric SSI studies in its wound-protector evidence set.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

## Evidence — SSI Reduction

### General abdominal / GI surgery

The Korean multicenter RCT randomized 458 patients undergoing open GI surgery; 457 were included in its intention-to-treat analysis. Thirty-day SSI occurred in 25/229 (10.9%) in the dual-ring group and 47/228 (20.6%) controls. This supports benefit in that population and does not extend to every operation or device.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Colorectal surgery

A 2026 meta-analysis of 6 RCTs (666 patients) reported lower SSI after open colorectal surgery (RR 0.44, 95% CI 0.22–0.88); its four-Alexis-study subgroup reported RR 0.21 (95% CI 0.09–0.48). Only the indexed abstract was accessible in this review, so risk-of-bias and subgroup methods remain unresolved.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> The ASCRS 2024 guideline states that wound protectors can decrease SSI after colorectal surgery, a strong recommendation based on high-quality evidence.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

The inherited Cheng colorectal trial remains relevant to this evidence base, but its complete methods and results were not reread in this review.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

### Cesarean section — mixed, BMI-dependent

- One 2016 cesarean comparison reported SSI 1% versus 8% favoring the plastic sheath over a metal retractor (p = 0.035); complete full-text methods were not reviewed here.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Obese (median BMI 40):** no benefit, 20.6% versus 17.6% (p = 0.62).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Meta-analysis (Waring 2018, 6 RCTs, 1,669 women):** no overall SSI reduction at cesarean (RR 0.76, 95% CI 0.34–1.70). A planned BMI &lt;35 subgroup favored O-ring use (RR 0.34, 95% CI 0.12–0.98), but the authors concluded that evidence did not justify routine cesarean use.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

### Dual-ring vs single-ring

A meta-analysis of 16 RCTs (3,695 patients) reported a larger subgroup effect estimate for dual-ring devices (RR 0.29, 95% CI 0.15–0.55) than for single-ring devices (RR 0.71, 95% CI 0.54–0.92), but the authors cautioned that lower-quality dual-ring data could explain part of the difference. This was indirect subgroup evidence and did not derive from a randomized dual-versus-single comparison.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> The WSES 2020 position paper states that dual-ring protectors appear superior (Grade 1B).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guideline Positioning

| Society | Recommendation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **WHO 2018 second edition** | Consider wound protectors in clean-contaminated, contaminated and dirty abdominal surgery | Conditional; very-low-quality evidence<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> |
| **WSES 2020** | Dual-ring devices appear superior to single-ring for SSI prevention | Grade 1B<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **ASCRS 2024** | Wound protectors decrease SSI after colorectal surgery | Strong (high quality)<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |

The 2016 WHO recommendations publication remains useful historical context, but the official 2018 second-edition guideline is the current source cited above.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

## Reported Adaptations

Published case series and feasibility reports describe uses beyond routine abdominal incision protection. These do not establish labeled indications or comparative benefit:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[6]](#ref6)[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

- **Transoral robotic surgery (TORS).** Intraoral self-retaining retraction for tonsillectomy, pharyngoplasty, and head-and-neck tumor resection; protects lips and mucosa; electrically non-conducting.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Transoral robotic thyroidectomy.** Vestibular-incision sealed tunnel that accommodates CO₂ insufflation around the SP cannula.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Ventriculoperitoneal shunt surgery.** Abdominal-zone visualization for single-surgeon free-hand placement, particularly in obese patients.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Giant ovarian-cyst aspiration and resection.** Alexis Laparoscopic System enables minimally invasive cyst control with oncologic safety, then converts to laparoscopy via the insufflation cap.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Advantages vs Traditional Metal Retractors

- **Circumferential retraction** without fixed lateral metal blades; this does not make the entire operation pressure- or neuropathy-free.
- **Physical barrier** against endogenous and exogenous wound contamination.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Distributed wound-edge contact** is a manufacturer-described design feature; perfusion superiority was not verified.
- Material and electrosurgery compatibility is product-specific and should be checked in the current IFU; a transoral case report is not a universal electrical-safety label.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Self-retaining once rolled.** Can reduce the need for continuous handheld wound-edge retraction.
- **Transparent.** Wound-edge visualization.

## Disadvantages

- **Disposable, single-use.** Higher per-case cost than reusable [Balfour](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/balfour) or [Collins](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/collins).
- **No central or cephalad blade.** Does not provide directed retraction of the bladder flap or any specific structure the way the Balfour family does; usually combined with handheld [Deaver](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/deaver) or [malleable](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/malleable) retractors for deep pelvic exposure.
- **Size-sensitive.** Under-sized devices retract inadequately; over-sized devices fail to anchor.
- **Cesarean evidence varies by population:** one obese-patient RCT was negative, while the 2018 meta-analysis found no overall benefit and a favorable BMI &lt;35 subgroup. This should not be extrapolated to all obese abdominal-surgery patients.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Contamination at insertion or removal.** Some bacterial transfer occurs at the moments the device is not yet (or no longer) sealed.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Cost-Effectiveness

Cost-effectiveness is context-dependent and should not be inferred from device price alone. Two analyses reached different conclusions because they evaluated different populations, devices, assumptions, health systems and time horizons.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

### Favorable evidence — colorectal / GI surgery

A decision-analytic model (Chomsky-Higgins & Kahn 2019) found double-ring devices clinically superior and cost-saving under its colorectal-surgery inputs:<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

- 230 QALYs gained per 1,000 patients and $2.2 million saved per 1,000, driven largely by modeled SSI cost.
- Dual-ring devices held a distinct advantage over single-ring alternatives.

### Unfavorable evidence — ROSSINI trial (mixed laparotomy, single-ring)

The ROSSINI trial economic evaluation (Gheorghe 2014, n = 760) in UK general laparotomy found wound protectors not cost-effective:<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

- Mean cost £5,420 (protector) versus £5,130 (standard).
- QALYs essentially identical (0.02131 versus 0.02133).
- More costly and equally effective; robust to sensitivity analysis.

ROSSINI used a single-ring device in mixed laparotomy. Its result should not automatically be generalized to a modern dual-ring colorectal program, but a favorable decision model likewise does not prove cost savings in every local setting.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

### Reconciling the two

| Factor | Favors cost-effectiveness | Against |
|---|---|---|
| **Device evidence** | Dual-ring subgroup estimate RR 0.29 | Single-ring subgroup estimate RR 0.71; these were indirect, not head-to-head estimates<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **Setting** | Colorectal or contaminated GI surgery with a high baseline SSI risk | Clean or mixed lower-risk laparotomy |
| **Patient/procedure evidence** | High baseline SSI and populations represented in positive trials | Populations with negative or uncertain trial evidence, including routine cesarean overall<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Economic model** | Inputs with high SSI-treatment cost and a large modeled treatment effect | Different treatment-effect assumptions, local costs or time horizon |

### Exposure-per-incision-length advantage

An animal-tissue exposure study measured more surgical surface area per incision length with an Alexis than with traditional metal self-retainers; for a 5-cm incision, the values were 12.25 cm² (Alexis small) versus 6.17 cm² (Mollison), p = 0.038. This bench model supports an exposure claim only; it did not test patient complications, recovery or cost.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

### Guideline cost framing

- **WHO 2018 second edition.** Notes that wound-protector use may not take priority over other interventions in resource-limited settings because of cost and availability.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
- **ASCRS 2024.** Makes a clinical recommendation for colorectal surgery but does not establish local cost-effectiveness.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **SHEA/IDSA/APIC 2022 update.** Includes impervious plastic wound protectors for gastrointestinal and biliary surgery among essential practices. Its evidence synthesis reports an approximately 30% reduction across 14 randomized trials; the dual-ring subgroup estimate was larger than the single-ring estimate, but this was not a direct device comparison.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

### RU / urogyn implications

No reviewed economic study modeled urinary diversion, augmentation, AUS, IPP, sacrocolpopexy or urethral diverticulectomy. Local use should therefore be based on labeling, incision fit, anticipated contamination, institutional SSI data and actual purchasing or reprocessing costs rather than an extrapolated claim of dominance.
