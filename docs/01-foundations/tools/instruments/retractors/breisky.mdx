# Breisky-Navratil Retractor

The Breisky-Navratil, often called a **Breisky**, is a long handheld vaginal retractor used for deep vaginal and paravaginal exposure. Its elongated working blade reaches beyond the introitus while the angled handle remains accessible to the assistant. A concave blade supports the vaginal wall rather than gripping it with teeth.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Design and available sizes

The name covers several lengths and widths. Teleflex lists P15933 with a 130 × 35 mm concave blade and 32 cm overall length. The Aesculap catalog includes 130 mm blades in widths from 20 to 40 mm and additional longer and shorter patterns.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Choose blade reach and width separately: the exposure may be deep but narrow, or shallow with a broad wall requiring support. “Longer” does not mean “safer” or automatically provide a better view; a tip extending beyond the visible plane can put pressure on structures outside the operative target.

## Uses in vaginal reconstruction

The Breisky is the usual handheld choice when exposure is deeper than a [Heaney](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/heaney-retractor) reaches. For superficial exposure (cervix, lower vaginal wall) a Heaney or [Sims](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/sims) is preferable.

**Apical suspension**

- **Sacrospinous fixation:** lateral and pararectal exposure while identifying the ischial spine and sacrospinous ligament. The corridor is deep and narrow, which suits a long narrow blade. The retractor creates the corridor; it does not determine a safe suture position.
- **Uterosacral suspension and McCall culdoplasty:** directed wall retraction for ligament identification and suture placement, and deep posterior cul-de-sac exposure.

**Vaginal hysterectomy and cuff**

- **Vaginal hysterectomy:** lateral and posterior wall retraction during deep pedicle dissection, particularly after the cervix has been mobilized.
- **Cuff closure:** deep exposure while closing the apex and incorporating the uterosacral ligaments.

**Vaginal reconstruction**

- **Anterior and posterior repair:** adjustable lateral wall retraction when a fixed speculum obscures the operative plane.
- **High vaginal fistula repair:** supplementary exposure when a fistula or mobilized flap lies beyond a shorter blade.
- **Female urethroplasty:** deep vaginal exposure when dissection extends proximally.
- **Mesh revision or excision:** a defined dissection corridor when retrieving high mesh limbs.

These are conventional applications. Selection depends on the patient's anatomy and the exposure the procedure needs.

## Practical setup and handling

1. Have more than one blade width and length available for deep cases. Narrower blades (about 2.5 cm) suit very deep, narrow corridors; wider blades (about 4 cm) give broader lateral exposure.
2. Establish the dissection plane before placing the tip deeply. Position the blade on the tissue being retracted, with the target visible beyond it.
3. Blades are commonly used in pairs, one on each side, held by assistants or one by the surgeon. A posterior [weighted speculum](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/weighted-speculum) can supply gravity-supported posterior retraction while the Breisky retracts laterally. Two Breiskys are not mandatory in every case.
4. When a Magrina-Bookwalter or other frame is in place, a Breisky can supplement it for deep apical exposure the frame blades cannot reach (see [Bookwalter](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/bookwalter)).
5. Keep the handle out of the needle or dissection path. Coordinate changes in direction with the surgeon rather than silently rotating or advancing the blade.
6. Water-soluble lubricant on the blade reduces mucosal friction in sustained retraction.
7. Use minimal effective pressure and periodically reassess tissue under the blade. Change position or size if exposure requires escalating force.
8. Release traction before removing or changing blades; protect suture loops and the adjacent wall during withdrawal.

## Limitations

- **Handheld:** it needs an assistant and fatigues over long cases. For prolonged retraction supplement or replace it with a self-retaining system (Magrina-Bookwalter, Lone Star).
- **Narrow blades concentrate pressure:** sustained force on the lateral vaginal wall can cause focal mucosal ischemia, so release periodically in long cases.
- **Vaginal approach only:** it is not an abdominal or open pelvic retractor.

## Comparison with other vaginal retractors

| Instrument | Useful distinction |
|---|---|
| Breisky | Long handheld blade for directed deep exposure; among the deepest vaginal patterns |
| [Heaney](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/heaney-retractor) | Angled vaginal blade; dimensions vary, including deeper Heaney-Simon patterns |
| [Sims](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/sims) | Curved wall retraction with an open working channel |
| [Auvard](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/weighted-speculum) | Gravity-supported posterior retraction |
| [Bookwalter](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/bookwalter) / [Lone Star](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/lone-star) | Sustained exposure with a frame or elastic stays |

Actual dimensions matter more than a universal “deepest retractor” ranking. Manufacturer photographs and catalogs are useful for identifying the pattern; procedure-specific anatomy determines placement.

## Historical note

The name honors the Vienna gynecologist August Breisky (1832–1889). In operative use the instrument is simply called a Breisky.
