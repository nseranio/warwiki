# Breisky-Navratil Retractor

The Breisky-Navratil, often called a **Breisky**, is a long handheld vaginal retractor used for deep vaginal and paravaginal exposure. Its elongated working blade reaches beyond the introitus while the angled handle remains accessible to the assistant. A concave blade supports the vaginal wall rather than gripping it with teeth.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Design and available sizes

The name covers several lengths and widths. Teleflex lists **P15933** with a **130 × 35 mm concave blade** and **32 cm overall length**. The Aesculap catalog includes 130 mm blades in widths from 20 to 40 mm and additional longer and shorter patterns.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Choose blade reach and width separately: the exposure may be deep but narrow, or shallow with a broad wall requiring support. “Longer” does not mean “safer” or automatically provide a better view; a tip extending beyond the visible plane can put pressure on structures outside the operative target.

## Uses in vaginal reconstruction

- **Sacrospinous fixation:** lateral vaginal and pararectal exposure during identification of the ischial spine and sacrospinous region. The retractor creates the corridor; it does not determine a safe suture position.
- **Uterosacral suspension and vaginal hysterectomy:** directed wall retraction during deep pedicle and apical work.
- **High vaginal fistula repair:** supplementary exposure when the fistula or mobilized flap lies beyond the reach of a shorter blade.
- **Mesh revision or excision:** maintaining a defined vaginal dissection corridor while protecting adjacent tissue.
- **Anterior or posterior repair:** adjustable lateral wall retraction when a fixed speculum obscures the operative plane.

These are conventional surgical applications; selection still depends on the patient's anatomy and the procedure-specific exposure.

## Practical setup and handling

1. Have more than one blade width and length available for deep cases.
2. Establish the dissection plane before placing the tip deeply. Position the blade on the tissue being retracted, with the intended target visible beyond it.
3. An assistant may use one blade or opposing blades; a posterior [weighted speculum](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/weighted-speculum) can provide complementary exposure where appropriate. Two Breiskys are not mandatory in every case.
4. Keep the handle out of the needle or dissection path. Coordinate changes in direction with the surgeon rather than silently rotating or advancing the blade.
5. Use minimal effective pressure and periodically reassess tissue under the blade. Change position or size if exposure requires escalating force.
6. Release traction before removing or changing blades; protect suture loops and the adjacent wall during withdrawal.

## Comparison with other vaginal retractors

| Instrument | Useful distinction |
|---|---|
| Breisky | Long handheld blade for directed deep exposure |
| [Heaney](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/heaney-retractor) | Angled vaginal blade; dimensions vary, including deeper Heaney-Simon patterns |
| [Sims](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/sims) | Curved wall retraction with an open working channel |
| [Auvard](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/weighted-speculum) | Gravity-supported posterior retraction |
| [Bookwalter](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/bookwalter) / [Lone Star](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/lone-star) | Sustained exposure with a frame or elastic stays |

Actual dimensions matter more than a universal “deepest retractor” ranking. Manufacturer photographs and catalogs are useful for identifying the pattern; procedure-specific anatomy determines placement.
