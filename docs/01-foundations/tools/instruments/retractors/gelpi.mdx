# Gelpi Retractor

The **Gelpi** is a self-retaining retractor with a single curved, sharp prong on each arm and a locking handle. It provides two opposing retraction directions in a relatively compact instrument without a table-mounted frame. The single-point tips distinguish it from the multiprong [Weitlaner](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/weitlaner).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Design and sizes

- **Single sharp tips** engage the selected tissues at two points.
- **Handle lock** maintains the selected spread after placement.
- **Different lengths and blade patterns** suit different access depths; a long or deep Gelpi variant is not interchangeable with the smallest pattern.
- **Ball-stop versions** are available alongside standard patterns.

Teleflex lists examples from 9 cm to 18.5 cm, including the 18 cm ball-stop model KM52769 and the standard Pilling 165440. Other Gelpi-family instruments, such as the deeper Wiltse-Gelpi, have different geometry. Use the exact catalog number when requesting the instrument.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Practical role in reconstructive surgery

A Gelpi can provide local two-sided exposure in perineal, groin, scrotal or muscle-harvest incisions when its size and tip geometry fit the wound. It may be used as an adjunct while a larger retractor maintains the main operative field. These are practical applications of the instrument, not manufacturer claims of superiority for a particular reconstruction.

For a long incision, more than one local retractor may be needed. For several directions of soft-tissue traction, a [Lone Star](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/lone-star) offers independently positioned elastic stays. Select by the tissue contact and access required rather than assuming one design replaces the others.

## Placement and handling

1. **Check the tips and lock** before use; identify any ball stops and confirm the available opening.
2. **Place both tips under direct vision** in tissue capable of holding the intended traction. Keep the points clear of vessels, nerves and other structures at risk.
3. **Increase spread gradually** until the exposure is adequate.
4. **Watch for tearing, slipping or excessive tension.** If purchase is poor, reposition or choose a different retractor rather than opening further.
5. **Reassess during the operation**, particularly when the target or tissue layer changes. Release the lock under control before repositioning or withdrawal.

These are general surgical handling principles. There is no universal tissue category, opening width or timed release interval that makes a sharp-point retractor safe in every incision.

## Gelpi and Weitlaner: useful distinctions

| Feature | Gelpi | Weitlaner |
|---|---|---|
| Tissue contact | One point per arm | Several prongs per blade |
| Exposure pattern | Two opposing points | Two opposing multiprong blades |
| Variants | Different lengths, depths and stop configurations | Different lengths and sharp/blunt prong configurations |
| Selection question | Can each tip obtain controlled purchase without injuring adjacent structures? | Does the blade fit the field, with appropriate contact at all prongs? |

A multiprong or blunt instrument can still cause pressure injury; the shape alone does not establish a lower complication rate.

## Prolonged retraction

Sharp tips can puncture or tear tissue, and prolonged traction can impair perfusion. Periodic assessment and release when retraction is unnecessary remain sensible operative principles. The lumbar-laminectomy study by Datta and colleagues concerns paraspinal muscle retraction; its findings should not be converted into a validated 60-minute limit for perineal or gracilis-harvest surgery.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Care

Confirm the reusable instrument's supplied cleaning and sterilization instructions. Protect the sharp points during transport, inspect them for damage and clean the locking joint before reuse. The [Teleflex IFU portal](https://ifu.teleflex.com/en/global?productCode=165440) can be searched using the instrument's product code.
