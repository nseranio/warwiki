# Nasal Speculum

The **nasal speculum** — including pediatric and other ENT variants — has been repurposed in reconstructive urology and urogynecology for **deep, narrow exposure**. Published urologic evidence is clearest for selected transurethral mesh-removal cases; use during deep posterior or redo urethroplasty is specialist operative teaching that remains incompletely documented in the sources reviewed here.

---

## Design

The nasal speculum has two short, blunt, parallel blades joined at a hinge, opened either by squeezing a handle or via a thumb-screw lock. Common variants relevant to urology:

| Variant | Blade length | Typical urology niche |
|---|---|---|
| **Cottle / pediatric** | Shorter, commonly spring-loaded | Published two-case transurethral mesh-removal use |
| **Vienna** | Longer; configurations vary | Narrow-corridor exposure described in operative teaching |
| **Killian** | Long-bladed ENT pattern | Deep perineal exposure described in operative teaching |

Exact blade length, opening mechanism and naming vary by manufacturer; inspect the actual instrument rather than selecting from the approximate historical labels alone.

---

## Transurethral Application — Midurethral-Sling Mesh Erosion

Urethral mesh erosion is an uncommon but technically difficult complication of synthetic midurethral slings (TVT, TOT, etc.). Incidence estimates depend on whether a study counts vaginal exposure, bladder/urethral erosion, diagnosis, removal or reoperation; those outcomes should not be collapsed into one percentage. Endoscopic / transurethral approaches are minimally invasive alternatives to transvaginal or open removal in selected patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### Published descriptions

- **Plowright 2013** — Pediatric nasal speculum inserted at the urethral meatus to provide working exposure for transurethral resection of eroded mesh. The report describes two cases with complete resection, symptom resolution and no recurrence during the authors' reported follow-up; it does not establish comparative effectiveness.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Solomon & Jelovsek 2015** — Misplaced retropubic midurethral sling within the urethral mucosa in a 63-year-old with gross hematuria; intraurethral mesh dissected out using a full ENT tray — **nasal speculum, ethmoid scissors, Blakesley graspers, and a headlamp**. The technique allowed mesh resection without incising the urethral sphincter.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### Why the nasal speculum fits

- **Narrow working profile** — permits direct access through the urethral meatus in the reported cases
- **Potentially stable exposure** when the selected model can be held or locked open
- **Compatible with adjunct instruments** — fine scissors and graspers fit alongside the blades
- **May avoid urethrotomy** in a selected accessible erosion, as described in the single Solomon/Jelovsek case

### Place in the mesh-erosion algorithm

Sobota's 2019 systematic review found heterogeneous, nonrandomized evidence and a trend toward better symptom resolution and fewer reinterventions with transvaginal than cystoscopic removal for urethral mesh erosion.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Karim's 2020 open systematic review found high reported final success for laser and other endoscopic methods, but all included studies were retrospective case series, 20–25% of patients could require more than one endoscopic procedure, and there was no open comparator group.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Technique and approach therefore require specialist assessment of location, tissue injury, fistula/infection and continence consequences; a nasal speculum is an exposure adjunct, not an algorithm by itself.

## Posterior / Redo Urethroplasty Application

In **posterior anastomotic urethroplasty** for pelvic-fracture urethral injury (PFUI) and in **redo / salvage urethroplasty** for proximal bulbar / membranous strictures, exposure of the proximal urethral stump deep in the perineum is the rate-limiting step. Once corporal splitting and inferior pubectomy are complete, the deepest part of the working corridor is **narrow and conical** — too tight for a Breisky or Heaney blade, and beyond the reach of Lone Star stays. The [Jordan-Bookwalter / Perineal Bookwalter](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/perineal-bookwalter) ring solves the superficial and mid-corridor exposure but does not reach the deep apex.

Some specialist operative teaching describes a **long-bladed nasal speculum** to retract tissue laterally at the deep apex without occupying the surgeon's line of sight. No primary comparative or formal technique source for this application was verified in this pass; the following are contextual uses, not a standard-of-care list:

- **Visualization of the proximal urethral stump** above the prostatic apex during transperineal anastomotic urethroplasty
- **Exposure of the membranous urethra and dorsal vein complex** in redo or radiation-injured fields where scar contracts the deep corridor
- **Dissection alongside the elevated bulbar urethra** during supracrural-rerouting and inferior-pubectomy steps when the field is funnel-shaped
- **Salvage prostatectomy and complex bladder-neck dissection** in limited-access posterior fields where line-of-sight is paramount

The concept resembles the historical [gorget](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/sounds-bougies/gorget) as a narrow-corridor exposure aid and may complement a perineal Bookwalter ring. The inherited claim about dissemination through a particular fellowship community was not source-verified and should not be treated as provenance.

## Pearls

- **Match the actual blade profile and opening range to the corridor**; manufacturer names alone do not guarantee dimensions
- **Use only the retention mechanism supplied for that model** and minimize opening force/duration to reduce pressure injury
- **Use with a headlamp or operative loupes** — the deep corridor swallows ambient OR light
- **Adapt the instrument tray to the field** — fine ENT scissors, Blakesley graspers, and bipolar forceps fit alongside the speculum blades where standard urology instruments do not
