# Thompson Retractor

**Table-mounted self-retaining retractor system** with **articulating arms on a rigid table-rail frame** plus a **dedicated "obesity bar" and sidebars** that allow lateral retraction of thick abdominal walls. Manufactured by **Thompson Surgical Instruments** (Traverse City, Michigan). One of the three major modular table-fixed platforms alongside the [Bookwalter](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/bookwalter) and [Omni-Tract](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/omni-tract); distinguished by its **obesity / large-patient accommodation** and **independently releasable joints**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Design

- **Table-mounted frame** clamps to the operating-table side rails; provides the rigid external anchor.
- **Articulating arms with mechanical joints** extend from the frame; lock at any chosen angle / depth; blades clip to the arms.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **"Obesity bar" and sidebars** — specialized frame extensions for larger patients; sidebars angle laterally and toward the floor, retracting thick abdominal-wall flaps **posteriorly** for maximum exposure.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Interchangeable blade library** — bladder blades for cephalad retraction, Richardson-style for lateral, deep blades for visceral, etc.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Independently releasable joints** — each arm releases without disturbing the rest of the setup, allowing intermittent tissue relaxation that reduces retraction-related ischemic injury.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Material**: surgical-grade stainless steel, autoclavable.

## Two Distinguishing Features

The Thompson's two architectural distinctions vs the Bookwalter and Omni-Tract:

1. **Obesity bar + sidebars** — purpose-built large-patient accommodation that ring-based systems lack. The sidebars retract thick abdominal-wall flaps posteriorly, an exposure geometry the Bookwalter ring cannot duplicate.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
2. **Independent joint release** — per-arm relaxation without disturbing the whole setup; built around the principle that **intermittent tissue relaxation reduces retraction injury**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Reconstructive-Urology and Urogyn Uses

The Thompson is the **multi-vector self-retaining retractor of choice for large-patient and complex multi-phase RU/urogyn cases**:

- **Open BNR, augmentation cystoplasty, urinary diversion, AUS pump-pouch / reservoir, ureteral reimplantation** in obese / thick-abdominal-wall patients — the obesity bar handles abdominal-wall mass that overwhelms Bookwalter / Omni-Tract ring blades.
- **Open radical prostatectomy and cystectomy** — sustained multi-vector retraction with independent blade relaxation across the multi-phase operation.
- **Open sacrocolpopexy** — abdominal-wall + bladder + bowel retraction with the multi-arm system; particularly useful in obese patients.
- **Open transabdominal VVF / RVF / RUF repair** — peri-fistula multi-vector retraction during dissection and during omental / peritoneal-flap mobilization.
- **Adjunctive hysterectomy** during complex pelvic reconstruction.
- **Re-do pelvic operations** for post-radiation hostile re-entry, fistula repair, adhesion takedown.
- **Complex oncologic resections requiring prolonged stable exposure** — the **Modified Makuuchi incision + Thompson with obesity bar / sidebars** is the canonical hepatobiliary application (Chang 2010) and the same exposure principle applies to extensive retroperitoneal RU dissection.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Spine-RU intersection

- **Anterior cervical spine surgery** — the **Thompson-Farley** variant uses arms with mechanical joints fixed to a table-mounted frame for ACDF. Tangential to RU/urogyn, but mentioned because the architectural philosophy (independent-joint release for intermittent tissue relaxation) crosses over.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Anterior / lateral lumbar spine approaches** (ALIF, lateral spine) that intersect RU surgical fields use Thompson-class retractors for the same reasons that drive ALIF use of malleable retractors — vessel / bowel retraction in the anterior corridor.

## Thompson vs Bookwalter vs Omni-Tract vs Balfour

| Feature | **Thompson** | [**Bookwalter**](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/bookwalter) | [**Omni-Tract**](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/omni-tract) | [**Balfour**](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/balfour) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mounting** | Table-mounted frame + sidebars | Table post + oval ring | Table post + articulating arms | Wound-edge resting |
| **Blade options** | Extensive modular | Extensive modular | Extensive modular | 2 lateral + 1 central |
| **Obesity accommodation** | **Dedicated obesity bar** | Standard ring sizes | Standard arms | Limited |
| **Independent blade relaxation** | **Yes** (per arm) | Possible (per blade) | Possible | No |
| **Setup speed** | Moderate-slow | Moderate-slow | Moderate | Fast |
| **Best fit** | **Obese / complex / multi-phase** | Major abdominal / pelvic | Multi-vector across procedures | Routine abdominal |

The Thompson's primary advantage over the Bookwalter is the **obesity bar / sidebar architecture** and the **explicit per-arm release**. Its trade-off vs the Bookwalter is modestly slower setup and a smaller blade-library footprint at many institutions.

## Setup and Technique

1. **Frame attachment** to the table side rails at appropriate height.
2. **Obesity bar / sidebar placement** if the patient's BMI demands posterior-flap retraction; angle sidebars laterally and toward the floor.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
3. **Articulating-arm positioning** over the wound at heights that do not impede the surgeon's hand position.
4. **Blade sequence**: anchor blades (anterior / lateral) first; deeper / angled blades as dissection advances.
5. **Per-arm release every 30–60 minutes** to allow intermittent tissue reperfusion — the design feature that the Thompson uniquely supports without disturbing the whole setup.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
6. **Combine with handheld retractors** ([Deaver](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/deaver), [Richardson](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/richardson)) for specific exposures the frame cannot provide.

## Safety

Standard Bookwalter-class retractor-safety principles apply (see the [Bookwalter page](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/bookwalter) for the full discussion):<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- **Bowel injury** from sustained blade pressure (particularly in retroperitoneal exposures where bowel is not directly visualized) — Noldus 2002 data.
- **Femoral / peroneal / pudendal neuropraxia** from deep lateral blades.
- **Heightened caution** in immunosuppression, diverticulitis, prior radiation, re-do fields, thin-bowel-wall patients.
- **Periodic per-arm release** is the Thompson-specific risk-mitigation lever — taking advantage of the independent-joint design.

## Historical Context

Thompson Surgical Instruments (Traverse City, Michigan) developed the system as a **multi-arm articulating alternative** to ring-based platforms, with the **obesity bar / sidebar** architecture engineered specifically for the operative-exposure challenges of larger patients. The Saunders 1986 *J Neurosurg* technical note on table-fixed noncranial retractors is part of the broader genealogy that produced the Thompson-Farley anterior-cervical-spine variant and the Thompson abdominal frame.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
