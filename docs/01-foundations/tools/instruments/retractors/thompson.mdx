# Thompson Retractor

The **Thompson Retractor is a modular, table-mounted, self-retaining platform** manufactured by Thompson Surgical Instruments. Current configurations use table-rail clamps with unilateral or bilateral crossbars, angled or articulating arms, blade handles and interchangeable blades. The catalog includes dedicated general, bariatric, urologic, gynecologic and other procedure sets; components vary by set.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Design

- **Table-rail clamps and crossbars/arms** form the external frame. Unilateral and bilateral abdominal setups are both documented.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Clip-on or slide-on handles** attach to the frame; S-Lock, Quick Angle and micro-adjustable options alter blade orientation or tension.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Interchangeable blade libraries** include abdominal-wall, bladder, organ and soft-tissue retractors; the actual tray determines what is available.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Bariatric configuration:** the current bariatric set uses bilateral rail clamps, a hinged crossbar, angled arms and larger blades. Chang's older modified-Makuuchi technique describes an oncology Thompson setup using an “obesity bar” and sidebars; that terminology should not be assumed for every current set.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Reprocessing:** the system is supplied nonsterile; use the current IFU for cleaning, sterilization and care.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Two Distinguishing Features

Two useful design features are:

1. **Configurable large-patient frames and blades** — a current bariatric set and an older oncology obesity-bar/side-bar technique both address thick abdominal walls, but no comparative study shows that another modular platform cannot provide adequate exposure.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
2. **Independently adjustable handles/joints** — individual blades can be repositioned or relaxed without dismantling the whole frame. In a cervical-retractor review, Thompson-Farley joints allowed independent relaxation; that observation is not an abdominal outcome trial.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Reconstructive-Urology and Urogyn Uses

The current manufacturer atlas includes urology, urologic-oncology and gynecology setups. It depicts perineal and retropubic prostatectomy and catalogs abdominal-wall, bladder and prostate blades.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> The following are practical exposure examples rather than proof that Thompson is the preferred system:

- **Open bladder reconstruction, urinary diversion and ureteral reconstruction** when a configurable table-mounted frame matches the incision and target.
- **Open radical prostatectomy and cystectomy** — sustained multi-vector retraction with independent blade relaxation across the multi-phase operation.
- **Open sacrocolpopexy** — abdominal-wall + bladder + bowel retraction with the multi-arm system; particularly useful in obese patients.
- **Open transabdominal VVF / RVF / RUF repair** — peri-fistula multi-vector retraction during dissection and during omental / peritoneal-flap mobilization.
- **Adjunctive hysterectomy** during complex pelvic reconstruction.
- **Re-do pelvic operations** for post-radiation hostile re-entry, fistula repair, adhesion takedown.
- **Complex upper-abdominal or oncologic exposure** — Chang describes a modified Makuuchi incision with an oncology Thompson, obesity bar and sidebars. That technical report supports its own foregut setup, not automatic extrapolation to every retroperitoneal reconstructive case.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Spine-RU intersection

- **Anterior cervical spine surgery** — the **Thompson-Farley** variant uses mechanical-joint arms on a table-mounted frame. Seex's review notes adjustability and independent relaxation, while also noting bulk, setup time and ease of excessive force.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Lumbar approaches were not source-mapped in this review.** Do not infer a Thompson-specific ALIF or lateral-spine indication from the cervical Thompson-Farley paper.

## Thompson vs Bookwalter vs Omni-Tract vs Balfour

| Feature | **Thompson** | [**Bookwalter**](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/bookwalter) | [**Omni-Tract**](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/omni-tract) | [**Balfour**](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/balfour) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mounting** | Rail clamps + configurable unilateral/bilateral frame | Table post + ring | Table post/support arm + configurable frame | Wound-edge resting |
| **Blade options** | Extensive modular | Extensive modular | Extensive modular | 2 lateral + 1 central |
| **Large-patient options** | Current bariatric frame/blade set; older obesity-bar technique | Ring/blade selection varies | Upper-abdominal/transplant system and obesity blades cataloged | Limited modularity |
| **Independent blade adjustment** | Yes, by handle/joint configuration | Yes, by blade holder | Yes, by holder/frame configuration | Limited |
| **Selection** | Incision, target, available set and operator familiarity | Same | Same | Shorter, less modular exposure |

These systems provide different frame geometries and blade libraries. No direct comparative evidence verified faster setup, a smaller institutional blade footprint or clinical superiority of one platform.

## Setup and Technique

1. **Frame attachment** to the table side rails at appropriate height.
2. **Frame selection and placement:** follow the setup for the available unilateral, bilateral, bariatric or other system. The legacy Chang obesity-bar/side-bar configuration applies only when those components are present.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
3. **Handle and blade positioning** over the wound at heights that do not impede the surgeon's hand position or compress the patient.
4. **Blade sequence**: anchor blades (anterior / lateral) first; deeper / angled blades as dissection advances.
5. **Relax retraction regularly:** the current user manual recommends releasing tension every **20 minutes** to support blood flow. This manufacturer instruction supersedes the former unsourced 30–60-minute rule.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
6. **Combine with handheld retractors** ([Deaver](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/deaver), [Richardson](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/richardson)) for specific exposures the frame cannot provide.

## Safety

General table-mounted-retractor safety principles apply. The Thompson manual specifically cautions against compressing the patient's body with frame components and recommends 20-minute tension relaxation.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> The Noldus paper is a Bookwalter series—four bowel injuries and one femoral neuropathy in more than 4,000 uses—and does not establish a Thompson complication rate.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- **Bowel injury and femoral neuropathy are documented with another table-mounted system:** the Noldus Bookwalter series reported four large-bowel injuries and one femoral neuropathy; it does not establish Thompson-specific rates or mechanisms.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Position and pressure injury remain general risks:** protect structures beneath deep blades, avoid patient compression by the frame and do not assign peroneal or pudendal neuropathy specifically to Thompson without device-specific evidence.
- **Heightened caution is reasonable when tissue is vulnerable or poorly visualized.** Noldus specifically discussed immunosuppression, diverticulitis and retroperitoneal exposure; prior radiation and redo fields were not quantified in that series.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Regular blade relaxation and repositioning** should follow the current manual and the operative field; avoid implying that adjustability eliminates pressure injury.

## Historical Context

Thompson Surgical Instruments is based in Traverse City, Michigan. Saunders's 1986 technical note describes the general principle of table-fixed noncranial retraction, but the accessible abstract does not establish a direct product genealogy. Current manufacturer manuals and catalogs are the appropriate sources for present components and setup.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
