# Perineal Bookwalter — Jordan and Brooke Modifications

The **perineal Bookwalter** is a table-fixed ring configured with blades for perineal exposure. Two names appear in the urologic literature:

- **Bookwalter-Jordan configuration.** Described in posterior-urethroplasty teaching as one option for static ring exposure.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Brooke Bookwalter blade.** Described by Thompson, Harris and colleagues in the late 1990s for radical perineal prostatectomy during the perineal-RP resurgence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Both use a table-fixed ring with blade selection tailored to the target. Both are exposure aids; no comparative data show superiority over other perineal retractors.

## The Two Modifications

### Jordan perineal modification — posterior urethroplasty

- **Bookwalter-style circumferential ring** mounted to a table-fixed post between the patient's legs.
- **Perineal blades.** Selected and positioned for skin, bulbospongiosus and deeper corporal or urethral exposure. A 2025 technique article depicts rake and Richardson blades on a perineal Bookwalter; an assistant holds a separate Deaver retractor against the corpus cavernosum.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Patient positioning and the ring are separate decisions.** Posterior urethroplasty may use lithotomy and a static ring, but blades should not be used to pull the legs or substitute for safe positioning.

Why it matters: posterior urethroplasty can require sustained exposure through a narrow perineal corridor. The 2018 open review states that a static ring such as Denis-Browne, Lone Star/Scott or Bookwalter-Jordan can facilitate the operation; it does not say that only one system can provide adequate exposure.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

**RU uses for the Jordan modification**:

- Posterior urethroplasty for pelvic-fracture urethral injuries (PFUI) is the signature application.
- **Re-do bulbar / membranous urethroplasty** through hostile re-operative perineal tissue.
- **Transperineal RUF repair** with gracilis interposition.
- **Complex perineal reconstruction** when a table-fixed ring and available blade set fit the required exposure.

### Brooke blade — radical / simple perineal prostatectomy

- **Specialized deep-retraction blade** that attaches to the standard Bookwalter ring at the perineal posterior position.
- Provides deep posterior or superior retraction that exposes the prostatic apex, prostatic pedicles, seminal vesicles, and vasa deferentia through the perineal approach.
- The 1997 technical report states that the blade permits mobilization of the seminal vesicles and vasa before apical dissection and facilitates bladder-neck-sparing prostatectomy; it does not itself prove better continence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Allows a single surgeon to perform the entire perineal prostatectomy without an assistant dedicated to retraction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- A review of the perineal approach notes that it avoids the dorsal venous complex and can ease the vesicourethral anastomosis; the surgeon uses a modified Belt approach.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

Radical prostatectomy is primarily cancer surgery. The Brooke blade matters to reconstructive practice in the following settings:

- **Simple perineal prostatectomy** is described in a 22-patient retrospective open-prostatectomy series, including six perineal cases. The report does not specify use of the Brooke blade.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Selected reconstructive or salvage perineal exposure** may use a table-fixed ring with deep blades. No source establishes that irradiated or post-prostatectomy cases require the Brooke blade specifically.

## Setup for the Perineal Case

The perineal Bookwalter configuration differs from the abdominal Bookwalter setup:

1. **Position**: use procedure-appropriate lithotomy with the perineum exposed and all pressure points protected. Exact hip and knee angles depend on the patient, table and operation.
2. **Post placement**: post and clamp attached to the side rail between the patient's legs.
3. **Ring orientation**: ring positioned over the perineal wound, parallel to the perineal skin, oriented for 360° circumferential retraction of perineum, scrotum, and surrounding soft tissues.
4. **Blade sequence.** Begin with superficial blades and exchange or add deeper blades only as the dissection requires. The Brooke report supports its prostatic/seminal-vesicle exposure; a modern posterior-stenosis technique shows rake-to-Richardson exchange and selective handheld Deaver use.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
5. **Reassess frequently**: keep blade tips and protected structures visible when possible, minimize force and duration, and coordinate retractor pressure with lithotomy-position checks. No universal reassessment interval has been established.

## Safety in the Perineal Corridor

The standard [Bookwalter safety profile](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/bookwalter) applies. The Noldus retrospective series found four large-bowel injuries and one femoral neuropathy in more than 4,000 uses over 10 years, mostly transperitoneal or retroperitoneal; it gives no perineal-Bookwalter event rate.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- **Rectum, urethra and adjacent soft tissue:** deep blades can injure structures outside the immediate view; insert and tension them under direct control and reassess after every change.
- **Position-related neuropathy.** Exaggerated lithotomy is associated with lower-extremity neuropraxia, rhabdomyolysis and acute renal failure. Inadequate gel padding with a beanbag and operations lasting more than 5 hours increase these risks.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Padding, symmetric positioning and repeated checks are needed. The series cited above reported a Bookwalter-associated femoral neuropathy; it does not establish a perineal-specific event rate.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Blade pressure.** Kaldany and colleagues use Bookwalter rake blades for initial exposure, exchange them for a ratcheted Richardson blade to retract the split bulbospongiosus atraumatically when circumferential exposure is needed, and use a separately held Deaver on the corpus cavernosum during unilateral dissection.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Comparison Within the Perineal-Retractor Family

| Retractor | Mounting | Frame / blades | Reported or typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Perineal Bookwalter — Jordan** | Table-fixed post | Ring + selected perineal blades | Posterior urethroplasty, PFUI repair |
| **Perineal Bookwalter — Brooke blade** | Table-fixed post | Standard ring + deep posterior prostatic blade | Radical perineal prostatectomy |
| [**Turner-Warwick**](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/turner-warwick) | Ring configuration varies by system | Interchangeable blades | Alternative perineal exposure platform |
| [**Lone Star**](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/lone-star) | Disposable plastic ring | Elastic stays with hooks | Soft-tissue perineal traction (urethroplasty, hypospadias, vaginal, anorectal) |
| **Young perineal retractor** | Handheld | Fixed perineal blade | Historical perineal prostatectomy; not self-retaining |
| **Adson-Beckman** | Self-retaining wedge | Two-blade frame | Superficial perineal exposure only |

## Why Two Modifications Belong Together

The shared architectural point is modularity: a table-fixed ring supports different blades for different perineal targets. The Brooke blade has a discrete technical publication; “Bookwalter-Jordan” is used in posterior-urethroplasty teaching. CS Surgical's 2013 catalog lists a Jordan-Simpson perineal set (CS1000-60) with a segmented ring and named blades compatible with Bookwalter and Simpson systems, but does not identify the original designer or manufacturer of the Bookwalter-Jordan configuration.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> No head-to-head comparison with other perineal rings is cited here.

## Historical Context

- **Bookwalter-Jordan.** The eponym appears in a posterior-urethroplasty review.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> No single inventor or original commercial specification is documented.
- Thompson and colleagues (1997) described the Brooke blade for radical perineal prostatectomy; Harris and Thompson's 1996 article describes the broader anatomic technique.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Check current availability with the tray or manufacturer.
