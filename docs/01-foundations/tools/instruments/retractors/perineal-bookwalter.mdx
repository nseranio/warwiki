# Perineal Bookwalter — Jordan and Brooke Modifications

The **perineal Bookwalter** is a table-fixed ring configured with blades for perineal exposure. Two names appear in the urologic literature:

- **Bookwalter-Jordan configuration** — described in posterior-urethroplasty teaching as one option for static ring exposure.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Brooke Bookwalter blade** — described by **Thompson, Harris and colleagues** in the late 1990s for **radical perineal prostatectomy** during the perineal-RP resurgence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Both use a table-fixed ring with blade selection tailored to the target. Published descriptions support their use as exposure aids; they do not establish superiority over every other perineal retractor.

## The Two Modifications

### Jordan perineal modification — posterior urethroplasty

- **Bookwalter-style circumferential ring** mounted to a table-fixed post between the patient's legs.
- **Perineal blades** selected and positioned for skin, bulbospongiosus and deeper corporal/urethral exposure. A recent technique article depicts rake, Richardson and Deaver components on a perineal Bookwalter.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Patient positioning and the ring are separate decisions.** Posterior urethroplasty may use lithotomy and a static ring, but blades should not be used to pull the legs or substitute for safe positioning.

Why it matters: posterior urethroplasty can require sustained exposure through a narrow perineal corridor. The 2018 open review states that a static ring such as Denis-Browne, Lone Star/Scott or Bookwalter-Jordan can facilitate the operation; it does **not** say that only one system can provide adequate exposure.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

**RU uses for the Jordan modification**:

- **Posterior urethroplasty** for pelvic-fracture urethral injuries (PFUI) — the signature application.
- **Re-do bulbar / membranous urethroplasty** through hostile re-operative perineal tissue.
- **Transperineal RUF repair** with gracilis interposition.
- **Complex perineal reconstruction** when a table-fixed ring and available blade set fit the required exposure.

### Brooke blade — radical / simple perineal prostatectomy

- **Specialized deep-retraction blade** that attaches to the standard Bookwalter ring at the perineal posterior position.
- Provides **deep posterior / superior retraction** that exposes the **prostatic apex, prostatic pedicles, seminal vesicles, and vasa deferentia** through the perineal approach.
- The 1997 technical report states that the blade permits mobilization of the seminal vesicles and vasa before apical dissection and facilitates bladder-neck-sparing prostatectomy; it does not itself prove better continence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Allows a **single surgeon to perform the entire perineal prostatectomy** without an assistant dedicated to retraction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Use cases relevant to the WARWIKI scope** (radical prostatectomy is primarily cancer surgery and out of scope as a primary topic; the Brooke blade enters reconstructive practice through):

- **Simple perineal prostatectomy** is described in a 22-patient retrospective open-prostatectomy series, including six perineal cases. Its abstract does not specify use of the Brooke blade or validate a reconstructive indication.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Selected reconstructive or salvage perineal exposure** may use a table-fixed ring, but the inherited claims that all post-prostatectomy reconstruction “inherits” the Brooke paradigm and that irradiated cases specifically require this blade were not supported by a verified source.

## Setup for the Perineal Case

The perineal Bookwalter configuration differs from the abdominal Bookwalter setup:

1. **Position**: use procedure-appropriate lithotomy with the perineum exposed and all pressure points protected. Exact hip and knee angles depend on the patient, table and operation; the reviewed sources do not establish a universal greater-than-90-degree rule.
2. **Post placement**: post and clamp attached to the side rail between the patient's legs.
3. **Ring orientation**: ring positioned over the perineal wound, parallel to the perineal skin, oriented for 360° circumferential retraction of perineum, scrotum, and surrounding soft tissues.
4. **Blade sequence**: begin with superficial blades and exchange/add deeper blades only as the dissection requires. The Brooke report supports its prostatic/seminal-vesicle exposure; a modern posterior-stenosis technique shows rake-to-Richardson exchange and selective handheld Deaver use.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
5. **Reassess frequently**: keep blade tips and protected structures visible when possible, minimize force and duration, and coordinate retractor pressure with lithotomy-position checks. No source reviewed here established a universal 30–60-minute interval.

## Safety in the Perineal Corridor

The standard [Bookwalter safety profile](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/bookwalter) applies. The Noldus retrospective series found four large-bowel injuries and one femoral neuropathy in more than 4,000 abdominal/retroperitoneal uses; it does not provide a perineal-Bookwalter event rate.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- **Rectum, urethra and adjacent soft tissue:** deep blades can injure structures outside the immediate view; insert and tension them under direct control and reassess after every change.
- **Position-related neuropathy:** prolonged lithotomy itself can injure lower-extremity nerves. Padding, symmetric positioning and repeated checks are essential; the reviewed sources do not assign femoral, peroneal or pudendal events specifically to this device.
- **Blade pressure:** replace a traumatic rake with a broader/handheld retractor when the target plane requires it, as illustrated in the recent posterior-stenosis technique.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Comparison Within the Perineal-Retractor Family

| Retractor | Mounting | Frame / blades | Reported or typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Perineal Bookwalter — Jordan** | Table-fixed post | Ring + selected perineal blades | Posterior urethroplasty, PFUI repair |
| **Perineal Bookwalter — Brooke blade** | Table-fixed post | Standard ring + deep posterior prostatic blade | Radical perineal prostatectomy; simple/salvage use was not verified |
| [**Turner-Warwick**](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/turner-warwick) | Ring configuration varies by system | Interchangeable blades | Alternative perineal exposure platform |
| [**Lone Star**](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/lone-star) | Disposable plastic ring | Elastic stays with hooks | Soft-tissue perineal traction (urethroplasty, hypospadias, vaginal, anorectal) |
| **Young perineal retractor** | Handheld | Fixed perineal blade | Historical perineal prostatectomy; not self-retaining |
| **Adson-Beckman** | Self-retaining wedge | Two-blade frame | Superficial perineal exposure only |

## Why Two Modifications Belong Together

The shared architectural point is modularity: a table-fixed ring supports different blades for different perineal targets. The Brooke blade has a discrete technical publication; “Bookwalter-Jordan” is used in posterior-urethroplasty teaching, but the exact original design/manufacturer provenance and a head-to-head comparison with other rings remain unresolved.

## Historical Context

- **Bookwalter-Jordan:** the eponym appears in reconstructive teaching and current posterior-urethroplasty literature.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> The reviewed sources do not establish a single inventor, original commercial specification or “gold-standard” superiority.
- **Thompson and colleagues (1997)** described the Brooke blade for radical perineal prostatectomy; Harris and Thompson's 1996 article describes the broader anatomic technique.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Current availability and use outside that operation require a current tray/manufacturer check.
