# Lone Star Retractor System

The **Lone Star** is a self-retaining ring-and-elastic-stay system supplied by CooperSurgical. Hooks engage tissue, while elastic leads secure in slots around the frame. Moving a stay to another slot changes its direction of pull; repositioning the hook changes which layer is exposed. Different frames and stay tips support perineal, urethral and vaginal exposure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Components and configurations

**Frame.** The disposable range includes articulated plastic frames in several shapes and sizes. These examples identify particular models, rather than a universal choice for a given operation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

| Model | Listed dimensions | Configuration or kit example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3307G | 14.1 × 14.1 cm | Square frame; included in the 3707 colorectal kit |
| 3304G | 32.5 × 18.3 cm | Large snowman-shaped frame; two catheter clips; included in the 3704 gynecology kit |
| 3308G | 28.3 × 18.3 cm | Articulated frame with two catheter clips |
| 3301G / 3302G | 25.3 × 11.3 / 22.0 × 11.3 cm | Alternative elongated configurations |
| 3310G | 25.0 × 25.0 cm | Larger frame |

The **APS** variant has an open end, backing to assist positioning, catheter clips and attachment holes. Its open end accommodates a weighted speculum or posterior access. The linked APS guide was revised in 2012, despite its newer website upload path.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

**Elastic stays.** There is a tissue-engaging tip at one end and an elastic lead that attaches to the ring; there is no tissue hook at both ends. The illustrated pocket guide distinguishes sharp, blunt, rake and blade patterns:<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

| Stay pattern | Examples in the manufacturer's guide |
| --- | --- |
| Sharp hook | 3 mm (3314-8G), 5 mm (3311-8G) |
| Blunt hook | 5 mm (3316-8G), 12 mm (3350-8G) |
| Semi-blunt PRO-Stay | 5 mm (3205-8G) |
| Rake patterns | Two-, three- and four-finger variants |
| Solid blade | 6.5 × 16 mm (3338-4G), 13 × 16 mm (3330-4G) |

Elongated- and dual-lead variants are also listed. Select the actual tip and frame needed for the exposure. The cited catalog does not establish a universal light, medium or heavy tension classification. Hook configuration and the amount of elastic stretch are separate considerations.

## Setup and adjustment

The manufacturer's IFU describes three core maneuvers:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

1. **Position and balance.** Place the ring around the site; attach stays in opposing quadrants with balanced tension to stabilize it.
2. **Follow the dissection.** As the working plane deepens, reposition the stays. Add or redirect stays to maintain balanced exposure.
3. **Maintain exposure during closure.** Keep opposing traction while suturing, adjusting the stays as the field changes.

The [illustrated IFU](https://www.coopersurgical.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Lone-Star-Instructions-for-Use.pdf) shows this sequence. It does not prescribe a universal hook depth, tissue bite or numerical traction force. Those decisions depend on tissue and operation; elastic traction does not guarantee freedom from pressure injury or hook pull-through.

## Reconstructive applications

| Setting | Practical role and source |
| --- | --- |
| Male and female urethroplasty; urethral repair | Adjustable exposure around the operative field; specifically listed in CooperSurgical's reconstructive-urology sheet.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Posterior urethroplasty | Gomez and Scarberry describe static ring retractors, including Scott/Lone Star, Denis Browne and Bookwalter–Jordan, during the perineal approach. This is operative technique teaching, not a trial establishing one system's superiority.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Artificial urinary sphincter and male sling insertion | Perineal exposure; both are listed manufacturer applications.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Vaginal and pelvic-floor procedures | The APS guide illustrates use for anterior/posterior repair, sling procedures, mesh excision, vaginal hysterectomy, biopsy and episiotomy repair.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| Vaginal fistula surgery | The teaching video below demonstrates application in a fistula field. Use the relevant repair page for the reconstruction itself. |

For deep perineal reconstruction, compare [Perineal Bookwalter](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/perineal-bookwalter) and [Denis Browne](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/denis-browne). Frame geometry, blade reach and the ability to redirect traction are useful selection considerations. The posterior urethroplasty review provides examples of the operative setting in which these systems are used.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Practical handling and limitations

- **Adjust the vector before adding force.** Repositioning a stay or frame may improve exposure without stretching an existing stay further, which is a practical application of the IFU's balanced-retraction instructions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Reassess tissue purchase during dissection.** Hook tips and elastic leads still transmit force. A blunt tip is a configuration choice, not proof that traction is harmless in thin, irradiated or scarred tissue.
- **Choose around the working space.** The square colorectal-kit frame is not the manufacturer's universal vaginal-surgery frame. Consider catheter management, instrument entry and posterior access.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Check the exact device label.** The disposable-system IFU prohibits reuse, reprocessing and resterilization, and excludes damaged devices or compromised sterile packaging. Historical reusable metal frames are a separate category with their own processing instructions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Keep rare reports in their original setting.** Tranchart and colleagues reported two perianal recurrences at hook sites after rectal-cancer surgery. This is a signal in a tumor-bearing colorectal field; it does not establish an incidence in reconstructive urology or prove that blunt hooks prevent implantation.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
