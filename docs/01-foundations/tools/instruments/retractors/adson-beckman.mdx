# Adson-Beckman Retractor

The Adson-Beckman, also catalogued as Beckman-Adson or Beckmann-Adson, is a self-retaining retractor with opposing multiprong blades and a handle locking mechanism. Hinged-arm configurations accommodate the depth and angle of a wound. Many catalogued models are relatively long instruments used for deeper soft-tissue or spinal exposure; the name should not be treated as a synonym for a small Weitlaner.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Design and variants

- **Opposing pronged blades** provide two main retraction directions.
- **Hinged arms** allow the blade-bearing portions to sit at an angle to the handles on the relevant models.
- **Sharp and blunt configurations** are available; select the actual catalogued pattern for the intended tissue.
- **Blade depth, width and overall length vary.** For example, Pilling 165402 has 4 × 4 blunt prongs, 3.2 cm blade depth and 33 cm overall length. Aspen 24-3446 is a blunt, hinged-arm laminectomy pattern with a 1¾-inch-wide blade.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The practical distinction from a [Gelpi](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/gelpi) is the multiprong blade rather than a single point per arm. A [Weitlaner](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/weitlaner) is another multiprong self-retainer, but size, depth and hinge geometry must be compared on the actual instruments.

## Role in reconstructive surgery

For a deeper perineal, groin or flap-harvest incision, a hinged multiprong retractor may help maintain two-sided exposure while keeping the handles away from the target. This is a surgical technique choice based on wound geometry and local instrument availability. A large laminectomy-pattern instrument may be poorly suited to a short scrotal or superficial incision; a smaller self-retainer or elastic-stay system may fit that field better.

For circumferential perineal traction, consider the different geometry of a [Lone Star](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/lone-star) or [Perineal Bookwalter](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/perineal-bookwalter). These are alternatives to assess, not an evidence-based hierarchy of devices.

## Practical handling

1. **Inspect the hinge, blade alignment and locking mechanism.** Confirm whether the prongs are sharp or blunt before placement.
2. **Place the blades under direct vision** against the intended tissue, keeping nearby vessels, nerves and other vulnerable structures free of the prongs.
3. **Open gradually** until exposure is adequate; do not use a predetermined number of ratchet clicks.
4. **Check every prong's purchase.** A single poorly positioned tip can carry disproportionate force even on a multiprong blade.
5. **Release and reposition as the operative layer changes**, and reassess pressure during prolonged retraction.
6. **Disengage the lock under control** before withdrawing the tips from tissue.

Blunt prongs can compress tissue, while sharp prongs can puncture or tear it. Neither configuration is intrinsically safe beside a nerve or vessel. If a blade is slipping, reassess its fit and tissue contact rather than automatically increasing the spread.

## Care and reprocessing

The cited Aspen instrument is reusable. Inspect joints and prongs for damage, clean hinge and locking surfaces, and follow the model-specific reprocessing instructions. Aspen's reusable-instrument IFU requires cleaning and sterilization before use and recommends steam for covered instruments.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
