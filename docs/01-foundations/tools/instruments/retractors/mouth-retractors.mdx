# Mouth Retractors for Buccal Mucosa Graft Harvest

Adequate intraoral exposure is an important part of [buccal mucosa graft (BMG)](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa) harvest. The aim is to see the donor surface clearly while protecting Stensen's duct (parotid orifice opposite the second maxillary molar) and the lip vermillion. The literature describes the general approach more than it compares specific devices head-to-head; most reconstructive urologists and oral or maxillofacial surgeons develop a preference based on mouth size, harvest side (unilateral versus bilateral), and available instrumentation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Retractor Options

### Dingman Mouth Gag

The Dingman is a self-retaining frame used in palatal surgery and adaptable to BMG exposure.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Its frame and tongue blades maintain mouth opening while separate cheek retraction can expose the donor surface. The Pilling 070450 set includes a frame and three blades; check the actual set rather than assuming every component is included.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

- **Good for**: bilateral harvest or sustained exposure when the frame fits the mouth and planned field
- **Trade-off.** Larger setup time and footprint than a simple bite-block plus cheek retractor

### Kilner-Doughty Mouth Gag

A self-retaining frame with dental or palatal supports and interchangeable tongue depressors. SURTEX describes a U-shaped frame with sliding tooth claws. Support and suspension depend on the actual system; use its compatible components rather than improvising a connection to a stand.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

- **Good for**: bilateral harvest, prolonged cases, oral-surgery-team-shared trays
- **Trade-off**: more components and setup than a handheld cheek retractor and bite block

### Denhardt Mouth Gag

A ratcheted mouth gag with opposing curved blades. The SURTEX Denhart pattern uses a lever-operated lock and serrated working surfaces; blade covers and tooth protection depend on the model.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

- **Good for**: solo or lightly assisted harvest, longer harvests, bilateral harvest
- **Trade-off**: wider profile; can obstruct posterior cheek mucosa access in a small mouth

### Molt Mouth Gag

A ratcheted spreader with curved blades and finger-ring handles. Different sizes allow selection for the available oral opening; it is not inherently safer or more stable than another gag.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

- **Good for**: small mouths, posterior cheek exposure
- **Trade-off**: check tooth contact, available working space and stability in the individual patient

### Jennings Mouth Gag

A wire-frame mouth gag with an adjustable opening. Assess where its frame and contact surfaces sit relative to the planned donor site rather than assuming that the midline or lateral cheek will remain unobstructed.

- **Good for**: an alternative when its configuration gives suitable access and the team is familiar with it

### Minnesota Cheek Retractor

A single-handed curved blade for lateral cheek retraction. It does not hold the mouth open on its own. Standard pairing with a bite-block or Dingman / Denhardt for intraoperative BMG access.

- **Good for**: assistant-driven lateral retraction during graft harvest

### Lone Star (Scott) Elastic Stay-Hook Adaptation

Some surgeons adapt the [Lone Star elastic stay-hook ring](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/lone-star) for cheek retraction during BMG harvest. The hooks evert and stabilize the buccal mucosa against the cheek, creating a taut donor surface without continuous handheld retraction.

- **Use considerations**: model-specific labeling and instructions, familiarity with the ring system, and careful control of hook position and tension

### Seldin / Lip Retractor

A simple lip retractor that pulls the lower or upper lip outward and helps distribute traction at the vermilion and oral commissure. Used only as an adjunct.

### Bite Block

Not strictly a retractor, this is a soft plastic or rubber block placed between the contralateral molars that holds the mouth open passively under anesthesia.

- **Good for**: small, quick harvests where a formal mouth gag is excessive
- **Trade-off**: does not retract the cheek or tongue; confirm position and maintain accountability for the block

### Specialized Oral Retractors

Eppley et al. (1997) described a dedicated specialized oral retractor for BMG harvest in their early urethroplasty series, reporting adequate graft size and no donor-site complications in 12 patients.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> This small series illustrates a useful technique; it does not establish superiority over general-purpose mouth gags.

## Workhorse Setup — Mouth Gag + Cheek Retractor + Lip Stay-Suture

One practical combined technique, adapted to the planned harvest:<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- **Standard mouth gag** (Dingman / Denhardt / Kilner-Doughty) holds the mouth open
- Stay sutures placed through the lateral edge of the lip further retract and stabilize the cheek. This combination provides visualization of the inner cheek
- **Lip retractor** to protect the ipsilateral corner of the mouth
- **Stay sutures at superior and inferior graft outline** tied to the retractor frame to flatten the donor surface for sharp dissection

## Practical Considerations for the Whole Harvest

Retractor choice is one part of exposure. Check dentition, mouth opening and the airway with the anesthesia team before placing or tightening a gag. Protect teeth, lips and tongue, and reassess pressure during the harvest. Account for any removable covers, blocks and packing. The following are practical harvesting principles, not comparative proof for a particular device:

- **Plan the airway with anesthesia.** Nasal intubation can clear the oral field when appropriate; an oral tube secured away from the harvest side may also be used. The airway choice depends on patient and procedural factors, and local-infiltration techniques have also been described.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Stensen's duct (parotid orifice opposite the second maxillary molar) identified and marked** before incision to avoid injury during harvest.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Submucosal infiltration or hydrodissection can help define the plane and provide local hemostasis. Select the local anesthetic, vasoconstrictor and total dose with the anesthesia team, accounting for all other administered local anesthetic.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Size the graft to the reconstructive defect and available donor tissue rather than treating a single dimension as standard. Single-cheek harvest is described in the donor-site literature.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- Plan unilateral versus bilateral harvest according to the graft needed and each cheek's usable area. The SGURS survey describes variation in practice, not a mandate or a comparison proving one retractor better.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Keep the dissection superficial to the buccinator muscle.** Reassess the plane and improve exposure if visibility is poor.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Donor-Site Morbidity — What the Data Show

These studies inform donor-site counseling. They do not compare the retractor options listed above:

- Barbagli 2014, n = 553 consecutive patients undergoing oral mucosa graft harvest: 98.2% patient satisfaction; 95.5% reported no late difficulty opening the mouth and 95.8% no dry mouth. This was a retrospective cohort using a nonvalidated questionnaire, with wound closure; these results are not guarantees for every technique.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- Soave 2018 RCT, 135 patients, compared closure with nonclosure of the inner-cheek donor site. Nonclosure was noninferior for intensity and affective quality of oral pain at the assessed time points. This addresses wound management, not choice of mouth gag.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- Fabbroni 2005 oral-surgery-driven series: mild trismus was reported early in 4 of 23 patients, with one late complaint. The series demonstrates possible donor-site morbidity; it does not isolate the effect of retraction.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Example Setup (At-a-Glance)

A practical starting arrangement, modified for the patient and planned BMG harvest:

| Element | Choice | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Mouth-holding | **Dingman** or Kilner-Doughty | Sustained self-retaining exposure |
| Cheek retraction | **Minnesota** (or Lone Star hook adaptation) | Lateral cheek eversion |
| Lip protection | Seldin / lip retractor + lateral-lip stay suture | Vermillion protection + further cheek stability |
| Donor-surface flattening | **Stay sutures at graft corners** | Taut surface for sharp dissection |
| Airway | Nasal or suitably positioned oral ETT, selected with anesthesia | Airway security with access to the donor site |
| Infiltration | Individualized local anesthetic/vasoconstrictor plan | Plane definition and hemostasis within the total dose limit |
| Anatomic landmark | **Stensen's duct marked** | Avoid parotid-orifice injury |

Local preference and institutional tray composition drive most variation. The constants: mouth held open stably, cheek retracted laterally, lip protected, donor surface taut and dry, Stensen's duct identified and avoided, dissection stays superficial to the buccinator.
