# Large Needle Driver (da Vinci)

**8 mm EndoWrist needle driver — the default intracorporeal suturing instrument across robotic urology, urogyn, and reconstructive surgery.** Standard tool for the **vesicourethral anastomosis at robotic RP**, the **ureteroneocystostomy at robotic ureteral reimplant**, the **mesh-to-promontory and mesh-to-vagina suturing at robotic sacrocolpopexy**, and virtually every other intracorporeal stitch in the robotic RU/urogyn portfolio. Mechanical only — no energy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Design

- **8 mm shaft** with full **EndoWrist articulation (7 DoF)**, motion scaling, and tremor elimination.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Strong tapered jaws** with cross-hatched gripping surface optimized for needle handling — driving, positioning, rotating through tissue.
- **No electrical energy**.
- **Standard use-life** (typically ~ 10 uses per instrument).
- **Sister variants**:
  - **Mega Needle Driver** — larger / stronger jaws for thicker tissue (fascia, bariatric).
  - **Mega SutureCut Needle Driver** — Mega jaw + integrated suture cutter.
  - **Black Diamond Micro Needle Driver** — fine jaws for microsurgery (7-0 / 9-0); used in microvascular anastomosis where the standard Large is too coarse.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Grip Force and Suture-Strength Caveats

The single most operationally important property of the Large Needle Driver is that the **da Vinci applies a maximally-compressive grasp on every needle / suture handling event** — there is **no haptic feedback**, so the surgeon cannot modulate grip force based on touch.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

Bench-tested consequences:

- **Repetitive robotic needle-driver manipulation reduces monofilament suture strength by 35%** (Ricchiuti 2010, p &lt; 0.05).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **ePTFE (Gore-Tex) sutures retain strength** after robotic manipulation; **Prolene and Ethibond lose significant strength** — Diks 2007 recommended ePTFE as first-choice for robotic vascular anastomosis.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Suture handling technique matters** — Abiri 2017 showed that pulling sutures directly through the jaws (looped or at the needle body) caused greater strength loss than grasping the suture end alone.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

**Practical implication**: minimize unnecessary suture re-grasping, especially on monofilaments; prefer braided or barbed sutures where appropriate; **grasp the suture tail, not the body**.

## Reconstructive-Urology and Urogyn Uses

### Vesicourethral anastomosis at robotic radical prostatectomy

- The workhorse instrument for the **VUA**.
- **Van Velthoven running anastomosis** (two pre-tied sutures run posterior-to-anterior) and **barbed V-Loc running anastomosis** are the dominant modern techniques.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Single-needle-driver V-Loc running suture** reduces anastomosis time vs two-needle-driver interrupted techniques: **8.5 vs 11.5 min** (p = 0.001) with equivalent continence and stenosis rates (Massoud 2013).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Cost-reduction three-instrument RARP** (needle driver + ProGrasp + monopolar scissors) reduces disposable instrument cost up to **40%** (Ramirez 2016).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

### Robotic reconstructive urology

- **Ureteral reimplantation, Boari flap, psoas hitch, ureteroureterostomy** — ureteroneocystostomy and refluxing / non-refluxing detrusor-tunnel sutures; barbed V-Loc commonly used.
- **Ileal-ureter substitution, augmentation cystoplasty, urinary diversion (Bricker / Wallace / neobladder)** — bowel-to-bladder anastomoses, ureteral implantation into bowel, neobladder neck construction.
- **Robotic vesicovaginal / ureterovaginal fistula repair** — multilayer closure.
- **Robotic transvaginal-mesh excision with reconstruction** — cystotomy and vaginotomy closure.

### Robotic urogyn

- **Sacrocolpopexy** — mesh-to-anterior-vaginal-wall, mesh-to-posterior-vaginal-wall, and mesh-to-sacral-promontory suturing (when not using a [tacker](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/sacrocolpopexy-tacker)).
- **Robotic uterosacral / sacrospinous suspension, paravaginal repair**.
- **Robotic hysterectomy vaginal cuff closure**.

### Robotic hernia / general surgery cross-relevance

- **Robotic TAPP inguinal hernia repair** — mesh fixation and peritoneal-flap closure. Lima 2022 found the da Vinci needle driver significantly faster than an articulating laparoscopic needle driver: **mesh fixation 258 vs 392 s; peritoneal closure 419 vs 635 s**.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Variants — When to Switch

| Instrument | Jaw / role | Choose when |
|---|---|---|
| **Large Needle Driver** | Standard 8 mm needle driver | Default for most intracorporeal sutures |
| **Mega Needle Driver** | Larger, stronger jaws | Fascia, thick tissue, large needle |
| **Mega SutureCut Needle Driver** | Mega + integrated cutter | Long running sutures where assistant scissor exchanges slow flow |
| **Black Diamond Micro Needle Driver** | Fine tips for 7-0 / 9-0 | Microvascular anastomosis (limited if vessel ≤ 1 mm)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **SP 6 mm Needle Driver** | Smaller profile for da Vinci SP | Single-Port confined corridors<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |

## Suturing Performance — Robot vs Standard Laparoscopy

- **Stefanidis 2010** — robotic-assisted intracorporeal suturing **0.05 errors per knot vs 1.15 with standard laparoscopy** (p &lt; 0.05), with decreased operator workload.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Dakin / Gagner 2003**; **Anderson 2016** — robotic precision matches or exceeds standard instruments; **biggest advantage on fine sutures (6-0 / 7-0)** and **challenging angles**.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

## Instrument Malfunction

- **Jaw fracture** of the Large Needle Driver during RARP urethrovesical anastomosis has been reported (Park 2008) — requires intraperitoneal retrieval and replacement.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Buffi 2025 systematic review** (&gt; 3.3 million da Vinci procedures) — overall malfunction rate **1.0%**, instrument-specific malfunctions **0.4%**, malfunction-related conversion **0.09%**, malfunction-related injury **0.01%**.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- **Friedman 2013 FDA MAUDE analysis** — wrist / tool-tip failures are the most common failure mode (285/565 in one analysis).<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

## Practical Pearls

- **Grasp the suture tail, not the body** — minimizes the 35% monofilament strength loss demonstrated by Ricchiuti.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Barbed (V-Loc) running suture** for VUA / ureteroneocystostomy reduces console time without compromising continence / stenosis (Massoud 2013).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **ePTFE for vascular** when robotic-applied force compromises monofilament integrity.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Mega SutureCut** when scissor exchanges would otherwise slow long running closures.
- **Black Diamond Micro** for microsurgical / 9-0 work — but acknowledge the ≤ 1 mm vessel limit.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Limitations

- **No haptic feedback** — maximally-applied grip cannot be modulated by touch.
- **Suture damage** — bench-confirmed; minimize re-grasping and choose suture material accordingly.
- **Use-life limitation** — typically ~ 10 uses per instrument.
- **Jaw failure** — rare but reported; intraperitoneal fragment retrieval contingency planning.
