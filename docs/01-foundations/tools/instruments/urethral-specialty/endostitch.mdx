# Endostitch™

The **Endo Stitch™** (Autosuture / Covidien / **Medtronic**) is a single-use endoscopic suturing device adapted for transvaginal sacrospinous ligament fixation (SSLF) in a small 1997 series. Its toggle-jaw mechanism transfers a needle between jaws. Endoscopic and vaginal use require different exposure and ergonomics; laparoscopic timing or animal closure findings should not be treated as SSLF clinical outcomes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Design

- **Pistol-grip handpiece** with **toggle lever** actuator
- **Two opposing jaws** at the distal end; needle automatically loaded for the next pass after each toggle
- **Short suture pre-loaded with needle** between the jaws (Polysorb, Biosyn, Surgipro variants)
- **10 mm endoscopic shaft** — current catalog lists a 34 cm standard shaft; verify the specific model and loading unit before use<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Single-use disposable**

## Mechanism Advantage — Per-Stitch Time

In Adams 1995, **41 Endo Stitch laparoscopic suturing maneuvers averaged 43 ± 27 seconds** versus **14 conventional laparoscopic needle-holder maneuvers averaging 151 ± 24 seconds** in urologic endoscopic procedures. This is not an SSLF time comparison or a universal per-stitch estimate.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Reconstructive / Urogyn Use — Vaginal SSLF

The Schlesinger 1997 transvaginal-SSLF series enrolled **17 patients**, all undergoing concomitant procedures. Its results therefore describe the combined operations, not an isolated device effect:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Endpoint | Result |
|---|---|
| **SSL plication time** | **18.8 ± 3.0 min** (range 14–25) |
| **Total-procedure EBL ≤ 100 mL** | **15/17 (88.2%)** |
| **Perioperative complications** | **None** |
| **Vault support maintained at mean 9.8 mo follow-up** | **15/17 (88.2%)** in this small series |
| **Recurrence** | **11.8%** (2 patients at 4 and 6 mo) |

Schlesinger suggested a shorter instrument would improve vaginal ergonomics for the model used then. This should not be assumed to describe every later model.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Vascular Safety — Manning 2014 Cadaveric Comparison

The **Manning 2014 cadaveric study** found the Endo Stitch passage remained within the ligament in the specimens examined, unlike i-Stitch penetration of coccygeal branches. This is anatomical observation, not a measured patient vascular-injury rate.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

However, the Endostitch was **slightly less ideal** than the Capio and Caspari on Manning's framework: the **ideal device should have the sharp penetrating component traverse the shortest distance at the shallowest depth**. The Caspari and Capio best satisfied that criterion; the Endostitch did so **"less so."**<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Broader Surgical Applications

Endoscopic suturing applications described in the cited literature or manufacturer catalog include:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

- **Laparoscopic pyeloplasty** — Anderson-Hynes ureteropelvic reconstruction
- **Laparoscopic bladder-neck suspension** (historic Burch / paravaginal)
- **GI viscerotomy closure** — Omotosho 2011 tested barbed versus nonbarbed closures in **dogs**, with shorter closure time; not human GI or SSLF evidence<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **General laparoscopic tissue approximation** across specialties

Göpel 2011 evaluated an **experimental motorized modification** in a surgeon task simulation; faster completion (p = 0.01) does not establish patient benefit or commercial availability.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

- **Length** designed for laparoscopic port access — somewhat cumbersome for vaginal SSLF (Schlesinger 1997 original-series caveat)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Ergonomics**: weight and size on the handpiece have been cited as drawbacks compared to purpose-built devices<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Single-use disposable** — recurring per-case cost<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Needle-hole concern** reported by Nagai 1999 for the historical model; not quantified for current devices here<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Less ergonomic profile than purpose-built vaginal SSLF devices** (Capio, i-Stitch) in surgeons who do transvaginal apical-suspension cases primarily

## Endostitch vs Adjacent SSLF Devices

| Feature | **Endostitch** | **[Capio](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/capio-device)** | **Caspari** | **[i-Stitch](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/i-stitch)** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Type** | Suture-capturing (toggle-jaw) | Suture-capturing (throw-catch-retrieve) | Suture-capturing | Suture-capturing (blunt ball-tip) |
| **Original design purpose** | **Laparoscopic surgery** | Vaginal SSLF | Vaginal SSLF | Vaginal SSLF |
| **Cadaveric trajectory (Manning 2014)** | Remained within ligament | Remained within ligament | Remained within ligament | Penetrated coccygeal branches in tested thin ligaments |
| **Penetration observation** | Less ideal than Capio/Caspari by authors' geometric criterion | Shorter/shallower path in this model | Shorter/shallower path in this model | Deeper path in this model |
| **Vaginal ergonomics** | Length concern for Schlesinger's 1997 model | Purpose-built | Purpose-built | Purpose-built |
| **Clinical inference** | Small 17-patient combined-operation series | Separate device-specific evidence needed | Separate device-specific evidence needed | Cadaveric finding is not patient injury incidence |

## Bottom Line

Endo Stitch is an endoscopic suturing option with limited published SSLF-specific clinical evidence. A 17-patient vaginal series and cadaveric trajectory study support technical feasibility, not a comparative patient-safety or durability claim. Use the current model-specific instructions and distinguish endoscopic, animal and vaginal evidence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
