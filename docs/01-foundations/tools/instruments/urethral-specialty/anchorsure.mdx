# Anchorsure® System

The **Anchorsure® System** (Neomedic International) is a PEEK anchor-based fixation device for attaching sutures to pelvic-floor ligaments, including the sacrospinous ligament in apical prolapse repair. Unlike a suture-capturing instrument such as [Capio](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/capio-device), it deploys an anchor carrying a polypropylene suture into the ligament. It is a fixation device, not a mesh; an anterior approach is one described application, not a proven comparative advantage.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Design

- **Straight, narrow delivery handle** for transvaginal ligament access.
- **PEEK (polyetheretherketone) anchor** with USP-0 monofilament polypropylene suture. The A-SURE kit contains a preloaded handle, extra anchor and surgical needle; A-SURE02 contains two anchors.
- **Native-tissue fixation** is possible without a mesh. Follow the particular product instructions: the available March 2016 English IFU describes the system as sterile, single-use, and not to be reprocessed or resterilized; request an updated IFU from the manufacturer before use.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Rationale — Anchor vs Suture Capture

Traditional suture-based SSLF requires tight suture knots around or through the ligament, which may:

- Compress or compromise neurovascular structures (pudendal nerve, inferior gluteal vessels)
- Contribute to gluteal or buttock pain, the characteristic SSLF complication
- Depend on ligament tissue quality to hold the knot long-term

The anchor embeds fixation within the ligament rather than encircling it, a proposed mechanical rationale for less pain, not an established clinical benefit. In a small randomized comparison (24 anchor, 23 suture capture), gluteal or posterior-thigh pain at one week did not differ significantly.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The IFU still warns of possible vessel or nerve puncture, pain, infection, fistula and extrusion.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Reconstructive / Urogyn Uses

- **Vaginal vault prolapse after hysterectomy** (apical suspension)
- **Uterovaginal prolapse** with sacrospinous fixation (Amreich–Richter technique)
- **Post-hysterectomy vault prolapse repair**
- **Anterior approach to SSLF** is a described access route; comparative mechanical or clinical superiority is not established<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For placement, the available IFU directs the surgeon to position and press the handle against the ligament, advance the delivery shaft completely, withdraw the handle, secure the proximal suture and attach its tip to the curved needle to suture the intended tissue. Confirm the current model's instructions and contraindications, including anticoagulation, pregnancy or planned pregnancy and connective-tissue autoimmune disease.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Efficacy

| Endpoint | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Sexual function at 3 mo (FSFI total score)** | **19.15 → 21.84** in a two-center **uncontrolled cohort** (115 enrolled, 104 completed follow-up); improvement after prolapse surgery cannot be attributed specifically to Anchorsure versus another fixation method | Ahmadian 2026<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Objective / subjective cure rates** | Lo's 676-patient mixed tacker cohort included only **19 Anchorsure-alone** procedures; its group-level cure percentages cannot be assigned to Anchorsure alone | Lo 2026<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |

## Complications

| Complication | Rate / Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Day-1 postoperative pain** | **Slightly higher with ABD (3.40 vs 1.60 SCD, p = 0.013)**; mean highest pain score similar overall | Evangelopoulos 2024 pilot case-control (n = 40)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Gluteal / posterior-thigh pain at 1 wk, 6 wk** | **No significant difference vs Capio** | Plair 2022 RCT<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Opioid use postoperatively** | Similar to Capio | Plair 2022 RCT<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Prolapse symptom scores** | Similar to Capio | Plair 2022 RCT<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Tacker dislodgement** | In a **mixed-device** cohort of 676, 27 patients were classified as having a dislodged tacker, with 2 bladder and 4 operator injuries in that group. The abstract also says 2.2% dislodgement, inconsistent with 27/676; the denominator and device-specific rate require full-text clarification | Lo 2026<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Nerve injury / reoperation** | Amiri reports **3.8% nerve injury overall across methods** and 1.2% reoperation overall, but its abstract does not provide an Anchorsure-specific comparative rate | Amiri 2024 SR + meta-analysis<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |

The Lo tacker-dislodgement signal warrants preparation for patient and operator injury, but its mixed-device cohort and internally inconsistent abstract do not yield an Anchorsure-specific incidence. The old statement that this series alone mandates post-deployment cystoscopy is not established by the abstract; apply the operative protocol and investigate suspected injury.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Anchorsure vs Capio — Head-to-Head Comparison

| Feature | **Anchorsure** (Anchor-Based) | **Capio** (Suture-Capturing) |
|---|---|---|
| **Mechanism** | Deploys anchor into SSL substance | Passes suture through SSL |
| **Gluteal / posterior-thigh pain** | No significant difference vs Capio<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | No significant difference vs Anchorsure<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Day-1 postop pain** | Slightly higher (3.40 vs 1.60)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Slightly lower<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Nerve injury** | No device-specific comparative percentage established here | No Capio-specific 3.8% rate: 3.8% is pooled across methods<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Reoperation** | No reliable head-to-head device rate established here | No reliable head-to-head device rate established here |
| **Anterior approach to SSL** | Described clinical use<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Feasibility depends on exposure and operator technique; no comparative mechanical advantage established |
| **Device-specific concern** | Tacker dislodgement in mixed-device cohort; Anchorsure rate unresolved<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | Neurovascular proximity applies to all SSLF instrumentation<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |

The small randomized trial found no detected short-term pain difference; it was not an equivalence trial for cure or rare complications. Approach geometry and surgeon familiarity remain practical considerations, not proven determinants of superiority.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Context Within POP Surgery

SSLF is a vaginal apical-support option that can be performed as native-tissue repair. The broader comparison with sacrocolpopexy concerns procedures, not Anchorsure versus another instrument; the specific pooled percentages and comparative harms in Zhang require source-level rechecking before being used to choose a device.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Other fixation or capture options include [Saffron](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/saffron-fixation-system), [Capio](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/capio-device), [Endostitch](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/endostitch) and [i-Stitch](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/i-stitch).

## Regulatory / Commercial Status

The available IFU has US prescription-sale caution language but does not, by itself, establish FDA approval or a current CE certificate. Check the jurisdiction, device model and current regulatory listing before procurement or use.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
