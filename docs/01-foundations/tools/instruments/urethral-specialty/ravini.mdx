# Ravini Lighted Urethral Speculum

The **RaViNi** is a self-retaining, lighted urethral speculum listed by Surgipros LLC for female urethroplasty; a posterior-urethral substitution technique also describes its use in a deep perineal corridor. The listed instrument family includes standard, blunt-suction, over-the-wire Council-tip and extra-long variants. Match a specific device to anatomy and the procedure, rather than assuming interchangeability.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Design

- **Self-retaining blades** hold the urethral corridor open; wider labial or vaginal exposure or an assistant may still be needed.
- **Integrated distal light source at the tip.** Illuminates the working surface directly, unlike the back-table line-of-sight illumination of an overhead light.
- **French calibre sizing.** The listed standard RaViNi is 14 Fr, while the Council-tip RaViNi-C is 12 Fr; confirm other dimensions with the current catalog.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Tip-style variants** (see below) for different dissection strategies.

## Variant Family

| Variant | Distinguishing feature | Best fit |
|---|---|---|
| **RaViNi** | 14 Fr standard | Routine female urethroplasty / introital corridor work |
| **RaViNi-B** | **Blunt tip with integrated suction adapter** | Blood-and-irrigation-clearing during ongoing dissection; posterior urethroplasty after stricture has been opened<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **RaViNi-C** | **12 Fr Council-tip for over-the-wire placement** | Initial stricture engagement — the device is passed over a previously placed guidewire, then sequential incisions are made through the stricture until the lumen accepts the larger RaViNi-B<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **RaViNi-π** | **Extra-long shaft** | Deep-corridor exposure where the extra length is appropriate; no comparative outcome advantage established |

## Reconstructive-Urology Uses

### Female Urethroplasty

The instrument's design problem: the female urethra is short, narrow, and angled cephalad through the introital corridor; conventional vaginal retractors ([Heaney](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/heaney-retractor), [Breisky-Navratil](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/breisky), [Auvard weighted speculum](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/weighted-speculum)) open the vaginal canal but do not provide a self-retaining illuminated channel into the urethra itself. The Ravini operationalizes that specific exposure.

**Reported over-the-wire workflow** in the demonstrated female case and posterior substitution technique (not a standardized sequence for every repair):<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

1. Cystoscopy and guidewire placement across the stricture.
2. **RaViNi-C (over-the-wire, 12 Fr)** is advanced into the stricture; sequential incisions are made under direct illumination.
3. Once the lumen accommodates a larger calibre, switch to RaViNi-B for the graft inset and full reconstruction.

An AUA 2025 video abstract described seven heterogeneous female-urethroplasty cases, with 5/7 (71%) reported as successful at a median 21.6-month follow-up. This uncontrolled case series cannot isolate the speculum's contribution or establish a general success rate for the instrument.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Posterior Urethroplasty / Deep Perineal Dissection

The Kaldany / Cedars / Nikolavsky 2025 technique paper on substitution urethroplasty for posterior urethral stenosis describes RaViNi-C over a wire, followed by RaViNi-B with its blunt suction tip after the lumen accommodates an 18 Fr instrument.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Its authors describe improved access and visibility in their technique; comparative benefit has not been demonstrated.

### Other Reconstructive Uses

The instrument may be considered when a narrow illuminated urethral corridor is useful in diverticulum, fistula, mesh-excision or female bladder-neck surgery, but the cited female-urethroplasty abstract and posterior technique do not establish outcomes or a standard RaViNi indication for these other operations. Conventional vaginal and [Lone Star](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/lone-star) exposure remains relevant; tailor exposure to the defect.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Technique Pearls

- **Match the variant to the dissection phase.** RaViNi-C over-the-wire for stricture engagement; RaViNi-B once the lumen is open enough for the blunt-suction tip.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Position for the target corridor** using the operative technique and patient-specific positioning constraints; high lithotomy is not a universal device requirement.
- **Keep the illuminated tip clear** if obscured by blood or debris, following the instrument's handling instructions.
- **Pair with a [Lone Star ring](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/lone-star)** for vulvar-skin retraction; the Ravini exposes the urethra but does not handle vulvar tissue.
- **Recheck blade position intermittently** during prolonged dissection; no fixed 20–30-minute interval is established by the cited sources.

## Limitations

- **Single-axis exposure.** The Ravini opens the urethral corridor but does not retract the vaginal walls or labia; supplement with a Lone Star or assistant retraction for the wider field.
- **No published comparative data** versus nasal speculum (Hoag 2016 vaginal-sparing technique<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>) or versus ring-retractor-only exposure; choice remains operator preference.
- **Acquisition and processing** vary by model and institutional arrangement; verify the current vendor listing and model-specific reprocessing instructions before procurement or use.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Alternatives for Female-Urethroplasty Exposure

| Instrument | Self-retaining | Integrated light | Best fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ravini lighted speculum** | Yes | Yes | Purpose-built option where available<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Nasal speculum** | No (assistant-held) | No | Low-cost / non-Ravini institutions; Hoag 2016 vaginal-sparing ventral BMG technique<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Weighted vaginal speculum + Scott / Lone Star** | Yes (gravity / elastic) | No | Diverticulum resection, broader vaginal exposure when urethral corridor is not the limiting step<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Heaney / Breisky-Navratil retractor** | No (handheld) | No | Apical vaginal exposure; does not address introital-urethral corridor |

## Historical / Manufacturer Context

The RaViNi family is listed by Surgipros LLC; the available catalog does not itself establish the legal manufacturer or a complete reprocessing IFU. The Kaldany 2025 paper notes that posterior-urethroplasty use had not previously been published as of that report.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
