# Gelman Adapter for Retrograde Urethrography

The **Gelman adapter** (CS Surgical) is a cone-shaped meatal-occlusion device developed by Joel Gelman in 1997 for retrograde urethrography (RUG). Seating the cone at the meatus permits contrast injection with penile stretch without an inflated Foley balloon in the fossa navicularis. Gelman's clinical-practice description favors this approach; comparative pain or diagnostic-accuracy superiority over other meatal-occlusion methods has not been established.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Design

- **Cone-shaped tip**, sized to seat against the external meatus and fossa navicularis.
- **Original plastic version** has been replaced by reusable stainless-steel adult and pediatric models (CS17075 and CS17076); confirm the specific instrument's processing instructions rather than assuming a sterilization cycle.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Luer-compatible proximal connection** for syringe-driven contrast injection.
- **No inflated intra-urethral balloon.** The cone seats at the meatal opening; it is not a catheter advanced into the urethra.

## Why It Exists — The Foley-Balloon RUG Problem

One RUG technique uses a small Foley catheter with a balloon inflated within the fossa navicularis to seal the meatus. Gelman's practice description reports a 2 mL balloon measuring about 50 Fr against a typical approximately 24 Fr fossa; a separate 2015 Gelman/Wisenbaugh article describes a Taylor adaptor and a different balloon example of approximately 59 Fr. These measurements are apparatus-dependent, not a universal balloon dimension.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Potential limitations include:

- **Fossa dilation, pain or mucosal injury** if a balloon is overinflated; avoid assuming every balloon placement causes injury.
- **Obscuration of distal urethral pathology** under a catheter or balloon and altered appearance if excessive pressure is used.
- **Dependence on technique**: seal placement, adequate urethral distension and projection affect measured stricture location and length.

The Gelman adapter avoids a balloon in the fossa, but a poor meatal seal, injection pressure or positioning can still compromise the examination.

## Reconstructive-Urology Uses

The Gelman adapter is one option for reconstructive imaging of anterior urethral strictures, selected recurrent strictures and postoperative assessment. Other meatal-seal methods may be appropriate when the cone does not seal or anatomy prevents its use.

- **Anterior urethral stricture workup.** Bulbar, penile, fossa navicularis, panurethral. The non-dilating technique preserves accurate length and location data used for planning [BMG urethroplasty](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts-gu-reconstruction) approach and stage selection.
- **Recurrent stricture evaluation** post-urethroplasty.
- **Post-DVIU or dilation surveillance**.
- **Pre-operative imaging for [Optilume DCB](/docs/surgical-techniques/urethral-reconstruction) candidacy.** Accurate length measurement determines balloon length selection.
- **Pelvic-fracture urethral-injury preoperative imaging** with simultaneous antegrade cystogram through SPT (the up-and-down-o-gram).
- **Post-prostatectomy bulbomembranous stricture / VUAS workup.** Non-traumatic imaging is especially important in the irradiated post-prostatectomy urethra.

## Technique

1. **Position.** Oblique, tilted approximately 45° (right anterior oblique is standard).
2. **Penis on stretch** toward the contralateral shoulder.
3. **Seat the Gelman adapter** against the meatus, gentle pressure to form the friction seal.
4. **Inject contrast gently under fluoroscopic observation**, titrating volume and pressure to a diagnostically distended urethra without forcing against resistance. The cited technique's 60 mL is a syringe capacity, not a prescribed injection volume.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
5. **Document proximal and distal stricture margins** on appropriately distended views. A separate voiding cystourethrogram or antegrade study may be needed for the proximal urethra; RUG and voluntary sphincter relaxation do not guarantee bladder-neck filling, especially with an obliterated posterior segment.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Gelman Adapter vs Alternative RUG Techniques

| Method | Mechanism | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gelman adapter** | Cone-shaped meatal occlusion without an inflated intra-urethral balloon<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Avoids balloon distension and balloon-related distal obscuration; reusable | Seal depends on anatomy and handling; model-specific processing |
| **Foley catheter balloon** | Balloon inflation in fossa navicularis | Widely available | Overinflation may cause pain/injury and can obscure distal pathology<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Penile clamp** | Padded ring at coronal sulcus | Simple, low-cost | Misses **2–4 cm of distal urethra inside the glans**; cannot evaluate fossa / meatal disease<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Sponge-plug method (Li 2023)** | Sterilized sponge with 7 Fr catheter inserted ~1.5 cm into anterior urethra | In an uncontrolled 40-person series: 72.5% reported no pain; all procedures yielded images | Intra-urethral component; study excluded relevant meatal stenosis and lacked direct comparison<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |

## Why the Gelman Technique Matters Reconstructively

An adequately distended, well-positioned RUG helps map stricture extent for reconstruction. Under-distension, obscured distal anatomy or an incomplete proximal study can mislead planning; no cited comparative study quantifies downstream wrong-incision or staging events attributable to an adapter choice.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> For pelvic-fracture urethral injury, pair RUG with appropriate antegrade or voiding assessment; when the bladder neck does not open, additional antegrade endoscopic evaluation may be required.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Historical Context — Joel Gelman

Gelman's center describes initial use of the adapter in 1997, the shift from a discontinued plastic design to a stainless-steel CS Surgical instrument, and his own account that he receives no royalties from the adapter. This is a practice-site disclosure, not a finding of the 2015 Gelman/Wisenbaugh paper, which illustrates a Taylor adaptor instead.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
