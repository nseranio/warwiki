# Capio / Capio SLIM Suture Capturing Device

The **Capio™ / Capio SLIM™** is a **sterile single-use suture-capturing instrument** from **Boston Scientific**. Its throw-catch-retrieve action passes a compatible needle/dart and suture through deep tissue with one hand, facilitating limited-access vaginal surgery; how much dissection is appropriate remains case- and technique-dependent. Published uses include **sacrospinous ligament fixation (SSLF)** for apical prolapse, hysteropexy and paravaginal repair.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Design

- **Slim-line, single-use** disposable shaft.
- **Distal hook** that catches the small curved needle on the opposite side of the target ligament after the plunger drives it through.
- **Plunger trigger** on the handle — **one actuation = pass + capture** in a single motion.
- **Model-specific Capio dart sutures** are listed by the manufacturer in polypropylene, braided polyester, PDO and PGA configurations; compatibility is not a license to load arbitrary needles or sutures.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Capio SLIM** = narrower-profile contemporary version for transvaginal apical POP work.

## How It Works

1. Expose and palpate the intended attachment, respecting nearby vessels and nerves; confirm the selected Capio model and compatible dart/suture.
2. Place the head against the target tissue and actuate the plunger to **throw and catch** the dart.
3. Retrieve the passed suture with the device and complete the suspension according to the operative technique and current instructions. A misfire or failed catch requires reassessment rather than repeated blind firing.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Reconstructive / Urogyn Uses

The dominant contemporary indications:

- **Sacrospinous colpopexy (SSLF) for vaginal vault prolapse** — the dominant use<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Sacrospinous hysteropexy** — uterine-sparing apical prolapse repair<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Vaginal paravaginal repair** for anterior compartment defects<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Uterosacral ligament suspension** (high uterosacral and Mayo-McCall variants)
- **Iliococcygeus fixation** and other deep-ligament pelvic suspensions

## Efficacy

| Endpoint | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Objective / subjective success at 3 yr** | **86.4% / 92.9% in the 44-patient Capio group**, versus 88.1% / 92.9% in 42 traditional-technique patients; not a device-equivalence trial | Leone Roberti Maggiore 2013<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Comparative cohort (n = 163 total)** | Retrospective, two-center **traditional and Capio groups together**; bleeding, length of stay and catheter time did not significantly differ | Luffarelli 2025<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Absorbable vs permanent suture** | Suture-material question rather than a stand-alone comparison of devices; check procedure groups and full text before assigning 12-month outcomes to Capio | Padoa 2023<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |

## Operative Time and Blood Loss in Comparative Cohorts

Two nonrandomized comparisons reported shorter operating time with Capio-associated techniques; differing exposure and concomitant procedures limit device-only inference:

| Metric | Capio | Traditional | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sacrospinous colpopexy time, isolated procedures** | **20.9 ± 3.3 min** | **39.1 ± 5.2 min** (p &lt; 0.001) | Leone Roberti Maggiore 2013, nonrandomized 86-patient comparison<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Total OR time** | **86 ± 17.4 min** | **98 ± 22.6 min** (p &lt; 0.001) | Luffarelli 2025, retrospective 163-patient combined cohort<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Intraoperative blood loss** | Lower in the 2013 comparison | **No significant group difference in the 2025 cohort** | Leone Roberti Maggiore 2013; Luffarelli 2025<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |

## Complications

| Complication | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Postoperative buttock pain** | **84% at one week; 16% at six weeks, 7% requiring analgesia** | Mowat's **51-patient prospective descriptive cohort**; these are cohort-specific observations, not universal Capio rates<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Gluteal / posterior-thigh pain vs anchor-based device (Anchorsure)** | **No significant difference** | Plair 2022 RCT — informs but does not resolve the suture-vs-anchor debate<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **Nerve injury** | **3.8% pooled across all studied methods**, not a Capio-specific incidence | Amiri 2024 abstract ranks Capio highest, but device-specific estimates and case mix require its full tables<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| **Blood transfusion / hematoma** | Possible SSLF complications | No Capio-specific rate can be inferred from the overall Amiri abstract<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |

The throw-catch mechanism does not eliminate neurovascular risk near the sacrospinous ligament. A **June 2024 manufacturer removal of specified Capio SLIM lots** cited failure to catch the dart; repeated attempts could lead to hemorrhage. The notice was **lot-specific**, not a withdrawal of all Capio devices. Check supplied product against current field notices and stop using affected lots.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Context Within POP Surgery

Capio can pass sutures for native-tissue repairs, but its use has also been described in other pelvic-floor operations; the instrument itself does not determine mesh use or the preferred prolapse operation. Procedure-level reviews should not be treated as Capio-versus-device trials. Adjacent tools include [Deschamps](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/deschamps-ligature-carrier), [Miya hook](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/miya-hook), [Anchorsure](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/anchorsure), [Saffron](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/saffron-fixation-system), [i-Stitch](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/i-stitch) and [Endostitch](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/endostitch).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Capio vs Adjacent SSLF Devices

| Device | Mechanism | Key trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **Capio / Capio SLIM** (Boston Scientific) | Suture-capturing throw-catch-retrieve | Shorter time in two nonrandomized comparisons; neurovascular risk and lot-specific 2024 dart-capture notice<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **[Deschamps ligature carrier](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/deschamps-ligature-carrier)** | Classic curved ligature-passing needle | Reusable, traditional; requires wider pararectal dissection |
| **[Miya hook](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/miya-hook)** | Curved suture-carrying needle | Historical workhorse; largely supplanted |
| **[Anchorsure](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/anchorsure)** (Neomedic / JUNE) | PEEK anchor in ligament substance | No through-pass; Plair 2022 RCT no pain difference vs Capio<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **[Saffron fixation system](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/saffron-fixation-system)** (Coloplast) | Anchor-based, two-piece reusable tool + single-use anchor | Newer anchor-based alternative |
| **[i-Stitch](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/i-stitch)** (A.M.I.) | Reusable instrument with single-use loading unit and blunt ball-shaped suture tip | Cadaveric vascular concern in thin ligaments; not a clinical comparative event rate |
| **[Endostitch](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/endostitch)** (Medtronic) | Laparoscopic automated suturing device | Repurposed for transvaginal SSLF |
