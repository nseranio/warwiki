# Uterine Manipulators

**Transvaginal instruments inserted through the cervix into the uterine cavity** to provide **mobilization, anteversion / retroversion, lateral deviation, and cephalic elevation** during gynecologic surgery; some models also delineate the vaginal fornices for colpotomy. Intra-abdominal graspers and myoma screws are alternatives, not transvaginal uterine-manipulator designs. Selection depends on the operation, anatomy and model-specific instructions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Despite their ubiquity, **there is no single "optimal" uterine manipulator** that is universally safe, efficient, and cost-effective across all procedures — the surgeon selects the manipulator that best suits the specific case.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Purpose and Rationale

- **Uterine mobilization** — multi-plane movement (anteversion, retroversion, lateral deviation, cephalic elevation) from below, providing dynamic exposure without additional laparoscopic instruments or extra ports.
- **Surgical exposure** — placing tension on the broad ligament, uterine vessels, cardinal and uterosacral ligaments, and peritoneal attachments to facilitate identification and dissection.
- **Vaginal-fornix delineation** — the cervical (colpotomy) cup delineates the fornices from below; provides a clear landmark for the laparoscopic colpotomy incision; maintains pneumoperitoneum after cuff opening.
- **Ureteral displacement** — in a nine-cadaver VCare Plus study, maximal cephalic elevation combined with anteversion or contralateral angulation increased measured separation at specified uterovaginal landmarks; this is an anatomic surrogate, **not a demonstrated reduction in ureteral injury** and not a universal distance for other devices or patients.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Pneumoperitoneum maintenance** — cervical cup or occluding balloon preserves visualization in the critical final steps of hysterectomy.

## Design Components

| Component | Function |
|---|---|
| **Intrauterine tip / obturator** | Rod or cannula through the cervical os into the uterine cavity — transmits manipulation forces. May incorporate a balloon for cavity occlusion / device fixation. |
| **Cervical (colpotomy) cup** | Rigid ring or disc sitting in the fornices; delineates the colpotomy line; available in small / medium / large sizes |
| **Handle / shaft** | External component the assistant holds; ergonomic pistol grips with articulating joints on the most refined designs |
| **Fixation mechanism** | Cervical-cup friction, intrauterine balloon, cervical tenaculum, or threaded cervical fixation |

## Approach and Classification

The literature documents at least **10 distinct uterine manipulators**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Broadly classified by approach:

| Class | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Vaginal (transvaginal)** | RUMI + Koh Colpotomizer, VCare / VCare Plus, Hohl, Clermont-Ferrand, Colpo-Probe | The dominant class for minimally invasive hysterectomy |
| **Intra-abdominal (manipulator-free)** | Myoma screw, Boztosun technique, tenaculum / grasper on the fundus | Avoids transvaginal instrumentation; potentially shorter operative time and lower cost<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |

## Major Named Systems

| System | Manufacturer | Defining feature |
|---|---|---|
| **RUMI / RUMI II + Koh Colpotomizer** | CooperSurgical (Hector Advincula design) | Hybrid (reusable handle + disposable components); balloon-fixed; pneumoperitoneum maintenance via Koh cup<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **VCare / VCare Plus** | CONMED | Single-use; cervical and separate vaginal sealing cups on the documented VCare configuration; VCare Plus was used in the Pan 2025 cadaver study. Check the exact generation's IFU.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Hohl** | KARL STORZ (Michael Hohl) | Reusable; tight-fitting cervical cup; **shorter insertion time (2 vs 6 min) vs Colpo-Probe** (Husslein 2017 RCT)<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **Clermont-Ferrand** | Université Clermont-Ferrand | Reusable European workhorse |
| **Colpo-Probe** | CooperSurgical | Loose-fitting; longer insertion time than Hohl in RCT<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **Multipurpose reusable stainless steel** | Various | Reusable; cross-procedure compatibility for supracervical hysterectomy / sacrocolpopexy / hysteropexy<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Myoma screw / Boztosun (intra-abdominal)** | Various | Manipulator-free intra-abdominal approach; shorter operative time<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |

## Reconstructive-Urology and Urogyn Uses

The uterine manipulator is **squarely in urogyn scope** — essential for the laparoscopic / robotic and open pelvic-reconstructive cases that intersect WARWIKI's core operative repertoire:

- **Laparoscopic / robotic sacrocolpopexy with concomitant hysterectomy** — manipulator delineates the vaginal apex for mesh fixation; mobilizes the uterus for the hysterectomy step.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Laparoscopic / robotic supracervical hysterectomy** — uterine mobilization without a colpotomy step; select a configuration indicated for this operation and protect the device from dissection energy.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Total laparoscopic hysterectomy (TLH)** as adjunct to pelvic reconstruction — the canonical use; provides exposure for colpotomy and pneumoperitoneum maintenance.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Laparoscopic / robotic hysteropexy** — uterine mobilization during a uterine-preserving suspension when trans-cervical placement is appropriate; after hysterectomy, vault prolapse repair instead requires a vaginal/apical delineator, **not an intrauterine tip**.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Endometriosis surgery during urogyn-adjunctive procedures** — exposure of the cul-de-sac, uterosacral ligaments, and rectovaginal septum during excision of deep infiltrating endometriosis encountered during pelvic-reconstructive cases.
- **Laparoscopic myomectomy** — uterine mobilization and exposure of myoma sites; myoma-screw alternative for intra-abdominal manipulation.
- **Total abdominal hysterectomy (TAH)** in selected benign cases — a review of four RCTs (n = 340) found shorter operative time, smaller hemoglobin reduction and greater postoperative vaginal length, but the authors noted high risk of bias from lack of blinding; these findings do not establish a universal requirement for a manipulator.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Environmental and Cost — Reusable vs Disposable

**Melnyk 2025** life-cycle assessment over 300 hysterectomies compared four manipulators (reusable stainless steel, two disposable, one hybrid):<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

- **Reusable stainless steel had the lowest carbon footprint** in every environmental category.
- **The two disposable models had global-warming emissions 2.39× and 4.24× higher** than the reusable model; the hybrid's estimate depended on reuse assumptions.
- Higher upfront cost but **$16,000–$43,000 institutional savings over 300 uses** for the reusable variant.

Practical implication for urogyn programs: a reusable model may reduce cost and modeled environmental impact at the study's use volume; **this analysis did not compare clinical performance**. Check local processing capacity, instrument life and procedure needs.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

The 2026 AUGS sustainability white paper, which shares an author with this analysis, lists reusable devices among suggested operating-room sustainability measures; its abstract names gowns, drapes and specula rather than manipulators.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

## The Endometrial-Cancer Manipulator-Safety Debate

Out of WARWIKI's primary scope (cancer surgery), but mentioned because it has spread to general urogyn awareness:

Concern: the intrauterine balloon / tip may disrupt tumor, forcing malignant cells through the fallopian tubes into the peritoneal cavity or LVSI. Evidence is **discordant**:

- **No statistically conclusive harm in some analyses:** Zorzato 2024 (14 studies, n = 5,019) estimated recurrence HR **1.52 (95% CI 0.99–2.33; p = .05)**, an imprecise borderline signal, not proof of safety; overall-survival HR 1.07 (95% CI 0.65–1.76). Scutiero 2022 and Uccella 2017 reported different null comparisons.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Possible harm** signals: Padilla-Iserte 2021 (n = 2,661) — higher recurrence (11.69% vs 7.4%).<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> Nagase 2026 (12 studies, n = 10,805) — DFS lower with manipulator (HR 1.18, 95% CI 1.01–1.38, p = 0.04); risk-of-bias caveats.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

**Current synthesis**: Crosbie 2022 *Lancet* review notes "discordant data" and positive peritoneal cytology of "uncertain clinical significance" since washings are no longer in FIGO staging.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> D'Indinosante 2026 recommends a **"selective, technique-conscious approach"** until definitive prospective data mature.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

These **tumor-dissemination and cancer-survival estimates** should not be extrapolated to a benign hysterectomy. An unexpected or suspected malignancy still requires an individualized oncologic plan; benign operative use does not eliminate the separate risks of perforation or bleeding.

## Manipulator-Free Techniques

For surgeons who prefer to avoid manipulators:

- **Manipulator-free TLH** — Erdem 2026 retrospective cohort (n = 244 manipulator vs 166 manipulator-free): **no significant differences** in operative time (79.6 vs 76.6 min), complications, or vaginal-cuff dehiscence. Manipulator-free TLH is safe in expert hands.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- **Myoma screw** — laparoscopic screw into uterine fundus for intra-abdominal manipulation; shorter operative time and lower cost vs vaginal manipulators.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Boztosun technique** — intra-abdominal Rein-technique modification; shorter operative and colpotomy times.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Complications

- **Uterine perforation / bleeding** — assess depth and direction with a separate sound before insertion; for example, the RUMI II handle IFU prohibits use in known/suspected pregnancy, an IUD in place or suspected pelvic infection, and the VCare IFU cautions against use if the cavity sounds under 4 cm or in a large postpartum uterus. These are **model-specific instructions**, not blanket contraindications for every design.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- **Cervical laceration** — forceful manipulation or cervical stenosis / atrophy.
- **Vaginal-cuff injury / dehiscence after hysterectomy** — a complication of the operation and closure, not a proven device-specific incidence attributable to manipulator selection; take care with colpotomy energy and cuff closure.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[18]](#ref18)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- **False passage** through cervical stroma in cervical stenosis.

## Technique

1. **Assess cervical access for the selected model**: the RUMI II handle IFU specifies dilation to Hegar/Hank 8; the documented VCare IFU calls for dilation **if necessary** to admit its 5-mm tube. Do not apply one dilation rule to all devices.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
2. **Sound the uterus separately** to determine cavity direction and depth; choose a tip no longer than the sounded depth for RUMI II, and follow the selected model's insertion and balloon instructions. RUMI II's tip is single-use; VCare's balloon uses **7–10 mL air, not saline**. Stop manipulation if the VCare balloon ruptures.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
3. **Position for the operative landmark** — cephalic elevation with anteversion or contralateral angulation increased measured ureteral separation in the VCare Plus cadaver experiment; visualize the ureter directly when risk warrants rather than treating a cadaveric distance as clinical protection.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
4. **Coordinate with assistant** — surgeon at the console directs specific manipulator movements ("antevert," "push cephalad," "deviate left") for each surgical step.
5. **Cup-size selection** by cervical/fornix fit per the chosen IFU; the cadaveric cup-size comparison does not establish that cup size is irrelevant to patient safety.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
6. **Minimize colpotomy thermal spread** — use the lowest effective energy settings to reduce vaginal-cuff thermal injury and dehiscence risk.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
7. **Consider manipulator-free** in oncologic cases of concern and in surgeons confident with intra-abdominal techniques.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

## Limitations

- **Requires an assistant** to control the vaginal handle.
- **No single optimal manipulator** for all procedures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Endometrial-cancer oncologic uncertainty** (out of WARWIKI scope but relevant to multidisciplinary practice).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Risk of uterine perforation, cervical laceration, false passage.**
- **Disposable-cost and environmental impact** — favor reusable systems where operative performance allows.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Cervical access limitations** — severe stenosis or absent cervix can preclude trans-cervical insertion; a prior trachelectomy requires anatomy-specific assessment. A vaginal delineator or intra-abdominal alternative may still be usable, depending on the operation and its own instructions.
