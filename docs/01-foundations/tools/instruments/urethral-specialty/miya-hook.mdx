# Miya Hook Ligature Carrier

The **Miya Hook** is a reusable ligature carrier described by **Frank S. Miyazaki in 1987** for transvaginal sacrospinous ligament fixation (SSLF). The described technique guides the hook by palpation through the coccygeus–sacrospinous complex. Historical clinical series and cadaver studies describe its use, but do not establish that it has the deepest evidence base or strongest patient fixation of all SSLF instruments.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Design

- **Long curved metal shaft** matching the sacrospinous-ligament approach angle
- **Hook-shaped distal end** with eye for suture engagement
- **Reusable** — follow the specific model's processing instructions
- **No power source / no integrated capture mechanism** — operator-driven mechanic

Miyazaki described the close anatomical relationship of the coccygeus muscle and sacrospinous ligament; later techniques still require attention to the adjacent neurovascular structures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Technique

### Original Miyazaki Technique

1. Thread suture through the hook eye.
2. Advance the hook into the paravaginal space under two-finger palpation; tip onto the sacrospinous ligament ~ 2 fingerbreadths medial to the ischial spine.
3. Drive the hook through the ligament complex and back out.
4. Disengage suture on the far side; retrieve.
5. Withdraw the hook, leaving the suture through the ligament.

### Mattox 1995 Modification

Mattox and colleagues modified the technique to **pass the hook unloaded first, then back-thread the hook with suture and withdraw**. Reported faster, fewer instruments required, no suture fraying, no complications in > 50 patients.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Clinical Evidence

| Series | Design | Key Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Miyazaki 1987 original** | 74 procedures over 24 mo, chiefly performed by residents in training | **No hemorrhages, no nerve injury, no rectal injury**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Hardiman & Drutz 1996** | 130 attempted vault suspensions; 125 completed (5 abandoned for technical difficulty); 6 mo–5 yr follow-up | **Recurrent vault prolapse 2.4%** and febrile morbidity 10% in a procedure series with concomitant operations; not isolated hook effects<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Pollak 2007 comparative** | Retrospective 240 pts — direct-vision needle driver (n=46) vs Deschamps (n=173) vs Miya Hook (n=21) | **No significant difference in intraoperative complications** across the three; Deschamps had significantly higher postoperative complications vs direct-vision (18% vs 2%, p=0.002)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |

## Biomechanical Pull-Out — Cadaveric Samples

**Lo 2026** measured pull-out forces in eight cadavers. Anchorsure SSL fixation was tested in **six cadavers** and Miya Hook SSL fixation in **two different cadavers**, so this was **not a paired head-to-head comparison**. Tissue and experimental conditions may account for some difference; no patient durability follows from these loads.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

| Device | Mean load at failure |
|---|---|
| **SSLF with Miya Hook** | **69.2 ± 5.9 N** |
| **SSLF with Anchorsure** | **44.0 ± 15.9 N** |

The study discusses tissue engagement as a possible explanation; it does not demonstrate clinical superiority of one device.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Vascular Safety — Manning 2014 Intermediate Position

In **Manning 2014's cadaveric thin-ligament model**, the Miya Hook impinged but deflected vessels behind the ligament. This is an observed trajectory, not a clinical vascular-injury rate.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

| Safety class | Devices |
|---|---|
| Remained within ligament in this model | Capio, Caspari, Endostitch |
| Impinged but deflected vessels in this model | **Miya Hook**, Deschamps |
| Penetrated coccygeal branches in this model | i-Stitch |

Manning's authors considered reusable carriers a possible option where cost matters; clinical risk and procurement cost require separate assessment.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Advantages and Limitations

| Dimension | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Cost** | Reusable; actual costs depend on instrument and processing |
| **Biomechanical fixation** | 69.2 N mean in two cadavers in Lo 2026; not a cross-device or patient ranking<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Clinical track record** | Historical procedure series, including Miyazaki, Hardiman and Pollak<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Cadaveric vessel trajectory** | Impinged but deflected vessels in Manning's thin-ligament model<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Visualization** | Palpation-guided only; no direct visualization |
| **Dissection required** | Depends on approach and exposure; no quantified comparative measure here |
| **Resource setting** | Reusability may be useful where supply cost constrains options<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |

## Position in the SSLF Device Family

| Device | Mechanism | What the cited evidence actually measures |
|---|---|---|
| **Miya Hook** | Palpation-guided carrier | 69.2 ± 5.9 N in two cadavers in Lo; vessel deflection in Manning cadavers<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **[Deschamps ligature carrier](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/deschamps-ligature-carrier)** | Curved carrier | Vessel deflection in Manning cadavers<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **[Capio](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/capio-device)**, **[Endostitch](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/endostitch)** | Suture capture | Remained within ligament in Manning cadavers; not a patient safety ranking<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **[Anchorsure](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/anchorsure)** | Permanent anchor | 44.0 ± 15.9 N in six different cadavers in Lo; a mixed-tacker cohort's 2.2% is not Anchorsure-specific<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **[Saffron](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/saffron-fixation-system)** | Permanent anchor | 17.9 ± 5.6 N in a separate cadaver study, not comparable with Lo's loads |
| **[i-Stitch](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/i-stitch)** | Suture capture | Coccygeal-branch penetration in Manning cadavers<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |

## Bottom Line

The Miya Hook occupies an important historical and practical niche:

- A historically important reusable SSLF carrier with published procedure series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Lo's cadaveric loads differed between Miya and Anchorsure samples, but were measured in different cadavers and cannot rank clinical durability.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Reusability can matter where cost constrains device choice; Manning's anatomical findings should not be converted into patient injury rates.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

Amiri 2024's pooled adverse-event results are across techniques; its abstract does not provide a Miya-specific clinical risk rate.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
