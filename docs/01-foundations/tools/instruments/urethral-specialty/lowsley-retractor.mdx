# Lowsley Retractor (Prostatic Tractor)

The **Lowsley retractor**, also called the **Lowsley prostatic tractor**, is a transurethrally passed instrument with **hinged distal jaws** that can grasp a catheter or traction suture. Developed in the era of perineal prostatectomy, it has documented roles in **inside-out suprapubic-tube placement** in selected patients with transurethral access and **posterior vesicourethral-anastomotic traction** in a laparoscopic-prostatectomy technical report. Neither role establishes superiority over alternative approaches in a scarred abdomen.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Design

- **Long curved shaft** matching the male urethral curve.
- **Two hinged wings at the distal tip** that lie flush during transurethral passage and **open / close via the proximal handle mechanism**.
- **Reusable metal instrument** in documented operative practice; confirm the specific manufacturer's construction, cleaning/sterilization and jaw-control instructions before use. For example, Avalign's Lowsley IFU specifies flush-port/lumen cleaning and advises against ultrasonic cleaning of that device; it does not govern other models.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Proximal jaw-control handle**, with variant details dependent on the available model.

## Reconstructive-Urology Uses

### Lowsley-Assisted Suprapubic Cystostomy

In the described **inside-out cutdown**, the retractor is passed transurethrally into the bladder with its jaws closed, the tip is brought against the anterior bladder/abdominal wall, and a cutdown exposes the palpable tip. A catheter is **secured in the opened jaws** and drawn back **into the bladder**, where its balloon is inflated; the catheter is released and the retractor alone withdrawn through the urethra. Confirm the balloon is intravesical. This maneuver does **not** itself rule out interposed bowel.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

**Why this matters reconstructively**:

- **Neurogenic-bladder patients with suitable urethral access** — Edokpolo and Foster reported 49 primary placements in 44 people; operative time **20.2 ± 5.5 min was documented for only 19 cases**, with no intraoperative complications or misplaced catheters in that selected retrospective series and one next-day significant hematuria. This is not a comparative bowel-safety trial.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Prior lower-abdominal surgery / possible interposed bowel** — the palpable tip does **not** eliminate bowel-injury risk. Review operative history and anatomy and consider contemporaneous ultrasound by a trained operator or open exposure according to the revised BAUS guideline; avoid treating a hostile abdomen as an automatic indication for Lowsley.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Poorly distensible bladder** — direct exposure or image-guided planning may help when a trocar corridor is unsafe; a Lowsley cutdown still needs a safe path and adequate transurethral passage.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

See [Suprapubic Tube / Suprapubic Cystostomy](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/urinary-catheters/suprapubic-catheter) for the catheter side of the procedure, and [Bladder Scanner](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/bladder-scanner) for noninvasive volume assessment. A scanner number alone does not establish a safe catheter track.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Vesicourethral-Anastomotic Tension Reduction

In the **laparoscopic radical prostatectomy technique report**, a Lowsley is passed through the urethral stump. A posterior bladder-neck traction stitch is grasped with its jaws and pulled toward the urethra while the 6-o'clock anastomotic suture is tied; 4- and 8-o'clock stitches are placed before release. The authors propose that this shifts traction off the posterior urethra and reduces tearing/gaps, but the paper is a **technical description, not comparative outcome proof**.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

Application to **redo bladder-neck reconstruction** or staged repair of membranous urethral disruption requires its own anatomy- and tissue-specific plan; Garrett's initial prostatectomy description does not validate these extensions.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### Other Reconstructive-Adjacent Uses

- **Transvesical exposure and tactile localization** may be possible in selected fistula/diverticulum repairs or planned open SPT, but no outcome source for these uses was found in the inherited references; confirm the specific operation and safe anatomy rather than relying on the instrument to exclude bowel.

## Historical Context — Perineal Prostatectomy

The Lowsley tractor is associated with **Oswald Swinney Lowsley (1884–1955)** and the historical perineal prostatectomy, passed transurethrally to help expose the prostate in the perineal field.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Retropubic, laparoscopic and robotic approaches have displaced much of that practice. The same jaw-control and tactile-localization principles explain its later **selected** SPT and traction uses, not a unique or universally safer indication.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Technique Pearls — Lowsley-Assisted SPT

1. **Plan a safe route** after reviewing lower-abdominal operations, bleeding risk and possible interposed bowel; image or expose the path when indicated. Do not assume a palpable tip makes blind incision safe.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
2. **Pass with jaws closed** only when urethral passage is safe and atraumatic; verify intravesical position by the operative method, not an assumed lumen/urine-return feature of every Lowsley model.
3. **Press the curved tip against the anterior bladder wall** and identify it through a deliberate suprapubic cutdown, protecting intervening structures. If the tip or pathway cannot be confirmed, change the approach.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
4. **Secure the catheter tip in the opened jaws** before controlled withdrawal of the retractor **into the bladder**; check for slippage. Inflate the balloon intravesically, release the catheter and remove the retractor per urethra.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
5. **Confirm intravesical catheter/balloon position**, with cystoscopy when feasible, before securing; monitor for bleeding or signs of visceral injury.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Safety and Contraindications

- **Inability to pass the retractor safely per urethra** — stricture, false passage or suspected disruption calls for another drainage route; an SPT itself may be appropriate in urethral trauma, but the transurethral Lowsley path is not.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Active urethral infection or recent urethral instrumentation** with mucosal trauma — risk of false-passage creation by the rigid instrument.
- **Bleeding disorder/anticoagulation** — evaluate and manage under the patient's anticoagulation and procedural policy; the BAUS SPC guideline lists uncorrected bleeding disorder/anticoagulation among contraindications but does not provide an automatic reversal order for all patients.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **NLUTD bladder and altered abdominal anatomy** — assess bladder distension, autonomic-dysreflexia risk when relevant, interposed bowel, instrument reach and catheter position; cystoscopy alone cannot exclude intervening bowel.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Lowsley vs Alternatives for Suprapubic-Tube Placement

| Technique | Best fit | Bowel-injury risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Lowsley-assisted cutdown** | Selected patient with safe transurethral passage and confirmed path; permits larger catheter<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | Bowel injury remains possible; no comparative rate established |
| **Percutaneous trocar / Seldinger** | Distensible palpable bladder and no high-risk track, with trained operator<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | Historical SPC series report **~2.4–2.7%** bowel injury across techniques, not a device-specific blind-route probability<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **Ultrasound-guided percutaneous** | Imaging by a trained operator when the bladder is not palpable or the track is uncertain<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | Helps identify a safe pathway; comparative safety benefit is not formally established |
| **Open cystostomy** | Interposed bowel/high-risk corridor or concomitant open operation<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | Direct visualization may mitigate injury but does not eliminate risk; wound/pain morbidity is greater |
