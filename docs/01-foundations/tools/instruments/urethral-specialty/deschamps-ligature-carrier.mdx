# Deschamps Ligature Carrier

The **Deschamps ligature carrier** is a reusable, curved suture-passing instrument used for transvaginal sacrospinous ligament fixation (SSLF). Named for the French surgeon Joseph-François-Louis Deschamps, it can pass suture through the coccygeus–sacrospinous complex using palpation when direct visualization is limited. Comparative series report slower placement than the particular automatic instruments tested; a later meta-analysis flagged a transfusion signal for Deschamps and modified Deschamps, but did not establish that the carrier itself caused bleeding.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Design

- **Right- and left-curved variants** — allow either-side approach depending on working angle
- **Curved blunt-tipped needle** with **eye near the tip** for suture engagement
- **Long handle** for deep pelvic / paravaginal approach
- **Reusable metal construction** — follow the specific instrument's processing instructions
- **Multiple sizes** — smaller for vaginal / pelvic work, larger for general-surgery pedicle ligation

## Technique

1. Suture threaded through the tip eye.
2. Surgeon palpates the sacrospinous ligament (~ 2 fingerbreadths medial to the ischial spine).
3. Carrier advanced through the coccygeus / sacrospinous complex with the curve matching the surrounding anatomy.
4. Suture disengaged from the eye on the far side, retrieved.
5. Carrier withdrawn, leaving the suture through the ligament.

This described passage is palpation-guided; exposure, the selected bite, and safe distance from the ischial spine remain important regardless of carrier.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Suture-Placement Time

In the Aksakal 2007 and Doğanay 2013 comparisons, reported suturing times were approximately **775–795 seconds with Deschamps** versus **47–49 seconds with the automatic instruments used in those studies**. These are study-specific procedural measurements, not universal per-stitch times or comparisons with every modern device.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Clinical Evidence

| Series | Design | Key Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Doğanay & Aksakal 2013** | Large prospective randomized, 1,464 pts over 10 yr — Deschamps n=762 vs automatic suturing instrument n=702 | Deschamps had **significantly longer suturing time, greater postoperative analgesic requirement, and longer hospital stay** (all p &lt; 0.05)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Pollak 2007 comparative** | Retrospective 240 pts — Deschamps (173) vs direct-vision needle driver (46) vs [Miya Hook](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/miya-hook) (21) | **No significant difference in intraoperative complications**; **Deschamps had significantly higher postoperative complications vs direct visualization (18% vs 2%, p=0.002)**<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Chopra 2019 Henry Ford** | 34 pts unilateral SSLF with Deschamps | Median preoperative POP-Q stage 3 improved to **postoperative stage 0** (p &lt; 0.05)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |

## Complication Profile — Amiri 2024 Meta-Analysis Signal

The **Amiri 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis** included **125 studies / 10,216 cases**. Its abstract ranks Deschamps and modified Deschamps highest for transfusion among the compared techniques. The pooled percentages below are **across devices**, not Deschamps-specific rates; full device tables and confounding assessment remain to be checked.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

| Pooled metric | Result |
|---|---|
| Overall reoperation rate | 1.2% |
| Overall nerve-injury rate | 3.8% across included studies; the abstract ranks Capio highest but gives no Capio-specific rate |
| Overall hematoma rate | 1.7% (highest with Laurus / Raz anchoring) |
| **Blood-transfusion rate** | **Highest with Modified Deschamps and Deschamps** |

This association does not demonstrate that longer placement time causes vessel impingement or transfusion.

## Vascular Safety — Manning 2014 Intermediate Position

In **Manning 2014's cadaveric comparison**, Deschamps and Miya impinged but deflected vessels behind thin ligaments. These observed trajectories are not patient complication rates or a validated clinical safety ranking.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

| Safety class | Devices |
|---|---|
| Remained within ligament in this model | [Capio](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/capio-device), Caspari, [Endostitch](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/endostitch) |
| Impinged but deflected vessels in this model | **Deschamps**, [Miya Hook](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/miya-hook) |
| Penetrated coccygeal branches in this model | [i-Stitch](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/i-stitch) |

Manning's authors considered reusable carriers a possible option where cost matters; actual instrument availability, processing and local technique determine that trade-off.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Related Device — Veronikis Ligature Carrier (VLC)

The **Veronikis Ligature Carrier (VLC)** was described by **Veronikis and Nichols (1997)** for transvaginal sacrospinous colpopexy:<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

- **Permits bite acquisition under direct surgical visualization** within the pararectal space
- Allows ample penetration through the coccygeus muscle **without restriction of suture type, size, or number**
- Original 71-pt series: **no operative complications**<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Long-term procedure-cohort outcomes** (Wu 2020, **n = 453**, often with concomitant repairs): objective cure **82.5%**; anterior recurrence **12.6%**. These are not isolated device effects.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- Chou 2010 clinical-outcomes confirmation in transvaginal SSLF with the VLC<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

The VLC provides a direct-vision carrier option; these series do not prove superiority over all other carriers.

## Other Reconstructive / General-Surgery Uses

- **Uterosacral ligament suspension** via the Deschamps technique
- **Deep pedicle ligation** in complex pelvic dissection
- **Pedicle encirclement** during hysterectomy or complex pelvic reconstruction when a right-angle clamp is too bulky

## Advantages and Limitations

| Dimension | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Cost** | Reusable carrier; actual cost depends on model and reprocessing |
| **Suture placement time** | Approximately 13 minutes versus under 1 minute for the tested automatic instruments in two studies; do not generalize to every device<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Postoperative complications** | 18% versus 2% for direct vision in Pollak's retrospective comparison; association, not proof of device causation<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Blood-transfusion signal** | Ranked high for Deschamps/modified Deschamps in Amiri's abstract; device-specific absolute rate not verified<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Cadaveric vessel trajectory** | Impinged but deflected vessels in Manning's thin-ligament model<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Visualization** | Palpation only — no direct vision |
| **Clinical track record** | Multiple clinical series; no verified all-device volume ranking |
| **Resource setting** | Reusability may help where supply costs constrain options<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |

## Position in the SSLF Device Family

| Device | Type | Relevant comparison | Evidence limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Deschamps** | Ligature carrier | Slower than tested automatic instruments | Transfusion rank from meta-analysis abstract; no verified absolute device rate<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **[Miya Hook](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/miya-hook)** | Ligature carrier | Vessel deflection in Manning cadavers | Not a clinical injury rate<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Veronikis (VLC)** | Direct-vision carrier | 71-patient initial series | No comparative device-superiority trial here<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **[Capio](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/capio-device)**, Caspari, **[Endostitch](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/endostitch)** | Suture-capturing | Stayed within ligament in Manning cadavers | The 3.8% pooled nerve-injury figure is not a Capio rate<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **[i-Stitch](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/i-stitch)** | Suture-capturing | Coccygeal-branch penetration in Manning cadavers | Cannot infer patient vascular-injury incidence<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **[Anchorsure](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/anchorsure)**, **[Saffron](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/saffron-fixation-system)** | Permanent anchors | Distinct fixation mechanism | Mixed-tacker dislodgement is not an Anchorsure-specific rate |

## Bottom Line

Deschamps remains a reusable palpation-guided option. In specific comparative cohorts it took longer to place sutures than automatic instruments, while a retrospective series associated it with more postoperative complications than direct vision. Amiri's abstract raises a transfusion signal but does not establish causation or a verified device-specific rate. The direct-vision VLC is another carrier option; choice should reflect exposure, anatomy, training, processing and available instruments.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
