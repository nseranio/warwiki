# Sacrocolpopexy Tacker

**Helical titanium tackers** are one method of securing a sacrocolpopexy mesh to the anterior longitudinal ligament at the sacral promontory; sutures are another. Devices used to fix the mesh to the vagina or cervix are anatomically distinct, and their measured strength or time cannot be assigned to sacral fixation. Choice of device, fixation site and depth must be individualized by an operator familiar with the local vascular and spinal anatomy.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

For broader descriptions of laparoscopic sacrocolpopexy technique and imaging, see references 1 and 2; neither provides the fixation-strength comparison below.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Tacker Family

| Device | Construction | Resorbable? | Attachment studied here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ProTack (Medtronic/Covidien)** | Helical titanium | **Non-absorbable** | Studied for sacral PVDF-tape fixation **and separately** for cervical PVDF-tape attachment; these are different experiments<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **AbsorbaTack (Medtronic/Covidien)** | Absorbable helical fastener | **Absorbable** | The cited 15 ± 8 N experiment is **cervical** tape attachment, not sacral fixation<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Barbed/absorbable mesh anchor** | Single-fire anchor | Device-specific | Berger studied **vaginal** mesh attachment, not the sacral anchor<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Suture** | Material and technique vary | Material-dependent | Comparators in distinct sacral and cervical experiments<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |

**Keep the sites separate.** Sebastian et al. tested PVDF-tape attachment to 28 fresh porcine cervices: sutures 64 ± 15 N (n=10), ProTack 41 ± 10 N (n=10), AbsorbaTack 15 ± 8 N (n=8), with fastener failure in all eight AbsorbaTack tests. These figures do not measure sacral-promontory fixation.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Jansen et al. separately tested PVDF tape on porcine sacral anterior longitudinal ligament: two sutures 65 ± 12 N, three titanium tacks in a row 25 ± 10 N, and three in a triangle 38 ± 12 N (n=10 each). Mesh failure was common in all three groups. Neither bench model establishes clinical recurrence or pain differences.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Reconstructive-Urology and Urogyn Uses

### Sacral-promontory fixation in laparoscopic / robotic sacrocolpopexy

- Yoshizawa 2021, a retrospective 77-patient comparison, studied sutures plus tackers (n=41) against sutures alone (n=36), not tacker-only fixation. The combination group had shorter mean operative time (104.9 ± 27.0 versus 147.5 ± 33.7 minutes); one-year anatomical success was 100% versus 94.4% (p=0.2153). The latter does not demonstrate tacker-only equivalence or a long-term durability advantage.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Vieillefosse 2015, a case-control series (35 tacker versus 65 suture patients), reported higher postoperative low-back-pain intensity in the tacker group (median VAS 4 versus 0, p=0.01); the difference in de novo pain incidence (50% versus 25%, p=0.11) and operative time (240 versus 210 minutes, p=0.18) were not statistically significant. An association does not establish that tack penetration caused pain.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### Vaginal-mesh attachment (anchor variants)

- Berger 2020 RCT (robotic sacrocolpopexy) compared anchors with sutures for vaginal mesh attachment; that step was faster with anchors (12.2 versus 21.2 minutes), whereas total sacrocolpopexy time did not differ (107.6 versus 109.8 minutes). This is not a trial of sacral tackers.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

### Interpreting the evidence

- The separate porcine sacral test favors two sutures over three titanium tacks in load to failure for one PVDF tape construct, but not every mesh/device pairing. Retrospective clinical comparisons are heterogeneous and cannot establish a universal fixation hierarchy.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- Moroni 2018 examined technical descriptions in 22 randomized trials and found substantial heterogeneity; it did not conduct a tacker-versus-suture efficacy or lumbar-pain meta-analysis. The full article's granular fixation counts remain to be reconciled; do not treat a proposed 11/18/4/3 breakdown as a verified worldwide practice rate.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Tacker vs Suture — The Decision

| Feature | **Helical tacker (ProTack)** | **Permanent suture** |
|---|---|---|
| **Sacral bench strength** | Three ProTack tacks: 25 ± 10 N (row) or 38 ± 12 N (triangle) | Two sutures: 65 ± 12 N, **same porcine PVDF-tape study**<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Cervical bench strength** | ProTack 41 ± 10 N; AbsorbaTack 15 ± 8 N | 64 ± 15 N, **different cervical experiment**<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Operative time** | Shorter in one **combination** cohort; not in the tacker-only case-control series | Comparator differed by cohort<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **One-year anatomy** | 100% in a combination cohort; not tacker-only equivalence | 94.4% in suture-only comparison, p=0.2153<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Lumbar pain intensity** | Higher median VAS in one case-control series | Lower median; causal mechanism not established<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **QoL** | Worse in the Vieillefosse comparison | Observational result, not universal prediction<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Spondylodiscitis risk** | Possible (case reports) | Possible (case reports) |

There is no Moroni systematic-review verdict of equivalence or a proven tacker-caused pain penalty; discuss site, material, surgeon technique and patient-specific risk rather than extrapolating across these studies.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Safety Profile

### Spondylodiscitis / lumbosacral osteomyelitis

A rare but devastating complication of any sacral fixation (suture or tack):

- Antosh 2024 reported three institutional cases and reviewed 30 additional published patients. Back pain, elevated ESR/CRP and variable timing were reported; in the reviewed cases, the majority required mesh excision and extended IV antibiotics. This complication has been described after different routes and both sutures and tackers, not proved specific to a device.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Propst 2014 review.** Pyogenic spondylodiscitis after sacral colpopexy and rectopexy; same management principle.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

### Vascular injury at the promontory

- Identify the promontory, anterior longitudinal ligament, nearby middle sacral vessels or presacral veins, ureter, iliac vessels and disc level before fixation. Avoid assuming a spine-screw study defines a validated tacker-safe zone or device-specific penetration depth.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

### Periosteal lumbar pain

- Vieillefosse observed more intense low-back pain in a tacker group, but did not demonstrate a periosteal mechanism.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### Other

- **Mesh exposure and fastener displacement** require attention to operative site and device instructions; the sources above do not justify a universal comparative rate.

## Emerging Alternatives

- **Cyanoacrylate glue for vaginal or anterior mesh attachment.** Not promontory tacking: a 54-patient RCT reported shorter anterior fixation time (4.6 versus 25.4 minutes) but no significant difference in total operative time or 24-month anatomical outcomes. It cannot be used to infer sacral fixation efficacy.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Technique Considerations

- Identify the promontory and expose the anterior longitudinal ligament while respecting the vessels, ureter and disc. Confirm an appropriate device indication and fixation depth in its current IFU, which is not established by the studies summarized here.
- Secure mesh without excess tension, inspect attachment, and cover the mesh with peritoneum according to the operative plan. The precise number or pattern of tacks varies; three-tack row/triangle configurations were bench-test constructs, not universal clinical instructions.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- If combining sutures with tackers, follow the actual operative method and material instructions; Yoshizawa compares combinations but does not validate a universal "one primary suture" rule.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Limitations

- Cost, pain and operative time vary by device and setting; a porcine pull-test is not a clinical selection rule. The cited absorbable-fastener experiment was at the cervix, and it does not establish when an absorbable device should be used at the promontory.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Comparative long-term recurrence, device-specific safety and the current promontory-use IFUs remain to be reconciled. Do not substitute this summary for a product's indications and operative training.
