# i-Stitch®

The **i-Stitch®** (Agency for Medical Innovations, A.M.I., Austria) is a reusable instrument with a single-use suture loading unit for pelvic-floor suspension sutures, including **sacrospinous ligament fixation (SSLF)**. The manufacturer's loading unit uses a needle slider and specially formed blunt, ball-shaped suture tip; the surgeon guides the instrument along a finger to the selected attachment point. A small cadaveric comparison identified possible vascular penetration behind thin ligaments, while a separate pilot has described pairing i-Stitch with endoscopic visual guidance. Neither establishes comparative clinical safety.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Design

- **Reusable instrument**, supplied with cleaning accessories, plus single-use loading unit; follow current model-specific processing instructions.
- **Needle slider with integrated suture clamp** and blunt ball-shaped suture tip. The loading unit contains a hollow needle slider: do not describe the whole system as needle-free.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **i-Stitch** has a caudally directed tip for Level I/II placement; **i-Stitch up** has a cranially directed tip for Level III. Manufacturer-listed loading units offer absorbable and nonabsorbable suture options.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Can be used for native-tissue repair or mesh placement. A.M.I. also describes pairing it with its BSC Mesh and InGYNious systems; this is product-use context, not evidence that mesh is required or superior.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Technique

1. Secure the suture in the suture clamp of the i-Stitch loading unit
2. Load the i-Stitch with the loading unit
3. **Guide the tip along the surgeon's finger** to the intended sacrospinous-ligament attachment point
4. Push the ball tip into tissue
5. Push the loading unit forward so the suture penetrates the intended tissue.
6. Release the suture from the clamp and remove the loading unit; carefully retrieve the instrument while leaving the suture in place.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Vascular Safety Concern — Manning 2014 Cadaveric Comparative Study

**Manning and Arnold (2014)** compared six sacrospinous suture devices in five preserved pelves/hemipelves with vessels attached. These are anatomy or simulation findings, not clinical injury rates:<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- **The i-Stitch was the only device observed to penetrate coccygeal branches of the inferior gluteal artery.**
- The Capio, Caspari, and Endostitch remained confined within the ligament even in thin specimens.
- The authors cautioned against i-Stitch in thin ligaments and noted that ligament thickness and vascular course vary; age alone does not supply an individual patient's ligament measurement.

The authors favored a compact device taking a shallow bite without penetrating behind the ligament; Capio and Caspari best satisfied their cadaveric criterion. Even a location two fingerbreadths medial to the ischial spine did not guarantee avoidance of vessels.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Novel Application — Visual Guidance with Endoscopy

Chene and colleagues described combining i-Stitch with a 1.9 mm NanoNeedle™ / NanoScope™ (Arthrex) for endoscopic landmark visualization during sacrospinous fixation. Visualizing the landmarks is technically plausible, but whether this prevents the behind-ligament vascular penetration seen in Manning's cadavers has not been shown in a comparative clinical study.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

| Study | Design | Key Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Chene 2024 anatomical** | 3 female cadavers, bilateral fixation with visual guidance | Ischial spine and SSL could be identified; feasibility does not establish reduced clinical injury<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Chene 2025 online / 2026 issue pilot** | **15 patients** with apical prolapse, guided anterior or posterior fixation | No complications or postoperative pain reported; **87% complete correction at 12 months** in a small uncontrolled series; rare harms and comparative benefit remain unknown<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |

This remains a technique-development pathway; a 15-person uncontrolled series cannot establish superiority to palpation-guided SSLF or a quantified vascular-risk reduction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Reconstructive / Urogyn Uses

- **Bilateral sacrospinous ligament fixation (SSLF).** Native-tissue or selected mesh-assisted approach; manufacturer describes BSC Mesh as one optional pairing
- **Apical vaginal vault suspension** (post-hysterectomy vault prolapse)
- **Uterovaginal prolapse repair**
- **Anterior and posterior SSLF** in the visual-guidance pilot (Chene, online 2025)

## SSLF Device Landscape — Safety Profile Comparison

| Device | Type | Key Manning 2014 / contemporary safety finding | Visual guidance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **i-Stitch** | Suture-placement instrument | Penetrated coccygeal vessels in Manning's preserved specimens | Described with NanoScope in one feasibility pilot |
| **[Capio](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/capio-device)** | Suture-capturing | Remained within ligament in Manning's specimens; Amiri's **3.8% is overall nerve injury**, not a Capio rate<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | Not compared in Chene pilot |
| **Caspari** | Suture-capturing | Remained within ligament in Manning's specimens | Not compared |
| **[Endostitch](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/endostitch)** | Suture-capturing | Remained within ligament in Manning's specimens | Not compared |
| **[Anchorsure](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/anchorsure)** | Anchor-based | Lo's 676 cases used several tacker systems, only 19 Anchorsure alone; **no Anchorsure-specific dislodgement rate**<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | Not compared |
| **[Deschamps ligature carrier](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/deschamps-ligature-carrier)** | Suture-passing | Impinged but deflected vessels in Manning's specimens | Not compared |

## Bottom Line

The i-Stitch has a mixed safety profile:

- **Cadaveric data raise vascular-penetration concern in thin ligaments** (Manning 2014).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Pairing with endoscopic visual guidance** demonstrates feasibility of landmark visualization but has not been shown to prevent vascular injury (Chene 2024 and online 2025).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Clinical evidence remains very limited.** A single 15-patient pilot study for the visual-guidance combination; larger comparative trials are required before the safety advantage translates into a clinical recommendation.

When selecting a device, account for patient-specific ligament and vascular anatomy and the Manning cadaveric warning. Neither that anatomical comparison nor the visual-guidance pilot establishes a clinically safer instrument or preferred approach.
