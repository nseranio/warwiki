# Saffron™ Fixation System

The **Saffron™ Fixation System** (**Coloplast**) attaches suture to pelvic-floor ligaments using a permanently implanted polysulfone anchor. It received US FDA **510(k) clearance K220420** for that indication, with Anchorsure as the predicate. Clearance was based on nonclinical substantial-equivalence testing, not a comparative patient-outcomes trial. The device may be used for sacrospinous ligament fixation (SSLF) in apical prolapse repair.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Design

- **Two-piece system**:
  1. **Saffron tool** — sterile, **single-use disposable delivery tool** that may place multiple anchors **in one patient's procedure**; do not reprocess between cases
  2. **Saffron anchors** — polysulfone, single-use, permanently implanted; sold separately in boxes of 12
- **Narrow distal tip** for clean entry into the dissection plane and accurate anchor placement
- **Curved body** matching the approach angle to the sacrospinous ligament
- **Suture compatibility:** most commercially available sutures up to size 0, **excluding barbed sutures**, per the current manufacturer page<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Evidence — Cadaveric Study and Regulatory Testing

The cited Saffron-specific outcome study (van Raalte 2023) is **cadaveric**. The FDA clearance summary describes bench, usability and cadaver work, and explicitly says **no animal or clinical testing was submitted for substantial equivalence**. Neither source demonstrates improved patient outcomes or clinical neurovascular safety versus other devices.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

| Endpoint | Result |
|---|---|
| **Anchor placement accuracy** | **19/20 anchors (95%) correctly placed**, by independent non-implanting-surgeon assessment |
| **Neurovascular distance** | >10 mm for measured nerves and vessels **in one dissected cadaver**, not a patient safety margin |
| **Pull-out force** | **17.9 ± 5.6 N** across 18 tested anchors in 3 other cadavers; manual pull rate not controlled |
| **Study design** | 4 previously frozen cadavers; 3 for placement/force and 1 for anatomical distances; no clinical follow-up |

The authors call for future clinical comparisons. Cross-study pull-out numbers obtained in different cadavers and protocols must not be ranked as device superiority.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Positioning in the SSLF Landscape

Saffron is an anchor-based alternative to suture-capturing devices ([Capio](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/capio-device), [i-Stitch](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/i-stitch), [Endostitch](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/endostitch)) and reusable carriers ([Miya Hook](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/miya-hook), [Deschamps](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/deschamps-ligature-carrier)). The fixation mechanism differs, but a reduction in buttock pain or other clinical complications has not been demonstrated for Saffron in the cited study.

## SSLF Device Landscape — Level of Evidence

| Device | Type | Manufacturer | Level of Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **[Capio / Capio SLIM](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/capio-device)** | Suture-capturing | Boston Scientific | Multiple clinical studies + systematic reviews |
| **[Anchorsure](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/anchorsure)** | Anchor-based | Neomedic | One device-comparative RCT (Plair 2022)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>, case-control (Evangelopoulos 2024)<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>; mixed-tacker retrospective results are not device-specific |
| **Saffron** | Anchor-based | **Coloplast** | Cadaveric study and nonclinical 510(k) testing reviewed here; clinical comparative outcome not established<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **EnPlace** | Suture-passing | Neomedic | Clinical cohort studies (Neuman 2025)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **[Deschamps ligature carrier](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/deschamps-ligature-carrier)** | Suture-passing | Various | Extensive long-term clinical data |

Amiri 2024 pools outcomes across different techniques; its abstract supplies a 3.8% **overall** nerve-injury rate and 1.2% **overall** reoperation rate, neither a Saffron-specific estimate nor proof of a device-class effect.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Clinical Status

The **95% placement, >10 mm anatomical distance and 17.9 N force** are preclinical observations with different denominators. They do not predict an individual patient's neurovascular risk, anchor durability or prolapse outcome. Select the device and counsel patients using the current manufacturer instructions, patient anatomy and the limits of available clinical evidence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

The manufacturer lists contraindications including pregnancy or plans for pregnancy, growth potential, polysulfone hypersensitivity, active infection, serious untreated anticoagulant disorder and connective-tissue autoimmune disease. Permanent-anchor risks include migration or exposure, bleeding, pain, infection and adjacent organ, nerve or vessel injury. This is a summary, **not the complete IFU**; consult the current device-specific IFU before use.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Use

- **Apical POP repair** — vaginal vault suspension after hysterectomy
- **Uterovaginal prolapse** fixation
- **Native-tissue (mesh-free)** apical suspension
