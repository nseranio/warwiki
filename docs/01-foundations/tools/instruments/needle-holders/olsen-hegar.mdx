# Olsen-Hegar Needle Holder

The Olsen-Hegar combines needle-holder jaws and suture scissors in one ring-handled instrument. The cutting portion lies proximal to the gripping tip, allowing selected closure steps without exchanging instruments. It is an option for accessible wound closure, not a required or proven superior emergency-room default.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Design

- **Distal jaws.** Needle-gripping surfaces vary by model, including tungsten-carbide (TC) versions. Do not assume the jaw dimensions equal a [Mayo-Hegar](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/needle-holders/mayo-hegar); Teleflex lists Olsen-Hegar TC examples with 11 × 3.2-mm and 11.3 × 3.2-mm jaws.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Proximal scissor blade.** The cutting portion is adjacent to, but distinct from, the distal needle-gripping tip.
- **Ratchet.** Ring-handled locking mechanism; engagement and release are model-specific.
- **Length.** The older Teleflex family catalog lists 11.5–16.5-cm variants, while individual current listings include 14.5 and 18 cm; choose reach and jaw size for the case rather than treating one length as standard.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### How It Functions in Two Modes

1. **Needle holding.** Seat a compatible needle in the distal gripping portion, keeping suture clear of the cutting portion.
2. **Suture cutting.** After tying and releasing the needle, deliberately place only the intended tail at the cutting portion and close the blades. Verify the selected instrument's IFU and function before use.

## Advantages

- **Fewer exchanges.** The integrated cutter can be convenient in selected accessible closures; no cited clinical comparison establishes faster or safer closure for the Olsen-Hegar.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Potentially fewer instruments** for a simple closure; downstream sterilization or procurement savings have not been measured by the cited sources.
- **Emergency-medicine precedent.** Abidin 1989 describes a different new holder with bonded TC jaws, incorporated scissors and an offset ringlet. It supports a combination-instrument concept, not a claim that Olsen-Hegar is the optimal ED holder.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

- **Inadvertent suture cutting.** The proximal cutting edge creates a plausible risk if a working strand enters it. Keep the working strand away from the cutter during needle driving and tying; inspect and test the actual model. The cited sources do not quantify this complication.
- **Gripping area.** Distinguish the distal needle seat from the cutting zone; actual usable jaw length is model-specific.
- **Scissor role.** Intended for suture cutting, not a substitute for dedicated tissue-dissecting scissors; confirm material compatibility and maintenance in the current IFU.
- **Access and precision limits.** A short combined holder may be awkward for deep pelvic suturing; select a compatible long [Ryder](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/needle-holders/ryder) or [Turner-Warwick-pattern holder](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/needle-holders/turner-warwick-ryder) if reach demands it. Microsurgical sutures usually require a finer matched model, such as a suitable [Castroviejo](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/needle-holders/castroviejo).

## Reconstructive-Urology and Urogyn Uses

- **Office and bedside procedures.** Meatotomy and meatoplasty trims, suprapubic-tube site closure, scrotal-laceration repair, simple hydrocele revision skin closure, foreskin-injury repair, vasectomy skin closure.
- **Emergency wound closure.** Perineal lacerations, scrotal lacerations, straddle-injury skin closure, post-fall genital lacerations.
- **Perineal and vulvar closure.** Selected obstetric-perineal-laceration, introital or vestibular-biopsy closures when the holder and cutter and needle are appropriate; no procedure-specific superiority is established here.
- **Running cutaneous closure** at the end of any open RU case where the closing surgeon is working solo or as the primary needle-driver: scrotal incisions for IPP / AUS / hydrocelectomy / varicocelectomy, suprapubic skin closure, inguinal incisions.

## Jaw-Surface Mechanics and Needle Security

Bench studies of particular needle holders and bonded jaw surfaces support matching gripping force to needle resistance, but Chen 1991 tested four holders from one manufacturer and did not establish an Olsen-Hegar-specific autoclave-life advantage.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Choose TC or steel jaws by the selected model's specifications and inspect for wear; no universal high-volume ranking follows.

## Technique

- **Grip.** A ring grip may place thumb and ring finger in the rings with the index finger guiding the shaft; hand position depends on access and selected model.
- **Needle placement.** Hold a compatible part of the needle in the distal gripping area, not the cutter; follow needle and holder instructions rather than one universal fraction of the needle body.
- **Keep the working strand clear** of the proximal cutting edges during tying; the frequency of accidental cuts is not quantified here.
- **Deliberate cut.** Release the needle and place only the intended tail in the scissor portion before closing it.

## Historical Context

The Olsen-Hegar is a combination holder-and-cutter pattern, not evidence of one established invention sequence. Edlich's review discusses needle and holder design; Owen 1984 describes a different Australian microneedleholder-scissors for microsurgery, not a small Olsen-Hegar or a comparative trial.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

See also: [Ryder](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/needle-holders/ryder), [Turner-Warwick Ryder](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/needle-holders/turner-warwick-ryder), [Castroviejo](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/needle-holders/castroviejo), [Heaney](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/needle-holders/heaney).
