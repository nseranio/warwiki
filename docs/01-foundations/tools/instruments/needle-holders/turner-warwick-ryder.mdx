# Turner-Warwick Ryder Needle Holder

The Turner-Warwick-pattern needle holder is a named pelvic-access instrument associated with [Richard Turner-Warwick](/docs/roots/surgeons/s-z/richard-turner-warwick). Current manufacturer listings specify curved shanks and TC jaws; a distributor also lists a “Turner Warwick with Ryder jaw” variant. These listings do not establish that every Turner-Warwick holder is Richard Turner-Warwick's direct modification of a Ryder or a required driver for all open reconstruction.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Design

- **Curved shank and offset rings** can change hand position and exposure. Surgical Holdings lists 200- and 230-mm TC models; a Sontec 8.5-in model has a curved shank, serrated TC tip and offset rings. Do not infer a universal 18–23-cm span or straight Ryder geometry.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Jaw design** varies by model. Inspect the actual working end and match the needle and suture; neither a universal 4-0-and-finer range nor a no-damage claim is supported for the entire family.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Lock and handling** are model-specific. The curved shank may help present a needle in a narrow perineal field, but clinical superiority over a straight holder has not been shown by the cited catalog entries.

## Potential Uses

These are possible access-based applications, not proof of routine use or a preferred holder for each operation. One contemporary perineal VUA-stricture report specifically describes a Turner-Warwick holder (or a purpose-modified alternative); its full article was access-limited.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

- **Posterior urethroplasty.** Anastomotic suture placement deep in the perineum, including the [Webster perineal](/docs/special-populations/05a-trauma-emergencies/pfui) approach
- **Vesicourethral anastomosis** after radical prostatectomy and at salvage VUA reconstruction
- **Deep bulbar anastomosis.** Non-transecting, augmented anastomotic, and related variants
- **Ureteroenteric anastomoses** in [ileal](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion/ileal-conduit) and [colon conduit](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion/colon-conduit) urinary diversions, only when the actual holder and access suit the delicate needle
- **Vesicovaginal and urethrovaginal fistula** repair with deep transabdominal exposure
- **Pediatric urology.** Hypospadias and ureteral reimplantation when delicate tissue handling is needed
- Selected deep pelvic reconstruction when reach and curvature improve the planned needle path

## Practical Considerations

- **Suture and needle compatibility.** Reconstructive operations may use fine absorbable sutures, but the listed 4-0–6-0 and SH/RB-1/UR-6 combinations were not established for every Turner-Warwick model. Confirm the selected jaw, needle and manufacturer's instructions rather than assuming a universal range.
- **Maintenance.** Inspect the actual TC jaw, alignment and ratchet for damage or unreliable grip; remove a faulty holder from service. The cited bench jaw studies do not provide a Turner-Warwick-specific service interval.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Separate combination device.** Abidin's 1989 emergency holder had incorporated scissors and an offset ringlet; it is not an Olsen-Hegar or Turner-Warwick model specification.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Reprocessing.** Follow the current model IFU and institutional process; the prior universal autoclave and open-unlocked instruction was not verified for all variants.

## History

Turner-Warwick (1925–2020) was influential in posterior urethroplasty and pelvic-access instrument design. Reid's 1975 60-patient Turner-Warwick urethroplasty series reports an operation and outcomes; it does not document this holder's invention, direct derivation from Ryder, or device-specific effectiveness.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The current eponym identifies a catalogued curved-shank pattern; personal design history and variant lineage need a separate primary source.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
