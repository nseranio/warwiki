# Wilson Backward-Cutting Scissors

**Heavy backward-cutting scissors** with outside blade edges that cut when the blades are spread in dense tissue, as well as conventional cutting on closure. Wilson and colleagues describe the instrument, also called the **Freeman-Kaye** or **Gourney** pattern, as one option to create a short starter space in severely fibrotic corpora before a cavernotome can engage the scar. It is **not** a substitute for controlled exposure or an automatic route to safe cylinder placement.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Design

- **Outer blade edges sharpened** — in Wilson's original description, the outside of each blade is sharp, allowing the blades to **cut scar on opening** as well as on closure; do not infer a quantified amount of tissue resection from the motion alone.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Heavy, robust construction** — built to push and spread against dense scar without deflecting, unlike fine dissecting scissors.
- **Model and material** — Wilson's 2020 operative paper describes **two sizes of heavy-duty titanium scissors**; the current Uramix store lists four Wilson-Mooreville size options. Confirm present-day geometry, material and reprocessing instructions from the actual supplied model rather than assuming a universal stainless-steel/autoclavable pattern.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Reconstructive-Urology Use

Effectively a **single-indication instrument in reconstructive urology**: establishing access in the **scarred corpus cavernosum** during inflatable or malleable penile prosthesis surgery. The corpora most often requiring it are those scarred by **prior device explantation for infection, ischemic priapism, prior shunt surgery, or trauma** — see the [corporal fibrosis section of the penile-prosthesis revision-scenarios page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/penile-implants/revision-scenarios#corporal-scarring--fibrosis) for the full management ladder.

When a standard [Hegar dilator](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/sounds-bougies/hegar-dilators) cannot traverse a densely scarred corpus, Wilson describes a **1–2 cm starter space** made with backward-cutting scissors **or** a #15-scalpel cruciate incision before a cutting cavernotome engages the scar. The choice and subsequent channel size depend on anatomy, device and exposure; forceful blind advancement is discouraged.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## How It Cuts — Two Modes

| Motion | Cutting surface | Use in scar |
|---|---|---|
| **Closing (conventional)** | Inner opposed edges | Divides scar bands directly under the tips |
| **Spreading (backward cutting)** | **Outside blade edges** | Cuts dense scar as blades open to establish a short starter space |

This dual action distinguishes the instrument from the usual blunt spreading with a [Metzenbaum](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/scissors/metzenbaum). Metzenbaum scissors can still have a role during exposed corporal scar dissection; they are not categorically useless in all fibrotic cases.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Technique

1. **Expose the corporotomy** and assess the fibrotic corporal lumen and adjacent urethra/tunica before selecting a cutting instrument.
2. **Create a short starter space** by inserting the appropriate closed scissors into the identified scar and spreading in controlled increments; Wilson describes about **1–2 cm** before a cavernotome can engage. A cruciate #15-blade incision is an alternative in his technique.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
3. **Keep the instrument within the intended corporal tract**, away from both the **urethra and tunica**. Do **not** direct a sharp cutting force laterally as a universal safety rule: that may risk tunical perforation. Avoid blind distal advancement; extend or add a corporotomy for direct visualization when necessary.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
4. **Hand off to a [cavernotome](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/sounds-bougies/cavernotomes)** if safe space exists, then size the tract to the actual insertion tool and chosen cylinder. Wilson's 2020 examples describe **9 mm for the Furlow tool** and **9–11 mm proximally depending on cylinder model**; these are source-specific examples, **not universal current AMS/Coloplast IFU limits**.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
5. **Stop rather than force** an instrument that cannot safely create a tract; extend the corporotomy or consider directly visualized excavation/grafting as appropriate. Wilson also describes irrigating to check for an unrecognized urethral injury before completing implantation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations and Safety

- **Specialized role** — described for a scarred corporal tract, not routine soft-tissue dissection; use [Metzenbaum](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/scissors/metzenbaum) or [Mayo](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/scissors/mayo) variants where those better fit the exposed layer.
- **Perforation risk** — sharp outside edges can injure either the **urethra** or **tunica** when the tract or depth is lost. Avoid forcing, keep exposure and check for injury.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Availability** — specialized equipment may need advance planning for known or anticipated fibrosis. The operative papers do not establish that every standard tray lacks the scissors.

## Naming

Wilson's original operative account calls the instrument **Freeman Kaye or Gourney** and describes its adaptation for scarred-corpora cylinder insertion; it does not establish which original eponymic designer produced each contemporary model.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
