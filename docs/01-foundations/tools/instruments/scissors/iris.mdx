# Iris Scissors

**Iris-pattern scissors** are short, fine cutting instruments developed for ophthalmic work and available for selected small-field reconstructive tasks. They are not a required default on every urology or urogynecology tray; select the actual tip, handle and length for the tissue and exposure. Iris forceps may be paired with them when a matching fine grasping instrument is useful.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Design

- **Length**: catalog examples include 8.9, 10.2 and 11 cm; length alone does not establish superior tactile feedback.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Blades and tips:** fine, but not universally sharp-pointed. Sklar lists sharp/sharp Iris and blunt/blunt Knapp-Iris examples; B. Braun lists a curved sharp/blunt Iris model.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Configurations:** straight and curved ring-handled models exist; some micro-miniature Iris models use a spring handle. Check the selected design rather than assuming one shape.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Material**: surgical-grade stainless steel; the fine tips require careful handling and storage to prevent bending.

## Reconstructive-Urology and Urogyn Uses

Possible small-field applications, provided the chosen tip and working length suit the anatomy, include:

- **Hypospadias and distal-urethral reconstruction.** Glanular and urethral-plate incisions during TIP / TIPU / Mathieu / onlay-island-flap; trim of glansplasty wing flaps; fistula-repair flap trimming.
- **Glans resurfacing, partial glansectomy, and glanuloplasty.** Fine glanular epithelial trim, tunica albuginea edge work.
- **Penile-shaft cosmetic and minor reconstructive work.** Fine inner-prepuce and preputial-skin trim during partial or radical circumcision revision, frenuloplasty, minor penile-skin reconstruction.
- **Vulvar and introital fine work.** Labiaplasty edge trim, vestibulectomy, posterior-vestibuloplasty mucosal-flap trim, Foldès clitoral reconstruction, post-defibulation introital closure.
- **Microsurgery-adjacent vasal and cord work.** Selected adventitial or cord-tissue trimming with an appropriate delicate model; microsurgical anastomoses need purpose-matched optics and instruments rather than a generic substitute.
- **Pediatric urology.** Orchidopexy, hydrocele and hernia, hypospadias, and ureteral reimplantation where the scaled-down field demands a scaled-down scissor.
- **Office and ED genital procedures.** Meatotomy and meatoplasty trim, foreskin-injury repair, condyloma excision, vestibular biopsy, urethral-caruncle excision, vaginal-cyst marsupialization.
- **Suture-trim in fine fields.** A manufacturer describes fine suture removal for its Iris model; reserve tissue-dedicated scissors when maintaining that edge is important.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Iris Scissors vs Adjacent Fine Cutting Instruments

| Scissor | Length | Tip | Best fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Iris** | ~9–11.5 cm in cited models | Fine; sharp/sharp, sharp/blunt or blunt/blunt variants | Selected fine skin or mucosal work |
| [**Metzenbaum**](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/scissors/metzenbaum) | Model-specific | Fine; tip varies | Delicate-tissue dissection |
| [**Mayo**](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/scissors/mayo) | Model-specific | Heavier; tip varies | Denser tissue or compatible utility cutting |
| [**Potts**](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/scissors/potts) | 18–25 cm | Angled, fine sharp | Vessel / ureter / urethra wall, spatulation |
| **Dedicated micro scissor** | Model-specific | Ultra-fine | Microsurgical cutting with matched equipment |

The named families overlap: an Iris-pattern micro-miniature spring scissor is sold for microsurgical use, so “Iris” does not by itself mean non-microsurgical. For vasal mucosa, epididymal tubules or lymphatics, choose a model matched to the tissue, optics and planned cut.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Technique

- **Grip**: for a ring-handled model, thumb and ring finger can control the rings with the index finger guiding the shank; a spring-handled micro-miniature model has a different grip.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Match the variant to the cut**: straight iris for straight cuts; curved iris when following an anatomic contour (glansplasty wing, labial-edge trim).
- **Controlled cuts**: stabilize the tissue and use a sharp, appropriately sized tip; avoid repeated uncontrolled bites at a visible edge. No comparative cosmetic-outcome result is claimed here.
- **Protect the tip**: inspect alignment and sharpness before use; follow the reusable model's care instructions, or discard a single-use model as labeled.
- **Preserve a tissue-dedicated edge** when practical. An absolute ban on suture cutting is incorrect for all Iris products: Sklar describes fine suture removal for model 22-1135.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Naming and Origin

The name reflects an ophthalmic origin; current catalogs use “Iris” for several fine scissor patterns, including blunt-tip and spring-handled versions. This history does not prove lower tissue trauma or a universal pairing with [iris forceps](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/forceps/iris).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
