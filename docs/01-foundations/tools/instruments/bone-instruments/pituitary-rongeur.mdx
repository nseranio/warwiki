# Pituitary Rongeur

A **pituitary rongeur** is a slender, long-shafted instrument for grasping or removing material in a confined space. Many models are intended for **disc and other soft/cartilaginous tissue**. They should not all be described as miniature cortical-bone cutters. Some stronger variants are marketed for small bone fragments, so the exact model's intended use matters.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Design

Jaw size, angle, length and bite geometry vary. The manufacturer offers straight, upward- and downward-angled patterns; a generic “2–3 mm cup” does not describe the entire family. A narrow profile can improve access but also makes it easier to lose sight of the distal jaws.

## Use in Reconstruction

A suitable model may help retrieve or remove selected tissue or loose fragments within a visible operative field. Do not prescribe a routine Leksell-to-pituitary sequence for pubectomy, or use a fine soft-tissue pituitary rongeur to contour hard cortical bone unless the device is intended for that task. A [bone rongeur](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/bone-instruments/rongeur), osteotome or drill may be required instead.

## Safety

- Confirm intended tissue and compatible use before selecting the instrument.
- Keep the jaws and the tissue being removed visible; avoid blind deep bites.
- Do not twist, lever or force closed jaws against material they cannot cut. Reassess the instrument and exposure.
- Inspect jaw integrity and account for removed fragments; follow the instrument's cleaning and sterilization instructions.

Manufacturer product descriptions establish differences between models, not evidence of better reconstructive outcomes or universally safer fine dissection.
