# Pituitary Rongeur

A **pituitary rongeur** is a slender, long-shafted forceps with small cupped jaws for grasping or removing material through a narrow corridor. It was designed for transsphenoidal pituitary surgery. It is longer, narrower and finer-tipped than a standard [rongeur](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/bone-instruments/rongeur). Many models are intended for disc and other soft or cartilaginous tissue, so a pituitary rongeur is not a miniature cortical-bone cutter. Some stronger variants are marketed for small bone fragments, and the exact model's intended use decides.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Design

- **Shaft:** long and slender to reach deep, narrow fields. One manufacturer lists 6 and 10 inch shafts.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Jaws:** small cups. The same manufacturer lists 1, 1.5 and 2 mm tips, so a generic 2–3 mm cup does not describe the family.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Pattern:** straight, up-angled and down-angled versions are available.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Trade-off:** the narrow profile improves access but makes it easier to lose sight of the distal jaws.

## Use in Reconstruction

In a visible operative field, a suitable model can remove selected tissue or loose fragments.

- **Partial pubectomy** for [urethropubic fistula](/docs/clinical-conditions/03f-fistulas/in-males/urethropubic): fine removal near periurethral tissue where a Leksell rongeur is too bulky, and detailed debridement after coarse removal.
- **[PFUI](/docs/special-populations/05a-trauma-emergencies/pfui) reconstruction:** removal of small bony fragments from the pelvic ring.
- **Clearing bone dust and chips** from the field, including bone chips mixed with scar or fibrin.

A common sequence is coarse removal with a Leksell rongeur followed by finer work with a pituitary rongeur, but this is a surgeon's practice and not a standard. A fine soft-tissue pituitary rongeur should not contour hard cortical bone unless the device is intended for it. A [bone rongeur](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/bone-instruments/rongeur), osteotome or drill may be needed instead. It is not the instrument for bulk debridement.

## Technique Pearls

- Confirm the intended tissue and compatible use before selecting the instrument.
- Keep the jaws and the tissue being removed visible, and avoid blind deep bites.
- Do not twist, lever or force closed jaws against material they cannot cut. Reassess the instrument and exposure.
- Inspect jaw integrity, account for removed fragments and follow the cleaning and sterilization instructions.

Manufacturer descriptions show differences between models. They are not evidence of better reconstructive outcomes.
