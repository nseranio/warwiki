# Rongeur

A **rongeur** (from the French *ronger*, "to gnaw") is a bone-biting forceps with a hinged handle and sharp cupped jaws that remove bone in small controlled bites. Jaw size, curvature and mechanical linkage differ between models. Leksell-type instruments commonly use a double-action mechanism, which multiplies mechanical advantage for harder bone. The exact device's intended tissue and dimensions should guide selection.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Instrument Families

- **Leksell.** Large curved jaws, a neurosurgical and spinal instrument that is commonly used for pubic bone debridement in urology.
- **Stille.** Straight, heavy-duty jaws.
- **Kerrison.** Thin upward-biting design with a thin footplate that fits narrow corridors, used mainly in spinal surgery and rarely at the pubis.
- **Lempert.** Smaller jaws for finer bone work.

Fine [pituitary rongeurs](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/bone-instruments/pituitary-rongeur) have a different intended use and are not automatic substitutes for cortical-bone cutting.

## Use in Reconstructive Urology

- **Partial pubectomy** for [urethropubic fistula](/docs/clinical-conditions/03f-fistulas/in-males/urethropubic) with osteomyelitis.
- **Pubic symphysis debridement** in urethropubic and pubovesical fistula, removing infected or nonviable bone.
- **Bone debridement during [PFUI](/docs/special-populations/05a-trauma-emergencies/pfui) reconstruction**, trimming bony fragments or a planned bony exposure that encroaches on the urethral field.
- **Pelvic ring exposure** during complex posterior urethroplasty, which is uncommon and is often deferred to orthopedics.

In urethropubic fistula with osteomyelitis, removal of infected or nonviable bone is part of a broader source-control and reconstruction strategy. Bone is debrided back to viable, bleeding cancellous bone. Bleeding is an assessment cue that is affected by tissue condition and prior treatment, so it is not the only definition of the resection boundary, and it does not mean all cortex must be removed.

## Handling Principles

- Take small bites. Each squeeze removes a discrete chunk, and oversized bites fracture bone or damage the instrument. Obtain a visible purchase suited to the jaw capacity.
- Protect adjacent soft tissue and keep it out of the jaws. Reassess a deep or poorly seen bite before closing.
- Irrigate and clear bone chips frequently to maintain visibility. Do not use the jaws as a lever against unexposed structures.
- Strip periosteum with a [periosteal elevator](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/bone-instruments/periosteal-elevator) only where the planned exposure requires it.
- Inspect the jaws and linkage before and after use, and follow model-specific reprocessing instructions.

The sources cited here do not compare speed or precision between a rongeur, an osteotome and a powered drill. Selection depends on the task, access, bone quality and operator experience.
