# Air Drill — Pneumatic Bone Drill

An **air drill** (pneumatic bone drill) is a handpiece driven by compressed gas that rotates a burr attachment to cut bone quickly under direct vision. It is a high-speed bone drill in the broader class of powered drills, which also includes electric and battery-driven systems. It is standard equipment in orthopedic, spine and neurosurgical practice and is borrowed for partial pubectomy and bone-corridor work in reconstructive urology.

## Devices

A brand family can contain more than one drive technology, so match the exact handpiece, attachment and burr and do not infer compatibility from the brand.

- **Midas Rex MR8** (Medtronic) is a high-speed pneumatic drill with a lightweight handpiece, marketed for cranial and spine procedures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **ANSPACH EG1** (DePuy Synthes) is an electric high-speed system.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Other manufacturers offer pneumatic, electric and battery-powered systems with interchangeable attachments. Portable battery systems avoid a gas line and hose.

## Burr Attachments

- **Cutting (fluted) burr:** removes bone fast and is used for bulk debridement.
- **Diamond burr:** cuts more slowly and smoothly and is chosen for finer work near neurovascular structures. A diamond burr does not establish a safe margin around a nerve, vessel or urethra.
- **Round (ball) burr:** volumetric debridement.
- **Side-cutting burr:** linear cuts.

Use only components compatible with the model, within its speed, direction and application limits.

## Use in Reconstructive Urology

- **Partial pubectomy** for [urethropubic fistula](/docs/clinical-conditions/03f-fistulas/in-males/urethropubic) with pubic osteomyelitis, usually alongside rongeurs at different stages of the dissection.
- **[PFUI](/docs/special-populations/05a-trauma-emergencies/pfui) reconstruction:** selective bony decompression of encroaching fragments.
- **Complex posterior urethroplasty:** an occasional adjunct to the rongeur and osteotome approach when inferior pubectomy is needed for exposure.
- **Exostoses** encountered during complex perineal dissection.

Whether bone is removed, and how much, follows the exposure or infection-control plan. Having a drill available is not an indication for pubectomy.

## Safety

- **Cooling.** High-speed burrs generate heat that can injure adjacent bone and soft tissue. Irrigate during cutting as the model's instructions direct, and avoid prolonged operation or persistent pressure on one point. Unexpected heat, vibration, resistance or loss of function calls for stopping and reassessment.
- **Visualization.** A burr does not stop at soft tissue. A slip can injure the iliac vessels, nerves or urethra. Protect adjacent structures, keep the cutting surface in view and stop when it cannot be seen.
- **Setup.** Verify assembly, burr retention and function before approaching tissue. Clear debris while preserving visibility.
- **Team.** Many urologists work with an orthopedic or spine-trained colleague who operates the drill while the urologist exposes and protects surrounding tissue. Competence with one system does not transfer automatically to another.
- **Reprocessing.** Follow component-specific rules. A reusable handpiece does not make a disposable burr reusable, and components are counted after use.

Speed, irrigation and duty-cycle limits are model-specific and are in the manufacturer's instructions for use. Confirm them for the drill in the room.

## Comparison with Hand Instruments

| Feature | Air drill | [Rongeur](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/bone-instruments/rongeur) | [Osteotome](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/bone-instruments/osteotome-and-mallet) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Speed of bone removal | Fast | Moderate | Slow to moderate |
| Thermal injury | Possible without irrigation | None | None |
| Bone dust | Generated | Fragments | Fragments |
| Operator skill | Practice with the system needed | Intuitive | Mallet technique needed |
| Equipment | Handpiece, power source, irrigation | Simple | Simple |

Hand instruments avoid the rotating-burr hazards but carry risks of penetration, fracture, crush injury and unintended tissue removal. Precision depends on the operator, the task and the burr, and no instrument type is universally safer.
