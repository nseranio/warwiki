# Periosteal Elevator

A **periosteal elevator** strips the periosteum, the fibrous covering of bone, off the underlying cortex. It is the usual first step before rongeur or osteotome work and one of the most-used instruments in bone-adjacent surgery. Tips range from blunt to sharp, and instruments vary in width, curvature and whether they are single- or double-ended.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Common Variants

Named patterns describe families, and none is a universal specification.

- **Key elevator:** broad, flat, straight tip for large bone surfaces such as the pubis, femur and tibia.
- **Freer elevator:** narrower, often double-ended (blunt on one side, semi-sharp on the other), for fine dissection in periosteum and soft-tissue planes.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Sayre elevator:** curved and heavier, for larger bone dissection.
- **Cobb elevator:** large and spoon-shaped, made for spinal work and sometimes used at the pubic symphysis.

## Selection

A narrower tip gives access in a confined space but concentrates force. A broader blade needs enough exposure to control its full edge.

## Use in Reconstructive Urology

- **Periosteal stripping** before [rongeur](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/bone-instruments/rongeur) debridement or an [osteotome](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/bone-instruments/osteotome-and-mallet) cut in pubic-bone work.
- **Separating bone from adherent periurethral and periosseous fibrous tissue** in [PFUI](/docs/special-populations/05a-trauma-emergencies/pfui) reconstruction.
- **Mobilizing the bladder off the pubic symphysis** in complex retropubic dissection after radiation or prior pelvic surgery.

Elevate only what the operation requires and preserve viable periosteum where possible. Complete stripping is not needed for every bone cut or fragment removal.

## Technique

- Engage the periosteum with the tip at the margin of the intended cut.
- Establish the plane under direct vision and advance with controlled force along the bone surface, keeping the tip against bone.
- Off-plane slippage into soft tissue risks injury to the urethra, bladder or iliac vessels. If tissue does not separate as expected, reassess the plane and surrounding anatomy instead of forcing the tip deeper. Scar, radiation injury and adherent urinary structures can obscure the usual boundary.
- Inspect the edge and handle before use, maintain visualization and follow the model's reprocessing instructions. "Blunt" describes the tip and does not guard against perforation or traction injury.

## Freer Elevator in Soft-Tissue Dissection

The Freer elevator is also used for fine soft-tissue plane development, for example in dartos and Buck fascia dissection in penile reconstruction. Some surgeons also use it in corporal mobilization and in dartos pocket creation for IPP and AUS placement. These are individual technique choices and are not standardized. Follow the anatomy and technique of the relevant operation.
