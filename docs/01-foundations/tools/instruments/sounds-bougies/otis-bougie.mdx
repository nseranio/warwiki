# Otis Bougie-à-Boule

A **bougie-à-boule** is a urethral exploring instrument with a bulbous distal tip. The bulb can provide tactile information as it passes into and back through a narrowing, but it does not replace urethrography or endoscopic assessment of stricture length, anatomy, or treatment suitability.

## Current catalog form

The cited Sklar hospital catalog lists a reusable chrome "Otis Bougie A Boule" in even sizes from 8 to 32 Fr, an eight-instrument 16–30 Fr set, and a thirteen-size set. Sklar's live listing for the thirteen-piece set (SKU 85-1636) instead gives a range of 8–34 Fr; confirm the contents of a local set before relying on either endpoint.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Catalog naming varies, so the maker's current catalog and reprocessing instructions should be checked for the exact instrument on a local tray.

## What the ball tip can and cannot show

- **Tactile localization:** resistance as the bulb crosses a narrowing can help identify its approximate position.
- **Calibration:** the largest bulb that passes gives a mechanical estimate of luminal caliber at that examination.
- **Important limitation:** spasm, pain, irregular scar, false passage, and examination technique can alter tactile findings. A ball-tip sound does not define the full extent of spongiofibrosis and should not be forced through resistance.

Modern stricture evaluation generally relies on history, uroflowmetry, retrograde urethrography/voiding cystourethrography, cystoscopy, or ultrasound as appropriate. The bougie remains an instrument option and is not an independent diagnostic endpoint.

## Naming and history

The commercial name Otis bougie-à-boule should not be read as proof that Fessenden Nott Otis invented the ball-tip concept. A historical review notes that Sir Charles Bell described a ball probe in 1807 and that the name *bougie-à-boule* came from Leroy d'Étiolles; Otis is separately associated with urethrometry and the dilating urethrotome.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
