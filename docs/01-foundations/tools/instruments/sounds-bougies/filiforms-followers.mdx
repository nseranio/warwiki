# Filiforms & Followers

**Two-component coaxial urethral-dilation system.** A thin, flexible filiform leader (2–6 Fr) negotiates a tight or near-obliterative stricture, followed by threaded hollow "followers" in graduated French sizes that screw onto the filiform's proximal threaded end and dilate the stricture sequentially. It is the classic instrument for a stricture too tight to admit a standard sound or catheter, and the historical fallback when bladder drainage is needed but a Foley will not pass.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Design

### Filiforms (leaders)

- **Diameter.** Extremely thin, typically 2–6 Fr (0.66–2 mm); the smallest urethral instruments available.
- **Material.** Traditionally woven silk or gum-elastic ("bougie"); modern leaders are plastic / polyethylene.
- **Flexibility.** Semi-rigid to flexible, allowing negotiation of tortuous strictures that rigid sounds cannot enter.
- **Tip configurations,** selected to match stricture geometry:
  - **Straight.** For axially aligned strictures.
  - **Coudé (curved).** Angled tip to clear an elevated bladder neck or eccentric stricture.
  - **Spiral / corkscrew.** For irregular or eccentric lumens.
  - **Filiform bougie.** Tapered whip-like tip for tight obliterative segments.
- **Proximal end.** Threaded fitting for a compatible follower; conventional Phillips-type filiforms have a female fitting, but connector orientation can vary by system.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Length.** Approximately 35 cm, reaching from meatus to bladder.

### Followers

- **Diameter.** Graduated, typically 6–8 Fr up to 24 Fr in 2-Fr increments.
- **Material.** Historically gum-elastic; modern followers are metal or rigid plastic.
- **Follower styles.** Bougie followers lack drainage eyes; catheter followers have a drainage lumen and permit bladder drainage. Each attaches to a compatible filiform with a threaded fitting.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Smooth taper** at the leading end matches the filiform diameter, creating a seamless transition that limits mucosal trauma during passage.

## Reconstructive-Urology Uses

Filiforms-and-followers are the dilation system for strictures too tight for any other instrument and the historical fallback for failed catheterization:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- **Very tight or near-complete urethral stricture.** Luminal diameter too narrow for the smallest [Van Buren](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/sounds-bougies/van-buren) or [Dittel](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/sounds-bougies/dittel) sound.
- **Failed catheterization with urgent need for bladder drainage.** Before flexible cystoscopy this was the standard fallback before suprapubic tube; it is now generally supplanted by bedside flexible cystoscopy with guidewire-assisted catheter placement when available.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Tortuous / irregular strictures.** The flexible filiform tip can find eccentric lumens that a rigid sound cannot.
- **Resource-limited / equipment-unavailable settings.** When flexible cystoscopy and fluoroscopy are not at hand, filiform-and-follower remains a valid blind technique.

For procedural context, see [DVIU and Urethral Dilation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/dviu).

## Technique

1. **Topical 2% lidocaine** instilled per urethra; supine position; generous sterile lubricant.
2. **Filiform passage.** Advance the leader gently through the urethra. If resistance is met at the stricture:
   - **Try a different tip configuration** (coudé, spiral) before forcing.
   - **Multiple-filiform technique.** Insert several filiforms simultaneously (up to 5–6). They fill the false passages and dead-ends; the next leader is more likely to find the true lumen. A classic urologic maneuver.
   - **Assess intravesical position.** Loss of resistance and insertion length are clues, not confirmation. If bladder placement remains uncertain, do not advance a follower; use cystoscopic or fluoroscopic assessment when available.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
3. **Follower attachment.** Once the filiform is confirmed in the bladder, screw the smallest follower onto its proximal end and advance.
4. **Sequential dilation.** Exchange followers in ascending French sizes while the filiform stays in place as the guide rail; keep its proximal end visible beyond the follower. The Cochrane review does not establish a routine 24 Fr endpoint.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> For bladder drainage, dilate only to the caliber needed for the intended catheter.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
5. **Catheter placement.** Pass a Council-tip catheter over a filiform only with a compatible threaded catheter guide, or exchange to a guidewire and pass the catheter over the wire for bladder drainage.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Comparison with Other Dilation Methods

| Feature | **Filiform + Followers** | [**Van Buren**](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/sounds-bougies/van-buren) | [**S-Shaped Coaxial**](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/sounds-bougies/s-dilators) | [**Balloon Dilator**](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/sounds-bougies/balloon-dilator) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Minimum stricture caliber** | **2–6 Fr (tightest accommodated)** | ~ 8 Fr (smallest sound) | Must accept guidewire (~ 3 Fr) | Must accept guidewire |
| **Guidance** | Blind (tactile, flexible leader) | Blind (tactile, rigid) | Over guidewire ± fluoroscopy | **Direct vision** or fluoroscopy |
| **Flexibility** | Semi-flexible filiform | Rigid | Flexible guidewire | Flexible balloon |
| **Components** | **Multi-component (filiform + threaded followers)** | Single instrument per size | Single instrument over wire | Wire + balloon + inflator |
| **Force mechanism** | Axial (coaxial shearing) | Axial (shearing) | Axial (shearing) | **Radial** |
| **False-passage risk** | Blind; the flexible tip may help | Rigid and blind | Wire-guided | Visualized and wire-guided |
| **Best use** | Very tight / near-obliterative stricture, failed catheterization | Routine moderate stricture; intraoperative calibration | Recurrent stricture with prior false-passage risk | Direct-vision dilation, drug-coated delivery |

## Outcomes

In the Heyns 1998 RCT, 210 men were randomized to filiform dilation (106) or internal urethrotomy (104). Among men still stricture-free at 3 months after a single treatment (about 70% of the cohort), the estimated stricture-free rate was 55–60% at 24 months and 50–60% at 48 months. For recurrence at 3 months, a second dilation or urethrotomy yielded an estimated 30–50% stricture-free rate at 24 months and 0–40% at 48 months; after recurrences at 3 and 6 months, a third treatment yielded 0% at 24 months.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> The Cochrane review of this trial found no significant difference between dilatation and urethrotomy in the proportion stricture-free at three years or in median time to recurrence.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

The 2014 SIU/ICUD consultation reviewed dilation, internal urethrotomy and stenting of male anterior urethral strictures and graded the evidence.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Safety Profile

Complications specific to filiforms-and-followers (these are practical hazards of the design, not figures from a trial):<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- **Filiform knotting in the urethra or bladder.** Particularly with excessive advance or intravesical coiling.
- **Filiform breakage.** Leaves a retained intraurethral or intravesical fragment that needs endoscopic retrieval.
- **Filiform bending / deformation.** Prevents follower passage and aborts the procedure.
- **False-passage creation.** Blind filiform-and-follower passage can create a false passage or urethral perforation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Urethral hemorrhage, UTI, bacteremia.** Standard urethral-instrumentation risks.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Modern Alternatives and Declining Use

Filiform-and-follower use has declined as wire- and vision-based alternatives have spread:<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- **Hydrophilic Glidewire technique (Freid & Smith 1996).** A hydrophilic guidewire passed per urethra in filiform-like fashion, exchanged for a standard wire, then coaxial dilation. Successful in 19 of 20 attempts in a small series, several after failed filiform passage. The authors recommended it over filiforms and followers when cystourethroscopy is not immediately available.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Bedside flexible cystoscopy with guidewire-assisted catheter placement (Beaghler 1994).** Successful in 52 of 54 patients with failed catheterization, with no complications reported; it has largely replaced the traditional filiform-and-follower role in the acute "can't pass a catheter" setting wherever a flexible scope is available.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Direct-vision balloon dilation.** Radial force under endoscopic vision.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **S-shaped coaxial dilators.** Single-instrument guidewire-tracked alternative for the curved male urethra.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Filiforms and followers remain on the urology tray for the case where modern equipment is unavailable or the stricture admits nothing else.
