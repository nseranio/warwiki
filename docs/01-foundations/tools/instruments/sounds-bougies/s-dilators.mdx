# S-Shaped Coaxial Dilators

**Double-curve ("S-shaped") urethral dilators** passed over a pre-placed guidewire, the engineering compromise between the anatomical conformity of the curved [Van Buren](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/sounds-bougies/van-buren) sound and the safety of guidewire-assisted dilation historically delivered by straight coaxial dilators or the [Balloon Dilator](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/sounds-bougies/balloon-dilator). The S-shape matches the natural double curve of the male urethra (pendulous urethra curving inferiorly, then bulbar urethra curving superiorly around the perineal body); after a guidewire is confirmed through the true lumen into the bladder under cystoscopic or fluoroscopic control, the coaxial track follows that wire and is intended to reduce false-passage creation during dilation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> Originally described by [Herschorn](/docs/roots/surgeons/h-r/sender-herschorn) and Carrington (2007).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Design and Rationale

- **Double-curve (S-shaped) profile** along the working length matches the pendulous-to-bulbar urethral curvature without the blind manipulation required by the single-curve Van Buren.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Coaxial central lumen** for over-the-wire passage. Herschorn and Carrington described a 0.038-inch wire placed under cystoscopic or fluoroscopic control; confirm the selected device's specified wire size.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **French sizing** in sequential graduated increments, allowing stepwise dilation from baseline caliber to the therapeutic endpoint.
- **Single-instrument-per-size** simplicity, with no threaded follower assembly as with [filiform and follower](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments) systems.

Herschorn and Carrington developed hydrophilic S-shaped prototypes and clinically appraised the final design in 30 men (18 bladder-neck, 10 bulbar, and two penile strictures).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Reconstructive-Urology Uses

- **Recurrent bulbar or pan-urethral stricture needing temporary access** may be dilated over a confirmed wire when blind sound passage has created a false passage or carries high risk and urethroplasty is unsuitable or declined; EAU 2026 strongly advises against dilation or DVIU as sole treatment for a long (&gt;2 cm) stricture and against more than two such procedures when urethroplasty is viable.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Dilation after failed DVIU** may be used for urgent catheter access; for recurrent anterior stricture, AUA 2023 recommends offering urethroplasty rather than repeated endoscopic management (Moderate Recommendation, Grade C).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Related pediatric sheath technique.** Dewan et al. used a straight flexi-tip wire and serial sheath dilators in 32 children, sharing the over-wire approach of adult S-shaped dilators.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> At mean 16-month follow-up, 13 had undergone more than four dilations and three had UTIs; no false passage or sepsis was reported.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Dilation before planned urethroplasty** may provide temporary relief, but urethral rest is needed afterward for stricture maturation and reconstruction planning; EAU guidance advises suprapubic catheterization for incapacitating obstruction or retention during rest.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Catheter exchange over wire** through a difficult-to-access stricture; the S-shape navigates the urethra without re-instrumenting blindly.

For full procedural context, see [DVIU and Urethral Dilation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/dviu).

## Comparison of Urethral Dilation Methods

| Feature | **S-shaped coaxial** | [**Van Buren**](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/sounds-bougies/van-buren) | **Straight coaxial** | **Filiform + followers** | [**Balloon dilator**](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/sounds-bougies/balloon-dilator) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Curvature** | **Double curve (S)** matching pendulous + bulbar | Single distal J-curve | Straight | Flexible filiform tip | N/A (inflatable balloon) |
| **Guidewire-guided** | **Yes** | No (blind) | Yes | No (threaded coaxial assembly) | Yes |
| **Path through the urethra** | S-shaped shaft over a wire | Curved sound passed manually | Straight shaft over a wire | Flexible filiform with threaded followers | Balloon positioned at the stricture |
| **False-passage risk** | Depends on confirmed true-lumen wire access and atraumatic passage | Blind, rigid passage can create a false passage | Depends on confirmed true-lumen wire access and atraumatic passage | Blind passage can create a false passage | Depends on true-lumen access and whether placement is visualized |
| **Force mechanism** | Axial (shearing) | Axial (shearing) | Axial (shearing) | Axial (coaxial) | **Radial** |
| **Setup overhead** | Single instrument over wire | Single instrument blind | Single instrument over wire | Multi-component threaded assembly | Wire + balloon + inflator |

The S-shaped dilator combines a guidewire track with a curved shaft; the Van Buren is a curved sound passed without a guidewire.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Herschorn and Carrington adapted straight coaxial dilators to an S shape that followed the male urethral outline and appraised the design in 30 men.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Compared with a [balloon dilator](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/sounds-bougies/balloon-dilator), S-shaped dilators pass sequentially over a wire without an inflator; balloons expand radially, and Gelman et al. described a balloon system that permits dilation under direct vision.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> For patients with a recurrent bulbar stricture &lt;3 cm after at least two prior endoscopic treatments who are unfit for or decline urethroplasty, EAU 2026 strongly recommends offering paclitaxel-coated balloon dilation.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Technique

1. **Cystoscopic or fluoroscopic guidewire placement** across the stricture into the bladder. For necessary catheter placement across a stricture, AUA 2023 Statement 5 (Expert Opinion) advises dilation over a guidewire to prevent false passage or rectal injury; Freid and Smith described the Glidewire technique for traversing urethral obstruction.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
2. **Sequential dilation.** For Cook's AQ-coated S~Curve, immerse each dilator in sterile water or isotonic saline. With the penis up, introduce the smallest appropriate dilator over the wire with its French-size stamp facing up; alternatively, hold the penis down and begin stamp-down, then rotate 180 degrees at the bulbar urethra. Advance into the bladder until urine drains, and maintain wire position while exchanging successively larger dilators.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
3. **Catheter placement.** After the desired caliber is achieved, a Foley can be passed over the same guidewire to maintain access.
4. **Infection prevention.** Before elective dilation that breaches urethral mucosa, obtain a urine culture and treat asymptomatic bacteriuria (EAU strong recommendation).<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> Select perioperative prophylaxis according to the procedure, culture findings, patient risk, and local guidance.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

## Safety Profile

- **False passage.** Dewan's 32-child straight-wire/sheath series reported no false passages.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> EAU 2026 reports low complication rates in visually controlled dilation series (level 3 evidence), notes the lack of direct comparison with blind dilation, and weakly recommends visual control.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Bleeding and infection** were reported in a retrospective series of 310 men undergoing attempted S-curved dilation: among 303 successfully dilated men, 33 had postdilation UTI (12 required admission) and 11 had hematuria lasting more than two days or requiring irrigation.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Stricture recurrence.** The underlying biologic problem (mucosal rupture followed by fibroblast activation) is not solved by changing the instrument; durability remains determined by stricture length, etiology, and prior treatment burden. Balloon dilation applies radial rather than axial force, but the 2026 EAU guideline found no evidence that dilation and DVIU differ, and long-term recurrence data favoring balloon over sound-based methods are limited.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Current Practice Context

Blind metal-bougie dilation remains common in practice. In the Kaçtan survey of Turkish urologists (222 respondents, 11% response rate; conducted in 2021), 47% used blind metal bougies, 23% plastic dilators over a guidewire and 26% disposable catheters.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Non-metal methods were more common in academic centers (p = 0.04).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> The 2026 EAU guideline recommends visually controlled dilation over blind dilation (weak recommendation) and finds no evidence that dilation is superior to DVIU or the reverse, so the indications for single dilation match those for DVIU.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Limitations

- **Does not address the fibrotic substrate.** Like all axial-shearing dilators, the S-shaped coaxial stretches and ruptures scar; recurrence remains the limiting factor in long, dense, or radiation-induced strictures.
- **Requires a guidewire to traverse the true lumen.** A very tight but traversable stricture may admit a filiform; complete obliteration requires evaluation for drainage or reconstruction, not blind passage of a filiform or coaxial dilator.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Not a substitute for urethroplasty** in recurrent, long, penile, ischemic, or radiation-induced disease; durability is best for short bulbar strictures regardless of dilation method.
