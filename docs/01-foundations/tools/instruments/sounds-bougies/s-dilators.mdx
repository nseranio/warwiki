# S-Shaped Coaxial Dilators

**Double-curve ("S-shaped") urethral dilators** passed over a pre-placed guidewire, the engineering compromise between the anatomical conformity of the curved [Van Buren](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/sounds-bougies/van-buren) sound and the safety of guidewire-assisted dilation historically delivered by straight coaxial dilators or the [Balloon Dilator](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/sounds-bougies/balloon-dilator). The S-shape matches the natural double curve of the male urethra (pendulous urethra curving inferiorly, then bulbar urethra curving superiorly around the perineal body); the coaxial track over a guidewire constrains the dilator to the true lumen and is intended to reduce the principal complication of blind metal-bougie dilation, false-passage creation. Originally described by [Herschorn](/docs/roots/surgeons/h-r/sender-herschorn) and Carrington (2007).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Design and Rationale

- **Double-curve (S-shaped) profile** along the working length matches the pendulous-to-bulbar urethral curvature without the blind manipulation required by the single-curve Van Buren.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Coaxial central lumen** for over-the-wire passage. Sizing of the wire varies by set; a standard 0.035" guidewire (PTFE-coated or hydrophilic Glidewire) is typical, pre-placed across the stricture under cystoscopic or fluoroscopic vision. Check the maker's instructions for the exact set.
- **French sizing** in sequential graduated increments, allowing stepwise dilation from baseline caliber to the therapeutic endpoint.
- **Single-instrument-per-size** simplicity, with no threaded follower assembly as with [filiform and follower](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments) systems.

The Herschorn / Carrington design emerged from prototype iteration to settle on the S-curve geometry and insertion technique that allowed smooth passage from meatus to bladder neck in routine practice.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Reconstructive-Urology Uses

- **Recurrent bulbar or pan-urethral stricture** requiring dilation when blind metal-bougie passage has previously created false passage or carries high risk.
- **Anterior-urethral dilation in the patient with prior failed DVIU** where preserving the true lumen matters for the next intervention.
- **Pediatric urethral dilation.** Guidewire-assisted sheath dilation was reported without false passage or sepsis in an uncontrolled pediatric series (Dewan 2003, 32 patients, mean follow-up 16 months); the S-shape applies the same guidewire principle to the adult urethra.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **As a bridge to definitive urethroplasty** in the symptomatic patient awaiting reconstruction.
- **Catheter exchange over wire** through a difficult-to-access stricture; the S-shape navigates the urethra without re-instrumenting blindly.

For full procedural context, see [DVIU and Urethral Dilation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/dviu).

## Comparison of Urethral Dilation Methods

| Feature | **S-shaped coaxial** | [**Van Buren**](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/sounds-bougies/van-buren) | **Straight coaxial** | **Filiform + followers** | [**Balloon dilator**](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/sounds-bougies/balloon-dilator) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Curvature** | **Double curve (S)** matching pendulous + bulbar | Single distal J-curve | Straight | Flexible filiform tip | N/A (inflatable balloon) |
| **Guidewire-guided** | **Yes** | No (blind) | Yes | No (threaded coaxial assembly) | Yes |
| **Conforms to male urethra** | **Excellent** | Good | Poor | Variable | N/A |
| **False-passage risk** | Depends on confirmed true-lumen wire access and atraumatic passage | Blind, rigid passage can create a false passage | Depends on confirmed true-lumen wire access and atraumatic passage | Blind passage can create a false passage | Depends on true-lumen access and whether placement is visualized |
| **Force mechanism** | Axial (shearing) | Axial (shearing) | Axial (shearing) | Axial (coaxial) | **Radial** |
| **Setup overhead** | Single instrument over wire | Single instrument blind | Single instrument over wire | Multi-component threaded assembly | Wire + balloon + inflator |

The defining advantages over the Van Buren are guidewire-tracked safety and better anatomic fit. The defining advantage over the straight coaxial dilator is anatomic conformity; the straight coaxial fights the bulbar curve and demands more axial force.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Compared with a [balloon dilator](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/sounds-bougies/balloon-dilator), S-shaped dilators pass sequentially over a wire without an inflator; balloons expand radially, and Gelman et al. described a balloon system that permits dilation under direct vision.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> The cited treatment reviews do not establish a device-level cost advantage.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Technique

1. **Cystoscopic or fluoroscopic guidewire placement** across the stricture into the bladder. The AUA 2023 guideline states, in the context of catheter placement, that dilation over a guidewire is recommended to prevent false-passage formation; Freid and Smith described the Glidewire technique for traversing urethral obstruction.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
2. **Sequential dilation.** Pass S-shaped dilators in ascending French sizes over the wire, oriented so the S-curve tracks the pendulous-to-bulbar bend.
3. **Catheter placement.** After the desired caliber is achieved, a Foley can be passed over the same guidewire to maintain access.
4. **Antibiotic prophylaxis.** Assess the need for prophylaxis using the planned procedure, urine findings, patient risk and local guidance.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Safety Profile

- **False passage.** Expected to be lower than with blind metal-bougie dilation because the dilator is constrained to the wire-defined true lumen. Direct comparisons with blind sounds are lacking; the EAU reports a low complication rate with visually controlled, wire-guided dilation (level 3 evidence).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Urethral hemorrhage, UTI, bacteremia.** Standard urethral-instrumentation risks; comparable to other dilation modalities.
- **Stricture recurrence.** The underlying biologic problem (mucosal rupture followed by fibroblast activation) is not solved by changing the instrument; durability remains determined by stricture length, etiology, and prior treatment burden. Balloon dilation applies radial rather than axial force, but the 2026 EAU guideline found no evidence that dilation and DVIU differ, and long-term recurrence data favoring balloon over sound-based methods are limited.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Current Practice Context

Blind metal-bougie dilation remains common in practice. In the Kaçtan survey of Turkish urologists (222 respondents, 11% response rate; conducted in 2021), 47% used blind metal bougies, 23% plastic dilators over a guidewire and 26% disposable catheters.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> Non-metal methods were more common in academic centers (p = 0.04).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> The 2026 EAU guideline recommends visually controlled dilation over blind dilation (weak recommendation) and finds no evidence that dilation is superior to DVIU or the reverse, so the indications for single dilation match those for DVIU.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Limitations

- **Does not address the fibrotic substrate.** Like all axial-shearing dilators, the S-shaped coaxial stretches and ruptures scar; recurrence remains the limiting factor in long, dense, or radiation-induced strictures.
- **Requires a guidewire to traverse the true lumen.** A very tight but traversable stricture may admit a filiform; complete obliteration requires evaluation for drainage or reconstruction, not blind passage of a filiform or coaxial dilator.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Not a substitute for urethroplasty** in recurrent, long, penile, ischemic, or radiation-induced disease; durability is best for short bulbar strictures regardless of dilation method.
