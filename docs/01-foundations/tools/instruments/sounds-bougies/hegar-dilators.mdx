# Hegar Dilators

**Blunt-tipped, cylindrical, graduated metal dilators sized in millimeters (1–26 mm)** for mechanical cervical dilation in gynecologic and obstetric procedures, and a recurring tool in urogyn / reconstructive-urology practice for postmenopausal labial-fusion separation, cervical dilation during concomitant hysterectomy, urethral calibration, and catheterizable-stoma assessment. Named for Ernst Ludwig Alfred Hegar (1830–1914), the German gynecologist who also lends his name to the [Mayo-Hegar](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/needle-holders/mayo-hegar) and [Olsen-Hegar](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/needle-holders/olsen-hegar) needle holders.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Design

- **Blunt, rounded tip.** Distinct from the tapered Pratt; the blunt nose lets the operator feel the loss-of-resistance pop at the internal cervical os (the principal tactile cue during cervical dilation).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Cylindrical shaft** with uniform working-length diameter; sizes step in 1 mm or 0.5 mm increments.
- **Double-ended.** One instrument carries two sizes; the standard set spans roughly 1–26 mm.
- **Stainless steel**, autoclavable.
- **Larger size steps** may reduce the number of passes compared with tapered dilators, but comparative speed has not been established; blunt Hegar dilators require more force than tapered Pratt dilators.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Sizing — Hegar (mm) vs Pratt (Fr)

Hegar dilators are sized in millimeters of diameter; Pratt dilators (and most urology sounds) use the French system (1 Fr = 0.33 mm).

| Hegar (mm) | Approx. French | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 12 Fr | Sound-only access; early diagnostic hysteroscopy |
| 6 | 18 Fr | Diagnostic hysteroscope insertion |
| 8 | 24 Fr | Operative hysteroscope |
| 9 | 27 Fr | Operative hysteroscope (resect-ready) |
| 10–11 | 30–33 Fr | In one operative-hysteroscopy trial, dilation to Hegar 10 or 11 mm preceded insertion of a 26 Fr resectoscope<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>; 7–8 mm outer-sheath models also exist<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |

The Hegar-to-French conversion is approximate (1 Fr = 0.33 mm), and the size targets above are common practice rather than a standard. A wider baseline cervical width lessens the need for further dilation and the risk of false passage.

## Reconstructive-Urology and Urogyn Uses

### Concomitant gynecologic dilation during urogyn surgery

- **Cervical dilation before hysteroscopy** as part of the workup for postmenopausal bleeding, mesh-erosion–related symptoms, or pre-prolapse-surgery endometrial assessment. Select any necessary cervical dilation according to the chosen instrument’s outside diameter; KARL STORZ lists a 2.9 mm diagnostic scope, a 6 mm operative shaver, and 5–8.5 mm bipolar resectoscopes.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Cervical dilation at the time of vaginal or abdominal hysterectomy** when concurrent endometrial sampling or hysteroscopic evaluation is planned.
- **Intraoperative dilation during elective cesarean** to aid lochia drainage. A Cochrane review of 8 trials (2,227 women) found very low-quality evidence and no clear effect on postpartum hemorrhage, transfusion, febrile morbidity or endometritis, so the benefit is unproven.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### Postmenopausal labial-fusion separation

- **Nonsurgical Hegar-dilator separation of complete labial fusion** in postmenopausal patients when topical estrogen has failed. Serial Hegar dilators are used for blunt dissection to restore introital anatomy without sharp incision, a urogyn-specific use case described by Kaplan and colleagues.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### Urethral and orifice calibration

- **Calibration of the female urethra.** The metric Hegar set is convenient for sizing the introital and meatal caliber during workup of recurrent UTI, urethral stricture / stenosis, and post-radiation atrophy. Most operative urethral work in the male still uses [Van Buren](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/sounds-bougies/van-buren) French-sized sounds.
- **Pediatric urethral calibration** before pediatric hypospadias / urethroplasty, where the millimeter scale aligns with how pediatric urethras are reported.
- **Catheterizable channel (Mitrofanoff / Monti) calibration** at the stoma in clinic; the blunt tip and 1 mm increments fit the small caliber.
- **Vaginal-introital calibration after gender-affirming vaginoplasty.** Hegar dilators are sometimes used clinically as an in-office calibration set alongside the patient's prescribed neovaginal dilator regimen.

### Diagnostic — cervical-incompetence assessment

- **Nonpregnant Hegar passage as a cervical-compliance test** has been used historically. It has not been validated for diagnosing cervical insufficiency.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> Mentioned here because urogyn / RU clinicians sometimes inherit referrals based on this practice.

## Hegar vs Pratt vs Plastic Dilators

| Feature | **Hegar** | **Pratt** | **Denniston (plastic)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tip** | Blunt, rounded | Gradually tapered | Gradually tapered |
| **Material** | Stainless steel (reusable) | Stainless steel (reusable) | Plastic (disposable) |
| **Sizing** | Millimeters (1–26 mm) | French (9–79 Fr) | French equivalent |
| **Increments** | 1 mm (or 0.5 mm) | Smaller | Similar to Pratt |
| **Force required** | More | Less | Less |
| **Tactile feedback at internal os** | Better — clear loss-of-resistance pop | Less distinct | Less distinct |
| **Dilation speed** | Faster (larger steps) | Slower | Slower |
| **North American practice** | Minority preference | Predominant for surgical abortion | Common alternative |

The Society of Family Planning guideline (2016 update) notes that cervical dilation before first-trimester suction aspiration is usually done with tapered mechanical dilators.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

## Technique Pearls

- **Start small and step up sequentially.** Begin at 2 mm and advance through ascending sizes, recording the largest dilator that passes the internal os without resistance as the cervical width.
- **Record the width as the last size that passed freely.** If Hegar 4 passes without resistance but Hegar 5 requires force, the width is 4 mm.
- **Resistance at 4 mm.** Try 3 mm and then 2 mm without force; if 2 mm also meets resistance, document that it did not pass freely and assess for cervical stenosis.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Stabilize the cervix with a [tenaculum](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments)** and straighten the canal along the uterine axis before passing each dilator.
- **Apply steady, gentle axial pressure.** Abrupt force risks perforation and false-passage creation.
- **Consider cervical priming before operative hysteroscopy.** In a Cochrane review of 19 RCTs (1,870 women), misoprostol versus placebo or no treatment reduced the need for mechanical dilation (OR 0.08, 95% CI 0.04 to 0.16; 5 RCTs, 441 women), intraoperative complications (OR 0.37; 12 RCTs, 901 women), cervical laceration and false-track formation, with more minor side effects (abdominal pain, vaginal bleeding). Perforation and bleeding did not differ. Doses and timing varied between trials; follow local protocol.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Safety Profile

Cervical dilation with Hegar dilators carries the following risk profile:

The first two figures are procedure-level complication rates during first-trimester surgical abortion, irrespective of the dilator used.

- **Uterine perforation.** 0.1–4 / 1000 procedures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Cervical injury during first-trimester suction abortion.** 0.1–10 per 1000 procedures; higher in adolescents.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Hemorrhagic areas on endocervical histology.** Samples after dilation showed hemorrhagic areas in 90% of the Hegar group versus 32.5% of the continuous controllable balloon group in a randomized study of 120 pregnant women (40 per group) run by the developers of the balloon device.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Epithelial and stromal damage.** Hegar dilation produced epithelial damage in 95%, basal-membrane damage in 82.5%, and stromal damage in 62.5% of histologic specimens, all significantly higher than balloon dilation.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **False-passage creation.** Particularly when dilation is difficult; misoprostol priming reduces this risk.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Postabortal pelvic inflammatory disease.** Significantly lower with laminaria pretreatment than with Hegar dilation in a prospective study of 519 women randomized before vacuum aspiration.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Limitations

- **Higher axial force requirement** than tapered dilators, the limiting factor in the stenotic postmenopausal cervix.
- **Stepwise tactile cue** (loss of resistance at each new size) can be misleading in a previously instrumented cervix with a false passage.
- **Metric-versus-French confusion** when mixed with urologic sounds on the same back table; label clearly to avoid sizing errors.

## Historical Context — Alfred Hegar

**Ernst Ludwig Alfred Hegar (1830–1914)** was a German gynecologist and professor at the University of Freiburg whose contributions to gynecologic instrumentation, vaginal hysterectomy technique, and the diagnostic sign of early pregnancy (Hegar's sign: softening of the lower uterine segment on bimanual exam) shaped late-19th-century gynecology. The cylindrical dilator set bearing his name remains the global standard for metric-sized cervical dilation. He is also the namesake of the [Mayo-Hegar](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/needle-holders/mayo-hegar) and [Olsen-Hegar](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/needle-holders/olsen-hegar) needle holders.
