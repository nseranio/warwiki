# Gorget (Gourget)

The **gorget** (also spelled **gourget**) is a tapered, grooved director/retractor with historical roots in perineal lithotomy and external urethrotomy. The **Teale** or **Turner-Warwick/Teale grooved gorget** remains a useful reconstructive instrument for exposing a deep urethral lumen, guiding a stricturotomy and facilitating suture placement.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> It is an alternative or companion to a narrow [nasal speculum](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/retractors/nasal-speculum), not a universal requirement for urethroplasty.

## Contemporary Use

- **Bulbar stricturotomy** — a technical description of non-transecting bulbar urethroplasty uses a gorget to extend the dorsal incision proximally and distally into healthy-caliber urethra.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Deep proximal exposure and suturing** — an operative teaching video shows a Gorget retractor facilitating sutures at the proximal spatulation near the bulbomembranous junction.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Posterior urethral visualization** — a contemporary posterior substitution-urethroplasty review lists a 12 Fr nasal speculum, Turner-Warwick/Teale grooved gorget and lighted urethral speculum as alternative exposure tools; the authors prefer the lighted speculum when available.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Selected female reconstruction** — a 13-patient technical series used a gorget to assist sharp dissection of an elliptical graft bed during transurethral dorsal buccal-mucosa grafting.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

These examples support specific exposure maneuvers; they do not establish comparative superiority or define a standard tray.

## Current Pattern Examples

Dimensions vary by maker and pattern. A STERIS catalog lists a **large Teales gorget** tapered from 17 to 7 mm with probe (PH252009) and a **small** version tapered from 11 to 4.5 mm (PH252008).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> These measurements apply to those catalog items only. Verify the exact instrument, groove, taper, processing instructions and compatibility on the local tray rather than assuming an eponym fixes the specification.

## Historical Context

Gorgets were among the grooved directors and dilators used in historical perineal lithotomy, a group of operations that changed substantially across eras and regions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The **Teale gorget** was developed for perineal lithotomy and was later used with Wheelhouse's operation for difficult urethral stricture, where the groove provided a path into the urethra and bladder.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

Endoscopic lithotripsy and other less invasive stone procedures displaced the gorget from routine bladder-stone surgery, but open stone surgery did not simply vanish and bladder calculi remain clinically relevant. Its durable modern role is therefore best described narrowly: a grooved exposure/directing instrument retained in some reconstructive urethral techniques, not an obsolete relic repurposed wholesale. Broader histories trace lithotomy across ancient Indian, Greek, Arabic and European surgical traditions and into nineteenth-century practice.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
