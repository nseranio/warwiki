# Gelman Urethral Sound

**Contemporary reconstructive urethral sound** — graduated sizes, designed for controlled urethral instrumentation in a reconstructive setting.

The Gelman sound belongs with the modern urethral sounds used for calibration, gentle dilation, and intraoperative assessment of urethral caliber. A fuller instrument profile should distinguish its curve, tip design, and handling characteristics from the Van Buren, Guyon, and Haygrove sound families.

## Role in the Sound Set

This page intentionally stays practical because WARWIKI does not yet have a device-specific primary-source profile for the Gelman sound. The reconstructive role can still be placed from the surrounding instrument family:

| Instrument | Best fit |
|---|---|
| [Van Buren sound](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/sounds-bougies/van-buren) | Routine urethral dilation and anterior urethral calibration |
| [Guyon sound](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/sounds-bougies/guyon) | Curved / malleable calibration through the male urethral curve |
| [Haygrove sound](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/sounds-bougies/haygrove) | Antegrade localization of the proximal stump during posterior urethroplasty |
| **Gelman sound** | Modern reconstructive calibration / gentle dilation when controlled passage and sequential sizing are the goal |

## Practical Uses

- Define distal and proximal caliber during urethroplasty planning.
- Calibrate the reconstructed segment without forcing a cystoscope through fresh repair.
- Confirm that a meatus, fossa navicularis, or reconstructed segment accepts the intended size.
- Serve as a controlled exchange instrument during reconstructive urethral instrumentation.

## Safety Rules

The same rules that govern all urethral sounds apply:

- Never use a sound to "discover" the lumen by force.
- Stop when resistance is fixed, eccentric, or painful.
- Use guidewire- or endoscope-assisted access for complex false passages, obliterative strictures, and radiated outlets.
- Avoid repeated blind dilation as a substitute for definitive reconstruction when the disease pattern calls for urethroplasty.
- Document the size that passes easily and the size at which resistance begins; the difference is often more useful than a single maximum French number.

## Current Positioning

The Gelman sound is best treated as part of a modern reconstructive urethral calibration tray rather than as a standalone therapy. It supports decision-making and operative assessment; it does not change the biology of recurrent stricture disease. For procedural context, see [DVIU and Urethral Dilation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/dviu), [Male Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/male-urethroplasty), and [Posterior Urethral Stenosis / BNC-VUAS reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction).
