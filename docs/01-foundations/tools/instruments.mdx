# Surgical Instruments — A Reconstructive Urologist's Reference

A working reference to the instruments seen on a functional-urology and genitourinary-reconstruction tray. Common instruments (Adson forceps, Allis clamps, Yankauer suction) get short entries — you know them already. The instruments that define reconstructive practice — **Turner-Warwick Ryder, Heaney driver, Haygrove sound, Lone Star retractor, perineal Bookwalter, Ravini lighted speculum, Raz-Pereyra trocar** — get expanded entries with history, design rationale, and specific reconstructive use cases.

See also: [Needles](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/needles), [Sutures](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/sutures), [Incisions &amp; Closure](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/incisions-closure), [Surgical Ergonomics](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/surgical-ergonomics), [Robotics](/docs/foundations/tools/robotics).

## Needle Holders

### Turner-Warwick Ryder

The **Turner-Warwick Ryder** is **Richard Turner-Warwick's** modification of the Ryder needle holder — elongated shanks, fine jaws, and a narrow box lock, designed specifically for **deep pelvic and posterior-urethral suture placement** in the restricted field of perineal urethroplasty. The elongated profile places the surgeon's hand well above the wound, preserving line-of-sight in the narrow perineal cone created by exaggerated lithotomy and retractor exposure. Turner-Warwick's UK urethroplasty tradition — refined at the Institute of Urology (UCL) and carried forward by Andrich, Mundy, and the contemporary British and North American reconstructive schools — built much of its technique around this one instrument.

**Use:** posterior urethroplasty, VUA reconstruction, deep bulbar anastomosis, any deep pelvic reconstruction where a standard-length needle driver runs out of reach.

### Ryder Needle Holder

The parent of the Turner-Warwick variant. **Fine, delicate jaws** designed for **small-diameter needles** (4-0 and finer), with a shorter shank than the Turner-Warwick modification. Workhorse for microsurgical and reconstructive anastomotic work where precise needle control matters more than gripping force.

### Heaney Needle Driver

A **curved-jaw, curved-handle** needle holder designed by **Noble Sproat Heaney** (1880–1955, Chicago) to place sutures around pedicles during vaginal and abdominal hysterectomy. The double curvature — jaws angled to the shaft, shaft angled to the handle — allows the needle to be driven **perpendicular to the tissue plane** while the surgeon's hand remains outside the pelvic cone. It remains one of the most useful needle holders on a **perineal urethroplasty, VVF repair, apical prolapse suspension, or deep-pelvic reconstruction** tray, where space is limited and suture angles are unusual.

### Castroviejo

Micro needle holder with **spring-loaded handles** (no ring closures) and **ultra-fine jaws** — designed by Ramón Castroviejo for ophthalmic microsurgery. In reconstructive urology it is used for **microsurgical vasovasostomy, vasoepididymostomy, and fine urethral work** with 8-0 to 10-0 suture.

### Olsen-Hegar

A **combined needle holder and scissors** — the jaws hold the needle, and a small pair of scissors sits just proximal to the jaws, allowing the surgeon to cut suture without exchanging instruments. Useful for the sole surgeon operating without a second assistant, and for high-volume running closure. Slightly less precise than a dedicated needle holder; the built-in scissors occasionally catch unintended tissue.

## Tissue Forceps

### Adson

**Fine, narrow tissue forceps** with small teeth — the standard for handling **skin edges and subcutaneous tissue** during wound closure. Thumb forceps with delicate tips.

### DeBakey

**Long, atraumatic vascular forceps** designed by Michael DeBakey for cardiovascular surgery. The **serrated-but-toothless jaws** hold tissue securely without crushing. Now universal across abdominal, pelvic, and reconstructive surgery — the default forceps for handling bladder, bowel, and deep pelvic tissues.

### Bonney

**Heavy, toothed tissue forceps** from the classic gynecologic tradition. Used for firm grasp of **fascia, vaginal cuff, and dense pelvic tissue** where Adsons are too delicate and DeBakeys lack purchase.

### Russian

Distinctive **"star-burst" or "saucer" tip** — flat, circular, with multiple small teeth on the working face. Spreads grasping force broadly; good for handling **thick, mobile tissue** (muscle, fascia) without slipping.

### Singley

**Long, angled tips with fine serrations** — designed for **atraumatic handling of bowel and ureter**. Frequently seen on urinary-diversion trays (ileal conduit, neobladder, Mitrofanoff).

### Wangensteen

**Long, fine-tipped tissue forceps** named for **Owen Wangensteen** (1898–1981, University of Minnesota). Used for **precision deep-pelvic dissection** and handling of fine pedicled structures. Wangensteen was also the namesake of the Wangensteen suction system and the transformer of American surgical education.

## Clamps

### Allis

**Toothed, angled tissue clamp** designed by **Oscar Huntington Allis** (1836–1921). The classic "hold-everything" clamp for skin, fascia, and tissue edges that need to stay put — heavier purchase than a Babcock, more traumatic than a DeBakey. Ubiquitous across general and reconstructive surgery.

### Babcock

**Atraumatic tissue-grasping clamp** designed by **William Wayne Babcock** (1872–1963, Temple University). The **fenestrated, ring-shaped jaw** distributes pressure and avoids crushing — the go-to clamp for **handling bowel, ureter, fallopian tube, and other delicate hollow viscera** during urinary diversion and reconstruction.

### Kocher

**Heavy, fully-toothed tissue clamp** designed by **Emil Theodor Kocher** (1841–1917, Bern; 1909 Nobel laureate). Used for **fascia, dense scar, and tissue that will be discarded or closely approximated** — the "grab and hold" clamp where tissue crushing doesn't matter because the tissue is either being removed or being included in a secure closure.

## Cautery &amp; Bipolar

### Electrosurgical Pencil

Standard **monopolar electrosurgical handpiece** — the workhorse for fascial incision, subcutaneous dissection, and non-critical hemostasis. Comes with various tip options (blade, needle, ball).

### Colorado Tip

An **ultra-fine needle-tip monopolar electrode** — essentially a sharpened wire that concentrates current into a millimeter-scale effect. Used for **precise cutting with minimal lateral thermal spread** in delicate fields (penile skin, urethral plate, vaginal wall).

### Gerald Bipolar Forceps

**Fine-tipped bipolar forceps** for precision hemostasis in delicate fields — vasal branches, dartos vessels, periurethral veins. Standard for microsurgical and reconstructive dissection where monopolar spread would damage critical structures.

### Malis Mirror-Finish Bipolar Forceps

**Polished, mirror-finish bipolar** — designed by **Leonard Malis** (neurosurgeon, Mount Sinai) with a highly polished working surface to **prevent tissue sticking and char buildup**. The signature feature allows repeated coagulation without interrupting workflow to clean the tips. Widely used in reconstructive and microsurgical fields where stickiness interrupts delicate dissection.

## Retractors

### Adson-Beckman

**Self-retaining wound retractor** with spring-opposed blades and a ratcheted frame. Useful for **scrotal, inguinal, and superficial perineal exposures** where the wound is shallow and a full self-retaining system is overkill.

### Lone Star Retractor System

The **Lone Star** (Lone Star Medical Products / CooperSurgical) is a **disposable elastic-stay retractor system** — a flexible plastic ring with a series of small elastic stays (hooks) that are anchored at any point around the ring to retract tissue edges. It has become the **dominant retractor for urethral and perineal reconstruction**: urethroplasty, VVF repair, vaginal reconstruction, phalloplasty pedicle dissection, hypospadias repair. The key advantages are the **infinite adjustability** of retraction angles, the **atraumatic nature** of the elastic stays compared with metal retractor blades, and the ability to be repositioned intraoperatively without re-setup.

**Use:** bulbar urethroplasty, perineal urethrostomy, VVF repair, vaginal prolapse repair, hypospadias repair, phalloplasty harvest and insetting, scrotoplasty, perineoplasty.

### Perineal Bookwalter (Jordan Retractor)

The **perineal Bookwalter** is a specialized **ring retractor system** for deep posterior urethroplasty, developed in close collaboration with **Gerald H. Jordan** (Eastern Virginia Medical School). The system pairs a Bookwalter-style ring with **specific perineal blades** that hook under the ischial tuberosities and retract the thighs, creating the exaggerated lithotomy cone that posterior urethroplasty requires. Essential for **posterior urethroplasty, perineal prostatectomy, and redo bulbar urethroplasty** where deep, stable exposure must be maintained for hours.

### Heaney Retractor

**Right-angled vaginal retractor** — flat blade with a handle perpendicular to the blade. Used for **retracting the anterior or posterior vaginal wall** during vaginal surgery. Also useful for retracting bladder or rectum during VVF or rectovaginal fistula repair.

### Breisky (Brisky) Retractor

**Long, narrow, right-angled vaginal retractor** — named for **August Breisky** (1832–1889, Vienna). The **elongated narrow blade** reaches the deepest vaginal recesses — essential for **apical exposure** during uterosacral ligament suspension, sacrospinous ligament fixation, and deep VVF or vaginal reconstruction.

### Weighted Speculum

**Self-retaining floor retractor** for vaginal surgery — a weighted posterior blade sits in the posterior fornix, using gravity to retract the posterior vaginal wall. Frees one assistant and provides stable posterior exposure for the duration of the case.

### Mouth Retractors

For **buccal mucosa graft (BMG) harvest** in urethroplasty, several intraoral retractors may be used: the **Denhardt mouth gag** (spring-loaded jaw opener), a **Molt mouth gag**, or simple **cheek retractors** to expose the buccal mucosa. See [Buccal Mucosa Graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa) for harvest technique.

### Nasal Speculum — Repurposed for Deep Narrow Exposure

The **nasal speculum** — ordinarily an ENT instrument — has found a specific niche in reconstructive urology for **deep, narrow exposure** where a standard vaginal or perineal retractor is too wide. Applications include **exposure of the dorsal vein complex and membranous urethra** during complex redo pelvic work, **small-field urethroplasty exposures**, and occasional use in **salvage prostatectomy dissection**. The slim parallel blades preserve line-of-sight in a corridor too narrow for standard retractors.

## Urethral Sounds &amp; Bougies

### Otis Bougie-à-Boule

**Ball-tip urethral sound**, designed by Fessenden Nott Otis (1825–1900). Used for **calibrated urethral luminal assessment** — the ball passes smoothly through a patent urethra and "hangs up" at a stricture, giving both location and caliber. Classic diagnostic and intraoperative tool before flexible cystoscopy.

### Van Buren Sound

**Curved-tip, tapered metal sound** in sequential French sizes. The workhorse for **urethral dilation** and for defining the normal-caliber urethra at urethroplasty. **Poorly suited for posterior urethroplasty in exaggerated lithotomy** — the Van Buren's curve is concentrated at the tip and does not follow the path from the suprapubic approach to the membranous urethra. The Haygrove sound (below) is preferred for that application.

### Guyon Sound

**Curved, malleable urethral sound** — named for **Jean Casimir Félix Guyon** (1831–1920, Paris), the founder of French urology. Comes in a set of graduated French sizes. Used for atraumatic urethral dilation and calibration.

### Haygrove Sound

The **semicircular Haygrove sound** is designed specifically for **posterior urethroplasty**. Unlike the Van Buren (which curves only at its tip), the Haygrove's full-length **semicircular profile** follows the natural path from the **suprapubic cystotomy down to the membranous urethra** in a patient positioned in exaggerated lithotomy. It is used to cannulate the proximal urethral stump through the bladder neck, define the normal proximal urethral caliber, and serve as an intraoperative locator for the anastomotic target during posterior urethroplasty.

### Gelman Urethral Sound

**Contemporary reconstructive urethral sound** — graduated sizes, designed for atraumatic urethral instrumentation in a reconstructive setting.

### Gouget / Gourget

*Entry incomplete — to be filled in.* A curved urethral instrument occasionally seen in European reconstructive practice; may be a regional or historical variant of the curved-sound family (Guyon, Béniqué lineage). If this is on your tray with a specific design or use case, please add detail.

## Urethral Specialty Instruments

### Ravini Lighted Urethral Speculum

The **Ravini** is a **self-retaining, lighted urethral speculum** — a contemporary instrument designed specifically to improve exposure during **female urethroplasty**. The built-in illumination and self-retaining profile provide direct visualization of the female urethra through a narrow introital corridor without requiring a separate assistant to hold a light or retractor. Available in several models (Ravini B, Ravini C) with different profiles.

**Use:** female urethral stricture repair, female urethral diverticulum resection, any exposure of the female urethra where visualization through the introitus is limited. Early single-center series report **71% success** across 7 female urethroplasties using the Ravini for exposure — small numbers but representative of its intended niche.

### Raz-Pereyra Trocar (Ligature Carrier)

The **Raz-Pereyra trocar** is the **ligature-carrying trocar** used for **needle suspension urethropexy** — specifically Shlomo Raz's modification of Armand Pereyra's original 1959 bladder-neck suspension. The trocar is passed transvaginally from a small anterior vaginal wall incision, up through the retropubic space, and out through a suprapubic skin stab; a braided suture previously anchored in the pubocervical fascia is loaded onto the trocar and pulled up to the rectus fascia for tying. Although the indications for needle-suspension procedures have narrowed considerably in the era of synthetic midurethral slings, the Raz-Pereyra technique remains a reference procedure in the urogynecologic literature and the trocar appears in complex redo or sling-revision cases.

**Shlomo Raz** (UCLA) is also the namesake of multiple other urogynecologic procedures and the informal head of the **URPS Raz lineage** (see [Surgical Genealogy](/docs/roots/surgical-lineage)).

## Suction

### Yankauer

**Rigid oral / field suction** with a curved tip and large bore — the standard for non-critical suction in an open wound. Rapid, forgiving, the default.

### Poole (Pool) Suction

**Multi-hole perforated suction** — a cylindrical tube with a perforated outer sleeve that prevents the suction tip from grabbing and injuring tissue when the abdomen is full of fluid. Used for **large-volume evacuation** (blood, ascites, irrigation) during abdominal cases.

### Frazier Suction

**Fine, tapered suction** with a thumb-controlled vent hole for suction modulation. The default for **delicate reconstructive fields** — urethroplasty, microsurgery, phalloplasty pedicle dissection — where the Yankauer's bore is too aggressive.

## Skin &amp; Graft Harvest

### Dermatome

**Powered or hand-held skin-harvesting instrument** — produces calibrated **split-thickness skin grafts (STSG)** of uniform thickness. Used in reconstructive urology for **scrotal reconstruction after Fournier's gangrene**, resurfacing after radical skin excision for hidradenitis or buried penis, and as coverage over tissue expanders. See [STSG](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/stsg).

## Historical Notes

Several of these instruments carry the names of defining figures in reconstructive urology and pelvic surgery:

- **Richard Turner-Warwick** (1925–2020) — Institute of Urology (UCL); founded the British school of posterior urethroplasty. The Turner-Warwick Ryder is one of several instruments he designed or modified. See [biography](/docs/roots/surgeons/s-z/richard-turner-warwick).
- **Noble Sproat Heaney** (1880–1955) — Chicago surgeon who developed the Heaney technique of vaginal hysterectomy and the associated retractor and driver.
- **Gerald H. Jordan** — EVMS; co-developer of the perineal Bookwalter system; founder of the Devine-Jordan reconstructive tradition. See [biography](/docs/roots/surgeons/h-r/gerald-jordan).
- **Shlomo Raz** — UCLA; developer of the Raz needle-suspension modification (and multiple other urogynecologic operations).
- **Armand Pereyra** — original 1959 needle-suspension urethropexy.
- **Jean Casimir Félix Guyon** (1831–1920) — Paris; founder of French urology and namesake of the Guyon sound.
- **Fessenden Nott Otis** (1825–1900) — New York; developer of the Otis urethrotome and bougie-à-boule.
- **Oscar Huntington Allis**, **William Wayne Babcock**, **Emil Theodor Kocher**, **Owen Wangensteen**, **August Breisky**, **Michael DeBakey**, **Ramón Castroviejo**, **Leonard Malis** — the broader pantheon of surgeons whose names remain on the tray.
