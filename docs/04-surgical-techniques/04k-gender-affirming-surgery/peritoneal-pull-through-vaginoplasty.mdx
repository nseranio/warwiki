# Robotic / Laparoscopic Peritoneal Pull-Through Vaginoplasty

**Robotic or laparoscopic peritoneal pull-through vaginoplasty** is an **emerging full-depth feminizing genital GAS technique** that uses **pedicled pelvic peritoneal flaps** — harvested abdominally — to line the proximal (or full) neovaginal canal, either **augmenting** standard penile inversion vaginoplasty (PIV) or **replacing** penile / scrotal skin as the canal lining.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Adapted from the **Davydov procedure** for cisgender women with Mayer–Rokitansky–Küster–Hauser (MRKH) syndrome, it has been rapidly adopted in transfeminine GAS since 2016–2017, primarily to address **genital skin insufficiency** and to deliver **consistent neovaginal depth (~14 cm) regardless of available natal tissue**.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The largest single-center experience encompasses **500 consecutive cases** with a quantified learning curve.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

This is the dedicated atlas page. For the cohort-level decision framework, see [Feminizing Gender-Affirming Surgery](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/feminizing-surgery). For the skin-lined gold standard, see [Penile Inversion Vaginoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/penile-inversion-vaginoplasty). For the canal-not-desired alternative, see [Zero-Depth Vulvoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/zero-depth-vulvoplasty).

## Historical Origins

The use of pelvic peritoneum for neovaginal construction was first described by **Davydov in 1969** for cisgender vaginal agenesis.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> The laparoscopic adaptation was matured by the Zhao MRKH series (n = 83) achieving mean neovaginal length 8.2 cm and 95.3% functional success.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

Adaptation to transfeminine GAS was pioneered by **Zhao and Bluebond-Langner (NYU Langone) in 2017**, combining robotic peritoneal-flap harvest with standard PIV.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The **Belgrade group (Bizic / Djordjevic)** independently developed a laparoscopic peritoneal pull-through technique beginning in 2016.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> These two centres anchor the two main contemporary variants — **robotic peritoneal-flap vaginoplasty (RPGAV)** and **laparoscopy-assisted peritoneal pull-through**.

## Indications

| Indication | Rationale | Anchor |
|---|---|---|
| **Penoscrotal hypoplasia from puberty suppression (GnRH agonists)** | Tanner-2/3-blocked patients have insufficient penile + scrotal skin for standard PIV; increasingly common indication | Dy 2023<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>; Castanon 2022<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Radical circumcision** | Loss of preputial / distal-shaft skin reduces canal-lining and vulvar-construction tissue (16/52 in Belgrade) | Castanon 2022<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Scrotal skin insufficiency** | Prior orchiectomy with atrophy, lichen sclerosus, or naturally small scrotum | Castanon 2022<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Prior orchiectomy as bridge** | 3× greater odds of needing extragenital tissue | Sineath 2022<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| **Patient preference** | Self-lubrication, hairless canal — 13/52 (25%) of Belgrade cohort chose peritoneal pull-through over PIV despite adequate skin | Castanon 2022<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Revision / salvage of failed PIV** | Refractory canal stenosis or depth loss after PIV; minimal donor-site morbidity vs sigmoid salvage | Dy 2021<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |

## Surgical Technique

All variants combine a perineal phase (standard PIV-style vulvoplasty + initial canal dissection) with an abdominal (robotic or laparoscopic) phase for peritoneal-flap harvest and transabdominal canal completion.

### A. Robotic Peritoneal-Flap Vaginoplasty (RPGAV, Zhao / NYU)

Single-stage combined perineal–abdominal procedure:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

**Perineal phase:**

1. Standard penile disassembly — orchiectomy, degloving, preservation of dorsal NVB with glans.
2. Corpora cavernosa excision.
3. Urethral shortening and repositioning.
4. Penile skin tube inversion (± scrotal FTSG) forming the **distal** canal.
5. Clitoroplasty, clitoral hood, labia minora, labia majora.

**Robotic abdominal phase:**

1. **Port placement** — da Vinci Xi (multiport) or **Single Port (SP)**. SP enables a **dual-surgeon two-team approach** (one abdominally, one perineally simultaneously), reducing operative time.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
2. **Peritoneal-flap harvest** — two ~6 × 8 cm flaps from:
   - The **posterior bladder wall** (anterior flap).
   - The **anterior rectosigmoid peritoneum / pararectal fossa** (posterior flap).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
3. **Transabdominal canal completion** — bladder–rectum space dissected from above under direct robotic vision, meeting the perineal dissection. Improves visualisation and may reduce rectal-injury risk vs blind perineal dissection.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
4. **Flap anastomosis** — inverted penile skin tube (± scrotal graft) sutured to peritoneal flaps which form the **apex**, adding **~5 cm of additional depth**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
5. **Apex closure** — proximal flap edges approximated to create a closed neovaginal dome (cul-de-sac).
6. Vaginal stent / conformer placement.

### B. Laparoscopy-Assisted Peritoneal Pull-Through (Belgrade)

1. Two peritoneal flaps from posterior bladder and anterior rectosigmoid harvested by standard laparoscopy.
2. Vaginal channel created by **combined perineal + laparoscopic** dissection.
3. Well-vascularised flaps **maximally mobilised and pulled through** to join inverted penile skin.
4. Flaps joined laterally to create the canal.
5. External genital reconstruction.
6. Vaginal packing for 7 days postoperatively.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### C. Single-Pedicled Peritoneal Flap (Morelli modification)

A single posterior-bladder peritoneal flap, pedicled on the neovaginal dome, overturned and sutured circumferentially to the skin cylinder. Simplifies the abdominal phase while preserving depth augmentation. In 8 patients, no intra- or postoperative complications.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

### D. Full-Length Peritoneal-Flap Vaginoplasty (Ratanalert 2025)

Anteriorly based peritoneal turnover flap creating a **full-length peritoneal lining** throughout the canal — **no skin lining required**. At 3 months, 80% maintained immediate-postoperative depth, with mucosal surface, high elasticity, and self-lubrication on internal exam.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

## Consistent Depth Regardless of Genital Tissue

The key reconstructive advantage: a Blasdel 2023 case-control study (43 patients with genital hypoplasia vs non-hypoplastic controls) found **equivalent neovaginal depth outcomes** after RPGAV — median depth **14.5 cm with no depth-revision surgeries** in the hypoplastic group.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> This represents a major reconstructive advantage over standard PIV, in which penoscrotal hypoplasia is a structural barrier to adequate depth.

## Complications

Generally comparable to or lower than standard PIV, with the addition of **intra-abdominal complications** unique to peritoneal-flap harvest:

| Complication | Incidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Vaginal stenosis | ~7% | Most common functional complication<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Blood transfusion | ~6% | Combined-procedure-related |
| **Intra-abdominal (total)** | **2.2% (6/274)** | Unique to peritoneal technique<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| Bowel obstruction | ~2% | Small-bowel; usually conservative |
| Internal hernia | 0.7% (2/274) | At flap closure or donor site; requires diagnostic laparoscopy + reduction<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| Pelvic abscess | ~1% | Laparoscopic drainage |
| Rectovaginal fistula | ~1% | Major; operative repair |
| Postoperative hematoma | ~1.1% | Occasionally reoperative |
| Labial hematoma (Belgrade) | 5.8% (3/52) | Resolved without surgery |
| Introital dehiscence (Belgrade) | 1.9% (1/52) | Conservative |

**Internal-hernia recognition** at the flap donor or closure site is the technique-specific safety pearl: in the Robinson 2022 274-patient series, 2 incarcerated internal hernias required diagnostic laparoscopy and reduction.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

## Histology and the Self-Lubrication Question

The widely cited "self-lubrication" advantage has been **substantially tempered** by recent histologic work:

- **Cisgender MRKH biopsies** showed peritoneal lining undergoing **metaplasia from mesothelial → stratified squamous epithelium** within 6–12 months, with glycogen storage and Schiller-test iodine positivity.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Transfeminine RPGAV biopsies (Dhami 2025; ≥ 12 mo post-op)** — **all 5 biopsies showed complete metaplasia to stratified squamous epithelium with no residual mesothelial cells**. The peritoneal neovagina **may produce fluid initially but is unlikely to maintain that capacity long-term**.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- **60% of biopsies** in the same series showed histologic features mimicking low-grade condylomatous dysplasia — likely **chronic reactive change** rather than true dysplasia; one case required p16 immunostaining to exclude high-grade dysplasia. Implications for **tailored cancer surveillance** are not yet defined.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- Despite this, the Belgrade series reported **~96% patient-reported lubrication satisfaction** at 29-month mean follow-up — clinical lubrication may remain adequate for some patients even as the histologic substrate squamatises.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Microbiome:** initial dysbiosis (64.5% in the first 2 yr) gradually normalises (39.1% > 2 yr), with ~57.4% achieving vaginal pH ≤ 4.5.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

## Direct Comparison vs Standard PIV

Zucchi 2025 (19 patients: 11 PIV, 8 RPGAV) — the **first direct head-to-head comparison**:<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

- **Significantly higher improvement** in "quality of sexual intercourse" (87.5% vs 27.3%, p = 0.04) and "overall sexual satisfaction" (87.5% vs 27.3%, p = 0.04) in RPGAV.
- **No significant difference** in orgasm quality, speed, or erogenous sensitivity.
- Similar complication rates; 1 Clavien–Dindo ≥ IIIb in the RPGAV arm.

Caveat: small, retrospective, single-centre — hypothesis-generating only.

## Learning Curve

The largest single-centre cohort (Hemal 2025; **500 consecutive cases**) quantified the learning curve:<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- **300 cases** were required to plateau operative time — a substantial curve reflecting technical complexity.
- After adjusting for the curve, each 1-point increase in **BMI added 1.4 min** (p < 0.05).
- Median operative time in the expert phase: **125 min** (IQR 105–181).

---

## Revision / Salvage Use

RPGAV is also a valuable **salvage technique** for refractory canal stenosis after failed PIV, with **minimal donor-site morbidity** vs sigmoid salvage:<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

- Dy 2021 (n = 24 revisions): mean postoperative depth **13.6 cm** (range 10.9–14.5), width 3.6 cm at mean follow-up 410 days.
- Median time from primary vaginoplasty to revision: **35.3 mo** (range 6–252).
- **No rectal injuries**; no intraoperative complications attributable to peritoneal-flap harvest.
- One patient returned to OR for postoperative canal bleeding.

## Advantages and Disadvantages

| Advantage | Mechanism / Anchor |
|---|---|
| Consistent ~14 cm depth regardless of donor skin | Blasdel 2023 hypoplasia equivalence<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Hairless canal lining | Peritoneum does not bear hair follicles<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| Transient self-lubrication | Peritoneal serous secretion before squamous metaplasia<sup>[[17]](#ref17)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Robust flap vascularity | Reduces stenosis vs free graft<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Minimal donor-site morbidity | No external donor scar<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Transabdominal canal vision | Improves rectal-space visualisation<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |

| Disadvantage | Anchor |
|---|---|
| Robotic / laparoscopic expertise required; 300-case learning curve | Hemal 2025<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| Intra-abdominal complications (2.2%) — internal hernia, SBO, abscess | Robinson 2022<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| Self-lubrication likely transient (mesothelial → squamous metaplasia) | Dhami 2025<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> |
| Reactive histologic atypia mimicking dysplasia — surveillance undefined | Dhami 2025<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> |
| Primary peritoneal graft predicts dilation difficulty (OR 3.20) | Shamamian 2025 (cited in feminizing-surgery DB) |
| Limited long-term data (< 5 yr median follow-up in most series) | Tay 2022 SR<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> |

## Comparison vs Intestinal (Sigmoid) Vaginoplasty

| Feature | Peritoneal | Sigmoid (Intestinal) |
|---|---|---|
| Depth | ~14 cm | 12–15 cm |
| Self-lubrication | Transient (squamatises) | Persistent (mucus-secreting mucosa) |
| Donor-site morbidity | Minimal | Significant (bowel anastomosis) |
| Surgical complexity | Moderate (robotic / laparoscopic) | High (laparotomy / laparoscopy + bowel) |
| Excessive discharge | No | Common (mucus overproduction) |
| Odor | Minimal | Can be significant |
| Stenosis risk | ~7% | Lower (De Rosa SR: 0.20%) |
| Diversion-colitis-like risk | No | Yes |
| Primary vs revision | Both | Primarily revision / salvage |

Anchors: Jacoby<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>, Castanon<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>, Morrison 2023 review<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>, van der Sluis intestinal review<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>, di Summa colic<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>.

---

## Evidence Limitations

The evidence base is **retrospective single-centre case series** with median follow-up < 5 years.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[25]](#ref25)</sup> Tay 2022 SR identified only level-4–5 evidence, with healing times poorly reported, dysphoria-alleviation under-discussed, and complications reported by only half of publications.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> Dhami 2025 histology challenges the widely cited self-lubrication advantage and raises surveillance questions that are not yet operationalised.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> Long-term depth maintenance, sexual function, and cancer-screening implications remain open questions.
