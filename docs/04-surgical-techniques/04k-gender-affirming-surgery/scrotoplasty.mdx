# Masculinizing Scrotoplasty

**Masculinizing scrotoplasty** in transmasculine patients uses the labia majora, embryologic homologs of the scrotum, to construct a neoscrotum, with the Hoebeke pedicled-labia-majora U-flap with 90° medial rotation as the modern canonical technique.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The Hoebeke technique was developed by the Ghent gender team (Selvaggi, Hoebeke, Ceulemans, Monstrey) based on 300+ scrotal reconstructions since 1993.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> It combines a V-Y advancement with a 90° medial rotation of superiorly based labia-majora flaps, addressing the two fundamental problems of earlier techniques: insufficient tissue volume and excessively posterior scrotal positioning.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In the Amsterdam series of testicular prostheses (Pigot 2019; 206 patients, 1992–2018), explantation occurred in 6 of 52 patients (11.5%) after Hoebeke scrotoplasty and in 20.8% overall across all techniques.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For the cohort-level framework, see [Masculinizing Gender-Affirming Surgery](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/masculinizing-surgery). For the downstream prosthetic component, see [Testicular Implants (Gender-Affirming)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/testicular-implants). For the cross-indication scrotal-reconstruction ladder, see [Scrotal Reconstruction Techniques](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/scrotal-reconstruction).

## Historical Context and Rationale

The core challenge of scrotoplasty in transmasculine patients is that the labia majora, embryologic homologs of the scrotum, are positioned too posteriorly (between the legs) and often provide insufficient tissue bulk to simulate a natural scrotum.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Era | Technique | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| **1993 (Hage bifid)** | Simple V-Y advancement of labial skin | **Bifid scrotum remains too posterior**; n = 50: implant expulsion **7%**, dislocation **11%**<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **1993 (Sengezer tissue expansion)** | Expanders placed in labia majora months before phalloplasty | Natural colour and hair match but **requires additional surgical stage** + expander complications<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **2009 (Hoebeke V-Y + 90° rotation)** | Cranially pedicled U-flaps with rotation + fat-pad relocation | **Anatomically correct anterior position + adequate bulk in a single stage**; the most frequently performed technique at the Amsterdam centre<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

The Hoebeke technique was developed specifically to overcome the posterior positioning of simple V-Y advancement and the staged-surgery requirement of tissue expansion.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgical Technique — Step by Step

Performed with the patient in lithotomy position, typically concurrent with phalloplasty (RFFF or alternative) and often with vaginectomy / colpocleisis.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Step 1 — Flap design

Three tissue components are designed:<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- **Bilateral cranially (superiorly) pedicled U-shaped labia-majora flaps.** Primary scrotal tissue.
- **Pedicled horseshoe-shaped pubic flap.** Superior / anterior scrotal coverage.
- **Clitoral-hood tissue.** Additional coverage incorporated into the reconstruction.

### Step 2 — Flap elevation

- U-shaped labia-majora flaps elevated bilaterally, maintaining the superior (cranial) pedicle to preserve blood supply from the external pudendal arterial system.
- **Inner part of labia minora resected** (or, in patients undergoing UL, used for the fixed part of the neourethra).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

### Step 3 — V-Y advancement

Labia-majora flaps advanced anteriorly using V-Y principle, moving tissue from native posterior position toward the anterior / inferior pubic region.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Step 4 — 90° medial rotation — the key innovation

Cranially pedicled U-flaps rotated 90° medially to bring the neoscrotum in front of the legs, the distinguishing step from simple V-Y advancement. Repositions the neoscrotum into the anatomically correct male position (anterior to thighs, inferior to neophallus).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Step 5 — Fat-pad relocation for bulk

Pedicled labia-majora fat pads released bilaterally and relocated into the neoscrotum for bulkiness simulating scrotal volume. Labial skin alone, even after advancement / rotation, does not provide sufficient volume.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Step 6 — Horseshoe pubic flap inset

Pedicled horseshoe-shaped pubic flap and clitoral-hood tissue incorporated into the superior aspect of the neoscrotum, giving additional skin coverage and a natural penoscrotal junction.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

### Step 7 — Perineal reconstruction

- **Without UL:** urethral meatus and vaginal orifice (if vaginectomy not performed) diverted underneath the scrotum; perineostomy created at the perineal-scrotal transition.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **With concurrent vaginectomy:** vaginal cavity obliterated; perineum closed in layers.

### Step 8 — Closure

Layered closure of all flaps with tension-free approximation. Resulting scars are minimal and hidden within scrotal folds and hair-bearing skin.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Horseshoe Pubic Flap — Component Detail

The horseshoe pubic flap is one of three tissue components within the Hoebeke composite scrotoplasty (alongside the cranially pedicled labia-majora U-flaps and clitoral-hood tissue). It is not a standalone scrotoplasty technique but is the component that provides:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- **Superior scrotal skin coverage.** Cranial / anterior portion of the neoscrotum.
- **Natural penoscrotal junction.** Transition zone between neophallus base and scrotum apex.
- **Additional tissue volume.** Supplements labial flaps when labial tissue alone is insufficient.
- **Hair-bearing, pliable skin.** Mons-pubis skin was described as pliable and hair-bearing when the flap was used for vulvar reconstruction, features that suit scrotal skin.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

### Vascular anatomy — superficial external pudendal artery (SEPA)

Axial pattern flap based on the **superficial external pudendal artery (SEPA)**, a femoral-artery branch:<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

- **La Falce 2006 cadaver quantitative anatomy.** SEPA was present in 92% of 50 dissected sides in male cadavers; diameter ranged from 1.2 to 3.8 mm; the artery was duplicated in 46%, present as a common trunk in 24% and single in 30%; distance from the SEPA to the inguinal ligament ranged from 0.8 to 8.5 cm (marked variability that affects flap design).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Deep external pudendal artery** (mean internal diameter 0.60 mm) provides additional anterior-perineal supply and contributes to the rich anastomotic network.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- Cadaver injection studies (Spear 1994) demonstrate the mons-pubis flap has a primary vascular territory (directly SEPA-supplied) and a secondary territory (supplied via anastomoses).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

### Innervation

Ilioinguinal nerve and genital branch of the genitofemoral nerve.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> Both nerves were identified as cutaneous nerves of the region in a cadaver microdissection study. Selvaggi and colleagues describe refinements intended to preserve sensation and report the possibility of enhanced erogenous sensitivity, but sensation was not measured in that series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Vulvoscrotoplasty with vaginal preservation (Chen / Berli 2021)

For patients who desire vaginal preservation alongside scrotoplasty (shaft-only phalloplasty without vaginectomy), the horseshoe pubic flap is modified to construct a masculine-appearing scrotum around the preserved vaginal opening (OHSU vulvoscrotoplasty technique, n = 4 reported).<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

### Vascular pitfalls during concurrent phalloplasty

In the Miller / Chen series (147 patients), 5 of 6 distal flap necroses (83%) occurred ipsilateral to the groin dissection required for phalloplasty.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Injury to the SEPA at its femoral origin is one plausible mechanism, but the series did not test it. Identifying and protecting the SEPA before groin dissection is a reasonable precaution, and duplex or CTA mapping can be considered in reoperative cases.

### Anatomic variability and counselling

- **SEPA absence.** The artery was absent in 8% of dissected sides in male cadavers, so its presence needs to be confirmed preoperatively or intraoperatively.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Body habitus.** Thick mons can produce excessive bulk; very thin patients may have insufficient tissue. Testosterone therapy may alter regional fat distribution.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Prior inguinal / groin surgery.** May have disrupted SEPA vessels and compromise flap viability.

## Key Technical Refinements

Over the 300+ case Ghent experience:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- **Sensation preservation.** The cranial pedicle is intended to maintain the ilioinguinal and genitofemoral nerve branches supplying the labia majora. The authors report the possibility of enhanced erogenous sensitivity; sensation was not measured in the series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Shaping refinements.** Progressive modifications to flap design improved 3D contour and natural pendulous appearance.
- **Fat-pad mobilisation.** A later refinement described to add bulk without tissue expanders.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Modification for Patients Without Urethral Lengthening (Pigot 2020 / Amsterdam)

Pigot 2020 published a stepwise modification for phalloplasty without UL, as a video description of one patient:<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- Inner labia-minora tissue (normally used for the fixed neourethra) is resected rather than incorporated.
- Meatus and vaginal orifice diverted underneath the scrotum.
- **Perineostomy** at the perineal-scrotal transition.
- Result: the report describes an augmented neoscrotum with minimal visible scars and proper neoperineal length.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Hage Bifid V-Y Advancement (1993) — Historical Predecessor

**Hage, Bouman, and Bloem (Free University Hospital, Amsterdam, 1993)** described an early scrotoplasty technique for transmasculine patients (n = 50). Use of the labia majora as donor tissue for the neoscrotum remains central to modern scrotoplasty techniques.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Published in the same year as the Sengezer tissue-expansion technique, the two 1993 publications established the two foundational approaches (direct advancement versus tissue expansion) from which all subsequent scrotoplasty evolved.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### The V-Y advancement principle

V-Y is a fundamental plastic-surgery technique:<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

- **V-shaped incision** adjacent to a defect creates a triangular flap.
- Triangular flap advanced to close the defect.
- Donor site closed in a Y-shaped configuration.
- Flap maintains blood supply through a deep subcutaneous or fascial pedicle, not detached from its base.
- Net effect: tissue recruitment from area of excess to area of deficiency.
- **Optimal apex angles 20°–60°.** Angles < 20° produce excessively long flaps; > 60° result in closure distances at the apex that exceed the original defect width.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

The V-Y principle allows tissue advancement without tissue expansion, the main Hage advantage over the contemporaneous Sengezer approach.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### Hage technique — step by step

Performed in lithotomy position, typically with lengthening of the pars fixa of the urethra:<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

1. **Incision design.** Bilateral V-shaped incisions on labial skin (labia majora), apex of each V directed posteriorly or inferiorly, base oriented anteriorly.
2. **Flap elevation.** Labial skin flaps elevated subcutaneously, deep pedicle preserved for external-pudendal blood supply.
3. **V-Y advancement.** Each flap advanced anteriorly toward midline, moving labial tissue from posterior native position toward more anterior or inferior location closer to the neophallus base.
4. **Bifid scrotum formation.** Two advanced flaps sutured to create a bifid scrotum with visible midline raphe / division and two distinct compartments for subsequent testicular-prosthesis placement.
5. **Immediate testicular-prosthesis implantation.** Prostheses placed into the two compartments at the same operative session (a difference from modern practice, which favours delayed implantation).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
6. **Closure.** Layered closure with small hidden scars in scrotal folds and hair-bearing skin.

### Original Hage outcomes (n = 50)

| Outcome | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Implant expulsion** | **7%** | Prosthesis extruded through neoscrotal skin |
| **Implant dislocation** | **11%** | Prosthesis migrated from intended position |
| **Combined implant-related complications** | **18%** | Sum of the two rates; superseded by later techniques |
| Major complications | Few | Technique described as having "few major drawbacks" |
| Scarring | Minimal | Hidden in scrotal folds and hair |
| Tissue expansion required | None | Deemed unnecessary with this technique |

### Limitations driving the Hage → Hoebeke evolution

The five limitations of the Hage technique that drove the development of the Hoebeke approach:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

1. **Posterior scrotal positioning.** V-Y advancement alone (without rotational component) leaves the neoscrotum between the legs rather than anterior to the thighs. Selvaggi and Hoebeke specifically identified this as the primary problem their 90° medial rotation was designed to solve.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
2. **Bifid appearance.** Can appear unnatural when the two halves don't fuse into a unified pendulous contour.
3. **Insufficient tissue volume.** Labial skin alone, even after advancement, may not provide enough bulk to simulate testes-containing scrotum; Hage technique lacks fat-pad relocation or horseshoe pubic flap.
4. **Immediate implant placement.** Earlier practice placed larger prostheses at scrotal reconstruction. Explantation rates fell over time as technique, prosthesis size and timing changed together (Amsterdam series, n = 206: explantation 20.8% across all techniques and 11.5% with the Hoebeke technique, which was followed by delayed implantation).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
5. **No rotational component.** Fundamental biomechanical limitation; cannot reliably reposition the neoscrotum to anatomically correct anterior position.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Evolution to modern Hoebeke technique

The Ghent team began performing scrotal reconstructions in November 1993 (same year as the Hage publication) and over 300+ cases progressively modified the Hage V-Y advancement by adding:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

1. **90° medial rotation** of the cranially pedicled labial flaps, solving the posterior-positioning problem.
2. **Horseshoe pubic flap**, giving superior coverage and a natural penoscrotal junction.
3. **Pedicled fat-pad relocation**, improving bulkiness without tissue expansion.
4. **Delayed implant placement**, reducing implant-related complications.
5. **Sensation-preserving refinements**, maintaining erogenous sensitivity via the cranial pedicle.

**Reported rates:** Hage 1993 reported implant expulsion in 7% and dislocation in 11%. The Hoebeke subset of the Amsterdam series (Pigot 2019, n = 52) had 11.5% explantation. The endpoints and populations differ, so the rates are not directly comparable.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Current role

The Hage bifid V-Y advancement is no longer the standard of care; it was superseded by the Hoebeke technique. Historical importance remains substantial: it established the fundamental principles (labia majora as donor tissue, V-Y advancement for tissue recruitment, avoidance of tissue expansion) upon which all modern scrotoplasty is built.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The V-Y advancement principle is itself preserved as a core component of the Hoebeke technique, and the Hoebeke approach is essentially a Hage V-Y advancement, 90° medial rotation, horseshoe pubic flap and fat-pad relocation. Hage described the technique as easy to perform, and that simplicity may still suit settings without expertise in rotational techniques.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Sengezer Tissue-Expansion Scrotoplasty (1993) — Historical Predecessor

**Sengezer and Sadove (1993)** published a two-stage tissue-expansion technique in which expanders are inserted into the labia majora several months before phalloplasty to pre-expand the labial tissue, creating sufficient volume and skin surface area for a natural-appearing neoscrotum.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Published in the same year as the Hage bifid V-Y advancement, the two 1993 publications established the two foundational paradigms for scrotoplasty:

- **Hage approach.** Direct V-Y tissue advancement relying on native labial volume.
- **Sengezer approach.** Pre-expansion of the labia majora with tissue expanders, biologically *growing* additional tissue before definitive reconstruction.

The technique drew on the broader tissue-expansion revolution in plastic surgery popularised by Radovan 1984, who demonstrated that intermittent inflation of subcutaneous silicone expanders could double the surface area of adjacent skin while preserving colour, texture, thickness, and sensation with minimal scarring.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

### Original series (n = 3)

The total published Sengezer experience is just 3 transmasculine patients.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Findings:

- Tissue expanders inserted into the labia majora several months before free-flap phalloplasty.
- **2 patients.** Expanders removed at phalloplasty and testicular implants placed into the pre-formed pockets.
- **1 patient.** Expanders left in place as permanent prostheses (functioning as both expansion device and testicular prosthesis).
- "Excellent aesthetic results": natural colour match, hair pattern, skin texture.
- Anatomical position "properly located in relation to surrounding anatomical structures", a significant advantage over the contemporaneous Hage technique (which Selvaggi / Hoebeke later criticised for posterior positioning).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Biology — mechanotransduction-driven skin growth

Tissue expansion is not merely stretching; it produces true biological growth of new skin via mechanotransduction:<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[22]](#ref22)[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

- **Mechanoreceptors** on keratinocytes and fibroblasts detect sustained overstretch.
- **Protein kinase C (PKC)** activated in an isoform-specific pattern; Takei 1998 mechanosignalling pathway.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- Growth-factor upregulation leading to cell proliferation; cytoskeletal reorganisation; collagen synthesis.
- Net result: net gain in epidermal and dermal tissue, not just elastic stretch.

**Janes 2020 porcine isogeometric-analysis temporal dynamics:**<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

| Timepoint | Basal-keratinocyte mitotic index |
|---|---|
| 1 h post-stretch | 4% (p = 0.022) |
| **Day 3 (peak)** | **26%** (p < 0.001) |

Cell proliferation showed dose-response to stretch magnitude; larger individual fills produce greater growth stimuli than smaller, more frequent fills, with clinical implications for expansion protocols.

**Tissue-biomechanics changes:**<sup>[[22]](#ref22)[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

- Acute elastic stretch approximately 25% (chronic approximately 10%).
- Acute thickness change −28% (chronic −12%).
- **Chronic fractional weight gain 3.3×** (dry weight); substantial new tissue generation.
- Longer expansion protocols produce more growth than shorter protocols at the same total volume.

### Sengezer technique — step by step

**Stage 1 (months before phalloplasty):**<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

1. Small incision in each labium majus.
2. Subcutaneous pocket dissected within each labium.
3. Silicone tissue expander (integrated or remote fill port) inserted.
4. Layered closure.
5. **Serial expansion.** Saline fills at outpatient visits; the original report does not give the schedule.

**Stage 2 (at phalloplasty):**<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- **Option A** (2/3 patients): expanders removed; pre-formed capsule-lined pockets used for testicular implants; phalloplasty performed concurrently.
- **Option B** (1/3 patients): expanders left in situ as permanent prostheses (novel dual-function concept that has not been widely adopted; expanders not designed for permanent implantation).

### Advantages

1. **Anatomically correct scrotal position.** Labia majora maintained in native anatomic position; correctly positioned scrotum (unlike Hage V-Y which left scrotum too posterior).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
2. **Adequate tissue volume.** Biological growth overcomes the fundamental native-labia-volume limitation.
3. **Natural tissue characteristics.** Expanded labial tissue retains colour, texture, hair pattern, and sensation.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
4. **Pre-formed pockets for implants.** Capsule-lined, ideally sized.
5. **Preserved sensation.** Nerve fibres elongate with the tissue.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
6. **Minimal scarring.** Donor tissue developed in situ.

### Limitations — why the technique was largely abandoned

**1. Additional surgical stage.** Separate operative procedure months before definitive reconstruction; prolonged timeline; multiple outpatient fill visits; patient discomfort during expansion. Hage explicitly noted tissue expansion is "unnecessary" with the V-Y advancement approach.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

**2. Tissue-expansion complications.** Huang 2011 systematic review and meta-analysis of 42 observational studies (n = 5,925): total average complication rate 17.44%.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

| Complication | Rate |
|---|---|
| **Infection** | **4.58%** |
| Extrusion / exposure | Variable (up to 17.7% paediatric) |
| Combined infection / extrusion (paediatric) | **23%** |
| Hematoma / seroma | Variable |
| Dehiscence | Variable |
| Device failure (rupture, port malfunction) | Rare |

Risk factors: lower-extremity location (OR 2.80; 95% CI 1.14–6.86), smoking, radiation.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> The perineal / genital region wasn't separately analysed but proximity to the perineum raises contamination and infection concerns. George 2026 paediatric series and Wang 2021 antibiotic-prophylaxis provide additional complication-rate context.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

**3. Very small series (n = 3).** Extremely limited evidence; no subsequent large series validated the technique.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

**4. Patient burden during expansion.** Weeks-to-months of progressively enlarging labial tissue; frequent appointments; visible expansion with social / psychological burden; expander-related complications can delay or prevent definitive reconstruction.

**5. Superseded by non-expansion techniques.** Hoebeke 2009 demonstrated that adequate scrotal volume and correct anatomic positioning could be achieved without expansion via V-Y advancement, 90° medial rotation, horseshoe pubic flap and fat-pad relocation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Tissue expansion remains valuable in cisgender scrotal reconstruction

While abandoned for transmasculine scrotoplasty, tissue expansion remains relevant for cisgender scrotal reconstruction when native tissue is truly deficient (Fournier's gangrene, trauma, tumour excision):<sup>[[28]](#ref28)[[29]](#ref29)[[30]](#ref30)</sup>

- **Rapp 2005.** Tissue-expanded V-Y scrotoplasty for scrotal-sac reconstruction after extensive skin loss; favourable functional / cosmetic outcomes.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup>
- **Kwon 2008.** Rapid intraoperative tissue expansion using 300 cc expanders inflated for only 20 min intraoperatively to expand residual scrotal / medial-thigh skin; enables tension-free closure of approximately 75% scrotal-skin-loss defects.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>
- **Atik 2006.** Expanded superthin groin flap for total scrotal-tissue loss with 3-week expansion period.<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup>

Modern gender-affirming scrotoplasty avoids expansion because flap-based techniques recruit tissue from multiple sources (labia majora, mons pubis and fat pads), making the biological-growth step unnecessary. Cisgender reconstruction lacks this multi-source-flap option when native tissue is destroyed.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Current role in gender-affirming surgery

The Sengezer tissue-expansion technique is no longer used in contemporary gender-affirming scrotoplasty. Entirely superseded by single-stage techniques (Hoebeke, Miller / Chen). Historical contributions:<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

1. First technique to demonstrate tissue expansion could be applied to the labia majora for scrotal construction.
2. Established that expanded labial tissue produces a natural-appearing neoscrotum in colour, texture, and hair pattern.
3. Demonstrated that labial-anatomic-position preservation produces a correctly positioned scrotum, a Hage-V-Y-limitation the Sengezer approach uniquely overcame at the time.
4. Introduced pre-formed pockets for testicular-implant placement, a concept later refined in staged-implant insertion protocols.

The technique's legacy: adequate tissue volume is needed for a natural-appearing neoscrotum, a principle that Hoebeke addresses through fat-pad relocation and horseshoe pubic flap rather than through expansion.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Miller / Chen Bilateral Rotational Advancement (2021) — Largest Dedicated Outcomes Series

**Miller / Chen 2021 (Chen group; 147 patients, October 2017 to December 2019; mean follow-up 12.5 months)** is a retrospective single-centre series of labia majora flap scrotoplasty with detailed scrotoplasty-specific complication data. The Ghent series of more than 300 scrotal reconstructions did not report such data, and the Amsterdam 206-patient cohort focused on testicular implants.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### Distinguishing features vs Hoebeke

- **Bilateral elevation and rotational advancement** (versus Hoebeke's cranially-pedicled U-flaps, formal V-Y, 90° rotation and horseshoe pubic flap composite).
- Creates a unified, pouch-like scrotum (versus Hoebeke composite three-tissue reconstruction or Hage bifid).
- **Integrated perineal reconstruction** as a co-step (multilayered closure with inner-thigh skin apposition), not separately described in Hoebeke or Hage publications.
- No fat-pad relocation, no horseshoe pubic flap; the bulk of the comparative simplicity.

### Concurrent procedures

- 133 / 147 (90.5%) performed concurrently with single-stage phalloplasty (typically RFFF or pedicled ALT, both requiring groin dissection).
- **14 / 147 (9.5%)** as secondary scrotoplasty after prior phalloplasty.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### Granular complication profile

| Complication | Rate | n/N | Management |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Distal flap necrosis** | **4.1%** | 6/147 | Conservative (all 6) |
| → **Ipsilateral to groin dissection** | **83% of necrosis cases** | 5/6 | — |
| Perineoscrotal-junction dehiscence (> 1 cm) | 4.7% | 7/147 | Conservative except 3 with fistulas |
| **Urethrocutaneous fistula at perineoscrotal junction** | **2.0%** | 3/147 | **Surgical fistula repair (all 3)** |
| Scrotal hematoma | 1.4% | 2/147 | **Operative evacuation (both)** |
| Perineal hematoma | 2.0% | 3/147 | **Operative evacuation (all 3)** |

Synthesis from Miller / Chen: "wounds that do not heal may be associated with urethral complications"; non-healing perineoscrotal junctions progressed to urethrocutaneous fistula in 3/7 (43%) of dehiscence cases.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Hematomas (3.4% combined) always required operative evacuation, unlike necrosis and dehiscence which were managed conservatively.

### The groin-dissection signal — practical implications

The ipsilateral-to-groin-dissection signal (5 of 6 necroses) rests on very small numbers and generates a hypothesis. The SEPA originates from the femoral artery and courses medially across the groin (see Horseshoe Pubic Flap — Component Detail above for the La Falce 2006 quantitative anatomy). Groin dissection for the phalloplasty pedicle anastomosis could injure the SEPA or disrupt the surrounding network, but the Miller / Chen series did not examine this.

**Four considerations that follow from this signal (technique suggestions, not tested in the series):**

1. **Preoperative flap-design planning.** Consider groin-dissection laterality; design the ipsilateral flap with wider base or more conservative advancement.
2. **Vascular preservation.** Identify and preserve the SEPA before groin dissection (where feasible given femoral exposure needs).
3. **Staged-approach consideration.** In patients at high risk for vascular compromise (prior groin surgery, obesity, smoking), consider staging scrotoplasty separately from phalloplasty.
4. **Contralateral planning.** If possible, position the phalloplasty vascular anastomosis contralateral to the dominant labial flap.

### Massie 2017 — bulbospongiosus-flap-from-vaginectomy reduces urethral complications

In a retrospective Chen-group series (Massie 2017; 224 patients with phalloplasty and urethral lengthening, of whom 215 had vaginectomy and 9 kept the vagina), vaginectomy with a vascularised bulbospongiosus flap was associated with lower urethral complication rates (27% vs 67%, OR 0.18, p = 0.02), including:<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

- **Decreased urethral stricture:** OR 0.25 (p = 0.047).
- **Decreased fistula formation:** OR 0.13 (p = 0.004) compared with vaginal preservation.

Implication for the [Chen / Berli vulvoscrotoplasty-with-vaginal-preservation variant](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/scrotoplasty#horseshoe-pubic-flap--component-detail): patients choosing vaginal preservation together with UL should be counselled about the higher urethral-complication risk, although only 9 patients in the Massie series kept the vagina.

### Ascha 2018 ALT-vs-RFFF comparison

Single-stage phalloplasty outcomes (same group):<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

- Pedicled ALT versus RFFF: both require groin dissection; both are compatible with concurrent Miller / Chen scrotoplasty.
- In that series (213 patients), partial or total neophallus loss was 3.4% with RFFF and 7.8% with ALT, and urethral complications were 31.5% and 32.8%.
- The ipsilateral-necrosis signal from the Miller / Chen series may be relevant to flap choice.

### Limitations of the Miller / Chen approach

- **No long-term testicular-implant data** (mean follow-up only 12.5 mo; implants placed at later stage), unlike Amsterdam 11.5-yr testicular-implant series.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **No fat-pad relocation or horseshoe pubic flap.** Lacks the bulkiness and penoscrotal-junction refinements of the Hoebeke approach.
- **No sensation data.** Neoscrotal sensation and erogenous sensitivity were not reported. Selvaggi describes the possibility of enhanced erogenous sensitivity with the Hoebeke technique.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Retrospective design** without validated PROMs.

## Outcomes — Hoebeke Technique Specifically

**Ghent / Amsterdam series:**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- **No major complications** related to scrotoplasty in the original 300+ patient series.
- All patients pleased at short- and long-term follow-up with scrotum in its natural anterior position.
- The Pigot 2020 single-patient report describes a neoscrotum that resembles the biological scrotum in bulkiness, size, shape, tactile sensation and position.

**Testicular-implant outcomes with Hoebeke scrotoplasty (Pigot 2019 Amsterdam, n = 52 Hoebeke subset):**<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- **Explantation rate 11.5% (6/52).** The overall explantation rate across all techniques in the same centre's 206-patient series was 20.8%.
- Explantation rates have fallen over the last decade as scrotoplasty technique changed and smaller, lighter prostheses with delayed implantation became more common.
- **Smoking** significant risk factor for postoperative infections and prosthesis explantation on univariate and multivariate analysis.

See the [Testicular Implants (Gender-Affirming) page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/testicular-implants) for the full implant-outcomes framework.

---

## Timing Within the Surgical Sequence

Hoebeke scrotoplasty is typically performed as part of the second surgical stage:<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

1. **Stage 1.** Chest reconstruction; [hysterectomy ± BSO](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/gender-affirming-hysterectomy).
2. **Stage 2.** Vaginectomy, scrotoplasty (Hoebeke technique), metoidioplasty or phalloplasty, and urethroplasty.
3. **Stage 3 (approximately 6 mo later).** [Testicular implant placement](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/testicular-implants).
4. **Stage 4 (9–12 mo after phalloplasty).** [Penile prosthesis insertion](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/penile-implant-after-phalloplasty) if phalloplasty performed.

Per ACOG, testicular implants can be placed approximately 6 months after scrotoplasty.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> In the original Ghent series, testicular implants and the erectile prosthesis were placed 12 months after the original operation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Concurrent Procedures and Vascular Considerations

When performed concurrently with phalloplasty, the scrotoplasty flap design must account for the groin dissection required for the phalloplasty vascular pedicle. Miller / Chen 2021 (n = 147): 5 of 6 distal flap necrosis cases (83%) occurred ipsilateral to the groin dissection; the phalloplasty pedicle harvest may compromise blood supply to the ipsilateral labial flap.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> This is relevant when Hoebeke scrotoplasty is planned concurrently with RFFF phalloplasty, because the vascular anastomosis is typically performed in the groin.

---

## Summary — Why Hoebeke Has Become Standard

The Hoebeke technique addresses all the main goals of scrotoplasty:<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

1. **Anatomically correct position.** 90° medial rotation brings neoscrotum anterior to legs.
2. **Adequate volume.** Fat-pad relocation provides natural bulk without tissue expanders.
3. **Sensation.** The cranial pedicle is intended to preserve innervation; the authors report the possibility of enhanced erogenous sensitivity.
4. **Single-stage reconstruction.** No prior tissue expansion needed.
5. **Minimal scarring.** Hidden in scrotal folds / hair-bearing skin.
6. **Low complication rate.** No major complications in the original series; 11.5% testicular-implant explantation versus 20.8% across all techniques.
7. **Compatibility with staged implant placement.** Neoscrotum designed to accommodate testicular prostheses at later stage.

---

## Evidence Limitations

- **Selvaggi 2009 / Hoebeke 300+ patient experience** is the seminal technique description but is single-centre and retrospective.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Pigot 2019 n = 52 Hoebeke subset** is the largest specific outcomes anchor; the 11.5% versus 20.8% explantation contrast reflects within-centre technique evolution and was not a controlled comparison.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **No randomised comparisons** between Hoebeke, Hage bifid, Miller bilateral-rotational, and Sengezer tissue-expansion techniques.
- **Long-term outcomes** (sensation durability, scrotal-skin remodelling) beyond 10 yr poorly characterised.
