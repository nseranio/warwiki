# Feminizing Gender-Affirming Surgery

**Feminizing genital gender-affirming surgery (GAS)** comprises bilateral orchiectomy, vulvoplasty (zero or shallow depth), full-depth vaginoplasty (penile inversion, peritoneal flap or intestinal) and the component procedures within them (clitoroplasty, urethroplasty, labiaplasty). Breast or chest, facial feminization and voice surgery are covered in the [Feminizing Procedures clinical-conditions article](/docs/special-populations/05c-gender-affirming/feminizing-procedures). Eligibility and perioperative hormone management follow WPATH SOC v8 (2022), the Endocrine Society 2017 clinical practice guideline (Hembree) and ACOG 2021.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

The sources used for decisions on this page are mainly retrospective series and reviews. They are the van der Sluis 2023 and Morrison 2023 reviews, Hehemann and Walsh 2019 on orchiectomy as a bridge to vaginoplasty, and Stelmar 2023 on offering shallow-depth vaginoplasty as a standard option (35 of 110 patients, 32%, chose it over full-depth).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> Opsomer 2021 reports the 15-year Ghent series of 384 penile inversion vaginoplasties: 97.2% reported penetrative intercourse, 1.6% had rectal perforation and 37.1% had late revision.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> Fakin 2021 is a retrospective single-centre comparison of single-stage and two-stage inversion vaginoplasty, in which depth was constant with the single-stage technique and fell by more than 30% in the first year with the two-stage technique.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> Peritoneal flap series include Castanon 2022, Jacoby 2019 and Ratanalert 2026.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> Lee 2025 is a prospective lower urinary tract cohort in which symptom scores and uroflowmetry did not worsen at 12 months after robotic peritoneal flap vaginoplasty.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> Shamamian 2025 analyzes predictors of dilation difficulty, and De Rosa 2024 is a systematic review of vaginal stenosis.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> Blasdel 2024 is a narrative review of surgeon-reported versus patient-reported urinary complications, and AFFIRM (Huber 2021) is a validated patient-reported instrument.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

## Decision Framework

### Eligibility and Readiness

Per WPATH SOC v8 and the Endocrine Society 2017 Clinical Practice Guideline:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- Persistent, well-documented gender incongruence
- At least one referral letter from a qualified mental-health provider (SOC v8 simplified the prior two-letter requirement)
- Age ≥ 18 (or local age of majority) for gonadectomy
- For gonadectomy or vaginoplasty: stable feminizing hormone therapy. SOC v8 suggests at least 6 months, or longer if needed for the surgical result, unless hormones are not desired or are contraindicated (SOC v7 required 12 months)
- Capacity to consent to the irreversibility of gonadectomy and to the lifelong dilation commitment required after full-depth vaginoplasty

### Goal-Directed GU Pathway Selection

| Patient Priority | Standalone Orchiectomy | Vulvoplasty (Zero/Shallow-Depth) | Full-Depth Vaginoplasty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eliminate endogenous testosterone / reduce antiandrogens | ✓ | ✓ (included) | ✓ (included) |
| **Feminine vulvar appearance only** | ✗ | ✓ | ✓ |
| **Receptive vaginal penetration** | ✗ | ✗ | ✓ |
| **Erogenous neoclitoral sensation** | ✗ | ✓ | ✓ |
| **Lowest complication burden / fastest recovery** | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ (highest of the three) |
| **No lifelong dilation commitment** | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ (required) |
| **Bridge to or option for future vaginoplasty** | ✓ (preserve scrotal skin) | ✗ (donor tissue used) | n/a |

Anchors: Saltman 2023 (NSQIP; 246 gender-affirming and 997 cisgender nononcologic orchiectomies; complication rate 3.7% for gender-affirming orchiectomy vs 4.4% for torsion, p = 0.6, and 5.9% for testicular pain, p = 0.2); Stelmar 2023 (single institution; 35 of 110 chose shallow-depth over full-depth; of 29 survey respondents, 86% would choose it again; 27% had complications needing further surgery, 82% of complications related to urinary spraying); van der Sluis 2020 (17 women over 30 years; 59% had no wish for receptive penetration; all 12 who reported satisfaction were satisfied); Hehemann and Walsh 2019 (orchiectomy as a bridge; preserve scrotal skin if future vaginoplasty is contemplated).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)[[20]](#ref20)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

### Vaginoplasty Technique Selection

| Clinical Scenario | First-Line | Alternative(s) | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Default candidate; adequate penile + scrotal skin (uncircumcised or sufficient tissue); standard depth (~14 cm) acceptable** | **Penile inversion vaginoplasty (PIV)** is the most established technique and is called the gold standard in several of the cited series. A full-thickness skin graft to lengthen the canal was needed in 85.7% of Ghent cases (Opsomer 2021). Single-stage PIV had fewer complications in a retrospective comparison (Fakin 2021: wound dehiscence 4.8% vs 33.9%; one revision 6.4% vs one or more reoperations 56.5%; stable depth at 3.5 yr) | Robotic peritoneal-augmented PIV (Jacoby 2019: +5 cm depth) | Two-stage PIV in routine candidates; PIV alone when donor tissue is clearly insufficient |
| **Insufficient penile / scrotal skin (radical circumcision, puberty-blocker exposure, lichen sclerosus, scrotal hypoplasia)** | **Robotic / laparoscopic peritoneal pull-through vaginoplasty** (Castanon 2022; Jacoby 2019; Ratanalert 2026). Castanon 2022 (n = 52): depth 14.7 ± 0.5 cm at 6 months, about 96% self-reported satisfaction, complications in 7 patients (13.5%), none needing further surgery. Lee 2025 (n = 43): no worsening of lower urinary tract function at 12 months | Augmentation of PIV with peritoneal flap (Jacoby) | Intestinal vaginoplasty as first-line in standard candidates (higher complication burden, mucus, rare neoplasia signal) |
| **Failed primary vaginoplasty with refractory stenosis; or maximal lubrication is the explicit priority** | **Intestinal (sigmoid) vaginoplasty** had a vaginal-stenosis rate of 0.20% vs 5.70% for PIV in a systematic review of primary vaginoplasty (De Rosa 2024); the mucosa is self-lubricating | Robotic peritoneal-flap revision | Sigmoid as first-line in standard candidates (laparotomy/laparoscopy + bowel anastomosis; diversion-colitis-like inflammation; mucus burden) |
| **Canal not desired** | **Vulvoplasty (zero- or shallow-depth)** (Stelmar 2023; van der Sluis 2020): high satisfaction, 86% of respondents would choose it again, and 27% had complications needing further surgery, mostly urinary spraying (82% of complications) | Standalone bilateral orchiectomy if external feminization is also deferred | Coercive canal creation; full-depth vaginoplasty when patient priorities don't include penetrative function |
| **Active smoker; insufficient electrolysis** | **Defer vaginoplasty** until smoking cessation ≥ 4–6 weeks documented and scrotal/perineal electrolysis (6–12 mo course) is complete | Standalone orchiectomy as bridge | Vaginoplasty without preoperative electrolysis (intravaginal hair growth) |
| **Prior pelvic radiation** | **Vulvoplasty** or peritoneal-flap-augmented vaginoplasty at high-volume center | Standalone orchiectomy | Sigmoid vaginoplasty in heavily irradiated bed without colorectal-surgery review |
| **Concurrent persistent vaginal cavity / fistula concern** | Vaginoplasty with formal closure of any retained vaginal cavity at index operation | Staged closure | Leaving a residual vaginal cavity adjacent to the neovaginal canal |

### Peritoneal vs Penile-Inversion vs Intestinal Sub-Comparison

| Feature | Penile Inversion (PIV) | Peritoneal Pull-Through (Robotic) | Sigmoid (Intestinal) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Depth (mean)** | About 14 cm target (Opsomer Ghent 15-yr) | 5 cm beyond the skin-graft length when used as augmentation (Jacoby, 41 patients, 114-day mean follow-up); 14.7 cm standalone (Castanon)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[11]](#ref11)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> | 12–15 cm; bowel-length-dependent |
| **Lubrication** | None (squamous epithelium) | Peritoneal serous secretion (modest, self-lubricating) | Mucinous (ample; may require panty-liner) |
| **Donor-tissue requirement** | Penile + scrotal skin (graft needed in 85.7%) | None — peritoneum ample | 15–20 cm sigmoid bowel segment |
| **Microsurgery / robotics** | None | Robotic platform (Da Vinci Xi or SP) or laparoscopic | Laparoscopic or open + bowel anastomosis |
| **Dilation requirement** | Lifelong, frequent | Lifelong, frequent (Shamamian 2025: primary peritoneal graft OR 3.20 for dilation difficulty) | Less aggressive (mucosa more compliant) |
| **Vaginal-stenosis rate (De Rosa 2024 SR)** | 5.70% (introital stenosis 3.13%) | Not reported separately | 0.20% (introital stenosis 4.7%) |
| **Rectoneovaginal fistula** | Fistula 0.9% (Gaither); rectal perforation 1.6% (Ghent) | Limited data | Anastomotic leak risk distinct from RVF |
| **LUT function (Lee 2025)** | n/a | No worsening at 12 mo (AUASI and UDI-6 declined, below the minimum important difference; uroflowmetry unchanged) | Limited prospective LUT data |
| **Late revision rate** | 37.1% (Opsomer Ghent, 2003 to 2017 cases) | Limited long-term data | Limited long-term data |
| **Ideal candidate** | Standard candidate with adequate donor tissue | Insufficient donor tissue; revision; depth augmentation | Revision after stenosis; maximal-lubrication priority |

### Component-Procedure Decisions Within Vaginoplasty

#### Clitoroplasty

| Anatomy | Recommended Technique | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Uncircumcised, or circumcised with > 2 cm inner preputial skin and at least 8 cm of shaft skin proximal to the scar** | **Preputial-skin clitoroplasty** with dorsal neurovascular pedicle glans-penis flap | Fascelli 2024 anatomy-guided framework<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>; the authors prefer it for greater coronal tissue volume and esthetics; 86% reported orgasm in a long-term series (Sigurjónsson 2017) |
| **Circumcised or limited preputial skin** | **Urethral-flap clitoroplasty** with dorsal NVB pedicle | Fascelli 2024; uses more corpus spongiosum, and the urethra forms the clitoral hood |
| **Concern for neoclitoral viability** | Dorsal NVB pedicle protection at every step; avoid two-stage approaches when feasible | Neoclitoral necrosis 4.8% in two-stage techniques (Fakin 2021) |

Sigurjónsson 2017 measured tactile and vibratory sensitivity in 22 patients at a mean of 37 months and reported that the neoclitoris provides erogenous sensation; 86% experienced orgasm.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

#### Urethroplasty (Urethral Shortening + Repositioning)

The neomeatus is moved to a feminine position and the urethra is shortened. In the Blasdel 2024 narrative review, urinary complication rates in cohorts outside the operating surgeon's own reports were higher than surgeon-reported rates:<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

| Outcome | Surgeon-Reported | Patient-Reported / Other Cohorts |
|---|---|---|
| **Meatal stenosis** | 5–16.3% (mean incidence in systematic reviews) | Not well characterised (4–40% across individual series, Nassiri 2020) |
| **Misdirected urinary stream** | 9.5–33% | 33–55% (AFFIRM: 68.9% reported misdirected stream)<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |
| **Voiding dysfunction** | 5.6–33% | 47–66% |
| **Urinary incontinence** | 4–19.3% | 23–33% |

Urethroplasty aims for a downward-directed stream, with adequate urethral shortening to avoid post-void dribbling and a feminine meatal position. Patients should be counseled that the AFFIRM-reported 68.9% misdirected-stream rate is higher than historical surgeon-reported rates.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

#### Labiaplasty

Labia majora are constructed from scrotal skin (or labia minora from preputial or urethral tissue) as the final step of vulvar reconstruction. Performed concurrently with vaginoplasty or vulvoplasty rather than staged.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

### Preoperative Preparation

| Intervention | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Scrotal / perineal electrolysis or laser hair removal** | Used before PIV, beginning 6 to 12 months before surgery, to prevent intravaginal hair growth |
| **Smoking cessation** | Modifiable risk factor for wound-healing complications; document ≥ 4–6 weeks abstinence |
| **BMI optimization** | BMI was not independently associated with complications in Gaither 2018 n = 330<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>; weight optimization remains a general perioperative goal |
| **Pelvic-floor physical therapy** | A narrative review reports that PFPT before and after surgery can reduce pelvic-floor dysfunction rates (Motiwala 2026)<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup> |
| **Dilation counseling** | Patients are counseled on the lifelong commitment. Early postoperative dilation averaged 6.6 days/week, 2.4 times/day and about 38 min/session in 17 patients (Gomez 2026 longitudinal patient-reported outcomes) |
| **Estradiol management** | WPATH SOC v8 favors estrogen continuation in most patients; selective hold based on VTE risk |

### Postoperative Dilation Protocol

Dilation maintains neovaginal patency after penile inversion vaginoplasty.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

- **Early postoperative regimen:** about 6.6 days/week, 2.4 times/day and about 38 min/session (Gomez 2026; 17 of 27 prospectively surveyed patients completed all time points, using an author-developed survey).
- **Early difficulty:** all 17 patients reported at least one dilation challenge (tightness 71%, bleeding 71%, pain 65%), and prevalence declined significantly after 6 months.
- **Predictors of dilation difficulty (Shamamian 2025, retrospective, n = 614):** unemployment OR 2.74, HIV OR 2.59, psychiatric diagnosis besides gender dysphoria OR 1.61 and primary peritoneal graft OR 3.20 (p = 0.019).
- **Stenosis.** Difficulty with dilation can lead to partial or complete closure of the neovaginal canal.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> Structured PFPT referral is appropriate for patients with persistent pain or difficulty.

### Long-Term Urologic Surveillance

| Concern | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Voiding dysfunction** (common, underrecognized) | AFFIRM-domain follow-up — misdirected stream 68.9%, nocturia 51.3%, urinary frequency 29.7%; trigger urology referral when symptomatic<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |
| **Prostate screening** | **Prostate is left in situ** during vaginoplasty and sits **anterior to the neovagina**. Digital examination should be performed **vaginally, not rectally**. Standard age- and risk-based screening guidelines apply<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Vaginal hygiene** | Skin-lined neovagina does **not self-lubricate**; douching with soapy water or dilute vinegar/betadine is adequate; empiric vaginal metronidazole for persistent odor<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **STI screening** | Standard guidelines apply; counseling adapted to neovaginal tissue type and sexual practices |
| **Periodic exam** | Annual or biennial pelvic examination to monitor for stenosis, granulation tissue, intravaginal hair regrowth, and prolapse |
| **Urethral red flags** | Urinary retention, postvoid dribbling, recurrent UTI, or persistent vaginal cavity warrants urgent urology referral; complications can present **months to years** after the index operation |
