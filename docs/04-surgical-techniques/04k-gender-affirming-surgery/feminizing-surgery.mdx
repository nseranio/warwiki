# Feminizing Gender-Affirming Surgery

**Feminizing genital gender-affirming surgery (GAS)** comprises bilateral orchiectomy, vulvoplasty (zero or shallow depth), full-depth vaginoplasty (penile inversion, peritoneal flap or intestinal) and the component procedures within them (clitoroplasty, urethroplasty, labiaplasty). Breast or chest, facial feminization and voice surgery are covered in the [Feminizing Procedures clinical-conditions article](/docs/special-populations/05c-gender-affirming/feminizing-procedures). Eligibility and perioperative hormone management follow WPATH SOC v8 (2022), the Endocrine Society 2017 clinical practice guideline (Hembree) and ACOG 2021.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

The sources used for decisions on this page are mainly retrospective series and reviews. They are the van der Sluis 2023 and Morrison 2023 reviews, Hehemann and Walsh 2019 on orchiectomy as a bridge to vaginoplasty, and Stelmar 2023 on offering shallow-depth vaginoplasty as a standard option (35 of 110 patients, 32%, chose it over full-depth).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> Opsomer 2021 reports the 15-year Ghent series of 384 penile inversion vaginoplasties: 97.2% reported being able to engage in penetrative intercourse, 1.6% had rectal perforation and 37.1% had late revision.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> Fakin 2021 is a retrospective single-centre comparison of single-stage and two-stage inversion vaginoplasty, in which depth was constant with the single-stage technique and fell by more than 30% in the first year with the two-stage technique.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> Peritoneal flap series include Castanon 2022, Jacoby 2019 and Ratanalert 2026.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> Lee 2025 is a prospective lower urinary tract cohort in which symptom scores and uroflowmetry did not worsen at 12 months after robotic peritoneal flap vaginoplasty.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> Shamamian 2025 analyzes predictors of dilation difficulty, and De Rosa 2024 is a systematic review of vaginal stenosis.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> Blasdel 2024 is a narrative review of surgeon-reported versus patient-reported urinary complications, and AFFIRM (Huber 2021) is a validated patient-reported instrument.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

## Decision Framework

### Eligibility and Readiness

Per WPATH SOC v8 and the Endocrine Society 2017 Clinical Practice Guideline:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- Persistent, well-documented gender incongruence
- A single assessment opinion from a professional competent in transgender health when documentation is required (SOC v8 statement 5.5; earlier standards required two letters for genital surgery)
- Age of majority for gonadectomy: the Endocrine Society 2017 guideline suggests delaying gonadectomy until then (recommendation 5.5), whereas SOC v8 statement 13.7 allows surgical interventions in eligible adolescents after multidisciplinary assessment
- For gonadectomy, SOC v8 suggests at least 6 months of hormone therapy (statement 13.5). For genital procedures such as vaginoplasty it suggests stability on the current regimen, which may include at least 6 months or longer if needed for the surgical result (13.6), unless hormones are not desired or are contraindicated. The Endocrine Society 2017 guideline and SOC v7 used 12 months
- Capacity to consent to the irreversibility of gonadectomy and to the lifelong dilation commitment required after full-depth vaginoplasty

### Goal-Directed GU Pathway Selection

| Patient Priority | Standalone Orchiectomy | Vulvoplasty (Zero/Shallow-Depth) | Full-Depth Vaginoplasty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Remove testicular testosterone production / reduce antiandrogens<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | ✓ | ✓ (included) | ✓ (included) |
| **Feminine vulvar appearance only** | ✗ | ✓ | ✓ |
| **Receptive vaginal penetration** | ✗ | ✗ | ✓ |
| **Erogenous neoclitoral sensation** | ✗ | ✓ | ✓ |
| **Tissue and aftercare burden** | Gonadectomy without canal construction | Vulvar reconstruction without a canal | Canal construction with ongoing dilation |
| **No lifelong dilation commitment** | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ (required) |
| **Bridge to or option for future vaginoplasty** | ✓ (preserve scrotal skin) | ✗ (donor tissue used) | n/a |

Anchors: Saltman 2023 (NSQIP; 246 gender-affirming and 997 cisgender nononcologic orchiectomies; complication rate 3.7% for gender-affirming orchiectomy vs 4.4% for torsion, p = 0.6, and 5.9% for testicular pain, p = 0.2); Stelmar 2023 (single institution; 35 of 110 chose shallow-depth over full-depth; of 29 survey respondents, 86% would choose it again; 27% had complications needing further surgery, 82% of complications related to urinary spraying); van der Sluis 2020 (17 women over 30 years; 59% had no wish for receptive penetration; all 12 who reported satisfaction were satisfied); Hehemann and Walsh 2019 (orchiectomy as a bridge; preserve scrotal skin if future vaginoplasty is contemplated).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)[[20]](#ref20)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

### Vaginoplasty Technique Selection

| Clinical Scenario | First-Line | Alternative(s) | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Default candidate; adequate penile + scrotal skin (uncircumcised or sufficient tissue); standard depth (~14 cm) acceptable** | **Penile inversion vaginoplasty (PIV)** is the most established technique and is called the gold standard in several of the cited series. A full-thickness skin graft to lengthen the canal was needed in 85.7% of 384 Ghent cases (Opsomer 2021).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> Single-stage PIV had fewer complications in a retrospective comparison (Fakin 2021: wound dehiscence 4.8% vs 33.9%; one revision 6.4% vs one or more reoperations 56.5%; depth constant during the first postoperative year)<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | Robotic peritoneal-augmented PIV (Jacoby 2019: +5 cm depth beyond skin-graft length in 41 combined PIV/peritoneal-flap cases at mean 114-day follow-up)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | PIV alone when donor tissue is clearly insufficient |
| **Insufficient penile / scrotal skin (radical circumcision, puberty-blocker exposure, lichen sclerosus, scrotal hypoplasia)** | **Robotic / laparoscopic peritoneal pull-through vaginoplasty** (Castanon 2022; Jacoby 2019; Ratanalert 2026). A retrospective robotic peritoneal-flap series identified intra-abdominal complications requiring readmission or reoperation in 6/274 patients (2.2%), including two incarcerated internal hernias requiring laparoscopic reduction.<sup>[[42]](#ref42)</sup> Castanon 2022 (n = 52): depth 14.7 ± 0.5 cm at 6 months, about 96% self-reported satisfaction, complications in 7 patients (13.5%), none of those complications needing further surgery.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> Lee 2025 (n = 43): no worsening of lower urinary tract function at 12 months<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> | Augmentation of PIV with peritoneal flap (Jacoby) | Intestinal vaginoplasty as first-line in standard candidates (higher complication burden, mucus, rare neoplasia signal) |
| **Failed primary vaginoplasty with refractory stenosis; or maximal lubrication is the explicit priority** | **Intestinal (sigmoid) vaginoplasty** is an option after refractory stenosis following PIV. In a retrospective comparison of 45 such revisions, short-term complication rates were comparable for intestinal-segment and peritoneal-flap revision, while recurrent vaginal stenosis was reported more often after peritoneal-flap revision.<sup>[[35]](#ref35)</sup> De Rosa reported 0.20% versus 5.70% vaginal stenosis for intestinal versus PIV procedures in a review limited to primary surgery; those rates do not estimate revision outcomes.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | Robotic peritoneal-flap revision | Sigmoid as first-line in standard candidates (laparotomy/laparoscopy + bowel anastomosis; diversion-colitis-like inflammation; mucus burden) |
| **Canal not desired** | **Vulvoplasty (zero- or shallow-depth)** (Stelmar 2023; van der Sluis 2020<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>): high satisfaction, 86% of 29 survey respondents (of 35 patients) would choose it again, and 27% had complications needing further surgery, mostly urinary spraying (82% of complications)<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | Standalone bilateral orchiectomy if external feminization is also deferred | Coercive canal creation; full-depth vaginoplasty when patient priorities don't include penetrative function |
| **Active smoker; insufficient electrolysis** | **Defer vaginoplasty** while actively smoking or before scrotal/perineal hair removal for the planned canal lining is complete; arrange preoperative smoking cessation and continue abstinence while the graft heals. In general-surgery trials, cessation for at least 4 weeks had greater benefit than shorter cessation.<sup>[[40]](#ref40)</sup> Smoking impairs fibroblast proliferation and collagen deposition even after inflammatory-cell function begins to recover.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup> | Standalone orchiectomy as bridge | Vaginoplasty without preoperative electrolysis (intravaginal hair growth); resuming smoking before the skin graft has fully healed |
| **Prior pelvic radiation** | **Vulvoplasty** or peritoneal-flap-augmented vaginoplasty at high-volume center | Standalone orchiectomy | Sigmoid vaginoplasty in heavily irradiated bed without colorectal-surgery review |
| **Prior radical prostatectomy** | Some authors recommend vulvoplasty in this setting, reasoning that dissecting a full-depth vaginal canal may injure the external urinary sphincter and cause incontinence; this is an author-specific selection consideration rather than a validated contraindication to full-depth surgery<sup>[[33]](#ref33)</sup> | Full-depth vaginoplasty with sphincter-sparing canal dissection at an experienced center | — |

### Peritoneal vs Penile-Inversion vs Intestinal Sub-Comparison

| Feature | Penile Inversion (PIV) | Peritoneal Pull-Through (Robotic) | Sigmoid (Intestinal) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Depth measure** | 14 cm intraoperative target in the Ghent series, not a measured mean (Opsomer 2021) | Jacoby reported 5 cm added beyond skin-graft length in 41 primary penile-inversion/peritoneal-flap cases (mean follow-up 114 days); Castanon reported mean depth 14.7 ± 0.5 cm at 6 months in 52 cases combining inverted penile skin with peritoneal flaps<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[11]](#ref11)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> | Mean 13.9 ± 2.0 cm at mean 6-month follow-up in 12 primary sigmoid cases (Salgado 2018)<sup>[[39]](#ref39)</sup> |
| **Fluid production** | Penile/scrotal skin is not intrinsically secretory; incorporated urethral tissue or retained glands may produce some fluid | Peritoneum can secrete fluid, but volume and functional lubrication after transfer are unquantified | Bowel mucosa produces continuous mucus that may require hygiene management; no lining has proven arousal-responsive lubrication comparable to a natal vagina<sup>[[36]](#ref36)</sup> |
| **Donor-tissue requirement** | Penile + scrotal skin (graft needed in 85.7% in the Ghent series)<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> | Peritoneal flaps augment canal lining, but the cited series also use inverted penile skin for the distal canal and genital skin for vulvar construction<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> | 12–15 cm sigmoid bowel segment in Salgado's primary series<sup>[[39]](#ref39)</sup> |
| **Microsurgery / robotics** | None | Robotic platform (Da Vinci Xi or SP) or laparoscopic | Laparoscopic or open + bowel anastomosis |
| **Dilation requirement** | Lifelong, frequent | Lifelong, frequent; in Shamamian's mixed-technique vaginoplasty cohort, primary peritoneal graft was associated with dilation difficulty (OR 3.20)<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> | Ongoing dilation through the introitus and bowel-to-introitus anastomosis remains necessary; De Rosa reported 4.7% introital stenosis after primary intestinal vaginoplasty<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| **Vaginal-stenosis rate (De Rosa 2024 SR)** | 5.70% (introital stenosis 3.13%) | Not reported separately | 0.20% (introital stenosis 4.7%)<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| **Rectoneovaginal fistula** | Rectoneovaginal fistula 3/330 (0.9%) after primary PIV (Gaither; median follow-up 3 months)<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>; rectal perforation 6/384 (1.6%) in Ghent PIV<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> | Limited data | Anastomotic leak risk distinct from RVF |
| **LUT function (Lee 2025)** | n/a | No worsening at 12 mo (AUASI and UDI-6 declined, below the minimum important difference; uroflowmetry unchanged) | Limited prospective LUT data |
| **Revision and follow-up** | Ghent PIV: 37.1% late revision.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> | Robotic peritoneal-flap cohort: 61/500 (12%) elective revisions; 425/500 (85%) followed at least 1 year. This is a different revision endpoint from the Ghent series, and longer follow-up remains limited.<sup>[[46]](#ref46)</sup> | Limited long-term data |
| **Ideal candidate** | Standard candidate with adequate donor tissue | Insufficient donor tissue; revision; depth augmentation | Revision after stenosis; maximal-lubrication priority |

### Component-Procedure Decisions Within Vaginoplasty

#### Clitoroplasty

| Anatomy | Recommended Technique | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Uncircumcised, or circumcised with &gt; 2 cm inner preputial skin and at least 8 cm of shaft skin proximal to the scar** | **Preputial-skin clitoroplasty** with dorsal neurovascular pedicle glans-penis flap | Fascelli 2024 anatomy-guided framework<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>; the authors prefer it for greater coronal tissue volume and esthetics. Separately, Sigurjónsson 2017 reported orgasm in 86% of 22 assessed patients after glans-based neoclitoroplasty<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> |
| **Circumcised or limited preputial skin** | **Urethral-flap clitoroplasty** with dorsal NVB pedicle | Fascelli 2024; uses more corpus spongiosum, and the urethra forms the clitoral hood |
| **Concern for neoclitoral viability** | Dorsal NVB pedicle protection at every step; avoid two-stage approaches when feasible | Neoclitoral necrosis 4.8% in the 62-patient two-stage PIV cohort (Fakin 2021)<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |

Sigurjónsson 2017 measured tactile and vibratory sensitivity in 22 patients at a mean of 37 months and reported erogenous neoclitoral sensation; 86% of these 22 assessed patients experienced orgasm.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

#### Urethroplasty (Urethral Shortening + Repositioning)

The neomeatus is moved to a feminine position and the urethra is shortened. In the Blasdel 2024 narrative review, urinary complication rates in cohorts outside the operating surgeon's own reports were higher than surgeon-reported rates:<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

| Outcome | Surgeon-Reported | Patient-Reported / Other Cohorts |
|---|---|---|
| **Meatal stenosis** | 5–16.3% (mean incidence across systematic reviews)<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> | Not well characterised (4–40% across individual series, Nassiri 2020)<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> |
| **Misdirected urinary stream** | 9.5–33% | 33–55% (AFFIRM: 68.9% reported misdirected stream)<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |
| **Voiding dysfunction** | 5.6–33%<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> | 47–66%<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| **Urinary incontinence** | 4–19.3%<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> | 23–33%<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |

Urethroplasty aims for a downward-directed stream, with adequate urethral shortening to avoid post-void dribbling and a feminine meatal position. Patients should be counseled that the AFFIRM-reported 68.9% misdirected-stream rate is higher than historical surgeon-reported rates.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

#### Labiaplasty

Labia majora are constructed from scrotal skin (or labia minora from preputial or urethral tissue) as the final step of vulvar reconstruction. Performed concurrently with vaginoplasty or vulvoplasty rather than staged.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

### Preoperative Preparation

| Intervention | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Scrotal / perineal electrolysis or laser hair removal** | Used before PIV, beginning 6 to 12 months before surgery, to prevent intravaginal hair growth |
| **Smoking cessation** | Offer cessation support and document abstinence; general-surgery trials found greater benefit with at least 4 weeks of cessation than with shorter periods.<sup>[[40]](#ref40)</sup> |
| **BMI optimization** | BMI was not independently associated with complications in Gaither 2018 n = 330<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>; weight optimization remains a general perioperative goal |
| **Pelvic-floor physical therapy** | A narrative review discusses PFPT before and after gender-affirming surgery.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup> In the FLOWER randomized trial of vaginoplasty, PFPT at 3 and 6 weeks after surgery showed no between-group differences in dilation ease, pain with dilation, vaginal length, or pelvic-floor symptoms at 12 weeks (20 PFPT, 17 controls assessed).<sup>[[41]](#ref41)</sup> |
| **Dilation counseling** | Patients are counseled on the lifelong commitment. Early postoperative dilation averaged 6.6 days/week, 2.4 times/day and about 38 min/session in 17 patients (Gomez 2026 longitudinal patient-reported outcomes) |
| **Estradiol and VTE planning** | SOC v8 suggests surgeon and prescriber collaborate (statement 12.18). It reports no higher VTE rate when hormones were continued and stresses continued sex-steroid therapy after gonadectomy; hold decisions are individualized by VTE risk.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> A 2026 surgeon Delphi consensus recommended perioperative enoxaparin prophylaxis but found no consensus on hormone interruption or restart timing.<sup>[[38]](#ref38)</sup> As one center protocol, Smith, Yuan, and Garcia describe holding injectable estrogen for 2 weeks or oral estrogen for 5 days before feminizing surgery, with resumption 3 weeks after surgery.<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup> |
| **Self-care feasibility for full-depth vaginoplasty** | Counseling covers whether the patient can sustain the dilation and douching expected after surgery, including mobility or dexterity limitations, cognitive or mental-health conditions, access to supplies, a private setting, and distance, cost or transport to experienced complication care; barriers are addressed with support planning, and shallow-depth vulvoplasty is offered when it better matches the patient's goals<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup> |

### Postoperative Dilation Protocol

Dilation maintains neovaginal patency after penile inversion vaginoplasty.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

- **Early postoperative regimen:** about 6.6 days/week, 2.4 times/day and about 38 min/session (Gomez 2026; 17 of 27 prospectively surveyed patients completed all time points, using an author-developed survey).
- **Early difficulty:** all 17 patients who completed every survey time point reported at least one dilation challenge (tightness 71%, bleeding 71%, pain 65%), and prevalence declined significantly after 6 months.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>
- **Predictors of dilation difficulty (Shamamian 2025, retrospective, n = 614):** unemployment OR 2.74, HIV OR 2.59, psychiatric diagnosis besides gender dysphoria OR 1.61 and primary peritoneal graft OR 3.20 (p = 0.019 for the peritoneal-graft association).<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- **Stenosis.** Difficulty with dilation can lead to partial or complete closure of the neovaginal canal.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> Structured PFPT referral is appropriate for patients with persistent pain or difficulty.

### Long-Term Urologic Surveillance

| Concern | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Voiding dysfunction** (common, underrecognized) | In the AFFIRM questionnaire-validation cohort of 102 postoperative women, reported symptoms included misdirected stream 68.9%, nocturia 51.3% and urinary frequency 29.7%; trigger urology referral when symptomatic<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |
| **Prostate screening** | **Prostate is left in situ** during vaginoplasty and sits **anterior to the neovagina**. Digital examination should be performed **vaginally, not rectally**. Discuss prostate cancer screening by organ inventory, age, risk factors and symptoms. Estrogen therapy lowers PSA, and no validated PSA threshold exists for this population.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[45]](#ref45)</sup> |
| **Vaginal hygiene** | Review the operating team's tissue-specific cleaning instructions and examine persistent odor or discharge before treatment. Published douching regimens vary widely in start time, frequency and solution, with no evidence-based standard; Jackson and colleagues describe soap or dilute vinegar/povidone-iodine and a 5-day empiric vaginal metronidazole course as one approach.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[37]](#ref37)</sup> |
| **STI screening** | Standard guidelines apply; counseling adapted to neovaginal tissue type and sexual practices |
| **Periodic exam** | Annual or biennial pelvic examination to monitor for stenosis, granulation tissue, intravaginal hair regrowth, and prolapse |
| **Urethral red flags** | New urinary retention, a persistently weak stream, or bothersome misdirected urination after vaginoplasty warrants urologic assessment; meatal stenosis and voiding dysfunction are reported complications.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| **Future radical prostatectomy** | Laparoscopic and robotic radical prostatectomy after penile-inversion vaginoplasty have been described in case reports.<sup>[[43]](#ref43)[[44]](#ref44)</sup> A 2021 chapter projected, on anatomical grounds, that the neovagina between the prostate and rectum may complicate posterior dissection and that a shortened, scarred or altered urethra and external sphincter could affect continence and vesicourethral-anastomotic healing. It considered retropubic robotic access anatomically feasible because the prostate and space of Retzius remain in their usual positions.<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup> Two case reports cannot estimate the frequency of these complications. |
