# Phallus-Preserving Vaginoplasty (PPV)

**Phallus-preserving vaginoplasty (PPV)** is an individually customized gender-affirming genital procedure that creates a neovaginal canal while retaining the native penis and, in some cases, the testes. It is designed primarily for nonbinary and gender-diverse individuals who want both a vaginal canal for receptive intercourse and preservation of penile function (erection, penetrative capability, voiding through the native urethra).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Published evidence is extremely limited. The Ascha 2024 report of customized procedures (n = 16, combining three procedure types) is the main reference.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

This is the dedicated atlas page. For the broader variant-GGAS framework, see [Non-Binary / Nullification Gender-Affirming Surgery](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/non-binary-nullification). For canonical neovaginal-canal-lining options, see [Peritoneal Pull-Through Vaginoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/peritoneal-pull-through-vaginoplasty), [Intestinal Vaginoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/intestinal-vaginoplasty), and [Skin Graft Vaginoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/skin-graft-vaginoplasty).

---

## Concept and Indications

Unlike standard penile inversion vaginoplasty (PIV), which disassembles the penis and scrotum to construct vulva, clitoris, labia, and canal, PPV preserves the phallus intact.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

**Indications:**

- **Nonbinary gender identity.** Patients who do not identify within a binary framework and want both sets of genital anatomy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Desire to preserve penile sexual function.** Erection, ejaculation and penetrative capability.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Desire for receptive vaginal intercourse.** A functional neovaginal canal is created.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Because penile skin is not available for inversion (the standard PIV lining source), PPV requires alternative tissue sources for the neovaginal canal. The three principal lining options are peritoneum, skin grafts and intestinal segments.

---

## Surgical Technique (Ascha 2024)

The core steps:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

1. **Perineal dissection.** Neovaginal space created between rectum posteriorly and prostate / urethra anteriorly (same plane as standard vaginoplasty), without penile disassembly.
2. **Neovaginal canal lining.** Penile skin unavailable; canal lined with peritoneal flap / skin graft / intestinal segment (see below).
3. **Preservation of the penis.** Glans, corpora cavernosa, and dorsal NVB left intact. The urethra continues to traverse the penis.
4. **Orchiectomy.** Optional. If testes removed, scrotal skin may become available for grafting or labiaplasty; if preserved, alternative tissue sources are required.

## Neovaginal Lining Options — Comparison

The choice of lining tissue is the main differentiating factor in PPV, as in all vaginoplasty.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

| Feature | Peritoneal flap | Skin graft | Intestinal (sigmoid) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tissue source** | Pelvic peritoneum (bladder, rectosigmoid) | Scrotal / abdominal / thigh skin | Sigmoid colon segment |
| **Surgical approach** | Laparoscopic / robotic + perineal | Perineal (± laparoscopic) | Laparoscopic / open abdominal + perineal |
| **Self-lubrication** | Yes (mucosal, transient) | No (keratinised skin) | **Yes (mucus-secreting, persistent)** |
| **Neovaginal depth** | ~14–15 cm | ~11–13 cm | ~12–16 cm |
| **Dilation requirement** | Required | **Intensive, lifelong** | Required; less intensive dilation is reported |
| **Hair-growth risk** | None | Yes (if hair-bearing skin) | None |
| **Unique complications** | Peritoneal granulation; potential bladder effects | Stenosis 1.2–12%; graft necrosis 0–22.8% | Mucosal prolapse 12%; introital stenosis 9%; malodor 8%; mucorrhea 8% |
| **Surgical complexity** | Moderate (requires laparoscopy) | Lower | Highest (bowel resection / anastomosis) |
| **Detailed atlas page** | [Peritoneal Pull-Through Vaginoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/peritoneal-pull-through-vaginoplasty) | [Skin Graft Vaginoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/skin-graft-vaginoplasty) | [Intestinal Vaginoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/intestinal-vaginoplasty) |

### Peritoneal flap — particularly well-suited for PPV

The peritoneal flap is adapted from the Davydov procedure for MRKH vaginal agenesis.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Two peritoneal flaps from the posterior bladder wall and anterior rectosigmoid are mobilized laparoscopically, pulled through the perineal dissection and sutured to form the canal.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

- **Castanon 2022 (Belgrade), n = 52 (penis not preserved; not a PPV series).** Neovaginal depth 14.7 cm and width 3.4 cm at 6 months; complications in 7 patients (13.5%), none needing further surgery; about 96% self-reported satisfaction.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Ratanalert 2025 full-length peritoneal turnover flap (n = 10).** The authors describe a hairless, mucosal lining with self-lubrication on internal examination; 80% maintained depth at 3 months. Follow-up is too short to judge durable lubrication.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

The peritoneal flap does not require penile or scrotal skin, which is why it is attractive when the penis is retained. PPV-specific outcomes with peritoneal lining are not reported separately in the sources cited here.

### Skin graft (extragenital when penile / scrotal tissue unavailable)

When peritoneal or intestinal approaches are not feasible, extragenital full-thickness skin grafts (abdomen, thigh, groin) can line the canal. A systematic review of skin grafts used as adjuncts in PIV reported stenosis in 1.2–12% and neovaginal necrosis in 0–22.8%, and patient satisfaction with lubrication was low in some studies.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Skin-lined neovaginas do not self-lubricate and require long-term dilation.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> In the PPV context, if orchiectomy is not performed, scrotal skin may not be available, necessitating extragenital donor sites with additional donor-site morbidity.

### Intestinal (sigmoid colon)

A sigmoid-colon segment is isolated on its vascular pedicle, closed at one end, and anastomosed to the perineum.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> It provides a self-lubricating, mucus-secreting lining, and less dilation has been reported than with skin-lined canals.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

- **Lava 2025, n = 119 (55.5% primary, 44.5% revision; median follow-up 9.3 months).** Short-term complications occurred in 17.7% and long-term complications in 24.4%. Mucosal prolapse (11.8%) and introital stenosis (9.2%) were the most common long-term complications, and 16.8% needed reoperation.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- Disadvantages: bowel resection and anastomosis (ileus, anastomotic leak), potential malodor, mucorrhea in approximately 8%.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Congenital vaginal absence (MRKH) comparisons.** A randomized trial of 40 patients (26 peritoneal, 14 sigmoid) found less blood loss and shorter surgery with peritoneal vaginoplasty, similar neovaginal length and sexual satisfaction, and more abdominal discomfort and odor with sigmoid.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> A retrospective comparison of 50 patients (20 peritoneal, 30 sigmoid) reported shorter operative time, earlier oral intake, shorter mold use and higher sexual satisfaction with peritoneal vaginoplasty.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> These populations differ from gender-affirming patients.

## Outcomes and Complications Specific to PPV

Published PPV-specific data are extremely limited. The Ascha 2024 series (n = 16; combined PPV, vagina-preserving phalloplasty and removal of genitalia with perineal urethrostomy) reports complications, but its small size limits generalizability.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Considerations unique to PPV versus standard vaginoplasty:**

- **Anatomical constraints.** Intact penis and urethra occupy space anteriorly, potentially limiting canal dimensions and increasing technical difficulty of perineal dissection.
- **Preserved erectile function.** Corpora cavernosa and NVB remain intact, the primary functional goal but adds surgical complexity.
- **Urethral considerations.** Urethra is not shortened or repositioned; voiding continues through the penile meatus.
- **Risk of rectovaginal or urethrovaginal fistula.** The dissection plane is the same as in standard vaginoplasty. Rectal perforation occurred in 1.6% (6 of 384) in a Ghent PIV series, and rectovaginal fistula is a recognized major complication.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Neovaginal stenosis.** Remains the most common long-term complication across all lining types.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

---

## Postoperative Care

Postoperative management parallels standard vaginoplasty with modifications:

- **Dilation.** Required for all lining types. A regimen of up to three times daily initially, tapering over months to years, is typical. Non-compliance is the strongest predictor of complications and reoperation.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- **Hygiene.** Skin-lined canals may accumulate sebum and dead skin, and dilute vinegar or povidone-iodine douching has been described. Intestinal-lined canals may produce excessive mucus.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **Sexual function.** Preservation of erection and ejaculation is the aim of the procedure, and receptive vaginal intercourse is attempted once healing is complete. Outcome data are lacking.
- **Surveillance.** Periodic pelvic exam to monitor for stenosis, granulation tissue, or other complications.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

---

## Evidence Gaps and Future Directions

PPV has very limited published evidence, primarily case series and technique descriptions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Unanswered questions include:

- **Long-term neovaginal patency and depth maintenance** with the phallus in situ.
- **Sexual function outcomes** (both penetrative penile and receptive vaginal).
- **Optimal tissue lining choice** specifically for PPV.
- **Psychological and QoL outcomes** in nonbinary patients undergoing this specific procedure.
- **Comparative complication rates** versus standard vaginoplasty.

A prospective single-center study of vaginoplasty reported improvement in quality-of-life measures (PHQ-9, GAD-7, body image, sexual function). Whether these benefits extend to PPV is not established.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
