# Phallus-Preserving Vaginoplasty (PPV)

**Phallus-preserving vaginoplasty (PPV)** is an individually customized gender-affirming genital procedure that creates a neovaginal canal while retaining the native penis and, in some cases, the testes. It is designed for nonbinary and gender-diverse people seeking a canal for receptive intercourse while retaining the native penis and penile urethra. Preserving erection, penetrative capability, and native urethral voiding are operative aims; PPV-specific functional outcomes have not been established in this report.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Published evidence is extremely limited. The Ascha 2024 report of customized procedures (n = 16, combining three procedure types) is the main reference.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

This is the dedicated atlas page. For the broader variant-GGAS framework, see [Non-Binary / Nullification Gender-Affirming Surgery](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/non-binary-nullification). For canonical neovaginal-canal-lining options, see [Peritoneal Pull-Through Vaginoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/peritoneal-pull-through-vaginoplasty), [Intestinal Vaginoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/intestinal-vaginoplasty), and [Skin Graft Vaginoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/skin-graft-vaginoplasty).

## Concept and Indications

Unlike standard penile inversion vaginoplasty (PIV), which disassembles the penis and scrotum to construct vulva, clitoris, labia, and canal, PPV preserves the phallus intact.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

**Indications:**

- **Nonbinary gender identity.** Patients who do not identify within a binary framework and want both sets of genital anatomy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Desire to preserve penile sexual function.** Erection, ejaculation and penetrative capability.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Desire for receptive vaginal intercourse.** A functional neovaginal canal is created.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Because penile skin is not available for inversion (the standard PIV lining source), PPV requires alternative tissue sources for the neovaginal canal. Peritoneum, skin grafts, and allografts are described as PPV canal-lining options; the intestinal figures below come from series that do not report PPV outcomes separately.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[12]](#ref12)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

## Surgical Technique (Ascha 2024)

The core steps:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

1. **Perineal dissection.** Neovaginal space created between rectum posteriorly and prostate / urethra anteriorly (same plane as standard vaginoplasty), without penile disassembly.
2. **Neovaginal canal lining.** Penile skin unavailable; canal lined with peritoneal flap / skin graft / intestinal segment (see below).
3. **Preservation of the penis.** Glans, corpora cavernosa, and dorsal NVB left intact. The urethra continues to traverse the penis.
4. **Orchiectomy decision.** Optional. If testes are removed, scrotal skin may become available for grafting or labiaplasty; if retained, alternative tissue sources may be required. Before bilateral removal, discuss fertility preservation and possible erectile effects, and plan ongoing sex-steroid therapy after gonadectomy to prevent hypogonadism.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[22]](#ref22)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

## Neovaginal Lining Options — Comparison

Lining-tissue choice shapes the PPV operation, but the depths, dilation burden, and complication figures below come from other vaginoplasty populations; they are planning context, not PPV-specific estimates.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

| Feature | Peritoneal flap | Skin graft | Intestinal (sigmoid) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tissue source** | Pelvic peritoneum (bladder, rectosigmoid) | Scrotal / abdominal / thigh skin | Sigmoid colon segment |
| **Surgical approach** | Laparoscopic / robotic + perineal | Perineal (± laparoscopic) | Laparoscopic / open abdominal + perineal |
| **Lubrication** | Lubricated mucosal surface observed on early examination in a 10-patient non-PPV series; durability unknown<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | No intrinsic mucosal lubrication<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | Mucus-secreting lining<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Neovaginal depth** | 13.41 cm pooled at last available follow-up in 10 robot-assisted peritoneal-flap studies (974 patients overall); not an isolated PPV estimate<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> | No PPV-specific estimate in the cited penile-inversion review<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | 12.5 cm mean in 36 mixed-indication rectosigmoid cases<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Dilation requirement** | Lifelong dilation advised for PPV in a surgical perspective; frequency unspecified<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> | Lifelong dilation advised for PPV in a surgical perspective; frequency unspecified<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> | Less long-term dilator use reported in a 36-patient mixed-indication rectosigmoid series; PPV-specific regimen unknown<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Hair-growth risk** | None | Yes (if hair-bearing skin) | None |
| **Unique complications** | Peritoneal granulation; potential bladder effects; risk of bowel, ureter, bladder, or rectal injury with a peritoneal approach<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>; prior abdominal surgery was a contraindication in one 10-patient non-PPV full-length turnover series<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | In skin-graft-augmented penile inversion series, neovaginal stenosis 1.2–12% and neovaginal necrosis 0–22.8%<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | Mucosal prolapse 11.8% and introital stenosis 9.2% in 119 sigmoid cases<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>; malodor and excessive mucosal discharge 8.3% each in 36 mixed-indication rectosigmoid cases<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Surgical complexity** | Moderate (requires laparoscopy) | Lower | Highest (bowel resection / anastomosis) |

### Peritoneal flap — particularly well-suited for PPV

The peritoneal flap is adapted from the Davydov procedure for MRKH vaginal agenesis.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> In the cited penile-inversion series, laparoscopically mobilized peritoneal flaps from the posterior bladder and anterior rectosigmoid formed the neovaginal apex and extended the canal beyond inverted penile skin or a skin graft.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

- **Castanon 2022 (Belgrade), n = 52 (penis not preserved; not a PPV series).** Neovaginal depth 14.7 cm and width 3.4 cm at 6 months; complications in 7 patients (13.5%), none needing further surgery; about 96% self-reported satisfaction.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Ratanalert 2025 full-length peritoneal turnover flap (n = 10).** The authors describe a hairless, mucosal lining with self-lubrication on internal examination; At 3 months, 8 of 10 patients retained immediate postoperative depth, 1 had mild depth loss, and 1 developed complete canal closure after stopping dilation; lubrication and depth durability beyond 3 months remain unknown.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

In a proposed PPV peritoneal-flap approach, peritoneum supplies much of the canal lining without penile skin; retained scrotal skin covers the perineum and introitus, and an extragenital skin graft or allograft may bridge the gap between flap and introitus.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> PPV-specific outcomes with peritoneal lining are not reported separately in the sources cited here.

### Skin graft (extragenital when penile / scrotal tissue unavailable)

When peritoneal or intestinal approaches are not feasible, extragenital full-thickness skin grafts (abdomen, thigh, groin) can line the canal. A systematic review of skin grafts used as adjuncts in PIV reported stenosis in 1.2–12% and neovaginal necrosis in 0–22.8%, and patient satisfaction with lubrication was low in some studies.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Skin-lined neovaginas do not self-lubricate and require long-term dilation.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> In the PPV context, if orchiectomy is not performed, scrotal skin may not be available, necessitating extragenital donor sites with additional donor-site morbidity.

### Intestinal (sigmoid colon)

A sigmoid-colon segment is isolated on its vascular pedicle, closed at one end, and anastomosed to the perineum.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> It provides a self-lubricating, mucus-secreting lining, and less dilation has been reported than with skin-lined canals.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

- **Lava 2025, n = 119 (55.5% primary, 44.5% revision; median follow-up 9.3 months).** Complications occurred in 17.7% before day 30 and 24.4% on or after day 30. Mucosal prolapse (11.8%) and introital stenosis (9.2%) were the most common events on or after day 30, and 16.8% needed reoperation.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- Disadvantages include bowel resection and anastomosis, with risks such as ileus or leak. In a 36-patient rectosigmoid series with mixed indications and one to ten years of follow-up, excessive discharge and malodor each occurred in 3/36 (8.3%); these are not PPV-specific rates.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Congenital vaginal absence (MRKH) comparisons.** A randomized trial of 40 patients (26 peritoneal, 14 sigmoid) found less blood loss and shorter surgery with peritoneal vaginoplasty, similar neovaginal length and sexual satisfaction, and more abdominal discomfort and odor with sigmoid.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> A retrospective comparison of 50 patients (20 peritoneal, 30 sigmoid) reported shorter operative time, earlier oral intake, shorter mold use and higher sexual satisfaction with peritoneal vaginoplasty.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> These populations differ from gender-affirming patients.

## Outcomes and Complications Specific to PPV

Published PPV-specific data are extremely limited. The Ascha 2024 series (n = 16; combined PPV, vagina-preserving phalloplasty and removal of genitalia with perineal urethrostomy) reports complications, but its small size limits generalizability.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Considerations unique to PPV versus standard vaginoplasty:**

- **Anatomical constraints.** Intact penis and urethra occupy space anteriorly, potentially limiting canal dimensions and increasing technical difficulty of perineal dissection.
- **Penile sexual function.** Preserving the corpora cavernosa and dorsal neurovascular bundle is an operative aim, but canal dissection can injure cavernosal nerves and cause postoperative erectile dysfunction.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- **Urethral considerations.** Urethra is not shortened or repositioned; voiding continues through the penile meatus.
- **Risk of rectovaginal or urethrovaginal fistula.** The dissection plane is the same as in standard vaginoplasty. Rectal perforation occurred in 1.6% (6 of 384) in a Ghent PIV series, and rectovaginal fistula is a recognized major complication.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Neovaginal stenosis.** A 2015 review of penile-inversion (1,461 patients) and bowel vaginoplasty (102 patients) identified stenosis as the most frequent complication in both groups.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

## Postoperative Care

Postoperative management parallels standard vaginoplasty with modifications:

- **Dilation.** Plan a lining-specific schedule with the operating team and monitor canal depth and caliber. The cited sources do not establish a PPV-specific frequency or duration. In a retrospective cohort of 240 penile-inversion patients with median follow-up of 87 days, combined nonadherence to dilation and activity restrictions was associated with complications or reoperation; this has not been tested in PPV.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- **Hygiene.** Skin-lined canals may accumulate sebum and desquamated cells; dilute vinegar and povidone-iodine douching are described practices, but timing, frequency, and solutions vary among centers without an established standard. Intestinal-lined canals may produce excessive mucus; use the operating team's tissue-specific care instructions.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[18]](#ref18)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- **Sexual function.** Preservation of erection and ejaculation is the aim of the procedure, and receptive vaginal intercourse is attempted once healing is complete. Outcome data are lacking.
- **Surveillance.** Assess the canal for stenosis and granulation according to symptoms and the operating team's plan. Document retained organs for routine risk-based screening, including the prostate; investigate persistent bleeding, discharge, or lesions according to the lining tissue.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

## Evidence Gaps and Future Directions

PPV has very limited published evidence, primarily case series and technique descriptions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Unanswered questions include:

- **Long-term neovaginal patency and depth maintenance** with the phallus in situ.
- **Sexual function outcomes** (both penetrative penile and receptive vaginal).
- **Optimal tissue lining choice** specifically for PPV.
- **Psychological and QoL outcomes** in nonbinary patients undergoing this specific procedure.
- **Comparative complication rates** versus standard vaginoplasty.

A prospective single-center study of vaginoplasty reported improvement in quality-of-life measures (PHQ-9, GAD-7, body image, sexual function). Whether these benefits extend to PPV is not established.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
