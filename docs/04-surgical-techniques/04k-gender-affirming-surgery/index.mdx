# Gender-Affirming Surgery

Gender-affirming surgery (GAS) is the operative arm of multidisciplinary gender-affirming care, governed by the **WPATH Standards of Care v8 (2022)**, the **ACOG 2021 Committee Opinion**, the **Endocrine Society 2017 Clinical Practice Guideline**, and the **AAFP 2023 Position Paper**. The atlas below splits the decision space into the two cohorts that drive technique selection: **masculinizing surgery** (defaults to the open tab) and **feminizing surgery**. Non-binary / nullification operations are referenced in their own subsection of the Special Populations module. The decision framework on each tab is goal-directed, prioritizing standing micturition, penetrative function, canal depth and lubrication, dysphoria-burden weighting, and the staged sequencing that minimizes urethral and prosthetic complications.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Master Decision Framework

The masculinizing GAS plan hinges on **four explicit patient priorities**: (1) standing micturition (drives urethral lengthening), (2) penetrative intercourse (drives phalloplasty + erectile prosthesis), (3) aesthetic phallus size (metoidioplasty ~5.6 cm vs phalloplasty ~14 cm), and (4) tolerance for staged morbidity (metoidioplasty single-stage with markedly lower complications vs phalloplasty multi-stage with high urethral burden).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The contemporary decision anchors are **WPATH SOC v8** for eligibility and timing, the **Bordas 2021 *Front Endocrinol* 813-patient metoidioplasty series**, the **Wang 2026 *Microsurgery* flap-vs-flap complication-rate analysis**, the **Berli 2025 Big Ben method** two-stage urethral lengthening (27% urologic complication rate; 96% standing micturition), the **Veerman 2020** and **Waterschoot 2021** complication-rate publications for phalloplasty and metoidioplasty respectively, and **Pigot / Levy 2026** consensus on penile-implant timing and technique.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

### Step 1 — Goal-Directed Pathway Selection

| Patient Priority | Recommended Pathway | Alternative | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Standing micturition without penetrative function; minimize complications** | **[Metoidioplasty](/docs/special-populations/05c-gender-affirming/masculinizing-procedures)** with urethral lengthening (UL) | Phalloplasty + UL (overkill if penetrative function is not a goal) | Single-stage; preserves erogenous sensation (4.8/5 satisfaction); lower urethral complication rates than phalloplasty<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Standing micturition + penetrative intercourse** | **Phalloplasty + UL** (staged), erectile prosthesis at 9–12 mo | Metoidioplasty first → conversion to phalloplasty later (option preserved) | Phalloplasty alone is non-functional for penetration without prosthesis; staged sequencing minimizes infection and prosthetic failure<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Penetrative intercourse without standing micturition** | Phalloplasty **without UL** + perineal urethrostomy + erectile prosthesis | Metoidioplasty + phalloplasty conversion | Markedly lower urethral complication rates when UL is omitted (UL is the single strongest predictor of fistula OR 6.07 / stricture OR 24.5; Waterschoot 2021)<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Maximal sensation prioritized; size acceptable** | **Metoidioplasty** | Phalloplasty (sensation 3.4/5; with neurorrhaphy can recover) | Metoidioplasty preserves native clitoral neurovasculature; sensation 4.8/5 (Robinson 2021)<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Active smoker** | **Defer all UL until cessation ≥4–6 weeks documented** | Metoidioplasty without UL or phalloplasty without UL while in cessation program | Smoking is the strongest modifiable risk factor — fistula OR 6.54, permanent fistula OR 3.76 (Waterschoot)<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Uncertain / wants to preserve pathway flexibility** | **Metoidioplasty** as first operation | — | Phalloplasty conversion remains feasible after metoidioplasty; the reverse is rarely possible<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

### Step 2 — Phalloplasty Flap Selection

| Clinical Scenario | First-Line Flap | Alternative(s) | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Default candidate; values aesthetics + sensation** | **[Radial forearm free flap (RFFF)](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/radial-forearm)** with tube-in-tube urethra (highest sensation; Garaffa 2010 UCL standing-tip-voiding 99%) | Pedicled [ALT](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/anterolateral-thigh) (concealed donor site) | Tube-in-tube ALT (D'Arpa: feasible in only 5.8% of harvests) |
| **Wants concealed donor site, accepts debulking** | **Pedicled [ALT](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/anterolateral-thigh)** with separate-flap urethra | RFFF with cosmetic forearm-scar counseling | ALT with single-stage tube-in-tube urethra |
| **Lowest overall complication rate prioritized** | **Pedicled abdominal flap** (Wang 2026 microsurgery — lowest overall complication 40.9%) | RFFF | Free-flap when patient cannot tolerate microsurgical operating time |
| **Contemporary urethral-stricture optimization** | **Two-stage "Big Ben Method"** (Berli 2025 — stricture 8%; fistula 16.4%; standing micturition 96%) | Single-stage RFFF | Single-stage tube-in-tube in active smokers |
| **Intrinsic rigidity preferred (no prosthesis required)** | **Fibula osteocutaneous free flap** (limited centers) | RFFF + delayed IPP | Fibula in patients with peripheral vascular disease |
| **Microsurgery not available / contraindicated** | **Pedicled ALT** or pedicled abdominal flap | — | RFFF / fibula at non-microsurgical centers |

### Step 3 — Urethral Lengthening Decision

| Clinical Scenario | First-Line | Alternative | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Standing micturition desired + non-smoker + adequate vaginal-wall tissue** | **Full UL with anterior vaginal-wall flap + labia minora flaps + buccal mucosa graft** (modified Belgrade for metoidioplasty); RFFF tube-in-tube or two-stage Big Ben for phalloplasty | UL with pre-laminated buccal mucosa onto the phalloplasty flap | UL in active smoker (defer) |
| **Standing micturition desired + previously failed UL** | Staged repair with [BMG urethroplasty](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa) (Sterling 2023 transmeatal ventral inlay; Daneshvar 2020 multi-institution validation) | Conversion to perineal urethrostomy | Repeat single-stage UL |
| **Penetrative function only; standing micturition not required** | **Phalloplasty without UL + perineal urethrostomy** (~5× lower complication rate) | Metoidioplasty without UL | Urethral lengthening "just in case" |
| **Preserves vaginal canal; non-binary anatomical goals** | **Metoidioplasty without UL or vaginectomy** | Shaft-only phalloplasty with vaginal preservation (Chen, Moorefield) | Coercive vaginectomy when not patient-aligned |

### Step 4 — Vaginectomy Timing & Technique

| Clinical Scenario | First-Line | Alternative | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Phalloplasty with UL planned** | **Concurrent transperineal vaginectomy** with anterior-vaginal-wall flap preserved for UL (sharp excision; Hougen 2020) | Electrocautery fulguration (Ho 2025; equivalent outcomes, shorter OR time, lower EBL) | Skipping vaginectomy when UL is planned (high fistula risk) |
| **Metoidioplasty + UL planned** | **Optional vaginectomy** based on dysphoria-burden weighting — fulguration preferred for shorter OR / EBL | Sharp excision when AVW flap is needed for UL | Mandating vaginectomy for all UL candidates |
| **Metoidioplasty without UL** | **Optional vaginectomy** — patient-aligned dysphoria-driven decision | Vaginectomy deferred indefinitely | Coercive vaginectomy |
| **Non-binary patient retaining vaginal canal** | **No vaginectomy** | — | Vaginectomy as default in non-binary care |
| **Concurrent laparoscopic hysterectomy** | **Laparoscopic-assisted vaginectomy** (Gomes da Costa 2016 — vaginal mucosa preserved for UL in 100%) | Two-stage (laparoscopic hysterectomy → later transperineal vaginectomy) | Open vaginectomy when laparoscopic platform is available |

### Step 5 — Scrotoplasty, Testicular Implants, Erectile Prosthesis (Staged Sequencing)

| Stage | Operation | Timing | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stage 1** | Hysterectomy ± BSO; can combine with chest surgery | Months 0–6 (before genital surgery if UL planned) | Mancini 2021: combined HBSO + chest surgery feasible / safe; complication 10.5%, satisfaction 9.9/10. Laparoscopic in ~96.5%<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Stage 2** | **Vaginectomy + scrotoplasty + metoidioplasty or phalloplasty ± UL** | Months 6–12 | Hoebeke pedicled-labia-majora U-flap scrotoplasty (most-common; explant 11.5% vs 20.8% other techniques)<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **Stage 3 (if phalloplasty)** | **Testicular implants** | **≥6 mo after scrotoplasty** | Trend toward smaller / lighter prostheses + delayed implantation has reduced complications. Smoking is a major risk factor<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **Stage 4 (if phalloplasty)** | **Erectile prosthesis** (3-piece IPP preferred; ZSI 100 FtM or malleable as alternatives) | **9–12 mo after phalloplasty** | Levy 2026 consensus protocol: confirm flap vascularity + reliable micturition before proceeding; cylinder wrapping + pubic-bone fixation are critical; 5-yr retention 42–78%; 83.9% achieve penetration<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |

### Step 6 — Long-Term Urologic Surveillance

All patients with UL require **lifelong urologic follow-up**. Red flags for urgent referral: urinary retention, postvoid dribbling, pelvic pain or fullness, recurrent UTI, persistent vaginal cavity, or cuffing of the neomeatus. Urethral complications may present **weeks to years** after the index operation.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

The feminizing GAS plan is organized around four anatomic domains: **breast / chest**, **genitalia (vaginoplasty / vulvoplasty)**, **face (FFS)**, and **neck / voice (chondrolaryngoplasty + voice surgery)**. Eligibility and timing follow **WPATH SOC v8 (2022)** and **ACOG 2021**; perioperative estrogen management is governed by the WPATH SOC v8 update that **estrogen continuation is preferred** in most patients, with selective hold for VTE-risk-stratified perioperative windows.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The contemporary decision anchors for genital surgery are the **Dy 2021** and **Castanon 2024** robotic peritoneal-flap series, the **Jacoby 2019** robotic-assisted-augmentation-of-penile-inversion (+5 cm canal depth), the **Haley 2025** prospective QoL series after penile-inversion vaginoplasty, the **Robinson 2022** report of intra-abdominal complications (2.2% in 274 patients), the **Ratanalert 2025** full-length peritoneal-pull-through series, and the **Coon 2020** + **Liu 2024 SR/meta** for breast augmentation.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)[[18]](#ref18)[[19]](#ref19)[[20]](#ref20)[[21]](#ref21)[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

### Step 1 — Domain-Driven Goal Sequencing

| Patient Priority | Recommended First Operation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Chest dysphoria dominant; minimal genital concerns** | **Breast augmentation** after ≥6 months of feminizing hormone therapy (some experts: 2–3 yr to maximize hormonal development) | ACOG 2021; transfeminine patients require larger implants (median 500 mL vs 350 mL) and inframammary fold lowering<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[20]](#ref20)</sup> |
| **Genital dysphoria dominant; desires neovagina + lubrication** | **Vaginoplasty** (penile inversion / peritoneal pull-through / intestinal — see Step 2) | Sequencing depends on facial / chest priorities; canal depth and lubrication are the technique-selection drivers |
| **Genital dysphoria; canal not desired** | **Vulvoplasty** (zero-depth or shallow-depth) | Shallow-depth variants (Stelmar / van der Sluis / Aaen) for selected patients; fewer dilation requirements |
| **Facial dysphoria dominant** | **FFS by facial third** (forehead / midface / lower-third + chin) — Rochlin 2022 secondary-FFS data documents staged approaches | Morrison QoL data is the contemporary anchor |
| **Voice dysphoria** | **Voice therapy first** → **glottoplasty (Wendler)** or **feminization laryngoplasty** if pitch elevation insufficient | Effect-size differences favor laryngoplasty for higher fundamental-frequency targets |
| **Tracheal-cartilage prominence** | **Chondrolaryngoplasty** (tracheal shave) — typically standalone or combined with FFS | Outpatient; voice not affected when performed below the vocal folds |

### Step 2 — Vaginoplasty Technique Selection

Each technique balances canal depth, lubrication mechanism, dilation requirements, complication profile, and prior orchiectomy / hormone status.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[18]](#ref18)[[19]](#ref19)[[22]](#ref22)[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

| Clinical Scenario | First-Line | Alternative(s) | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Default candidate; adequate penile + scrotal skin; standard depth (~10–14 cm) acceptable** | **[Penile inversion vaginoplasty](/docs/special-populations/05c-gender-affirming/feminizing-procedures)** (penile-skin tube + scrotal full-thickness graft for canal extension) | **Robotic peritoneal-pull-through assisted penile inversion** (Jacoby 2019 — adds +5 cm depth) | Penile inversion alone in patients with insufficient penile / scrotal donor tissue |
| **Insufficient penile / scrotal donor tissue (e.g., prior orchiectomy, neoadjuvant hormone use, smaller native anatomy)** | **Robotic peritoneal-pull-through vaginoplasty** (Da Vinci Xi or SP) — Dy 2021 100-pt series, Castanon 2024 52-pt series, Ratanalert 2025 full-length series<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)[[22]](#ref22)</sup> | Augmentation of penile inversion with peritoneal flap (Jacoby 2019) | Intestinal vaginoplasty as first-line in standard candidates (higher complication burden) |
| **Maximal lubrication required; prior vaginoplasty failure with stenosis** | **Sigmoid (intestinal) vaginoplasty** | Robotic peritoneal-flap revision | Sigmoid as first-line (long-term mucus, diversion-colitis-like inflammation, rare neoplasia signal) |
| **Canal not desired; minimal external feminization wanted** | **Vulvoplasty** (zero- or shallow-depth) — Stelmar / van der Sluis / Aaen variants | Vaginoplasty deferred indefinitely | Coercive canal creation |
| **Revision after stenosis** | **Robotic peritoneal-flap revision** or sigmoid revision (Keller 2024 algorithmic framework) — see [Revision & Salvage GAS](/docs/special-populations/05c-gender-affirming/revision-salvage-gas) | Repeat penile-inversion (often donor-tissue-limited) | Repeat skin-graft-only revision in irradiated bed |
| **Rectoneovaginal fistula** | **Diversion + tissue interposition (gracilis / Martius)** at high-volume center | Direct repair with omental interposition | Direct primary repair without diversion |
| **Concurrent mesh-eligible prolapse risk (rare)** | Sacrocolpopexy adapted to neovagina if prolapse develops | Pessary | (No high-quality data — case reports only) |

### Step 3 — Peritoneal vs Penile-Inversion vs Intestinal Sub-Comparison

| Feature | Penile Inversion | Peritoneal Pull-Through (Robotic) | Sigmoid (Intestinal) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Depth** | ~10–14 cm (donor-tissue-dependent) | **+5 cm over penile inversion when used as augmentation; full-length when standalone**<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[22]](#ref22)</sup> | 12–15 cm; bowel-length-dependent |
| **Lubrication** | None (squamous epithelium) | Peritoneal serous secretion (modest) | Mucinous (ample; may require panty-liner) |
| **Donor-tissue requirement** | Penile + scrotal skin; insufficient in prior-orchiectomy / neoadjuvant-hormone patients | None — peritoneum ample | 15–20 cm sigmoid bowel segment |
| **Microsurgery / robotics** | None | **Robotic platform required** (Da Vinci Xi or SP) | Laparoscopic or open bowel anastomosis |
| **Dilation requirements** | Lifelong, frequent | Lifelong, frequent | Less aggressive (mucosa more compliant) |
| **Key risks** | Stenosis (5–15%), rectoneovaginal fistula (~1%), urethral stricture | Robinson 2022 intra-abdominal complications 2.2% in 274 pts (including unique internal hernia)<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> | Diversion-colitis-like inflammation, mucus burden, rare neoplasia, anastomotic leak |
| **Ideal candidate** | Standard candidate with adequate donor tissue | Insufficient donor tissue; revision; depth augmentation | Sigmoid as second-line / revision; maximal-lubrication priority |

### Step 4 — Breast Augmentation Decision

| Clinical Scenario | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Newly initiating feminizing hormones** | Defer surgery **≥6 months** post-hormone-initiation per ACOG 2021 (some experts: 2–3 yr to maximize Tanner-stage development before surgical augmentation) |
| **Sustained hormonal development with stable but inadequate breast tissue** | **Subglandular or subpectoral implant placement**; transfeminine-specific considerations: wider base width, longer sternal-notch–nipple distance, implant median 500 mL, routine inframammary-fold lowering<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[20]](#ref20)</sup> |
| **Strongly hormone-resistant** | Earlier surgical augmentation (no rigid 6-month rule when hormonal response is poor) |
| **Capsular-contracture or asymmetry-revision indication** | Standard plastic-surgery revision principles; meta-analysis pooled rates: capsular contracture 3.6%, hematoma 0.6%, malposition 3.9%<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> |

### Step 5 — Facial Feminization Surgery (FFS) Framework

| Facial Third | Operations | Anchors |
|---|---|---|
| **Upper third** (frontal bossing, brow position, hairline) | Frontal-sinus setback (Type III), brow lift, hairline advancement | Morrison QoL data; standard anchor for surgical planning |
| **Midface** (zygoma, nose, lip-philtrum) | Rhinoplasty, malar augmentation or contouring, lip lift, philtrum shortening | Patient-photo-driven planning is standard |
| **Lower third** (mandible, chin, masseter) | Genioplasty, mandibular-angle reduction, masseter reduction | Combined with upper-third in single-stage procedures when feasible |
| **Neck** (chondrolaryngoplasty / tracheal shave) | Reduction of thyroid-cartilage prominence | Outpatient; voice not affected when performed below vocal folds |

### Step 6 — Voice Surgery

| Approach | Mechanism | Effect Size | Best Indication |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Voice therapy first** | Behavioral pitch elevation | Modest (mean +20–40 Hz) | All patients before considering surgery |
| **Wendler glottoplasty** | Anterior-commissure web shortening of vocal-fold vibrating length | +30–60 Hz fundamental frequency | Failed voice therapy; modest pitch-elevation goal |
| **Feminization laryngoplasty (cricothyroid approximation)** | Increases vocal-fold tension | Larger pitch elevation than glottoplasty | Higher fundamental-frequency targets |
| **Combined approach** | Glottoplasty + cricothyroid approximation | Largest effect-size series | Recalcitrant patients |
