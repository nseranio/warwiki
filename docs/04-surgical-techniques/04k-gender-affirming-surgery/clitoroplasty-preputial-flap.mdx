# Clitoroplasty — Preputial-Skin Flap with Dorsal Neurovascular-Bundle Pedicle

**Clitoroplasty using a preputial-skin flap with dorsal neurovascular bundle (NVB) pedicle** is a described technique for constructing a sensate neoclitoris during feminizing genital surgery. The preputial-skin hood is preferred by the Fascelli group when sufficient skin is available; a urethral-flap hood is another described option.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The pedicled island neurovascular flap of the glans penis has been the standard clitoroplasty technique for more than a decade.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> A pedicled island neurovascular flap of the glans penis, with the paired dorsal penile nerves and dorsal penile arteries preserved as the vascular and sensory pedicle, is combined with preputial (foreskin) or distal-shaft skin to simultaneously construct the neoclitoris, clitoral hood, and labia minora.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Clitoral sensitivity was preserved in 96% of 84 patients at 12 months and in all 97 patients at 6 months in two prospective series from one center (Mañero), and the second series reported no deep or total flap necrosis.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Orgasm was reported by 83–86% of patients in the Ghent 15-year and Sigurjónsson series.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

This is the dedicated atlas page covering the canonical preputial-skin technique and its named variants. For the cohort-level decision framework, see [Feminizing Gender-Affirming Surgery](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/feminizing-surgery). For the host PIV operation, see [Penile Inversion Vaginoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/penile-inversion-vaginoplasty).

## Anatomic Foundation

The technique is grounded in the embryologic homology between the glans penis and glans clitoris, and between the penile prepuce and the clitoral hood / labia minora.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### Dorsal neurovascular bundle (NVB)

- **Dorsal nerves of the penis.** Paired somatic sensory nerves, terminal branches of the pudendal nerve (S2–S4), that provide the primary sensory supply to the glans and are homologous to the dorsal nerves of the clitoris. They run dorsally along the penile shaft, deep to Buck's fascia, on either side of the deep dorsal vein.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Dorsal arteries of the penis.** Paired branches of the internal pudendal artery within Buck's fascia, providing the primary arterial supply to the glans.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Deep dorsal vein.** Midline venous drainage between the paired dorsal arteries and nerves.
- The NVB fans out into the glans. In the natal clitoris (unembalmed cadavers), the dorsal nerve measures about 3 mm (range 2–4) at the lateral crura and 1 mm (range 1–2) at the distal clitoral body.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- In the same cadaver series, nerve density was significantly greater in the dorsal than the ventral half of the clitoral body, which is consistent with dorsal-based flap harvest.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Glans penis and prepuce

- **Glans.** The penile glans contains corpus spongiosum and should not be called non-erectile; the glans clitoris and its deeper erectile structures have different anatomy.<sup>[[32]](#ref32)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Corona (coronal ridge).** Regarded as particularly rich in sensory innervation; the Andalusia group built its technique on preserving it.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Inner preputial layer.** Thin, pliable mucocutaneous tissue with natural oily secretions; embryologically homologous to the clitoral hood and contributes to the labia minora.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Surgical Technique — Fascelli/Dugi Preputial-Skin Clitoroplasty (Modified Ghent Technique)

This preputial-skin technique was developed at Ghent University Hospital by Monstrey and colleagues and subsequently modified by multiple groups.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[6]](#ref6)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

### Uncircumcised patient

1. **Penile degloving.** Penile skin degloved as a tube; prepuce kept attached to the glans.
2. **Dorsal NVB dissection.** Paired dorsal nerves, paired dorsal arteries, and deep dorsal vein dissected off the dorsal corpora cavernosa from pubic root to glans. The bundle is elevated with a strip of tunica albuginea to protect the nerves.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
3. **Corpora cavernosa excision.** Resect the corpora cavernosa while retaining the dorsal NVB with its tunica-albuginea strip, the glans, and the urethra.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[33]](#ref33)</sup>
4. **Glans design.** Excise medial glans and urethral mucosa while retaining coronal tissue for neoclitoral construction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
5. **Neoclitoris formation.** Corona approximated approximately 1 cm from midline to create a visible ovoid neoclitoris; lateral coronal tissue is preserved as buried "clitoral corpora" providing additional erogenous sensation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
6. **Neoclitoral anchoring and NVB fixation.** The neoclitoris is anchored to proximal tunica at the adductor-longus-tendon level; the folded dorsal NVB is fixed in the suprapubic area.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
7. **Clitoral hood and labia minora.** Inner preputial skin used for:
   - **Clitoral hood.** Draped over the neoclitoris superiorly.
   - **Labia minora.** Extending inferiorly to frame the vestibule and introitus.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
8. **Clitoro-urethral inset.** Spatulate the ventral urethra and approximate it to the clitoris; suture preputial skin proximally as tension allows and inset the complex into the penile skin flap. Limit the superior midline split and confirm tension-free hood coverage, since an excessively high split or flap tension can leave the neoclitoris exposed after healing.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[33]](#ref33)</sup>

### Circumcised patient

Two pathways depending on residual tissue (Fascelli 2024 anatomy-guided framework):<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- **For circumcised patients, Fascelli et al. describe more than 2 cm of inner preputial skin in Methods and at least 2 cm of skin distal to the circumcision scar in Results; both specify at least 8 cm of shaft skin proximal to the scar.** Preputial-skin clitoroplasty is still performed using residual inner preputial skin for the hood and distal-shaft skin for labia minora.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Limited preputial skin.** Distal shaft skin can form the hood and labia minora after circumcision when enough skin remains for the introitus. When skin is insufficient, Fascelli et al. use a dorsal urethral flap and sew a small residual preputial collar to the spongiosum and urethral mucosa.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[2]](#ref2)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

| **B. M-Flap (Mañero)** | Central triangle of M-shaped flap | Both legs of M + attached prepuce | Uncircumcised; aesthetic refinement | Single composite flap; 0% deep or total necrosis in n = 97<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |

| **F. Urethral flap (Brassard)** | Standard glans reduction | Urethra creates hood | **Circumcised / insufficient skin** | Uses urethral tissue when preputial skin is insufficient; Fascelli retains a small preputial-skin collar for inset<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

### B. M-Flap (Mañero / IMCLINIC)

Pedicled island neurovascular flap of the glans penis harvested in a letter-"M" fashion with attached preputial skin; the central triangle of the M forms the neoclitoris and both legs plus attached prepuce form the labia minora and clitoral prepuce. n = 97: 0% total / deep flap necrosis; 8.2% partial / superficial skin necrosis without sequelae; 1 labial abscess; 1 urethral hematoma. 100% tactile and erogenous sensitivity at 6 months.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### C. Corona glans (Giraldo / Andalusia)

Instead of a dorsal glans chip, bifid dorsolateral coronal flaps ("open lotus" / "bull's horns") and a semicircular preputial flap are retained to improve vestibular cosmesis and prevent perineoclitoral hair; a small dorsal urethral spongiomucosal flap is added between the neoclitoris and urethral neomeatus. Giraldo's 2.5–3.0 cm NVB pedicle includes Buck's fascia but no tunica albuginea, and the authors advise against simultaneous anterior vulvar W-commissuroplasty.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> **Rationale.** Giraldo et al. designed paired dorsolateral coronal flaps to retain tissue supplied by intraglandular branches of the dorsal penile nerves; their series did not establish a sensory advantage over a dorsal glans flap.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### D. Mons veneris (Soli / Bologna)

A strip of tunica albuginea carrying the dorsal NVB and a small glans portion is bent on itself and fixed in the suprapubic area, creating both mons veneris and neoclitoris. Saves 30–45 min versus standard. n = 15: 100% preserved neoclitoral trophism / sensitivity; 47% reported some form of climax; 73% satisfied with mons appearance; short transient postoperative hypersensitivity.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

### E. Butterfly flap (Balik) — for circumcised patients

Superior-180° NVB-supplied butterfly flap creates the sensitive inner surface of the labia minora; most of the urethra is used as a mesh graft for additional coverage. In 19 primary cases, 14/19 (74%) reported erogenous labia-minora sensation distinct from penile-body touch; the 10 patients who attended examination underwent Semmes–Weinstein testing during the first postoperative year.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

### F. Urethral-flap clitoroplasty (Brassard / GRS Montréal)

**Indications and rationale.** Fascelli et al. use the urethral-flap variant when available skin is insufficient for their preputial-skin technique, including in circumcised patients.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Particularly relevant in populations with high circumcision rates (North America, Middle East, parts of Africa and Southeast Asia) where most patients presenting for vaginoplasty may be circumcised.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

**Anatomic basis.** The technique leverages two structures available regardless of circumcision status:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[25]](#ref25)[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

- **Penile urethra and corpus spongiosum.** The male urethra is shortened from approximately 20 cm to a female-appropriate approximately 4 cm during PIV, with excess tissue typically discarded; the Brassard variant repurposes that excess urethra and surrounding spongiosum to create a well-vascularised mucosal clitoral hood instead of discarding them.
- **Dorsal NVB pedicle.** Both variants retain a dorsal-NVB-pedicled glans neoclitoris, but the urethral-flap design retains less coronal tissue; equivalent sensory outcomes have not been demonstrated.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

**Step-by-step (deviations from canonical Ghent technique highlighted):**

1. **Penile disassembly and NVB dissection.** Identical to canonical (dorsal NVB elevated with a strip of tunica albuginea).
2. **Glans reduction and neoclitoris formation.** Similar to canonical but the neoclitoris–urethra relationship is configured differently.
3. **Urethral preparation.** This step distinguishes the technique:
   - **More corpus spongiosum is preserved** than in the canonical technique.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
   - A dorsal urethrotomy is performed (urethra opened along its dorsal surface).
   - This creates a urethral flap that can be draped over and around the neoclitoris to form the hood.
4. **Neoclitoris inset.** Neoclitoris on its NVB pedicle inset following the dorsal urethrotomy; a small collar of preputial skin (whatever residual tissue remains) is sewn to the spongiosum and urethral mucosa, bridging mucosal-to-skin transition.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
5. **Urethral transection and urethroplasty.** Transect the urethra approximately 1 cm distal to the hood construction and spatulate the ventral bulbar urethra inferiorly until the neomeatus has a straight course toward the sphincter. Preserve spongiosum needed for the hood while reducing excess tissue beneath the neomeatus that could cause an anterior urinary stream or an engorging introital bulge.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[33]](#ref33)</sup>
6. **NVB folding and fixation.** Identical to canonical (suprapubic fixation at adductor-longus-tendon level).

**Do not confuse with the Perovic 2000 urethral-flap *vaginoplasty*.** That is a distinct operation that uses a long vascularised urethral flap to line the deep neovaginal canal (not the clitoral hood); the urethral flap is embedded into the inverted penile-skin tube and the resulting neovagina is fixed to the sacrospinous ligament. Perovic n = 89: good cosmetic and functional results in 87% (77/89); one rectovaginal fistula; mean follow-up 4.6 yr.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

**Trieste Y-shaped urethral-flap modification (Ongaro 2021).** The Trieste group (Ongaro, Garaffa, Liguori) uses a Y-shaped urethral flap as part of a modified vaginoplasty technique. n = 47 since 2014; 88.9% (40/45) reached climax at 12 mo; 75.6% (34/45) having neovaginal intercourse; median depth 11 cm (IQR 9–13.25) with no significant decrease at follow-up; only 1/45 dissatisfied with aesthetics; no severe (Clavien–Dindo ≥ III) complications.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

**Spongiosum pedicle versus dorsal-NVB pedicle: historical context.** The Brassard technique should be distinguished from the earlier spongiosum-pedicled clitoroplasty (Rubin 1980) which used the corpus spongiosum as the *vascular pedicle for the neoclitoris itself*. Fang et al. identified urine leakage as a complication of Rubin's spongiosum-pedicled approach but did not report its frequency or establish its mechanism.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> Fang 1992 (variant G above) switched to the dorsal NVB pedicle; no urine leakage occurred among the nine patients in that series, while the authors reported preserved sensation.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> All 9 neoclitorides survived with preserved light-touch and sexual sensation and no urine leakage.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> The Brassard urethral-flap technique is therefore a hybrid: neoclitoris on the dorsal-NVB pedicle (Fang-style) with the surrounding hood constructed from urethral / spongiosal tissue (leveraging the Rubin concept for the hood, not the pedicle).

**Trade-offs versus canonical (Fascelli / Dugi OHSU framework):**<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- **Less coronal tissue volume** for erogenous sensation (Fascelli / Dugi explicitly prefer canonical for this reason).
- **Potentially inferior aesthetics** (same group prefers canonical for "better esthetics").
- **Increased urethral manipulation.** The urethral-flap variant adds dorsal urethrotomy and retains more corpus spongiosum. The cited cohorts do not establish whether this changes meatal-stenosis, stricture, or stream-direction risk compared with preputial-skin construction.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[27]](#ref27)[[28]](#ref28)</sup>
- **Skin allocation.** A dorsal urethral flap can reserve penile shaft skin for the introitus and may reduce grafting, which Fascelli et al. identify as an introital-stenosis risk. If skin cannot reach without excessive tension, Peters et al. prefer grafting because tension raises wound-breakdown and stenosis risk.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[33]](#ref33)</sup>
- **Identical sensory foundation.** The neoclitoris remains on the dorsal NVB.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Urethral-flap healing.** Peters et al. describe venous congestion and raw flap edges that can fuse over the neoclitoris; examine for adhesions during follow-up and release them if present.<sup>[[33]](#ref33)</sup>

**Urinary outcomes.** In the MtF surgical studies reviewed by Nassiri, meatal stenosis rates range 4–40% and usually require meatotomy.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup> **Misdirected urinary stream**: rates were 9.5–33% in surgeon-reported cohorts versus 33–55% in other reports not from the primary surgeon in the Blasdel 2024 "surgical complication blind spots" analysis.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup> In a retrospective before-and-after comparison (64 patients with labia minora and clitoral hood creation versus 103 without), Raigosa 2020 reported lower neomeatal stenosis (1.5% versus 15.5%, p = .003), lower hemorrhage (12.5% versus 31%, p = .006) and fewer aesthetic refinements (4.6% versus 20.3%, p = .004) with labia-minora and clitoral-hood creation.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup> Wound dehiscence was higher in the labia-minora group (20.3% versus 9.7%, p = .05).<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup> This is the closest existing evidence on how periclitoral construction technique affects urethral outcomes. Raigosa compared PIV with and without simultaneous labia-minora and hood construction; that comparison cannot estimate meatal-stenosis risk for urethral-flap versus preputial-skin clitoroplasty.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

**Evidence-base caveat.** The Brassard urethral-flap technique is attributed to Pierre Brassard (GRS Montréal) but has no dedicated published outcomes series under his name. The most detailed description is the Fascelli / Dugi 2024 anatomy-guided framework, which presents it as one of two options while explicitly preferring the canonical preputial-skin technique. Sensory outcomes specific to the urethral-flap variant have not been separately reported. All available sensory data come from mixed series using the dorsal-NVB-pedicled glans flap regardless of hood-construction method.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

### G. Classic dorsal-glans chip (Fang 1992)

Historical baseline; a dorsal chip of glans on the NVB pedicle. Simple design; Giraldo’s later paired coronal flaps retain more coronal tissue, but comparative sensory outcomes were not established.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### H. Dorsolateral glans design (Bluebond-Langner / Zhao)

A 1.5-cm dorsolateral glans edge is used for the clitoris; the lateral glans wings are deepithelialized to preserve the dorsal nerves, and ventromedial glans tissue is excised. The neurovascular bundle is anchored to abdominal-wall fascia at the level of the adductor longus tendon. This design has been described by Bluebond-Langner and Zhao; no comparative sensory or functional outcomes have been reported for it.<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup>

## Postoperative Neoclitoral Anatomy

Relevant to gynecologists providing ongoing care:<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

- **Neoclitoris.** Reduced glans penis at the anterior commissure, analogous to the cisgender clitoris.
- **Dorsal NVB.** Folded and buried suprapubically; may be palpable as a cord beneath the mons pubis.
- **Clitoral hood.** Preputial skin or urethral tissue, draping the neoclitoris.
- **Labia minora.** Preputial skin or shaft skin, extending inferiorly.
- **No crura or vestibular bulbs.** The neoclitoris is a surface structure without deep erectile extensions.
- **Variable residual erectile capacity** from preserved spongiosum.
- In a six-patient pilot study, electrical perceptual thresholds were lower at the neoclitoris than at neovaginal or anal sites; this measure does not establish the relative contribution of each site to orgasm.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

**Cross-study findings:**

- Sensory recovery yields high orgasmic ability, largely through the neoclitoris, plus a neovagina with vibratory and pressure sensation similar to natal vagina.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- **SSEPs confirm intact large-fibre dorsal-column / medial-lemniscus pathway** after surgery, with the neoclitoris showing the lowest perceptual thresholds among genital sites.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- Genitoplasty patients appear to experience faster and more complete sensory recovery than other peripheral-nerve-regeneration scenarios; mechanisms are incompletely understood.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- No statistical correlation was found between objective pressure / vibratory thresholds and subjective neoclitoral satisfaction; perception is multifactorial.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Complications

| Complication | Incidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Partial clitoral necrosis / superficial flap skin necrosis | 2/103 (1.9%) partial clitoral necrosis in Raigosa; 8/97 (8.2%) superficial flap skin necrosis in Mañero | Mañero complications healed without sequelae<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[27]](#ref27)</sup> |
| Neoclitoral necrosis | No deep or total flap necrosis in Mañero (n = 97); neoclitoral necrosis in 2/10 in the early Rehman series | Rehman reported unsatisfactory outcomes in the two patients with necrosis<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[17]](#ref17)</sup> |
| Clitoral hypersensitivity | Common early | Usually transient<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Clitoral sensitivity at 12 mo | 96% reported clitoral sensitivity | Mañero 2022: 84 primary surgeries across three vaginoplasty techniques<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| Wound dehiscence | 9.7–20.3% | Higher in the cohort with labia-minora creation<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup> |
| Hemorrhage surrounding urethra and labia | 12.5% (8/64) with labia-minora/hood creation; 31% (32/103) without | Lower in the group with labia-minora/hood creation<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup> |
| Buried / retracted clitoris | Common revision indication | Repositioning surgery<sup>[[20]](#ref20)[[21]](#ref21)</sup> |
| Excessive clitoral prominence | Common revision indication | Reduction / repositioning |
| Inadequate hooding | Common revision indication | Hood reconstruction |
| Diverted urinary stream | 5.6% (Ghent, late revision for diverted stream) | Clitoro-urethral-complex-related<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |

**Two-stage caveat.** In a retrospective comparison of complete PIV pathways, 3/62 patients (4.8%) in the two-stage group lacked neoclitoral sensitivity because of necrosis, versus 0/63 in the single-stage group. The comparison does not isolate the effect of clitoroplasty timing; see the [PIV page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/penile-inversion-vaginoplasty) single-stage-versus-two-stage section.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup>

### Revision clitoroplasty

Clitoral revision is the second most common cosmetic revision after labiaplasty, at 23.3% of all cosmetic revisions in Mañero's series (354 primary vaginoplasties, plus 44 patients from other centers) and a clitoral concern in 57.1% of the 35 patients revised in the NYU series.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)[[21]](#ref21)</sup> Common indications include a buried or retracted clitoris, excessive prominence, inadequate hooding, and malposition. Mañero et al. assess clitoral presence, size, position, and hood coverage before revision; for a cranially malpositioned clitoris, they describe V-Y descent with careful dissection of its neurovascular pedicle.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> Boas 2019 (n = 117 primary PIV): 28/117 (23.9%) underwent revision labiaplasty and/or clitoroplasty; 9/117 (7.7%) had both; minor postoperative complications were more common among patients who underwent revision; among revision-group survey respondents, 82.4% reported satisfaction with their final surgical outcome and 76.5% reported resolution of genital-related dysphoria.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

## Impact of Circumcision Status

| Status | Tissue availability | Preferred technique |
|---|---|---|
| **Uncircumcised** | Full prepuce | Preputial-skin clitoroplasty (Ghent A) — preferred |
| **Circumcised with &gt; 2 cm inner prepuce and ≥ 8 cm proximal shaft** | Meets Fascelli's Methods selection criteria | Preputial-skin clitoroplasty (Ghent A)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Circumcised with insufficient skin** | Critical tissue deficit | Urethral-flap (Brassard F) or butterfly flap (Balik E) |

The Ghent series (n = 161, Opsomer 2018) reported that creation of labia minora and clitoral hood was achievable in all patients regardless of circumcision status, using distal shaft skin when prepuce was absent.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> In Balik’s 19-patient butterfly-flap series for circumcised patients, 14/19 reported erogenous labial sensation distinct from tactile sensation on the penile shaft; 10/19 who could attend follow-up underwent monofilament testing during the first year.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Electrophysiologic Evidence

Canale 2022 is a pilot study of six patients using SSEPs:<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

- SSEPs confirmed intact large-fibre dorsal-column / medial-lemniscus pathway after surgery.
- **Perceptual thresholds much lower at the neoclitoris** than at neovagina or anal sites.
- A trend toward correlation between clitoral SSEPs and self-perceived orgasmic intensity (Orgasmometer) at the clitoral level, but not at vaginal or anal levels.
- These findings support preservation of a large-fibre afferent pathway after surgery; the six-patient pilot cannot establish the relative contribution of the neoclitoris to orgasm.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

## Evidence Limitations

Retrospective single-centre case series with heterogeneous techniques, variable follow-up, and non-standardised outcome measures.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)[[24]](#ref24)</sup> No RCTs compare clitoroplasty variants. Sensory outcomes use heterogeneous instruments (Semmes–Weinstein monofilaments, Bio-Thesiometer, SSEPs, self-report) that don't permit clean cross-study comparison.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[19]](#ref19)</sup> The SSEP study included only 6 patients.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> Long-term sensory outcomes beyond 5 years are poorly characterised. Patient-reported outcomes in the series above include 7/15 (47%) reporting some form of climax during intercourse in Soli at 3–20 months and 86% of 22 Sigurjónsson follow-up participants reporting orgasm after surgery at a mean 37 months; these endpoints are not directly comparable.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)[[19]](#ref19)</sup> Procedure-specific instruments now include the Italian oMtFSFI and English SatisFunction survey.<sup>[[34]](#ref34)[[35]](#ref35)</sup> The long-term impact of circumcision status has not been formally compared in a controlled study.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
