# Neovaginal Stenosis Management — Stepwise

**Neovaginal stenosis** is the most clinically significant long-term challenge after feminizing genital surgery. The De Rosa 2024 systematic review of primary vaginoplasty (59 studies, 7,338 patients) reports an overall vaginal-stenosis incidence of 5.83% (range 0–34.2%) and a cumulative 9.68% when introital stenosis and contracture are included.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> A narrative review cites rates of up to 30% in some series, and stenosis or shortening of the canal is the most common functional reason for revision vaginoplasty.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> In the Levy series of 240 penile inversion vaginoplasties, non-compliance with dilation and activity restriction was the only factor independently associated with complications or reoperation.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

This is the dedicated management-pathway page. For the host operations, see [Penile Inversion Vaginoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/penile-inversion-vaginoplasty), [Peritoneal Pull-Through Vaginoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/peritoneal-pull-through-vaginoplasty), and [Intestinal Vaginoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/intestinal-vaginoplasty). For the periurethral / periclitoral architecture that drives introital outcomes, see [Feminizing Labiaplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/feminizing-labiaplasty) and [Feminizing Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/feminizing-urethroplasty).

## Definitions and Classification

A limitation is the lack of a standardised definition.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- **Vaginal stenosis (VS):** narrowing or shortening of the canal (mid-canal or apical).
- **Introital stenosis (IS):** narrowing at the mucocutaneous junction between vulva and canal.
- **Vaginal contracture:** complete or near-complete obliteration.
- **Loss of depth:** progressive shortening without narrowing.

**By location:**<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- **Introital (distal).** Perineal suture-line scar contracture; most amenable to office-based or minor surgical intervention.
- **Mid-canal.** Skin-graft contracture, circumferential scarring, granulation tissue.
- **Apical (proximal).** Inadequate initial depth, graft necrosis, apex scar contracture.
- **Complete obliteration.** Total closure; requires major revision.

**By severity:**<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

- **Mild.** Responds to increased dilation frequency and/or smaller dilators.
- **Moderate.** Requires procedural intervention (EUA dilation, scar lysis).
- **Severe or complete.** Requires major revision vaginoplasty.

## Incidence by Surgical Technique

| Technique | Vaginal stenosis | Introital stenosis | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Penile inversion vaginoplasty (PIV) — overall | **5.70%** | 3.13% | De Rosa SR — most data<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| PIV with skin-graft augmentation | 1.2–12% | — | Neovaginal necrosis 0–22.8%<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| PIV with scrotal flap | 0–6.3% | — | Limited data<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Primary intestinal vaginoplasty | **0.20%** | 4.7% | Self-lubricating mucosa<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Peritoneal-flap vaginoplasty | Low (limited data) | — | Emerging technique<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Vaginoplasty (OHSU, n = 200) | 4.0% | 1.5% | Single surgeon<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| PIV (Levy n = 240) | 2.1% requiring reoperation | — | Median follow-up 87 days<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |

The canal-stenosis rate was 0.20% in primary intestinal vaginoplasty versus 5.70% in PIV, a descriptive comparison across studies. Introital-stenosis rates were similar (PIV 3.13% versus intestinal 4.7%); the review authors suggest that multiple suture lines and dilation through the anastomosis may explain this.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Risk Factors

### Dilation non-compliance — the dominant risk factor

**Non-compliance is the only factor consistently and independently associated with stenosis and reoperation:**<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

- **Levy 2019 (n = 240).** Non-compliance with dilation and activity restrictions was the only factor significantly associated with increased complications or reoperation on multivariate analysis.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Permanent stenosis** is framed as a direct complication of not dilating.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- The AAFP review advises increasing dilation frequency, with pelvic-floor PT, as first-line treatment.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

### Predictors of dilation difficulty (Shamamian 2025, n = 614)<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

Independent predictors on multivariate analysis:

- **Unemployment.** OR 2.740 (95% CI 1.587–4.732, p < .001)
- **HIV.** OR 2.588 (1.290–5.190, p = 0.007)
- **Psychiatric comorbidity besides gender dysphoria.** OR 1.606 (1.001–2.577, p = 0.049)
- **Primary peritoneal graft.** OR 3.202 (1.212–8.460, p = 0.019)

### Surgical / patient factors

- **Skin-graft use.** Neovaginal stenosis 1.2–12% when grafts augment PIV; precursor neovaginal necrosis 0–22.8%.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Granulation tissue.** In Boas 2019 (117 patients), those needing revision labiaplasty or clitoroplasty were more likely to have had granulation tissue (p = 0.006), intravaginal scarring (p < 0.001) and complete vaginal stenosis (p = 0.008).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Wound dehiscence or infection.** Disrupts neovaginal lining and drives scar contracture.
- **Insufficient initial depth.** Inadequate canal dissection or donor tissue.
- **BMI > 24.9, diabetes, smoking, cardiovascular disease.** Identified as significant risk factors for postoperative complications in the 2026 international Delphi consensus (27 surgeons).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Age, BMI, HRT duration.** Not associated with complications in Gaither 2018 (n = 330; median follow-up 3 months).<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **History of abuse.** Significantly higher preoperative pelvic-floor dysfunction (**91% versus 31%, p < 0.001**).<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

## Prevention — Dilation Protocols

### Standard schedule

The 2026 international Delphi consensus (27 surgeons) advised a dilation protocol of three daily 30-min sessions.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> ACOG Committee Opinion 823 (2021) states that recovery requires a regimen of up to three times per day.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

| Interval | Frequency |
|---|---|
| Weeks 1–4 (after packing / stent removal) | 3× daily, 15–30 min |
| Months 2–3 | 2–3× daily |
| Months 3–6 | 1–2× daily |
| Months 6–12 | 1× daily |
| > 12 months | Lifelong maintenance — several times per week, or as needed if regular vaginal intercourse |

Anchors: AAFP<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>, ACOG<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>, Delphi<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>.

### Dilator sizing

Begin smaller; gradually increase. For struggling patients, ACOG advises increasing lubricant and downsizing dilators to enable more frequent or deeper dilation, then re-escalating size.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> A standardised 13 cm dilator set is commonly used.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

### The patient-reported dilation experience (Gomez 2026: prospective survey, 17 of 27 patients completing all time points)<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

- Mean dilation: 6.6 ± 1.4 days/week, 2.4 ± 0.9 times/day.
- Session duration: 38.3 ± 16 min.
- Mean depth achieved: 4.05 ± 1.32 on a standardised 13 cm dilator set, with no significant decline over time.
- All 17 patients reported at least one dilation difficulty.
- Most common challenges: tightness 71%, bleeding 71%, pain 65%, discharge 47%, logistical barriers 24%.
- Challenges declined significantly after 6 months (p < 0.05).

### Pelvic-floor physical therapy (PFPT)

**Jiang / OHSU program (n = 77)**:<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

- 94% attended PFPT at least once.
- Preoperative screening identified 42% with pelvic-floor dysfunction and 37% with bowel dysfunction.
- Among patients with dysfunction preoperatively, resolution by the first postoperative visit was 69% for pelvic-floor and 73% for bowel dysfunction.
- Pre- and postoperative PFPT versus postoperative-only: significantly lower postoperative pelvic-floor dysfunction (28% versus 86%, p = 0.006).
- Successful dilation at 3 months: 89%.

**FLOWER Trial (RCT, n = 41; the only RCT in this space):**<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

- Randomised to PFPT at 3 and 6 wk postoperatively versus no PFPT; 37 of 41 enrolled patients (20 PFPT, 17 no PFPT) had 12-week data.
- At 12 wk: median vaginal length 12.5 cm, ease of dilation 7.3/10, pain with dilation 2.4/10.
- **No significant differences** in length, ease, pain, or pelvic-floor symptoms between groups.
- The authors concluded that routine postoperative PFPT did not improve outcomes. The trial was small and did not test subgroups such as patients with preoperative pelvic-floor dysfunction.

**Manrique 2019 (n = 40):**<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

- High preoperative pelvic-floor dysfunction.
- PFPT reduced symptom severity (p < 0.01).

The observational programs (Jiang, Manrique) support pre- and postoperative PFPT, whereas the one randomised trial (FLOWER) found no benefit from postoperative-only PFPT; evidence is insufficient to determine which patients benefit.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[17]](#ref17)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

## Stepwise Management Algorithm

### Step 1 — Conservative (first-line)

For mild stenosis or early loss of depth / caliber:<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

- **Increase dilation frequency.** The single most important intervention.
- **Downsize dilator.** Smaller dilator, gradual re-escalation.
- **Generous lubricant.** Water- or silicone-based.
- **Pelvic-floor PT referral.** Especially for pain, tightness, difficulty relaxing.
- **Treat contributing factors.** Granulation tissue (see below), infection, hair regrowth.
- **Persistent pain or dilation difficulty.** Refer to surgeon.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

### Step 2 — Office-based procedures

For moderate stenosis not responding to conservative measures:

- **Dilation under anaesthesia (EUA).** Examination and serial dilation; may be combined with scar lysis.
- **Scar lysis or incision.** Sharp or electrocautery incision of circumferential scar bands.
- **Granulation treatment.** Silver nitrate cautery ± betamethasone suppositories (see Granulation section).
- **Introital revision.** Local tissue rearrangement, Z-plasty, or V-flap advancement for isolated introital stenosis.

### Step 3 — Surgical revision for moderate stenosis

For moderate stenosis with preserved canal space but inadequate dimensions:

- **Full-thickness skin-graft (FTSG) revision vaginoplasty (van der Sluis 2016 comparative)**:<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
  - Perineal approach; OR 131 ± 35 min.
  - Successful reconstruction in 81% (26/32).
  - Mean neovaginal depth 12.5 ± 2.8 cm.
  - **Rectal perforation 19%.** Higher than intestinal approach.
  - Donor sites: groin, inner thigh, lower abdomen.
- **Robotic peritoneal-flap revision vaginoplasty (Dy 2021 NYU; Celis 2026 step-by-step):**<sup>[[20]](#ref20)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
  - n = 24; all had prior PIV; revision at median 35.3 months after primary; mean follow-up 410 days.
  - Flaps from posterior bladder and pararectal fossa, advanced to stenosed-cavity edges.
  - **Depth 13.6 cm** (range 10.9–14.5); width 3.6 cm.
  - **No intraoperative complications** from peritoneal-flap harvest; no rectal injuries.
  - 1 return-to-OR for canal bleeding.
  - OR time 5 h initially and 3 h in the Celis step-by-step technique paper.
  - Discharge within first week; serial vaginoscopies for healing assessment.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### Step 4 — Severe stenosis / complete obliteration

- **Laparoscopic intestinal (sigmoid) revision vaginoplasty (van der Sluis 2016):**<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
  - Successful reconstruction in 91% (19/21).
  - Mean depth 15.9 ± 1.4 cm, significantly deeper than FTSG (p < 0.01).
  - OR 191 ± 45 min; rectal perforation 10%.
- **Biological-graft (UBM, urinary bladder matrix) reconstruction (Gupta 2019 case report):**<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
  - Novel approach for recurrent introital stenosis with granulation tissue.
  - UBM acts as scaffold for smooth-muscle and matrix regeneration.
  - Successful reconstruction of neovaginal introitus after failed prior revisions.
  - Improvement in comfort, hygiene, and QoL.

| Technique | Success | Depth | OR time | Rectal perforation | Key advantage | Key disadvantage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **FTSG revision** | 81% | 12.5 ± 2.8 cm | 131 ± 35 min | **19%** | Shorter OR; perineal | Less depth; higher rectal-injury rate; requires dilation<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |
| **Laparoscopic intestinal revision** | **91%** | **15.9 ± 1.4 cm** | 191 ± 45 min | 10% | Greater depth; self-lubricating | Longer OR; bowel anastomosis; mucorrhea<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |
| **Robotic peritoneal-flap revision** | Not defined (24 patients, Dy 2021) | 13.6 cm | 180–300 min | **0%** | No rectal injury; no bowel anastomosis; minimal donor-site morbidity | Robotic platform; limited long-term data<sup>[[20]](#ref20)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Biological graft (UBM)** | Case report | — | — | — | Scaffold for recurrent introital stenosis | Single case<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> |

## Granulation Tissue — Key Precursor to Stenosis

Granulation tissue (hypergranulation) is a common minor postoperative finding and is associated with later revision surgery and with complete vaginal stenosis (Boas 2019).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[11]](#ref11)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

**Timing.** In Gaither 2018 (n = 330), the median time to any complication was 4.4 months and most complications developed within the first 4 months.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

**Management:**<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

- **Silver nitrate cautery.** First-line; via speculum; often repeated.
- **Betamethasone suppositories and silver nitrate.** Gharavi 2026 retrospective matched cohort (16 per arm): adding betamethasone significantly improved resolution (p = 0.035 after excluding cases requiring anaesthetic cautery). Moderate and severe hypergranulation resolved more often in the betamethasone group (86% / 50%) than silver-nitrate-alone (60% / 25%), with fewer median treatments (2 versus 3.5 moderate; 4 versus 5 severe).<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- **Cauterisation under anaesthesia.** Severe or refractory.
- **Routine monitoring.** Yearly or biennial pelvic exam.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Introital Stenosis — Specific Considerations

Introital stenosis occurs at a similar rate across PIV (3.13%) and intestinal vaginoplasty (4.7%), confirming that the introital suture line, common to all techniques, is the primary driver.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Pathophysiology:** circumferential scarring at the mucocutaneous junction; recurrent granulation tissue at the introitus (reported after colonic vaginoplasty<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>); suture-line tension; inadequate primary introital caliber.

**Management:**

- **Conservative.** Increased dilation focused on introital caliber.
- **Office-based.** Scar lysis, Z-plasty, V-flap meatoplasty.
- **Surgical.** Local tissue rearrangement, posterior web release, biological graft (UBM) for recurrent cases.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- **The Raigosa refinement (n = 167) demonstrates that incorporating labia-minora and clitoral-hood creation significantly reduces introital complications.** Meatal stenosis fell from 15.5% to 1.5% (p = 0.003), hemorrhage from 31% to 12.5% (p = 0.006), and aesthetic revision from 20.3% to 4.6% (p = 0.004); see the [Feminizing Labiaplasty page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/feminizing-labiaplasty) for the full dataset.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

## Special Populations

**Peritoneal-flap vaginoplasty patients.** Higher odds of dilation difficulty with primary peritoneal vaginoplasty than with other techniques (Shamamian OR 3.20, 95% CI 1.21–8.46, p = 0.019). Peritoneum may be more prone to contracture and requires more rigorous dilation adherence.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

**Intestinal vaginoplasty patients.** Canal stenosis was 0.20% in the De Rosa review of primary vaginoplasty, but the technique carries its own complications that contribute to revision (Robinson 2023 SR):<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

- **Stenosis** (any location) was the most common complication (11% of 654 vaginoplasties).
- Mucorrhea 7%, prolapse 6%, malodor 5%.
- **Diversion colitis** can present as neovaginal bleeding years later.
- Average return-to-OR rate 18%; 6 segments developed vascular compromise; 2 reported mortalities.

**Adolescents or post-puberty-suppression cohorts.** Limited donor tissue may necessitate alternative canal lining for the primary procedure, with downstream stenosis-risk implications. The Delphi consensus advises comorbidity-based assessment for each patient.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Long-Term Surveillance

AAFP review and ACOG advice:<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

- **Lifelong dilation.** Permanent stenosis is the complication of not dilating.
- **Pelvic examinations** yearly or every other year for stenosis, granulation tissue, hair regrowth.
- Canal should accommodate a Pederson speculum; an anoscope is a more slender alternative for narrower canals.
- New vaginal symptoms: pelvic examination.
- Persistent pain or dilation difficulty: surgical referral.
- For peritoneum- or colon-lined neovaginas, vaginal bleeding should prompt biopsy to evaluate for bowel pathology (adenocarcinoma, IBD); see the [Intestinal Vaginoplasty page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/intestinal-vaginoplasty) neoplasia and diversion-colitis subsections.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Evidence Limitations

- **No standardised definition** of vaginal stenosis: studies variably use symptoms, exam findings, dilator size, or need for intervention; cross-study comparisons unreliable.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **No validated grading system** for severity.
- **No comparative trials** between revision techniques (FTSG versus intestinal versus peritoneal flap).
- **No standardised dilation protocols.** Schedules are expert opinion, not comparative evidence.
- **Long-term outcomes** of revision beyond 3–5 yr poorly characterised.
- **The FLOWER RCT is the only randomised trial** of PFPT identified; it found no benefit for routine postoperative PFPT, in contrast to the observational data.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- **PROMs are underrepresented.** Gomez 2026, a prospective survey of 17 completers, describes the dilation experience.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- The 2026 international Delphi consensus reached agreement on 63 of 68 statements; no consensus was reached on the timing of hormone therapy interruption or re-initiation.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
