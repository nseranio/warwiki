# Skin Graft Vaginoplasty (FTSG / STSG Canal Augmentation)

**Skin graft vaginoplasty** uses a **full-thickness (FTSG)** or **split-thickness (STSG)** skin graft to **augment or replace penile skin** as neovaginal canal lining when there is insufficient genital skin to achieve adequate depth during penile inversion vaginoplasty (PIV).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> It is the **most common adjunct to PIV** — required in **85.7%** of the 15-year Ghent series (n = 384) to reach the 14 cm depth target<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> — and also serves as a **standalone revision technique** after failed primary vaginoplasty (van der Sluis 2016 perineal FTSG: 81% success, 12.5 cm depth, 19% rectal perforation).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

This is the dedicated atlas page. For the host operation, see [Penile Inversion Vaginoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/penile-inversion-vaginoplasty). For the principal alternatives when donor tissue is insufficient, see [Peritoneal Pull-Through Vaginoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/peritoneal-pull-through-vaginoplasty) and [Intestinal Vaginoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/intestinal-vaginoplasty). For stenosis-management context, see [Neovaginal Stenosis Management](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/neovaginal-stenosis-management).

---

## Indications

Skin-graft augmentation is indicated when penile skin alone cannot provide adequate neovaginal depth. Risk factors for insufficient genital skin:<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- **Prior circumcision** — the most common reason for needing supplemental graft.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Prior orchiectomy** — **3× greater odds** of needing extragenital skin graft (Sineath 2022).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Stretched penile skin length < 10 cm**.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Pubertal suppression** — early hormone-blockade-driven genital hypoplasia.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Lichen sclerosus** or other conditions affecting genital-skin quality.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Decision threshold:** the option to add a graft is typically offered when penile skin length falls between **7 and 12 cm**.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> In the Ghent 15-year series, **85.7%** required FTSG to reach 14 cm.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Graft Types and Donor Sites

| Graft type | Donor site | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Full-thickness skin graft (FTSG)** — most commonly used | **Scrotal skin** (preferred — hairless after electrolysis, readily available); abdominal / lower-abdomen / groin | Scrotal FTSG: hairless canal, no vaginal-wall-prolapse risk<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| **Split-thickness skin graft (STSG)** | Thigh, buttock | More common in modified Abbe–McIndoe technique for vaginal agenesis; also applicable in transgender vaginoplasty<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Extragenital FTSG** | Flank, iliac crest, thigh | When scrotal skin is unavailable / insufficient<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |

**Preoperative electrolysis of the donor site (especially scrotum) is essential** to prevent intravaginal hair growth.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

---

## Surgical Technique

1. **Donor-site harvest** — scrotal-skin FTSG most common; epilation completed ≥ 6 weeks preoperatively.
2. **Graft preparation** — defat (FTSG); fenestrate as needed.
3. **Anastomosis to inverted penile skin tube** — graft sutured to the distal end of the inverted penile skin tube, extending the canal lining.
4. **Inversion into the canal** — composite tube (penile skin + graft) inverted into the dissected rectoprostatic space.
5. **Stent / mold** placed to maintain canal during initial healing.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

## Outcomes

| Outcome | Skin-graft vaginoplasty | Comparator |
|---|---|---|
| Intraoperative neovaginal depth | ~13.8 cm (with or without graft) | — |
| 1-year depth | ~11.5 cm (15% decline, mostly in first 3 wk) | — |
| Revision FTSG depth | **12.5 ± 2.8 cm** | Intestinal revision 15.9 ± 1.4 cm (p < 0.01)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Penetrative intercourse (Ghent 15-yr) | **97.2%** | — |
| Orgasm (Ghent 15-yr) | **83.4%** | — |
| Aesthetic / functional satisfaction | **No significant difference with vs without graft** (Buncamper 2017) | — |

Anchors: Buncamper 2017<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>, Opsomer Ghent 15-yr<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>, van der Sluis 2016 revision comparison<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>.

## Complications

Across a Salibian 2021 systematic review of 1,622 patients receiving skin grafts as adjuncts to PIV:<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

| Complication | Incidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Neovaginal stenosis | **1.2–12%** | Most common; dilation intensification or revision |
| Neovaginal necrosis (partial / complete) | **0–22.8%** | Significant concern unique to grafted tissue<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Lubrication satisfaction | Low | Skin-lined canals do not self-lubricate<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Rectal perforation — primary | 1.6% | Ghent series<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| Rectal perforation — revision FTSG | **19%** | vs 10% for intestinal revision<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Donor-site morbidity | Minimal | Scarring possible<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Late revision surgery | **37.1%** | Diabetes the only independent risk factor (Ghent)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |

## Graft vs No Graft — The Buncamper 2017 Controversy

Prospective Amsterdam study (n = 100; 32 with vs 68 without additional FTSG):<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

- **No significant difference** in neovaginal shrinkage, aesthetic outcome, sexual function, or genital self-image.
- The addition of a skin graft does **not worsen** outcomes through contraction or insensitivity.
- It also does **not clearly improve** aesthetic results by preserving more penile skin for vulvar construction.
- This contradicts **both sides** of the longstanding debate — argues against doctrinaire stances either for or against routine graft use.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Skin Graft vs Alternative Revision Techniques

van der Sluis 2016 head-to-head revision comparison (n = 53):<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

| Outcome | Perineal FTSG revision (n = 32) | Laparoscopic intestinal revision (n = 21) |
|---|---|---|
| Success | 81% | **91%** |
| Depth | 12.5 ± 2.8 cm | **15.9 ± 1.4 cm** (p < 0.01) |
| OR time | **131 ± 35 min** | 191 ± 45 min |
| Rectal perforation | **19%** | 10% |

FTSG revision is **shortest OR time** but has lower success, less depth, and highest rectal-perforation rate. For revision technique selection see the [Neovaginal Stenosis Management](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/neovaginal-stenosis-management) page (Step 3 framework: peritoneal-flap first-line, sigmoid for complete obliteration, FTSG when shortest OR is the priority).

## Postoperative Care

- **Lifelong vaginal dilation** — up to 3× daily initially, tapering over months. Failure to dilate → stenosis and depth loss.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **External lubricant always required** — the skin-lined canal does not self-lubricate.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Pelvic-floor PT** for persistent pain or dilation difficulty.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

See the [PIV page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/penile-inversion-vaginoplasty) dilation-protocol table for the full schedule and the [Neovaginal Stenosis Management](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/neovaginal-stenosis-management) page for the PFPT framework (Jiang OHSU pre + post 28% vs 86%, FLOWER RCT no-routine-benefit signal, Shamamian dilation-difficulty predictors).

---

## Evidence Limitations

- **Salibian 2021 SR** of 1,622 graft-augmented PIV patients is the largest aggregate; component series are heterogeneous in graft technique, donor site, and outcome definitions.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Buncamper 2017 (n = 100)** is the only direct graft-vs-no-graft comparison and found no significant difference in outcomes.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **van der Sluis 2016 (n = 53)** is the only head-to-head FTSG-vs-intestinal revision comparison.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **No standardised outcome definitions** for "neovaginal necrosis" — wide 0–22.8% range reflects definitional heterogeneity.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Long-term outcomes** beyond 5 yr poorly characterised across most graft series.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
