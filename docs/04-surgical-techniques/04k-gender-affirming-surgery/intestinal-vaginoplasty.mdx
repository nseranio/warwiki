# Intestinal Vaginoplasty (Sigmoid / Ileum / Right Colon)

**Intestinal vaginoplasty** uses a pedicled segment of bowel, most often sigmoid colon and less often ileum or right colon, to construct a mucus-secreting neovaginal canal.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Squamous metaplasia was found in one postoperative biopsy in a 12-patient rectosigmoid series; that series does not quantify risk after transfeminine surgery.<sup>[[52]](#ref52)</sup> Reported neovaginal depths in recent series are 14.6–17.6 cm.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The operation is used as a primary procedure (55.5% of cases in the largest series) and as a revision or salvage procedure (44.5%) after failed penile inversion vaginoplasty (PIV).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> A recent single-centre series (Lava 2025; 119 patients, median follow-up 9.3 months) reported short-term complications in 17.7%, complications beginning at 30 days or later in 24.4%, and reoperation in 16.8%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> A systematic review of 654 intestinal vaginoplasties reported 215/654 (33%) complications; among studies reporting each outcome, 108/608 (18%) returned to the operating room and 2/69 deaths were reported.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

This is the dedicated atlas page. For the cohort-level decision framework, see [Feminizing Gender-Affirming Surgery](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/feminizing-surgery). For the standard skin-lined technique, see [Penile Inversion Vaginoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/penile-inversion-vaginoplasty). For the principal alternative for insufficient donor tissue, see [Peritoneal Pull-Through Vaginoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/peritoneal-pull-through-vaginoplasty).

## Historical Context and Technical Evolution

Intestinal vaginoplasty was first described in cisgender vaginal-agenesis cohorts in the early 20th century and adapted to transfeminine GAS in the 1970s–80s via open laparotomy.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Milestones:

- **Open abdominoperineal era.** Large incisions, prolonged hospitalisation, significant morbidity.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Total laparoscopic sigmoid vaginoplasty (Amsterdam / VUmc, 2008–present).** Bouman, Meijerink, and van der Sluis matured the minimally invasive approach (42 primary and 21 secondary procedures with no conversions to laparotomy).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **3D laparoscopic technique (Kim 2021).** Initial intraoperative mean depth was 15.2 cm in n = 17.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Robot-assisted (Kim 2026; Sljivich 2025).** Kim n = 12 mean depth 14.6 cm; Sljivich n = 36 (25 revisions) mean depth 17.6 cm with LOS 3.7 d.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **ICG fluorescence-guided perfusion assessment.** Flor-Lorente 2021 and van der Sluis 2019 use indocyanine green fluorescent angiography to confirm perfusion of the pedicled sigmoid; in the original series of six patients, one had inadequate vascularisation and the reconstruction was aborted.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

| **Revision / salvage** | Failed primary PIV with complete stenosis, inadequate depth, or canal loss; failed peritoneal vaginoplasty | Lava 2025<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>; Robinson SR<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>; van der Sluis 2019<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |

- **Lava 2025.** 44.5% of 119 patients were revision cases, with complication rates similar between primary and revision cohorts.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgical Technique

Combined abdominoperineal operation, typically two-team:<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

**Perineal phase (reconstructive surgeon):**

1. Bilateral orchiectomy (if not previously done).
2. Penile disassembly: degloving, dorsal-NVB preservation with the glans for clitoroplasty, corpora cavernosa excision.
3. Urethral shortening and repositioning.
4. **Neovaginal cavity dissection.** Dissection between rectum and bladder/prostate, meeting the abdominal dissection.
5. Vulvar construction: clitoroplasty, clitoral hood, labia minora, labia majora.

**Abdominal phase (colorectal / laparoscopic surgeon):**

6. **Ports.** Laparoscopic (3–4) or robotic (da Vinci Xi).
7. **Sigmoid mobilisation.** Identify a 12–15 cm segment (up to 30 cm in some techniques).
8. **Vascular pedicle.** Based on sigmoid arteries (IMA branches); ICG angiography confirms perfusion.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> If the segment will not reach the perineum without tension, transecting the IMA proximal to the first sigmoid branch has been described as a historical maneuver to gain immediate mobility, relying on retrograde supply from the middle and inferior rectal arterial systems. In the original mixed series of 13 patients (2 for gender dysphoria, 10 for Müllerian dysgenesis, 1 true hermaphrodite; follow-up 1–19 years), 12 reported a satisfactory result and no vaginal loss was attributed to ischemia. This small, mostly non-GAS series does not establish the safety of IMA ligation for a contemporary gender-affirming cohort and does not substitute for direct perfusion assessment.<sup>[[47]](#ref47)</sup>
9. **Segment isolation.** Endoscopic staplers at both ends; vascular pedicle preserved.
10. **Orientation.** Isoperistaltic (Amsterdam technique) or antiperistaltic after 180° rotation of the segment (Flor-Lorente technique).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
11. **Colovestibular anastomosis.** Distal staple line opened and interdigitated to perineal skin / inverted penile skin.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
12. **Length tailoring.** Measured with transilluminated dildo / conformer; stapled at the apex.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
13. **Neovaginopexy.** Apex fixed to the sacral promontory to prevent prolapse.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
14. **Bowel continuity.** Intra-abdominal side-to-side oversewn stapled anastomosis.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
15. Vaginal stent / conformer placement.

## Choice of Bowel Segment

| Feature | **Sigmoid** | Ileum | Right colon (cecum) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diameter | Large (matches vaginal caliber) | Small (may be too narrow) | Large |
| Mucus production | Mucus-secreting<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | Mucus-secreting; comparative burden versus sigmoid is not established<sup>[[37]](#ref37)</sup> | Mucus-secreting<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| Mesenteric reach | Shorter | **Long** (long perineal reach) | Long (ileocolic) |
| Vascular pedicle | Reliable (sigmoid arteries) | SMA branches; variable | Reliable (ileocolic artery) |
| Reported stenosis (different endpoints and cohorts) | 2014 pooled review: 4.1% introital requiring surgery<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | 2014 pooled review: 1.2% introital requiring surgery; 2018 revision-heavy series: 4/32 (12.5%) any introital stenosis<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[37]](#ref37)</sup> | 2021 single series: 4/22 (18%) any stenosis (2 introital, 2 extrinsic)<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| Prolapse | 2.5% vaginal prolapse in pooled intestinal studies<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>; 11.8% mucosal prolapse in one sigmoid cohort<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Not established for ileum alone | **22.7% (5/22)**<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| Status | **Standard (primary + revision)** | Niche; predominantly **revision / salvage** (29/32, 90.6%, in a mixed transgender/DSD cohort)<sup>[[37]](#ref37)</sup> | Emerging alternative |

Sigmoid is canonical: adequate diameter, reliable pedicle, manageable mucus, perineal proximity.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Ileum has historically been disfavoured for excessive mucus production and small luminal caliber, but several technical modifications (Schneider J-pouch, Trombetta Monti retubularization, Ozkan free jejunal flap, Cho free jejunal graft) directly address these limitations; see the dedicated ileal-variants section below.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[37]](#ref37)[[38]](#ref38)[[39]](#ref39)[[40]](#ref40)</sup> Right-colon vaginoplasty has been described with a long ileocolic pedicle and an ileocolic anastomosis in the right upper quadrant; the Garcia technique includes appendectomy and a stapled neovaginal end.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> The only outcomes series to date (Garcia 2021; 22 women who had previously undergone primary vaginoplasty) reported neovaginal prolapse in 22.7% (5/22) and stenosis in 18% (4/22).<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

## Ileal Vaginoplasty — Variants, Outcomes, and Current Role

In the van der Sluis 2018 international multicentre ileal series, 29/32 (90.6%) procedures were revisions in a mixed cohort of 27 transgender women and five patients with DSD; indications included insufficient depth or complete neovaginal stenosis after prior vaginoplasty. Ileum may also be considered when sigmoid is unavailable (prior resection or pathology).<sup>[[37]](#ref37)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> The largest dedicated ileal series are Zhao YZ 2011 (n = 82, laparoscope-assisted pedicled ileum) and van der Sluis 2018 (n = 32 international multicentre).<sup>[[37]](#ref37)[[41]](#ref41)</sup>

### Rationale and resurgence

Ileum was among the earliest intestinal segments used (Baldwin loop, early 20th century). The ileal segment and its vascular pedicle can be mobilized for tension-free perineal transfer; sigmoid has a larger diameter and is used more often, while comparative evidence has not established which intestinal segment is superior.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[37]](#ref37)</sup> Several modern modifications address these limitations directly.<sup>[[37]](#ref37)[[38]](#ref38)[[39]](#ref39)[[40]](#ref40)[[42]](#ref42)</sup>

### Variants

**A. Standard pedicled ileal segment (single lumen).** The most common variant (27/32 in van der Sluis 2018). Distal-ileal 12–15 cm segment isolated on a mesenteric pedicle (SMA branches) approximately 15–20 cm proximal to the ileocecal valve, transposed caudally through the neovaginal tunnel; open end sutured to perineal skin (ileocutaneous anastomosis); ileo-ileal continuity restored. Open laparotomy (25/32) or laparoscopic (7/32).<sup>[[37]](#ref37)</sup>

**B. Ileal U-pouch.** Two adjacent ileal segments folded into a U-configuration and anastomosed side-to-side to create a wider lumen, directly addressing the single-lumen caliber problem (5/32 in van der Sluis 2018).<sup>[[37]](#ref37)</sup>

**C. Ileal J-pouch (Schneider 2009).** Terminal-ileum J-pouch pedicled on the ileocolic artery and accompanying nervous plexus. The authors chose terminal ileum for its balanced fluid resorption and mucus secretion. This was a single-patient report (pelvic exenteration for vaginal carcinoma); at 1 year the patient had adequate vaginal size, lubrication and no fecal odor.<sup>[[38]](#ref38)</sup>

**D. Transverse retubularized ileal vaginoplasty, Monti principle (Trombetta / Trieste 2005).** Applies the Monti channel principle (originally for continent urinary diversion) to vaginal reconstruction: ileal segment isolated on its mesenteric pedicle, longitudinally detubularized along the antimesenteric border, then transversely retubularized perpendicular to its original axis to create a wider-caliber tube from a shorter segment. n = 6 patients referred for vaginal stenosis, mean follow-up 16 mo; OR time 220 min; no intraoperative complications; all had patent, moist neovaginas and excessive mucus was not a problem in any patient.<sup>[[39]](#ref39)</sup> The authors propose that reducing mucosal surface area limits secretions. The retubularized segment forms the roof of the neovagina.<sup>[[39]](#ref39)[[43]](#ref43)</sup>

**E. Laparoscope-assisted pedicled-ileum transfer (Zhao YZ 2011, the largest series).** n = 82 (2002–2010). Simultaneous abdominal-perineal approach; ileal segment harvested and transposed under laparoscopic guidance; silicone vaginal tutor maintained continuously for 2–3 months postoperatively. Mean depth 15 cm, width 3.2 cm. Complications in 16/82 (19.5%): rectal or bladder injury, acute renal failure, delayed ileocutaneous-anastomosis healing, introital stenosis, and partial or complete bowel obstruction. The neovagina was described as patent, soft, moist and flexible. The authors position the technique for congenital vaginal atresia and secondary reconstruction, with primary transfeminine surgery as an alternative use.<sup>[[41]](#ref41)</sup>

**F. Free jejunal graft vaginoplasty (Cho / Korea 2025).** Novel two-stage technique using a free (non-pedicled) jejunal segment with microvascular anastomosis. Stage 1: orchiectomy, urethroplasty, neovaginal-canal dissection. Stage 2 (separate operation): approximately 14.3 cm of jejunum harvested via single-port laparoscopy, remodelled, placed in the canal. n = 10; Stage 1 OR 654 min (range 330–920); Stage 2 OR 283 min (range 150–441); no major complications; 2 patients had transient paralytic ileus (resolved within 1 wk); follow-up 6–18 mo.<sup>[[40]](#ref40)</sup>

**G. Free vascularized jejunal flap (Ozkan / Turkey).** Microsurgical free jejunal flap with anastomosis to recipient vessels (typically inferior epigastric). In the 2013 series, n = 34 women aged 16–31 years (29 with MRKH), mean follow-up 50 mo; 100% flap survival (3 reoperations for venous compromise, all salvaged); 17.6% (6/34) reported jejunal hypersecretion; 1 mucosal prolapse; 1 vaginal constriction; 27/34 married and sexually active; FSFI ≥ 25 in 11/20 respondents.<sup>[[42]](#ref42)</sup> An earlier 22-patient series reported hypersecretion gradually diminishing, especially after the first 3 mo.<sup>[[44]](#ref44)</sup>

### Ileal-specific complications

- Ileal segment necrosis from vascular compromise is the technique-defining catastrophic complication. Karateke 2010: one of two ileal patients experienced 40 cm of ileal necrosis requiring bilateral ileostomy for 2 months, leading the authors to conclude sigmoid is preferable.<sup>[[35]](#ref35)</sup>
- van der Sluis 2018 multicentre (n = 32): introital stenosis 12.5% (4/32); recto-neovaginal fistula 3.1% (required temporary ileostomy); iatrogenic bladder injury 3.1%; intraoperative hemorrhage requiring transfusion 3.1%; median hospitalisation 12 d (range 6–30).<sup>[[37]](#ref37)</sup>
- Ozkan free jejunal flap: in the 34-patient 2013 series, 17.6% (6/34) reported jejunal hypersecretion and 8.8% (3/34) had venous compromise requiring reoperation, all salvaged.<sup>[[42]](#ref42)</sup> An earlier 22-patient series reported hypersecretion diminishing after the first 3 mo.<sup>[[44]](#ref44)</sup>

### Mucus secretion — the central trade-off

Excessive mucus has been reported after small-bowel vaginoplasty, but these cited series do not establish a quantitative secretion difference between ileal and sigmoid neovaginas.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[37]](#ref37)[[42]](#ref42)</sup> The Hensle 23-year bowel-vaginoplasty experience favoured colonic, particularly sigmoid, segments for technical reasons; reported pad use for mucus came from respondents across the mixed bowel cohort.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Three technical strategies attenuate the problem:

1. **Terminal-ileum J-pouch** (Schneider). Terminal ileum has more balanced resorption / secretion than proximal ileum.<sup>[[38]](#ref38)</sup>
2. **Monti retubularization** (Trombetta). Reduces mucosal surface area; 0/6 had excessive mucus.<sup>[[39]](#ref39)</sup>
3. **Time.** The 34-patient Akar series recorded six reports of jejunal hypersecretion.<sup>[[42]](#ref42)</sup> In an earlier 22-patient Ozkan series, hypersecretion gradually diminished, especially after the first 3 mo.<sup>[[44]](#ref44)</sup>

### Current role

Ileal vaginoplasty occupies a niche role, used predominantly:<sup>[[37]](#ref37)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

- As revision / salvage after failed primary PIV.
- When sigmoid is unavailable because of prior resection or localized pathology and the candidate ileal segment is suitable; a history of IBD was an exclusion in the cited multicentre ileal cohort.<sup>[[37]](#ref37)</sup>
- When peritoneal-flap salvage has failed or is not feasible.
- At centres with specific ileal-technique expertise (Trieste, Belgrade, Korea).
- When anatomic geometry favours the longer ileal mesentery for pedicle reach.

The emergence of robotic peritoneal-flap vaginoplasty as a lower-donor-site-morbidity revision option has further narrowed ileum's role.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Sigmoid Revision Vaginoplasty

Sigmoid is the intestinal segment most often used for revision vaginoplasty after failed primary PIV. Reported mean depths were 15.9 cm in a mixed laparoscopic intestinal revision cohort (18 sigmoid, 3 ileal) and 17.6 cm in a robot-assisted sigmoid cohort (11 primary, 25 revision); successful reconstruction was 91% (19/21) in the mixed intestinal revision cohort.<sup>[[45]](#ref45)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The mucosa is self-lubricating.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

### Indications for sigmoid revision (vs other revision techniques)

| Setting | Preferred revision technique | Anchor |
|---|---|---|
| Short or stenotic vagina, or absent canal after PIV | **Robotic peritoneal-flap revision** has been described in 24 patients: no bowel anastomosis, no rectal injury observed, mean depth 13.6 cm at mean 410-day follow-up | Dy 2021 NYU n = 24<sup>[[46]](#ref46)</sup> |
| **Complete canal obliteration** | **Sigmoid revision** when the canal is completely obliterated and insufficient tissue remains for perineal-only revision | van der Sluis 2016<sup>[[45]](#ref45)</sup>; Sljivich 2025<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Failed prior revision** (recurrent stenosis after FTSG or peritoneal-flap revision) | **Sigmoid revision** | van der Sluis 2019 review<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Prostato-vaginal or rectovaginal fistula | **Sigmoid revision** addresses fistula and stenosis simultaneously | Sljivich 2025 (4 fistula patients)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Max-lubrication priority | **Sigmoid revision** provides mucus-secreting bowel lining; peritoneum can undergo squamous metaplasia | Hensle 2006: 32/36 questionnaire respondents after bowel vaginoplasty (sigmoid, ileum or cecum) reported adequate lubrication for intercourse<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |

**Relative contraindications:** prior pelvic radiation, sigmoid IBD or diverticular disease, dense pelvic adhesions, significant medical comorbidities precluding abdominal surgery.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

### Technique adaptations specific to revision

- **Preoperative colonoscopy and abdominal CT.** Exclude sigmoid pathology and characterise adhesions from prior surgery.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Re-entry of the scarred rectovesical space.** Incise or excise scar tissue to re-establish the canal space.
- **Exaggerated interdigitating colovestibular anastomosis.** Sigmoid mucosa sutured to perineal skin in interdigitating (zigzag) fashion rather than circumferential end-to-end, intended to distribute tension at the colo-perineal junction and reduce introital stenosis.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Sacropromontory neovaginopexy.** The apex is fixed to the promontory in both the Amsterdam and Flor-Lorente techniques to prevent prolapse.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Isoperistaltic (Bouman, Amsterdam) versus antiperistaltic (Flor-Lorente) orientation.** Comparative data are lacking.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **ICG fluorescence angiography as safety adjunct.** van der Sluis 2019 (n = 6): reconstruction was aborted in 1/6 for inadequate perfusion; Flor-Lorente 2021 (two patients) also used ICG for selective vessel ligation enabling 180° sigmoid rotation while guaranteeing irrigation.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **"Virtual ileostomy."** Some groups prepare but do not open a loop ileostomy as a safety measure.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

### Revision-specific outcomes

**van der Sluis 2016: retrospective comparison of two revision techniques (n = 53; 50 transgender and 3 cisgender women):**<sup>[[45]](#ref45)</sup>

| Outcome | Laparoscopic intestinal revision (n = 21) | Perineal FTSG revision (n = 32) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Success | 91% (19/21) | 81% (26/32) | NS |
| Mean neovaginal depth | **15.9 ± 1.4 cm** | 12.5 ± 2.8 cm | **&lt;0.01** |
| OR time | 191 ± 45 min | **131 ± 35 min** | **&lt;0.01** |
| Rectal perforation | 10% (2/21) | **19% (6/32)** | NS |
| Mortality | 0% | 0% | — |
| Median follow-up | 3.2 yr | 3.2 yr | — |

**Sljivich 2025 robot-assisted sigmoid vaginoplasty (n = 36: 11 primary and 25 revisions for vaginal stenosis; 4 had prostato-vaginal fistula):**<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- Mean age 36.1 yr; mean OR time 272.9 min; mean LOS 3.7 d.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Mean postoperative depth 17.6 cm (SD 3.7) in all 36 patients; mean preoperative depth in the revisions for stenosis was 3.4 cm (SD 3.3).
- Sigmoid-skin anastomotic stricture requiring intervention occurred in 2 patients.
- No fistula developed after revision for stenosis. No patient reported a change in bowel habits or vaginitis or discharge persisting beyond 3 months, and none had diversion neovaginal colitis on postoperative vaginoscopy (follow-up duration is not given in the abstract).
- Most expressed satisfaction.

**Lava 2025 primary-vs-revision comparison (n = 119; 66 primary, 53 revision):**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- OR time was shorter for revision (162 versus 209 min primary, p &lt; 0.001), possibly because scrotal-skin labial reconstruction and clitoroplasty are not repeated in most revision cases.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Short-term complications (&lt;30 d) approximately 17.7% overall: full-thickness dehiscence 8.4%, ileus 5.0%, short-term reoperation 5.0%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Long-term complications (≥30 d) approximately 24.4%: mucosal prolapse 11.8%, introital stenosis 9.2%, overall reoperation 16.8%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Complication rates were similar between primary and revision cohorts at a median follow-up of 9.3 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Kim 2026 robot-assisted series (n = 12):**<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

- Mean OR time 544.8 min, longer than in the Sljivich series.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Depth 14.6 cm; vaginal stenosis 0%; positive sexual activity 75%.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- Overall complication rate 41.7% (5/12): ileus 16.7%, prolapse 8.3%, anastomotic leak 8.3% and colon-flap necrosis 8.3% (leak and necrosis both Clavien IIIb).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### Decision framework: peritoneal-flap vs sigmoid for revision

A 2024 retrospective study directly compared 21 peritoneal-flap and 24 intestinal-segment revisions after failed PIV for stenosis. Mean depth and time to first successful dilation were comparable; late vaginal stenosis was reported in 7/21 peritoneal-flap patients and late introital stenosis in 3/24 intestinal patients. The small, nonrandomized groups and differently labeled stenosis endpoints limit treatment-selection inference.<sup>[[50]](#ref50)</sup> The table below places two separate robotic series side by side:

| Feature | Robotic peritoneal-flap revision (Dy 2021) | Robot-assisted sigmoid revision (Sljivich 2025) |
|---|---|---|
| Mean depth | 13.6 cm in 24 revisions<sup>[[46]](#ref46)</sup> | 17.6 cm across all 36 patients (11 primary, 25 revision); revision-only mean not reported<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Bowel anastomosis | No | Yes |
| Rectal injury | 0/24 reported in Dy’s peritoneal-flap revision series<sup>[[46]](#ref46)</sup> | Not stated in Sljivich’s published abstract<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Sigmoid-skin anastomotic stricture | N/A | 2 patients |
| Self-lubrication | Transient (mesothelial → squamous metaplasia) | **Permanent** (colonic mucosa) |
| LOS | ~ 1 wk | **3.7 d** |
| Diversion colitis | N/A | None on vaginoscopy (Sljivich); endoscopic inflammation in 65% of a prospective sigmoid series<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |

**Suggested selection (this page's synthesis of the series above; no comparative trial exists):**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[12]](#ref12)[[14]](#ref14)[[45]](#ref45)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- **Peritoneal-flap revision** is an option for moderate stenosis with preserved canal, avoiding a bowel anastomosis.
- **Sigmoid revision** is used for complete obliteration, failed peritoneal-flap salvage, combined fistula and stenosis, or when permanent self-lubrication is the priority.
- **FTSG revision** had the shortest OR time (131 min) in the van der Sluis comparison, with lower success (81%), less depth (12.5 cm) and more rectal perforations (19% versus 10%, not significant).<sup>[[45]](#ref45)</sup>

### Mortality and major flap-loss signal

The Robinson 2023 systematic review reported 2 deaths among 69 patients in studies reporting mortality (2.9%) and 5 intestinal flap-necrosis cases among 464 patients in studies reporting that endpoint (1.1%).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> In the Amsterdam ICG series, 1 of 6 reconstructions was aborted before transposition for inadequate perfusion; whether ICG lowers flap loss is not established.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

### Obesity and revision-context selection

Because the technique does not depend on genital tissue availability, sigmoid vaginoplasty may be preferable with penoscrotal hypoplasty, higher BMI or complete vaginal stenosis (Lava 2025; median BMI 28.5 kg/m²).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Robinson 2023 SR (10 studies): 215 reported complications across 654 intestinal vaginoplasties (33 events per 100 patients); 108/608 patients in studies reporting reoperation returned to the OR (17.8%). The review warns that intra-abdominal complications, including bowel leak and abscess, may arise years later, be life-threatening, and require prompt diagnosis and management.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Diversion Neovaginitis (Diversion Colitis of the Neovagina)

Endoscopic inflammation is common after sigmoid vaginoplasty (65% in one prospective series) and resembles diversion colitis of any bowel segment surgically diverted from the fecal stream.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

**Pathophysiology.** Loss of luminal exposure to short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs), particularly butyrate, deprives colonocytes of their primary energy source, driving mucosal atrophy, inflammation, and characteristic histologic change.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

**Endoscopic findings (van der Sluis 2016 prospective study, n = 34; mean 23 months after surgery):**<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

- 65% with endoscopic inflammation (MAYO ≥ 1): 35% MAYO 0, 56% MAYO I, 9% MAYO II, 0% MAYO III.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- Features: diminished vascular pattern, edema, granularity, friability, decreased resilience, erythema.
- The remaining in-stream rectosigmoid showed no concurrent abnormalities, confirming specificity to the diverted segment.
- Neovaginal discharge and malodor correlated with inflammatory change.

**Histology:**<sup>[[17]](#ref17)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

- Lymphocytic infiltration in all biopsies.
- Acute inflammatory infiltrate in lamina propria in 31% (4/13).<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- Lymphoid follicular hyperplasia; lymphoplasmacellular infiltrates.
- Spectrum from mild chronic inflammation to active colitis.

**Microbiome (van der Sluis 2019):**<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

- Significantly reduced Bacteroidetes abundance and diversity in the diverted neovaginal segment versus in-stream rectum (Shannon 2.18 vs 2.76).

**Management:**<sup>[[19]](#ref19)[[20]](#ref20)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

- **SCFA (butyrate) irrigation.** Proposed on the basis of the mechanism above.
- **Topical mesalamine.** A single case report (a 21-year-old with a colonic vaginoplasty performed in infancy) described complete symptom resolution.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- **Topical steroids** (suppositories or enemas) have been used, including in children with colovaginoplasty.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- **Regular douching.** Used for discharge and odor control.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

## Ulcerative Colitis in the Sigmoid Neovagina

De novo UC has been reported in the sigmoid neovagina concurrently with the remaining colon: cases developed 10 and 14 years after reconstruction, and another affected patient was 17 years old after earlier sigmoid graft vaginoplasty.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)[[23]](#ref23)[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

- Symptoms: bloody vaginal discharge, bloody diarrhea, abdominal pain, weight loss.
- In severe cases, total proctocolectomy with neovaginectomy is required; colectomy alone is insufficient when the neovaginal segment is also involved.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- Because the neovagina is completely diverted from the fecal stream, these cases argue against a requirement for direct exposure to alimentary antigens in UC.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

---

## Neovaginal Prolapse

Robinson reported vaginal prolapse in 14/565 intestinal vaginoplasties (2.5%); Lava reported mucosal prolapse in 11.8% of 119 sigmoid vaginoplasties at a median follow-up of 9.3 months. The selected prolapse cases in the Drusany Starič review and the long-follow-up Osswald cohort describe different populations and endpoints.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[25]](#ref25)[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

**Risk factors:** inadequate neovaginopexy at primary surgery; functional elongation during intercourse; inherent colonic redundancy. In Osswald 2025 (68 transfeminine patients, median follow-up 27.5 years), 13 of 68 had genital prolapse.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup> In the Osswald cohort, 6 patients had a peritoneal neovagina and 7 had an intestinal neovagina; genital prolapse was observed in 83% and 14.3% of these groups, respectively.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

**Management:**<sup>[[25]](#ref25)[[27]](#ref27)[[28]](#ref28)[[29]](#ref29)</sup>

- **Laparoscopic sacrocolpopexy (mesh).** The 17-case review included several prolapse repairs; no recurrence was reported among its laparoscopic sacropexy cases. The vascular pedicle must be spared, and adhesions may preclude a laparoscopic approach.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)[[27]](#ref27)</sup>
- **Altemeier procedure (vaginal resection of the redundant sigmoid).** A review excluding transfeminine patients described this as its most definitive vaginal repair approach, although recurrence was reported in three cases.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>
- **Sacrospinous ligament fixation.** Alternative vaginal approach.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>
- **Cooper-ligament suspension.** Historical approach with reported recurrence.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup>
- The review authors report that recurrence after repair is higher than previously reported.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

---

## Neoplasia Risk

Adenocarcinoma arising in a sigmoid neovagina is rare but documented. Dawson-Gore 2025 SR identified 16 case reports over 35 years:<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup>

- **69%** (11/16) occurred in sigmoid neovaginas.<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup>
- Symptom onset 7–53 years after reconstruction.
- **Bleeding** was the most common presenting symptom (12 of 16 reviewed cases).<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup>
- 50% local disease, 31% locoregional, 6% metastasis at presentation.<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup>
- Most underwent surgical resection; half of the 10 patients with reported survival had recurrence. Reconstruction indications were MRKH (5), vaginal agenesis (5), gynecologic carcinoma (3) and cloaca (3).
- Histology typically adenocarcinoma (colonic-mucosa origin), in contrast to HPV-related SCC seen in skin-lined neovaginas.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)[[32]](#ref32)</sup>

**Surveillance.** A 2025 German S2k consensus guideline recommends preoperative counseling about bowel-neovagina cancer screening and genital cancer surveillance during follow-up, with vaginoscopy when indicated.<sup>[[51]](#ref51)</sup> It sets no fixed neovaginoscopy interval; published approaches vary:<sup>[[30]](#ref30)[[31]](#ref31)[[32]](#ref32)[[33]](#ref33)</sup><sup>[[30]](#ref30)[[31]](#ref31)[[32]](#ref32)[[33]](#ref33)</sup>

- Annual speculum examination is commonly advised.
- Some authors advocate periodic neovaginoscopy with biopsy, particularly more than 10 years after construction.
- HPV screening of the skin-lined introital portion has been suggested.

## Functional and Sexual Outcomes

**Lubrication.** The sigmoid neovagina provides persistent, durable self-lubrication from mucus-secreting colonic mucosa. In the Hensle 23-year bowel-vaginoplasty experience (57 patients, most with MRKH; sigmoid 39, ileum 9, cecum 9), 32 of 36 questionnaire respondents (89%) reported adequate lubrication for intercourse, 20 (56%) required pads for mucus and 34 (94%) used home douching.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

**Hensle sexual function (36 respondents; mean follow-up of the 57-patient cohort 8.8 years):**<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- 78% sexual desire and satisfaction.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- 56% frequent orgasms; 22% occasional; 22% no orgasms.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- 33% reported sexual arousal and confidence.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Only 2 patients reported dyspareunia.

**di Summa 2019 (n = 43; 28 evaluated, mean follow-up 32.6 months).** Sigmoid as a secondary procedure was associated with less sexual pain during intercourse than PIV. Other aesthetic and functional outcomes did not differ significantly, and most patients were satisfied.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

**Vallinga 2026 (35 of 58 eligible primary sigmoid recipients matched to 35 PIV recipients; median follow-up 10 years).** In this single-center cross-sectional survey, vaginal satisfaction, genital self-image, sexual well-being and satisfaction with surgery did not differ significantly between groups; 69% in each group reported genital complaints. Response and selection bias limit comparative inference.<sup>[[49]](#ref49)</sup>

**Yinuo 2025 (peritoneal n = 20 versus sigmoid n = 30 in congenital absence of the vagina).** The peritoneal group had shorter OR time, earlier return of oral intake, shorter mold use and higher sexual satisfaction; blood loss, hospital stay and discharge odor did not differ. The cohort was cisgender, so generalisability to transfeminine GAS is limited.<sup>[[34]](#ref34)</sup>

## Postoperative Care

Differs from PIV in several ways:<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[21]](#ref21)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Immediate:** stent 3–7 d; Foley; bed rest; DVT prophylaxis; antibiotics; NPO, then graded diet as bowel function returns; some protocols use VAC dressing.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

**Dilation:** required but described as less intensive than after PIV.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Introital stenosis occurred in 9.2% of Lava’s sigmoid-vaginoplasty cohort at a median follow-up of 9.3 months; Robinson reported vaginal stenosis in 68/621 intestinal vaginoplasties (11%), without classifying every case as requiring intervention.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> In the Karateke MRKH series, introital stenosis occurred in 15 unmarried patients (79%) and in none of the married patients; all responded to finger dilatation.<sup>[[35]](#ref35)</sup>

**Long-term:**

- **Regular douching.** In Hensle’s predominantly congenital-indication bowel-vaginoplasty cohort, 34 of 36 questionnaire respondents (94%) reported home douching.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Pads** for mucus were required by 20 of 36 Hensle questionnaire respondents (56%) in a predominantly congenital-indication bowel-vaginoplasty cohort.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Annual neovaginoscopy recommended by some centers for diversion-neovaginitis and neoplasia surveillance.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[30]](#ref30)</sup>
- Prostate examinations performed vaginally (anterior to neovagina).<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

## Evidence Limitations

The evidence base is dominated by retrospective single-centre case series with heterogeneous techniques, variable follow-up, and non-standardised outcome measures.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> No RCTs compare intestinal vaginoplasty to PIV or peritoneal vaginoplasty. The Bouman 2014 SR (21 included studies) noted all were retrospective and low-quality, with sexual satisfaction rarely assessed using standardised questionnaires and QoL not reported.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> The 2025 Yinuo comparison of peritoneal versus sigmoid vaginoplasty (20 versus 30 patients) was performed in cisgender women with congenital absence of the vagina; generalisability to transfeminine GAS is limited.<sup>[[34]](#ref34)</sup> Long-term surveillance protocols for neoplasia and diversion colitis remain undefined.<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup>
