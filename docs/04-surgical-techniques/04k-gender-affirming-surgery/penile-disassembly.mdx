# Penile Disassembly (Penectomy / Tissue Redistribution Step)

**Penile disassembly** is the tissue-redistribution step of feminizing vaginoplasty and vulvoplasty, in which the penile structures are separated and the tissues needed for neovulvar reconstruction are preserved.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The reconstructive aim differs from oncologic penectomy: the glans, dorsal neurovascular bundle (NVB), urethra, penile shaft skin, and prepuce when present are preserved and redistributed across downstream component procedures (clitoroplasty, urethroplasty, labiaplasty, canal lining). The extent of cavernosal resection and tunical or proximal corporal preservation varies by technique.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> For canal-forming vaginoplasty, Raigosa et al. described five major steps: (1) orchiectomy, (2) penile disassembly, (3) neovaginal-cavity creation, (4) urethral-meatus repositioning, and (5) clitorolabioplasty.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

This is the dedicated atlas page for the orchestrating step. For downstream procedures, see [Clitoroplasty (Preputial-Skin Flap, Dorsal NVB)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/clitoroplasty-preputial-flap), [Feminizing Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/feminizing-urethroplasty), [Feminizing Labiaplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/feminizing-labiaplasty), [Bilateral Simple Orchiectomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/simple-orchiectomy), and [Penile Inversion Vaginoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/penile-inversion-vaginoplasty).

## Surgical Technique

### 1. Degloving and penile-skin-tube harvest

For canal-forming penile-inversion vaginoplasty, a circumferential incision is made and the penile skin is degloved as a tube, preserving its full length for neovaginal lining.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Preoperative laser hair removal or electrolysis of penile and scrotal skin intended for canal lining is used to limit potential hair-related complications.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> Vulvoplasty omits canal-lining harvest and avoids canal dissection and lifelong dilation, but does not create a neovaginal canal.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> The skin is separated from the underlying structures while maintaining its blood supply.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> See the [Skin Graft Vaginoplasty page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/skin-graft-vaginoplasty) for canal-lining augmentation when penile skin is insufficient.

### 2. Separation of the urethra from the corpora

The urethra (with surrounding corpus spongiosum) is carefully dissected from the ventral groove between the corpora cavernosa, from glans distally to penoscrotal junction proximally, preserving spongiosal blood supply ("dismembering of the urethra from the corpora" in the Baudet step framework).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### 3. Management of the corpora cavernosa

Huang's 1972–1977 technique transected the corpora cavernosa at the pubic symphysis; beginning in 1978, he retained a proximal corporal segment for clitoral reconstruction.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> Jun et al. described removing cavernosal erectile tissue through corporotomies while retaining and closing the tunica albuginea.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> In Karim's 13-patient Amsterdam revision series, 12 had undergone primary surgery elsewhere; all had difficulty during sexual activity. All improved after repeat surgery with near-complete excision of the corpus spongiosum (and, in three, of the corpus cavernosum), and the authors emphasized total elimination of erectile tissue.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Retained erectile tissue has been associated with:

- Difficulty with sexual activity (Karim).
- Painful engorgement and bulging during arousal.
- Visible periurethral fullness contributing to a misdirected stream.

Complete disassembly with removal of the corporal crura to their attachments on the pubic rami has been recommended to reduce the chance of symptomatic erectile corporal remnants; this is an operative recommendation rather than evidence that a particular resection extent improves outcomes.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> Electrocautery to destroy residual erectile tissue after dividing the corpora, followed by bilateral figure-of-eight sutures placed in the remaining corporal tissue, has also been described to limit arousal-related engorgement and bulging, again without comparative evidence that these adjuncts change outcomes.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

### 4. Preservation of the glans on the dorsal NVB

The glans is not removed; it is dissected off the distal corpora cavernosa while meticulously preserving the dorsal neurovascular bundle (paired dorsal nerves, paired dorsal arteries, deep dorsal vein), then reduced in volume (reduction glansplasty) and fashioned into the neoclitoris. See [Clitoroplasty (Preputial-Skin Flap, Dorsal NVB)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/clitoroplasty-preputial-flap) for the named variants (Ghent / Mañero M-flap / Giraldo corona / Soli mons / Balik butterfly / Brassard urethral-flap / Fang classic); for the glans-reduction technique described above, see Rehman.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### 5. Partial preservation of the corpus spongiosum

A small cuff of corpus spongiosum surrounding the urethra is preserved to maintain urethral blood supply, but the bulk is resected to prevent unwanted engorgement (Karim).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Anatomic Foundation — Dorsal-Nerve Histomorphometry

Ferrin 2026 is a cadaveric histomorphometry study (12 fresh frozen cadavers, 23 samples) relevant to sensory preservation in neoclitoris construction:<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

- The mean myelinated axon count of the dorsal nerve of the penis was 3,634 per side (SD 1,575).
- The estimated bilateral mean was 7,076 myelinated axons (SD 2,634) in the dorsal nerves supplying the glans; unmyelinated axons were not counted.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- Counts varied more between individuals than in the same group's dorsal nerve of the clitoris work. The authors suggest that this variability may contribute to outcome variability in nerve-based reconstruction.
- In a prospective single-center series (Mañero 2018, n = 97), there were no cases of deep or total necrosis of the glans/preputial flap; 8 patients developed partial superficial flap-skin necrosis. The dorsal neurovascular bundle was preserved.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

## Tissue-Redistribution Schema

| Penile structure | Fate | Resulting anatomy | Anchor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Penile shaft skin | **Degloved and inverted** | Neovaginal canal lining | PIV<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Corpora cavernosa** | Total resection advocated by Karim; proximal segment retained in Huang's later technique | Resected tissue discarded; retained segment used for clitoral reconstruction | Karim / Huang<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Glans penis | Reduced in volume; preserved on dorsal NVB | **Neoclitoris** | Rehman / Ghent<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Dorsal NVB | Entire length preserved in Rehman's clitoroplasty | Neoclitoral sensation | Rehman<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Urethra (corpus spongiosum) | Shortened, repositioned; **bulk of spongiosum resected** | Female urethral meatus | Karim<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Prepuce (if present), or distal penile-shaft skin if circumcised | Inner preputial skin or distal penile-shaft skin | Clitoral hood + labia minora | Ghent<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| Scrotal skin | Tailored separately | Labia majora | Amend<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

Each row links to its dedicated component-procedure atlas page (see top navigation).

## Outcomes After Feminizing Genital Surgery

| Outcome | Result | Anchor |
|---|---|---|
| Neoclitoral sensation | Amend reported good/excellent sensation in 97% (24-patient series; median follow-up 39.7 months); Goddard reported 86.3% of assessed neoclitorides sensate at first review (median 56 days) | Amend, Goddard<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| Orgasm capability at long-term follow-up | **86%** (Sigurjónsson, n = 22, mean 37 mo); 48% (Goddard, 31 of 64 reached) | <sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| Neoclitoral or glans/preputial-flap necrosis | Rehman reported neoclitoral necrosis in 2 of 10 patients; Mañero reported no deep or total flap necrosis but partial superficial flap-skin necrosis in 8 of 97 patients | <sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| Goddard neoclitoral sensation | 86.3% of assessed neoclitorides were sensitive at early review (median 56 days); 63/64 (98%) among clitoroplasty recipients reached by phone (70/233 contacted; median 36 months) | <sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| Residual-erectile-tissue complications | **All 13 patients improved** after near-complete spongiosum excision (corpora also excised in 3) | Karim 1991<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |

After feminizing genitoplasty, Goddard reported urethral stenosis in 18.3% and urinary spraying in 5.6% among 197 patients with a recorded first follow-up; among 70 telephone respondents at a median of 36 months, 23% reported stenosis and 20% spraying.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> These whole-operation outcomes cannot be isolated to disassembly. See the [Feminizing Urethroplasty page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/feminizing-urethroplasty) for urinary complications and the Blasdel 2024 patient-versus-surgeon reporting gap.

## Phallus-Preserving Alternative (Ascha 2024)

For nonbinary or gender-diverse patients who desire a vaginal canal but wish to retain the phallus, phallus-preserving vaginoplasty has been described:<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

- The penile-disassembly step is omitted entirely; the penis remains intact.
- The neovaginal canal is created using alternative tissue sources (peritoneum, intestine); see [Peritoneal Pull-Through Vaginoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/peritoneal-pull-through-vaginoplasty) and [Intestinal Vaginoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/intestinal-vaginoplasty). A technical perspective also describes skin grafts and allografts for penile-sparing canal lining and notes that a peritoneal flap may need a graft or allograft to bridge to the introitus.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> See [Phallus-Preserving Vaginoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/phallus-preserving-vaginoplasty).
- This option creates a canal while retaining the phallus. Ramirez et al. describe potential urethral, rectal, and pelvic-nerve injury during canal dissection, including erectile dysfunction from cavernosal-nerve injury, and lifelong dilation to reduce stenosis risk. Outcome data specific to phallus-preserving vaginoplasty are limited.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

## Prerequisites and Eligibility

WPATH SOC 8 (2022) suggests considering genital surgery for eligible adults who are stable on their current treatment regimen, which may include at least 6 months of hormones or longer when needed for the desired surgical result; hormones are not required when undesired or medically contraindicated (Statement 13.6).<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> The Endocrine Society's 2017 guideline advised at least one year of hormone therapy unless undesired or medically contraindicated; WPATH SOC 7 specified 12 months of hormones as appropriate to the patient's gender goals unless not clinically indicated, plus 12 months living in a congruent gender role.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[25]](#ref25)</sup> For penile disassembly as part of vaginoplasty, SOC 8 eligibility assessment includes:

- Marked and sustained gender incongruence; severe distress is not required.
- Capacity to consent after discussion of irreversible effects and reproductive options.
- Adult eligibility under SOC 8; adolescent requests involve the multidisciplinary assessment in Statement 13.7.
- Assessment of physical and mental health conditions that could affect surgical outcome, with risks and benefits discussed.
- Stability on the current treatment regimen; hormone therapy is not required when undesired or medically contraindicated. Program and payer requirements may differ.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

Separately, a 2026 Delphi panel of specialist urologic surgeons considered prior pelvic surgery or radiotherapy and a Charlson Comorbidity Index of 3 or more relative contraindications for canal-forming vaginoplasty, but not vulvoplasty. These are expert-consensus judgments, not absolute exclusions.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

WPATH SOC 8 recommends that surgeons confirm reproductive options have been discussed before gonadectomy.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

## Two-Stage Cautionary Note

In a retrospective single-center comparison of two penile-inversion vaginoplasty techniques (Fakin 2021; 63 single-stage and 62 two-stage patients), all single-stage patients reported neoclitoral sensitivity, while 3 two-stage patients (4.8%) had loss of sensitivity attributed to neoclitoral necrosis.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> Dorsal-pedicle compromise from clitoroplasty on previously operated tissue is a plausible explanation. See the [PIV page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/penile-inversion-vaginoplasty) single-stage-vs-two-stage section for the full comparison.

## Evidence Limitations

- **Karim 1991** is a 13-patient revision series and is the basis for the near-total-spongiosum, total-corpora principle.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Ferrin 2026 dorsal-nerve histomorphometry** is a cadaveric study of 12 specimens, and few quantitative nerve-density data exist for the gender-affirming context.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **No comparative studies** evaluate variations in disassembly technique (timing of NVB elevation, extent of spongiosum resection, glans-reduction approach).
- **Long-term outcomes** beyond 5 years for the disassembly step itself are conflated with downstream component-procedure outcomes in most series.
- **Ascha 2024 phallus-preserving vaginoplasty** is described in a single technique-and-considerations paper; outcomes data not yet mature.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
