# Penile Disassembly (Penectomy / Tissue Redistribution Step)

**Penile disassembly** is the tissue-redistribution step of feminizing vaginoplasty and vulvoplasty, in which the penile structures are separated and the tissues needed for neovulvar reconstruction are preserved.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> It differs from oncologic penectomy: the corpora cavernosa are completely resected and discarded, while the glans, dorsal neurovascular bundle (NVB), urethra, penile shaft skin, and prepuce are preserved and redistributed across downstream component procedures (clitoroplasty, urethroplasty, labiaplasty, canal lining). The five major steps of feminizing genital surgery are: (1) orchiectomy, (2) penile disassembly, (3) neovaginal-cavity creation, (4) urethral-meatus repositioning, and (5) clitorolabioplasty.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

This is the dedicated atlas page for the orchestrating step. For downstream procedures, see [Clitoroplasty (Preputial-Skin Flap, Dorsal NVB)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/clitoroplasty-preputial-flap), [Feminizing Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/feminizing-urethroplasty), [Feminizing Labiaplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/feminizing-labiaplasty), [Bilateral Simple Orchiectomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/simple-orchiectomy), and [Penile Inversion Vaginoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/penile-inversion-vaginoplasty).

## Surgical Technique

### 1. Degloving and penile-skin-tube harvest

A circumferential incision is made and the penile skin is degloved as a tube preserving full length for use as the neovaginal lining (penile inversion). The skin is separated from the underlying structures while maintaining its blood supply.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> See the [Skin Graft Vaginoplasty page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/skin-graft-vaginoplasty) for canal-lining augmentation when penile skin is insufficient.

### 2. Separation of the urethra from the corpora

The urethra (with surrounding corpus spongiosum) is carefully dissected from the ventral groove between the corpora cavernosa, from glans distally to penoscrotal junction proximally, preserving spongiosal blood supply ("dismembering of the urethra from the corpora" in the Baudet step framework).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### 3. Complete resection of the corpora cavernosa

Both corpora cavernosa are completely resected, historically transected at the pubic symphysis.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> In the Karim 1991 Amsterdam revision series, 13 patients operated on elsewhere had difficulties during sexual activity. All improved after repeat surgery with near-complete excision of the corpus spongiosum (and, in three, of the corpus cavernosum), and the authors emphasized total elimination of erectile tissue.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Retained erectile tissue has been associated with:

- Difficulty with sexual activity (Karim).
- Painful engorgement and bulging during arousal.
- Visible periurethral fullness contributing to a misdirected stream.

### 4. Preservation of the glans on the dorsal NVB

The glans is not removed; it is dissected off the distal corpora cavernosa while meticulously preserving the dorsal neurovascular bundle (paired dorsal nerves, paired dorsal arteries, deep dorsal vein), then reduced in volume (reduction glansplasty) and fashioned into the neoclitoris. See [Clitoroplasty (Preputial-Skin Flap, Dorsal NVB)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/clitoroplasty-preputial-flap) for the named variants (Ghent / Mañero M-flap / Giraldo corona / Soli mons / Balik butterfly / Brassard urethral-flap / Fang classic).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### 5. Partial preservation of the corpus spongiosum

A small cuff of corpus spongiosum surrounding the urethra is preserved to maintain urethral blood supply, but the bulk is resected to prevent unwanted engorgement (Karim).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Anatomic Foundation — Dorsal-Nerve Histomorphometry

Ferrin 2026 is a cadaveric histomorphometry study (12 fresh frozen cadavers, 23 samples) relevant to sensory preservation in neoclitoris construction:<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

- The mean myelinated axon count of the dorsal nerve of the penis was 3,634 per side (SD 1,575).
- The mean total across both sides was 7,076 axons (SD 2,634), the innervation of the glans.
- Counts varied more between individuals than in the same group's dorsal nerve of the clitoris work. The authors suggest that this variability may contribute to outcome variability in nerve-based reconstruction.
- Neoclitoral necrosis was 0 for deep or total flap loss in a prospective single-center series (Mañero 2018, n = 97), in which the dorsal neurovascular bundle was preserved; 8 patients had partial superficial skin necrosis.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

Cross-link to the [Clitoroplasty page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/clitoroplasty-preputial-flap) Anatomic Foundation section for the full nerve-density / O'Connell / Sedý anatomic framework.

## Tissue-Redistribution Schema

| Penile structure | Fate | Resulting anatomy | Anchor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Penile shaft skin | **Degloved and inverted** | Neovaginal canal lining | PIV<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Corpora cavernosa** | **Completely resected** | Discarded | Karim<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Glans penis | Reduced in volume; preserved on dorsal NVB | **Neoclitoris** | Rehman / Ghent<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Dorsal NVB | **Preserved in entirety** | Neoclitoral sensation | Ferrin<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Urethra (corpus spongiosum) | Shortened, repositioned; **bulk of spongiosum resected** | Female urethral meatus | Karim<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Prepuce (if present) | Inner preputial skin | Clitoral hood + labia minora | Ghent<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| Scrotal skin | Tailored separately | Labia majora | Amend<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

Each row links to its dedicated component-procedure atlas page (see top navigation).

## Outcomes Attributable to the Disassembly Step

| Outcome | Result | Anchor |
|---|---|---|
| Neoclitoral sensation (good / excellent) | **86–98%** across series | Amend, Goddard<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| Orgasm capability at long-term follow-up | **86%** (Sigurjónsson, n = 22, mean 37 mo); 48% (Goddard, 31 of 64 reached) | <sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| Neoclitoral necrosis | 2 of 10 in Rehman 1999; no deep or total necrosis in Mañero 2018 (n = 97) | <sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| Goddard long-term neoclitoral-sensitivity preservation | First follow-up 86.3%; long-term phone follow-up **98%** | <sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| Residual-erectile-tissue complications | **All 13 patients improved** after near-complete spongiosum excision (corpora also excised in 3) | Karim 1991<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |

Urethral complications attributable to the disassembly step (urethral stenosis 18–26%, urinary spraying 6–20%) are detailed on the [Feminizing Urethroplasty page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/feminizing-urethroplasty), particularly the Blasdel 2024 "surgical complication blind spots" patient-vs-surgeon gap.

## Phallus-Preserving Alternative (Ascha 2024)

For nonbinary or gender-diverse patients who desire a vaginal canal but wish to retain the phallus, phallus-preserving vaginoplasty has been described:<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

- The penile-disassembly step is omitted entirely; the penis remains intact.
- The neovaginal canal is created using alternative tissue sources (peritoneum, intestine); see [Peritoneal Pull-Through Vaginoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/peritoneal-pull-through-vaginoplasty) and [Intestinal Vaginoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/intestinal-vaginoplasty).
- Indicated for nonbinary individuals seeking canal creation without feminisation of external genitalia.

## Prerequisites and Eligibility

WPATH SOC 8 (2022) suggests that genital surgery be considered for eligible adults, defined by the criteria below, and hormone therapy of at least 6 months or longer as needed for the desired surgical result (Statements 13.5 and 13.6).<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> The Endocrine Society 2017 guideline and earlier WPATH SOC 7 criteria specified 12 months of hormone therapy and 12 months in a congruent gender role.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> Penile disassembly as part of vaginoplasty is considered for patients who meet these criteria:

- Persistent, well-documented gender dysphoria.
- Capacity to make a fully informed decision and consent (fertility-affecting, irreversible).
- Age of majority (≥ 18 yr in most jurisdictions).
- Well-controlled medical or mental-health conditions.
- Hormone therapy for the interval required by the program or payer, unless contraindicated.

WPATH SOC 8 recommends that surgeons confirm reproductive options have been discussed before gonadectomy.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

## Two-Stage Cautionary Note

Performing components of penile disassembly across two operations (the historical two-stage PIV approach) has been associated with neoclitoral loss: a retrospective single-center comparison (Fakin 2021; 63 single-stage and 62 two-stage patients) found no neoclitoral necrosis in the single-stage group and 4.8% (3 patients) in the two-stage group.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> The authors did not establish the mechanism; dorsal-pedicle compromise from clitoroplasty on previously operated tissue is a plausible explanation. See the [PIV page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/penile-inversion-vaginoplasty) single-stage-vs-two-stage section for the full comparison.

## Evidence Limitations

- **Karim 1991** is a 13-patient revision series and is the basis for the near-total-spongiosum, total-corpora principle.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Ferrin 2026 dorsal-nerve histomorphometry** is a cadaveric study of 12 specimens, and few quantitative nerve-density data exist for the gender-affirming context.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **No comparative studies** evaluate variations in disassembly technique (timing of NVB elevation, extent of spongiosum resection, glans-reduction approach).
- **Long-term outcomes** beyond 5 years for the disassembly step itself are conflated with downstream component-procedure outcomes in most series.
- **Ascha 2024 phallus-preserving vaginoplasty** is described in a single technique-and-considerations paper; outcomes data not yet mature.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
