# Shaft-Only Phalloplasty with Vaginal Preservation (Chen-Berli / Moorefield)

**Shaft-only phalloplasty with vaginal preservation** is a customised gender-affirming genital procedure that creates a neophallus from a donor flap while intentionally preserving the vaginal canal, without urethral lengthening (UL).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> It is designed for transmasculine and nonbinary individuals who seek a neophallus, wish to retain the vaginal canal, and may later consider an erectile prosthesis while avoiding neourethral reconstruction. Insertive and receptive sexual outcomes for this configuration remain unreported.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For the broader variant-GGAS framework, see [Non-Binary / Nullification Gender-Affirming Surgery](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/non-binary-nullification). For the downstream erectile-prosthesis component, see [Penile Implant After Phalloplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/penile-implant-after-phalloplasty).

---

## Concept and Indications

Standard phalloplasty typically includes vaginectomy (colpocleisis), scrotoplasty, urethral lengthening, and clitoral burial.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Shaft-only phalloplasty with vulvoscrotoplasty departs from this paradigm in 4 ways:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

1. **No urethral lengthening.** Patients void through the native meatus. Omitting UL avoids construction of a neourethra and its fistula or stricture risk, but does not exclude other urinary problems. Veerman reported revision for fistula or stricture in 46/63 (73%) patients after mixed genital procedures with UL.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Hu separately pooled fistula at 23.9% and stricture/stenosis at 25.0% after phalloplasty with urethral reconstruction; these are not a measured patient-level composite risk.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Other urethral-construction cohorts use different techniques and endpoints.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
2. **Vaginal preservation.** Canal retention aims to preserve the option of receptive vaginal intercourse; postoperative sexual function was not reported for this four-patient technique.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
3. **Vulvoscrotoplasty.** Labia majora reconfigured into a scrotum-like structure framing the preserved vaginal introitus, creating a masculinised perineal appearance while maintaining vaginal access.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
4. **Clitoral management.** Clitoris may be buried beneath the neophallus or left exposed, depending on patient preference and variant chosen.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

**Primary indications:** patients who desire a phallus with retained vaginal anatomy, wish to avoid neourethral reconstruction, or prioritise natal genital access.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> **Preoperative assessment:** A 2026 expert Delphi consensus for masculinising genital surgery identified smoking, diabetes, and cardiovascular disease as risk factors, considered Charlson Comorbidity Index ≥3 a relative contraindication to phalloplasty, and endorsed multidisciplinary assessment; this is expert consensus, not shaft-only outcome evidence.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

## Patient Goals and Decision-Making

**Brown 2025 survey (n = 248 individuals considering phalloplasty or metoidioplasty)**: significant differences between transgender men and nonbinary individuals in surgical goals:<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

| Goal | Transgender men | Nonbinary |
|---|---|---|
| **Retaining vaginal canal** | Less prioritised | **Significantly more prioritised** for receptive penetration and gender-identity affirmation |
| Standing urination | Strong priority | Less prioritised |
| Being seen as male | Strong priority | Less prioritised |
| Dysphoria resolution | Strong priority | Less prioritised |
| **Tactile and erogenous sensation** | **Top priority** | **Top priority** |

Nonbinary respondents placed more weight on retaining the vaginal canal, which is the rationale for offering vaginal-preserving options.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> The survey was cross-sectional and included people who had not yet had surgery.

## Variant A — Chen / Berli OHSU 2021 (Canonical Vulvoscrotoplasty)

Published as a technical description in 4 patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Stage 1 — Shaft-only phalloplasty

1. **Flap harvest.** Most common donor sites:
   - **Radial forearm free flap (RFFF).** The most widely used flap for phalloplasty: thin, pliable, with reliable neurovascular anatomy. It requires microsurgical anastomosis and leaves a conspicuous forearm donor scar.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
   - **Anterolateral thigh (ALT) pedicled flap.** Avoids microsurgery; concealable scar; often bulky requiring later debulking; in Ascha's retrospective cohort with vaginectomy and full-length urethroplasty, ALT had higher odds of urethral fistula, nonurethral complications, and phallus wound dehiscence than RFFF, while overall urethral complication rates were similar; those results do not directly transfer to shaft-only surgery.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
   - **Superficial circumflex iliac artery perforator (SCIP) flap.** In De Gelder's retrospective 2012–2022 series, 55 SCIP procedures included 10 unilateral flaps for shaft reconstruction and eight bilateral flaps for combined shaft and urethra creation. This pedicled approach avoids microsurgery; donor sites were closed primarily in all patients, and the authors noted potentially lower shaft sensation.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> In de Haseth's separate 47-patient shaft-only SCIA cohort, 45/47 had successful reconstruction using one flap, one had total and one partial flap necrosis, and 17/47 had phallus-scrotum junction dehiscence managed conservatively.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup> That center performed scrotoplasty and perineostomy, so these are not vaginal-preserving outcome estimates.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>
2. **Phallus construction.** Flap shaped into a tube (shaft only, no neourethra incorporated). Since UL is omitted, flap design is simplified versus tube-in-tube.
3. **Neurotisation.** Flap sensory nerve coapted to one or both dorsal clitoral nerves and/or the ilioinguinal nerve for protective and potentially erogenous sensation.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
4. **Phallus inset.** At pubic region, typically at or above the clitoral position.

### Stage 2 (or concurrent) — Vulvoscrotoplasty with Vaginal Preservation

1. **Labial reconfiguration.** Labia majora mobilised and reconfigured into a bifid or unified scrotum constructed around the preserved vaginal introitus (not after vaginectomy as in standard scrotoplasty).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In Peters's 2026 OHSU technique, the superior labial commissure and urethral plate are released for clitoral mobility; 1–2 mm of skin is retained lateral to the labia minora, the superior commissure is re-formed, and the closure avoids undue tension to prevent introital gaping.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
2. **Vaginal canal preservation.** Intact and patent; introitus accessible between or posterior to the neo-scrotal structures.
3. **Clitoral placement.** The Chen-Berli description places the clitoris beneath the neophallus.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Peters's later OHSU technique permits leaving the clitoris in its natal position and rotating the scrotum above it when burial is not desired.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
4. **Testicular implants.** Staged approximately 6 mo later into the neo-scrotal compartments.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### Subsequent stages (optional)

- **Glansplasty.** Sculpting of the distal neophallus to simulate a glans.
- **Erectile prosthesis.** Semi-rigid or inflatable penile prosthesis (typically 9–12 mo post-phalloplasty); see [Penile Implant After Phalloplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/penile-implant-after-phalloplasty).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Tattoo.** Medical tattooing for colour matching and aesthetic refinement.

## Variant B — Moorefield 2024 Y-to-V Advancement (Vaginal Preservation **Without** Scrotoplasty / **Without** Clitoral Burial)

Published in *Plast Reconstr Surg* 2024 as a refinement of the Chen-Berli approach for patients who wish to retain a more natal-appearing vulva and direct clitoral access.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### How Moorefield differs from Chen-Berli (3 critical departures)

| Element | Chen-Berli (Variant A) | Moorefield (Variant B) |
|---|---|---|
| **Scrotoplasty** | Yes (labia majora → scrotum around vaginal introitus) | **No** (labia preserved in natal-appearing configuration) |
| **Clitoral management** | Optional burial beneath neophallus | **Degloved and inset at ventral phallus base — exposed** |
| **Hood-redundancy management** | Not specifically addressed | **Y-to-V advancement + reduction labiaplasty** |

Designed for patients who want direct clitoral access for stimulation and a more natal-appearing vulva rather than a masculinised perineum.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### 6-step Moorefield technique

1. **Shaft-only phalloplasty.** Same flap construction as Chen-Berli; no neourethra incorporated; single-tube design (no tube-in-tube).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
2. **Clitoral degloving and repositioning.** Clitoral shaft degloved (prepuce separated from underlying clitoral body); degloved clitoral shaft inset at the ventral base of the neophallus in an exposed position for direct erogenous access. Creates a specific aesthetic problem; degloving leaves redundant prepucial tissue that, if unaddressed, produces an unsightly excess at the phallus base.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
3. **Y-to-V adjacent tissue transfer (the core innovation).** Redundant clitoral-hood tissue marked in a Y-shaped pattern and excised; remaining flaps advanced and closed in a V configuration. This obliterates the dead space created by degloving, tightens tissue at the phallus base, and creates a smooth transition between neophallus and underlying vulvar anatomy. The Y-to-V principle is a plastic-surgery tool for tissue rearrangement and scar lengthening, adapted here to the GAS context.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
4. **Reduction labiaplasty.** Labia minora trimmed and resuspended anteriorly, improving aesthetic contour and creating a streamlined appearance that integrates with the phallus base; labia repositioned to frame the preserved introitus.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
5. **Vaginal preservation.** Canal left completely intact and patent.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
6. **Neurotisation.** Phallus neurotised with options:<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
   - **Unilateral clitoral nerve.** One dorsal clitoral nerve sacrificed for coaptation to the flap nerve; contralateral dorsal clitoral nerve preserved to maintain direct clitoral erogenous sensation. This provides the neophallus with potential erogenous sensation while preserving direct clitoral sensation through the contralateral nerve.
   - **Ilioinguinal nerve.** Alternative or additional recipient providing protective sensation.

### Neurotisation and sensation — Moorefield-specific framework

**Unilateral-clitoral-nerve-sacrifice rationale.** In standard phalloplasty with clitoral burial, the clitoris is buried beneath the phallus and the flap nerves are coapted to the ilioinguinal nerve and one of the two dorsal clitoral nerves (Ghent technique). Erogenous sensation is reached indirectly through pressure on the neophallus transmitted to the buried clitoris.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> In Moorefield, erogenous sensation is available through two pathways: (1) direct stimulation of the exposed non-buried clitoris via the preserved contralateral dorsal clitoral nerve, and (2) neophallus sensation via the coapted flap nerve.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

**Sensory-outcomes evidence (broader phalloplasty literature):**

- **Ferrin 2026 SR on neurorrhaphy in phalloplasty.** Nerve coaptation consistently improves sensation; significant heterogeneity in techniques and reported outcomes.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- **Orgasmic ability.** In a Ghent sensory study of 27 RFFF phalli with a buried clitoris, all participants who attempted orgasm reported it; the authors attributed this in part to clitoral preservation. Moorefield's exposed-clitoris variant has no reported orgasm outcomes, so this result cannot be transferred to that technique.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Tactile sensation.** In the same Ghent series, the mean pressure threshold at the phallus tip was 11.1 g/mm² and the mean vibration threshold was 3 µm, compared with 3.25 g/mm² and 0.6 µm at the contralateral nongrafted forearm.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- **Zack 2025 sexual-health-outcomes SR.** Erogenous-sensation ratings 53–100% across phalloplasty studies; orgasm during masturbation reported in 50–93% of patients.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

### Patient selection — Moorefield-specific

Suited for patients who:<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

- **Desire a phallus** for gender affirmation and/or future insertive intercourse (with erectile prosthesis).
- **Want to preserve the vagina** for receptive intercourse and, if reproductive organs are retained, possible childbearing; discuss any planned hysterectomy and reproductive goals separately.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Do not want scrotoplasty.** Prefer natal-appearing vulva.
- **Want the clitoris exposed** for direct stimulation rather than buried.
- **Do not prioritise standing urination.** Accept seated voiding through the native meatus.
- **Want to avoid urethral-reconstruction and colpectomy complications:** Hu separately pooled fistula at 23.9% and stricture/stenosis at 25.0% across different phalloplasty study sets; in Nikkels 2019, 143 patients underwent colpectomy (34 with other procedures), with 15/143 (10%) major perioperative, 17/143 (12%) major postoperative and 50/143 (35%) minor postoperative complications.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

Brown's cross-sectional survey of 248 people who had considered but not undergone metoidioplasty or phalloplasty found that nonbinary respondents more often prioritised vaginal-canal retention, while both groups rated tactile and erogenous sensation among their top goals; the survey did not evaluate the Moorefield variant.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### Moorefield-specific advantages

1. **Preserved direct clitoral access.** Exposed clitoris allows direct stimulation, potentially enhancing sexual satisfaction for patients who value clitoral orgasm.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
2. **Potential sensory pathways.** Moorefield's technique leaves the clitoris exposed and allows phallic nerve coaptation to a unilateral dorsal clitoral nerve and/or the ilioinguinal nerve. Sensory and sexual outcomes have not been measured for this variant; results from buried-clitoris phalloplasty cannot establish them.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
3. **Avoidance of scrotoplasty complications.** No scrotal wound dehiscence, implant extrusion, or asymmetry.
4. **Avoidance of neourethral fistula and stricture.** Both shaft-only variants omit urethral lengthening; other urinary symptoms can still occur.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
5. **Avoidance of vaginectomy morbidity.** Nikkels 2019 colpectomy complication burden.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
6. **Aesthetic refinement.** Y-to-V specifically addresses redundant-tissue problem that would otherwise result from degloving without burial.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
7. **Fewer surgical stages.** No UL, no vaginectomy, no scrotoplasty.

### Moorefield-specific limitations and considerations

- **No standing urination.**
- **No testicular implants** (no scrotal compartment).
- **Aesthetic compromise** for patients who desire a fully masculinised perineum.
- **Clitoral exposure** at the ventral phallus base may be subject to friction or irritation (not specifically reported).
- **Unilateral clitoral-nerve coaptation.** The flap may be coapted to a nerve from one clitoral side, but direct clitoral sensory outcomes for this variant have not been measured; diminished sensation remains a possible risk.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- **Extremely limited evidence.** Single technical note without reported outcomes data, complication rates, or PRO satisfaction measures.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Shaft-Only vs Standard Phalloplasty Comparison

| Feature | Shaft-Only + Vulvoscrotoplasty | Standard Phalloplasty |
|---|---|---|
| **Urethral lengthening** | **No** | Yes |
| **Standing urination** | No (seated) | Yes (achieved in about 91.5% in a pooled analysis)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Vaginectomy** | No (vagina preserved) | Yes (typically) |
| **Scrotoplasty** | Yes (around vaginal introitus) | Yes (after vaginectomy) |
| **Neourethral fistula or stricture** | No neourethra is constructed; other urinary complications remain possible | Separate pooled estimates after urethral reconstruction: fistula 23.9% and stricture/stenosis 25.0%, with unknown patient-level overlap<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Erectile prosthesis** | Possible | Possible |
| **Penetrative (insertive) intercourse** | Outcomes unreported for the Chen-Berli vaginal-preserving series<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Reported with and without an erectile prosthesis in broader phalloplasty studies<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> |
| **Receptive vaginal intercourse** | Canal retained; postoperative function unreported<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Not possible after vaginectomy; depends on whether the canal is retained |
| **Clitoral sensation** | Clitoris retained (may be buried); postoperative sensation not reported in Chen-Berli's four-patient series<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Typically buried |
| **Number of stages** | Can be staged, with flap transfer before genital modification in the OHSU approach<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> | Single-stage phalloplasty with UL has been reported; other approaches are staged<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[24]](#ref24)</sup> |

## Outcomes (Chen-Berli Series — Preliminary)

Published OHSU experience: 4 patients with shaft-only phalloplasty, vaginal preservation and vulvoscrotoplasty.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Given the extremely small sample size, outcomes are preliminary:

- All 4 patients had successful phallus construction with flap survival.
- **Vaginal patency maintained** in all cases.
- Specific complication rates, PROMs, sexual function data, and long-term follow-up not yet published in detail.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Complications and Considerations

Because UL is omitted, the most morbid component of standard phalloplasty is avoided. In the Berli 2025 single-surgeon retrospective series of Big Ben two-stage urethral lengthening (n = 73), 27% had a urologic complication (stricture 8%, fistula 16.4% among the 71 who had the second stage) and 96% achieved standing urination.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> Shaft-only phalloplasty omits neourethral construction and its associated fistula and stricture sites, but other urinary complications remain possible.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> In de Rooij's retrospective cohort of 91 phalloplasties with UL and 45 without, reoperation occurred in 81% versus 27%, meatal or perineal-orifice stenosis in 29% versus 11%, and one patient without UL had a fistula; all patients without UL had a perineostomy, so these rates do not describe vaginal-preserving surgery.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

**Remaining complications relevant to shaft-only phalloplasty:**

- **Flap-related.** In Ascha’s single-center retrospective series of single-stage phalloplasty with vaginectomy and full-length urethroplasty (at least 6 months of follow-up), partial or total neophallus loss was 3.4% with RFFF and 7.8% with pedicled ALT; wound dehiscence, hematoma, infection.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Donor-site morbidity.** Forearm scarring and potential functional deficits (RFFF); thigh contour deformity and need for debulking (ALT).<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Vaginal-preservation-specific.** Claeys 2025 SR found that both phalloplasty and metoidioplasty carried a higher risk of urethral complications when the vagina was preserved, but this applies primarily to procedures that include UL. For shaft-only without UL, this risk is not applicable. All studies in that review had a high risk of bias.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Persistent vaginal cavity issues.** In standard phalloplasty with vaginectomy, incomplete obliteration causes recurrent UTIs, pelvic pain, post-void dribbling. In vaginal-preserving procedures, the vagina is intentionally left open; this is not a "complication" per se but ongoing gynecologic care remains necessary.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Erectile prosthesis complications.** An earlier pooled phalloplasty review estimated 27.9%.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> A 2024 meta-analysis of transmasculine recipients reported overall complications of 38% (95% CI 21–59%) with inflatable and 37% (18–62%) with malleable devices; explantation for any reason was 19% (9–38%) and 13% (4–33%), respectively.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> These estimates are not specific to shaft-only surgery. See [Penile Implant After Phalloplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/penile-implant-after-phalloplasty).<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

## Broader Context — Variant Gender-Affirming Genital Surgery

**Claeys 2025 SR (variant GGAS; 23 case series: 17 masculinising, 6 feminising)** confirmed patients choose these procedures primarily out of:<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

- **Personal desire**
- **To avoid complication risk** (e.g., UL complications)
- Because they lack dysphoria about certain natal genital structures

The review emphasised that complications in masculinising surgeries primarily arise from the extended urethra, which can be mitigated through primary perineal urethrostomy or, as in the Chen-Berli approach, by omitting urethral lengthening entirely.

**Ascha 2024** described the broader framework of individually customised procedures (vagina-preserving phalloplasty, phallus-preserving vaginoplasty, genital nullification) in 16 patients, reinforcing that these procedures may better affirm the identities of gender-diverse patients while preserving desired sexual function.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

---

## Evidence Gaps

Outcome evidence for the Chen-Berli vaginal-preserving vulvoscrotoplasty remains limited to its four-patient technical description; Moorefield reported a distinct no-scrotoplasty technical modification without an outcome series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

Unanswered questions:

- Long-term vaginal patency and function after vulvoscrotoplasty.
- Sexual function outcomes (both insertive with prosthesis and receptive vaginal).
- PROMs and satisfaction data.
- Aesthetic outcomes and revision rates.
- Optimal flap choice specifically for shaft-only (without neourethral-construction constraint).
- Feasibility and outcomes of erectile prosthesis placement in this configuration.
- Conversion rates to other procedures (e.g., later vaginectomy or UL).
