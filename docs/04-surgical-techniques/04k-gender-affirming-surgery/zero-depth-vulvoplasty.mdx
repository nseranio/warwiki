# Zero-Depth / Shallow-Depth Vulvoplasty (Gender-Confirming Vulvoplasty)

**Zero-depth vulvoplasty** (also termed shallow-depth vaginoplasty, minimal-depth vaginoplasty, or gender-confirming vulvoplasty) is a feminizing genital gender-affirming surgery that creates the external appearance of female genitalia (vulva, clitoris, clitoral hood, labia majora, labia minora, and urethral meatus) without creation of a neovaginal canal.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> One retrospective series argues that it should be offered as a standard option alongside full-depth vaginoplasty (FDV). It is associated with high satisfaction, shorter recovery and no lifelong vaginal dilation in retrospective series.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For the cohort-level decision framework and treatment database, see [Feminizing Gender-Affirming Surgery](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/feminizing-surgery). For the clinical-conditions overview, see [Feminizing Procedures](/docs/special-populations/05c-gender-affirming/feminizing-procedures).

---

## Definition and Terminology

The nomenclature remains unstandardized. Terms used interchangeably include zero-depth vaginoplasty, shallow-depth vaginoplasty (SDV), minimal-depth vaginoplasty, and gender-confirming vulvoplasty (GCV).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Some techniques create a small dimpled introitus (a shallow vestibular depression) rather than a completely flat closure, which is the distinction between "shallow-depth" and truly "zero-depth" approaches.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> A survey of WPATH-affiliated practitioners identified a lack of standardized terminology and surgical techniques for this procedure.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Indications and Patient Selection

Vulvoplasty is chosen for two broad categories of reasons:

- **Patient preference (majority of cases).** In the first reported series (30 patients completed or scheduled for vulvoplasty), 63% chose vulvoplasty despite having no contraindications to full-depth vaginoplasty.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Reported motivations:
  - No desire for receptive vaginal penetration (59% of 17 patients in the Dutch series)<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
  - Sexual preference toward women without a wish for penetrative intercourse (35% in the Dutch series)<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
  - Aversion to lifelong vaginal dilation (54% in a Danish survey)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
  - Belief in lower surgical risk (71% in the Danish survey)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
  - Negative past sexual experiences (24% in the Dutch series)<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Surgeon recommendation because of medical risk factors (approximately 37% in the first series, 24% in the Dutch series).** Examples include prior pelvic radiation, high BMI, advanced age, significant medical comorbidities, or insufficient donor tissue for canal creation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Patients choosing vulvoplasty tend to be older and have higher BMI than those seeking full-depth vaginoplasty.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In one cohort, all but one SDV survey respondent met at least one of these: age ≥ 40, exclusively feminine-identifying partners, or significant aversion to long-term dilation and douching.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgical Technique

The procedure shares many steps with penile-inversion vaginoplasty but omits dissection of a neovaginal canal between the bladder and rectum:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

1. **Orchiectomy** with ligation of the spermatic cords.
2. **Penectomy** with preservation of the dorsal neurovascular bundle of the glans penis for neoclitoral construction.
3. **Clitoroplasty.** The glans penis is reduced and repositioned as a sensate neoclitoris, preserving the dorsal neurovascular pedicle.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
4. **Clitoral hood and labia minora.** Preputial or distal penile shaft skin is used for the clitoral hood and medial labia minora; proximal penile shaft skin forms the lateral surface. Horizontal mattress quilting sutures define the labia minora as distinct subunits.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
5. **Labia majora.** Constructed from scrotal skin, pulled inferiorly and medially toward the perineum. Excess defatting is avoided because the labia majora are subject to initial edema and often atrophy with time.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
6. **Urethral shortening and repositioning.** The urethra is shortened and spatulated to create a downward-directed feminine stream.
7. **Introitus.** In the shallow-depth technique, a small dimpled introitus is created without dissecting a canal. In truly zero-depth approaches, the perineal skin is closed flat.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
8. **No vaginal canal dissection** is performed. This removes the risk of rectal injury and rectovaginal fistula from canal creation, and the need for postoperative dilation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Advantages Over Full-Depth Vaginoplasty

- **Dilation.** No lifelong dilation is required.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Dissection.** No canal is dissected; the operation is less extensive than FDV.
- **Major complications.** The rectal injury, rectovaginal fistula and vaginal canal stenosis that complicate FDV do not arise without a canal.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Recovery.** Recovery is shorter.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Douching.** Vaginal douching is not needed.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Orgasm.** In a retrospective cohort that included 41 vulvoplasty-only patients, 90% of the combined cohort reported the ability to orgasm at 6 months.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

---

## Complications

Complications are reported to be less frequent than after FDV, but they do occur. The rows below come from small retrospective series (Dutch series n = 17; multidisciplinary program n = 21).

| Complication | Frequency | Management | References |
|---|---|---|---|
| Urinary spraying / stream abnormality | Most common (82% of the complications that required additional surgery in the SDV cohort) | Surgical revision | <sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Wound dehiscence | 3/17 (18%), minor | Conservative | <sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Meatal stenosis | 2/17 (12%) | Surgical correction | <sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Granulation tissue | 2/21 (about 10%) | Silver nitrate or excision | <sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| UTI | 3/21 (14%) in the multidisciplinary program; 1/17 in the Dutch series | Oral antibiotics | <sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Remnant corpus spongiosum | 1/17 | Surgical excision | <sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |

In the Dutch series, 11 of 17 patients (65%) had an uncomplicated course at median follow-up of 34 months.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> In a multidisciplinary program, the 30-day complication rate for vulvoplasty was 57%, and 92% of complications were Clavien–Dindo grade I or II.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> In the largest SDV cohort (n = 35), 27% had complications that required additional surgery, and 82% of those complications were related to urinary spraying.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Patient Satisfaction and Functional Outcomes

Satisfaction is high in these series, but the data are retrospective, mostly from small samples, and use non-validated questions:

- In the first reported vulvoplasty series, 93% of those who completed surgery were satisfied with the surgery and with their decision.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- All 12 Dutch patients with satisfaction data (of 17) were satisfied and graded the result 8.2 ± 0.9 out of 10.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- In the SDV questionnaire (29 of 35 responded), 86% would choose SDV again. All but one of the 14% who would choose FDV instead reported new interest in receptive vaginal intercourse after finding masculine-identifying partners postoperatively.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- In a retrospective cohort of 223 patients (41 vulvoplasty alone, 182 vulvoplasty with vaginoplasty), 90% reported the ability to orgasm within 6 months of surgery. Inability to orgasm was associated with older age, higher BMI and medical comorbidities. It was also associated with vulvoplasty alone, but that association was not significant after controlling for age and comorbidities.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- In a cross-sectional survey using the validated GENDER-Q (295 respondents, 39% response rate; 103 vulvoplasty, 192 vaginoplasty), sexual satisfaction did not differ by surgery type.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

Respondents in the SDV questionnaire ranked long-term outcomes (no dilation, comparable appearance, sexual function) above short-term risk factors when choosing SDV.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Prevalence and Trends

Vulvoplasty is a minority of feminizing genital surgeries in the reported series. In one U.S. center, 32% (35/110) of patients undergoing primary feminizing genital surgery chose SDV.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In another program, vulvoplasty accounted for 27% (21/77) of procedures.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> In an early series, 39 of 486 consultations (8%) requested vulvoplasty.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In a Danish online survey (134 complete responses), 26% preferred vulvoplasty.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

---

## Conversion to Full-Depth Vaginoplasty

A preoperative counseling point is whether future conversion to full-depth vaginoplasty is possible. Vulvoplasty preserves some penile and scrotal skin that could theoretically be used for future canal creation. Conversion typically requires alternative tissue sources (peritoneal flaps, intestinal grafts) because some donor tissue is consumed in the primary vulvoplasty.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> Conversion may be technically feasible, but it is a more complex secondary procedure than a primary vaginoplasty, and patients are counseled accordingly.

---

## Postoperative Care

Postoperative care is simpler than after full-depth vaginoplasty:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- No vaginal dilation is required.
- Wound care with sitz baths
- Foley catheter for approximately 5–7 days.
- Activity is restricted for several weeks.
- Surgeon follow-up is regular during the first year. Routine follow-up beyond the first year is typically not required.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Hormone therapy (estradiol) continues after gonadectomy to prevent osteoporosis and other sequelae of hypogonadism.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Prostate screening** remains relevant, and the approach to prostate examination differs in vulvoplasty-only patients compared with those with a vaginal canal.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

---

## Practitioner Attitudes

In a survey of WPATH-affiliated practitioners (198 responses, 20.7% response rate), 41.7% of surgeons had performed vulvoplasty, and "patient request" was the most common reason for recommending it. For patients aged 18–21, surgeons were more likely to agree and perform it (30.4%) or to reject it (32.1%), whereas other practitioners were more often "unsure" (68.5%).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Evidence Limitations

The evidence base consists primarily of single-center retrospective case series with small samples, short follow-up and non-validated outcome measures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Outcome reporting for feminizing genital surgery is heterogeneous. The GenderCOS project agreed a core outcome set of 11 outcomes, six of them patient-reported, to standardize future studies.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
