# Vagina-Preserving Metoidioplasty

**Vagina-preserving metoidioplasty** is a variant of standard metoidioplasty in which the vaginal canal is intentionally left intact while the hormonally hypertrophied clitoris is released and elongated to create a neophallus.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> It is designed for transmasculine and nonbinary individuals who desire masculinised external genitalia and/or standing urination while retaining the ability for receptive vaginal intercourse.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

Unlike standard ("full") metoidioplasty, which typically includes vaginectomy, urethral lengthening (UL), and scrotoplasty as a single-stage procedure, vagina-preserving metoidioplasty omits the vaginectomy and may or may not include UL and scrotoplasty, depending on patient goals.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

This is the dedicated atlas page. For the broader variant-GGAS framework, see [Non-Binary / Nullification Gender-Affirming Surgery](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/non-binary-nullification). For the related vagina-preserving phalloplasty options, see [Shaft-Only Phalloplasty with Vaginal Preservation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/shaft-only-phalloplasty) and [Phallus-Preserving Vaginoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/phallus-preserving-vaginoplasty). For the vaginectomy component (omitted here), see [Vaginectomy / Colpectomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/vaginectomy).

## Concept and Spectrum

Standard metoidioplasty (Belgrade / Djordjevic) is a one-stage procedure that creates a neophallus from the testosterone-enlarged clitoris, lengthens the urethra to the tip of the neophallus, constructs a scrotum from the labia majora with testicular implants, and removes the vagina.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> Vagina-preserving metoidioplasty covers a spectrum of variants:

| Variant | Clitoral release | UL | Scrotoplasty | Vaginectomy | Standing urination |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Simple metoidioplasty (clitoral release only)** | ✓ | — | — | — | No |
| **Meta + UL, no vaginectomy** | ✓ | ✓ | optional | — | Yes (target) |
| **Meta + scrotoplasty, no vaginectomy** | ✓ | optional | ✓ | — | Variable |
| **Full meta without vaginectomy** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | — | Yes (target) |
| **Standard (full) meta (Belgrade)** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | **✓** | Yes (target) |

### Indications

- **Nonbinary identity.** Desire both neophallus and vagina.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Desire for receptive vaginal intercourse**.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Avoidance of vaginectomy morbidity.** Vaginectomy carries 10% major perioperative and 12% major postoperative complication rates (Nikkels 2019 n = 143).<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- **Absence of vaginal dysphoria.**
- **Desire for standing urination.** Achievable with UL even without vaginectomy, but at substantially higher complication cost (see below).

## Preoperative Requirements

Testosterone therapy precedes metoidioplasty to induce clitoral hypertrophy. The institutional series of Lin-Brande required at least 1 year of testosterone before surgery.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Target levels are in the physiologic male range (300–1,000 ng/dL).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the Bordas 2021 series (n = 813, Belgrade center), mean neophallus length after metoidioplasty was 5.6 cm (range 4.8–10.2 cm).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Surgical Technique

Core steps parallel standard metoidioplasty, with omission of the vaginectomy.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[8]](#ref8)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Step 1 — Clitoral release and lengthening

1. **Degloving.** Clitoral skin (prepuce / hood) separated from the clitoral body, exposing the corpora.
2. **Division of the suspensory ligament.** Superficial suspensory ligament divided to allow the clitoral body to project further anteriorly and inferiorly, maximising phallic length.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
3. **Division of the urethral plate.** Short urethral plate (ventral chordee-like structure) tethering the clitoris divided, allowing further straightening and lengthening.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Step 2 — Urethral lengthening (optional)

If standing urination is desired, the urethra must be extended from its native position to the tip of the neophallus.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- **Belgrade technique.** Urethral defect bridged with buccal mucosa graft (harvested from inner cheek); anterior vaginal wall (AVW) flap mobilised and combined with labia minora flaps to construct the ventral neourethra, tubularised over a catheter.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Ring flap technique (Demzik 2021).** Bilateral anteriorly based labia minora and vestibular flaps create the neourethra without buccal mucosa. In the reported 52 patients the technique was combined with vaginectomy; urethrocutaneous fistula developed in 13% and stricture in 8% at median follow-up 22 months.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Vaginal-mucosal graft (Nakamura 2025).** This approach uses vaginal mucosal grafts for UL and avoids buccal donor-site morbidity. In the 7-patient series it was performed with hysterectomy, oophorectomy and vaginal closure, so it does not apply to vagina-preserving procedures.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

**Technical challenge in vagina-preserving UL.** The AVW flap is a tissue source for urethral construction in the Belgrade technique. When the vagina is preserved, the flap must be harvested without compromising vaginal integrity, which constrains the available tissue. This has been proposed as an explanation for the higher urethral-complication rates (see below).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

### Step 3 — Shaft coverage

Neophallus covered with local skin flaps, typically from labia minora. Medial aspect of contralateral labium minus is de-epithelialised and wrapped around the urethra for ventral shaft coverage.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### Step 4 — Scrotoplasty (optional)

If desired, labia majora rotated anteriorly and superiorly to create a neoscrotum using the Ghent / Hoebeke technique; see [Scrotoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/scrotoplasty).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> In vagina-preserving procedures, the scrotum is constructed around the preserved vaginal introitus. Testicular implants placed in staged fashion (approximately 6 mo later); see [Testicular Implants (Gender-Affirming)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/testicular-implants).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Step 5 — Vaginal preservation

Vaginal canal left completely intact. No colpocleisis or mucosal excision. The introitus lies between or posterior to the neoscrotal structures if scrotoplasty is performed, or in its native position if it is omitted.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## The Critical Issue — Urethral Complications with Vaginal Preservation

A systematic review of variant genital surgery found a higher risk of urethral complications when the vagina was preserved in both phalloplasty and metoidioplasty.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> The main supporting data are below.

### Phalloplasty data — Massie 2017 (the foundational signal)

In a single-center retrospective study of 224 patients undergoing phalloplasty with UL, 215 had vaginectomy and only 9 had vaginal preservation. Urethral complications occurred in:<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

- 27% with vaginectomy versus 67% with vaginal preservation (OR 0.18, p = 0.02)
- Stricture OR 0.25 (p = 0.047), decreased with vaginectomy
- Fistula OR 0.13 (p = 0.004), decreased with vaginectomy

**Proposed mechanism.** The authors attribute the difference most likely to vaginectomy affording additional vascularized vestibular tissue (a bulbospongiosus flap) for coverage of the horizontal urethroplasty suture line.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

### Metoidioplasty data — Waterschoot 2021 (n = 74)

Urethral complications of any kind occurred in 56.8% overall in this retrospective series.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

- Additional urethral lengthening (AUL, performed in 48.6%) was a significant predictor of complications:
  - OR 15.5 for all urethral complications
  - OR 24.5 for strictures
  - OR 6.07 for all fistulas
- Concomitant vaginectomy was among the predictors tested; the abstract reports only AUL and smoking as significant.
- Smoking was a predictor of fistula formation (OR 6.54).

### Claeys 2025 SR synthesis

The review included 23 case series, all at high risk of bias. Both phalloplasty and metoidioplasty carried a higher risk of urethral complications when the vagina was preserved.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Complications in masculinizing variant surgeries primarily arose from the extended urethra, which could be mitigated through:

- **Primary perineal urethrostomy** (accepting seated voiding).
- **Omitting UL entirely** (the simple-metoidioplasty path).

### Fundamental trade-off

Standing urination is associated with a higher complication rate in vagina-preserving procedures. Vaginectomy provides tissue that can reinforce the urethral suture line, and this tissue is not available when the vagina is kept (Massie, above).

## Outcomes

### Satisfaction

**Bordas 2021 Belgrade n = 813 (largest metoidioplasty series):**<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- Of 655 patients who answered the questionnaire, 79% were totally satisfied and 20% mainly satisfied.
- All patients reported voiding in the standing position and good sexual arousal of the neophallus; penetrative intercourse was not possible because of neophallus size.

Series primarily included standard metoidioplasty with vaginectomy; vagina-preserving-specific satisfaction data are extremely limited.

### Sexual function

- **Erogenous sensation.** In the Robinson 2021 multicenter survey (n = 129; metoidioplasty only, n = 32), erogenous sensation was rated 4.8/5 after metoidioplasty versus 3.4/5 after phalloplasty (p < .00001).<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- In the 82-patient Belgrade series, all patients reported preserved sensation and normal postoperative erection of the neophallus.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- Some studies report successful penetrative intercourse after metoidioplasty, but the Bordas series found it was not possible because of small neophallus size (mean 5.6 cm).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

### PROs with vs without UL

**de Rooij 2021 Dutch cross-sectional survey (n = 102; 28 metoidioplasty, 74 phalloplasty; UL in 56):**<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

- No significant differences in satisfaction between those who chose UL and those who did not (appearance, voiding, QoL, masculinity, sexuality).
- Significantly higher complications and reoperations in the UL group:
  - Mean complications 2.7 versus 1
  - Mean reoperations 1.6 versus 0.4 (both p < .001).

### Sexuality after GGAS

**van de Grift 2017 (longitudinal, n = 21, mostly phalloplasty).** After genital GAS, transmasculine patients were more sexually active, used their genitals more frequently during sex (31% preop to 78% postop), and reported improvements in arousability, sexual interest, and pleasure.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

## Postoperative Care — Vagina-Preserving Metoidioplasty-Specific

- **STI screening.** For patients post-metoidioplasty without vaginectomy who have receptive vaginal sex, STI testing with physician- or patient-collected vaginal swabs.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Urologic follow-up.** Ongoing with a urologist familiar with GAS, particularly for patients with UL. Urgent referral is indicated for urinary retention, post-void dribbling, pelvic pain with fullness, and recurrent UTI.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Gynecologic care.** A preserved vagina requires ongoing surveillance; cervical-cancer screening if the cervix is retained (no hysterectomy).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Testosterone-related vaginal atrophy.** Testosterone causes atrophy that may affect comfort during receptive intercourse. Topical vaginal estrogen may be considered for symptomatic atrophy, though data in this population are limited.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Simple Metoidioplasty (Without UL) — The Lowest-Risk Vagina-Preserving Option

For patients who do not prioritise standing urination, simple metoidioplasty (clitoral release only, without UL) combined with vaginal preservation is the lowest-risk variant. Lin-Brande 2021 reported 12 patients undergoing simple metoidioplasty and 21 with UL; complications were reported for the UL group (10 of 21 had a postoperative complication, 2 required revision urethroplasty).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> In a separate retrospective cohort of 68 men who had genital surgery without UL, urologic complications occurred in 12%, and storage and voiding function was unchanged.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

This option provides:

- A masculinized external genital appearance (released, prominent neophallus).
- Preserved erogenous sensation (native clitoral innervation intact).
- Preserved vaginal function.
- No urethroplasty, and therefore no neourethral fistula or stricture risk.
- Seated voiding (native meatus position unchanged).
- Retention of the option of later UL or phalloplasty.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Broader Context — Individually Customised Procedures

Ascha 2024 described techniques for individually customized genital procedures (phallus-preserving vaginoplasty, vagina-preserving phalloplasty, and genital removal with perineal urethrostomy; 16 patients). The authors suggest that these procedures may better affirm the identities of some gender-diverse patients and may preserve desired sexual function of natal genitalia. Vagina-preserving metoidioplasty was not among the procedures described.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

**Brown 2025 survey n = 248.** In this survey of people who had considered but not undergone surgery, nonbinary individuals were significantly more likely to prioritize retaining a vaginal canal to receive penetration and for gender-identity affirmation; both transgender men and nonbinary individuals rated maintaining tactile and erogenous sensation as a top priority.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> These preferences are relevant to preoperative counseling about vagina-preserving options.

---

## Evidence Gaps

- **Long-term urethral complication rates in vagina-preserving metoidioplasty specifically.** Most data are extrapolated from a small phalloplasty comparison (Massie 2017, 9 patients with vaginal preservation) or mixed cohorts (Waterschoot 2021).
- **Optimal UL technique when the vagina is preserved.** The AVW-flap-tissue-source constraint is the central mechanistic challenge.
- **Long-term vaginal function and sexual satisfaction** with receptive intercourse after metoidioplasty.
- **Comparative satisfaction** between vagina-preserving and standard metoidioplasty in matched cohorts.
- **Impact of testosterone-induced vaginal atrophy** on long-term vaginal function.
- **Rates of subsequent vaginectomy or conversion** to standard metoidioplasty / phalloplasty.
- **Validated PROMs** specific to this population.
