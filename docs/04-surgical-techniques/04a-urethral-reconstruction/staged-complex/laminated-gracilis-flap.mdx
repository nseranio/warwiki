# Laminated Gracilis Flap Urethroplasty

A **prelaminated gracilis–buccal mucosa composite** is a rare staged salvage option for a devastated bulbar urethra when a viable native plate or local graft bed is lacking. Unlike a gracilis flap placed beneath a conventional onlay in the same operation, the mucosal graft is allowed to vascularize on the muscle before transfer.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Terminology

Pribaz and Fine introduced *prelamination* for the implantation of tissue or other devices into a flap before transfer, and proposed restricting *prefabrication* to the implantation of a vascular pedicle.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> By that definition the gracilis–buccal composite is a prelaminated flap: the muscle keeps its native pedicle and only the mucosal lining is added. The 2022 multicenter report uses the term "prefabricated composite gracilis–buccal mucosal flap" for the same operation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Indications

The published cases involve a devastated bulbar urethra: bulbar necrosis, failed fasciocutaneous repairs or a "watering can" perineum, in which reliable local tissue is scarce.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In the five-patient series, all men had a suprapubic tube and at least one prior failed repair (1–9).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Gracilis is a reliable, expendable muscle that lies outside many pelvic radiation fields, which is why it is also used for interposition in radiated urethral beds and in complex fistula repair (see [Gracilis Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/gracilis)). The prelaminated composite itself has been reported only in the devastated bulbar urethra.

Bulbar urethral necrosis, the first of these settings, is a vascular complication of pelvic fracture urethral injury. In a tertiary series of 145 men with bulbar urethral necrosis treated from 1995 to 2023, necrosis occurred in 10.8% of all pelvic fracture urethral injuries and 40.3% of complex injuries; the authors proposed a radiological classification based on retrograde urethrography.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> A further case report described the same two-stage reconstruction in a 67-year-old man with a 5-cm missing bulbar segment and a double block after trauma and a previous urethral diverticulectomy.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Preoperative Evaluation

- **Urinary drainage and infection.** Suprapubic drainage is established and infection controlled before stage 1.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Defect mapping.** Combined antegrade and retrograde imaging defines the length of the missing segment and any proximal or distal block; in the 2025 case it showed a 5-cm gap with obstruction at both ends.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Donor sites.** The oral cavity is assessed for two buccal grafts, and the medial thigh for prior surgery, scarring or vascular disease that could compromise the gracilis pedicle (see [Gracilis Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/gracilis)).
- **Counseling.** The operation needs at least two stages, a buccal and a thigh donor site, and suprapubic drainage between stages; the alternatives below are discussed at the same visit.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Relevant Gracilis Anatomy

The dominant pedicle of the gracilis is usually the descending branch of the medial circumflex femoral artery; a direct branch from the profunda femoris has also been described. It is typically found about 10 cm inferior to the pubic tubercle, between adductor longus and adductor magnus.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Study-by-study variation in pedicle origin and entry point is summarized on the [Gracilis Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/gracilis) page.

## Reconstructive Concept

After controlling infection and establishing urinary drainage, stage 1 creates two independently supported mucosal surfaces: BMG quilted to the corporal or urethral bed forms a dorsal plate and temporary augmented perineal outlet; a second BMG is quilted to exposed distal gracilis. Once both surfaces have healed, the muscle–graft composite is mobilized on its proximal vascular pedicle and tunneled into the perineum. Its mucosal edges are joined to the dorsal plate to reconstruct the ventral urethra. The result is two mucosa-lined hemi-plates joined to each other, not a circumferential muscle tube.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The published initial case used an eight-week interval; the later five-patient cohort averaged 6.2 weeks. The interval in the later series ranged from 1 to 10 weeks. Healing, graft take and patient factors determine timing, and an observed leak should resolve before catheter removal.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Operative Steps

The sequence below follows the published descriptions; it is a summary for orientation and not a substitute for the original reports.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

**Stage 1**

1. Excise the devastated, scarred or necrotic segment back to healthy proximal and distal urethra.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
2. Quilt a buccal mucosal graft onto the corpora cavernosa, or onto the residual urethral bed, to form the dorsal neourethral plate, creating a temporary augmented perineal urethrostomy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
3. Expose the distal gracilis through a medial thigh incision and quilt a second buccal graft onto the muscle surface to form the future ventral plate.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
4. Keep suprapubic drainage in place while both grafts mature.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

**Stage 2**

1. After graft take is confirmed, at 8 weeks in the index case, a mean of 6.2 weeks in the series and 12 weeks in the 2025 case, mobilize the gracilis–BMG composite on its proximal pedicle.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
2. Tunnel the composite into the perineum.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
3. Anastomose the mucosal edges of the gracilis plate to the edges of the dorsal plate to close the neourethra.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
4. Obtain contrast imaging before catheter removal; in the 2025 case, urethrography at five weeks showed no leak.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Evidence and Counseling

The 2022 two-center series included five men with prior failed repairs and mean stricture length 7.2 cm. No recurrence was reported at mean 61-month follow-up; four completed postoperative questionnaires. This supports feasibility in highly selected patients but does not show superiority over other salvage options or a predictable success rate. Counseling covers donor-site morbidity, multiple operations, recurrent narrowing or fistula, and the alternatives of a permanent perineal outlet or urinary diversion.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The index patient, a 57-year-old man with penetrating trauma to a previously reconstructed urethra, had normal voiding and continence at 8 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The 2025 patient voided normally without stricture or complication at 12 months.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> The published human experience identified for this page consists of two case reports and one five-patient series.

## Complications

- **Thigh donor site.** In a series of 104 gracilis donor sites, early complications occurred in 9.6%.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> The series was not specific to urethral reconstruction.
- **Graft failure, fistula or recurrent narrowing.** These are the expected failure modes of any two-plate reconstruction and are listed in the counseling above.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Staged burden.** The patient lives with a perineal outlet and suprapubic drainage between stages.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Comparison With Alternatives

| Option | Evidence | Reported result | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prelaminated gracilis–BMG composite | 5-patient series; 2 case reports<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | No recurrence at mean 61 months in the series | Staged; muscle and buccal donor sites |
| [Ventral BMG with same-stage gracilis support](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/vanni-zinman-ventral-bmg-gracilis) | Single-center series, n = 20 and n = 30<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | Success 80% at mean 40 months; 76.7% at mean 32 months, with 23.3% later needing an AUS for incontinence | Single stage; requires a usable dorsal urethral or corporal surface |
| Pedicled tubularized penile flaps for bulbar necrosis | Series, n = 41<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | Primary success 66%, secondary 78%; preputial tube 76%, Q-flap 75%, distal penile skin 40%; complications 14.6% | Requires adequate penile skin |
| Colonic substitution (entero-urethroplasty) | Case report<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> | Ascending colon substitution described in one man with ischemic bulbar necrosis after failed anastomotic urethroplasty | Adds a bowel anastomosis |
| [Perineal urethrostomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/seven-flap-perineal-urethrostomy) | Retrospective comparison, 45 PU vs 64 BMG urethroplasty<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | Satisfied or very satisfied 86.7% vs 89.1% | Single stage; permanent perineal meatus |

None of these options has been compared with the prelaminated composite in a controlled study. A staged composite is most relevant when penile skin is unusable, the dorsal bed cannot support a same-stage graft, and the patient declines a permanent perineal outlet or diversion.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
