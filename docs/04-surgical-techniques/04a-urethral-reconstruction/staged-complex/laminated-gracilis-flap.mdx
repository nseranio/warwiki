# Laminated Gracilis Flap Urethroplasty

A **prelaminated gracilis–buccal mucosa composite** is a rare staged salvage option for a devastated bulbar urethra when a viable native plate or local graft bed is lacking. Unlike a gracilis flap placed beneath a conventional onlay in the same operation, the mucosal graft is allowed to vascularize on the muscle before transfer.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Indications

The published cases involve a devastated bulbar urethra: bulbar necrosis, failed fasciocutaneous repairs or a "watering can" perineum, in which reliable local tissue is scarce.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In the five-patient series, all men had a suprapubic tube and at least one prior failed repair (1–9).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Gracilis is a reliable, expendable muscle that lies outside many pelvic radiation fields, which is why it is also used for interposition in radiated urethral beds and in complex fistula repair (see [Gracilis Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/gracilis)). The prelaminated composite itself has been reported only in the devastated bulbar urethra.

## Reconstructive Concept

After controlling infection and establishing urinary drainage, stage 1 creates two independently supported mucosal surfaces: BMG quilted to the corporal or urethral bed forms a dorsal plate and temporary augmented perineal outlet; a second BMG is quilted to exposed distal gracilis. Once both surfaces have healed, the muscle–graft composite is mobilized on its proximal vascular pedicle and tunneled into the perineum. Its mucosal edges are joined to the dorsal plate to reconstruct the ventral urethra. The result is two mucosa-lined hemi-plates joined to each other, not a circumferential muscle tube.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The published initial case used an eight-week interval; the later five-patient cohort averaged 6.2 weeks. The interval in the later series ranged from 1 to 10 weeks. Healing, graft take and patient factors determine timing, and an observed leak should resolve before catheter removal.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Evidence and Counseling

The 2022 two-center series included five men with prior failed repairs and mean stricture length 7.2 cm. No recurrence was reported at mean 61-month follow-up; four completed postoperative questionnaires. This supports feasibility in highly selected patients but does not show superiority over other salvage options or a predictable success rate. Counseling covers donor-site morbidity, multiple operations, recurrent narrowing or fistula, and the alternatives of a permanent perineal outlet or urinary diversion.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
