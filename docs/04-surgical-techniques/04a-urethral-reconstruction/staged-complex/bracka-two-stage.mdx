# Bracka Two-Stage Urethroplasty

The **Bracka two-stage urethroplasty** is a two-stage reconstruction described by Arul Bracka in 1995 for complex hypospadias and later adopted for adult urethral stricture disease.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> It is distinguished from the classic [Johanson two-stage urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/johanson-two-stage) by the active placement of a free graft at Stage 1 to create a new urethral plate, rather than marsupializing the native urethra. The original two-stage free-graft concept was first described by Cloutier in 1962, but Bracka popularized and refined it into the modern paradigm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For the BMG / oral-mucosa graft material details, see [Buccal Mucosa Graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa). For the Johanson-style marsupialization variant, see [Johanson Two-Stage Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/johanson-two-stage). For the related Kulkarni one-sided dorsolateral approach for panurethral disease, see [Kulkarni One-Sided Dorsolateral BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/kulkarni-one-stage).

---

## Fundamental Principles

A free graft (originally inner preputial skin, now preferably buccal mucosa) is placed onto the corpora cavernosa at Stage 1, allowed to mature and revascularize, then tubularized into a neourethra at Stage 2. This creates a grafted plate when native tissue is inadequate; modern staged operations often combine graft augmentation with preservation of useful native plate rather than fit a strict eponym distinction.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Surgical Technique

### Stage 1 — Chordee correction, scar excision, and graft placement

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1. Degloving | Circumcoronal or ventral midline incision; degloving; full exposure of the strictured / scarred urethral segment |
| 2. Chordee correction | Ventral curvature corrected. In hypospadias cases, may require urethral plate transection and dorsal plication. In stricture cases, preserve usable native plate and vascular support; excision depends on tissue quality, hair and the planned reconstruction<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| 3. Scar excision | Remove tissue that cannot contribute to a viable reconstruction, while preserving suitable plate and graft bed; a universal wide excision of all fibrosis is not required<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| 4. Proximal urethrostomy | Cutaneous urethrostomy created proximally for voiding during the maturation period<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| 5. Graft harvest | Originally inner preputial skin; modern practice: buccal mucosa from the inner cheek (or inner lip for glanular reconstruction)<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| 6. Graft inset | Defat and quilt the graft (multiple interrupted fixation sutures) onto the ventral surface of the corpora cavernosa, creating a flat neourethral plate from the proximal urethrostomy to the glans tip<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| 7. Glans split (hypospadias) | In hypospadias cases, glans is split ventrally so that the graft can extend to the tip and a terminal slit-like meatus can be created at Stage 2<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| 8. Bolster dressing | Tie-over or compressive bolster over the graft to ensure contact with the corporal bed and promote imbibition / inosculation<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |

### Maturation interval

Healing and the plate are assessed before closure. Staged-repair intervals vary. The EAU guideline suggests at least four to six months before tubularization when the first stage has healed uneventfully (weak recommendation).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[15]](#ref15)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> In Palminteri’s small LS cohort, a first-to-last-stage interval under 12 months was associated with failure (adjusted OR 27; 95% CI 1.36–537.55). This is imprecise observational evidence and does not establish a 12-month minimum.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

### Stage 2 — Tubularization

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1. Graft assessment | Inspect matured graft. Complete take in ~ 88% of cases; focal scar / contracture can be patched with additional graft before proceeding<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| 2. Tubularization | Tubularize the graft plate over a catheter (8–10 Fr in children, 14–16 Fr in adults) with running or interrupted absorbable suture (e.g., 6-0 polyglactin) |
| 3. Waterproofing layer | Mobilize a dartos fascia or tunica vaginalis flap as a second layer over the suture line. Snodgrass: one fistula among 18 second-stage patients with a barrier layer; not a randomized comparison<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| 4. Glansplasty | Close glans wings over the neourethra to create a conical glans with a vertical slit neomeatus<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| 5. Closure | Close penile skin; catheter for ~ 7–14 days (children) or up to 3 weeks (adults) |

## Indications

- **Proximal hypospadias with severe chordee** requiring urethral plate transection.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Hypospadias cripples.** Patients with multiple (3–16) prior failed hypospadias repairs.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Complex penile urethral strictures.** Particularly circumferential strictures requiring complete urethral plate reconstruction.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Lichen sclerosus (BXO)** with adverse local conditions precluding a suitable single-stage oral-graft repair; avoid genital skin.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Failed prior urethroplasty** with a scarred or absent urethral plate.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Severe proximal hypospadias** as primary repair when one-stage techniques are not feasible.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Graft Material — Preputial Skin vs Buccal Mucosa

The original Bracka description used inner preputial skin. Contemporary practice favors buccal mucosa:<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

| Feature | Inner Preputial Skin | Buccal Mucosa |
|---|---|---|
| Complication rate (Bracka hypospadias) | 31% | 20% |
| Cosmetic result (HOPE scale) | Good | Superior |
| LS-related recurrence | Genital skin unsuitable in LS | Oral mucosa preferred, but recurrence can still occur |
| Graft take | Depends on bed and fixation | Depends on bed and fixation; small histologic series are not a universal 100% take estimate |
| Histology after maturation | Keratinization possible | Minimal keratinization, good vascularity |
| Availability | Requires suitable prepuce | May be limited by oral disease or prior harvest |
| Thickness for glanular urethra | May be too thick | Inner-lip mucosa is thinner but has its own donor morbidity; select individually |

Manasherova compared 108 patients with preputial grafts to 112 with BMG in Bracka hypospadias repair: 23/112 (20%) versus 33/108 (31%) complications, with more favorable cosmetic scores in the later BMG cohort. The graft groups were treated in different eras (2013–2016 versus 2001–2013), limiting causal comparison.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> In 31 patients, Mokhless reported excellent BMG uptake within 5 days, good vascularization at 6 months, and only mild focal keratinization after prolonged air exposure.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

The AUA urethral stricture guideline (amended 2023) states that surgeons should use oral mucosa as the first choice when using grafts (Expert Opinion) and may use buccal or lingual mucosa as equivalent alternatives (Strong Recommendation; Grade A). It states that surgeons should not use genital skin for reconstruction in lichen sclerosus-proven stricture (Strong Recommendation; Grade B).<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> The EAU guideline gives the same advice on genital skin in LS (strong).<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

## Outcomes — Hypospadias Repair

| Series | n | Population | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gill / Hameed 2011<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | 100 | Hypospadias cripples (3–16 prior surgeries) | Meatal tip 94%, straightening 96%, fistula 9% |
| Snodgrass / Elmore 2004<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | 25 first stage; 20 second stage | Failed hypospadias (avg 4.4 prior surgeries) | Graft take 22/25; one fistula in 18 with barrier flap; four partial glans dehiscences |
| Manasherova 2020<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | 220 | Proximal hypospadias (preputial vs BMG) | Complications: preputial 31% vs BMG 20% |
| Wani 2020 RCT<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | 142 | Bracka vs Byars (proximal hypospadias) | Fistula: Bracka 6.8% vs Byars 10.2% (p = 0.63) |
| Johal 2006<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | 62 | Primary severe hypospadias | 100% graft take, low complications |
| Castagnetti 2013<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | 18 | Primary proximal hypospadias | Best parent-reported cosmetic scores among 4 techniques |

## Outcomes — Adult Urethral Stricture

| Series | n | Population | Success | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Greenwell 1999<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | 26 | Penile strictures (circumferential) | Two-stage much better than one-stage | — |
| Palminteri 2002<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> | 24 | Complex bulbar strictures | 23/24 reported stricture-free | Median 18 mo |
| Furr 2021<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> | 49 | Staged anterior urethroplasty | BMG-only 96.4% vs STSG 53% | Median 57 mo |
| Figler 2018<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | 20 first stage; 12 second stage | Mixed, including LS and failed hypospadias | Second-stage fistula 1/12, dehiscence 2/12, meatal stenosis 1/12 | Median 520 days |
| Palminteri 2022<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | 25 followed of 35 | LS penile strictures (multi-stage) | 20/25 no further stricture treatment; interval association is observational | Median 134 mo |

Furr reported 96.4% long-term success with BMG-only versus 53% with any STSG, but STSG patients had longer or more often panurethral disease and longer follow-up. This is an association, not an isolated causal graft effect. Of 85 first-stage patients, 57 completed both stages and 49 entered follow-up analysis; patients lacking at least one year were excluded from the long-term estimate. Late recurrence (median 78 months among failures) supports extended follow-up.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

## Complications

- **Fistula formation** is the most common complication (5–9% in the cited series). A vascularized barrier layer is commonly used, but these series do not show that it reduces fistula rates.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Wound and glans dehiscence.** Partial glans dehiscence occurred in 4 of 25 boys in Snodgrass's series (all with cheek graft in the glanular urethra) and wound dehiscence in 2 of 12 second-stage patients in Figler's. Snodgrass suggested that thinner inner-lip graft may be a better source for the glanular urethra, but no comparative study has tested this.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Meatal stenosis** occurred in 3–8% in the cited series.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Graft contracture or scar.** Focal scar or contracture requiring patching before tubularization occurred in 3 of 25 patients (12%) in Snodgrass's series.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Urethral stricture** occurred in about 1% in Wani's randomized comparison.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Diverticulum** occurred in 0–2%.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Residual chordee** occurred in 0–5%.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Bracka vs Byars Two-Stage Repair

A randomized comparative trial (Wani 2020, n = 142, proximal hypospadias with severe chordee) compared Bracka (n = 74) versus Byars (n = 68). Conceptual difference: Bracka uses a free graft placed on the corpora; Byars uses a pedicled dorsal preputial flap transposed ventrally.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

| Endpoint | Bracka | Byars | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fistula | 6.8% | 10.2% | 0.63 |
| Meatal stenosis | 4% | 3% | NS |
| Stricture | 1% | 1% | NS |
| Diverticulum | 0% | 2% | NS |

No statistically significant difference was detected; the trial did not establish equivalence. Choice also depends on anatomy, donor tissue and experience.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Bracka vs Classic Johanson

| Feature | Classic Johanson | Bracka Two-Stage |
|---|---|---|
| Stage 1 concept | Marsupialization (urethra opened to skin) | Free graft placement on corpora |
| Graft at Stage 1 | No (originally) | Yes (preputial skin or BMG) |
| Urethral plate quality | Relies on native tissue | Creates new plate from graft |
| Suitability for LS | Limited (native skin diseased) | Oral mucosa preferred; LS-related recurrence remains possible |
| Need for additional stages | Depends on plate quality | May require regrafting or delayed closure; counsel before starting |
| Primary application | Adult urethral strictures | Hypospadias + strictures |

## Modern Modifications and Hybrid Approaches

- **Mitsukawa modification.** Combines a modified Bracka method (oral-mucosal graft) with a modified Byars flap of the dorsal foreskin for severe proximal hypospadias requiring urethral plate resection.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Palminteri 2-stage BMG urethroplasty.** Adapts the Bracka concept to adult bulbar strictures with a 2 × 6 cm BMG sutured to the urethral mucosal plate margin: 23/24 reported stricture-free success.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Johanson-Bracka hybrid.** In contemporary stricture surgery, the first stage often combines Johanson marsupialization with Bracka-type BMG grafting. If a Johanson-only first stage is performed without grafting, a three-stage approach may be needed (marsupialization, then grafting, then tubularization).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Tunica vaginalis as alternative free graft.** Rosito rabbit-model work shows good graft uptake with minimal retraction, stratified non-keratinized epithelium development (metaplasia), and good vascularization, which is experimental evidence that does not establish routine human substitution when oral mucosa is unavailable.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

---

## Key Takeaways

Staged oral-graft reconstruction is used when tissue quality makes a single-stage repair unsuitable. The EAU 2026 guideline advises offering staged urethroplasty to men with complex anterior stricture who are not suitable for a single stage (weak), and advises against staged bulbar urethroplasty for LS when a single stage is possible (weak). It also advises warning patients that staging may take more than two operations; up to 45.5% of men elect not to proceed to second-stage tubularization.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> The AUA guideline lets surgeons choose one-stage or multi-stage techniques for long multi-segment strictures (Moderate Recommendation; Grade C).<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
