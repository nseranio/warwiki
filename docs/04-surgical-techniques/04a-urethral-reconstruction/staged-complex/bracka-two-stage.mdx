# Bracka Two-Stage Urethroplasty

The **Bracka two-stage urethroplasty** is a landmark reconstructive technique originally described by **Arul Bracka in 1995** for the repair of complex hypospadias and subsequently widely adopted for **adult urethral stricture disease**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> It is distinguished from the classic [Johanson two-stage urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/johanson-two-stage) by the **active placement of a free graft at Stage 1** to create a new urethral plate, rather than simply marsupializing the native urethra. The original two-stage free-graft concept was first described by Cloutier in 1962, but Bracka popularized and refined it into the modern paradigm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For the BMG / oral-mucosa graft material details, see [Buccal Mucosa Graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa). For the Johanson-style marsupialization variant, see [Johanson Two-Stage Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/johanson-two-stage). For the related Kulkarni one-sided dorsolateral approach for panurethral disease, see [Kulkarni One-Sided Dorsolateral BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/kulkarni-one-stage).

---

## Fundamental Principles

A **free graft** (originally inner preputial skin, now preferably buccal mucosa) is placed onto the corpora cavernosa at Stage 1, allowed to mature and revascularize, then tubularized into a neourethra at Stage 2. This produces a **wider, well-vascularized urethral plate** than the Johanson marsupialization, which relies on native (often scarred or diseased) urethral tissue.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Surgical Technique

### Stage 1 — Chordee correction, scar excision, and graft placement

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1. Degloving | Circumcoronal or ventral midline incision; degloving; full exposure of the strictured / scarred urethral segment |
| 2. Chordee correction | Ventral curvature corrected. In hypospadias cases, may require urethral plate transection and dorsal plication. In stricture cases, all fibrotic spongiosal tissue is excised<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| 3. Scar excision | All visibly scarred / fibrotic / LS-affected tissue excised until healthy, well-vascularized tissue is reached<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| 4. Proximal urethrostomy | Cutaneous urethrostomy created proximally for voiding during the maturation period<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| 5. Graft harvest | Originally inner preputial skin; **modern practice: buccal mucosa** from the inner cheek (or inner lip for glanular reconstruction)<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| 6. Graft inset | Defat and **quilt** the graft (multiple interrupted fixation sutures) onto the ventral surface of the corpora cavernosa, creating a flat neourethral plate from the proximal urethrostomy to the glans tip<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| 7. Glans split (hypospadias) | In hypospadias cases, glans is split ventrally to allow the graft to extend to the tip — enabling creation of a terminal slit-like meatus at Stage 2<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| 8. Bolster dressing | Tie-over or compressive bolster over the graft to ensure contact with the corporal bed and promote imbibition / inosculation<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |

### Maturation interval

Minimum **6 months** to allow complete graft maturation and revascularization.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Palminteri found that an interval **&lt; 12 months** between first and last stages is an independent predictor of failure.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

### Stage 2 — Tubularization

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1. Graft assessment | Inspect matured graft. Complete take in ~ **88%** of cases; focal scar / contracture can be patched with additional graft before proceeding<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| 2. Tubularization | Tubularize the graft plate over a catheter (8–10 Fr in children, 14–16 Fr in adults) with running or interrupted absorbable suture (e.g., 6-0 polyglactin) |
| 3. Waterproofing layer | Mobilize a **dartos fascia or tunica vaginalis flap** as a second layer over the suture line. Snodgrass: fistula rate **5%** with a barrier layer<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| 4. Glansplasty | Close glans wings over the neourethra to create a conical glans with a vertical slit neomeatus<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| 5. Closure | Close penile skin; catheter for ~ 7–14 days (children) or up to 3 weeks (adults) |

## Indications

- **Proximal hypospadias with severe chordee** requiring urethral plate transection.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Hypospadias cripples** — patients with multiple (3–16) prior failed hypospadias repairs.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Complex penile urethral strictures** — particularly circumferential strictures requiring complete urethral plate reconstruction.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Lichen sclerosus (BXO)** — genital skin diseased, oral mucosa mandatory.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Failed prior urethroplasty** with a scarred or absent urethral plate.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Severe proximal hypospadias** as primary repair when one-stage techniques are not feasible.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Graft Material — Preputial Skin vs Buccal Mucosa

The original Bracka description used **inner preputial skin**. Modern practice has shifted decisively to **buccal mucosa**:<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

| Feature | Inner Preputial Skin | Buccal Mucosa |
|---|---|---|
| Complication rate (Bracka hypospadias) | 31% | **20%** |
| Cosmetic result (HOPE scale) | Good | **Superior** |
| Susceptibility to LS | Yes (skin) | **No** (mucosa) |
| Graft take | Good | **Excellent** (100% at 5 days) |
| Histology after maturation | Keratinization possible | Minimal keratinization, good vascularity |
| Availability | Requires intact prepuce | Always available (both cheeks) |
| Thickness for glanular urethra | May be too thick | Inner-lip mucosa thinner; preferred for glans |

Manasherova compared 108 patients with preputial grafts to 112 with BMG in Bracka hypospadias repair: **20% vs 31% complication rate** and superior cosmetic outcomes with BMG.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> Mokhless demonstrated that BMG shows excellent uptake within 5 days, develops good vascularization, and undergoes only mild focal keratinization after prolonged air exposure.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

The **AUA 2023 urethral stricture guideline amendment** recommends **oral mucosa as the first-choice graft material**, with buccal and lingual mucosa equivalent alternatives. **Genital skin should be avoided in lichen sclerosus** due to high long-term failure.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

## Outcomes — Hypospadias Repair

| Series | n | Population | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gill / Hameed 2011<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | 100 | Hypospadias cripples (3–16 prior surgeries) | Meatal tip 94%, straightening 96%, fistula **9%** |
| Snodgrass / Elmore 2004<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | 25 | Failed hypospadias (avg 4.4 prior surgeries) | Graft take 88%, fistula **5%**, no stricture / stenosis |
| Manasherova 2020<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | 220 | Proximal hypospadias (preputial vs BMG) | Complications: preputial 31% vs BMG **20%** |
| Wani 2020 RCT<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | 142 | Bracka vs Byars (proximal hypospadias) | Fistula: Bracka **6.8%** vs Byars 10.2% (p = 0.63) |
| Johal 2006<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | 62 | Primary severe hypospadias | 100% graft take, low complications |
| Castagnetti 2013<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | 18 | Primary proximal hypospadias | **Best cosmetic results** among 4 techniques |

## Outcomes — Adult Urethral Stricture

| Series | n | Population | Success | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Greenwell 1999<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | 26 | Penile strictures (circumferential) | Two-stage **much better** than one-stage | — |
| Palminteri 2002<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> | 24 | Complex bulbar strictures | **92.8%** | Median 18 mo |
| Furr 2021<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> | 49 | Staged anterior urethroplasty | **BMG-only 96.4%** vs STSG 53% | Median 57 mo |
| Figler 2018<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | 20 | LS + failed hypospadias | Fistula 8%, dehiscence 17%, stenosis 8% | Median 520 days |
| Palminteri 2022<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | 25 | LS penile strictures (multi-stage) | **80%** (interval &lt; 12 mo predicts failure) | Long-term |

A critical finding from Furr 2021: long-term success is **96.4% with BMG-only** but only **53% when STSG is incorporated**. All recurrences occurred after the initial 4-month cystoscopy with a **median time to recurrence of 78 months** — underscoring the need for prolonged follow-up.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

## Complications

- **Fistula formation** — most common, **5–9%**; reduced by use of a dartos / tunica vaginalis barrier flap.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Wound / glans dehiscence** — partial glans dehiscence ~ 6–17%, particularly in prepubertal boys when thicker cheek BMG is used for the glanular urethra; inner-lip graft preferable for this location.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Meatal stenosis** — 3–8%.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Graft contracture / scar** — ~ 12% may have focal scar or contracture requiring patching before tubularization.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Urethral stricture** — ~ 1% in hypospadias series.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Diverticulum** — rare, 0–2%.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Residual chordee** — 0–5%.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Bracka vs Byars Two-Stage Repair

The only randomized comparative trial (Wani 2020, n = 142, proximal hypospadias with severe chordee) compared Bracka (n = 74) vs Byars (n = 68). Key conceptual difference: Bracka uses a **free graft** placed on the corpora; Byars uses a **pedicled dorsal preputial flap** transposed ventrally.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

| Endpoint | Bracka | Byars | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fistula | 6.8% | 10.2% | 0.63 |
| Meatal stenosis | 4% | 3% | NS |
| Stricture | 1% | 1% | NS |
| Diverticulum | 0% | 2% | NS |

No statistically significant differences. Choice depends on surgeon preference and experience.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Bracka vs Classic Johanson

| Feature | Classic Johanson | Bracka Two-Stage |
|---|---|---|
| Stage 1 concept | Marsupialization (urethra opened to skin) | **Free graft placement on corpora** |
| Graft at Stage 1 | No (originally) | **Yes** (preputial skin or BMG) |
| Urethral plate quality | Relies on native tissue | **Creates new plate** from graft |
| Suitability for LS | Limited (native skin diseased) | **Yes** (BMG resistant to LS) |
| Need for third stage | Sometimes (if plate inadequate) | Rarely |
| Primary application | Adult urethral strictures | Hypospadias + strictures |

## Modern Modifications and Hybrid Approaches

- **Mitsukawa modification** — combines a modified Bracka method (oral-mucosal graft) with a modified Byars flap of the dorsal foreskin for severe proximal hypospadias requiring urethral plate resection.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Palminteri 2-stage BMG urethroplasty** — adapts the Bracka concept to adult bulbar strictures with a 2 × 6 cm BMG sutured to the urethral mucosal plate margin: **92.8%** success.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Johanson-Bracka hybrid** — in contemporary stricture surgery, the first stage often combines Johanson marsupialization with Bracka-type BMG grafting. If a Johanson-only first stage is performed without grafting, a three-stage approach may be needed (marsupialization → grafting → tubularization).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Tunica vaginalis as alternative free graft** — Rosito rabbit-model work shows good graft uptake with minimal retraction, stratified non-keratinized epithelium development (metaplasia), and good vascularization — a possible alternative when oral mucosa is unavailable.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

---

## Key Takeaways

The Bracka technique is versatile, reproducible, and applicable to the most challenging reconstructive scenarios. It is described as **"relatively easy to learn"** and applicable in difficult salvage cases.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The shift from preputial skin to buccal mucosa has further improved outcomes. The technique remains the **gold standard for staged urethral reconstruction when one-stage repair is not feasible**, supported by the AUA 2023 amendment's endorsement of multi-stage techniques with oral mucosal grafts for complex and long-segment strictures.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
