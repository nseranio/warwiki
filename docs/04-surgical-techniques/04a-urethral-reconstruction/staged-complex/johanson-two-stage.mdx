# Johanson Two-Stage Urethroplasty

The **Johanson two-stage urethroplasty** is a classic reconstructive technique for complex anterior urethral strictures, originally described by Bengt Johanson in 1953 for the management of penile urethral strictures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> It is used mainly when single-stage repair is not feasible.

For the BMG / oral-mucosa graft material, see [Buccal Mucosa Graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa). For the related panurethral one-sided BMG approach, see [Kulkarni One-Sided Dorsolateral BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/kulkarni-one-stage). For the variant with active graft placement at Stage 1 (used for hypospadias reoperations and for LS strictures unsuitable for a single stage), see [Bracka Two-Stage Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/bracka-two-stage). For permanent first-stage-only solutions, see [Blandy Perineal Urethrostomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/blandy-perineal-urethrostomy) and [Midline Perineal Urethrostomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/seven-flap-perineal-urethrostomy).

---

## Historical Background

Johanson originally described the procedure as a marsupialization-and-closure technique for penile urethral strictures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The concept was later modified by Leadbetter (the "Johanson-Leadbetter" technique) and by Fernandes and Draper for bulbomembranous strictures without splitting the scrotum.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The technique has evolved to incorporate modern graft materials, particularly buccal mucosal grafts (BMG), replacing the original reliance on local genital skin.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

---

## Surgical Technique

The procedure is performed in two distinct stages, separated by a maturation interval.

### Stage 1 — Marsupialization

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1 | Strictured segment exposed via ventral midline incision along the penile shaft (or perineum for bulbar strictures) |
| 2 | Urethra opened longitudinally through the full length of the stricture until healthy mucosa is reached proximally and distally |
| 3 | Release the stricture and preserve useful native plate and vascular support; excise tissue that is unusable or hair-bearing |
| 4 | Marsupialization: urethral edges are sutured to surrounding penile skin, creating a flat open urethral plate (essentially a hypospadiac-type urethrostomy) |
| 5 | In modern practice, a buccal mucosa graft may be quilted onto the opened urethral bed to augment the plate when native tissue is insufficient or diseased<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| 6 | Drain through the first-stage outlet as appropriate. Uncomplicated first-stage/perineostomy catheterization may be brief; grafting or wound concerns require a tailored plan<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |

### Maturation interval

Healing, width and disease activity are assessed before closure. The EAU guideline suggests an interval of at least four to six months before tubularization when the first stage has healed uneventfully (weak recommendation).<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[17]](#ref17)</sup> In Palminteri’s LS cohort, a first-to-last-stage interval under 12 months was associated with failure, but the adjusted estimate was very imprecise (OR 27; 95% CI 1.36–537.55) and does not establish a universal delay.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Al-Ali performed the second stage 2–3 months after the first in a mainly war-injury salvage series.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### Stage 2 — Tubularization

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1 | Matured urethral plate (with or without incorporated graft) tubularized over a 14–16 Fr catheter |
| 2 | Plate edges mobilized and closed in a tension-free, watertight fashion with absorbable suture |
| 3 | Dartos fascia or spongiosal flap interposed as a second layer to reinforce closure and reduce fistula risk |
| 4 | Urethral stent and/or suprapubic drainage according to the reconstruction; assess for extravasation before removing the catheter after tubularization<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |

## Indications

Staged reconstruction is considered when local tissue quality or associated pathology makes a suitable single-stage repair impractical. Length or LS alone does not mandate staging:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- **Complex penile urethral strictures.** Particularly circumferential strictures requiring complete urethral plate reconstruction.
- **Lichen sclerosus (BXO).** Genital skin is diseased and unsuitable for flap-based repair. The EAU guideline recommends single-stage oral mucosa graft urethroplasty for LS strictures when there are no adverse local conditions (weak), and advises against staged bulbar urethroplasty for LS when a single stage is possible (weak).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[9]](#ref9)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- **Failed hypospadias repair.** Local tissue is scarred and unhealthy.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Long-segment strictures** (≥ 4–8 cm), multiple strictures, or panurethral disease.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Salvage** after multiple failed prior urethroplasties or endoscopic treatments.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Unhealthy local tissue.** Causes include fistulae, scarring, or prior radiation.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Patient preference.** The EAU guideline suggests considering a staged procedure in patients unsure between perineal urethrostomy and reconstruction (weak); medical comorbidities that make a long single-stage operation undesirable may also favor staging.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

The AUA urethral stricture guideline (amended 2023) states that surgeons may reconstruct long multi-segment strictures with one-stage or multi-stage techniques using oral mucosal grafts, penile fasciocutaneous flaps, or a combination (Moderate Recommendation; Grade C). It states that surgeons should use oral mucosa as the first choice when using grafts (Expert Opinion).<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

## Outcomes

| Outcome | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Anatomical success (staged urethroplasty, mixed cohorts) | 89/100 at median 53 mo | Minami 2025<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| Long-term success — **BMG-only** staged repair | 96.4% in the long-term subset; median follow-up 45 mo in the BMG group | Furr 2021<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| Long-term success — **STSG** staged repair | 53% | Furr 2021<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| Two-stage **penile** urethroplasty (systematic review) | 90.5% vs 75.7% one-stage penile | Mangera 2011<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| Salvage cohort, improved urine flow | 94% (mean Qmax 17.4 mL/s) | Al-Ali 2001<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Patient satisfaction (PRO) | 93.5% satisfied / very satisfied | Minami 2025<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |

A multi-institutional analysis of long-segment strictures (≥ 8 cm, n = 466) found that second-stage Johanson urethroplasty had a higher recurrence rate (35.7%) than one-stage BMG urethroplasty (17.5%) (p &lt; 0.05).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> Selection and differing tissue quality limit comparison; these percentages do not isolate the effect of staging.

Furr’s BMG-only 96.4% versus any-STSG 53% long-term success is observational: STSG patients had longer or more panurethral disease and longer follow-up. Of 85 first-stage patients, 57 completed both stages and 49 were eligible for follow-up analysis; the long-term estimate excluded patients followed less than one year. These rates are not outcomes for everyone starting staged repair.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

## Complications

- **Fistula** was the main second-stage complication in the cited series: 1 of 12 second-stage patients in Figler, and revision for fistula or recurrence in 4 of 68 (6%) in Al-Ali.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Wound dehiscence** occurred in 2 of 12 patients who completed stage 2 in Figler, from 20 who underwent stage 1.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Meatal stenosis** occurred in 1 of 12 second-stage patients in Figler.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Postoperative UTI** occurred in about 9% in Al-Ali.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **First-stage revision before tubularization** was required in 17–19% (Minami 17%, Kozinn 18.7%).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Late recurrence** tends to be delayed (median time to recurrence about 78 months among failures), so long-term follow-up is needed.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Abandoned second stage.** In the EAU 2026 summary of evidence, up to 45.5% of men elect not to proceed to second-stage tubularization after a successful first stage.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- **Erectile function.** SHIM scores showed no significant change after surgery in Minami's series, which does not exclude deterioration in individual patients.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Cosmetic concerns.** Aesthetic appearance was the most affected patient-reported dimension in Palminteri's LS cohort.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Modern Modifications and Graft Choice

The original Johanson concept used local tissue; modern variants incorporate oral grafts when native plate is inadequate. AUA guidance favors oral mucosa for graft urethroplasty and avoidance of genital skin in LS. Oral mucosa can nevertheless develop disease-associated recurrence; its relatively thin lamina propria supports graft take, but neither material nor technique guarantees durability.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[13]](#ref13)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

Some patients require a three-stage approach. When the first-stage Johanson alone does not provide an adequate urethral plate, an additional grafting stage is performed before final tubularization.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

---

## Comparison With Alternatives

- **Penile strictures.** The Mangera pooled series reported 90.5% staged versus 75.7% one-stage success; heterogeneity, selection and follow-up prevent a causal comparison.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Bulbar strictures.** One-stage [dorsal](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/dorsal-onlay-omg) or [ventral](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/ventral-onlay-omg) onlay BMG urethroplasty is generally preferred (approximately 88% success with the advantage of a single operation).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Permanent perineal urethrostomy.** Essentially a permanent first-stage Johanson; an option when the patient is not a candidate for or declines second-stage closure.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
