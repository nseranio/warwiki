# Johanson Two-Stage Urethroplasty

The **Johanson two-stage urethroplasty** is a classic reconstructive technique for complex anterior urethral strictures, originally described by **Bengt Johanson in 1953** for the management of penile urethral strictures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> It remains a cornerstone of urethral reconstruction, particularly when single-stage repair is not feasible.

For the BMG / oral-mucosa graft material, see [Buccal Mucosa Graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa). For the related panurethral one-sided BMG approach, see [Kulkarni One-Sided Dorsolateral BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/kulkarni-one-stage). For the active-Stage-1-grafting variant (preferred for hypospadias cripples and LS), see [Bracka Two-Stage Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/bracka-two-stage). For permanent first-stage-only solutions, see [Blandy Perineal Urethrostomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/blandy-perineal-urethrostomy) and [7-Flap Perineal Urethrostomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/seven-flap-perineal-urethrostomy).

---

## Historical Background

Johanson originally described the procedure as a **marsupialization-and-closure** technique for penile urethral strictures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The concept was later modified by Leadbetter (the "Johanson-Leadbetter" technique) and by Fernandes and Draper for bulbomembranous strictures without splitting the scrotum.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The technique has evolved to incorporate modern graft materials — particularly **buccal mucosal grafts (BMG)** — replacing the original reliance on local genital skin.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

---

## Surgical Technique

The procedure is performed in two distinct stages, separated by a maturation interval.

### Stage 1 — Marsupialization

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1 | Strictured segment exposed via ventral midline incision along the penile shaft (or perineum for bulbar strictures) |
| 2 | Urethra opened longitudinally through the **full length of the stricture** until healthy mucosa is reached proximally and distally |
| 3 | All fibrotic / spongiofibrotic tissue is excised or incised |
| 4 | **Marsupialization** — urethral edges sutured to surrounding penile skin, creating a flat open urethral plate (essentially a hypospadiac-type urethrostomy) |
| 5 | In modern practice, a **buccal mucosa graft** may be quilted onto the opened urethral bed to augment the plate when native tissue is insufficient or diseased<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| 6 | Suprapubic catheter for ~ 3 weeks of urinary diversion<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |

### Maturation interval

Stage 2 is typically performed **2–6 months** after Stage 1 to allow graft / marsupialized tissue maturation and adequate vascularity.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> A time interval **shorter than 12 months** between first and last stages has been identified as an independent predictor of treatment failure.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

### Stage 2 — Tubularization

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1 | Matured urethral plate (with or without incorporated graft) tubularized over a 14–16 Fr catheter |
| 2 | Plate edges mobilized and closed in a tension-free, watertight fashion with absorbable suture |
| 3 | **Dartos fascia or spongiosal flap interposed** as a second layer to reinforce closure and reduce fistula risk |
| 4 | Suprapubic catheter for ~ 3 weeks<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |

## Indications

The Johanson two-stage approach is preferred over single-stage repair in the following scenarios:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- **Complex penile urethral strictures** — particularly circumferential strictures requiring complete urethral plate reconstruction.
- **Lichen sclerosus (BXO)** — genital skin diseased and unsuitable for flap-based repair.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Failed hypospadias repair** — scarred, unhealthy local tissue.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Long-segment strictures** (≥ 4–8 cm), multiple strictures, or panurethral disease.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Salvage** after multiple failed prior urethroplasties or endoscopic treatments.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Unhealthy local tissue** — fistulae, scarring, or prior radiation.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Patient preference** or medical comorbidities precluding extended single-stage operative time.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

The **AUA 2023 urethral stricture guideline amendment** recommends that surgeons may reconstruct long, multi-segment strictures with one-stage or multi-stage techniques using oral mucosal grafts, penile fasciocutaneous flaps, or combinations (Moderate Recommendation, Grade C). **Oral mucosa is the first-choice graft material**.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

## Outcomes

| Outcome | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Anatomical success (staged urethroplasty, mixed cohorts) | **89%** at median 53 mo | Zimmerman 2010<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Long-term success — **BMG-only** staged repair | **96.4%** at median 57 mo | Furr 2021<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| Long-term success — **STSG** staged repair | 53% | Furr 2021<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| Two-stage **penile** urethroplasty (systematic review) | **90.5%** vs 75.7% one-stage penile | Mangera 2011<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| Salvage cohort, improved urine flow | 94% (mean Qmax 17.4 mL/s) | Al-Ali 2001<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Patient satisfaction (PRO) | **93.5%** satisfied / very satisfied | Minami 2025<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |

A multi-institutional analysis of long-segment strictures (≥ 8 cm, n = 466) found that **second-stage Johanson urethroplasty had a higher recurrence rate (35.7%)** vs **17.5% for one-stage BMG urethroplasty** (p &lt; 0.05).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> This likely reflects selection bias — staged repairs are reserved for the most complex cases.

When BMG-only staged repairs are analyzed separately, long-term success is excellent at **96.4%**, while the addition of a split-thickness skin graft significantly reduces success to 53% (Furr 2021).<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

## Complications

- **Fistula formation** — most common Stage 2 complication, 6–8%.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Wound dehiscence** — ~ 17% in some series.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Meatal stenosis** — ~ 8%.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Postoperative UTI** — ~ 9%.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **First-stage revision before tubularization** — required in **17–19%**.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Late recurrence** — recurrences tend to be delayed (median time to recurrence ~ 78 months); reinforces the need for long-term follow-up.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Erectile function** — SHIM scores show no significant change postoperatively, indicating preservation of EF.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Cosmetic concerns** — aesthetic appearance is the most affected patient-reported dimension.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

---

## Modern Modifications and Graft Choice

The original Johanson technique used local penile or scrotal skin for the urethral plate. Modern practice has shifted decisively toward **buccal mucosal grafts**, which offer superior outcomes due to wet epithelial surface, thick lamina propria, resistance to lichen sclerosus, and favorable graft-take characteristics.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> The AUA 2023 amendment recommends oral mucosa as the first-choice graft, with buccal and lingual mucosa considered equivalent alternatives.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

Some patients require a **three-stage approach** — when the first-stage Johanson alone does not provide an adequate urethral plate, an additional grafting stage is performed before final tubularization.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

---

## Comparison With Alternatives

- **Penile strictures** — two-stage Johanson reports significantly better outcomes than one-stage approaches (90.5% vs 75.7%), though follow-up tends to be shorter for two-stage series.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Bulbar strictures** — one-stage [dorsal](docs/04-surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/graft/dorsal-onlay-omg.mdx) or [ventral](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/ventral-onlay-omg) onlay BMG urethroplasty is generally preferred (~ 88% success with the advantage of a single operation).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Permanent perineal urethrostomy** — essentially a permanent first-stage Johanson; an option when the patient is not a candidate for or declines second-stage closure.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
