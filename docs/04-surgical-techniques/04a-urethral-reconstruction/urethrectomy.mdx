# Urethrectomy

Urethrectomy is partial or total removal of the urethra. This page addresses reconstructive participation: defining resection extent, protecting viable tissues and planning urinary diversion or genital reconstruction with the oncologic team.

This page covers (1) when urethrectomy is indicated, (2) the technical approaches male and female, and (3) the reconstructive consequences of the operation.

---

## Indications

### At Radical Cystectomy

The 2024 AUA / ASCO / SUO MIBC guideline recommends urethrectomy when:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- **High-grade cancer at the apical urethral margin** (frozen-section or final pathology).
- **Women not undergoing neobladder reconstruction.**

Historical risk factors described by Ahlering / Skinner include the following; these do not all constitute automatic contemporary indications for total urethrectomy:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- Clinically overt urethral involvement.
- Prostatic stromal invasion.
- CIS of the prostatic urethra.
- Bloody urethral discharge after cystectomy.

Additional risk factors: multifocal tumors, diffuse CIS, and trigone / bladder-neck involvement.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### Primary Urethral Carcinoma

Resection depends on histology, location, stage and ability to achieve negative margins. Current EAU guidance permits distal urethrectomy as an alternative to penile amputation for selected localized distal male tumors. In women, radical urethrectomy remains an option, while distal urethra-sparing surgery requires careful selection and margin assessment. Locally advanced disease needs specialist multidisciplinary planning; a stage-only table cannot prescribe the same chemoradiation strategy for every histology.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

## Surgical Approaches — Male

### Prepubic Approach (workhorse)

A prepubic approach permits urethrectomy during cystoprostatectomy without a separate perineal exposure. The urethra and associated spongiosal tissue are mobilized along the corporal surface and delivered with the planned specimen. Preserve tissue planes appropriate to tumor extent; an en-bloc operation must not first divide the specimen in a way that defeats its oncologic purpose. A 180-patient retrospective series reported a mean 17-minute additional operative time.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>



**Hiebl urethral-stripping modification.** For difficult bulbar dissection, after periurethral mobilization, the urethra is cannulated with an 18 Fr catheter, sutured distally, and stripped free (described as a technical variation); avoid blind stripping where tumor extent requires controlled en-bloc margins.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

**Prepubic versus perineal comparative outcomes** (Elshal 2011, modified Clavien):<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

| Metric | Prepubic | Perineal | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operative time | 174 min | 209 min | 0.003 |
| Hospital stay | 14.5 d | 17.6 d | 0.047 |
| Grade 4–5 events among reported complications | 2/21 events (9.5%) | 7/25 events (28%) | 0.033 as reported |
| Added OR time vs cystectomy alone | ~17 min (15–25) | 35+ min | — |
| Repositioning to lithotomy | No | Yes | — |

### Perineal Approach (traditional)

1. **Lithotomy** positioning.
2. **Midline perineal incision**; dissect through subcutaneous tissue to the bulbospongiosus muscle.
3. Incise the bulbospongiosus muscle longitudinally at the midline and separate from corpus spongiosum.
4. **Circumferentially detach** the urethra from the corporal bodies; mobilize from the penoscrotal angle to the urogenital diaphragm.
5. For the penile urethra, circumferential subcoronal incision approximately 0.5 cm below the glans allows degloving along Buck's fascia.
6. Progressively dissect the corpus spongiosum containing the urethra off the corpora cavernosa; the penis is essentially turned inside out to achieve complete excision.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### En Bloc Transpubic Approach

**Partial pubectomy** achieves en bloc urethrectomy with cystectomy and avoids a separate perineal incision (and its contamination risk) while providing exposure comparable to the perineal route. Adds approximately 30 min to operative time.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

**Phallus preservation for locally advanced proximal urethral carcinoma.** En bloc pubectomy with complete perineal urethrectomy can be combined with phallic preservation. The remnant penis survives on arterial supply from the superficial penile arteries arising from the external pudendal arteries, a reconstructive-urology technique that turns a mutilating operation into a phallus-preserving one in selected patients.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

### Minimally Invasive

- **Laparoscopic.** 5-port transperitoneal cystoprostatectomy and lymphadenectomy with perineal urethrectomy and specimen extraction. Total operative time 4.5–4.8 h.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Robot-assisted.** Side-docking provides better perineal access than central docking, allowing simultaneous urethrectomy without undocking. A novel urethra-preserving robotic technique reported zero urethral recurrences (0 / 134) with favorable perioperative outcomes versus robotic prophylactic urethrectomy.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Surgical Approaches — Female

Historical anterior exenteration removed adjacent reproductive organs and vaginal wall with the bladder. Current guidance supports preserving uninvolved organs in eligible women; extent is determined by oncologic margins and the agreed diversion or reconstruction plan.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup><sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

### Key Anatomy

- The vaginal-wall smooth muscle contributes to urethral wall formation, particularly when the detrusor is poorly developed.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- The middle urethra is tightly attached to vaginal smooth muscle with abundant veins at the interface, a major source of bleeding.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- The rhabdosphincter fibers are concentrated in the middle and caudal thirds of the urethra; continence can be maintained if innervation remains intact when only the bladder neck and proximal urethra are removed.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

### Technique

- **Antegrade dissection** from bladder neck distally allows direct vision. Hinata’s 965 mL mean blood loss was for 12 radical-cystectomy operations, not urethrectomy alone.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- The vaginal wall is taken with the specimen when oncologically indicated.
- **Reconstruction.** When significant vaginal resection is required, a rectus abdominis myocutaneous flap vaginoplasty restores vaginal length and provides perineal-pelvic bulk.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

### Distal Urethrectomy for Distal Lesions

For distal female urethral lesions, distal urethrectomy with advancement meatoplasty may preserve an outlet, but continence depends on resection extent and remaining sphincter function; see [Distal Urethrectomy + Advancement Meatoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/distal-urethrectomy-advancement).

## Oncologic Outcomes

### Urethrectomy at Cystectomy — Risk-Stratified Benefit

- **Multicenter (n = 887, Laukhtina 2022):** urethrectomy at radical cystectomy did not improve PFS, CSS, or OS in the overall cohort; in the high-risk subgroup (urethral invasion, bladder-neck invasion, multifocality, or prostatic urethra involvement), urethrectomy was associated with lower progression risk (HR 0.58, p = 0.04).<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- **Propensity-matched (Hakozaki 2021):** prophylactic urethrectomy was associated with improved OS in patients with multiple tumors and/or concomitant CIS (5-yr OS benefit, p = 0.021), particularly those not receiving neoadjuvant chemotherapy.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **Large single-center propensity-matched (n = 332, Mennes 2025):** 10-yr OS was higher in the prophylactic-urethrectomy group (47.3% versus 27.5%, p = 0.002); CSS and RFS differences were not significant.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

These retrospective associations do not establish a survival benefit from routine prophylactic urethrectomy. Recurrence risk depends on margins, urethral involvement and surveillance; older 4–18% estimates should not be applied to every contemporary diversion population.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[17]](#ref17)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

### Primary Urethral Carcinoma (rare)

A 2026 meeting abstract described 35 patients with urethral SCC and heterogeneous multimodal treatment. Its five-year survival estimate, based on median 2.4-year follow-up, is not a urethrectomy-specific treatment result or a general prognosis for urethral cancer.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

## Complications

Separate urethrectomy-specific morbidity from the entire cystectomy operation. Joniau reported ten local complications in 180 prepubic cases, including penile or scrotal hematomas. Elshal compared 71 prepubic with 115 perineal cysto-urethrectomies: its 9.5% versus 28% high-grade figures were 2/21 versus 7/25 reported complication events, not percentages of all operated patients. Cross-study tables mixing these denominators are misleading.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

Total and segmental resections have different functional consequences. Avoid blanket reassurance that urethrectomy cannot affect continence or sexual function; counsel according to the actual structures removed and reconstructed.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

---


## Reconstructive Implications

- **Total urethrectomy precludes an orthotopic neobladder anastomosed to the native urethra.** A reconstructionist asked to perform or assist with the urethrectomy step should confirm the diversion plan first; once the urethra is out, the patient is committed to a [conduit](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion/ileal-conduit), [continent cutaneous pouch](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion/indiana-pouch) or another suitable diversion.
- **Frozen-section apical urethral margin** is the most reliable intraoperative tool for selecting urethrectomy candidates at the time of radical cystoprostatectomy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Urethra-preserving robotic technique** (Zennami 2024) is a reasonable strategy when oncologic criteria do not mandate urethrectomy and neobladder candidacy is being preserved.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Phallus preservation in proximal urethral carcinoma** turns a traditionally mutilating operation into a reconstructive one; the external pudendal-superficial penile arterial supply is the basis for the technique.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Female surgery with a substantial vaginal defect** may benefit from a [rectus abdominis flap vaginoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair) or pelvic reconstruction, a reconstructionist's contribution to the multidisciplinary cystectomy.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
