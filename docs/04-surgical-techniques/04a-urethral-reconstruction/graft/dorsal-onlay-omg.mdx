# Dorsal Onlay Oral Mucosal Graft Urethroplasty

**Dorsal onlay oral mucosal graft (OMG) urethroplasty** is a widely used substitution urethroplasty technique for anterior urethral strictures not amenable to [excision and primary anastomosis](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/excision-primary-anastomosis). [Barbagli](/docs/roots/surgeons/a-g/guido-barbagli) introduced it in 1996 (see the [History of Urethral Surgery](/docs/roots/history/urethral-surgery#guido-barbagli--dorsal-onlay-urethroplasty-19951996)).<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup> A free oral mucosal graft is placed on the dorsal surface of the opened urethra and secured against the corpora cavernosa.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In a 2024 GURS survey, 66% of respondents preferred dorsal placement for bulbar grafting (134 responses from 350 invitees). The survey measures the practice of responding surgeons and does not show superiority.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

For graft details, see [Buccal Mucosa Graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa). For the augmented anastomotic alternative, see [Augmented Anastomotic Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/augmented-anastomotic). For the muscle-sparing one-sided variant for long-segment / panurethral disease, see [Muscle-Sparing Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/muscle-sparing-urethroplasty).

---

## Indications

- **Bulbar urethral strictures** too long for EPA (generally &gt;2 cm)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Penile urethral strictures.** One-stage dorsal onlay or staged procedures<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Panurethral or long-segment strictures.** Using the Kulkarni modification<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Selected nonobliterative anastomotic / bulbomembranous stenosis after both prostatectomy and radiation**<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- Etiology and tissue quality guide selection; single-stage oral mucosa remains an option in lichen sclerosus when local conditions are suitable.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

The AUA Urethral Stricture Disease Guideline (2023 amendment) states that surgeons should use oral mucosa as the first choice when using grafts (Expert Opinion) and may use buccal or lingual mucosal grafts as equivalent alternatives (Strong Recommendation; Grade A).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

### Why buccal / oral mucosa?

Buccal mucosa has these properties as a graft material:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

- Favorable mucosal wound-healing biology, although clinical repairs can still scar or recur
- Thick non-keratinized epithelium with thin lamina propria, facilitating graft take via imbibition and inosculation
- Adapted to a moist environment, although infection can still compromise reconstruction
- Accessible donor site, with possible pain, numbness and restricted mouth opening
- 99% of respondents in the GURS survey selected buccal mucosa as the primary graft site<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Surgical Technique

Three principal dorsal approaches exist, each with distinct technical features.

### 1. Barbagli technique (classic dorsal onlay)

- Lithotomy position; perineal midline incision.
- The bulbar urethra is fully mobilized circumferentially from the corpora cavernosa.
- The urethra is rotated 180° to expose the dorsal surface.
- A dorsal urethrotomy is performed through the strictured segment.
- The buccal mucosal graft (harvested from the inner cheek) is sutured to the edges of the urethrotomy and quilted to the tunica albuginea of the corpora cavernosa, providing a well-vascularized bed.
- The urethra is rotated back and both margins of the dorsal urethrotomy are apposed to the graft edges, completing the augmented lumen. The ventral urethral wall remains intact in the classic dorsal-onlay operation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

The original 1996 report placed free skin grafts dorsally against the corpora cavernosa to reduce urethrocele formation. It included 25 men, 12 with penile and 13 with bulbar strictures (6 dorsal tube grafts and 7 dorsal patch grafts in the bulbar group). At a mean follow-up of 35.8 months, 5 temporary fistulas seen on postoperative urethrography had closed spontaneously, and no post-void dribbling or diminished ejaculation suggesting graft weakening was reported.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup> Buccal mucosa was reported as a dorsal onlay graft in the 1998 series.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Dorsal mobilization can be difficult after repeated, deep internal urethrotomies at 12 o'clock, which may leave the urethral mucosa adherent to the tunica albuginea, or when an indwelling urethral stent impedes dorsal exposure; a deep, flat perineum in an obese patient can also make separation from the corpora difficult. In these settings, a ventral or lateral approach is advised rather than forcing the dorsal dissection, and any tunica albuginea injury should be recognized and repaired.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>

A published variant secures the graft with fibrin glue rather than extensive quilting. In a one-sided dissection, 2 mL of fibrin glue is spread over the exposed tunica albuginea before the graft is laid on the bed; the graft apices are sutured to the urethrotomy apices and its margins to the urethral mucosal plate. After the urethra is rotated back over the graft, the margins are stabilized to the corpora with interrupted 5-0 polyglactin sutures, and a second 2 mL layer of fibrin glue is applied over the suture line. The authors describe glue fixation as simplifying the operation compared with placing numerous interrupted quilting sutures; this has not been directly compared with suture quilting for outcome.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

### 2. Kulkarni technique (dorsolateral onlay, one-sided dissection)

- Designed for long-segment and panurethral strictures.
- The urethra is mobilized from one side only, preserving the contralateral blood supply and the central perineal tendon attachments.
- **Penile invagination** through the perineal incision allows access to the entire anterior urethra without a separate penile incision.
- The graft is placed in a dorsolateral position.
- A retrospective comparison reported shorter operating time and less blood loss than circumferential dissection; preservation of contralateral attachments is the anatomical rationale, not proof that sexual complications are prevented.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

### 3. Asopa technique (dorsal inlay)

- A ventral sagittal urethrotomy is performed without mobilizing the urethra from the corpora.
- Through this ventral opening, a dorsal urethrotomy is made, and the graft is inlaid into the dorsal wall and sutured to the corpora cavernosa.
- Avoids circumferential urethral mobilization entirely.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Penile repairs.** In an editorial comment, Brandes reports that insufficient penile stretch while suturing a dorsal graft produced ventral chordee in his experience, and he places a temporary glans-traction stitch before suturing to maintain stretch throughout the case.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

### Graft harvest

- Buccal mucosa is harvested from the inner cheek (most common); grafts typically 2.5–13 cm in length.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- For long grafts, 56% of surgeons prefer harvesting from both cheeks.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- The donor site may be left open or closed. In a randomized trial of 135 patients, non-closure was noninferior to closure for intensity and quality of oral pain.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

## Outcomes

| Technique | Success | Follow-up | Key Findings |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dorsal onlay (Barbagli) — bulbar<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> | 83–97% | 21–79 mo | ≤20% recurrence at medium term |
| Dorsal onlay — penile<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | 67–87% | variable | Best results with staged approach for complex cases |
| Kulkarni (dorsolateral) — long / panurethral<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)[[16]](#ref16)</sup> | 87–90% | 12–60 mo | Less blood loss, shorter OR time vs classic Barbagli |
| Dorsal onlay — after both prostatectomy and radiation<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | 84% (7/45 recurrences) | median 21 mo among those without recurrence | 28/45 were already incontinent; no new incontinence observed |
| Dorsal vs ventral onlay — meta-analysis<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> | RR 1.00 (no difference) | variable | No significant success difference; imprecise sexual outcomes |

A single-center retrospective study included 507 bulbar graft repairs: 221 augmented anastomotic and 286 pure dorsal onlay. The combined success was 93.9% at mean 78.9 months. Augmented anastomotic repair was associated with recurrence after adjustment (HR 4.8, p = 0.002), but treatment selection and an institutional technique change over time limit causal interpretation.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

A 2025 meta-analysis of 8 studies (655 patients) found no significant difference in success between dorsal and ventral onlay (RR 1.00, 95% CI 0.94–1.06), with comparable Qmax at 3 and 12 months. However, transient ED was significantly lower with the ventral technique (RR 0.24, p = 0.006), while the estimate for permanent ED was too imprecise to establish a difference or equivalence (RR 0.57, 95% CI 0.03–12.20).<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

## Complications

- **Stricture recurrence.** ≤20% at medium term; rates increase with longer follow-up.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Sexual-function change.** Counsel and measure against baseline; transient and persistent ED estimates vary substantially between cohorts. The meta-analysis does not establish a universal dorsal-onlay ED rate or recovery deadline.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[19]](#ref19)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

- **90-day complications.** 17.6% overall in a retrospective series of 165 bulbar onlay repairs (15.5% dorsal, 19.4% ventral).<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **Postvoid dribbling.** Up to 45% with long-segment repairs.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Chordee.** Approximately 25% (usually transient) with panurethral repairs.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Fistula.** Rare (approximately 2%).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Donor-site morbidity.** Oral pain (median VAS 3/10 at first follow-up, resolving to 0 by 17 months), transient numbness (approximately 9%), temporary difficulty with mouth opening; generally self-limited.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[17]](#ref17)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

The EAU-panel systematic review (41 studies, 3,683 patients, low-quality evidence) found no single graft placement superior for bulbar strictures.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Postoperative Management

Preoperative infection or bacteriuria is treated according to culture, and an intraoperative prophylactic regimen is chosen for local resistance patterns. EAU 2026 finds no evidence that routine prolonged antibiotics through catheter removal reduce infections.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

A urethral catheter commonly remains for two to three weeks, adjusted to repair complexity and healing. Assess for extravasation with validated urethrography before removal; maintain drainage and reassess significant leakage. Follow-up includes symptoms, uroflowmetry and PVR, with anatomical reassessment guided by the reconstruction and recurrence risk.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

Patient-reported voiding improvement is common. Sexual recovery is variable, and no fixed recovery date can be promised.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

## Special Considerations

- **Lichen sclerosus.** EAU 2026 weakly recommends single-stage oral mucosa graft urethroplasty when local conditions permit, and staged reconstruction remains an option for adverse local conditions. Genital skin is not used (EAU strong; AUA Strong Recommendation). An older 13-patient LS subgroup cannot support a general claim that staged repair is superior.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Pediatric.** Dorsal onlay BMG urethroplasty (Barbagli and Kulkarni techniques) is safe and feasible in children with long-segment non-hypospadias strictures, with success rates of 83–88% at median follow-up 90–96 months in a retrospective series of 28 circumcised children.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- **After prostatectomy plus radiation.** The 45-patient Sterling cohort supports feasibility in selected patients; 7 recurred, and 28 already had incontinence. No new incontinence was observed in this retrospective cohort.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Incomplete membranous strictures.** For catheter-related narrowing or an incomplete urethral rupture, splitting the stricture exactly in the midline and augmenting it with a buccal-mucosa onlay, an inner-preputial patch, or a staged mesh graft has been described. Staying in the midline is intended to preserve tissue important to erectile function and urethral blood flow. This has not been validated against alternative approaches and should not be extended to a complete distraction defect.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>
