# Palminteri Double-Face (Two-Sided) BMG Urethroplasty

The **Palminteri double-face urethroplasty** is a single-stage urethral-reconstruction technique that uses two buccal mucosal grafts (BMGs) placed on both the dorsal and ventral surfaces of the urethra to augment tight or near-obliterative bulbar strictures while preserving the native urethral plate.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For the single-sided alternatives, see [Dorsal Onlay BMG (Barbagli)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/dorsal-onlay-omg) and [Ventral Onlay BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/ventral-onlay-omg). For the dorsal-inlay variant on which the dorsal half of the double-face technique is built, see [Asopa Dorsal Inlay BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/asopa-dorsal-inlay-omg). For the obliterative-bulbar transecting alternative, see [Augmented Anastomotic Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/augmented-anastomotic). For the canonical graft material, see [Buccal Mucosa Graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa).

---

## Concept and Rationale

Palminteri's rationale is that a single dorsal or ventral graft may not provide an adequate lumen in a very narrow (tight) urethral stricture.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> He also notes that anastomotic techniques (EPA and AAU) create wide lumens but may cause sexual complications after urethral transection and mobilization.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The double-face technique does not transect the urethra, preserves the urethral plate and augments the urethra from both sides in one operation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Indications

- **Tight or near-obliterative bulbar urethral strictures** where a single-sided graft would be insufficient to create an adequate lumen.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Bulbar strictures 2–10 cm long in the original series (mean 3.3–3.65 cm).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- An alternative to augmented anastomotic urethroplasty for tight or near-obliterative segments. The technique depends on a salvageable, if narrow, urethral plate.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Adapted for post-phalloplasty anastomotic strictures in transgender patients, where excisional techniques have high failure rates due to poor neourethral vascularization.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Surgical Technique

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1. Positioning | Lithotomy; perineal incision to expose the bulbar urethra<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| 2. Ventral urethrotomy | Open the stenotic urethral segment along its **ventral surface** through the spongiosum, exposing the lumen and the dorsal urethral wall<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| 3. Dorsal **Asopa-type** incision | Incise the exposed dorsal urethra in the **midline from the luminal side**, creating an elliptical raw area directly over the **tunica albuginea** of the corpora cavernosa<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| 4. Dorsal **inlay** BMG | Place the first BMG into the dorsal incision as a dorsal inlay; quilt to the underlying tunica albuginea / corpora cavernosa to augment the dorsal urethral plate<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| 5. Ventral **onlay** BMG | Suture a second BMG to the lateral urethral margins as a ventral onlay, completing circumferential augmentation<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| 6. Spongiosal closure | Close the corpus spongiosum over the ventral graft for vascular support and mechanical protection<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| 7. Catheter | Urethral catheter for ~ 3 weeks<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

---

## Graft Material

- **Buccal mucosa** is the graft material in the original series. The AUA 2023 guideline names oral mucosa as the first-choice graft (Expert Opinion).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Two graft segments are needed. In a 2022 survey of Society of Genitourinary Reconstructive Surgeons members (134 respondents), 56% preferred both cheeks when harvesting long grafts.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Chen / Santucci combined ventral BMG with dorsal full-thickness skin graft in 15 patients, compared retrospectively with 115 ventral BMG repairs. Overall failure was 21% versus 17%; a subgroup of strictures ≥6 cm favored double grafting (0% versus 24%). The subgroup is small and does not establish a general benefit.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Outcomes

| Series | n | Population | Mean follow-up | Mean stricture | Success | Re-stricture |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Palminteri 2008<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | 48 | Bulbar | 22 mo | 3.65 cm | **89.6%** | 10.4% |
| Palminteri 2011<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 73 analyzed of 105 operated | Bulbar | 48.9 mo | 3.3 cm | **88%** | 12% |
| Schardein 2020<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | 8 followed of 9 operated | Post-phalloplasty | 31 mo | — | **75%** | 25% |

A 2025 retrospective series of 52 patients used double-sided BMG for near-obliterated (&lt; 6 Fr) long anterior strictures (&gt; 2 cm; 40 bulbar, 12 bulbo-penile), with dorsal inlay plus ventral onlay in 30 and ventral inlay plus dorsal onlay in 22. Recurrence occurred in 4 (7.7%) at median 24 months, all in patients with prior failed urethroplasty and longer strictures, and IIEF scores were preserved or improved in most.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

A systematic review reported a success rate of 90.1% for the Palminteri technique in 53 patients at a mean follow-up of 21.9 months, compared with 86.7% for the Asopa technique in 89 patients.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

In the long-term series, 49 sexually active men reported no postoperative erectile impairment. This selected, uncontrolled denominator does not establish superiority over transecting repair. The early and later Palminteri cohorts overlap and should not be summed as independent patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Key Advantages

- **Additional augmentation.** Two grafts can provide adequate caliber when one graft would not, while retaining usable native plate.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Preservation of the urethral plate.** This supports graft take and preserves urethral blood supply.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Non-transecting.** The technique preserves urethral continuity. A comparative reduction in sexual dysfunction has not been established for this technique.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Single-stage.** Reconstruction is completed in one operation in suitable patients; comparative morbidity has not been established.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Complications

- **Re-stricture.** This is the primary failure mode, approximately 10–12% in the bulbar urethra and up to 25% in the post-phalloplasty setting.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Fistula on voiding cystourethrography.** At catheter removal 3 weeks after surgery, 3 of 48 patients (6.3%) in the initial series had a fistula on the study, and all resolved with prolonged catheterization.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **BMG donor-site morbidity.** In a prospective single-surgeon cohort of 40 patients (mixed stricture sites), donor-site pain peaked at 1 week in 28/40 and 35/40 were pain-free by 3 months. Eating, speaking and cleaning teeth were the most affected activities. Long-term difficulty with mouth opening occurred in 3/40 (7.5%).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Erectile dysfunction.** None was reported by the 49 sexually active respondents in Palminteri 2011; the small uncontrolled sample cannot exclude risk.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Where It Fits

For typical bulbar strictures, dorsal and ventral onlay BMG urethroplasty have similar success in a meta-analysis (8 studies, 655 patients; RR 1.00) and in a systematic review of mostly case series; transient erectile dysfunction was lower with ventral onlay in the meta-analysis.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> The double-face technique is used when a single graft would be insufficient, namely in tight or near-obliterative strictures.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

A single-center retrospective cohort (221 augmented anastomotic repairs and 286 dorsal onlays) associated augmented anastomotic repair with more recurrence than pure dorsal onlay (adjusted HR 4.8, p = 0.002). It did not evaluate double-face repair and cannot establish superiority of this technique.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

---

## Post-Phalloplasty Application

The technique has been adapted for neophallus anastomotic strictures after radial forearm flap phalloplasty. The pars fixa is exposed through a perineal dissection, and surrounding tissue analogous to a Martius flap is rotated to support the ventral graft. Schardein 2020 reported 6 of 8 evaluable patients without recurrence at mean 31 months, with a mean postoperative IPSS of 3.1. The two recurrences were treated with perineal urethrostomy and staged urethroplasty. IPSS measures urinary symptoms, not satisfaction.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The EAU 2026 guideline reports patency of 56–100% for graft augmentation of neophallic anastomotic strictures longer than 2 cm and advises supporting the graft with a local fasciocutaneous flap when ventral tissue is insufficient.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
