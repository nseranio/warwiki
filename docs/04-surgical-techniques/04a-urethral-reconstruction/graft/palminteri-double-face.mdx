# Palminteri Double-Face (Two-Sided) BMG Urethroplasty

The **Palminteri double-face urethroplasty** is a single-stage urethral-reconstruction technique that uses **two buccal mucosal grafts (BMGs) placed on both the dorsal and ventral surfaces** of the urethra to augment **tight or near-obliterative bulbar strictures** while preserving the native urethral plate.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For the single-sided alternatives, see [Dorsal Onlay BMG (Barbagli)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/dorsal-onlay-omg) and [Ventral Onlay BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/ventral-onlay-omg). For the dorsal-inlay variant on which the dorsal half of the double-face technique is built, see [Asopa Dorsal Inlay BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/asopa-dorsal-inlay-omg). For the obliterative-bulbar transecting alternative, see [Augmented Anastomotic Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/augmented-anastomotic). For the canonical graft material, see [Buccal Mucosa Graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa).

---

## Concept and Rationale

The technique addresses a key limitation of single-sided graft urethroplasties: in **very narrow (tight) urethral strictures**, a single dorsal or ventral graft alone may not provide an adequate urethral lumen.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Anastomotic techniques (EPA / AAU) achieve wide lumens but carry sexual-complication risk from urethral transection and mobilization.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The double-face technique **avoids transection**, **preserves the urethral plate**, and **augments the urethra from both sides simultaneously**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Indications

- **Tight or near-obliterative bulbar urethral strictures** where a single-sided graft would be insufficient to create an adequate lumen.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Strictures typically **≥ 2 cm** in length (mean stricture length 3.3–3.65 cm, range 2–10 cm in the Palminteri series).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- An alternative to **augmented anastomotic urethroplasty** for obliterative segments — avoids urethral transection.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Adapted for **post-phalloplasty anastomotic strictures** in transgender patients, where excisional techniques have high failure rates due to poor neourethral vascularization.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Surgical Technique

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1. Positioning | Lithotomy; perineal incision to expose the bulbar urethra<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| 2. Ventral urethrotomy | Open the stenotic urethral segment along its **ventral surface** through the spongiosum, exposing the lumen and the dorsal urethral wall<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| 3. Dorsal **Asopa-type** incision | Incise the exposed dorsal urethra in the **midline from the luminal side**, creating an elliptical raw area directly over the **tunica albuginea** of the corpora cavernosa<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| 4. Dorsal **inlay** BMG | Place the first BMG into the dorsal incision as a dorsal inlay; quilt to the underlying tunica albuginea / corpora cavernosa to augment the dorsal urethral plate<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| 5. Ventral **onlay** BMG | Suture a second BMG to the lateral urethral margins as a ventral onlay, completing circumferential augmentation<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| 6. Spongiosal closure | Close the corpus spongiosum over the ventral graft for vascular support and mechanical protection<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| 7. Catheter | Urethral catheter for ~ 3 weeks<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

---

## Graft Material

- **Buccal mucosa** is the standard graft material — consistent with AUA 2023 first-choice oral-mucosa recommendation. Two grafts are needed, so harvesting from **both cheeks** is often required.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- A variation by Chen / Santucci using a ventral BMG onlay combined with a dorsal **full-thickness skin graft (FTSG) inlay** has also been described, with particular benefit for strictures **≥ 6 cm**: 0% failure vs 24% for single BMG alone in their cohort.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Outcomes

| Series | n | Population | Mean follow-up | Mean stricture | Success | Re-stricture |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Palminteri 2008<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | 48 | Bulbar | 22 mo | 3.65 cm | **89.6%** | 10.4% |
| Palminteri 2011<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 73 | Bulbar | 48.9 mo | 3.3 cm | **88%** | 12% |
| Schardein 2020<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | 8 | Post-phalloplasty | 31 mo | — | **75%** | 25% |

A systematic review reported a pooled success of **90.1%** for the Palminteri technique across 53 patients at mean follow-up 21.9 mo.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

In Palminteri's long-term series, **no patients reported postoperative erectile impairment** — a notable advantage over transecting approaches.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Key Advantages

- **Maximal urethral augmentation** — grafting both surfaces achieves a wider lumen than single-sided approaches, critical for tight strictures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Preservation of the urethral plate** — supports graft take and preserves urethral blood supply.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Non-transecting** — associated with lower rates of postoperative sexual dysfunction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Single-stage** — avoids the morbidity of staged reconstruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Complications

- **Re-stricture** — primary failure mode, ~ **10–12%** in the bulbar urethra; up to **25%** in the post-phalloplasty setting.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Urethrocutaneous fistula** — 3 / 48 (6.3%) in the initial series, all resolved with prolonged catheterization.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Bilateral BMG donor-site morbidity** — transient oral pain peaking at 1 wk and resolving by 3 mo in most; temporary difficulty with eating / speaking; occasional long-term oral numbness or restricted mouth opening (~ 7.5%).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Erectile dysfunction** — not reported in the available literature, a notable advantage over transecting approaches.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Where It Fits

Single-sided dorsal and ventral BMG urethroplasties have equivalent success rates (~ 88%) for typical bulbar strictures.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> The double-face technique is reserved for cases where a single graft would be insufficient — namely **tight, near-obliterative, or longer strictures**.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The non-transecting nature is particularly valuable given the **Redmond / Rourke 2020 multicenter signal** that augmented anastomotic urethroplasty (AAU) is independently associated with failure (HR 4.8, p = 0.002) compared with non-transecting dorsal onlay for long bulbar strictures.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

---

## Post-Phalloplasty Application

The technique has been adapted for **neophallus anastomotic strictures** following gender-affirming phalloplasty. The pars fixa is exposed through a perineal dissection, and surrounding tissue analogous to a Martius flap is rotated to support the ventral graft. Schardein 2020 reported **75% success at mean 31 mo** with high satisfaction (mean IPSS 3.1 postoperatively).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
