# Sliding-T Dorsal Inlay Urethroplasty

The **"Sliding-T" dorsal inlay urethroplasty** is a single-stage buccal-mucosal graft repair for fossa navicularis strictures, described by Hoare, Fersovich, Saavedra and Rourke. Their retrospective series reported success in 25/27 patients at mean 29.6 months. It should be distinguished from separately described glans-sparing transurethral and subcoronal techniques.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

For the broader Asopa dorsal-inlay principle, see [Asopa Dorsal Inlay OMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/asopa-dorsal-inlay-omg). For graft material, see [Buccal Mucosa Graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa). For the transurethral variant, see [Transmeatal Ventral Inlay OMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/transmeatal-omg-ventral-inlay).

## The Challenge of Fossa Navicularis Strictures

Fossa navicularis strictures are among the most technically challenging urethral strictures to reconstruct. The ideal repair must simultaneously achieve four goals:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

1. An unobstructed urethra
2. A slit-like meatus (cosmetically normal)
3. **Preservation of sexual function** (erectile function, glans sensation)
4. A cosmetically acceptable glans, with counseling about altered appearance and wound healing

The authors note that endoscopic treatment typically offers only temporary relief and may necessitate lifelong instrumentation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Meatotomy or meatoplasty can be definitive for selected distal disease: in Meeks's two-institution series, meatotomy succeeded in 87% and meatoplasty in 75% at mean 61 months.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> The AUA Urethral Stricture Disease Guideline Amendment (2023) states that surgeons should offer urethroplasty to patients with recurrent meatal or fossa navicularis strictures (Moderate Recommendation; Grade C). It also notes that lichen sclerosus (LS) strictures are less likely to be reconstructed successfully with genital skin, and that success of oral mucosal grafts of 83–100% has been reported.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Operative Concept

The operation augments the distal urethra with a dorsal inlay BMG in a T-shaped reconstruction, applying the Asopa dorsal inlay principle to the fossa navicularis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Distal graft placement requires suitable glanular tissue, a tension-free mucosal repair and adequate meatal caliber. The graft bed at the fossa navicularis is glanular tissue, not the tunica albuginea of the corpora cavernosa that supports a penile or bulbar dorsal inlay.

The incision sequence is shown in the [original operative video article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.urology.2020.12.031), whose published abstract does not give the individual steps. The transurethral inlay and subcoronal dorsal onlay repairs are separate operations with their own incision and closure steps.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Outcomes (Rourke Series)

| Parameter | Result |
|---|---|
| Number of patients | 27 |
| Mean age | 47.3 yr (range 19–71) |
| Mean stricture length | 3.2 cm ± 1.2 (range 1–4) |
| Stricture etiology | Lichen sclerosus 70.4%; iatrogenic 18.5%; idiopathic 11.1% |
| Prior failed endoscopic treatment | 92.6% |
| Prior failed urethroplasty | 7.4% |
| **Success rate** | **25/27 (approximately 93%)** |
| Mean follow-up | 29.6 mo (range 12–60) |
| Recurrence management | Both recurrences managed with meatotomy |
| 90-day complications (Clavien ≥2) | 7.4% (2/27); surgical site infection treated with antibiotics |
| De novo erectile dysfunction | 3.7% (1/27) |
| Chordee | 3.7% (1/27); mild |
| Patient satisfaction | 96.3% (26/27) satisfied |

<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Nineteen of 27 patients had LS. This supports feasibility in selected LS strictures but does not establish superiority over other distal repairs. In a separate mixed cohort of 733 urethroplasties from the same institution (median follow-up 12.3 years), LS was independently associated with late recurrence (HR 4.46, 95% CI 2.25–9.53). That estimate is not specific to the Sliding-T operation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Other Fossa Navicularis Techniques

The selected nonrandomized series below differ in anatomy, etiology, success definitions and follow-up, so the percentages cannot rank the operations.

| Technique | n | Stricture length | Success | Follow-up | Key features |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sliding-T dorsal inlay BMG (Rourke)**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 27 | 3.2 cm | 25/27 | 29.6 mo | T-shaped dorsal inlay; single-stage; 19/27 had LS |
| Transurethral dorsal inlay BMG (Vanni)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | 16 | 1.7 cm | 93.8% anatomic; 100% functional | 28.8 mo | Transurethral; glans-sparing; shorter strictures |
| Dorsal inlay BMG (Zumstein / Hamburg)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | 32 | — | 69% | 42 mo | 50% hypospadias-associated; mixed etiology; longer follow-up |
| Dorsal inlay oral mucosa (Wirtz / Belgium)<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | 40 | meatal only | 82.5% (5-yr RFS 85%) | 85 mo | Longest follow-up; 5-yr RFS 96% with buccal (n = 25) versus 65% with lingual (n = 15) mucosa; nonrandomized |
| Dorsal BMG onlay by subcoronal approach (Favre)<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | 16 | 5.5 cm | 100% | 41.5 mo | Subcoronal approach; longer strictures extending into penile urethra |
| Meatotomy (Meeks / Barbagli)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | 73 | short | 87% | 61 mo | Simplest approach; 84% satisfied or very satisfied in the questionnaire subset; higher recurrence in LS (20.5% versus 7.5%) across all distal repairs |
| Extended meatotomy / first-stage Johanson (Morey)<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | 16 | complex | 87% (14/16) | 52 mo | Salvage for complex or reoperative distal strictures |
| Ventral transverse island flap (Jordan)<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | 5 | variable | 100% | 17 mo | Classic flap technique; requires glans splitting; small series |
| Ventral island flap + glanuloplasty (Armenakas / McAninch)<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> | 19 | variable | 94.7% | 42.7 mo | Tailored approach; glans cap or wings; fasciocutaneous flap |
| Excision + circumferential BMG (Ehlers / Figler)<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | — | severe | — | — | Glans-sparing transurethral excision; for severe / obliterative strictures |
| Distal one-stage hybrid (Hofer)<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | 27 | 0.68 cm | 82.4% | 42.5 mo | No graft / flap needed for most short strictures; 17.6% recurrence |

## Potential Role

The Sliding-T offers one-stage oral mucosa reconstruction for selected recurrent fossa navicularis strictures, including LS strictures in which genital skin is avoided. In the initial cohort, 26/27 patients were satisfied, two developed infections requiring antibiotics, one reported new erectile dysfunction and one mild chordee. The small numbers and single-center design limit precision and comparison with other repairs.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Limitations and Considerations

- **Single-center experience.** Only one group (Rourke, University of Alberta) has described the technique, in 27 patients, and independent validation is lacking.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Stricture length.** Mean length was 3.2 cm (range 1–4 cm). In Morey's series, onlay reconstruction succeeded in 10/11 strictures of 2.5 cm or less and in 7/13 strictures longer than 2.5 cm.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> For longer distal strictures, a subcoronal dorsal onlay (Favre: 16 patients, median length 5.5 cm, all stricture free at 41.5 months) or staged urethroplasty has been used.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Recurrence at the meatus.** Both recurrences in the Rourke series were managed with meatotomy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Staged repair.** Staged urethroplasty may be needed for complex LS-related strictures with extensive glanular involvement, when local tissue loss or scarring prevents a sound one-stage repair. A one-stage Sliding-T requires a dorsal urethral plate and glans tissue of sufficient quality to support graft take.
- **LS recurrence risk.** LS is a chronic, progressive disease, and follow-up beyond 5 years is needed to assess durability. The recurrence association cited above comes from mixed contemporary urethroplasties.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

---

## Patient Selection

The reported experience is in the following selected patients:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- **Recurrent fossa navicularis strictures** that have failed endoscopic management
- **Stricture length 1–4 cm** confined to the fossa navicularis with or without the meatus
- **Lichen sclerosus etiology** (where genital skin should be avoided)
- **Patients who choose single-stage repair** after discussion of cosmetic and functional trade-offs
- **Adequate dorsal urethral plate quality** to support graft take (if the plate is severely diseased or absent, staged repair may be preferable)

## Postoperative Management

After urethroplasty an indwelling catheter is commonly left for two to three weeks. The EAU 2026 guideline states that urinary extravasation should be assessed before catheter removal; with significant persistent extravasation the catheter is kept or reinserted and the study repeated after one week, whereas low-grade ("wisp-like") extravasation does not appear to affect long-term restricture rate.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

Symptoms, flow and residual urine are monitored, with anatomical reassessment chosen according to recurrence risk. LS needs ongoing assessment, and external genital LS may need topical therapy. Cystoscopy is selected according to the follow-up protocol and clinical findings.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
