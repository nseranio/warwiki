# Ventral Onlay Oral Mucosal Graft Urethroplasty

**Ventral onlay oral mucosal graft (OMG) urethroplasty** is a substitution urethroplasty technique in which a free buccal (or lingual) mucosal graft is placed on the ventral surface of the opened urethra, supported by the underlying corpus spongiosum. In a 2024 GURS survey, 34% of 134 respondents preferred ventral placement for bulbar grafting. Comparative evidence has not shown a clear difference in patency; sexual-function results vary.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For the dorsal alternative, see [Dorsal Onlay OMG Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/dorsal-onlay-omg). For graft material, see [Buccal Mucosa Graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa). For the focal-obliterative-segment hybrid, see [Augmented Anastomotic Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/augmented-anastomotic).

---

## Indications

- **Proximal bulbar urethral strictures.** The thick corpus spongiosum provides mechanical and vascular support to the graft.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Bulbar strictures** too long for excision and primary anastomosis (generally &gt;2 cm)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Selected bulbomembranous strictures after TURP**<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Non-traumatic strictures** of various etiologies (idiopathic, iatrogenic, inflammatory)<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Female urethral strictures.** One option using periurethral fascia support; neither ventral nor dorsal repair is preferred for every woman<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

The AUA Urethral Stricture Disease Guideline (2023) recommends oral mucosa as the first-choice graft material for substitution urethroplasty, with buccal and lingual mucosal grafts considered equivalent alternatives.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### Rationale for ventral placement

The ventral approach is particularly suited to the proximal bulbar urethra because:<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- The corpus spongiosum is thickest in the proximal bulb, providing a vascularized bed for graft take via imbibition and inosculation.
- The technique requires less urethral mobilization than the classic dorsal onlay (Barbagli); the urethra does not need to be rotated 180°.
- There is no dissection of the intercrural space, which may reduce the risk of injury to the cavernosal neurovascular bundles.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- The ventral surface is more directly accessible through a perineal approach.

## Surgical Technique

### Positioning and exposure

- Patient in lithotomy; perineal midline incision.
- The bulbospongiosus muscle is divided in the midline to expose the corpus spongiosum.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### Key steps

1. **Ventral urethrotomy.** The corpus spongiosum is incised ventrally in the midline through the strictured segment. The urethrotomy extends into healthy urethra proximally and distally.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
2. **Stricture assessment.** The urethral lumen is inspected and the length and severity of the stricture are confirmed. Unlike in dorsal onlay, the urethra is not mobilized circumferentially from the corpora cavernosa.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
3. **Graft harvest.** Buccal mucosa from the inner cheek (or lingual mucosa from the ventrolateral tongue). The graft is defatted and tailored, typically matching the length and width of the urethrotomy.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
4. **Graft placement.** The oral mucosal graft is placed on the ventral urethral surface with the epithelial side facing the lumen. Sutured to the urethrotomy edges with interrupted or running absorbable sutures (typically 5-0 or 6-0 polyglycolic acid).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
5. **Spongioplasty.** The corpus spongiosum is closed over the graft in the midline, providing a vascularized tissue bed that supports graft take and helps limit graft sacculation or diverticulum formation. This step is unique to the ventral approach.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
6. **Closure.** Bulbospongiosus muscle and perineal tissues are closed in layers. A urethral catheter (typically 16–18 Fr) is left in place.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### Spongioplasty and pseudospongioplasty

Spongioplasty, closure of the corpus spongiosum over the ventral graft, is needed for graft stabilization and vascularization. However, in the pendulous and distal bulbar urethra, the spongiosum is often too thin for adequate coverage. In these cases, pseudospongioplasty can be performed: periurethral vascularized tissue flaps are mobilized bilaterally and sutured together in the midline to cover the graft. A retrospective comparison reported success in 37/46 pseudospongioplasties versus 47/56 conventional repairs (p = 0.645), with different stricture distributions. This does not establish equivalence.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Outcomes

| Study | n | Success | Follow-up | Key Findings |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Barbagli 2013 — single center<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | 214 | 85.5% | median 54 mo | Preoperative Qmax is the only significant predictor of outcome (OR 1.35, p = 0.001) |
| Vasudeva 2015 — RCT ventral vs dorsal<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | 80 (40 / arm) | 90% ventral vs 92.5% dorsal | 12 mo | No significant difference in IPSS, Qmax, or success |
| Shalkamy 2023 — retrospective<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | 165 | 87.1% ventral vs 88.3% dorsal | mean 52 mo | Comparable ED and voiding outcomes |
| Mousa 2025 — retrospective, proximal bulbar in sexually active men<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | 133 | 91.7% ventral vs 90.4% dorsal | 12 mo | Ventral had significantly better IIEF and lower ED rates |
| Hassan 2025 — meta-analysis<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 655 | RR 1.00 (no difference) | variable | Transient ED significantly lower with ventral (RR 0.24, p = 0.006) |
| Mousa 2024 — post-TURP bulbomembranous<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | 30 | 90% (3 recurrences) | 12 mo | Excellent functional outcomes; 2 patients with incontinence |
| Cordon 2014 — pseudospongioplasty<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> | 102 | 80–84% | 39–41 mo | Nonrandomized selected groups; no significant success difference |

The European Urology systematic review of 41 studies (3,683 patients) concluded that no single technique appears superior for bulbar free-graft urethroplasty, with both dorsal and ventral onlay achieving ≤20% recurrence over medium-term follow-up.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Advantages

- **Less urethral mobilization.** The urethra is not circumferentially mobilized or rotated, potentially preserving dorsal blood supply and reducing operative complexity.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **No intercrural-space dissection.** This may explain lower transient-ED rates.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Lower transient ED.** Hassan 2025 meta-analysis: RR 0.24 (95% CI 0.08–0.67, p = 0.006) versus dorsal.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Technically straightforward access.** The ventral surface is directly accessible through a standard perineal incision without urethral rotation.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Graft bed in the proximal bulb.** Thick spongiosum provides vascular support.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Applicable to female urethral strictures.** One 42-patient, single-surgeon cohort reported 41/42 without recurrence at mean 38.1 months.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

---

## Disadvantages and Limitations

- **Risk of graft sacculation or diverticulum.** Without the rigid support of the corpora cavernosa (as in dorsal onlay), the graft may sacculate ventrally, particularly if spongioplasty is inadequate. This is the most commonly cited theoretical disadvantage.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Dependent on adequate spongiosum.** In the pendulous urethra and distal bulbar urethra, the thin spongiosum may not provide sufficient graft coverage, necessitating pseudospongioplasty.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Not ideal for penile strictures.** Ventral onlay of buccal mucosa in the penile urethra is associated with higher recurrence; dorsal onlay or staged procedures are preferred for penile strictures.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Technique selection.** Both approaches require reconstructive expertise; rigid support alone does not establish that dorsal onlay is the appropriate learning operation.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Evidence limits.** Comparative series have substantial selection and follow-up differences; they do not establish a general excess of late complications from ventral placement.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

---

## Complications

- **Stricture recurrence.** 10–20% over medium-term follow-up; rates increase with longer follow-up.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Transient ED.** 1.6–5%, significantly lower than dorsal onlay in meta-analysis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[10]](#ref10)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Persistent ED.** The pooled comparison is very imprecise (RR 0.57, 95% CI 0.03–12.20); absence of statistical significance does not demonstrate equivalent risk.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Graft sacculation or diverticulum.** A potential complication, not exclusive to ventral placement; minimized by adequate spongioplasty.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Wound complications.** Clavien-Dindo I–II in approximately 15–19%.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Urinary incontinence.** 2/30 patients in the post-TURP cohort; counsel specifically when operating near a compromised continence mechanism.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Fistula.** Rare.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Donor-site morbidity.** Similar to dorsal onlay (transient oral pain, numbness, mouth-opening difficulty), generally self-limited.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Ventral vs. Dorsal Onlay — Evidence Summary

The ranges below describe different selected studies, not a single pooled comparison or an individual risk prediction.

| Parameter | Ventral Onlay | Dorsal Onlay |
|---|---|---|
| Success rate | 85–92% | 83–94% |
| Meta-analysis RR (success) | 1.00 (no difference) | — |
| Transient ED | 1.6–5% | 3.9–25% |
| Permanent ED | 1.7–3.2% | 1.9–13.7% |
| Qmax at 12 months | comparable (MD −0.57, p = 0.43) | — |
| Urethral mobilization | minimal (no rotation) | full circumferential + 180° rotation |
| Graft bed | corpus spongiosum | tunica albuginea of corpora cavernosa |
| Risk of sacculation | higher (theoretical) | lower (rigid corporal support) |
| Best location | proximal bulbar | any bulbar; penile |
| Surgeon preference (GURS) | 34% | 66% |

<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

The data on erectile function are conflicting across studies. While the Hassan 2025 meta-analysis and Mousa 2025 found significantly lower transient ED with ventral onlay, Shalkamy 2023 found no significant difference in IIEF scores or ED rates between the two techniques (transient ED 1.6% ventral versus 3.9% dorsal, p = 0.41).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[10]](#ref10)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> This discrepancy may reflect differences in patient populations, stricture locations (proximal versus distal bulbar), and the extent of urethral mobilization performed.

## Circumferential Grafting (Gelman) and the Palminteri Distinction

Gelman and Siegel described circumferential BMG replacement for selected totally or nearly obliterated anterior segments with intact corpus spongiosum, unsuitable for excisional or augmented anastomotic repair. The spongiosum was opened dorsally without transection: one graft was quilted to the corporal bodies and the other to the opened spongiosum, then their edges joined. Among 18 patients, one early recurrence was treated with DVIU; initial success was 94%, and all were subsequently free of obstructive stricture symptoms at mean 50 months.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

This differs from Palminteri double-face augmentation, which widens a salvageable native plate through ventral access. Do not transfer the plate requirement or incision sequence between the two operations.
---

## Special Considerations

- **Proximal bulbar strictures in sexually active men.** Mousa’s 133-patient retrospective study associated ventral placement with better erectile scores. It supports discussion of the approach but does not establish a universal preference or guaranteed nerve preservation.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Post-TURP bulbomembranous strictures.** 3/30 recurred and 2/30 developed postoperative incontinence in the cited cohort; this is not evidence about radical-prostatectomy stenosis.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Female urethral strictures.** 41/42 were stricture-free and no new incontinence was observed in the cited cohort. EAU advises choosing technique according to tissue quality, available material and surgeon experience; these data do not make ventral onlay the default for all women.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Penile strictures.** Thin ventral spongiosum may make dorsal or staged augmentation preferable; selected ventral repairs need an adequate vascular bed and coverage.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Preoperative Qmax as a predictor.** In Barbagli's landmark series, preoperative Qmax was the only significant predictor of surgical outcome (OR 1.352, p = 0.001), while age, stricture length, type of stenosis, and previous treatment were not significant predictors.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
