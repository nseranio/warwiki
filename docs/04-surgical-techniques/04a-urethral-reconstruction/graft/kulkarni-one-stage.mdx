# Kulkarni One-Stage Panurethral Urethroplasty

**Kulkarni's one-stage panurethral urethroplasty** is a single-stage reconstruction for long-segment and panurethral anterior urethral strictures using one-sided urethral dissection, penile invagination through a perineal approach, and dorsal or dorsolateral oral mucosa graft (OMG) onlay. Sanjay Kulkarni and colleagues in India developed it, and the original series dates to 1998.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The technique reaches the entire urethra from meatus to bulb without a separate penile incision by delivering the penile shaft into the perineal wound. One-sided dissection is intended to preserve the contralateral neurovascular supply. A 2026 review by the originating group reports long-term success of 80–85% in large series.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

---

## Indications

- Panurethral strictures involving both the penile and bulbar urethra, typically longer than 8–10 cm (mean 14 cm in Kulkarni's series; more than 8 cm in the multicenter series)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Complex anterior peno-bulbar strictures<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Most common etiologies: lichen sclerosus (especially in South Asia) and iatrogenic causes (instrumentation, catheterization, prior failed repairs)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Primary and redo settings. Success was 86.5% for primary repair and 61.5% after a previously failed urethroplasty<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Pediatric long-segment non-hypospadias strictures (28 children in a retrospective series; the Kulkarni technique was used for the peno-bulbar group)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

---

## Surgical Technique

One-sided urethral dissection combined with penile invagination allows the surgeon to open and graft the entire anterior urethra through a single perineal incision. The one-sided dissection is intended to preserve the contralateral blood supply and the dorsal nerve of the penis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

### Step-by-Step

**1. Positioning and incision**

Lithotomy position. Midline perineal incision to expose the bulbar urethra. (This is the standard access; the penoscrotal, distal-shaft, and circumcoronal-degloving alternatives are compared under [Incisions and Access Options](#incisions-and-access-options) below.)

**2. One-sided urethral mobilization**

The urethra is mobilized from the corpora cavernosa on one side only (typically the left), preserving the contralateral lateral urethral blood supply and the central perineal tendon attachments. This is the distinction from the Barbagli dorsal onlay, which requires circumferential mobilization; the one-sided approach retains contralateral attachments and is intended to reduce vascular disruption, but it does not eliminate graft failure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

**3. Penile invagination**

The penile shaft is invaginated retrogradely through the perineal incision, delivering the penile urethra into the operative field. This maneuver brings the entire anterior urethra, from the external meatus to the bulbomembranous junction, into the perineal wound and removes the need for a separate penile or penoscrotal incision.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[3]](#ref3)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**4. Dorsal urethrotomy**

The urethra is opened along its dorsolateral aspect on the side of dissection through the entire strictured segment.

**5. Graft harvest and placement**

One or two OMGs (buccal or lingual) are harvested and placed as a dorsal onlay on the opened urethral plate. The graft is secured to the corporal bed, and the urethral mucosal margins on both sides are sutured to the graft to complete the augmented lumen. Kulkarni's series placed two oral mucosa grafts dorsally (mean stricture length 14 cm).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Penile skin grafts have been described when oral mucosa is insufficient (Takekawa used either graft in a mixed group of 39, with success of 80.0% for oral mucosa and 95.8% for penile skin at median 12 months).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The EAU 2026 guideline gives a strong recommendation against genital skin in men with lichen sclerosus.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

**6. Closure**

The augmented urethra is closed over a urethral catheter. A suprapubic catheter may provide additional bladder drainage; it does not pass through the repaired urethra. The penis is reduced to its anatomic position and the perineal wound closed in layers.

## Incisions and Access Options

The Kulkarni family reconstructs the penile urethra without a penile skin incision. Delivering the penis into a more proximal wound and applying the one-sided dorsal onlay from there is intended to avoid the degloving morbidity of the traditional approach (skin-flap necrosis, lymphedema, altered sensation).<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> The same one-sided principle can be delivered through several incisions; the choice is driven chiefly by stricture location.

| Access | Route to the urethra | Best suited for |
|---|---|---|
| **Circumcoronal degloving** | Subcoronal incision ~0.5 cm below the corona; deglove along Buck's fascia | Isolated penile strictures; when penile delivery into a proximal wound is difficult |
| **Perineal + penile invagination** (standard Kulkarni) | Midline perineal incision; penis invaginated retrogradely into the wound | Panurethral or peno-bulbar strictures; the whole anterior urethra through one incision |
| **Penoscrotal + penile inversion** | Penoscrotal incision; penis inverted into the wound (supine) | Penile-urethral strictures; no penile incision; supine positioning |
| **"Mini-Kulkarni"** (distal-shaft, glans-sparing) | Longitudinal distal penile-shaft incision over the prepuce; distal urethra rotated laterally | Isolated fossa-navicularis / meatal strictures |

### Circumcoronal degloving — the traditional penile approach

Classic access to the penile urethra is a circumferential subcoronal incision approximately 0.5 cm below the corona, degloving the penis along Buck's fascia.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> It gives direct exposure but carries the degloving-specific morbidity the invagination and inversion approaches were designed to avoid; it remains reasonable for isolated penile strictures or when delivering the penis into a proximal wound is difficult.

### Penoscrotal inversion (Warner)

Warner and colleagues described a one-sided anterior dorsal oral-mucosa graft urethroplasty performed by inverting the penis into a penoscrotal wound. Like the perineal Kulkarni it requires no penile skin incision. The authors list a single-stage repair in the supine position rather than lithotomy as further advantages.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> In the initial series of 5 patients (mean stricture 3 cm; etiologies instrumentation, lichen sclerosus and failed hypospadias; mean follow-up 6 months), 4 of 5 (80%) had a successful outcome, defined as no postoperative instrumentation. Mathur described a midline penoscrotal incision to reach the anterior urethra for a repair that uses the tunica albuginea of the corpora cavernosa as the augmentation tissue (94.9% overall success in 79 patients, falling to 89.9% at 24 months).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

### The "Mini-Kulkarni" — distal / fossa-navicularis application

The **"Mini-Kulkarni"** applies the one-sided dissection and dorsal-onlay principle to isolated fossa-navicularis and meatal strictures without splitting the glans. Through a longitudinal incision in the distal third of the penile shaft over the prepuce, the distal urethra is rotated laterally and a dorsal buccal-mucosa graft is laid against the glanular bed. The contralateral blood and nerve supply is preserved.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> In the initial report (6 patients), mean Qmax rose from 7.8 to 15.2 mL/s at 6 months, with one urinary-spray complaint and one recurrence, and no fistula or glans dehiscence.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

> **Evidence caveat.** The Mini-Kulkarni is described in a 2025 preprint (n = 6, 6-month follow-up, not peer-reviewed in the cited version). It is an emerging glans-sparing option and not an established standard. For better-validated distal techniques, see the dorsal-inlay and meatal approaches: [Asopa dorsal inlay](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/asopa-dorsal-inlay-omg) and [meatotomy / meatoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/meatotomy).

## Comparison with Barbagli Dorsal Onlay

Kartal et al. compared one-sided dorsolateral onlay (Kulkarni) versus standard circumferential dorsal onlay (Barbagli) retrospectively in 68 patients with strictures longer than 8 cm (31 Kulkarni, 37 Barbagli):<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

| Parameter | Kulkarni One-Sided | Barbagli Dorsal Onlay |
|---|---|---|
| Operative time | 180 min | 196 min (p = 0.037) |
| Estimated blood loss | 164 mL | 202 mL (p = 0.033) |
| Hospital stay | 3.5 days | 4.4 days (p = 0.002) |
| Perioperative complications | 16.1% | 37.8% (p = 0.046) |
| Success rate (~5 yr) | 87.1% | 70.3% |

The anatomical rationale is preservation of contralateral attachments. This nonrandomized comparison does not prove a causal advantage in patency or nerve preservation.

## Outcomes

Case series report success of 83–90%, but populations, endpoints and follow-up differ:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

| Series | n | Stricture Length | Follow-up | Success |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kulkarni 2012<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 117 | Panurethral (mean 14 cm) | Median 59 mo | 83.7% overall; 86.5% primary, 61.5% redo |
| Spencer 2018<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | 73 | Mean 13.6 cm | Mean 44 mo | 64/73 (88%) without recurrence; 4-institution retrospective review |
| Kartal 2020<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | 31 | Mean 14 cm | ~5 yr | 87.1% |
| Zumrutbas 2020<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | 35 | Mean 13.6 cm | Minimum 6 mo | 6 recurrences (29/35 without recurrence) |
| Takekawa 2025<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | 39 | Median 4.4 cm; complex anterior | Median 12 mo (IQR 6–34) | 89.7% overall; oral and penile-skin grafts mixed |

Most recurrent strictures develop at the proximal graft margin (bulbar end).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> One small cohort found more recurrence above age 65 (5/14 versus 1/21); this association does not establish an age-based contraindication.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Functional and Patient-Reported Outcomes

- **Voiding function.** Mean Qmax was 25.4 mL/s at the first postoperative visit in one series.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> Mean IPSS fell from 23 to 10 (p &lt; 0.001) in the multicenter series.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Erectile function.** Takekawa found no overall SHIM change. Spencer reported increased SHIM in 8/42 respondents (21.4%) and decreased SHIM in 6/42 (14.3%), so one-sided dissection does not guarantee preserved erectile function.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Ejaculatory function.** MSHQ ejaculatory scores improved from 8 to 11 (p = 0.004).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Patient satisfaction.** 88.6% of patients were satisfied and 94.3% would choose the procedure again.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Complications

| Complication | Rate |
|---|---|
| Post-void dribbling | 45%<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Transient penile chordee | 25%; described as transient<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Donor-site complications | 2/39 (5.2%), both grade 2<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| Stricture recurrence | Varies with primary versus redo repair and follow-up; managed with dilation, DVIU, meatoplasty, or re-urethroplasty<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |

## Guideline Context

The AUA 2023 Urethral Stricture Guideline Amendment states that surgeons may reconstruct long multi-segment strictures with one-stage or multi-stage techniques using oral mucosal grafts, penile fasciocutaneous flaps, or a combination (Moderate Recommendation; Grade C). Oral mucosa is the first-choice graft material (Expert Opinion), and buccal and lingual mucosal grafts are equivalent alternatives (Strong Recommendation; Grade A).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> The guideline states that complex reconstruction of long multi-segment strictures should be performed only at established high-volume reconstructive centers.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

---

## Relative Contraindications and Limitations

- **LS and local tissue quality.** The EAU 2026 guideline recommends oral mucosa for LS and gives a weak recommendation for single-stage oral mucosa graft urethroplasty in the absence of adverse local conditions. Staged repair is used when the plate or surrounding tissues cannot support augmentation.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Prior failed urethroplasty.** Success was 61.5% versus 86.5% for primary repair.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Age and health.** Fitness, tissue quality and patient priorities are assessed individually. The small age subgroup above is not a reason by itself to deny reconstruction.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Medical comorbidities that preclude extended operative time.** [Perineal urethrostomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/blandy-perineal-urethrostomy) is a long-term alternative to urethroplasty (AUA conditional recommendation; expert opinion in high-risk populations).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
