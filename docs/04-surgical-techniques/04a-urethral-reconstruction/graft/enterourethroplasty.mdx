# Enterourethroplasty

**Enterourethroplasty** is the use of intestinal tissue (bowel segments or intestinal mucosa) for urethral reconstruction. The techniques range from full-thickness intestinal flap interposition (tubularized bowel segments on a vascular pedicle) to free mucosal grafts harvested from the colon, rectum, or other gastrointestinal segments. It is a salvage approach for otherwise unsalvageable urethral strictures or defects when conventional techniques (buccal mucosa grafts, skin flaps) have failed or are contraindicated.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For the graft-material framing of rectal mucosa (history, harvest-technique comparison, BMG-versus-RMG selection), see [Grafts in GU Reconstruction → Rectal Mucosa Graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts-gu-reconstruction#rectal-mucosa-graft). For other graft material details, see [Buccal Mucosa Graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa). For appendiceal use in upper-tract reconstruction, see [Appendiceal / Ileal Onlay Ureteroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/appendiceal-ileal-onlay). For catheterizable-channel applications, see [Continent Catheterizable Channels](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/catheterizable-channels). For metabolic management, see [Mucus Management](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/urinary-diversion-specific/mucus-management), [Vitamin B12 Supplementation](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/urinary-diversion-specific/vitamin-b12-supplementation), and [Urinary Acidifiers & Alkalinizers](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/urinary-diversion-specific/urinary-acidifiers).

## Clinical Context

Published salvage approaches include colonic mucosal grafting, full-thickness bowel interposition and microvascular intestinal transfer. These operations differ substantially in donor morbidity, tissue support and urinary contact, and their outcomes cannot be pooled as a single intervention.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Types of Enterourethroplasty

### 1. Full-thickness intestinal flap interposition (Mundy / Andrich technique)

A full-thickness segment of bowel on its mesenteric vascular pedicle is harvested, tailored to an appropriate urethral caliber, and interposed as a tubular neourethra.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- **Bowel segments.** Ileum, stomach, right colon, or (preferably) sigmoid colon were used.
- **Preferred segment.** Mundy and Andrich preferred sigmoid colon.
- **Graft dimensions.** The flap averaged 8 cm in length and was tailored to 26–30 Fr caliber.
- **Route.** The neourethra follows the normal perineal route or a direct transpubic route through a trench cut in the superior pubic ramus.
- **Anastomosis.** The proximal end is sutured to the prostatic stump and the distal end to the distal bulbar or proximal pendulous urethra.

**Outcomes (Mundy / Andrich, 11 patients over 10 years, otherwise unsalvageable bulbomembranous strictures or defects):**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- 3/11 developed proximal anastomotic contracture (1 managed with dilation, 2 required revision)
- 2/11 developed stones in the gut segment (1 caused irreparable damage to the neourethra during removal)
- The authors described the results as otherwise satisfactory

### 2. Colonic mucosal graft urethroplasty (Xu et al.)

The colonic mucosal series below are from [Yue-Min Xu](/docs/roots/surgeons/s-z/yue-min-xu)'s group. Xu's colonic mucosal series used free grafts harvested from the colon (typically sigmoid); the initial seven clinical grafts were tubularized over 18–22 Fr stents.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> The EAU 2026 guideline strongly recommends against single-stage tubularized grafts because complications are more frequent than with onlay grafts.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

- **Graft length.** 10–20 cm (mean 13–15 cm), which may provide a larger single graft than one oral donor site<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Harvest.** Historically required sigmoid colectomy for retrieval, the major limitation of the technique<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

| Study | n | Mean Graft Length | Success | Follow-up | Key Findings |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Xu 2003 (initial)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | 7 | 13.1 cm | 6/7 without reported meatal stenosis; no formal success proportion | mean 8.5 mo (3–18) | Metaplasia at 12 weeks was observed in the companion dog experiment, not established in these patients |
| Xu 2004 (expanded)<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | 16 | median 13 cm | No formal success proportion reported in the cited abstract | 6–33 mo | One meatal stenosis required reoperation; a separate verumontanum hyperplasia required colliculectomy; the other patients voided with Qmax &gt;15 mL/s |
| Xu 2007 (comparative)<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | 65 mixed repairs, including 28 colonic grafts | — | 50/65 (76.9%) overall; not colonic-specific | mean 4.8 yr | Outcomes include mucosal grafts, skin flaps and staged procedures |
| Xu 2009 (long-term)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | 35 followed of 36 operated | 15.1 cm | 30/35 (85.7%) normal voiding without any postoperative procedure | mean 53.6 mo | Most common complications: meatal and anastomotic stenosis |

### 3. Rectal mucosal graft urethroplasty (Vanni / Palmer / Zhao)

Minimally invasive transanal harvest of rectal mucosa has been described to avoid the morbidity of colonic resection.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

**Harvest techniques:**

- **Transanal endoscopic microsurgery (TEM).** Introduced clinically in 2013 and reported by Palmer in 2016; rectal mucosa harvested endoscopically through a transanal platform.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Robotic transanal minimally invasive surgery (R-TAMIS).** Howard and colleagues described submucosal hydrodissection in six urethral and one vaginal repair and confirmed donor-site hemostasis by flexible sigmoidoscopy; the series did not compare harvest platforms.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Single-port endorobotic harvest.** Ozgur and colleagues described submucosal lift followed by dissection beginning 2 cm proximal to the sphincter complex.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> In a separate single-port report, Emrich Accioly and colleagues began posterior submucosal dissection proximal to the dentate line, obtained hemostasis, and thinned the graft before urethroplasty.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

| Study | n | Graft Length | Success | Follow-up | Key Findings |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Palmer 2016 (initial TEM)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | 4 | graft length not reported in abstract; median stricture length 13.5 cm | 3/4 without in-graft recurrence (failure defined as inability to pass a 16 Fr cystoscope) | median 18 mo | First TEM rectal mucosa harvest for urethroplasty; no colorectal complications |
| Granieri 2019 (TURNS multi-institutional)<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | 13 | mean 10.6 cm | 11/13 (85%) without reported stricture recurrence across mixed onlay/two-stage repairs | median 13.5 mo | 69% had prior failed BMG; 46% lichen sclerosus; bowel safety assessed only in small cohorts |
| Howard 2019 (R-TAMIS)<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | 6 urethral + 1 vaginal | mean 11.4 × 3.0 cm (all 7 grafts) | All 7 grafts took; urethral patency not separately reported | median 17 mo (all 7) | First robotic rectal mucosa harvest; patients with prior BMG self-reported less pain than after their prior BMG harvest |
| Ozgur 2023 (single-port endorobotic)<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> | 2 | — | Urethral patency not reported in the cited abstract | — | Technical report of two harvests; no harvest-related complication stated for one patient |

### 4. Appendiceal interposition urethroplasty

The **vermiform appendix** has been used as a pedicled or free vascularized flap for urethral substitution, particularly for posterior urethral defects. Human experience is limited to case reports and small series; the posterior urethral experience cited from Büyükünal 1995 is a rabbit experiment.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

- **Anatomy and tradeoffs.** Natural tube structure with mucosal lining, muscular wall, an identifiable vascular pedicle (appendicular artery); no hair; rich vascularization; potential donor-site morbidity requiring separate assessment.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Applications.** Posterior urethral reconstruction after PFUI, penile or phallus reconstruction in selected cases of severe fibrosis, and catheterizable channels (Mitrofanoff principle).<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- **Technique.** Appendix mobilized on its mesenteric pedicle, transposed to the perineum, and anastomosed between the prostatic urethra and bulbar or penile urethra. Omentum may be wrapped around the anastomosis.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Outcomes.** Reports are limited to small series. Aggarwal reported normal micturition and continence in two boys with PFUI at 1–3 years; one required a single dilation after reconstruction. Koshima used free vascularized appendix transfer with microvascular anastomosis, combined with a radial forearm osteocutaneous flap, for penile urethral reconstruction in 2 patients with severe fibrosis and reported successful results.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

### 5. Jejunal free flap urethroplasty (Bales / Gottlieb)

A segment of jejunum harvested on its vascular pedicle, plicated to an appropriate urethral caliber, and transferred as a free flap with microvascular anastomosis to recipient vessels in the perineum.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- Requires a multidisciplinary team (reconstructive urologist and microsurgeon)
- Both patients in the initial series (n = 2) had satisfactory functional and cosmetic outcomes at short follow-up, with patent urethral lumens and standing voiding<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Selection.** Bales and colleagues described jejunal free transfer in two cases and proposed it as a salvage option when conventional grafts or local flaps have failed or are contraindicated.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Indications

Enterourethroplasty has been used when conventional graft materials are unavailable, insufficient, or contraindicated:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

- **Panurethral or very long-segment strictures** (&gt;10–15 cm) may require more than one graft or technique. Intestinal mucosa is a selected option when adequate oral mucosa and penile skin are unavailable, unsuitable, or undesired.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)[[25]](#ref25)</sup>
- **Prior failed buccal-graft urethroplasty.** Nine of 13 TURNS patients (69%) had a failed BMG repair; remaining oral donor tissue should be assessed separately before selecting bowel mucosa.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[25]](#ref25)</sup>
- **Oral pathology** precluding buccal harvest: oral lichen planus, submucous fibrosis, heavy tobacco use, prior oral radiation<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Otherwise unsalvageable bulbomembranous strictures** or urethral defects after trauma<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Failed multiple prior urethroplasties** (patients averaged 2.5–3 prior failed repairs in colonic-mucosa series)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Strictures after gender-affirming phalloplasty.** Rectal mucosal grafting has been described for these repairs.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> In the TURNS cohort, three patients had strictures at the fixed-urethra–neophallus junction extending into the neophallus.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> The EAU 2026 guideline strongly recommends against endoscopic incision or urethroplasty within six months after neophalloplasty and weakly recommends staged urethroplasty for strictures within the neophallic urethra.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>

## Properties of Intestinal Mucosa for Urethral Reconstruction

| Property | Colonic / Rectal Mucosa | Buccal Mucosa |
|---|---|---|
| Reported graft lengths | 10–20 cm for colonic grafts in Xu 2009<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>; 3–16 cm for rectal grafts in TURNS<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>, and a 21 × 3 cm rectal graft in one single-port case<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup> | Cheek-only grafts ranged from 2–13 cm in a 12-patient series<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup> |
| Epithelial type | Columnar; 12-week transitional metaplasia is animal evidence | Stratified squamous (non-keratinized) |
| Graft thickness | Thin mucosal layer | Thick, resilient |
| Harvest morbidity | Colectomy is more invasive; transanal approaches have encouraging but small safety cohorts | Low (oral pain, numbness) |
| Mucus production | Present (may cause postvoid dribbling) | Minimal |
| Stone formation risk | Reported in bowel segments | Not an intrinsic mucus-secreting graft; stones can still occur with obstruction, infection or foreign material |
| Metabolic complications | Metabolic effects of urinary contact with bowel are described mainly for reservoirs and diversions; risk after urethral free grafts or short interpositions is not quantified in the cited reviews<sup>[[19]](#ref19)[[20]](#ref20)</sup> | None |
| Availability | Limited by safe harvest, bowel health and donor morbidity | Limited by oral cavity size and tissue quality |

## Histological Adaptation

In Xu’s 10-dog experiment, colonic mucosa demonstrated metaplastic transformation after urethral replacement:<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

- At 8 weeks: colonic plicae and unilaminar cylindric epithelium still visible
- By 12 weeks: plicae and cylindric epithelium disappear, replaced by metaplastic transitional epithelium covering most of the urethral mucosa
- Urodynamics in the nine dogs without stricture showed no significant change in maximum urethral pressure before and after the operation (p &gt; 0.05); this cannot guarantee continence after human reconstruction

These findings establish experimental feasibility, not a predictable human remodeling interval or proof of superior graft biology.

## Choice of Intestinal Segment

| Segment | Advantages | Disadvantages | Best Application |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sigmoid colon** (full-thickness preferred) | Proximity to perineum; robust blood supply; appropriate caliber | Requires laparotomy; mucus production; stone risk | Full-thickness interposition for bulbomembranous defects<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Colonic mucosa** (free graft) | Large graft (10–20 cm); selected long-segment feasibility data | Historically required colectomy for harvest | Long-segment / pan-urethral strictures<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Rectal mucosa** (free graft) | Minimally invasive harvest (TEM / R-TAMIS); no colectomy; large graft (3–16 cm in the TURNS cohort); bowel safety assessed only in small cohorts<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | Limited long-term data; newer technique | Alternative to BMG when oral mucosa unavailable<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Ileum** | Well-vascularized; familiar to urologists | Terminal ileal resection can cause B12 deficiency, diarrhea and cholelithiasis; urine-exposed ileum produces mucus. Rates after short urethral interposition are unquantified | Rarely used for urethroplasty; more common for bladder augmentation<sup>[[19]](#ref19)[[20]](#ref20)</sup> |
| **Stomach** | Decreased urinary pH and no mucus problems were reported in 22 adults after gastric bladder augmentation or reservoir reconstruction at mean 9.8-month follow-up<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> | Hypochloremic alkalosis in 2/22 in that early cohort; hematuria-dysuria in 7/29 and reservoir malignancy in 3/29 in a separate long-term gastrocystoplasty/reservoir cohort; not urethroplasty-specific rates | Castellan and colleagues do not recommend gastric segments for lower urinary tract reconstruction<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[22]](#ref22)</sup> |
| **Appendix** | Natural tube; muscular wall; consistent pedicle; no hair | Limited length; single-use organ; not always available | Posterior urethral defects; catheterizable channels<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |

## Complications Specific to Enterourethroplasty

Beyond the standard urethroplasty complications, enterourethroplasty carries unique risks related to intestinal tissue.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[19]](#ref19)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

### Urethral / reconstructive

- **Anastomotic contracture.** 27% in the Mundy series (3/11), the most common complication of full-thickness interposition<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Stone formation in the gut segment.** Mucus production by intestinal epithelium serves as a nidus; reported in 2/11 patients (Mundy)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Meatal and anastomotic stenosis.** These were the most common complications of colonic mucosal graft urethroplasty. Meatal stenosis occurred in 1 of 16 patients in Xu 2004, and meatal, bulbar or bulbomembranous and anastomotic stenoses together occurred in 5 of 35 evaluated patients in Xu 2009.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Graft sacculation / pseudodiverticulum.** Xu reported 2/65 after mixed substitution procedures, not a colonic-graft-specific rate. A separate five-patient rectal-graft series reported one small diverticulum; its authors noted that thin rectal mucosa needs adequate tissue support.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[30]](#ref30)</sup>
- **Mucus production.** May cause postvoid dribbling and serve as a nidus for infection and stone formation<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

### Bowel-related / metabolic

- **Metabolic imbalance.** In urinary reservoirs or diversions, ileal or colonic segments can cause hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis, while gastric segments can cause hypochloremic alkalosis. Risk varies with bowel surface area and urine-contact time; rates after a urethral free mucosal graft or short interposition are not quantified in the cited reviews.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)[[20]](#ref20)[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- **Vitamin B12 deficiency.** Assess whether and how much terminal ileum was removed, baseline absorption and nutritional status; there is no validated universal 10-cm cutoff for these operations. Use the linked B12 companion for risk-based surveillance.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- **Cholelithiasis.** Derangements in bile salt metabolism with ileal resection.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- **Diarrhea.** Secretory-osmotic diarrhea with ileal resection and loss of ileocecal valve.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- **Malignancy.** Castellan 2012 reported 3 malignancies in 29 patients (10.3%) undergoing gastric segment lower-urinary-tract reconstruction at 11–14 yr, all of whom died of metastasis. The authors recommended against using gastric segments for lower urinary tract reconstruction.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

### Contraindications to gastric segments

Based on Castellan 2012, the authors recommended against gastric segments for lower urinary tract reconstruction after their reservoir or augmentation cohort; this is not a 29-patient enterourethroplasty series:<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

- **51.7% complication rate** (15/29 patients)<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- **Hematuria-dysuria syndrome** in 7 patients (intractable in 1)
- Reservoir malignancy in 3 of 29 gastric-segment reconstruction patients (10.3%) at 11–14 yr after gastrocystoplasty; all 3 died of metastasis<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- Multiple major reoperations required

## Current Role and Future Directions

The AUA Urethral Stricture Disease Guideline (2023 amendment) states that surgeons should use oral mucosa as the first choice when using grafts (Expert Opinion).<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> Enterourethroplasty remains a niche salvage technique. For complex anterior strictures, the EAU 2026 guideline strongly recommends offering [perineal urethrostomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/blandy-perineal-urethrostomy) as a management option.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> Published intestinal techniques include:

- Rectal mucosal grafts (TEM or R-TAMIS) provide a large graft without colectomy. The TURNS cohort had 11/13 without recurrence at median 13.5 months and no reported bowel complication, but did report glans dehiscence, fistula and compartment syndrome in 1 patient each. The series are small and short-term.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- Xu 2009 reported normal voiding without further procedure in 30/35 followed colonic-graft patients at mean 53.6 months.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> A 2026 multicentre retrospective comparison of one-stage repairs for &gt;10 cm strictures reported normal voiding and urethrogram without reintervention in 59/69 colonic-graft patients at mean 145 months and 184/225 oral-graft patients at mean 58 months; patient overlap with the earlier Xu cohort is possible, and unequal follow-up limits direct efficacy comparison.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[29]](#ref29)</sup> The need for colectomy has limited adoption of colonic grafts.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> The EAU guideline reports a colonic-abdominal fistula in 1/35 patients and advises considering other grafts only when oral mucosa and penile skin are unavailable, unsuitable, or undesired.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
- **Full-thickness intestinal interposition** (Mundy technique) is a last-resort salvage procedure for otherwise unsalvageable bulbomembranous defects.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Tissue-engineered grafts** are under investigation and may reduce the amount of autologous tissue harvested. The EAU 2026 guideline strongly recommends against autologous tissue-engineered oral mucosa grafts outside a clinical trial and weakly recommends against cell-free grafts after failed urethroplasty, with extensive spongiofibrosis, or for strictures &gt;4 cm.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
