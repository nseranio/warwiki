# Male Urethroplasty — Incisions & Approaches

Male urethroplasty begins with two decisions: how to incise the skin and how to reach the urethra. The choices are organized here by skin incision, penile-stricture exposure, anterior urethrotomy approach, posterior approach, graft or tissue placement, flap-based technique, and one-stage versus staged repair. Outcomes for individual named techniques are on their own pages, cross-linked below.

Decision principles (graft selection, tension, spongiofibrosis) are in [Principles of Urethral Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/urethral-reconstruction-principles). The [Master Decision Framework](/docs/surgical-techniques/urethral-reconstruction) covers technique selection.

---

## 1. Perineal Skin Incisions

Three principal perineal skin incisions are described for bulbar and posterior urethroplasty:

| Incision | Description | Reported endpoint | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Midline perineal vertical** | Longitudinal incision in the perineal raphe | SSI 1.9% in bulbar and 3.1% in posterior repairs | Lin: matched retrospective urethroplasty cohort, not a PU comparison; lower infection rates than inverted-U in that cohort<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Inverted-U (curvilinear)** | U-shaped incision curving around the perineum | SSI 18.6% in bulbar and 16.4% in posterior repairs | Lin: corresponding inverted-U groups; cadaveric incisions crossed more nerves and vessels<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Lambda perineal incision (LPI)** | Inverted-Y / lambda shape extending laterally from midline | Minor wound complications 23.3% (vs 11.9% midline), not SSI alone | Bascom 2016 (n = 540 bulbar urethroplasties): independently associated with increased 90-day wound complications, primarily superficial wound-edge separation (10.7% vs 0%); **no urethroplasty-success advantage**<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

![Perineal incision shapes: Male perineum, inferior view; scrotum anterior](/img/diagrams/perineal-incisions.svg)

*Midline, inverted-U and lambda incisions illustrate different ways to obtain perineal exposure. Choice depends on exposure, prior scars, patient anatomy and reconstruction. (Original WARWIKI schematic; see the figure source record and review limits.)*

A **midline vertical incision** is a useful option for bulbar perineal access and permits the loop or 7-flap decision after exposure. Incision choice remains individualized; retrospective urethroplasty wound data do not establish superiority of one PU reconstruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

For penile urethral strictures, the three penile-skin incisions are described in the next section.

## 2. Penile-Stricture Exposure — Three Approaches

For penile urethral strictures specifically, three exposure strategies dominate contemporary practice. In a retrospective 13-institution review of 222 non-hypospadiac penile strictures (median follow-up 49 months, overall success 80.2%), Abramowitz found no significant difference in outcomes by incision approach. Choice is driven by stricture characteristics and surgeon experience.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### 2a. Degloving / circumcising incision

**Subcoronal circumferential incision** (identical to a circumcision incision); penile skin retracted proximally to "deglove" the shaft, exposing the entire penile urethra from glans to penoscrotal junction.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- **Advantages:** circumferential exposure; standard for penile-skin-flap harvest ([Orandi](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/orandi-flap), [Quartey](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/quartey-flap), [Q-flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/q-flap), [McAninch circular](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/mcaninch-circular-flap)) and for [staged urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/johanson-two-stage); allows simultaneous fasciocutaneous flap harvest.
- **Disadvantages:** disrupts dartos blood supply circumferentially; risk of penile edema / skin necrosis (especially in redo cases); preputial preservation depends on the incision and reconstruction.
- **Best for:** isolated penile strictures, [staged repair](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/bracka-two-stage), cases requiring fasciocutaneous flap harvest, selected cases requiring wide penile exposure; LS alone does not require complete plate excision.

### 2b. Kulkarni penile invagination (perineal-only)

Standard perineal midline incision with the penis invaginated (telescoped) into the perineal wound and no penile incision. One-sided urethral mobilization preserves the contralateral neurovascular bundle and central perineal tendon attachments. The graft is placed as a dorsolateral onlay.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- **Advantages:** avoids a separate penile skin incision and permits long-segment oral-graft reconstruction with one-sided urethral mobilization. Functional outcomes and recurrence remain important counseling points; the technique does not guarantee preservation of erectile function. See the technique page for study-specific results.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Limitations:** distal access can be difficult, and meatal or fossa-navicularis disease may require an additional distal maneuver. LS involving the meatus is not by itself a categorical exclusion; tissue quality determines the reconstruction.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
- **Best for:** long-segment and panurethral strictures (especially &gt; 8 cm), peno-bulbar strictures, and cases where penile-skin preservation is a priority.
- **Canonical technique page:** [Kulkarni One-Sided Dorsolateral BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/kulkarni-one-stage).

### 2c. Midline ventral penile incision

A direct longitudinal incision along the ventral raphe preserves lateral dartos pedicles and avoids circumferential degloving. It is used for modified Orandi dorsal-skin-flap repair, penile fracture repair, and penoscrotal-junction strictures.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- **Advantages:** preserves lateral penile-skin vascularity; avoids circumcision; can be combined with perineal invagination for penoscrotal-junction strictures.
- **Disadvantages:** limited exposure for long penile strictures; visible ventral scar; the incision must accommodate any planned flap and preserve its dartos pedicle.
- **Best for:** focal / short penile strictures, modified Orandi dorsal-skin-flap repairs, penoscrotal-junction strictures, penile-fracture repair.

## 3. Anterior Urethrotomy Approaches

How the urethra itself is opened once exposed drives graft placement, blood-supply preservation, and operative time.

| Approach | Description | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Ventral urethrotomy** | Urethra opened on its ventral surface | Traditional approach; supports [ventral onlay BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/ventral-onlay-omg) and the [Asopa dorsal inlay](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/asopa-dorsal-inlay-omg) (dorsal wall incised through the ventral opening). Less urethral mobilization required<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **Dorsal urethrotomy (Barbagli)** | Urethra mobilized circumferentially and rotated 180° to expose the dorsal surface | More extensive dissection; supports [dorsal onlay BMG (Barbagli)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/dorsal-onlay-omg)<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| **Dorsolateral (Kulkarni)** | One-sided urethral dissection; contralateral blood supply preserved | Minimizes urethral devascularization; particularly useful for long penile strictures; the basis of the [Kulkarni one-sided dorsolateral BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/kulkarni-one-stage) technique<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Non-transecting** | Corpus spongiosum is not fully transected, so antegrade blood flow is preserved | Cotter 2019 multi-institutional data: 2,098 anterior urethroplasties (10 surgeons, 2010-2017): bulbar excisional repairs performed without transection rose 430%. Includes [Jordan vessel-sparing EPA](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/jordan-vessel-sparing-epa), [non-transecting bulbar](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/non-transecting-bulbar), and the entire [ANTA / MsANTA / MANTA / ntAAU](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/manta) family<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |

## 4. Posterior Urethroplasty Approaches

For posterior urethral distraction defects (PFUI) and post-prostatectomy or post-radiation membranous-urethral or VUAS strictures:

| Approach | Used in | Indication |
|---|---|---|
| **Perineal anastomotic** | Usual reconstructive access for PFUI | Obtain healthy ends and a tension-free anastomosis; required maneuvers depend on fibrosis, displacement and urethral elasticity rather than a universal gap-length cutoff<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)[[24]](#ref24)</sup> |
| **Elaborated perineal** (Webster steps 1–4) | Webster step framework | Bulbar mobilization → corporal splitting → inferior pubectomy → supracrural rerouting. In one 142-patient multicenter series, inferior pubectomy was used in 10% and corporal splitting in 17%; anastomosis after scar excision and mobilization alone sufficed in 67%<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Supracrural rerouting (step 4)** | Rare adjunct | Mobilized urethra routed around a crus when other maneuvers do not provide reach. Kizer reported recurrence in 3/4 rerouted cases; this very small selected subgroup cannot establish comparative inferiority to an abdominal approach<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Transpubic (abdominoperineal)** | Selected complex defects | Consider when adequate exposure or a tension-free repair cannot be obtained perineally, including selected fistulas or cavities. Defect length alone is insufficient. See [Abdominoperineal Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/abdominoperineal-urethroplasty)<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)[[24]](#ref24)</sup> |
| **Modified perineal (Quartey 1988)** | Niche | Standard perineal-prostatectomy incision with division of the urogenital diaphragm in the midline; improved membranous-urethra exposure without pubectomy<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| **Robotic transabdominal** | Contemporary alternative for VUAS / BNC | AUA 2023: surgeons may perform robotic or open reconstruction for recalcitrant bladder neck or VUAS stenosis (Conditional Recommendation, Evidence Level Grade C)<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>. In one single-center series of 105 men, 39% needed a combined abdominoperineal approach<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>. See [Robotic Posterior Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/robotic-posterior-urethroplasty) |

## 5. Graft / Tissue Placement Configurations

For substitution urethroplasty, the graft (most commonly buccal mucosa; the AUA guideline names oral mucosa as the first choice, Expert Opinion<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>) can be placed in several configurations:

| Configuration | Eponym | Vascular bed | Indication |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dorsal onlay** | Barbagli | Tunica albuginea | Bulbar strictures, ~ 88% success<sup>[[17]](#ref17)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| **Ventral onlay** | Morey / McAninch | Corpus spongiosum | Bulbar strictures, ~ 89%; lower transient ED than dorsal onlay in a meta-analysis (permanent ED similar)<sup>[[19]](#ref19)[[20]](#ref20)[[17]](#ref17)</sup> |
| **Dorsal inlay** | Asopa | Tunica albuginea exposed by incising the dorsal plate through a ventral urethrotomy | Shorter operative time and less blood loss than dorsal onlay in one randomized study (n = 47); ~ 87% success<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| **Dorsolateral onlay** | Kulkarni | Corporal bed, with contralateral urethral attachments retained | Long penile / panurethral; preserves contralateral NVB<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Two-sided dorsal + ventral** | Palminteri double-face | Tunica + spongiosum | Tight / near-obliterative bulbar strictures; 88% success (64/73 patients with buccal grafts, mean follow-up 48.9 months); none of the 49 preoperatively sexually active men reported postoperative erectile impairment. See [Palminteri Double-Face](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/palminteri-double-face)<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[17]](#ref17)</sup> |
| **Augmented anastomotic** | Guralnick / Webster | Hybrid — excise + onlay | Selected focal obliteration within a longer bulbar stricture; avoid unnecessary transection. See the dedicated augmented-anastomotic pages for comparative evidence and tissue-preserving alternatives<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[24]](#ref24)</sup> |

A systematic review of free-graft bulbar urethroplasty found no technique superior. Dorsal and ventral onlay had recurrence of 20% or less at medium-term follow-up; evidence for dorsal inlay and dorsolateral onlay was too sparse for recommendations, and recurrence rose with longer follow-up.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

For combined graft-and-flap configurations, see [Combined Dorsal BMG + Ventral Fasciocutaneous Flap (Erickson)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/erickson-dorsal-bmg-ventral-flap) and [Ventral BMG + Gracilis Muscle Flap (Vanni / Zinman)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/vanni-zinman-ventral-bmg-gracilis).

## 6. Flap-Based Techniques — Declining Role

**Penile fasciocutaneous flaps** (Orandi, Quartey, Q-flap, McAninch circular) historically dominated long penile and panurethral reconstruction. In the Cotter series of 2,098 anterior urethroplasties from 2010 to 2017, penile-urethral fasciocutaneous flap use declined 86% and single-stage dorsal repairs increased 280%.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> In the bulbar urethra, dorsal graft placement increased 95% and ventral placement fell 75%.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

In a retrospective multi-institutional penile series, success was 83% with buccal mucosa graft and 52% with fasciocutaneous flap.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> In a 2022 survey of GURS members (134 respondents, 38% response rate), 90% preferred multiple buccal grafts over combined graft and flap for panurethral strictures.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> Flap-based techniques remain useful in specific scenarios:

- **Compromised graft bed** (radiation, severe spongiofibrosis). The flap brings its own blood supply.
- **Depleted oral mucosa.** Preputial or penile flap or [preputial spiral graft](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/preputial-spiral-graft).
- **Resource-limited settings.** No specialized graft material is required.

## 7. One-Stage vs Staged

| Setting | Preferred |
|---|---|
| Most bulbar strictures | **One-stage** dorsal / ventral / dorsolateral onlay BMG (~ 88%) |
| Distal / primary penile strictures | One-stage |
| Complex penile strictures with extensive spongiofibrosis | **Staged** ([Bracka](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/bracka-two-stage) or [Johanson](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/johanson-two-stage)) |
| **Lichen sclerosus** | Oral mucosa; single-stage reconstruction if local tissue is suitable, staged repair when adverse local conditions preclude it. Avoid genital skin<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> |
| Failed hypospadias repair | Individualize single-stage versus staged reconstruction to plate, skin, scarring and chordee; refer complex cases to an experienced center<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> |
| Panurethral with healthy plate | One-stage Kulkarni dorsolateral BMG |
| Panurethral, plate destroyed | Combined graft + flap (Erickson) or staged |

Mangera 2011 reported pooled success of 90.5% for two-stage (n = 129) versus 75.7% for one-stage (n = 432) penile repairs across heterogeneous series, with shorter follow-up for the staged group (22.2 vs 32.8 months). These are not randomized comparative estimates and should not dictate staging for an individual patient.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

## Contemporary Trends — Summary

Practice patterns in the TURNS series changed over 2010-2017:<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

- Single-stage dorsal penile repairs increased 280% and penile fasciocutaneous flap use decreased 86%.
- Bulbar excisional repairs without transection increased 430%.

Retrospective series associate the midline perineal incision with fewer wound complications than the lambda or inverted-U incision.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The AUA guideline names oral mucosa as the first-choice graft.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
