# Penile Urethroplasty — Approaches to Exposure

For penile urethral strictures, the surgeon's first decision is **how to expose the urethra**. Three approaches dominate contemporary practice — each with a distinct trade-off in exposure, tissue preservation, and applicability:

1. **Degloving / circumcising incision** — the traditional subcoronal circumferential approach.
2. **Kulkarni penile invagination** — perineal-only, with the penis invaginated into the perineal wound.
3. **Midline ventral penile incision** — direct longitudinal access along the ventral raphe.

Notably, the Abramowitz multi-institutional review found **no statistically significant difference in outcomes based on incisional approach** for penile urethroplasty.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The choice is driven primarily by **stricture length, location, etiology, and surgeon experience**, not by published outcome differences.

For the broader principles of urethral reconstruction (graft selection, tension, spongiofibrosis, etc.), see [Principles of Urethral Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/urethral-reconstruction-principles). For the [Master Decision Framework](/docs/surgical-techniques/urethral-reconstruction). For the Kulkarni one-sided dorsolateral BMG technique that defines the invagination approach, see [Kulkarni One-Sided Dorsolateral BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/kulkarni-one-stage).

## 1. Degloving / Circumcising Incision

The **traditional and most widely used** approach. A subcoronal circumferential incision (identical to a circumcision incision) is made and the penile skin is retracted proximally — "degloving" the shaft to expose the entire penile urethra from glans to penoscrotal junction.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

**Technique.** Subcoronal sulcus incision (or at the existing circumcision scar in previously circumcised patients). Penile skin and dartos fascia are mobilized off Buck's fascia, exposing both ventral and dorsal surfaces. The urethra can then be opened ventrally or dorsally depending on the planned repair (Asopa dorsal inlay, Barbagli dorsal onlay, ventral onlay, or flap).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

**Advantages.**

- Excellent **circumferential exposure** of the entire penile urethra.
- Familiar to most urologists; standard for **penile-skin-flap harvest** ([Orandi](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/orandi-flap), [Quartey](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/quartey-flap), [Q-flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/q-flap), [McAninch circular](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/mcaninch-circular-flap)) and for [staged urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/johanson-two-stage).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Allows **simultaneous fasciocutaneous flap harvest** when needed.
- Ideal for **staged repair** — the urethra is laid open ventrally and grafted in Stage 1 ([Bracka](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/bracka-two-stage)).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

**Disadvantages.**

- **Disrupts the dartos blood supply** circumferentially → may compromise penile-skin vascularity, particularly in previously operated or circumcised patients.
- Risk of **penile edema, skin necrosis**, and wound complications (especially in redo cases).
- Requires circumcision in uncircumcised patients (or uses the existing circumcision scar).
- Not ideal for very long strictures extending into the bulbar urethra — a separate perineal incision is then needed.

**Best suited for** isolated penile strictures, [staged urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/johanson-two-stage), cases requiring fasciocutaneous flap harvest, and lichen sclerosus strictures requiring complete urethral plate excision.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## 2. Kulkarni Penile Invagination (Perineal Approach)

Described by **Kulkarni**, this technique accesses the penile urethra **entirely through a perineal incision** by invaginating (telescoping) the penis into the perineal wound — avoiding a penile skin incision altogether.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

**Technique.** Standard perineal midline incision. The bulbar urethra is exposed and mobilized on **one side only** (preserving the contralateral neurovascular bundle). The penis is then invaginated — pushed inward through the perineal wound — so that the penile urethra is delivered into the operative field. The strictured segment is opened along its **dorsolateral surface**, and a buccal mucosal graft (or penile skin graft) is placed as a **dorsolateral onlay**. The penis is returned to its normal position.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

**Advantages.**

- **No penile incision** — preserves penile-skin blood supply and dartos integrity.
- **One-sided dissection** preserves the contralateral urethral blood supply and the central perineal tendon attachments — theoretically reducing erectile-dysfunction risk.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Single-stage repair of panurethral and peno-bulbar strictures** (mean lengths 13–14 cm) through a single perineal incision.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- Shorter operative time, less blood loss, and lower complication rates vs conventional Barbagli dorsal onlay for long strictures (179 vs 196 min, p = 0.037; complications 16% vs 38%, p = 0.046).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Erectile function preserved** — SHIM scores unchanged postoperatively.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- Success **84–90%** at long-term follow-up for panurethral strictures.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

**Disadvantages.**

- **Penile chordee** — up to **25%** (usually transient).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Post-void dribbling** — up to **45%**, likely related to long graft segment.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Distal penile / fossa-navicularis exposure is limited** — works best when the stricture does not extend to the meatus.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- Requires surgeon familiarity with the invagination maneuver — steep learning curve.
- **Not suitable for LS involving the glans / meatus**, where the diseased tissue must be excised and staged repair is preferred.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

**Best suited for** long-segment and panurethral strictures (especially > 8 cm), peno-bulbar strictures, and cases where penile-skin preservation is a priority.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## 3. Midline Ventral Penile Incision

A direct **longitudinal incision along the ventral raphe** of the penile shaft, providing targeted access without circumferential degloving.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

**Technique.** Longitudinal incision along the ventral raphe directly over the urethra. Skin and dartos divided in the midline; corpus spongiosum exposed. The urethra is then dissected off the corpora cavernosa and opened dorsally or ventrally as needed. Used for **dorsal-skin-flap urethroplasty (modified Orandi)**, **penile-fracture repair**, and **penoscrotal-junction strictures**.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

**Advantages.**

- **Preserves lateral penile-skin blood supply** — only the midline raphe is divided; lateral dartos pedicles intact.
- **Simpler and faster** than full degloving — operative time as short as 60 min in one series.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- Avoids circumcision in uncircumcised patients.
- Useful for **targeted access** to a focal penile-stricture segment without the morbidity of full degloving.
- Can be combined with **penile invagination from a perineal approach** for penoscrotal-junction strictures.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

**Disadvantages.**

- **Limited exposure** vs degloving — not ideal for very long penile strictures or when circumferential access is needed.
- **Visible ventral scar** on the penile shaft (cosmetic concern).
- Not suitable for fasciocutaneous flap harvest (which requires circumferential skin mobilization).
- Less well-studied; smaller case series available.

**Best suited for** focal / short penile strictures, modified Orandi dorsal skin-flap repairs, penoscrotal-junction strictures, and penile-fracture repair.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Comparative Summary

| Feature | Degloving / Circumcising | Kulkarni Invagination | Midline Ventral Incision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Incision site | Subcoronal circumferential | Perineal (no penile incision) | Ventral midline penile shaft |
| Penile-skin disruption | Circumferential (maximal) | None | Minimal (midline only) |
| Exposure quality | Excellent for entire penile urethra | Good for peno-bulbar; limited distally | Targeted / focal |
| Flap harvest possible | Yes (fasciocutaneous) | No | No |
| Ideal stricture length | Any penile length | Long / panurethral (&gt; 8 cm) | Short / focal penile |
| Staged repair feasible | Yes (standard approach) | No (single-stage design) | No |
| Chordee risk | Low | Up to 25% | Low |
| Post-void dribbling | Low | Up to 45% | Low |
| Erectile-function impact | Minimal | Preserved (SHIM unchanged) | Preserved |
| Reported success | ~ 80% overall | 84–90% | ~ 83% (limited data) |

## Contemporary Trends

A multi-institutional surgical-outcomes-research group review (Cotter 2019) showed a striking shift over a 7-year period:<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- **Fasciocutaneous-flap use declined ~ 86%**.
- **Dorsal-graft repairs increased ~ 280%**.

This trend has made the **Kulkarni invagination approach increasingly relevant**, since it facilitates dorsal graft placement for long strictures without the morbidity of degloving.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The **AUA 2023 urethral stricture guideline amendment** notes that success rates appear similar regardless of which technique is used for penile urethroplasty.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

## Quick Selection Guide

| Stricture pattern | Preferred approach |
|---|---|
| Short / focal penile, uncircumcised | Midline ventral incision |
| Mid-length penile, primary repair | Degloving / circumcising |
| Penile + fasciocutaneous-flap harvest planned | Degloving / circumcising |
| Long penile or panurethral (&gt; 8 cm) | Kulkarni invagination |
| Peno-bulbar continuous stricture | Kulkarni invagination |
| LS with glans / meatal involvement | Degloving + staged BMG ([Bracka](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/bracka-two-stage) / [Johanson](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/johanson-two-stage)) |
| Penoscrotal-junction stricture | Midline ventral ± perineal invagination |
