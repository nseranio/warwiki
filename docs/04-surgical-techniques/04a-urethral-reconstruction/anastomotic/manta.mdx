# MANTA Urethroplasty (Mucomucosal Anastomotic Non-Transecting Augmentation)

The **MANTA urethroplasty** — **Mucomucosal Anastomotic Non-Transecting Augmentation** — is a non-transecting bulbar urethroplasty described by **Marks, Dahlem, and Janisch in 2023** (Hamburg) for bulbar strictures ≥2 cm with a focal **obliterative segment ≤1.5 cm**. It combines a **dorsal mucomucosal anastomosis** at the obliterative core with a **ventral onlay BMG**, all approached ventrally and preserving the corpus spongiosum.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For the broader non-transecting family, see [Non-Transecting Bulbar Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/non-transecting-bulbar), [ANTA](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/anta), [MsANTA / Joshi Step](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/joshi-step-msanta), and [ntAAU](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/ntaau). For the transecting alternative, see [Augmented Anastomotic Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/augmented-anastomotic). For graft material, see [Buccal Mucosa Graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa).

---

## Concept and Rationale

MANTA was developed for a specific anatomic problem: the bulbar stricture that contains a **short obliterative core (≤1.5 cm) within a longer overall stricture (≥2 cm)**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- **Pure EPA / VS-EPA** would not span the longer surrounding narrowing.
- **Pure dorsal onlay BMG** cannot reconstruct the obliterative core.
- **Transecting AAU** carries the Redmond / Rourke 2020 HR 4.8 recurrence signal compared with pure dorsal onlay.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **MANTA** anastomoses the dorsal mucosa across the obliterative segment without transecting the spongiosum, then augments the entire strictured length with a ventral onlay graft.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

This preserves spongiosal continuity. Because dorsal scar and mucosa are excised, it should not be described as preserving every mucosal communicant vessel; mucosal preservation is the distinguishing aim of MsANTA.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

---

## Indications

- Bulbar strictures **≥2 cm total length** with a focal **obliterative segment ≤1.5 cm**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Penobulbar location** is feasible (30% of the original Marks 2023 cohort)
- **Redo cases** (26% of the Marks 2023 cohort)
- Patients in whom preservation of the spongiosum is prioritized to reduce sexual dysfunction<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### Contraindications / limitations

- Obliterative segment **&gt;1.5 cm** lies outside the original study criteria; do not extrapolate its results to longer cores. Tension and viable tissue determine feasibility.
- Lichen sclerosus requires assessment of the full diseased segment and an oral-mucosa reconstruction plan; it is not an automatic contraindication to single-stage oral grafting. Avoid genital skin in LS. <sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- Strictures suitable for pure dorsal onlay BMG (where transection / anastomosis is not needed)

## Surgical Technique

**Step 1 — Ventral approach.** Stricture approached ventrally to avoid extensive dorsal mobilization. A perineal midline incision is made; the corpus spongiosum is exposed without transection.

**Step 2 — Ventral urethrotomy.** A ventral midline urethrotomy is performed through the strictured segment, opening the lumen for direct visualization.

**Step 3 — Dorsal scar excision (superficial).** At the level of the obliterative core, the **dorsal scar is superficially excised** while the spongiosum is **left intact** (non-transecting). Healthy dorsal mucosal edges are exposed proximal and distal to the obliterative segment.

**Step 4 — Dorsal mucomucosal anastomosis.** The healthy dorsal mucosal edges are brought together in a **dorsal mucomucosal anastomosis** — the "anastomotic" half of the technique. A short segment alone does not guarantee feasibility: confirm viable edges and a tension-free repair.

**Step 5 — Ventral onlay BMG.** A buccal mucosal graft is placed as a **ventral onlay** to augment the entire strictured segment (including the just-anastomosed dorsal core) — the "augmentation" half of the technique. The graft is sutured to the ventral urethrotomy edges with absorbable sutures (5-0 / 6-0 PDS or Vicryl).

**Step 6 — Closure.** The corpus spongiosum is closed over the graft (spongioplasty); bulbospongiosus muscle and perineal tissues approximated. Catheter duration depends on repair complexity and healing; 2–3 weeks is common after graft reconstruction. Assess for extravasation by urethrography before removal. <sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

The result is a **neourethra of triangular cross-section** — dorsal mucomucosal anastomosis + ventral graft — without spongiosal transection.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Outcomes — Marks 2023 Hamburg Series

| Parameter | Result |
|---|---|
| Number of patients | 54 |
| Median follow-up | 41 months |
| Stricture inclusion criteria | ≥2 cm with obliterative segment ≤1.5 cm |
| Penobulbar location | 30% |
| Redo cases | 26% |
| **Functional success (no retreatment)** | **93%** |
| LUTS score (pre → post) | 13 → 3.5 (p &lt; 0.001) |
| IIEF-EF (pre → post) | 27 → 24 (NS, p ≥ 0.4) |
| Reported satisfaction | All reported respondents satisfied/very satisfied; interpret within this single cohort |

## MANTA vs. Other Non-Transecting Variants

| Feature | VS-EPA / ntEPA<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | ANTA<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | MsANTA<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | **MANTA**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | ntAAU<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Initial report | VS-EPA 2007 / ntEPA 2012 | 2012 | 2022 | **2023** | 2025 |
| Spongiosum | Preserved | Preserved | Preserved | **Preserved** | Preserved |
| Mucosa | Excised | Excised | **Preserved** (incised, not excised) | Dorsal scar superficially excised | Excised |
| Approach | Dorsal or ventral | Dorsal | Dorsal or ventral | **Ventral** | Dorsal |
| Graft | None | Dorsal onlay BMG | Onlay BMG | **Ventral onlay BMG** | Dorsal onlay BMG |
| Anastomosis | Mucosa-to-mucosa | Mucosal | Mucosal | **Dorsal mucomucosal at obliterative core** | Mucosal |
| Ideal stricture | Short ≤2 cm | &gt;2 cm with obliterative core | Narrowed but non-obliterated | **≥2 cm with obliterative ≤1.5 cm** | Long bulbar narrowing with a short critical segment |

## Why MANTA Matters

In the retrospective **Redmond / Rourke 2020** comparison (n = 507), **transecting augmented anastomotic urethroplasty (AAU)** was independently associated with stricture recurrence (HR **4.8**, p = 0.002) compared to pure non-transecting dorsal onlay.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> MANTA has reported 93% functional success in a separate cohort. It was not tested against AAU in that study, so superiority and a causal effect of avoiding transection are unproven.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> For the broader non-transecting evidence base (Chapman 2019 sexual-dysfunction signal,<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Oszczudlowski 2023 meta-analysis,<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Scandinavian RCT), see the umbrella [Non-Transecting Bulbar Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/non-transecting-bulbar) article.

*Named variants overlap; their small cohorts, endpoints and follow-up differ and do not establish a ranking of procedures.*

## Key Takeaways

- MANTA is a **2023 described modification** for the specific scenario of bulbar strictures ≥2 cm with an obliterative segment ≤1.5 cm.
- **Ventral approach with dorsal mucomucosal anastomosis + ventral onlay BMG** — circumferential reconstruction without spongiosal transection.
- **93% functional success** in 54 patients at median 41 months; no significant change in IIEF-EF; all reported respondents satisfied or very satisfied.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Versatile — successfully used in 26% redo cases and 30% penobulbar strictures.
- Sits alongside ANTA, MsANTA, and ntAAU as variants of the augmented non-transecting concept; differentiated by ventral approach + dorsal mucomucosal anastomosis configuration.
