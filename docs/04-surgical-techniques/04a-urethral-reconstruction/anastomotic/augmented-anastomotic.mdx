# Augmented Anastomotic Urethroplasty

**Augmented anastomotic urethroplasty (AAU)** is a hybrid technique that combines excision of the most fibrotic or obliterative segment of a stricture with an onlay graft to reconstruct longer bulbar urethral strictures that are too long for simple [excision and primary anastomosis (EPA)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/excision-primary-anastomosis) alone. Originally described by Guralnick and Webster in 2001, it achieves success rates of 90–97% depending on series and technique, though recent large-scale data suggest the transecting variant may be independently associated with higher recurrence than pure non-transecting dorsal onlay.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For the buccal graft itself, see [Buccal Mucosa Graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa). For vessel-sparing variants, see [Non-Transecting Bulbar Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/non-transecting-bulbar) and [Jordan Vessel-Sparing EPA](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/jordan-vessel-sparing-epa).

---

## Definition and Concept

The core principle of AAU is to address strictures that contain a focal segment of dense fibrosis or obliteration within a longer area of narrowing. Rather than excising the entire stricture (excessive tension) or relying solely on a graft over heavily scarred tissue (poor graft take), AAU excises the worst 1–2 cm segment, performs a partial anastomosis (roof or floor strip), and augments the remaining defect with an onlay graft.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

As Guralnick and Webster originally described it: a short bulbar stricture ≤1 cm is best managed by EPA, but when total stricture length is too great for excision and anastomosis, the surgeon can perform "stricture excision with roof or floor strip anastomosis augmented by an onlay."<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Indications

- **Bulbar urethral strictures &gt;2 cm** with a focal obliterative or densely fibrotic segment not amenable to EPA alone<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Strictures where the obliterative segment is 1–2 cm within a longer area of narrowing (total length typically 2–5+ cm)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Recurrent strictures** after prior endoscopic treatment or urethroplasty<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Strictures where a pure onlay graft would otherwise be placed over heavily scarred, poorly vascularized tissue (the anastomotic component removes that segment)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- The AUA Urethral Stricture Disease Guideline (2023) recommends that surgeons offer urethroplasty as the initial treatment for long (≥2 cm) bulbar strictures (Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level Grade C); it does not specify the urethroplasty technique<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

---

## Surgical Technique

Two main configurations exist, defined by which urethral wall is preserved for the anastomosis and which side receives the graft.

### Configuration 1 — Roof Strip (Dorsal) Anastomosis + Ventral Onlay

1. The strictured segment is exposed via a perineal approach.
2. The corpus spongiosum is fully transected through the most fibrotic segment.
3. The most diseased portion is excised.
4. The dorsal (roof) urethral plate is preserved and anastomosed end-to-end, creating a "roof strip" anastomosis.
5. This leaves an oval ventral defect that is augmented with an onlay graft (buccal mucosa or pedicled skin flap).
6. The graft is sutured to the edges of the ventral defect.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

### Configuration 2 — Floor Strip (Ventral) Anastomosis + Dorsal Onlay

1. Same exposure and excision of the most fibrotic segment.
2. The ventral (floor) urethral wall is preserved and anastomosed.
3. The resulting dorsal defect is augmented with a dorsal onlay graft (typically buccal mucosa placed on the tunica albuginea of the corpora cavernosa, per the Barbagli technique).
4. This configuration is more commonly used in contemporary practice.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

In the original Guralnick / Webster series, 20 of 29 patients received a floor strip anastomosis with dorsal onlay, and 9 received a roof strip anastomosis with ventral onlay. Mean excised length was 1.2 cm and mean onlay length was 4.5 cm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### The Augmented Russell Technique (El-Kassaby)

A specific variant in 234 patients with long bulbar strictures (mean 4.2 cm): excision of most of the diseased segment (mean 2.8 cm) with dorsal strip anastomosis, augmented by a ventral buccal mucosal patch (mean 4.7 cm). The success rate was 93.7% (223 patients completed the follow-up protocol) at mean 36 months.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## The Critical Controversy — AAU vs. Pure Dorsal Onlay

A large retrospective comparison by Redmond and Rourke (2020; 507 patients, mean follow-up 78.9 months) questioned the role of transecting AAU:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- **Transecting AAU was independently associated with stricture recurrence** on multivariate analysis (HR 4.8, p = 0.002)
- Other independent predictors of failure: increasing stricture length (HR 1.2, p = 0.002) and iatrogenic etiology (HR 3.2, p = 0.03)
- **No difference** in Clavien ≥2 complications (3.6% versus 4.2%, p = 0.74) or de novo ED (5.9% versus 5.6%, p = 0.89)
- The institution shifted from routinely performing transecting AAU to non-transecting dorsal onlay around 2011

The institutional shift around 2011 creates potential era and selection confounding. This study supports careful selection of the excisional component; it does not prove that transection itself causes failure, and it did not compare ANTA, MANTA or ntAAU with AAU.

## Complications

Complication rates must remain attached to the operation and cohort studied. In Guralnick and Webster’s 29 AAU repairs, postvoid dribbling occurred in 13 patients, pseudodiverticulum in two, subjective shortening in five and new erectile dysfunction in one.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In Hoy’s 163-patient dorsal-onlay augmented anastomotic series, the reported rates were 41.7% dribbling, 3.7% UTI, 3.1% ED, 10.4% orchalgia and 4.3% donor-site morbidity; these are not rates for pure dorsal onlay.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The direct Redmond comparison reported Clavien ≥2 events in 3.6% versus 4.2% and de novo ED at six months in 5.9% versus 5.6% (AAU versus dorsal onlay), without significant differences.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Avoid using these separate series to claim a universal complication-rate advantage.

---

## Evolution — Non-Transecting Augmented Techniques

Several vessel-sparing augmented modifications have been described subsequently.

**1. ANTA (Augmented Non-Transected Anastomotic), Welk / Kodama 2012.** ANTA was described as the first non-transecting augmented technique. It involves complete stricture excision (mucosectomy) without transecting the spongiosum, combined with dorsal onlay BMG. In the ANTA subgroup, 20/21 required no further treatment (95.2%; 93% was the combined 44-patient ANTA and onlay cohort). The median buccal graft was modestly shorter (4.5 cm versus 5.0 cm, p = 0.047).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

**2. MsANTA (Mucosal-Sparing ANTA), Joshi / Kulkarni 2022.** MsANTA has been described as a refinement in which the native urethral mucosa is neither transected nor resected but reconstructed with a direct mucosa-to-mucosa anastomosis, preserving the communicant vessels from the corpus spongiosum to the urethral mucosa.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

**3. MANTA (Mucomucosal Anastomotic Non-Transecting Augmentation), Marks 2023.** A ventral approach for strictures ≥2 cm with an obliterative segment ≤1.5 cm. The scar is superficially excised dorsally while the spongiosum is left intact. Dorsal mucomucosal anastomosis is complemented by a ventral onlay graft. In 54 patients at median 41 months: 93% functional success, no change in erectile function (IIEF-EF 27 to 24, p ≥ 0.4), and 100% patient satisfaction.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

**4. ntAAU (Non-Transecting Augmented Anastomotic), Baudry 2025.** Non-transecting AAU with dorsal onlay BMG for selected long bulbar strictures with a short critical segment. In 42 patients: 90.5% anatomical success, 83.3% functional success at median 18 months. Complication rate 14.2%. Median postoperative IIEF-5 was 22 in 31 respondents; a postoperative score alone does not establish preserved erectile function.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Graft Placement — Dorsal vs. Ventral Onlay

A 2025 meta-analysis of 8 studies and 655 patients comparing dorsal versus ventral onlay BMG urethroplasty found:<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

- **Success rates.** No significant difference (RR 1.00, 95% CI 0.94–1.06, p = 0.97)
- **Qmax.** Comparable at 3 and 12 months
- **Transient ED.** Significantly lower with ventral technique (RR 0.24, 95% CI 0.08–0.67, p = 0.006)
- **Permanent ED.** No difference (RR 0.57, p = 0.72)

Among GURS members, dorsal graft placement is preferred (66%) over ventral (34%) for bulbar urethroplasty with BMG.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> Choose the graft position according to anatomy, tissue quality and surgeon experience; EAU states that the best location remains to be determined.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

The meta-analysis concerns graft position, not the benefit of adding an anastomotic component. The permanent-ED estimate was imprecise (95% CI 0.03–12.20); absence of a significant difference is not proof of equal risk.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Predictors of Failure

- **Transecting AAU versus dorsal onlay in the Redmond cohort** (adjusted HR 4.8, p = 0.002; association, not a causal effect of all transection)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Increasing stricture length** (HR 1.2, p = 0.002); strictures ≥5 cm are particularly prone to recurrence (p = 0.010)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Iatrogenic etiology** (HR 3.2, p = 0.03)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Older age** (mean 52 versus 39 yr in failures versus successes, p = 0.02)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Postoperative UTI** (28% in failures versus 3.2% in successes, p = 0.05)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Lichen sclerosus** requires a specific oral-mucosa reconstruction plan; one-stage repair is not uniformly contraindicated, and a single series should not define its expected failure rate.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

## Choosing the Reconstruction

| Stricture pattern | Reconstruction to consider |
|---|---|
| Short, traumatic bulbar stricture with nearly complete obliteration and full-thickness fibrosis | Transecting EPA |
| Short bulbar stricture without straddle injury | Non-transecting EPA or a free graft |
| Longer bulbar narrowing with a viable plate | Free-graft augmentation; dorsal, dorsolateral or ventral according to anatomy |
| Longer narrowing with a short nearly obliterative focus | Selected augmented non-transecting repair if viable tissue and a tension-free partial anastomosis are achievable |
| Extensive obliteration or adverse local tissues | Individualized complex or staged reconstruction; do not choose a named variant from length alone |

These selections follow the EAU framework. The augmented non-transecting recommendation is weak, reflecting limited comparative evidence. Total stricture length and length of the obliterative core are different measurements.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

---

## Key Takeaways

- AAU bridges the gap between EPA (short strictures) and pure onlay grafting (longer strictures) by addressing the focal obliterative component that limits graft take.
- Transecting AAU series report 90–94% success, but transecting AAU was independently associated with higher recurrence (HR 4.8) compared to pure non-transecting dorsal onlay in the largest comparative study.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- The field has shifted toward non-transecting augmented techniques (ANTA, MsANTA, MANTA, ntAAU) that preserve the corpus spongiosum while combining mucosectomy / anastomosis with onlay grafting, with favorable outcomes reported in small cohorts; these are not comparative proof of superior patency or sexual function.
- Discuss dribbling, sexual and penile symptoms as well as patency; rates depend on technique, baseline symptoms and measurement method.
- For longer strictures without a focal obliterative segment, EAU recommends free graft urethroplasty (strong) for strictures not amenable to EPA, and the Redmond cohort associated transecting AAU with more recurrence than dorsal onlay.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- Longer strictures were associated with recurrence in the cited AAU cohorts; a universal 5 cm threshold across techniques is not established.
