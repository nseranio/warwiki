# ntAAU Urethroplasty (Non-Transecting Augmented Anastomotic)

The **ntAAU urethroplasty** (Non-Transecting Augmented Anastomotic) is a variant of non-transecting bulbar urethroplasty described by Baudry, Schirmann, and Guillot-Tantay in 2025. It applies the vessel-sparing principle specifically to the traditional augmented anastomotic urethroplasty with dorsal onlay buccal graft: the obliterative segment is excised through the spongiosum without transecting it, and the remaining strictured urethra is augmented with a dorsal onlay BMG.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For the broader non-transecting family, see [Non-Transecting Bulbar Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/non-transecting-bulbar), [ANTA](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/anta), [MsANTA / Joshi Step](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/joshi-step-msanta), and [MANTA](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/manta). For the transecting alternative, see [Augmented Anastomotic Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/augmented-anastomotic). For graft material, see [Buccal Mucosa Graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa).

---

## Concept and Rationale

ntAAU and ANTA (Welk / Kodama 2012) share the same conceptual framework: non-transecting excision of the stricture segment combined with dorsal onlay BMG augmentation. The Baudry 2025 series, which has no comparison group, reports short- to intermediate-term outcomes in selected long bulbar strictures with a critical segment, where pure EPA cannot bridge the defect but the surgeon wishes to avoid full spongiosal transection.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The clinical importance of avoiding spongiosal transection (the Chapman 2019 sexual-dysfunction signal,<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> the Oszczudlowski 2023 meta-analysis,<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> and the Scandinavian RCT) is housed in the umbrella [Non-Transecting Bulbar Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/non-transecting-bulbar) article. The signal most directly relevant to ntAAU is Redmond / Rourke 2020 (n = 507): transecting AAU was independently associated with stricture recurrence (HR 4.8, p = 0.002) versus pure non-transecting dorsal onlay.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Redmond did not include a separate ntAAU arm; that association cannot prove that transection caused recurrence or that ntAAU prevents it.

## Indications

- Longer bulbar strictures with a short, nearly obliterative critical segment that can be excised and reapproximated without tension while preserving viable spongiosum. Do not interpret total stricture length &gt;2 cm as permission to excise a &gt;2 cm obliterative core. <sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- The surgeon wishes to avoid full spongiosal transection with the aim of preserving vascularity; a sexual-function benefit for this specific variant is unproven.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Adequate native urethral plate exists for the dorsal onlay graft to engraft against.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Contraindications / limitations

- Lichen sclerosus requires assessment of the full diseased segment and an oral-mucosa reconstruction plan; it is not an automatic contraindication to single-stage oral grafting. Avoid genital skin in LS. <sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- Strictures suitable for pure dorsal onlay BMG (where the excisional component is unnecessary)
- Strictures where the obliterative segment is too long for safe excision through a non-transected spongiosum

## Surgical Technique

The technique combines the principles of non-transecting excision with dorsal onlay buccal graft augmentation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Step 1. Approach.** Bulbar urethra exposed via a perineal midline incision. The corpus spongiosum is mobilized but not transected.

**Step 2. Mucosectomy of the obliterative segment.** The obliterative segment is excised through the spongiosum (similar to ANTA), removing the fibrotic mucosal core while the outer spongy tissue remains in continuity.

**Step 3. Mucosal anastomosis at the obliterative core.** The healthy mucosal edges at the site of excision are spatulated and anastomosed in a tension-free, mucosa-to-mucosa fashion, the "anastomotic" component.

**Step 4. Dorsal onlay BMG augmentation.** A buccal mucosal graft is placed as a dorsal onlay to augment the remaining strictured urethra, the "augmentation" component. The graft is sutured to the urethral plate edges and quilted to the underlying tunica albuginea of the corpora cavernosa.

**Step 5. Closure.** The dorsal graft lies against the corporal tunica; secure the urethral margins to the graft and close the remaining tissues without constriction. Bulbospongiosus and perineal tissues are approximated. Catheter duration depends on repair complexity and healing; 2–3 weeks is common after graft reconstruction. Assess for extravasation by urethrography before removal. <sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

The spongiosum remains in continuity throughout, preserving the bulbar arteries and the dorsal vascular supply to the urethral mucosa.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Outcomes — Baudry 2025

The Baudry / Schirmann / Guillot-Tantay 2025 series:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Parameter | Result |
|---|---|
| Number of patients | 42 |
| Median follow-up | 18 months |
| Design | Single-arm cohort; no comparator |
| Anatomical success (16.5 Fr cystoscope passage) | 90.5% |
| Functional success (Qmax &gt;15 mL/s) | 83.3% |
| Complication rate | 14.2% (infections, abscess, bleeding) |
| Postoperative median IIEF-5 | 22 among 31 questionnaire respondents |
| Salvage of failures | Redo urethroplasty |

A postoperative IIEF-5 score of 22 does not establish preservation without a paired baseline comparison. Anatomical and functional success use different definitions and should not be interchanged.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## ntAAU vs. ANTA

ntAAU and ANTA are conceptually similar: both excise the strictured segment without transecting the spongiosum and augment with dorsal onlay BMG. The two reports differ in cohort size, follow-up and outcome definitions.

| Feature | ANTA<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | **ntAAU**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
|---|---|---|
| Year | 2012 | **2025** |
| Spongiosum | Preserved | **Preserved** |
| Mucosa | Excised (mucosectomy) | **Excised (mucosectomy)** |
| Approach | Dorsal | **Dorsal** |
| Graft | Dorsal onlay BMG | **Dorsal onlay BMG** |
| Series indication | Bulbar stricture treated by complete excision plus onlay | **Long bulbar stricture with a critical segment** |
| Original n | 21 | **42** |
| Success | 20/21 without retreatment; median cohort follow-up 2.3 years | **90.5% anatomical / 83.3% functional at 18 months** |

The Baudry 2025 series provides larger contemporary outcome data for the indication that ANTA originally targeted, with postoperative IIEF-5 documentation but no proof of an erectile-function advantage.

*Named variants overlap; their small cohorts, endpoints and follow-up differ and do not establish a ranking of procedures.*

## Key Takeaways

- ntAAU is a 2025 reported non-transecting augmented anastomotic technique (Baudry 2025).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- ntAAU is conceptually similar to ANTA (2012): non-transecting excision and dorsal onlay BMG. Selection rests on total stricture length, the short critical segment and viable residual spongiosum.
- Anatomical success was 90.5% and functional success 83.3% in 42 patients at a median follow-up of 18 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The EAU 2026 guideline reports 87% of patients satisfied or very satisfied and makes a weak recommendation for augmented non-transecting anastomotic repair in short, nearly obliterative segments (level of evidence 3).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- Median postoperative IIEF-5 was 22 in 31 respondents; preserved function or superiority over transecting AAU cannot be inferred.
- No direct comparative advantage over transecting AAU can be inferred from the separate Redmond cohort.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Failures were managed with redo urethroplasty. The complication rate was 14.2%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
