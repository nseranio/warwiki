# ntAAU Urethroplasty (Non-Transecting Augmented Anastomotic)

The **ntAAU urethroplasty** (Non-Transecting Augmented Anastomotic) is a variant of non-transecting bulbar urethroplasty described by Baudry, Schirmann, and Guillot-Tantay in 2025. It applies the vessel-sparing principle specifically to the traditional augmented anastomotic urethroplasty with dorsal onlay buccal graft: the stenotic mucosa and superficial spongiofibrosis are excised while preserving the deeper spongiosum in continuity, and the remaining stricture is augmented with a dorsal onlay BMG.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For the broader non-transecting family, see [Non-Transecting Bulbar Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/non-transecting-bulbar), [ANTA](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/anta), [MsANTA / Joshi Step](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/joshi-step-msanta), and [MANTA](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/manta). For the transecting alternative, see [Augmented Anastomotic Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/augmented-anastomotic). For graft material, see [Buccal Mucosa Graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa).

---

## Concept and Rationale

ntAAU and ANTA (Welk / Kodama 2012) share the same conceptual framework: non-transecting excision of the stricture segment combined with dorsal onlay BMG augmentation. The single-arm Baudry 2025 series reports outcomes at a median 18 months in 42 patients with bulbar strictures containing a nearly obliterative or obliterative segment; its 90.5% anatomical success is reported for the full cohort.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> For a longer stricture whose critical segment is short, ntAAU combines focal excision with dorsal graft augmentation without full spongiosal transection.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The clinical importance of avoiding spongiosal transection (the Chapman 2019 sexual-dysfunction signal,<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> the Oszczudlowski 2023 meta-analysis,<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> and the 151-patient Scandinavian RCT of transecting EPA versus BMG for unreconstructed bulbar strictures ≤2 cm<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>) is housed in the umbrella [Non-Transecting Bulbar Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/non-transecting-bulbar) article. In Redmond / Rourke's retrospective 507-patient comparison of transecting augmented anastomotic urethroplasty and pure dorsal onlay BMG, the anastomotic group had higher adjusted recurrence (HR 4.8, p = 0.002).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Redmond did not include a separate ntAAU arm; that association cannot prove that transection caused recurrence or that ntAAU prevents it.

---

## Indications

- For bulbar strictures not amenable to EPA, the EAU 2026 guideline strongly recommends free graft urethroplasty and weakly recommends augmented non-transecting anastomosis when a short, nearly obliterative core can be excised and reapproximated without tension while preserving viable spongiosum. Its level-3 evidence summary concerns total length &gt;2 cm with a critical area &lt;1 cm.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- The surgeon wishes to avoid full spongiosal transection with the aim of preserving vascularity; a sexual-function benefit for this specific variant is unproven.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Viable urethral plate edges remain for graft suturing, and the corporal tunica provides the bed for the dorsal onlay graft.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Contraindications / limitations

- For LS-related disease, assess the full affected segment and local tissue quality. For penile strictures due to LS, EAU 2026 weakly recommends single-stage oral mucosa graft urethroplasty in the absence of adverse local conditions; for LS-proven urethral stricture, AUA 2023 strongly recommends against genital skin reconstruction.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- Strictures suitable for pure dorsal onlay BMG (where the excisional component is unnecessary)
- Strictures whose obliterative core is too long for tension-free mucosal anastomosis while retaining viable spongiosum
- Short post-traumatic bulbar strictures with nearly complete or complete obliteration and full-thickness spongiofibrosis: use transecting EPA (EAU 2026 strong recommendation).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Surgical Technique

The technique combines the principles of non-transecting excision with dorsal onlay buccal graft augmentation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Step 1. Approach.** Bulbar urethra exposed via a perineal midline incision. The urethra is mobilized through the bulbospongiosus while preserving lateral structures and keeping the corpus spongiosum in continuity.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

**Step 2. Dorsal urethrotomy and focal mucosectomy.** Open the bulbar urethra dorsally to assess the most stenotic segment. As in ANTA, excise the scarred mucosa and superficial spongiofibrosis while preserving the deeper spongiosum in continuity.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

**Step 3. Mucosal anastomosis at the obliterative core.** The healthy mucosal edges at the site of excision are spatulated and anastomosed in a tension-free, mucosa-to-mucosa fashion, the "anastomotic" component.

**Step 4. Dorsal onlay BMG augmentation.** A buccal mucosal graft is placed as a dorsal onlay to augment the remaining strictured urethra, the "augmentation" component. The graft is sutured to the urethral plate edges and quilted to the underlying tunica albuginea of the corpora cavernosa.

**Step 5. Closure.** The dorsal graft lies against the corporal tunica; secure the urethral margins to the graft and close the remaining tissues without constriction. Bulbospongiosus and perineal tissues are approximated. Catheter duration depends on repair complexity and healing; 2–3 weeks is common after graft reconstruction. In Baudry’s ntAAU series, a suprapubic catheter was placed at surgery, the urethral catheter was removed at one week, and VCUG through the suprapubic tube at three weeks preceded suprapubic catheter removal if no extravasation was seen.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> Assess for extravasation by urethrography before removal. <sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

The spongiosum remains in continuity throughout, avoiding complete spongiosal transection. Preserving bulbar arterial flow is the operative rationale; perfusion was not measured in the Baudry series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Outcomes — Baudry 2025

The Baudry / Schirmann / Guillot-Tantay 2025 series:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Parameter | Result |
|---|---|
| Number of patients | 42 |
| Median follow-up | 18 months |
| Design | Retrospective, single-center, single-surgeon cohort; no comparator<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Anatomical success (16.5 Fr cystoscope passage) | 90.5%<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Functional success (Qmax &gt;15 mL/s) | 83.3%<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Complications | 6/42 (14.2%) early events (urinary infection, suprapubic catheter obliteration, abscess, bleeding); the authors' 2024 preprint specifies 2/42 (4.8%) Clavien–Dindo grade III events (hematuria and abscess requiring reoperation)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Postoperative median IIEF-5 | 22 among sexually active respondents in a cohort prescribed tadalafil 5 mg daily after catheter removal for six months to one year<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Salvage of failures | Redo urethroplasty |

Anatomical and functional success use different definitions and should not be interchanged.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> After graft urethroplasty, EAU 2026 strongly recommends at least one year of routine follow-up and weakly recommends flexible cystoscopy at three months.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> Its proposed standard-risk graft schedule includes uroflowmetry, PROMs, and anatomical evaluation at 3, 12, and 24 months, followed by uroflowmetry and PROMs at five years with anatomical evaluation on indication.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

## ntAAU vs. ANTA

ntAAU and ANTA are conceptually similar: both excise the strictured segment without transecting the spongiosum and augment with dorsal onlay BMG. The two reports differ in cohort size, follow-up and outcome definitions.

| Feature | ANTA<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | **ntAAU**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
|---|---|---|
| Year | 2012 | **2025** |
| Spongiosum | Preserved | **Preserved** |
| Mucosa | Excised (mucosectomy) | **Excised (mucosectomy)** |
| Approach | Dorsal | **Dorsal** |
| Graft | Dorsal onlay BMG | **Dorsal onlay BMG** |
| Series indication | Bulbar stricture treated by complete excision plus onlay | **Bulbar stricture &gt;1 cm with a nearly obliterative or obliterative segment; reported mean total length 19.8 mm**<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Original n | 21 | **42** |
| Success | 20/21 without retreatment; median cohort follow-up 2.3 years<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | **90.5% anatomical / 83.3% functional; median follow-up 18 months**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |

The Baudry 2025 series provides larger contemporary outcome data for the indication that ANTA originally targeted, with postoperative IIEF-5 documentation but no proof of an erectile-function advantage.

*Named variants overlap; their small cohorts, endpoints and follow-up differ and do not establish a ranking of procedures.*

## Key Takeaways

- ntAAU is a 2025 reported non-transecting augmented anastomotic technique (Baudry 2025).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- ntAAU is conceptually similar to ANTA (2012): non-transecting excision and dorsal onlay BMG. Selection rests on total stricture length, the short critical segment and viable residual spongiosum.
- Anatomical success was 90.5% and functional success 83.3% in 42 patients at a median follow-up of 18 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In the Baudry series, 27 of 31 questionnaire respondents (87%; 31 of 42 treated patients responded) were satisfied or very satisfied.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The EAU 2026 guideline cites this result and makes a weak recommendation for augmented non-transecting anastomotic repair in short, nearly obliterative segments (level of evidence 3).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- In Baudry's cohort, postoperative median IIEF-5 was 22 among sexually active questionnaire respondents prescribed tadalafil 5 mg daily after catheter removal.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- No direct comparative advantage over transecting AAU can be inferred from the separate Redmond cohort.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Failures were managed with redo urethroplasty. The complication rate was 14.2%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
