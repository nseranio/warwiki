# Non-Transecting Bulbar Urethroplasty

Non-transecting bulbar urethroplasty comprises repairs that preserve spongiosal continuity while reconstructing the narrowed urethra. Comparative evidence suggests fewer penile complications, with no detected difference in recurrence; an erectile-function benefit is less consistent. The operation can involve mucosal excision, a limited stricturoplasty or graft augmentation according to the defect, so the variants are not one identical procedure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Historical Development and Rationale

Spongiosum-preserving anastomotic urethroplasty was introduced by Jordan et al. in 2007 and described in a preliminary series of 22 patients by Andrich and Mundy in 2012.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Preserving viable spongiosum avoids unnecessary disruption of urethral blood supply, which is particularly relevant in non-traumatic strictures with superficial fibrosis. Nearly obliterative short straddle-injury strictures with full-thickness fibrosis remain an indication for tEPA (EAU strong recommendation). <sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> Penile shortening and reduced glans filling are reported outcomes; a single ischemic mechanism has not been proven to explain all postoperative sexual symptoms.

By preserving the corpus spongiosum and its vasculature, ntBU aims to:

- Maintain bulbar artery blood flow and limit glans ischemia
- Reduce de novo erectile dysfunction
- Preserve a well-vascularized tissue bed for future redo surgery with grafts
- Reduce penile shortening and chordee

These are proposed benefits. Prospective comparisons with validated questionnaires were lacking when the technique was described, and the randomized data address penile complications rather than erectile function.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Indications

- Short bulbar strictures, usually around 2 cm or less, without full-thickness traumatic obliteration; longer defects may need a graft <sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- Isolated short posterior urethral strictures (88.5% success in 26 patients treated with vessel-sparing EPA)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Both primary and recurrent strictures
- Long (&gt;2 cm) bulbar strictures with a short critical segment, using augmented non-transecting variants. In a retrospective single-surgeon series of 97 patients (32 with strictures &gt;2 cm), functional success was 94% for lengthy and 97% for short strictures at a median follow-up of 8.4 months.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- Can be combined with buccal mucosal grafts for longer strictures (ANTA, MANTA, and ntAAU variants; see below)<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

## Surgical Technique

The procedure is performed through a perineal incision in lithotomy position. Two main approaches exist, dorsal and ventral, sharing the core principle of spongiosal preservation.

### Dorsal Non-Transecting Anastomotic Approach

Jordan’s vessel-sparing repair and subsequent non-transecting modifications share a vascular-preservation aim but differ in dissection. The following summarizes the dorsal approach; scar depth and tissue viability determine how much spongiosum can be preserved.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

1. **Exposure:** Midline perineal incision; bulbospongiosus muscle divided in the midline; corpus spongiosum fully mobilized from the corpora cavernosa.
2. **Dorsal urethrotomy:** Expose the dorsal surface without excessive torsion of the preserved attachments. A dorsal midline urethrotomy is made through the strictured segment, extending into healthy urethra proximally and distally. The spongiosum is not transected.
3. **Mucosectomy:** Fibrotic mucosal tissue is excised from within the intact spongiosa. The outer spongiosal shell and its blood supply are preserved throughout.
4. **Spatulation:** Healthy mucosal edges are spatulated on opposite sides to widen the lumen.
5. **Anastomosis:** A tension-free, mucosa-to-mucosa anastomosis is performed using 5-0 absorbable sutures (interrupted or running). The dorsal urethrotomy is then closed.
6. **Spongioplasty:** The corpus spongiosum is re-approximated over the anastomosis with 4-0 absorbable sutures.
7. **Catheter:** Catheter size and duration are tailored to the repair; selected uncomplicated short repairs may allow early removal. Urethrography should assess extravasation before removal. <sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

### Ventral Approach

Described independently by Morán et al. and Bogdanov et al. as an alternative that avoids extensive dorsal mobilization of the corpus spongiosum.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

1. A ventral midline urethrotomy is made through the stricture.
2. Mucosal scar tissue is excised (mucosectomy) while preserving the dorsal spongiosum.
3. Healthy mucosal edges are approximated without tension.
4. Mucosal closure is performed in a Heineke–Mikulicz fashion using 5-0 absorbable monofilament; spongioplasty with 4-0 absorbable suture.

The ventral approach avoids circumferential spongiosa mobilization, which may be advantageous in patients with prior ventral dissection or anatomic constraints.

## Augmented Non-Transecting Techniques

For strictures &gt;2 cm or those with an obliterative component, the non-transecting principle has been extended by combining internal mucosectomy with buccal mucosal graft augmentation.

| Variant | Description | Key Outcome Data |
|---|---|---|
| **ANTA** (augmented non-transected anastomotic urethroplasty) | Mucosectomy + dorsal onlay BMG; smaller graft than pure onlay BMG | 20/21 without further treatment; 93% was the combined 44-patient ANTA/onlay cohort. Median graft 4.5 versus 5 cm (p = 0.047)<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **MANTA** (mucomucosal anastomotic non-transecting augmentation) | For strictures ≥2 cm with obliterative segment ≤1.5 cm; ventral modification | 93% functional success at median 41 months; IIEF-EF 27 → 24 (p ≥ 0.4); all patients satisfied or very satisfied<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **ntAAU** (non-transecting augmented anastomotic urethroplasty with dorsal onlay BMG) | 42 patients; dorsal onlay technique | 90.5% anatomical success at median 18 months; median IIEF-5 of 22 postoperatively<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |

An important caveat for augmented repairs: in long bulbar strictures, transecting augmented anastomotic urethroplasty was independently associated with failure compared to non-transecting dorsal onlay (HR 4.8, p = 0.002), in a retrospective institutional comparison with an era change. It did not compare the named non-transecting augmented variants, and cannot establish transection as the cause of failure.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

## Outcomes

### Stricture-Free Rates

Multiple meta-analyses and comparative studies consistently demonstrate no significant difference in stricture recurrence between transecting and non-transecting approaches:

- Retrospective multi-institutional comparison (352 patients): 97.9% with non-transecting versus 93.8% with transecting repair (p = 0.18)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Meta-analysis of 19 studies (Oszczudlowski et al.): pooled RR 1.06 (95% CI 0.82–1.36); no difference<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Meta-analysis of 13 studies, 1,683 patients (Zhao et al.): no significant difference in success rate, Qmax, or overall complications<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### Sexual Function and Penile Outcomes

This is the primary domain of benefit for ntBU:

- **Multi-institutional analysis (Chapman et al., 352 patients):** An adverse change in sexual function was reported by 4.3% after non-transecting versus 14.3% after transecting repair (p = 0.008). On multivariate analysis, only the transecting technique was independently associated with sexual dysfunction (p = 0.01); age, stricture length, etiology, and surgeon were not.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Meta-analysis (Zhao et al., 1,683 patients):** ntBU had a significantly lower incidence of erectile dysfunction compared to transecting bulbar urethroplasty (tBU); no differences in other perioperative outcomes.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Meta-analysis (Oszczudlowski et al., 19 studies):** ED risk ratio 0.73 (95% CI 0.49–1.08), trending toward benefit but not statistically significant. Penile complications were significantly lower: RR 0.47 (95% CI 0.28–0.76, p &lt; 0.05).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Scandinavian Urethroplasty Study (Nilsen et al.), randomized trial (151 patients, strictures ≤2 cm, 12-month follow-up):** Compared transecting EPA to non-transecting BMG. tEPA had significantly more penile complications (p = 0.02), particularly reduced glans filling (p = 0.03) and shortened penis (p = 0.001). No difference in IIEF-5 scores. Recurrence was 12.9% in both groups, but the study was not designed to detect differences in recurrence. The penile complications questionnaire was not validated.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Lumen et al. (75 patients, 55 bulbar and 20 posterior, median follow-up 30 months):** Stricture recurred in 8%. De novo ED was reported by 7 of 32 evaluable patients (21.9%) and post-void dribbling by 2 of 42 (4.7%) at 3 months.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Frankiewicz et al. multicenter IIEF analysis:** Preoperative erectile function and younger age were the strongest predictors of erectile function preservation; diabetes was associated with worse short-term outcomes.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

**2026 long-term randomized follow-up:** Holm and colleagues again report more penile shortening and reduced glans filling after tEPA than BMG, with similar recurrence. This extends the original trial; it is not a randomized comparison of ntEPA, ANTA or MANTA. <sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

## Predictors of Outcomes

- Preoperative erectile function and younger age are the strongest predictors of postoperative erectile function preservation<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Diabetes** is associated with worse short-term erectile function outcomes<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Complex strictures** involving both penile and bulbar regions adversely affect long-term patency<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Stricture length ≥5 cm** is associated with lower patency rates when augmented techniques are applied<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- A small single-center analysis did not detect a learning-curve effect; this does not imply the operation needs no supervised learning<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

---

## Practice Trends

In a 2022 survey of Society of Genitourinary Reconstructive Surgeons (GURS) members (134 of 350 invited responded; published 2024), dorsal graft placement was preferred (66%) over ventral (34%) for bulbar urethroplasty with BMG. Anastomotic urethroplasty was preferred over BMG for short bulbomembranous strictures after radiotherapy (63% versus 37%).<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> The survey did not ask about transecting versus non-transecting technique. Meta-analyses associate non-transecting repair with fewer penile complications and similar recurrence. For longer strictures needing augmentation, a retrospective comparison associated dorsal onlay with less recurrence than transecting augmented anastomotic repair, subject to selection and era confounding.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[8]](#ref8)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
