# Muscle-Sparing Urethroplasty

**Muscle-sparing urethroplasty** encompasses two related but distinct concepts: (1) the Barbagli muscle- and nerve-sparing bulbar urethroplasty (preserving the bulbospongiosus muscle during standard bulbar urethroplasty) and (2) the Kulkarni one-sided dorsolateral dissection technique (preserving the contralateral bulbospongiosus muscle, neurovascular bundle, and central tendon). Evidence on whether bulbospongiosus muscle sparing improves functional outcomes is conflicting; some studies show significant reductions in postvoid dribbling and ejaculatory dysfunction, while a matched multicenter study and a later single-center comparative study with alternating allocation found no significant benefit.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

For non-transecting anastomotic alternatives that also preserve corporal vascular supply, see [Non-Transecting Bulbar Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/non-transecting-bulbar) and [Jordan Vessel-Sparing EPA](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/jordan-vessel-sparing-epa). For augmented variants, see [Augmented Anastomotic Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/augmented-anastomotic). For graft details, see [Buccal Mucosa Graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa).

## Anatomy and Rationale

The **bulbospongiosus (BS) muscle** is a paired, striated muscle that envelops the ventral surface of the bulbar urethra and corpus spongiosum. It is innervated by the perineal branch of the pudendal nerve and plays a role in:<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- **Urinary expulsion.** Compresses the bulbar urethra during the final phase of micturition to expel residual urine
- **Ejaculation.** Rhythmic contraction propels semen through the urethra
- **Erection support.** Contributes to penile rigidity by compressing the bulb of the penis

In standard bulbar urethroplasty, the BS muscle is divided in the midline raphe to expose the underlying corpus spongiosum. The concern is that midline division damages the muscle's innervation and vascularization, leading to postoperative postvoid dribbling (PVD) and ejaculatory dysfunction (EjD).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Technique 1 — Barbagli Muscle- and Nerve-Sparing Bulbar Urethroplasty (2008)

First described by [Barbagli](/docs/roots/surgeons/a-g/guido-barbagli) et al. in 2008 in *European Urology*, this technique avoids dissection of the BS muscle from the corpus spongiosum entirely.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### Surgical steps

1. **Perineal incision.** Standard midline perineal incision.
2. **Muscle preservation.** Instead of splitting the BS muscle in the midline, access the bulbar urethra by dissecting between the BS muscle and the ischiocavernosus muscle on one side, leaving the BS muscle attached to the corpus spongiosum and the central tendon of the perineum intact.
3. **One-sided mobilization.** The urethra is mobilized from the corpora cavernosa on one side only, preserving the contralateral neurovascular attachments.
4. **Urethrotomy and graft placement.** A dorsal or ventral urethrotomy is performed, and an oral mucosal graft is placed as an onlay.
5. **Closure.** The graft is sutured in place and the wound is closed without disrupting the BS muscle.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### Original series (Barbagli 2008)

- 12 patients, mean age 43.6 yr, mean stricture length 4.47 cm
- 6 ventral onlay + 6 dorsal onlay BMG
- 0% postvoid dribbling and 0% semen sequestration at 6 and 12 months
- **0% stricture recurrence** at mean 15.25 mo
- No urethral sacculation on voiding cystourethrography<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Technique 2 — Kulkarni One-Sided Dorsolateral Onlay Urethroplasty

Kulkarni et al. described one-sided dorsal oral-mucosa grafting for both bulbar and panurethral strictures; their original series included mean lengths of 4.2 cm and 10 cm, respectively. Later long-segment cohorts selected strictures &gt;8 cm.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> It inherently spares the contralateral BS muscle and neurovascular bundle through one-sided dissection.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### Surgical steps

1. **Perineal approach with penile invagination.** The entire anterior urethra is accessed through a single perineal incision by invaginating the penile skin.
2. **One-sided urethral mobilization.** The urethra is mobilized from the corpora cavernosa on one side only (typically the left), preserving the contralateral lateral vascular supply, BS muscle attachment, and perineal nerve branches.
3. **Dorsolateral urethrotomy.** The strictured urethra is opened along the dorsolateral surface.
4. **Graft placement.** An oral mucosal graft (selected non-LS cases may use hairless penile skin) is placed as a dorsolateral onlay, sutured to the tunica albuginea of the corpora cavernosa on one side and the urethral margin on the other. The cited penile-skin series excluded LS-related disease, unavailable penile skin, and obliterative strictures.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
5. **Closure.** The urethra is rotated back to its anatomic position.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### Outcomes of the Kulkarni technique

| Study | n | Mean Stricture Length | Success | Follow-up | Key Findings |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spencer 2018 (multi-institutional)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | 73 | 13.6 cm | 88% | 44 mo | IPSS improved 23 → 10 (p &lt; 0.001); PVD 45%; chordee 25% (described as transient in the abstract) |
| Kartal 2020 (vs Barbagli)<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | 31 vs 37 | Mean not reported; enrolled strictures &gt;8 cm | 87.1% vs 70.3% | ~60 mo | Shorter OR time, less blood loss, fewer complications with Kulkarni |
| Zumrutbas 2020<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> | 35 | 13.6 cm | 82.9% | ≥6 mo | 88.6% satisfaction; recurrence 35.7% in age &gt;65 vs 4.8% in ≤65 |
| Takekawa 2025<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | 39 | 44 mm (median) | 89.7% | 12 mo (median) | SHIM unchanged; OMG 80% vs PSG 95.8% success |
| Palminteri 2016 (penile skin)<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> | 37 | 5 cm (median) | 92% | 21 mo | Safe with penile skin graft; 81% had prior treatments |

### Kulkarni vs. conventional Barbagli dorsal onlay

In the retrospective comparison by Kartal et al. (2020) of strictures &gt;8 cm (31 Kulkarni versus 37 conventional Barbagli dorsal onlay), the Kulkarni technique had lower operative time, blood loss, hospital stay and perioperative complications:<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

- **Shorter operative time:** 179.5 versus 195.5 min (p = 0.037)
- **Less blood loss:** 164.3 versus 202.4 mL (p = 0.033)<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Shorter hospital stay:** 3.5 versus 4.4 days (p = 0.002)
- **Fewer perioperative complications:** 16.1% versus 37.8% (p = 0.046)<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Success:** 87.1% versus 70.3% at a mean follow-up of about 60 months (the abstract gives no p value)<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Technique 3 — Proximal Dorsal, Two-Sleeve-Preserving Approach (Bandini / Joshi)

For proximal bulbar strictures below the bulbocavernosus sleeves, Bandini and Joshi describe a dorsal approach that preserves both bulbospongiosus sleeves rather than dividing the midline sleeve or incising a single side: the perineal dissection begins below the sleeves, the dorsal urethra is exposed, a 12-o'clock urethrotomy is performed, a buccal graft is placed over the corpora cavernosa, and the urethra is closed over a 14 Fr silicone catheter. The bulbospongiosus muscle itself is not reconstructed. The preliminary chapter report describes 17 positive outcomes among 19 patients and no associated complications, without outcome definitions, follow-up duration, a comparator or a complication denominator for comparison with the Barbagli or Kulkarni approaches above.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

## The Controversy — Does BS Muscle Sparing Actually Matter?

This is the central debate. The evidence is conflicting.

| Study | Design | n | Technique | PVD (sparing vs non-sparing) | EjD (sparing vs non-sparing) | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Barbagli 2008<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | Case series | 12 | Dorsal or ventral onlay | 0/12 (PVD) | 0/12 (semen sequestration) | Feasibility series; symptoms assessed with a nonvalidated questionnaire at 6 and 12 months |
| Fredrick / Vanni 2017<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | Matched case-control | 50 | Mixed EPA / augmentation | No significant difference reported | MSHQ p = 0.90 | No difference detected |
| Elkady 2019<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | Randomized trial | 50 | Ventral onlay BMG | 1/25 vs 9/25 (PVD) | 2/25 vs 10/25 (semen sequestration) | Less PVD and semen sequestration at the reported follow-up |
| Farias / Martins 2025<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Prospective, alternating allocation | 70 | EPA | 2.9% vs 5.7% (p = 0.64) | No difference (p = 0.72) | No statistically significant benefit detected for PVD or MSHQ-EjD-SF score change at 3–15 mo |
| Theisen 2021<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | Prospective cohort | 728 | All anterior | — | — | EjD/PVD occur equally after penile repairs (no BS muscle involved) |

### Studies supporting muscle sparing

- **Elkady 2019.** Prospective RCT, 50 patients (ventral onlay BMG): PVD in 1/25 (4%) muscle-sparing versus 9/25 (36%) non-sparing; semen sequestration in 2/25 (8%) versus 10/25 (40%). Both differences statistically significant.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Barbagli 2008.** 0% PVD and 0% semen sequestration in all 12 muscle-sparing patients.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### Studies showing no benefit

- **Fredrick / Vanni 2017.** Multi-institutional matched case-control, 50 patients (25 versus 25), with postoperative assessment at 3 to 12 months: MSHQ scores were similar between groups (15.24 versus 15.40, p = 0.90). PVD rates and perceived ejaculatory function were also similar.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Farias / Martins 2025.** Prospective comparative study with alternating allocation, 70 patients (35 versus 35, EPA): MSHQ-EjD-SF decrease was 0.59 versus 0.67 (p = 0.72). PVD: 1 versus 2 patients (p = 0.64). The results did not show an advantage for the muscle-sparing group. Although the abstract calls assignment random, the Methods describe alternating consecutive patients; this predictable allocation limits causal inference. The outcomes do not directly measure or exclude injury to innervation or vascularity.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Evidence suggesting more than one mechanism

- **Theisen 2021.** 728 patients: EjD and PVD occurred at similar rates after penile and bulbar urethroplasty. Since the BS muscle is not involved in penile repairs, this argues against BS muscle damage as the primary etiology of these symptoms. The authors found a significant association between EjD and PVD (p &lt; 0.05).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Postvoid Dribbling and Ejaculatory Dysfunction — The Bigger Picture

Questionnaire studies report substantial postoperative PVD and EjD. These are symptom prevalence figures, not necessarily new symptoms caused by surgery, and definitions vary:<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

- **PVD:** 8–70% depending on series, technique and definition. In the Theisen cohort it was more common after bulbar augmentation than after anastomotic repair (70% versus 52%, p = 0.0001).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **EjD:** 67% overall in a 728-patient series, where the single questionnaire item assessed ejaculatory force.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Among survey respondents (32 dorsal buccal onlay; 96 anastomotic; 71.5% of the eligible cohort), PVD most or all of the time was reported by 28.1% versus 8.3%, respectively (p &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- Among postoperative questionnaire respondents (71.5% of the 179 eligible patients), tethering with erections was reported more often after anastomotic repair (23.4%) than dorsal buccal onlay (3.1%, p = 0.008).<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

EjD and PVD occur at similar rates after penile repairs, where the BS muscle is not involved, and after anastomotic repairs. Theisen and colleagues read this as evidence against BS muscle damage as the sole cause; they found EjD and PVD associated with each other and conclude that the cause remains unknown.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

Persistent, bothersome or delayed-onset dribbling after urethroplasty should prompt reassessment rather than an assumption of a benign muscle-related symptom: pooling can be mistaken for urinary incontinence, and new or delayed dribbling can also signal stricture recurrence or a urethral sacculation, which urethrography can distinguish. Perineal compression after voiding is a possible conservative measure once pooling is confirmed and recurrence or sacculation have been excluded, not a first response to a new symptom.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

For the broader non-transecting evidence base on sexual dysfunction after bulbar urethroplasty, see Chapman 2019 (multi-institutional NT versus T comparative analysis), Oszczudlowski 2023 meta-analysis, Virasoro / DeLong 2020 review, the Andrich / Mundy 2012 preliminary NT report, and Verla 2019 vessel-sparing operative video.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

## Practical Considerations

### When to consider muscle sparing

- The Kulkarni approach permits reconstruction of long anterior strictures through a perineal incision. Lower blood loss and shorter operative time were observed in a small comparative series; universal superiority has not been established.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- For standard bulbar urethroplasty, the evidence does not strongly support routine muscle sparing as a standalone modification, given the conflicting data.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### Technical limitations

- Muscle-sparing approaches may provide limited exposure, particularly for complex or recurrent strictures.
- Penile invagination is used when distal or penile access is required; it is not necessary for every isolated bulbar one-sided repair.
- Extent of mobilization must be tailored to scar, graft position and safe exposure.

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Barbagli's muscle- and nerve-sparing technique** (2008) avoids midline BS muscle splitting by accessing the urethra between the BS and ischiocavernosus muscles, preserving the central tendon.
- **Kulkarni's one-sided dorsolateral technique** inherently spares the contralateral BS muscle and is used for long anterior strictures; reported success and functional outcomes come from heterogeneous, mostly retrospective cohorts.
- The evidence on whether BS muscle sparing improves PVD and EjD is conflicting: one RCT (Elkady) shows significant benefit, while two comparative studies (Fredrick / Vanni, Farias / Martins) show no difference.
- In a 728-patient cohort (Theisen), EjD and PVD occurred at similar rates after penile repairs, where the BS muscle is not involved, which argues against BS muscle injury as the sole cause.
- The evidence is insufficient to recommend routine muscle or nerve sparing solely to prevent dribbling or ejaculatory change. <sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
