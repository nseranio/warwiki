# Orandi Flap (Longitudinal Ventral Penile Skin Flap)

The **Orandi flap** (longitudinal ventral penile skin island flap) is an early pedicled penile skin flap for urethral reconstruction, described by Amin Orandi in 1968.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> It is a vascularized island of penile skin on a dartos fascia pedicle, used for single-stage urethroplasty, and it influenced later penile skin-flap designs. Reported success rates for penile skin flaps range from 78% to 90% at intermediate follow-up, and Greenwell, Venn and Mundy called the Orandi procedure the "gold standard" for patch urethroplasty of an uncomplicated penile urethral stricture in 1999.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

For the transverse / circular variants, see [McAninch Transverse Circular Penile Skin Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/mcaninch-circular-flap). For the broader penile / preputial flap anatomy, see [Pedicled Penile / Preputial Skin Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/penile-preputial). For graft alternatives, see [Dorsal Onlay OMG Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/dorsal-onlay-omg).

## Historical Significance and Evolution

### The original Orandi technique (1968)

**Amin Orandi** described a one-stage urethroplasty using a longitudinally oriented island of ventral penile skin, raised on a pedicle of dartos fascia, and transposed as a ventral onlay patch to augment the strictured urethra.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> The technique made several contributions:

- It was an early pedicled island-flap technique for adult stricture reconstruction.
- It showed that dartos fascia can serve as a vascular pedicle, carrying the subdermal vascular plexus to the skin island.
- It was an early example of single-stage substitution urethroplasty, in which the urethra is reconstructed in one operation instead of two.
- It showed that hairless penile skin can serve as a urethral substitute.

### Subsequent developments

The Orandi flap directly inspired the next generation of penile skin flap techniques:

- **Quartey 1983.** Extended the concept to a transverse distal penile or preputial island flap supplied by the superficial external pudendal vessels, which in the original report reached from the meatus to the prostatic urethra.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **McAninch 1993.** Developed the circular fasciocutaneous flap, a hairless distal penile flap that uses Buck's fascia as its vascular conduit and provides up to 15 cm of length.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Srivastava 2012.** Described the circumpenile flap for circumcised patients, using remaining penile shaft skin as a dorsal onlay flap.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Barbagli / Kulkarni 2019.** Modified the Orandi flap to a dorsal onlay configuration, intended to reduce sacculation or diverticulum formation of the classic ventral placement.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Anatomy and Vascular Basis

### Dartos fascia — the universal penile flap pedicle

All penile skin flaps are fundamentally based on the dartos fascia, which Joshi, Bandini, and Kulkarni (2022) described as "the winner" among all pedicles in genitourinary reconstruction.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- **Dartos fascia** lies between the penile skin and Buck's fascia.
- It contains a rich subdermal vascular plexus fed by branches of the external pudendal arteries.
- The dartos is continuous, highly elastic, and can be mobilized extensively without compromising its blood supply.
- When a skin island is raised on a dartos pedicle, the pedicle maintains perfusion through the subdermal plexus, the fundamental Orandi flap principle.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

### Flap dimensions

- **Orientation.** Longitudinal (along the long axis of the penis); the defining feature distinguishing the Orandi flap from the transverse Quartey and circular McAninch flaps.
- **Width.** Typically 1.5–2.5 cm (sufficient for onlay augmentation).
- **Length.** Up to 6–8 cm, limited by the longitudinal dimension of the penile shaft skin. Circular flaps can reach up to 15 cm.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Skin source.** Ventral penile shaft skin (classic Orandi) or lateral or dorsolateral penile skin (modifications).

## Surgical Technique — Classic Ventral Onlay

**Step 1 — Positioning and exposure.** Supine or lithotomy depending on stricture location; penis degloved through a circumcoronal or ventral midline incision; strictured urethra identified by palpation, urethral sound, or RUG.

**Step 2 — Flap design.** Longitudinal skin island marked on the ventral penile shaft skin, oriented parallel to the long axis. Width 1.5–2.5 cm; length matches stricture length (up to 6–8 cm).

**Step 3 — Flap harvest.** Skin island incised circumferentially. Dissection deep to the skin but superficial to Buck's fascia, preserving the dartos fascia attached to the deep surface. Dartos pedicle developed by dissecting it off the underlying Buck's fascia, maintaining continuity with the skin island. Pedicle developed with sufficient length to allow tension-free reach to the strictured segment.

**Step 4 — Urethrotomy.** Strictured urethra opened along its ventral surface through the full length of the stricture; lumen inspected and spongiofibrosis assessed.

**Step 5 — Flap placement (ventral onlay).** Skin island rotated on its dartos pedicle and laid over the ventral urethrotomy. Flap edges sutured to urethrotomy edges with absorbable sutures (5-0 or 6-0 PDS or Vicryl). Skin surface of the flap faces the urethral lumen (epithelial side inward). Dartos pedicle covers the external surface.

**Step 6 — Closure and catheterization.** Penile skin closed over the repair; urethral catheter (16–18 Fr) placed; catheter removal at 2–3 weeks after VCUG confirms no extravasation.

## Barbagli / Kulkarni Modification — Dorsal Onlay (2019)

Barbagli, Joshi, and Kulkarni 2019 described a modification, repositioning the longitudinal penile skin flap as a dorsal onlay rather than the traditional ventral onlay. The modification was designed to provide dorsal support and reduce sacculation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Modified technique

1. Midline ventral penile incision.
2. Urethra fully dissected from the corpora cavernosa (circumferential mobilization).
3. Urethra opened longitudinally along its dorsal surface.
4. **Longitudinal penile skin island on the dartos pedicle** harvested from the ventral penile skin (same as classic Orandi).
5. Skin island moved over the corpora cavernosa to the dorsal surface.
6. Opened urethra rotated and sutured over the penile skin flap; the flap now sits dorsally, supported by the corpora cavernosa.

### Results

| Parameter | Result |
|---|---|
| Number of patients | 12 |
| Success rate | **83.3% (10/12)** |
| Failures | 2 |
| Operating time | **60 min** |
| Fistula | **0%** |
| Urethral diverticulum | **0%** |

<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

No fistula or diverticulum was observed among 12 patients. This small uncontrolled series does not prove elimination of either complication or establish shorter operating time than other procedures.

## Dorsal vs. Ventral Onlay — The Sacculation Problem

The main clinical distinction between dorsal and ventral flap placement is the risk of pseudo-diverticulum or sacculation with postvoid dribbling. Bhandari, Dubey and Verma 2001 examined it in a small nonrandomized comparison of 40 patients with recurrent pendulous or bulbar strictures, treated with longitudinal penile or circumpenile flaps.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

| Parameter | Ventral Onlay (n = 21) | Dorsal Onlay (n = 19) | p value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stricture recurrence | 3 patients reported; the abstract gives 24%, which does not match 3/21 | 2/19 (11%) | &gt; 0.05 (NS) |
| Pseudo-diverticulum / sacculation | **29% (6/21)** | **0% (0/19)** | **0.01** |
| Postvoid dribbling | Present in sacculation cases | None | — |
| Urethrocutaneous fistula | 1 case | 0 | — |

### Why ventral onlay causes sacculation

A ventral flap lacks the rigid support of the corpora cavernosa. The proposed mechanism is that intraluminal pressure during voiding balloons the unsupported patch outward, forming a pseudo-diverticulum that traps urine. In the Bhandari series, sacculation or pseudo-diverticulum with postvoid dribbling occurred in 6 of 21 ventral onlays and 0 of 19 dorsal onlays.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### Why dorsal support may reduce sacculation

When the flap is placed dorsally, it is supported by the rigid tunica albuginea of the corpora cavernosa, providing a mechanical backstop that may limit outward ballooning. Dorsal graft placement is based on a similar rationale.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Combination Technique — Orandi Flap + Dorsal BMG

For narrow penile strictures, combining a dorsal BMG with a ventral Orandi flap has been described as a single-stage alternative to staged urethroplasty. Karapanos 2024 described this combined approach with preservation of the native urethral plate and corpus spongiosum:<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

### Technique

1. **Dorsal onlay BMG** quilted to the corpora cavernosa.
2. **Longitudinal ventral penile skin flap (Orandi-type)** transposed ventrally.
3. The flap is sutured to the scarred native urethral mucosa on one side and to the BMG on the other, forming a neourethra of triangular cross-section.
4. The preserved corpus spongiosum is wrapped around the flap ventrally for reinforcement.

### Results (12 patients, median 38-mo follow-up)

- Success was 91.7% (11 of 12).
- No sacculation or diverticula were observed. A protective effect of the spongiosum wrap was not tested independently.
- Three transient fistulas healed with prolonged urinary diversion.
- Postvoid dribbling occurred in 5 of 12 patients (41.7%) despite the absence of sacculation. The cause is not established.
- Median stricture length was 5 cm (IQR 3.8–7) and median operating time 205 min.

Related combinations have been described. Iselin and Webster 1999 combined dorsal onlay penile skin grafts with an Orandi flap in 4 of 29 bulbar strictures that extended significantly into the penile urethra.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> Morey 2001 used a dorsal graft (BMG in 3, cadaveric dermis in 1) to salvage an inadequate urethral plate during transverse penile island flap onlay in 4 of 37 patients.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> A 2025 case report combined BMG with a Q-flap.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

## Advantages

1. **Technical simplicity.** Barbagli reported a 60-min operation in a small penile-stricture cohort. Cross-study operating times cannot establish an advantage over circular flaps used for different defects.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
2. **No prepuce required.** Unlike Quartey, the Orandi flap uses penile shaft skin and can be performed in circumcised patients.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
3. **Reliable dartos pedicle.** Consistent vascularity.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
4. **Versatility.** Ventral onlay (classic), dorsal onlay (Barbagli modification), or combined with dorsal BMG for complex strictures.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
5. **Penile urethra.** Greenwell, Venn and Mundy 1999 stated that "for a patch urethroplasty of an uncomplicated stricture in the penile urethra, the Orandi procedure remains the 'gold standard'".<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
6. **Sexual outcomes.** Published series provide limited evidence and cannot exclude new erectile or ejaculatory symptoms.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

---

## Limitations

1. **Limited length (6–8 cm).** Longitudinal orientation restricts the flap to shorter strictures. For strictures &gt;8 cm, the McAninch circular flap or combined techniques are preferred.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
2. **Sacculation with ventral placement.** One small ventral-onlay cohort reported a 29% rate of pseudo-diverticulum and postvoid dribbling, potentially reduced by dorsal placement; zero events in small cohorts do not establish zero risk.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
3. **Lichen sclerosus.** The AUA guideline states that surgeons should not use genital skin for reconstruction in proven lichen sclerosus stricture (Strong Recommendation). Oral mucosa is preferred.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
4. **Requires healthy penile skin.** Patients with prior radiation, extensive scarring, dermatologic conditions, or insufficient skin are not candidates.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
5. **Hair-bearing skin risk.** AUA Clinical Principle: surgeons should not use hair-bearing skin for substitution urethroplasty. Care to harvest only hairless distal penile skin.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
6. **Declining use.** In a single tertiary center's 2007-2017 series, penile skin flaps made up 21.6% of complex anterior reconstructions and were used less often over time.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Orandi Flap vs. BMG

The Dubey 2007 randomized trial (55 patients, dorsal onlay BMG vs dorsal onlay penile skin flap) reported:<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- **Success.** 89.9% for BMG versus 85.6% for the flap (p &gt; 0.05).
- **Operating time.** 162 min for BMG versus 224 min for the flap (p = 0.001).
- **Morbidity.** In the flap group, 6 of 28 patients (21%) had superficial penile skin necrosis, 1 needed skin grafting and 2 (7%) had penile torsion. The BMG group had minor morbidity in 25.6%, resolved by 4 weeks.
- **Postvoid dribbling.** 14.8% for BMG versus 34.1% for the flap (p = 0.001).
- **Recommendation to another patient.** 89% for BMG versus 65% for the flap (p = 0.001).

A pedicled flap carries its own blood supply, which is the usual rationale for choosing it in scarred, poorly vascularized urethral beds where a free graft might not take (failed prior urethroplasty, extensive spongiofibrosis). This is reasoning from flap physiology and expert teaching; no comparative data are cited for it.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Radiation-exposed skin is unsuitable for flap harvest (see Limitations).

## Current Role and Guideline Context

### AUA Guideline 2023<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

- Surgeons may reconstruct long multi-segment strictures with one-stage or multi-stage techniques using oral mucosal grafts, penile fasciocutaneous flaps, or a combination (Moderate Recommendation; Grade C).
- Surgeons should use oral mucosa as the first choice when using grafts (Expert Opinion).
- Surgeons should not use genital skin for reconstruction in lichen sclerosus-proven stricture (Strong Recommendation).
- Surgeons should not use hair-bearing skin for substitution urethroplasty (Clinical Principle).

### Contemporary practice trends

Fuchs 2018 documented the evolving landscape over a decade:<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

- BMG accounted for 61.3% of complex reconstructions and its share stayed stable
- Penile skin flaps accounted for 21.6% and were used less often over time
- Perineal urethrostomy use increased (4.3% to 38.7%, p = 0.01)
- Observed success: PU 94.8%, BMG 78.5%, skin flap 78.2% (p = 0.003); nonrandomized treatment selection limits causal ranking

Despite the decline, penile skin flaps remain an option where oral mucosa is unavailable (for example, dyskeratotic oral changes from gutkha, tobacco and betel-nut use, per Mathur), in patients with depleted oral donor sites, and in complex strictures needing vascularized tissue.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Special Considerations

### Circumcised patients

The classic Orandi flap can be performed in circumcised patients using remaining penile shaft skin. Srivastava 2012 specifically studied the circumpenile flap in 30 circumcised patients, achieving 93.3% at 1 yr and 86.7% at 3 yr; this is a selected-cohort result, not proof of superiority over preputial flaps.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### Regional considerations

In South Asia, gutkha, tobacco, pan masala and betel nut consumption is associated with dyskeratotic oral changes, and buccal mucosa may be unsuitable for grafting. Penile and preputial skin flaps remain an alternative there. Mathur 2014, whose series used preputial flaps, wrote: "We prefer this technique in this part of the world where buccal mucosa cannot be used because of dyskeratotic changes."<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Key Takeaways

The Orandi longitudinal ventral penile skin island flap is an early pedicled flap urethroplasty, described in 1968. Its reported strengths are a short operation (60 min in the Barbagli dorsal modification), dartos-based vascularity, and use in circumcised patients and those with unsuitable oral mucosa.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> The Barbagli dorsal onlay modification (2019) places the flap on the corpora for dorsal support. Small series found less sacculation with dorsal placement, but they do not show that the risk is eliminated.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> BMG urethroplasty has largely replaced penile skin flaps as first-line substitution material, and in the one randomized trial it had lower morbidity and higher patient-reported recommendation with similar success. The Orandi flap is still used in complex strictures, depleted oral donor sites, and populations where oral mucosa is unavailable.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> Genital skin is not used in lichen sclerosus.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
