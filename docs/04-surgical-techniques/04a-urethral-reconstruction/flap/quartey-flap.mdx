# Quartey Flap (Transverse Preputial / Distal Penile Cutaneous Island Flap)

**Quartey flap versus Q-flap.** The Quartey flap (Quartey 1983) and the Q-flap (Morey, Tran and Zinman 2000) are related but not identical. The Quartey flap is the 1983 transverse preputial or distal-penile island flap. The Q-flap is a 2000 modification that adds a ventral midline extension to a circumferential flap (creating the "Q" outline) to lengthen reach for panurethral strictures (mean 17 cm, range 15–24 cm). For the modification, see [Q-Flap (Morey)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/q-flap).

The **Quartey flap** is a single-stage, pedicled fasciocutaneous flap for urethral reconstruction, first described by John K. Quartey of Accra, Ghana in 1983.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> It uses a transversely oriented island of distal penile or preputial skin supplied axially by the superficial external pudendal vessels. Its pedicle can be constructed so that the skin reaches anywhere from the external meatus to the prostatic urethra. In the original 10-patient series, all flaps survived and there was no permanent fistula.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For the longitudinal precursor, see [Orandi Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/orandi-flap). For the circular variant, see [McAninch Transverse Circular Penile Skin Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/mcaninch-circular-flap). For the Q-flap modification for panurethral strictures, see [Q-Flap (Morey)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/q-flap). For the broader anatomy and other penile flap variants, see [Pedicled Penile / Preputial Skin Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/penile-preputial). For graft alternatives, see [Buccal Mucosa Graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa).

## Historical Context and Significance

Quartey's flap followed the Orandi longitudinal flap (1968) and preceded the McAninch circular flap (1993):<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

- **Transverse orientation.** A transverse island uses the circumference of the distal penis and prepuce, so its length is not limited by the length of the shaft skin, as a longitudinal flap's is. In the original report the pedicle reached from the meatus to the prostatic urethra.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Axial blood supply from the superficial external pudendal vessels.** Quartey identified that the distal penile / preputial skin is supplied axially by the superficial external pudendal arteries (branches of the femoral artery), providing a reliable named-vessel pedicle rather than relying solely on the random-pattern subdermal plexus.
- **Hairless tissue.** Distal penile and preputial skin is relatively hairless. Three fine hairs were nonetheless seen in 2 of the original 10 patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Versatility as patch or tube.** The flap could be configured as either an onlay patch (augmentation) or tubularized (substitution).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Long reach.** The pedicle can be constructed so the flap reaches from the external meatus to the prostatic urethra, which is the entire length of the male urethra.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Quartey's place in the evolution of penile skin flaps

| Surgeon | Year | Flap Type | Orientation | Pedicle | Max Length |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Orandi<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup> | 1968 | Longitudinal ventral penile skin | Longitudinal | Dartos fascia | Limited by shaft length (6–8 cm is the usual teaching value) |
| **Quartey**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | 1983 | Transverse preputial island | Transverse | Superficial external pudendal vessels | Meatus to prostatic urethra in the original report |
| McAninch<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup> | 1993 | Circular fasciocutaneous | Circumferential | Buck's fascia as the main vascular conduit | Up to 15 cm |
| Srivastava<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | 2012 | Circumpenile | Circumferential | Dartos fascia | variable |

Joshi, Bandini and Kulkarni 2022 described the dartos fascia as "the winner" among pedicles in genitourinary reconstruction, and all penile flaps as dartos-based.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Anatomy and Vascular Basis

### Superficial external pudendal artery (SEPA)

The Quartey flap is based on the SEPA, the named axial vessel supplying the distal penile and preputial skin:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- **Cadaveric study.** The artery was found on 46 of 50 sides (92%); 45 arose from the femoral artery and one from the deep femoral artery. Origin was 0.8–8.5 cm below the inguinal ligament
- **Diameter.** 1.2 to 3.8 mm at its origin (sufficient for a reliable axial pedicle)
- **Variants.** Single artery in 30%, duplicated in 46%, common trunk in 24%
- **Course.** Courses medially from the femoral triangle, crosses the spermatic cord, and enters the penile skin where it ramifies into the subdermal plexus of the dartos fascia
- **Preputial supply.** The preputial skin receives additional blood supply from perforators of the dorsal penile artery, creating a dual blood supply to the distal penile and preputial skin.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### Dartos fascia as the pedicle conduit

While Quartey described the flap as supplied by the superficial external pudendal vessels, the practical pedicle is the dartos fascia, which contains the subdermal vascular plexus fed by the SEPA. The dartos serves as the conduit through which the SEPA branches reach the skin island.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

McAninch's 1993 description of the circular flap names Buck's fascia as its major vascular conduit.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup>

## Surgical Technique

### Patient positioning and exposure

- Supine or lithotomy depending on stricture location
- Penis degloved through a circumcoronal incision (uncircumcised patients) or a subcoronal / ventral midline incision
- Strictured urethra identified

### Flap design

- Skin island designed on the inner preputial skin (uncircumcised) or distal penile shaft skin (circumcised, more challenging)
- Oriented transversely, perpendicular to the long axis of the penis
- **Width.** Typically 2–3 cm for onlay (teaching value); wider if tubularization is planned
- **Length.** Determined by the circumference of the distal penis and prepuce
- The Morey Q-flap is a distinct extension of a circumferential skin island; the Quartey flap should not be relabeled as that modification.

### Flap harvest

- Skin island incised circumferentially
- Dissection proceeds in the plane between the skin and the dartos fascia on the outer surface of the flap, while preserving the dartos fascia attached to the deep surface of the skin island
- The dartos pedicle is developed by dissecting it off the underlying Buck's fascia
- Pedicle developed proximally toward the base of the penis, maintaining the axial blood supply from the superficial external pudendal vessels
- Pedicle must be of sufficient length to allow the flap to reach the strictured segment without tension

### Urethrotomy and stricture assessment

- Strictured urethra opened longitudinally (ventral or dorsal urethrotomy depending on planned configuration)
- Stricture length and extent of spongiofibrosis assessed
- Urethral plate quality evaluated to determine onlay versus tubularization

### Flap transfer and configuration

| Configuration | Indication | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Ventral onlay** (classic Quartey) | Augmentation with intact dorsal plate | Flap rotated and laid over ventral urethrotomy; skin surface faces lumen; sutured with 5-0 / 6-0 PDS / Vicryl<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Dorsal onlay** (Tijani / Bhandari modification) | Augmentation; urethra mobilized circumferentially | Flap placed dorsally against corpora cavernosa; urethra rotated back over the flap. Less sacculation observed in a small comparison (0/19 vs 6/21; not proof of zero risk)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **Tubularized substitution** | Obliterative strictures with no urethral plate | Flap tubularized around catheter to create neo-urethra. Recurrence was higher than with onlay in retrospective series: 58% versus 13% (McAninch 1998, circular flap) and 60% at 30 months in 5 tubes versus none among available onlay follow-up (Tijani)<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| **Combined with dorsal BMG** | Complex strictures with damaged urethral plate | BMG augments urethral plate dorsally while flap provides ventral coverage<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |

### Closure and postoperative care

- Penile skin closed over the flap pedicle
- A urethral catheter (16–18 Fr) is placed and removed at about 2–3 weeks after a VCUG (typical practice)

## Dorsal vs. Ventral Onlay — The Critical Distinction

Dorsal support may reduce sacculation. The principal comparisons are small observational cohorts, not definitive trials of flap orientation.

Bhandari 2001, a nonrandomized comparison of 40 patients:<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

- No significant recurrence difference. The abstract inconsistently reports three VO recurrences as 24% of 21; an exact VO rate cannot be confidently extracted
- Pseudo-diverticulum or sacculation: VO 29% versus DO 0% (p = 0.01, highly significant)
- Postvoid dribbling: present in all sacculation cases (VO group only)
- Fistula: 1 case in VO group, 0 in DO group

The corpora cavernosa may provide rigid support that limits outward ballooning of a dorsally placed flap during voiding. This mechanism is proposed, not demonstrated.

**Tijani 2009** reported, in circumcised West African men, no recurrence among dorsal-onlay patients available at 30 months versus 40% success with tubularization. The comparison involves different defects and only five tubularized cases, and it cannot establish that dorsal placement eliminates dribbling.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

Srivastava 2012 reported dorsal onlay results in 60 TPIF patients (90% at 1 yr, 85% at 3 yr) and concluded that dorsal onlay flaps are less likely than tubularized flaps to lead to diverticulum formation and postvoid dribbling.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Quartey Flap + BMG Combination

For complex strictures with a damaged urethral plate, a penile skin flap combined with a dorsal BMG has been described as a single-stage strategy. The BMG augments the urethral plate dorsally and the pedicled flap provides vascularized ventral coverage.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

**Anadani 2025.** Case report of a combined dorsal BMG and ventral onlay Q penile skin flap (see [Q-Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/q-flap)) in a 37-year-old man with a 5 cm stricture that had failed urethroplasty, with an extra-anatomical bypass and a diverticulum on retrograde urethrography. The urethra was well patent at 3 months.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

**Erickson, Breyer, McAninch 2012.** Single-stage segmental urethral replacement using dorsal onlay BMG and ventral onlay fasciocutaneous flap in 14 men with long-segment strictures (mean 9.75 cm):<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

- Primary success was 64% (9/14) at median 2.5 yr.
- Overall success including a single endoscopic procedure was 79% (11/14).
- Patients whose strictures recurred had longer strictures (12.8 versus 8.7 cm, p = 0.04).

**Iselin and Webster 1999.** In 4 of 29 patients undergoing dorsal onlay graft urethroplasty, the procedure was combined with an Orandi flap because the stricture extended significantly into the penile urethra. Outcome was favorable in 97% (28/29) at median 19 months.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

The AUA guideline permits combinations for selected multi-segment strictures, without endorsing a particular named combined operation: surgeons may reconstruct long multi-segment strictures with oral mucosal grafts, penile fasciocutaneous flaps, or a combination (Moderate Recommendation; Grade C).<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

## Quartey-Type Flap in Hypospadias Repair

The transverse preputial island flap (TPIF), described by Duckett in 1980 for hypospadias (see [Pedicled Preputial Tube Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/pedicled-prepucial-tube)), uses a similar preputial island. It has the largest body of outcome data for a Quartey-type flap, but the clinical context differs from adult stricture disease.

| Study | n | Application | Complication | Fistula | Stricture | Diverticulum | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wang 2019 (15-yr experience)<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> | 320 | Proximal hypospadias | 39.1% | 16.6% | 9.7% | 12.8% | 40.2 mo |
| Wang 2019 (staged TPIF)<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> | 102 | Proximal hypospadias (staged) | 14.7% (2nd stage) | 7.8% | 4.9% | 1.9% | 52.4 mo |
| Chen 2016 (staged TPIF vs Byars)<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> | 87 (42 TPIF, 45 Byars) | Proximal hypospadias with severe chordee | Reoperation: TPIF **7.1%** vs Byars **26.7%** (p &lt; 0.05) | 4.8% vs 23.2% (p &lt; 0.05) | 2.4% vs 4.4% (NS) | 2.4% vs 0% | — |
| Wang 2023 (3 techniques)<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | 152 | Proximal hypospadias | Staged TPIF **11.1%** vs TPIF + Duplay **40%** vs Koyanagi **50%** (p &lt; 0.01) | 4.9% vs 21.8% (p = 0.01) | — | — | — |

Findings:

- Selected retrospective proximal-hypospadias cohorts reported lower complications after staged TPIF; treatment selection and surgeon effects limit generalization.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- In Wang 2023, defect length &gt;4.55 cm was a data-derived univariate cutoff associated with complications, not a universally validated threshold.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **Diverticula and strictures are associated.** 31.7% of patients with diverticula also had strictures.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- In the 320-patient cohort, 20.8% of patients with complications presented after 1 year or more, and 12.8% after 5 years or more. Long-term follow-up is therefore needed.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

## Comparison with Other Techniques

### Quartey vs McAninch circular flap

| Feature | Quartey (Transverse Preputial) | McAninch (Circular Fasciocutaneous) |
|---|---|---|
| Year | 1983<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 1993<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Pedicle | External-pudendal branches in dartos | Buck's fascia as the main vascular conduit (1993 description)<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup> |
| Length | Meatus to prostatic urethra in the original report | Up to 15 cm<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup> |
| Requires prepuce | Typically yes (best with intact prepuce) | No (can use in circumcised) |
| Vascular principle | Preserve superficial axial branches and subdermal plexus | Preserve superficial axial branches and subdermal plexus |
| Technical complexity | Moderate (expert opinion) | Most complex (expert opinion) |
| Long-term data | Limited (mostly intermediate-term series) | Extensive (79% at 10 yr, Whitson 2008)<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |
| Best indication | Long anterior strictures in uncircumcised patients | Long / complex anterior strictures regardless of circumcision status |

### Quartey vs BMG urethroplasty

These trials compare different tissue transfers. Only Dubey tests a penile skin flap versus BMG. PeeBuSt and Alrefaey test free penile skin grafts, so their findings cannot establish flap outcomes. Nonsignificant differences do not prove equivalence:

| RCT | n | Comparison | Success | Key signals |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dubey 2007<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> | 55 | DO penile skin flap vs DO BMG | 85.6% vs 89.9% (NS) | Flap: 224 vs 162 min OR (p = 0.001); skin necrosis 21% vs 0%; penile torsion 7% vs 0%; PVD 34% vs 15% (p = 0.001); 65% vs 89% would recommend (p = 0.001) |
| Tyagi 2022 PeeBuSt<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup> | 100 randomized; 95 analyzed | Penile skin graft vs BMG | 42/47 vs 44/48 at 18 mo (p = 0.70) | Powered for a large difference, not equivalence |
| Alrefaey 2025<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup> | 98 randomized; 92 analyzed | Penile skin graft vs BMG | 41/44 vs 47/48 at 12 mo (p = 0.346) | Mean stricture length 6.4 vs 7.9 cm (p = 0.11); median follow-up 20 mo |

**Synthesis.** Dubey found no significant success difference, but more morbidity with flaps (skin necrosis, penile torsion, postvoid dribbling, longer OR time). The usual rationale for a pedicled flap is its own blood supply in scarred, poorly vascularized urethral beds, where a free graft may not take. No comparative data show a benefit in that setting.

**Hussein 2011.** Penile circular graft (n = 18) versus penile circular flap (n = 19) for long bulbo-penile strictures. OR time PCG 203 versus PCF 282 min (p = 0.000); skin necrosis 0% versus 16%; recurrence 27.7% versus 21% (NS). The authors concluded that both had similar and high success, with a low complication rate.<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup>

## Advantages

1. **Length.** The pedicle can reach from the meatus to the prostatic urethra, and the transverse orientation is not limited by shaft length as the longitudinal Orandi flap is.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
2. **Named-vessel axial pedicle.** SEPA provides reliable, anatomically defined blood supply.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
3. **Hairless tissue.** Inner preputial skin is naturally hairless.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
4. **Versatility.** Onlay (patch) or tubularized (tube); reach from meatus to prostatic urethra.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
5. **Pedicled (carries own blood supply).** The flap does not depend on the recipient bed for initial survival, which is the rationale for its use in scarred, poorly vascularized beds.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
6. **Combinable with BMG.** Can be combined with dorsal BMG for complex strictures with damaged urethral plates.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
7. **Moderate technical complexity.** It is generally regarded as less demanding than the McAninch circular flap (expert opinion).

---

## Limitations

1. **Requires intact prepuce (ideally).** Optimized for uncircumcised patients with abundant preputial skin. In circumcised patients, available tissue is significantly reduced; alternative flaps (circumpenile flap of Srivastava, McAninch circular) may be preferred.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
2. **Lichen sclerosus.** The AUA guideline states that surgeons should not use genital skin for reconstruction in lichen sclerosus-proven stricture (Strong Recommendation). Oral mucosa is the preferred substitute.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[22]](#ref22)[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
3. **Sacculation with ventral placement.** One small ventral-onlay cohort reported 6/21 cases of pseudo-diverticulum; potentially reduced by dorsal placement, without proof that risk is eliminated.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
4. **Tubularization has higher failure rates.** In retrospective series, tubularized flaps recurred more often than onlay flaps (58% versus 13% in McAninch 1998), although the groups differed in urethral-plate quality.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
5. **Hair-bearing skin risk.** AUA Clinical Principle: surgeons should not use hair-bearing skin. While inner preputial skin is hairless, care must be taken not to incorporate hair-bearing penile shaft skin.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
6. **Declining use.** Penile skin flaps are used less often over time at one tertiary center.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> In the randomized Dubey trial, BMG had similar success with shorter operating time, less morbidity and higher patient recommendation.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
7. **Limited long-term data.** Unlike the McAninch circular flap (which has 10-yr Kaplan-Meier data from UCSF), the Quartey flap lacks large, long-term outcome studies from a single institution.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

## Preputial Spiral Graft — A Modern Evolution

The **preputial spiral graft urethroplasty (PSGU)**, described by Kulkarni and Bandini in 2023 and 2025, uses the same donor tissue as the Quartey flap. This technique uses the prepuce as a free graft (not a pedicled flap) harvested in a spiral or helicoidal configuration to maximize length from a 5-cm-wide preputial skin strip, achieving grafts up to 20 cm.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

| Feature | Quartey Flap (Pedicled) | Preputial Spiral Graft (Free Graft) |
|---|---|---|
| Tissue type | Pedicled fasciocutaneous flap | Free mucocutaneous graft |
| Blood supply | Carries own (SEPA / dartos pedicle) | Depends on recipient bed (imbibition → neovascularization) |
| Length | Meatus to prostatic urethra in the original report | Up to 20 cm |
| Stricture length treated | variable | median 16 cm (panurethral) |
| Success rate | 81–100% in small series of varying design and follow-up | **90.4%** (114 patients, 5 centers; median follow-up 16 mo) |
| Requires prepuce | Yes (ideally) | Yes (mandatory) |
| LS contraindication | Yes | Avoid genital skin in LS-related strictures |
| Key advantage | Pedicled blood supply for scarred beds | Single-graft reach up to 20 cm |

PSGU uses the same donor tissue (prepuce) as a free graft rather than a pedicled flap, harvested in a spiral to gain length.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

## Current Role and Indications

### Ideal indications

1. **Long anterior urethral strictures (&gt;5 cm)** in uncircumcised patients with abundant preputial skin, where the flap's advantages are its reach and the quality of inner preputial tissue.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
2. **Strictures with a compromised urethral bed**, where spongiofibrosis is severe and a free graft (BMG) may not take; the pedicled flap brings its own blood supply.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
3. **Failed prior urethroplasty.** A well-vascularized flap can overcome the scarred tissue bed.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
4. **Patients with limited or unsuitable oral mucosa**, when BMG donor sites are depleted, or oral pathology (e.g., submucous fibrosis from gutkha or betel nut) precludes BMG harvest.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
5. **Combined with dorsal BMG**, for complex strictures with damaged urethral plates requiring both dorsal augmentation and ventral coverage.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
6. **Resource-limited settings.** No graft harvest is needed, and the flap uses local penile skin (Tijani's series comes from such a center).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### When to choose alternatives

- **Circumcised patients.** Consider McAninch circular flap or Srivastava circumpenile flap.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Lichen sclerosus.** Oral mucosa is used; genital skin is not.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[22]](#ref22)[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
- **Short bulbar strictures.** Consider anastomotic or graft-based options according to etiology and obliteration. Do not apply this rule to penile strictures, where excision may cause shortening or curvature.
- **Available oral mucosa with healthy graft bed.** BMG urethroplasty is technically simpler and patient-preferred.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

## Guideline Context

The AUA Urethral Stricture Disease Guideline Amendment (2023):<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

- Surgeons may reconstruct long multi-segment strictures with one-stage or multi-stage techniques using oral mucosal grafts, penile fasciocutaneous flaps, or a combination (Moderate Recommendation; Grade C).
- Surgeons should use oral mucosa as the first choice when using grafts for urethroplasty (Expert Opinion).
- Surgeons should not use genital skin for reconstruction in lichen sclerosus-proven stricture (Strong Recommendation).
- Surgeons should not use hair-bearing skin for substitution urethroplasty (Clinical Principle).

## Key Takeaways

The Quartey flap is a transverse penile or preputial island flap described in 1983, after the longitudinal Orandi flap (1968) and before the circular McAninch flap (1993). Its features are a pedicle that reaches the whole male urethra, a named-vessel axial supply (SEPA via dartos fascia), and use in long anterior strictures in uncircumcised patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The Bhandari 2001 dorsal-versus-ventral data (29% versus 0% sacculation) and Tijani 2009 100%-DO-versus-40%-tubularized result support dorsal-onlay consideration but do not establish universal superiority.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> BMG urethroplasty has largely replaced penile skin flaps as first-line substitution. In the one randomized trial, BMG had similar success with less morbidity and higher patient recommendation. Penile skin flaps are still used in complex strictures, depleted oral donor sites, populations where oral mucosa is unsuitable (the Mathur series), and combinations with dorsal BMG.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[10]](#ref10)[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)[[20]](#ref20)</sup> Genital skin is not used in lichen sclerosus.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
