# Q-Flap (Morey / Tran / Zinman)

The **Q-flap** is a circumferential distal penile skin flap with a ventral midline extension (the resulting outline resembles the letter "Q"), designed by [Allen Morey](/docs/roots/surgeons/h-r/allen-morey), Tran, and [Zinman](/docs/roots/surgeons/s-z/leonard-zinman) in 2000 for single-stage reconstruction of panurethral strictures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The ventral extension gave a mean flap length of 17 cm (range 15–24 cm), and no supplemental graft was needed in the original selected cohort of 15 men.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Q-flap versus Quartey flap.** The Q-flap is not the same as the [Quartey flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/quartey-flap) (Quartey 1983). The Quartey flap is the original 1983 transverse preputial / distal-penile island flap. The Q-flap is a Morey 2000 modification that extends the circumferential flap with a ventral midline limb, creating the "Q" outline and adding length specifically for panurethral disease. The terms are sometimes used loosely in practice, but the two are distinct flap designs with different lengths and indications.

For the original 1983 Quartey flap, see [Quartey Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/quartey-flap). For the alternative free-graft panurethral strategy, see [Preputial Spiral Graft Urethroplasty (PSGU)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/preputial-spiral-graft) and [Kulkarni One-Sided Dorsolateral BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/kulkarni-one-stage). For broader penile-flap principles, see [Pedicled Penile / Preputial Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/penile-preputial). For combined-tissue-transfer alternatives, see [Combined Dorsal BMG + Ventral Fasciocutaneous Flap (Erickson)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/erickson-dorsal-bmg-ventral-flap).

## Design — Why the "Q" Shape

The Q-flap starts with a distal circumferential penile skin flap (analogous to the McAninch and Quartey concept), then adds a ventral midline extension along the penile shaft. The resulting outline on the marked penile skin resembles the letter "Q": circumferential loop with a tail.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The added ventral limb provides additional skin length that the circumferential portion alone cannot supply, which allowed single-stage reconstruction of the entire anterior urethra in the original series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The ventral segment has been described as extending up to 8 cm along the penile shaft, in addition to the circumferential portion.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Indications

- **Panurethral anterior urethral strictures** were repaired with a single-stage Q-flap in Morey’s selected 15-patient series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Multiple oral-mucosal grafts are another option for long defects.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Uncircumcised patients** with adequate distal penile and preputial skin are candidates. All 15 patients in the original series were uncircumcised.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Surgeon preference.** The Q-flap suits a surgeon who prefers a single-stage flap-based panurethral repair over combined graft and flap (Erickson, [Combined Dorsal BMG + Ventral Fasciocutaneous Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/erickson-dorsal-bmg-ventral-flap)) or staged reconstruction ([Bracka two-stage](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/bracka-two-stage), [Johanson two-stage](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/johanson-two-stage)).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The 2026 EAU guideline recommends panurethral repair in a specialised centre and combining techniques if one cannot treat the full stricture (both Weak).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

The Q-flap requires adequate healthy distal penile or preputial skin and ventral shaft skin; in a circumcised patient, feasibility depends on the remaining tissue. Before a planned single-stage repair for a penile stricture, counsel that adverse intraoperative findings may require conversion to a staged procedure (2026 EAU: Strong).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> For lichen sclerosus-related penile strictures, the 2026 EAU guideline recommends against genital-skin augmentation (Strong) and recommends single-stage oral-mucosal graft repair when local conditions permit (Weak).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> All 15 men in Morey’s Q-flap series were uncircumcised.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Technique Highlights

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1. Marking | Outline a circumferential distal penile skin flap with a **ventral midline extension** along the shaft — the "Q" shape; the flaps obtained measured up to 24 cm<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| 2. Flap harvest | Raise as a **fasciocutaneous flap on the dartos pedicle**, approximately 2 cm wide and tailored to the urethral plate, with its axial blood supply from the superficial external pudendal vessels (same vascular basis as the Quartey flap)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| 3. Urethrotomy and positioning | Open the panurethral stricture along its full length. Limit time in high lithotomy during the repair; a related 66-patient circular-flap series reported four lower-extremity neurovascular complications after prolonged high lithotomy.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> In a separate retrospective series of 185 urethroplasties, 18 position-related lower-extremity complications occurred (four severe), and longer lithotomy time was associated with risk; the authors harvested penile flaps supine before repositioning for perineal transfer.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| 4. Flap inset | Configure as an **onlay** to augment the urethral plate; the ventral limb of the "Q" reaches the proximal extent of the stricture, the circumferential portion covers the more distal segments. An onlay presupposes an adequate urethral plate; if the plate is compromised, reassess the reconstructive plan rather than extending an onlay across that segment.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> The flap is kept on stretch during suturing to limit redundancy and sacculation, and the dartos pedicle is mobilized far enough to remain tension-free with the penis fully stretched, since inadequate mobilization can rotate the penis toward the pedicle during erection; where the flap is transferred through a scrotal tunnel for proximal reach, a tunnel of approximately 4 cm in diameter has been described.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> If the repair reaches the fossa navicularis, plan distal inset with glans wings or a glans-preserving approach.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| 5. Closure | Standard layered closure with a urethral catheter |

## Outcomes

Morey 2000 reported 15 uncircumcised men without prior urethroplasty. No supplemental graft was used. Ten had excellent results. Complications were recurrent stricture in two, and one each of urethrocutaneous fistula, meatal stenosis, femoral neuropathy, cerebrovascular accident and prolonged catheterization for focal extravasation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The broader penile-skin-flap literature lists the Q-flap as a pedicled option for selected panurethral disease when single-stage flap-only reconstruction is desired.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Long-term and comparative cohort data specific to the Q-flap are limited. In a 2022 survey of Society of Genitourinary Reconstructive Surgeons members (134 responses from 350 invited), 90% preferred multiple buccal grafts over a combined graft and flap for panurethral strictures.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> [Preputial Spiral Graft Urethroplasty (PSGU)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/preputial-spiral-graft) is a free-graft alternative using preputial tissue, but the published method requires an intact prepuce and excludes previously circumcised patients.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

## Comparison Table — Quartey vs Q-Flap

| Feature | Quartey Flap (1983) | Q-Flap (2000) |
|---|---|---|
| Originator | J.K.M. Quartey | Morey, Tran, Zinman |
| Flap design | Transverse preputial / penile island | **Circumferential flap + ventral midline extension ("Q" shape)** |
| Length | Not standardized; the original report describes reach from the meatus to the prostatic urethra<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | **Mean 17 cm (15–24 cm)**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Primary indication | Anterior urethral strictures (segmental, long) | **Panurethral strictures** |
| Need for supplemental grafts | May be required for very long strictures | Not required in the original 15 patients; not guaranteed for every defect |
| Vascular pedicle | Dartos / superficial external pudendal vessels | Same (dartos / SEPA) |
| Prerequisite | Adequate distal penile or preputial skin | Adequate healthy distal and ventral shaft skin; original cohort entirely uncircumcised<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |

## Limitations

- The flap requires enough healthy, hairless distal penile and ventral shaft skin; all 15 men in the original series were uncircumcised, so outcomes in circumcised men were not reported.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Lichen sclerosus.** Penile skin is unreliable in lichen sclerosus, so oral mucosa is preferred.
- Proximal penile skin ischemia or necrosis can occur after extensive pedicled penile-flap reconstruction and may lead to wound infection or flap disruption.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- Published long-term data are limited compared with the McAninch circular flap and BMG urethroplasty.
- Choice among oral-mucosal grafts, pedicled flaps, staged reconstruction and perineal urethrostomy depends on tissue quality and patient priorities. Superiority of the newer spiral graft over the Q-flap has not been shown.
