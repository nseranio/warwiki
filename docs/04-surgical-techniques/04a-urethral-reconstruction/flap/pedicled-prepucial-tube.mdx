# Pedicled Preputial Tube Urethroplasty

The **pedicled preputial tube urethroplasty** is a **single-stage urethral reconstruction technique** in which a **transverse island of inner preputial skin**, raised on a **dartos fascia pedicle**, is **tubularized around a catheter** to create a complete **neo-urethra** that replaces the excised strictured segment. It is primarily indicated for **obliterative strictures where no usable urethral plate remains**. While it provides single-stage circumferential urethral replacement with vascularized tissue, it carries higher recurrence in selected historical cohorts than onlay augmentation; the groups differ in urethral-plate quality and are not directly interchangeable, with particular susceptibility to **diverticulum formation, fistula, and stricture recurrence**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

For onlay alternatives, see [Quartey Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/quartey-flap), [McAninch Transverse Circular Penile Skin Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/mcaninch-circular-flap), and [Orandi Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/orandi-flap). For staged BMG-based circumferential reconstruction, see the staged-complex section. For the modern free-graft alternative, see [Buccal Mucosa Graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa).

## Historical Context and Origins

Two parallel historical lineages — adult urethral stricture disease and pediatric hypospadias repair.

### Adult stricture disease

- **Quartey 1983** — in his landmark transverse preputial island flap description, Quartey explicitly stated the pedicle could be constructed so the skin reaches from the external meatus to the prostatic urethra "for reconstruction as a **patch or tube**." An early adult-stricture description of both configurations.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **McAninch 1993, 1998** — extended the concept with the circular fasciocutaneous penile flap, which could also be tubularized. In the 1998 series of 66 patients, 12 underwent tubularized repairs with a **58% recurrence rate** vs 13% for the 54 onlay repairs.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

### Pediatric hypospadias

- **Duckett 1980** — described the **transverse preputial island flap (TPIF)** technique for one-stage repair of severe hypospadias, using inner preputial skin tubularized around a catheter to bridge the gap between the proximal meatus and the glans tip. Became known as the **"Duckett procedure"**.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Glassberg 1987** — described the **"Augmented Duckett Repair"** for very proximal hypospadias where insufficient inner foreskin existed for the full pedicle tube. A proximal add-on neo-urethra fashioned from non-hair-bearing interscrotal tissue or ventral penile skin (Thiersch-Duplay) was anastomosed to the distal Duckett tube.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Anatomy and Vascular Basis

### Tissue source — inner preputial skin

- The **inner (mucosal) surface of the prepuce** is the preferred tissue — **hairless**, thin, pliable, with a mucosal-like quality well-suited for urethral reconstruction.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- Inner preputial skin is specialized skin, not urethral mucosa; assess its quality, hair and inflammatory disease rather than assuming mucosal equivalence.

### Pedicle — dartos fascia

- The flap is raised on a **dartos fascia pedicle** containing the **subdermal vascular plexus** fed by branches of the **superficial external pudendal artery (SEPA)**.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- Joshi, Bandini, and Kulkarni 2022 described the dartos fascia as **"the winner"** among all pedicles in genitourinary reconstruction.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

### Flap dimensions for tubularization

- **Width** — must be sufficient to create a tube of adequate caliber when rolled around a catheter. Typically **2.5–3.5 cm** of skin width is needed to create a tube that accommodates a 16–18 Fr catheter.
- **Length** — determined by the stricture length; the transverse preputial harvest allows up to **12–15 cm**.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Surgical Technique

### Positioning and exposure

Supine or lithotomy; penis degloved through a circumcoronal incision; full extent of the strictured urethra assessed (RUG, urethroscopy, intraoperative palpation).

### Stricture excision

Unlike onlay techniques (which preserve the urethral plate), the tubularized approach requires **complete circumferential excision of the strictured segment**. The proximal and distal healthy urethral ends are spatulated to facilitate anastomosis. This step is necessary because tubularization is indicated when **no usable urethral plate remains** (obliterative stricture, dense spongiofibrosis, severely damaged plate).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

### Flap harvest

A transverse island of inner preputial skin is incised and raised with the **dartos pedicle** attached to its deep surface. The pedicle is developed by dissecting the dartos off the underlying Buck's fascia, maintaining continuity with the axial blood supply. The pedicle must be of sufficient length and mobility to allow the tubularized flap to reach the stricture site without tension or kinking.

### Tubularization

- The skin island is **rolled around a 16–18 Fr urethral catheter** with the **epithelial surface facing inward** (toward the lumen).
- Edges sutured together with continuous or interrupted absorbable sutures (5-0 or 6-0 PDS / Vicryl) to create a watertight tube.
- Suture line typically placed along the ventral or lateral aspect.
- **Hayashi 2001** reported **one complication among 13 pediatric hypospadias repairs** using two-layer closure and spongiosal coverage; the uncontrolled series does not demonstrate a reduction or establish adult-stricture outcomes.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

### Anastomosis

- **Proximal anastomosis** — tube sutured to the spatulated proximal urethra with absorbable sutures.
- **Distal anastomosis** — tube sutured to the spatulated distal urethra or brought out to the glans tip (in hypospadias repair).
- Both anastomoses performed over the indwelling catheter.

### Pedicle coverage and closure

- The dartos pedicle is wrapped around the exterior of the tubularized neo-urethra, providing a second vascularized tissue layer that reinforces the repair and may reduce fistula risk.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- Penile skin closed over the repair.
- Urethral catheter left in place for **2–3 weeks**, with removal after VCUG confirms no extravasation.

## Outcomes — Adult Urethral Stricture Disease

The following retrospective series generally favor onlay when a viable plate remains. Tubularized repairs treat more severe tissue loss, so these comparisons do not isolate a causal effect of configuration.

| Study | n (tube) | Configuration | Success (tube) | Comparator (onlay) | Follow-up | Key Findings |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| McAninch / Morey 1998<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | 12 | Tubularized circular flap | **42% (5/12)** | 87% (47/54) onlay | 41 mo | 58% recurrence in tube vs 13% onlay; "onlay repairs appear to be more successful" |
| Srivastava 2012<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 54 | Tubularized preputial flap | **75% at 3 yr** | 85% DOF at 3 yr | 40.1 mo | DOF less likely to lead to diverticula and postvoid dribbling; TF higher failure rate |
| Tijani 2009<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | 5 | Tubularized Quartey modification | **40% at 30 mo** | No recurrence among available dorsal-onlay follow-up | 19 mo | Only five tube repairs; 30-month denominator for onlay follow-up not specified |
| Greenwell / Mundy 1999<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | — | Two-stage free graft vs one-stage flap | — | — | — | "Two-stage repairs gave **much better results than one-stage repairs** for total circumferential reconstruction of the penile urethra" |

These heterogeneous series should not be pooled into a fixed relative-failure multiplier. Carney and McAninch 2002 stated explicitly: "**Onlay reconstruction is preferable to flap tubularization** and has provided a better initial and long-term outcome."<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Outcomes — Hypospadias Repair (Duckett TPIF)

The largest body of outcome data for the pedicled preputial tube comes from the pediatric hypospadias literature.

| Study | n | Application | Overall complication | Fistula | Stricture | Diverticulum | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wang 2019 (15-yr experience)<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | 320 | Proximal hypospadias (TPIF) | **39.1%** | 16.6% | 9.7% | 12.8% | 40.2 mo |
| Ghali 1999 (12-yr)<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | 148 (Duckett) | Primary hypospadias | **22% initial** (95% final after reoperations) | higher than onlay | higher than onlay | higher than onlay | 23 mo |
| Wiener 1997<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> | 74 (tube) vs 58 (onlay) | Proximal hypospadias | Tube **36%** vs Onlay **31%** | 14% vs 17% (NS) | NS | **Tube 12.2% vs Onlay 0% (p = 0.016)** | 20.3 mo |
| Zheng 2013<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> | 53 (25 primary, 28 secondary) | Primary and secondary | Primary 24%, **Secondary 53.6% (p = 0.028)** | — | — | — | 38.7 mo |
| Hayashi 2001 (modified TPTIF)<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> | 13 | Moderately severe hypospadias | **7.7% (1/13)** | 1 fistula | 0 | 0 | — |
| Soutis 2003 (salvage)<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> | 21 | Multiple failed urethroplasties (3–13 prior) | **24% (5/21)** | 1 fistula | 1 stenosis | 2 diverticula | — |

### Key lessons from the hypospadias literature

1. **Diverticulum formation is the hallmark complication of tubularization** — Wiener 1997 found diverticula in **12.2% of tubularized vs 0% of onlay repairs** (p = 0.016). Wang 2019 reported 12.8% diverticula in 320 patients, with 31.7% of diverticula associated with concomitant strictures (p &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
2. **Duckett's tubularized repair has higher complication rates than onlay** — Ghali 1999 directly compared 148 Duckett tubularized repairs with 42 onlay preputial island flaps and found significantly higher overall complications including fistulae, strictures, meatal stenoses, and tubular abnormalities (p &lt; 0.05).<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
3. **Late complications are common** — Wang 2019 found 20.8% of complications presented after ≥1 year, and 12.8% after ≥5 years, emphasizing the need for **long-term follow-up** well beyond the typical 2–3 year window.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
4. **Secondary (reoperative) cases have worse outcomes** — Zheng 2013: complication rates 24% primary vs **53.6% secondary** hypospadias (p = 0.028) using Duckett.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

## Why Configuration Matters

A tubularized flap supplies the entire urethral circumference and requires a longitudinal tube closure plus proximal and distal anastomoses. An onlay retains a viable native urethral plate. These are anatomically different repairs; there is no valid general formula that multiplies circumference by stricture length to calculate suture-line length.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Potential problems include anastomotic restenosis, ischemia if the pedicle is compromised, fistula, and ballooning of an unsupported segment. Distal obstruction may coexist with or contribute to diverticulum formation. Dorsal support may limit sacculation but does not guarantee its absence.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[12]](#ref12)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

Adult stricture outcomes and pediatric hypospadias complication rates should be discussed separately. A catheter size appropriate for an adult reconstruction must not be transferred to a child.

## Modifications to Improve Tubularized Outcomes

1. **Two-layer closure (Hayashi 2001)** — neo-urethra with two-layer closure plus corpus spongiosum wrapping at the proximal anastomosis. **7.7% complication rate (1/13)** — no meatal stenosis, stricture, or diverticulum.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
2. **Augmented Duckett (Glassberg 1987)** — proximal Thiersch-Duplay neo-urethra (from ventral penile skin) anastomosed to the distal Duckett tube; bridges larger gaps without a free graft.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
3. **Dartos fascia wrap** — wrapped circumferentially around the tubularized neo-urethra, providing a second vascularized tissue layer that reinforces the suture line.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
4. **Staged hypospadias reconstruction** — can be considered for severe curvature and an unsuitable plate. The 39.1% complication rate in the cited 320-patient single-stage cohort is not a randomized comparison against the separate staged series.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
5. **Combined dorsal BMG + ventral flap (Erickson, Breyer, McAninch 2012)** — a dorsal BMG replaces an unsalvageable plate segment against the corpora and a ventral skin flap completes the lumen. This is circumferential replacement using two tissue sources; a triangular plate-preserving variant belongs to the later Karapanos technique, not the original Erickson operation. **64% primary / 79% secondary success** at median 2.5 yr for strictures averaging 9.75 cm.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

## Alternatives and Limits of Cross-Study Comparison

| Technique | Stages | Tissue Source | Success | Key Advantage | Key Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pedicled preputial tube**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | 1 | Inner preputial skin (pedicled) | 40–75% | Single-stage; carries own blood supply | Highest failure rate; diverticula; fistula |
| **Two-stage BMG urethroplasty**<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | 2 | Buccal mucosa (free graft) | 80–90% | Oral mucosa avoids genital skin and hair; LS may still affect the repair | Requires 2 operations; 4–6 mo interval |
| **Two-stage mesh graft**<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> | 2 | Split-thickness skin graft | 80% | Non-hair-bearing; ample tissue | Requires 2 operations; graft contracture risk |
| **Combined dorsal BMG + ventral flap**<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | 1 | BMG + penile skin flap | 64–79% | Single-stage; avoids full tubularization | Requires both healthy oral mucosa and penile skin |


These are noncomparative study estimates with different populations, follow-up and success definitions. They cannot rank procedures for an individual defect. A **preputial spiral free graft** augments a usable urethral plate; it is not an established single-stage tube substitute for complete circumferential tissue loss.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

Greenwell, Venn, and Mundy 1999 concluded: "**Two-stage repairs gave much better results than one-stage repairs for total circumferential reconstruction** of the penile urethra."<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Indications and Contraindications

### Indications

1. **Obliterative urethral stricture** with no usable urethral plate — the primary indication, where augmentation (onlay) is not possible because there is no native tissue to augment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
2. **Severe spongiofibrosis** with complete destruction of the urethral lumen — where the scarred urethral bed cannot support a free graft.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
3. **Proximal hypospadias** with severe chordee requiring urethral plate transection — the classic Duckett indication.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
4. **Failed prior urethroplasty** with loss of the urethral plate — particularly when oral mucosa donor sites are depleted or unsuitable.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
5. **Patients with unsuitable oral mucosa** (e.g., oral submucosal fibrosis from gutkha / betel nut consumption) who require circumferential reconstruction and cannot undergo staged BMG urethroplasty.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

### Contraindications

1. **Lichen sclerosus** — genital skin flaps and grafts should be avoided; AUA advises against genital skin for LS-related strictures; oral mucosa is the preferred graft.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
2. **Circumcised patients** — insufficient preputial tissue for tubularization (though dorsal penile skin can sometimes be used as salvage).<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
3. **Preserved urethral plate** — if a usable plate exists, **consider onlay augmentation** before sacrificing viable tissue.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
4. **Hair-bearing skin** — AUA Clinical Principle: surgeons should not use hair-bearing skin for substitution urethroplasty.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

---

## Complications

- **Restenosis:** determine site, length and degree of obliteration before choosing an endoscopic salvage or redo reconstruction. Repeated endoscopy is not a durable default for long recurrent penile disease.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
- **Diverticulum and postvoid pooling:** assess for distal obstruction as well as inadequate tissue support. Wang's pediatric cohort found concurrent stricture in 13/41 diverticula; this is not an adult tube-specific rate.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Fistula, skin loss and torsion:** risks depend on skin quality, flap perfusion, tension and operative design. The Dubey trial's complications occurred after dorsal onlay skin flaps and should not be relabeled as tube-repair rates.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- **Hair, stones and infection:** avoid hair-bearing tissue for the lumen and investigate late obstructive or infectious symptoms.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

## Acquired Urethral Diverticula — The Long-Term Consequence

**Cinman, McAninch, Glass, Zaid, Breyer 2012** (UCSF) described 22 men with acquired urethral diverticula over an 11-year period — many resulting from prior tubularized urethroplasty or hypospadias repair:<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

- Most common presentations — recurrent UTI, urinary dribbling, incontinence, weak stream
- **54.5% (12/22)** underwent diverticulectomy and urethroplasty
- **13.5% (3/22)** required ileal conduit urinary diversion
- **32% (7/22)** managed nonoperatively with postvoid manual decompression
- 91% diverticulum recurrence-free rate at mean 2.3 yr

Diverticula from tubularized repairs can present **years to decades** after the original surgery and may require complex secondary reconstruction.

## Current Role and Guideline Context

### AUA Urethral Stricture Disease Guideline Amendment 2023<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

- Surgeons may reconstruct long multi-segment strictures with one-stage or multi-stage techniques using **oral mucosal grafts, penile fasciocutaneous flaps, or a combination** (Moderate Recommendation; Grade C).
- The guideline permits individualized single- or multistage repair; do not attribute a technique-specific preference to an inferred guideline statement.
- Surgeons should use **oral mucosa as the first choice** when using grafts (Expert Opinion).
- Genital skin should be **avoided in LS-related strictures**.

### Contemporary practice

The pedicled preputial tube urethroplasty has been **largely supplanted** by:

1. **Two-stage BMG urethroplasty** — for circumferential penile urethral reconstruction (current standard).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
2. **Combined dorsal BMG + ventral onlay flap** — selected single-stage circumferential replacement with a graft-supported dorsal wall and a vascularized flap floor.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
3. **Preputial spiral graft urethroplasty (Kulkarni / Bandini 2023–2025)** — a selected augmentation option when a viable plate remains; its 90.4% success in a 114-patient cohort does not establish replacement of tubularized reconstruction.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

The technique retains a niche role:

- **Oral mucosa unavailable** (depleted donor sites, oral submucosal fibrosis)<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- **Single-stage strongly preferred** with a completely destroyed urethral plate<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Resource-limited settings** where staged procedures impose unacceptable logistical burdens<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Salvage of multiple failed urethroplasties** — Soutis 2003 demonstrated **76% success** using the Duckett island-flap technique in 21 patients with 3–13 prior failed repairs (in 4 patients the prepuce had been resected and dorsal penile skin was used instead)<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

## Key Takeaways

The **pedicled preputial tube urethroplasty** is a single-stage technique for **circumferential urethral replacement** using a tubularized island of inner preputial skin on a dartos fascia pedicle. Originally described by **Quartey 1983** for adult strictures and **Duckett 1980** for hypospadias, it provides the advantage of **vascularized, hairless tissue** that can bridge long urethral defects in a single operation.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> However, it carries **significantly higher failure rates (40–58%) compared to onlay repairs (11–15%)**, with particular susceptibility to diverticulum, fistula and recurrent narrowing; exact risks vary by population and technique.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The fundamental problem: 100% of the urethral circumference depends on substitute tissue, with a long suture line under radial tension and no spongiosal support.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> Greenwell and Mundy 1999 concluded that **two-stage free graft repairs give better results than one-stage flap tubularization** for circumferential penile urethral reconstruction — a principle that has driven the shift toward **staged BMG urethroplasty** as the current standard for obliterative strictures.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The technique remains valuable in specific scenarios (salvage cases, patients with unsuitable oral mucosa, resource-limited settings) but should be considered a **second-line option** when onlay augmentation or staged BMG reconstruction is feasible.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[16]](#ref16)[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
