# Propeller Flap Urethroplasty

**Propeller flap urethroplasty** applies the propeller flap concept from plastic and reconstructive surgery to one urethroplasty scenario: revision of a stenotic perineal urethrostomy (PU) when local perineal tissues are scarred or depleted. It is not a standardized urethroplasty class. The published urethroplasty experience is a single case report by Schulster 2021.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For the propeller flap concept itself, including the 2009 Tokyo Consensus definition, geometric principles, the perforator systems used across penoscrotal, vulvar and perineal reconstruction (IPAP gluteal-fold, SCIP, vPMT / PMTP, EPAP), and the general complication and venous-congestion literature, see [Propeller Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/propeller-flap) in foundations. This page is limited to the urethroplasty application.

For the commonly used pedicled flap option in urethroplasty, see [McAninch Distal Penile Circular Fasciocutaneous Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/mcaninch-circular-flap). For the canonical PU technique, see [Blandy Perineal Urethrostomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/blandy-perineal-urethrostomy) and [Midline Perineal Urethrostomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/seven-flap-perineal-urethrostomy).

## Schulster 2021 — Propeller Flap PU Revision

**Indication.** For a stenotic PU, management options include periodic dilation, V-Y plasty, augmentation, complete redo and urinary diversion.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> A posterior-thigh propeller flap has been described for one complex revision at high risk for re-stenosis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Schulster's index case had a long history of failed urethral reconstructions with scarred and depleted local perineal tissue, and standard revision approaches were considered unlikely to succeed.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Technique.**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| Preparation and positioning | In the reported case, culture-driven antibiotic prophylaxis was given, the patient was placed in lithotomy, and the perineum plus medial and posterior thighs were prepared and draped.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Stenosis assessment | In the reported case, bougie-à-boule calibration identified an 8 Fr stenosis with maximal narrowing 1.75 cm proximal to the skin. The authors dilated the PU to 16 Fr and used flexible cystourethroscopy to confirm proximal urethral and bladder patency.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Stenosis exposure | Incise the stenotic PU to expose the urethral lumen |
| Perforator mapping | Identify **posterior thigh perforators**; confirm the individual vascular anatomy with handheld Doppler |
| Flap design | Island flap with long axis along the posterior thigh, designed around the perforator closest to the defect |
| Elevation and rotation | Raise on the perforator pedicle and rotate axially to place the flap apex into the urethrostomy defect |
| Tubularization | Partially tubularize the flap apex with absorbable sutures at the level of the urethrotomy, **calibrated to 30 Fr** |
| Donor site | Closed primarily |

**Outcome.** Cystoscopy showed a patent PU at 6 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> At 17 months, the single patient reported good voiding and continence, with postvoid residuals below 100 mL.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Why a Propeller Flap for PU Revision

The rationale for a propeller flap in PU revision is the one that drives propeller flap use elsewhere in perineal reconstruction:

- **Tissue from outside the zone of injury.** A posterior-thigh flap can recruit healthy tissue outside the local injury zone when that donor site is suitable.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Perforator-based blood supply.** The flap keeps an identified vessel. Superiority over other local flaps has not been shown.
- **Single stage.** No delay or staged division is needed.
- **Primary donor closure.** The authors described minimal morbidity in the single reported patient; donor-site morbidity for this technique cannot be estimated from one case.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Thickness.** Posterior-thigh tissue augments urethrostomy caliber without excess bulk.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For broader complication, perforator-anatomy, and venous-congestion considerations, refer to the [foundations propeller flap article](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/propeller-flap).

## Comparison With Established Urethroplasty Flaps

| Feature | Propeller Flap (Schulster) | [Distal Penile Circular Fasciocutaneous (McAninch)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/mcaninch-circular-flap) | [Dartos / Penile Skin Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/penile-preputial) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pedicle | Perforator-based island | Axial dartos fascia | Axial dartos fascia |
| Tissue source | Posterior thigh / regional perforator | Distal penile skin | Penile skin |
| Zone of injury | Outside | Within or adjacent | Within or adjacent |
| Movement | 90–180° axial rotation | Transposition / rotation | Transposition |
| Hair-bearing | Must assess donor hair; posterior thigh may be hair-bearing | Select hairless distal penile skin | Select hairless penile skin |
| Primary indication | Revision / salvage PU | Primary complex stricture | Primary complex stricture |
| Evidence base | Single case report (urethral)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Retrospective series of 124 men without lichen sclerosus; 79% estimated overall stricture-free survival at 10 years (median follow-up 7.3 years)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | Multiple retrospective series |

The McAninch distal penile circular fasciocutaneous flap has substantially more published urethroplasty outcome data, with 95%, 84% and 79% stricture-free survival at 1, 5 and 10 years in the largest long-term series.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The AUA 2023 urethral stricture guideline amendment states that success rates of single-stage penile fasciocutaneous flaps and oral mucosal grafts appear similar in properly selected patients.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> In Schulster’s single case, a posterior-thigh flap was used to revise a complex stenotic PU considered at high risk for re-stenosis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations and Current Status

- The published urethroplasty experience is limited to a single case report.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- No comparative data exist against any standard flap or graft urethroplasty.
- The technique is a salvage or revision option, not a primary reconstructive approach.
- Collaboration with plastic surgery may be needed for perforator dissection.
- Long-term stricture recurrence and functional voiding outcomes in larger cohorts are unknown.
