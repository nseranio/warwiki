# Single-Stage Preputial Flap Urethroplasty

The **single-stage preputial flap urethroplasty** is a one-stage urethral reconstruction using a **pedicled island of inner preputial skin on a dartos fascia vascular pedicle** to augment or replace the strictured urethra. It encompasses multiple configurations — **ventral onlay, dorsal onlay, dorsolateral onlay, and tubularized** — and achieves **success rates of 81–93%** depending on configuration and patient selection, with dorsal onlay being the preferred placement. It is particularly valuable in regions where **buccal mucosa is unsuitable** due to oral dyskeratosis from gutkha / betel-nut consumption.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For named-flap variants, see [Quartey 'Q' Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/quartey-flap), [Orandi Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/orandi-flap), [McAninch Transverse Circular Penile Skin Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/mcaninch-circular-flap). For full circumferential tubularization, see [Pedicled Preputial Tube Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/pedicled-prepucial-tube). For the broader penile / preputial flap anatomy, see [Pedicled Penile / Preputial Skin Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/penile-preputial). For graft alternatives, see [Buccal Mucosa Graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa).

---

## Definition and Scope

The key distinguishing feature from preputial *grafts* is that the flap **retains its vascular pedicle** (dartos fascia containing the subdermal plexus fed by the superficial external pudendal artery), meaning it carries its own blood supply to the recipient site rather than depending on the recipient bed for revascularization.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

Configurations:

- **Ventral onlay** (classic Quartey / Orandi position) — flap laid over a ventral urethrotomy
- **Dorsal onlay** (Bhandari modification) — flap placed dorsally against the corpora cavernosa
- **Dorsolateral onlay** — intermediate position
- **Tubularized** (Duckett / Quartey tube) — flap rolled into a complete tube for circumferential replacement
- **Combined with BMG** — dorsal BMG + ventral preputial flap for complex strictures

## Anatomy and Vascular Basis

### Inner preputial skin — the tissue source

The inner (mucosal) surface of the prepuce is the preferred tissue for the flap. **Hairless, thin, pliable**, with a mucosal-like quality well-suited for urethral reconstruction. Quartey 1983 noted that this portion of the skin is hairless, though 3 fine hairs were seen in 2 of his original 10 patients.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### Dartos fascia — the pedicle

The flap is raised on a **dartos fascia pedicle** containing the subdermal vascular plexus fed by branches of the **superficial external pudendal artery (SEPA)**. Joshi, Bandini, and Kulkarni 2022 described the dartos fascia as **"the winner"** among all pedicles in genitourinary reconstruction.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### Flap dimensions

- **Width** — typically 2–3 cm for onlay; 2.5–3.5 cm for tubularization
- **Length** — transverse orientation allows up to **12–15 cm**<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Pedicle can be developed to allow the flap to reach from the **external meatus to the prostatic urethra**

## Surgical Technique — Dorsal Onlay (Preferred)

The dorsal onlay configuration has emerged as the preferred approach based on comparative data.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

**Step 1 — Exposure and degloving.** Supine or lithotomy depending on stricture location. Circumcoronal incision; penis degloved to expose the urethra. Stricture identified by palpation, urethroscopy, or intraoperative assessment.

**Step 2 — Flap harvest.** Transverse island of inner preputial skin marked. Skin island incised and raised with the **dartos pedicle** attached to its deep surface. Pedicle developed by dissecting dartos off the underlying Buck's fascia, maintaining axial blood supply. Pedicle must reach the stricture site without tension.

**Step 3 — Urethrotomy and stricture assessment.** Urethra mobilized circumferentially at the stricture site. **Dorsal urethrotomy** through the full length of the stricture. Urethral plate quality assessed — adequate plate → onlay; destroyed → tubularization.

**Step 4 — Dorsal flap placement.** Flap transferred on its pedicle and placed **dorsally against the tunica albuginea of the corpora cavernosa**. Epithelial surface faces the urethral lumen. Flap edges sutured to urethrotomy edges with 5-0 or 6-0 absorbable sutures (PDS or Vicryl). The corpora cavernosa provide a **rigid backstop** that prevents sacculation — the key advantage of dorsal placement.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

**Step 5 — Urethral closure and catheterization.** Urethra rotated back over the flap. **16–18 Fr urethral catheter** placed. Penile skin closed over the repair.

**Step 6 — Postoperative care.** Catheter removal at 2–3 weeks after VCUG confirms no extravasation. Follow-up with uroflowmetry, RUG, and symptom assessment.

## Flap Configurations — Comparative Outcomes

| Configuration | Indication | Success | Sacculation | Postvoid Dribbling | Key Advantage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Dorsal onlay (DOF)** | Stricture with preserved urethral plate | **85–93% at 3 yr** | **0%** | rare | Corpora provide rigid backstop; no sacculation<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Ventral onlay (VOF) | Stricture with preserved urethral plate | 76–86% | **29%** | 34–39% | Technically simpler; no circumferential urethral mobilization<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Tubularized (TF) | Obliterative stricture; no urethral plate | 40–75% | 12–29% | common | Only option when urethral plate destroyed<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| Circumpenile DOF | Circumcised patients | 87–93% at 3 yr | low | low | Applicable in circumcised patients<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Combined DOF + BMG | Complex stricture with damaged plate | 64–79% | low | low | Single-stage circumferential reconstruction<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |

## Major Clinical Series

### Srivastava 2012 — largest single-institution series (144 patients)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Most comprehensive single-stage preputial flap series, from SGPGIMS, Lucknow:

- **Group 1** — transverse preputial DOF (n = 60) — **90% at 1 yr; 85% at 3 yr**
- **Group 2** — tubularized flap (n = 54) — **85% at 1 yr; 75% at 3 yr**
- **Group 3** — circumpenile DOF in circumcised patients (n = 30) — **93.3% at 1 yr; 86.7% at 3 yr**
- Mean follow-up 40.1 mo (range 36–84). Half of recurrences managed with single VIU / dilation. Overall success (including secondary procedures): 90%, 85%, 93.3% respectively. 75% completed 60 mo follow-up with no additional recurrence.
- Key conclusion — DOF is less likely to lead to diverticula formation and postvoid dribbling than TF.

### Mathur 2014 — predictors of failure (58 patients)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- Median stricture length 48.5 mm (range 26–85). Median follow-up 42 mo. Median operative time **90 min**.
- Overall success **81%**; patient satisfaction **88%**.

| Predictor | RR | p value |
|---|---|---|
| Diabetes mellitus | 5.21 (CI 2.31–64.68) | 0.003 |
| Smoking | 4.19 (CI 1.54–45.0) | 0.01 |
| Stricture length &gt;70 mm | 3.25 | — |
| Panurethral stricture | 2.73 | — |
| Previous urethroplasty | 2.4 | — |
| Severe peri-urethral fibrosis | 2.37 | — |
| Postinfective aetiology | 2.19 | — |

Key conclusion — "We prefer this technique in this part of the world where buccal mucosa cannot be used because of **dyskeratotic changes as a result of consumption of gutkha, tobacco, pan masala, betel nut**."

### Bhandari 2001 — dorsal vs ventral onlay (40 patients)<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- 19 dorsal onlay (DO) vs 21 ventral onlay (VO)
- Stricture recurrence — VO 24% vs DO 11% (p &gt; 0.05, NS)
- **Pseudo-diverticulum / sacculation — VO 29% vs DO 0% (p = 0.01)**
- Fistula — 1 case in VO, 0 in DO
- Conclusion — "Dorsal placement of the pedicled flap is anatomically and functionally more appropriate than the traditional ventral onlay placement."

### Barbagli 1998 — dorsal onlay preputial skin (37 patients)<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

- 31 preputial skin grafts + 6 BMG (when local skin unavailable). All dorsal onlay placement.
- **92% success** at mean 21.5 mo. Preputial skin graft length 2.5–12 cm (avg 4.7 cm).

### Berger 2005 — post-traumatic strictures (153 patients)<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

Compared end-to-end (86), dorsal onlay graft (40), ventral BMG (7), ventral fasciocutaneous flap (20). Mean follow-up 75.2 mo.

- Re-stricture — **dorsal onlay 5%** vs end-to-end 27% vs fasciocutaneous flap 15% vs ventral BMG 57%
- Conclusion — "In patients with strictures too long to be excised and re-anastomosed, **tension-free dorsal onlay urethroplasty is better** than ventral graft or flap techniques."

## Preputial Flap vs Buccal Mucosa Graft

Three RCTs directly compare penile skin (flap or graft) with BMG.

### Dubey 2007 RCT — dorsal onlay BMG vs penile skin flap (55 patients)<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

| Outcome | Penile Skin Flap (n = 28) | BMG (n = 27) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Success rate | 85.6% | 89.9% | &gt; 0.05 (NS) |
| Operative time | 224 min | 162 min | 0.001 |
| Superficial skin necrosis | 21% (6/28) | 0% | — |
| Extensive skin loss | 3.6% (1/28) | 0% | — |
| Penile torsion | 7% (2/28) | 0% | — |
| Postvoid dribbling | 34.1% (9/28) | 14.8% (4/27) | 0.001 |
| Would recommend procedure | 65% | 89% | 0.001 |

Key conclusion — "Compared to buccal mucosa, penile flap procedures are **technically complex, associated with higher morbidity, and less preferred by patients**."

### Tyagi 2022 PeeBuSt RCT — penile skin graft vs BMG (100 patients)<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

- Success at 18 mo — PSG **89%** vs BMG **91%** (p = 0.70, NS)
- No difference in Qmax (p = 0.06), IPSS (p = 0.43), IIEF erectile domain (p = 0.07), MSHQ-EJD (p = 0.20)
- Clavien-Dindo I — 12.7% vs 16.7%
- Key conclusion — Level 1 evidence of no statistically significant difference in outcomes of augmentation urethroplasty using BMG or penile skin graft.

### Alrefaey 2025 RCT — penile skin graft vs BMG (98 patients)<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

- Success — PSG **93.2%** vs BMG **97.9%** (p = 0.346)
- Kaplan-Meier — no significant difference (HR 1.19, p = 0.275)
- Patient satisfaction — PSG 90.9% vs BMG 93.8% (p = 0.5)
- Conclusion — "PSG and BMG techniques for augmentation anterior urethroplasty have **high and comparable success rates**, with equal patient satisfaction and similarly low morbidity."

## Preputial Flap vs Preputial Graft — Pedicled or Free?

| Feature | Preputial Flap (Pedicled) | Preputial Graft (Free) |
|---|---|---|
| Blood supply | Carries own (dartos pedicle) | Depends on recipient bed (imbibition → neovascularization) |
| Indication | Scarred / poorly vascularized beds; failed prior grafts | Well-vascularized beds; primary strictures |
| Technical complexity | Higher (pedicle dissection, rotation) | Lower (simple harvest and placement) |
| Operative time | Longer (224 min in Dubey RCT) | Shorter |
| Skin necrosis risk | 21% (pedicle compromise) | minimal |
| Penile torsion risk | 7% | minimal |
| Success | 81–93% | 87–93% |
| Key advantage | Survives in scarred beds | Simpler; less morbidity |

### Tyagi 2021 — inner preputial graft (80 patients)<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

- Dorsolateral inner preputial graft (IPG) urethroplasty
- Mean stricture length 65 mm; mean follow-up 48 mo
- **Success 87% (69/80)**
- Sexual function unaffected (IIEF and MSHQ-EJD stable)
- Conclusion — "Preputial graft is a tissue familiar to the urologist, located very close to the surgical field, easily harvested and operated under regional anaesthesia. Overall success outcomes are acceptable to BMG urethroplasty."

### Hussein 2011 RCT — penile circular graft vs flap (37 patients)<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

- 18 penile circular graft (PCG) vs 19 penile circular flap (PCF)
- Stricture length — PCG 15.2 cm vs PCF 14.1 cm
- Operative time — PCG **203 min** vs PCF **282 min** (p = 0.000)
- Superficial skin necrosis — PCG 0% vs PCF 16% (3 cases)
- Stricture recurrence — PCG 27.7% vs PCF 21% (p = 0.249, NS)

## Dorsal vs Ventral Onlay — Functional Considerations

The dorsal-vs-ventral debate extends beyond stricture recurrence to **functional outcomes**, particularly erectile function.

### Dubey 2003 — critical appraisal of techniques (109 patients)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

Ventral onlay associated with significantly higher rates of:

- Postvoid dribbling — 39% vs 23% (p = 0.01)
- Ejaculatory dysfunction — 20% vs 5% (p = 0.03)
- Pseudo-diverticulum / out-pouching — 26% vs 2.6% (p = 0.01)

Conclusion — "Dorsal free graft / flap onlay urethroplasty gives better results than ventrally placed free grafts / flaps."

### Mousa 2025 — ventral vs dorsal BMG for proximal bulbar (133 patients)<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

A counterpoint specifically for **proximal bulbar** strictures:

- Ventral approach had **better erectile outcomes** — permanent ED 1.7% vs 13.7% (p = 0.012)
- IIEF significantly higher with ventral at 3 and 12 mo (p &lt; 0.05)

## Emerging Innovation — ICG-Guided Flap Perfusion

A significant recent advance is **indocyanine green (ICG) near-infrared fluorescence angiography** to assess flap perfusion intraoperatively.

### Paraboschi 2023 — first ICG-guided preputial island flap urethroplasty<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

- ICG (0.15 mg/kg IV) used to assess vascular perfusion in a 13-month-old boy undergoing onlay urethroplasty for mid-shaft hypospadias
- Perfusion visualized 80 sec after ICG injection (EleVision IR system)
- ICG-based laser angiography helped define the proximal resection margin
- Conclusion — "Useful adjunct for tissue perfusion evaluation and intraoperative decision-making during the onlay preputial island flap urethroplasty."

### Zhang 2024 — semiquantitative ICG perfusion assessment<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

- Standardized protocol for ICG use in open urethral reconstruction
- Proposed perfusion cutoffs — **≥60% for graft beds** (relative to erectile bodies) and **≥40% for flaps**
- Facilitates intraoperative decisions on extent of excision and graft / flap sizing

## Modified Circular Fasciocutaneous Flap (Zhao 2026)<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

- Circular fasciocutaneous flap **longitudinally split on the dorsal side** and transferred ventrally
- 54 patients
- **0% stricture recurrence** at mean 15 mo
- No penile skin necrosis or fistula
- Postmicturition dribbling 24.1%, mild penile torsion &lt; 30°
- Successfully performed in 6 circumcised patients without skin shortage

## Contraindications and Limitations

### Absolute contraindications

1. **Lichen sclerosus** — AUA guideline states genital skin flaps and grafts should be avoided in LS due to **very high long-term failure rates**. Oral mucosa is preferred. Kulkarni 2009 confirmed in 215 LS patients that oral mucosal grafts achieve **91–100% success** while genital skin remains prone to LS recurrence.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[22]](#ref22)[[23]](#ref23)[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
2. **Hair-bearing skin** — AUA Clinical Principle: surgeons should not use hair-bearing skin for substitution urethroplasty.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

### Relative contraindications

3. **Circumcised patients** — insufficient preputial tissue; however, **circumpenile flap** (using dorsal penile shaft skin) achieves 86.7–93.3% in circumcised patients.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
4. **Diabetes mellitus** — RR 5.21 for failure (p = 0.003)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
5. **Smoking** — RR 4.19 (p = 0.01); also Whitson / McAninch HR 4.0 (p = 0.02)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[25]](#ref25)</sup>
6. **Stricture length &gt;70 mm** — RR 3.25<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
7. **Previous urethroplasty** — RR 2.4<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Comparison with Alternative Single-Stage Techniques

| Technique | Tissue | Pedicled? | Success | Best Indication | Key Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Preputial DOF**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | Inner preputial skin | Yes (dartos) | 85–93% at 3 yr | Long anterior strictures; scarred beds; unsuitable oral mucosa | Requires prepuce; higher morbidity than BMG |
| Dorsal onlay BMG<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | Buccal mucosa | No (free graft) | 86–92% | Standard for most anterior strictures | Depends on recipient bed vascularity; oral donor morbidity |
| Preputial spiral graft<sup>[[26]](#ref26)[[27]](#ref27)</sup> | Preputial skin | No (free graft) | 90.4% at 16 mo | Panurethral strictures up to 20 cm | Requires prepuce; no LS; intermediate follow-up only |
| McAninch circular flap<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[25]](#ref25)</sup> | Penile skin | Yes (Buck's fascia) | 79% at 10 yr | Complex anterior strictures regardless of circumcision | Most technically complex; declining use |
| Combined BMG + flap<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> | BMG + penile skin | mixed | 64–79% | Long-segment with damaged plate; single-stage desired | Requires both healthy oral mucosa and penile skin |

## Guideline Context

The **AUA Urethral Stricture Disease Guideline Amendment (2023)**:<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

- Surgeons may reconstruct long multi-segment strictures with **one-stage or multi-stage techniques** using oral mucosal grafts, penile fasciocutaneous flaps, or a combination (Moderate Recommendation; Grade C)
- Surgeons should use **oral mucosa as the first choice** when using grafts (Expert Opinion)
- Genital skin flaps and grafts **should be avoided in LS-related strictures**
- Surgeons **should not use hair-bearing skin** for substitution urethroplasty (Clinical Principle)

### Current practice trends

**Berg 2024** surveyed 134 GURS members:<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>

- **99% prefer buccal mucosa** as the primary graft site
- BMG favored over fasciocutaneous flap for penile urethroplasty regardless of circumcision status (**95% with circumcision, 84% without**)
- For panurethral strictures, **90% preferred multiple BMGs** over combined graft / flap

**Fuchs 2018** documented declining use of penile skin flaps with **perineal urethrostomy increasingly preferred** for complex strictures (4.3% → 38.7% over a decade), while BMG use remained stable.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup>

**Greenwell, Venn, Mundy 1999** — "For a patch urethroplasty of an uncomplicated stricture in the penile urethra the **Orandi procedure remains the 'gold standard'**. For a circumferential repair of the urethra, particularly the penile urethra, a **two-stage repair using a free graft gives better results** than a one-stage repair using a flap."<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Current Indications

The single-stage preputial flap retains important indications despite the shift toward BMG:

1. **Scarred, poorly vascularized urethral beds** — where a free graft may not take; the pedicled flap brings its own blood supply.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
2. **Unsuitable oral mucosa** — oral submucosal fibrosis from gutkha / betel-nut / pan-masala consumption (common in South Asia); oral pathology precluding BMG harvest.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
3. **Failed prior BMG urethroplasty** — well-vascularized flap can overcome the scarred tissue bed that contributed to prior graft failure.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
4. **Long anterior strictures (&gt;5 cm) in uncircumcised patients** — flap's greatest advantage is its length (up to 12–15 cm) and the quality of inner preputial tissue.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
5. **Combined with dorsal BMG** — for complex strictures requiring both dorsal augmentation and ventral coverage in a single stage.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
6. **Resource-limited settings** — no specialized graft material required.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Key Takeaways

The single-stage preputial flap urethroplasty achieves **81–93% success** depending on configuration, with **dorsal onlay placement** preferred due to elimination of sacculation (0% vs 29% with ventral, p = 0.01).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Three RCTs demonstrate that penile skin (flap or graft) achieves **comparable success rates to BMG** (85–93% vs 89–98%), though BMG is associated with shorter operative times, less morbidity, and higher patient satisfaction.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> The preputial flap's unique advantage is its **pedicled blood supply**, indispensable for scarred urethral beds, failed prior grafts, and patients with unsuitable oral mucosa.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> **Diabetes (RR 5.21)** and **smoking (RR 4.19)** are the strongest predictors of failure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The technique is **contraindicated in lichen sclerosus** and should not incorporate hair-bearing skin.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> While contemporary practice has shifted toward BMG as the first-line substitute, the single-stage preputial flap remains a critical tool for complex cases where grafts alone are insufficient.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[28]](#ref28)[[29]](#ref29)</sup>
