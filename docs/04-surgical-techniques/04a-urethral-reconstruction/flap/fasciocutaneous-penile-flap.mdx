# Jordan Fasciocutaneous Ventral Penile / Preputial Island Flap

The Jordan flap is a transverse ventral penile or preputial fasciocutaneous skin island elevated on a dartos fascia pedicle, designed for single-stage reconstruction of fossa navicularis and meatal strictures. Gerald H. Jordan first described it in *J Urol* in 1987, and the later series in this article use the same flap.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Jordan described the flap because earlier flap procedures for fossa navicularis strictures did not leave a normal-appearing penis. The goal is to restore urethral patency and keep a cosmetically normal glans.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

This article consolidates the prior separately-listed entries for the Jordan & Schlossberg 1987 transverse-island variant and the Jordan 1999 penile-skin-flap variant, which are the same flap family applied to the same anatomic problem.

For BMG alternatives in the same anatomic location, see [Sliding-T Dorsal Inlay (Hoare / Rourke)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/sliding-t-dorsal-inlay) and [Transmeatal OMG Ventral Inlay (Nikolavsky)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/transmeatal-omg-ventral-inlay). For the broader penile / preputial pedicled-flap family principles, see [Penile / Preputial Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/penile-preputial). For [Meatotomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/meatotomy) as the simpler first-line option for short meatal stenosis.

## Surgical Technique

The Jordan flap is a transverse skin island on a broad dartos pedicle.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1. Skin-island harvest | Mark a transverse island of skin on the ventral surface of the distal penis / prepuce: hairless, thin and pliable skin suited to urethral substitution |
| 2. Pedicle dissection | Elevate the island on a broad dartos fascia pedicle for reliable, well-vascularized blood supply; dissect the pedicle proximally for adequate mobilization |
| 3. Stricture exposure | Split the glans ventrally or use a glans-cap technique to expose the fossa-navicularis stricture; incise the strictured segment ventrally |
| 4. Flap inlay | Rotate the skin island into the defect and suture as a ventral onlay to augment the strictured urethra, preserving the dorsal urethral wall. Fine absorbable sutures to the urethrotomy edges |
| 5. Glansplasty | Reconstruct the glans over the flap to restore a normal slit-like meatus and cosmetic glans contour. Armenakas / McAninch advocate a glans-cap repair for limited dissection and minimal blood loss<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| 6. Catheter | Urethral catheter typically removed at 2–3 weeks<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |

## Indications

- **Meatal stenosis** and fossa navicularis strictures, most reliably when short. Morey reported reliable onlay results for isolated strictures ≤ 2.5 cm, and the Onol cohort had strictures < 1.5 cm.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Recurrent strictures** after failed meatotomy or dilation. AUA guidelines state that surgeons should offer urethroplasty for recurrent meatal or fossa navicularis strictures (Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Distal urethral strictures after surgery for BPH (the Onol cohort; not specifically radical prostatectomy) involving the meatus or fossa.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Requires intact, healthy penile or preputial skin (uncircumcised or sufficient remaining ventral penile skin).

---

## Contraindications and Limitations

- **Lichen sclerosus (BXO)** is the dominant limitation. Jordan's own long-term series (Virasoro / Eltahawy / Jordan 2007) showed 100% excellent results in non-LS patients (23 / 23) at mean 10.2 years but 50% LS (BXO) recurrence (6 / 12) in LS patients. However, 17/35 patients (48%) were lost to follow-up and three died, limiting durability inference. In lichen sclerosus-proven urethral stricture, the AUA states that surgeons should not use genital skin for reconstruction (Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Circumcised patients** with insufficient remaining ventral penile skin.
- **Strictures > 2.5 cm.** Morey 2007 reported that onlay reconstruction (a mixed flap or graft cohort) is reliable for short isolated fossa-navicularis strictures ≤ 2.5 cm (91% success, 10 / 11) but significantly less effective for longer strictures > 2.5 cm (54% success, 7 / 13, p &lt; 0.05).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Diseased or scarred penile skin.** Extragenital tissue (buccal mucosa) preferred.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Outcomes

| Series | n | FU (mean) | Success | Notable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jordan 1987 (original)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 5 | 17 mo (median) | 100% | First description; excellent cosmetic / functional outcomes |
| Fiala 2003<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | 21 | 35 mo | 100% | Meatal and distal strictures; no recurrences; all patients satisfied with cosmesis |
| Virasoro / Eltahawy / Jordan 2007<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | 35 | 10.2 yr | 83% overall | 100% non-LS (23 / 23); 50% LS recurrence (6 / 12); LS and non-LS outcomes diverge |
| Onol 2008<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | 26 | 30.2 mo | 96% | Distal strictures < 1.5 cm after surgery for BPH; 1 flap necrosis / fistula; mild spraying in all, improved within 6 weeks |

## Jordan Flap vs Buccal Mucosa Graft for Fossa Navicularis

There is no randomized comparative data; the choice is etiology-driven:

- **Non-LS strictures with healthy penile skin.** The flap is a reasonable option. All 23 non-LS patients in Jordan's retrospective series had excellent results, but 17 of the 35 patients assessed were lost to follow-up.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **LS-related strictures.** Buccal mucosa graft is preferred because genital skin is susceptible to LS recurrence. Contemporary single-stage BMG techniques for fossa-navicularis disease (dorsal inlay, ventral inlay, sliding-T) report success of 69–95% depending on series and technique (dorsal inlay 69%, sliding-T 92%, transurethral ventral inlay 95%).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Armenakas / McAninch approach.** If penile skin is healthy, they prefer the fasciocutaneous ventral transverse island (Jordan) flap as the urethral substitute. If there is a suggestion of penile-skin inflammation or scarring, they advise considering extragenital tissue transfer such as buccal mucosa.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Summary

The Jordan flap is a well-vascularized, single-stage technique for short fossa-navicularis and meatal strictures in patients with healthy genital skin. Series report functional success with a normal-appearing glans and slit-like meatus. Its main limitation is LS (BXO): in Virasoro's series, LS/BXO recurred at the fossa or meatus in 6 of 12 patients, and the AUA advises against genital skin in LS-proven stricture. Buccal mucosa graft is the usual substitute.
