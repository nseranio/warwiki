# Jordan Fasciocutaneous Ventral Penile / Preputial Island Flap

The Jordan flap is a transverse ventral penile or preputial fasciocutaneous skin island elevated on a dartos fascia pedicle, designed for single-stage reconstruction of fossa navicularis and meatal strictures. [Gerald H. Jordan](/docs/roots/surgeons/h-r/gerald-jordan) first described it in *J Urol* in 1987, and the later series in this article use the same flap.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Jordan described the flap because earlier flap procedures for fossa navicularis strictures did not leave a normal-appearing penis. The goal is to restore urethral patency and keep a cosmetically normal glans.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

This article consolidates the prior separately-listed entries for the Jordan & Schlossberg 1987 transverse-island variant and the Jordan 1999 penile-skin-flap variant, which are the same flap family applied to the same anatomic problem.

For BMG alternatives in the same anatomic location, see [Sliding-T Dorsal Inlay (Hoare / Rourke)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/sliding-t-dorsal-inlay) and [Transmeatal OMG Ventral Inlay (Nikolavsky)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/transmeatal-omg-ventral-inlay). For the broader penile / preputial pedicled-flap family principles, see [Penile / Preputial Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/penile-preputial). For an uncomplicated first-time meatal or fossa-navicularis stricture, [meatotomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/meatotomy) or dilation may be used initially.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Surgical Technique

The Jordan flap is a transverse skin island on a broad dartos pedicle.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1. Skin-island harvest | Mark a transverse island of thin, pliable distal ventral penile or preputial skin, and confirm that the selected donor skin is non-hair-bearing before urethral substitution.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> As a starting template, an island about 1.5 cm wide suits an onlay and about 2.5 cm suits complete circumferential substitution fashioned over an 18-Fr Foley, sized to the measured urethral circumference; excess width can leave sacculation<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| 2. Pedicle dissection | Elevate the island on a broad dartos fascia pedicle for reliable, well-vascularized blood supply; dissect the pedicle proximally for adequate mobilization |
| 3. Stricture exposure | Split the glans ventrally or use a glans-cap technique to expose the fossa-navicularis stricture; incise ventrally through the full diseased length to healthy proximal urethra, preserving the dorsal wall.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| 4. Flap onlay | Rotate the skin island into the defect and suture as a ventral onlay to augment the strictured urethra, preserving the dorsal urethral wall. Fine absorbable sutures to the urethrotomy edges |
| 5. Glansplasty | Reconstruct the glans over the flap to restore a normal slit-like meatus and cosmetic glans contour. Armenakas / McAninch advocate a glans-cap repair for limited dissection and minimal blood loss.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> A conical glans without major scarring or distortion favors glans-cap, which preserves the glans and limits dissection; a flattened or severely diseased glans favors glans-wings, which allows excision of fibrotic tissue and anatomic resculpting. An incision directly on the glans is avoided when feasible, for cosmetic reasons<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| 6. Catheter | In Onol's 26-patient series, the urethral catheter was removed within three weeks.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> In a 2024 atlas protocol, the urethral stent is removed at 7–10 days and VCUG is obtained at suprapubic-catheter removal at 10–14 days, after stent removal.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> EAU 2026 instead recommends validated urethrography to assess extravasation before urethral-catheter removal after urethroplasty (strong); significant extravasation calls for continued drainage and repeat imaging after one week.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |

Degloving to the penile base permits tension-free flap rotation. The transverse island is elevated on ventral dartos while preserving a subdermal plexus in the proximal skin to reduce ischemia risk. When glans wings are selected, the lateral glans wings are widely mobilized in the relatively avascular plane between the corporal tips and the glanular erectile tissue. The flap is then transposed and inverted into the defect rather than advanced under tension; a 28–30 Fr neofossa template and a 28-Fr sound guide closure. The pedicle is secured over the urethral suture line to help prevent fistula. Urethral-edge sutures are 5-0 or 6-0 polyglactin; glans closure uses 4-0 polyglactin 910.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

## Indications

- **Meatal stenosis** and fossa navicularis strictures, most reliably when short. Morey reported reliable onlay results for isolated strictures ≤ 2.5 cm, and the Onol cohort had strictures < 1.5 cm.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> Jordan and Rourke's 2006 technical chapter states that the flap can be used for strictures up to 4.5 cm; this predates the length-stratified outcome data above and is not a validated success threshold.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Recurrent strictures** after failed meatotomy or dilation. AUA guidelines state that surgeons should offer urethroplasty for recurrent meatal or fossa navicularis strictures (Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Distal urethral strictures after surgery for BPH (the Onol cohort; not specifically radical prostatectomy) involving the meatus or fossa.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Requires intact, healthy penile or preputial skin (uncircumcised or sufficient remaining ventral penile skin).

---

## Contraindications and Limitations

- **Lichen sclerosus (BXO)** is the dominant limitation. Jordan's own long-term series (Virasoro / Eltahawy / Jordan 2007) showed 100% excellent results in non-LS patients (23 / 23) at mean 10.2 years but 50% LS (BXO) recurrence (6 / 12) in LS patients. However, 17/35 patients (48%) were lost to follow-up and three died, limiting durability inference. In lichen sclerosus-proven urethral stricture, the AUA states that surgeons should not use genital skin for reconstruction (Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Circumcised patients** with insufficient remaining ventral penile skin.
- **Extension into the pendulous urethra.** Morey reported onlay success in 10/11 isolated fossa strictures of 1.5–2.5 cm versus 7/13 longer pendulous strictures extending into the fossa (mean 7.4 cm, range 4–16 cm) in a mixed flap-and-graft cohort. The poorer outcomes apply to long pendulous extension; the study provides no isolated-fossa result above 2.5 cm.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Diseased or scarred penile skin.** Extragenital tissue (buccal mucosa) preferred.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Outcomes

| Series | n | FU (mean) | Success | Notable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jordan 1987 (original)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 5 | 17 mo (median) | 100% | First description; excellent cosmetic / functional outcomes |
| Fiala 2003<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | 21 | 35 mo | 100% | Meatal and distal strictures; no recurrences; all patients satisfied with cosmesis |
| Virasoro / Eltahawy / Jordan 2007<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | 35 files assessed (45 operated) | 10.2 yr (reported mean) | 83% reported success among 35 assessed files (caliber at least 16 Fr without another procedure) | 23/23 non-LS had excellent results; LS recurred in 6/12; 17/35 were lost to follow-up and three died |
| Onol 2008<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | 26 | 30.2 mo | 25/26 (96%) functional and cosmetic outcome | Distal strictures &lt;1.5 cm after surgery for BPH; 1 flap necrosis and fistula; no stricture evident in the other 25; mild spraying in all, improved within 6 weeks |

## Jordan Flap vs Buccal Mucosa Graft for Fossa Navicularis

There is no randomized comparison specific to Jordan flap versus buccal graft for isolated fossa-navicularis strictures. For urethroplasty generally, EAU 2026 recommends a graft when both options are equally indicated (strong) and a flap for a poorly vascularized urethral bed (weak).<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> Distal-site selection also depends on etiology and donor-skin quality:

- **Non-LS strictures with healthy penile skin.** The flap is a reasonable option. All 23 non-LS patients in Jordan's retrospective series had excellent results, but 17 of the 35 patients assessed were lost to follow-up.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **LS-related strictures.** Buccal mucosa graft is preferred because genital skin is susceptible to LS recurrence. In separate single-stage buccal-graft series, dorsal inlay left 22/32 (69%) free of symptom-driven instrumentation at median 42 months.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> Sliding-T allowed passage of a 16-Fr cystoscope in 25/27 at mean 29.6 months.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Transurethral ventral inlay left 95% of 44 patients patent without further intervention at mean 36 months.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> These cohorts used different populations and endpoints.
- **Armenakas / McAninch approach.** If penile skin is healthy, they prefer the fasciocutaneous ventral transverse island (Jordan) flap as the urethral substitute. If there is a suggestion of penile-skin inflammation or scarring, they advise considering extragenital tissue transfer such as buccal mucosa.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Summary

The Jordan flap is a well-vascularized, single-stage technique for short fossa-navicularis and meatal strictures in patients with healthy genital skin. Series report functional success with a normal-appearing glans and slit-like meatus. Its main limitation is LS (BXO): in Virasoro’s series, LS/BXO recurred at the fossa or meatus in 6 of 12 patients,<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> and the AUA advises against genital skin in LS-proven stricture.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Buccal mucosa graft is the usual substitute. After urethroplasty, monitor for symptomatic stricture recurrence (AUA 2023 Expert Opinion).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
