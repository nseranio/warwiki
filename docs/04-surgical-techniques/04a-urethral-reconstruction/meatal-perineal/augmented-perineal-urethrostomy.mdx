# Augmented Perineal Urethrostomy

**Augmented perineal urethrostomy** combines a standard perineal urethrostomy (PU) with a dorsal onlay buccal-mucosal graft (BMG) to widen the proximal urethral opening and support perineal-stoma construction in patients with complex anterior urethral stricture disease.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> It modifies the standard PU to reduce stomal stenosis by lining part of the neomeatus with hairless oral mucosa. Superiority over an otherwise suitable non-augmented PU has not been established; LS-associated recurrence can occur despite buccal grafting.

Augmented PU is approach-agnostic: the dorsal BMG can be incorporated through either a midline incision (preserving the algorithmic loop or 7-flap framework) or an inverted-U incision ([Blandy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/blandy-perineal-urethrostomy)). It is therefore a graft-augmentation option layered onto a PU, not a distinct incision or flap technique. It is best understood as a PU-shaped first stage of urethroplasty: the patient leaves the operating room with a definitive perineal stoma whose lumen has been augmented with buccal mucosa, with no obligatory second-stage tubularization unless later elected.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For the non-augmented techniques, see [Midline Perineal Urethrostomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/seven-flap-perineal-urethrostomy) (loop / 7-flap) and [Blandy Perineal Urethrostomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/blandy-perineal-urethrostomy) (inverted-U).

## Indications

Augmented PU is reserved for situations where standard skin-only PU is at elevated risk of stenosis or where standard urethroplasty is not feasible or has failed:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- **Pananterior (long-segment) anterior urethral strictures.** All 44 patients in the DeLong series had pananterior disease.
- **Lichen sclerosus** with extensive urethral involvement (approximately 23% of the DeLong cohort).
- **Failed prior hypospadias repairs** ("hypospadias cripples") with depleted local tissue and a hairless requirement.
- **Multiple failed urethroplasties**.
- **Fournier's gangrene** with urethral destruction; extensive urethral excision for urethral or penile cancer.
- **Older or comorbid patients** who are poor candidates for prolonged or staged reconstruction and accept sitting to void.

The AUA 2023 urethral stricture guideline amendment endorses PU as a long-term option, either as an alternative to urethroplasty (Conditional Recommendation) or for patients at high risk for reconstruction failure (Expert Opinion); graft augmentation is a refinement within that framework.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Surgical Technique

The technique described by DeLong, Jordan, McCammon, and Virasoro layers a dorsal BMG onlay onto a PU.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

1. **Positioning and incision.** Dorsal lithotomy. A midline or inverted-U perineal incision is made and the bulbospongiosus divided to expose the bulbar urethra.
2. **Urethral exposure and urethrotomy.** The bulbar urethra is mobilized and opened; the urethrotomy is extended proximally until healthy, non-strictured urethra is reached. Only the segment needed to establish a patent proximal outlet is opened: a PU bypasses distal disease and does not require opening the entire penile stricture.
3. **Dorsal preparation.** Expose the dorsal urethral surface and corporal graft bed with the mobilization required for the chosen reconstruction; preserve remaining spongiosal and lateral vascular attachments where possible. A free graft subsequently relies on a viable recipient bed, not an intact pedicle of its own.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
4. **BMG harvest.** A buccal-mucosal graft is harvested from the inner cheek (one or both cheeks for long strictures), defatted, and tailored. Buccal mucosa was the preferred graft in approximately 99% of the 134 respondents to a GURS survey; that is a survey response, not a population-wide estimate.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
5. **Dorsal onlay graft placement.** The BMG is quilted onto the dorsal corporal bed and sutured to the edges of the dorsal urethrotomy with fine absorbable suture (5-0/6-0 polyglactin or polydioxanone). Dorsal placement provides a well-vascularized, mechanically supported bed for graft take.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
6. **Urethrostomy maturation.** The ventral urethral edges are matured to the perineal skin, creating a composite stoma of skin and buccal mucosa in which the graft forms the widened dorsal wall of the neomeatus.
7. **Catheter and closure.** A 16–18 Fr catheter is placed through the urethrostomy; the perineal wound is closed in layers and the catheter typically removed at approximately 2–3 weeks.

A related minimal-access dorsal BMG approach via a ventral sagittal urethrotomy (Gupta) shares the same dorsal-onlay, vascularized-bed principle.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Rationale for Dorsal Graft Augmentation

The distinguishing step is the dorsal BMG, which addresses stomal stenosis, the failure mode of skin-only PU (reintervention in 4.9% of 103 men in Joshi;<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> postoperative stenosis in 7 of 40 men, 18%, in Myers<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>). Buccal mucosa offers several advantages over perineal or scrotal skin:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

- **Preferred to genital skin in LS**, but not immune to disease-associated recurrence; recurrent LS or stricture in a first-stage BMG was reported in 9/37 patients in one LS series.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Thick, resilient epithelium** with a thin, highly vascular lamina propria that promotes reliable graft take.
- **Hairless.** Avoids intra-stomal hair growth and its sequelae.
- Can widen an otherwise inadequate proximal urethral segment; comparative durability versus skin-only PU remains uncertain.

## Outcomes

| Series | n | Cohort | FU | Success |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DeLong 2017 (augmented PU)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 44 | Pananterior; 23% LS; mean age 60 | 45 mo | 35/44 (80%) primary; four successful revisions among nine recurrences |
| Joshi 2024 (standard midline PU)<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | 103 | Mixed midline algorithmic | 61 mo at survey in 49 respondents | 98/103 (95.1%) |
| Fuchs 2018 (standard PU)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | 77 | Mixed | 50.7 mo | 94.8% |

These are separate retrospective cohorts with different case mix, definitions and follow-up; they cannot rank augmented versus non-augmented PU. In Joshi, only 49/103 men completed the telephone survey: 42/49 were satisfied and 12/49 reported de novo ED; 31/41 answering the sitting-to-void question were unbothered. These responder outcomes are not augmented-PU-specific risks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The EAU guideline summarizes PU patency as 51.5-100% at 13-62 months in retrospective, heterogeneous series, with lower patency in series with longer follow-up.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Selection and Lichen Sclerosus

Consider augmentation when a healthy, adequately wide proximal opening cannot be fashioned with local tissue alone, particularly with proximal bulbar extension or a stenosed prior PU. The EAU guideline describes the Gil-Vernet-Blandy PU with a dorsal or ventral free oral-mucosa graft ("augmented Blandy") for strictures after radiotherapy or from LS, and for PU stenosis or stricture extending into the proximal bulbar or membranous urethra. It describes the 7-flap PU for very obese men or for men of any BMI with proximal extension. It gives a weak recommendation to consider augmented Gil-Vernet-Blandy or 7-flap PU for proximal bulbar or membranous disease. Little data determine the best PU technique, and augmentation is not required for every LS case.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

Prior radiation was associated with PU stenosis in the small Myers cohort (OR 11.2; 95% CI 1.4–87.2), an imprecise risk signal that does not show that a graft overcomes radiation injury.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> In Patel's LS series, 13/14 PU patients and 15/20 single-stage BMG patients met the study success definition; selection and small numbers prevent a comparative superiority claim. LS-related recurrence can occur in BMG and at a PU, so continuing follow-up is necessary.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> A pooled analysis of one-stage BMG urethroplasty for LS (21 studies, 625 patients) found 10% recurrence overall and 18% in studies with follow-up of 24 months or more.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

PU can be discussed as a primary long-term choice, alongside reconstruction, when the patient accepts a perineal outlet. A healthy, tension-free non-augmented stoma avoids oral donor morbidity. For graft biology and donor counseling, see [Buccal Mucosa Graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa).

| Technique | Incision | Key feature | Best suited for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **[Blandy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/blandy-perineal-urethrostomy)** | Inverted-U | Posteriorly based inverted-U flap; apex parachuted to opened urethra | Standard PU with adequate perineal skin |
| **[Johanson](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/johanson-two-stage)** | Midline or inverted-U | Simple marsupialization of opened urethra to skin (no formal flap) | Historical / staged-urethroplasty first stage |
| **[7-flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/seven-flap-perineal-urethrostomy)** | Midline | Laterally based "7" skin flap advanced into the depth of the wound | Long urethra-to-skin distance; obesity; proximal transection |
| **[Loop](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/seven-flap-perineal-urethrostomy)** | Midline | Dorsal plate preserved; urethra matured directly to skin, no flap | Distal strictures; low BMI |
| **Augmented (this page)** | Midline *or* inverted-U | Dorsal onlay BMG + skin maturation → composite stoma | LS; failed hypospadias; high stomal-stenosis risk |
| **[Propeller flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/propeller-flap)** | Revision | Posterior-thigh perforator flap rotated into a scarred perineum | Complex PU revision when local tissue is depleted |

## Revision Setting

The same dorsal-BMG principle salvages an already-stenotic perineal urethrostomy. Kamat described BMG widening of a stenotic stoma to create a composite skin-and-mucosa neomeatus, particularly useful when lichen sclerosus or BXO threatens the skin component of the stoma.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> When local perineal tissue is wholly depleted (radiated, multiply operated), salvage escalates to a [posterior-thigh propeller flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/propeller-flap).
