# Meatotomy & Meatoplasty

**Meatotomy** is the surgical incision of the urethral meatus, while **meatoplasty** is the formal reconstruction of the urethral meatus, including local-flap, free-graft, and BMG-based variants. Both target meatal stenosis (narrowing of the external urethral opening) and apply across pediatric and adult populations. A retrospective pediatric comparison (Dothan 2023, n = 2,211) reported no reoperations after meatoplasty versus 0.58% after meatotomy; selection differed between groups, and these results do not establish a universal preference across children and adults.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For longer fossa-navicularis disease that extends beyond the meatus, see [Sliding-T Dorsal Inlay (Hoare / Rourke)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/sliding-t-dorsal-inlay) and [Transmeatal OMG Ventral Inlay (Nikolavsky)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/transmeatal-omg-ventral-inlay). For the Jordan flap meatoplasty (ventral preputial island), see [Jordan Fasciocutaneous Ventral Penile / Preputial Island Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/fasciocutaneous-penile-flap). For lichen-sclerosus-driven distal disease, see [Lichen Sclerosus](/docs/clinical-conditions/03g-genital-scrotal/lichen-sclerosus). For the broader urethral-stricture treatment framework, see the [Master Decision Framework](/docs/surgical-techniques/urethral-reconstruction).

## Epidemiology and Pathophysiology

- **Post-circumcision.** Circumcision is the setting in which meatal stenosis is most often reported in boys. A meta-analysis of 27 studies estimated the risk after circumcision at 0.66% (95% CI 0.44–0.91); comparative evidence that circumcision raises the risk is weak (three observational studies, OR 3.20, 95% CI 0.73–13.9). The overall circumcision complication rate is 3.84%, and 7.47% for therapeutic circumcisions, in which meatal stenosis is among the most frequent complications. The traditional chemical or mechanical-trauma-from-diaper hypothesis has been challenged.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Post-hypospadias repair.** Meatal stenosis is a recognized complication, often from circumferential scar contracture at the neomeatus.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Lichen sclerosus (BXO).** A major cause in adults and older boys. Up to 1 in 5 boys circumcised for LS later require a meatal procedure; in adults, LS-associated strictures recur at 20.5% versus 7.5% (p = 0.04).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Iatrogenic.** Prior urethral instrumentation, catheterization, transurethral surgery.
- **Atopic or inflammatory.** Boys with meatal stenosis have a 61% history of allergic disease versus 13% of controls.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

Children typically present with deviated urinary stream (most common, approximately 67%), dysuria, difficult-to-aim stream, frequency, and occasionally retention; physical examination reveals a pinpoint meatus with an upward thin forceful stream.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Indications

Per the AUA 2023 urethral stricture guideline amendment:<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

- **First-time, uncomplicated** meatal or fossa-navicularis stricture: surgeons may treat with dilation or meatotomy (Clinical Principle), unless it is associated with prior hypospadias repair, failed endoscopic treatment, prior urethroplasty, or LS.
- **Recurrent** meatal or fossa-navicularis strictures: surgeons should offer urethroplasty (Moderate Recommendation, Evidence Level Grade C). The guideline text lists meatoplasty, extended meatotomy and several urethroplasty variations as reconstructive options.
- **LS-associated** strictures: the guideline text states that oral mucosal grafts are more likely to achieve successful reconstruction.

## Meatotomy vs Meatoplasty — Dothan 2023 Comparison

The retrospective Dothan cohort provides useful pediatric reoperation data, with different presentations associated with the procedure chosen; it does not measure complete anatomic patency or establish causation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

| Parameter | Meatotomy (n = 1 906) | Meatoplasty (n = 305) |
|---|---|---|
| Reoperation rate | 0.58% (11 patients) | 0% |
| Manual meatal spreading needed postop | 3.6% (69 patients) | Not reported |
| Operative duration | No difference | No difference |
| Recovery time | No difference | No difference |
| Presentation associated with selection | Deviated stream (OR 1.47) | Urinary retention (OR 4.5) |

## Standard Ventral Meatotomy — Step-by-Step

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1. Anesthesia | Local (EMLA cream applied 1 hr prior under occlusive dressing), local + sedation, dorsal penile nerve block, or general. A prospective RCT showed equivalent intraoperative pain control between EMLA / sedation and general anesthesia (no pain in 92–93%).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| 2. Positioning | Supine; glans and meatus prepped with antiseptic |
| 3. Crush and incise | Place a fine straight hemostat into the meatus along the **ventral midline (6 o'clock)** — one jaw inside the urethra, one on the ventral glans. Close for 30–60 seconds to crush and pre-hemostase the line. Sharp incision with fine scissors along the crushed line opens the meatus ventrally |
| 4. Extent | Open the stenotic segment to healthy tissue; choose caliber for the child’s age or adult anatomy and the extent of disease, rather than a fixed incision depth |
| 5. Hemostasis | Primary hemostasis from the crush; electrocautery or silver nitrate for any persistent bleeders |
| 6. Maturation | In sutured meatoplasty, approximate mucosa to glans epithelium with fine absorbable suture. The retrospective reoperation comparison does not isolate sutures as the cause of any difference |
| 7. Postoperative | Topical antibiotic ointment or petroleum jelly to the meatus 1–2 weeks; gentle meatal spreading 2–3× daily for several weeks to prevent re-adhesion |

## Meatoplasty Techniques

### Standard ventral meatoplasty

A ventral incision through the stenotic meatus, excision of fibrotic tissue, and suture approximation of urethral mucosa to glans skin to create a wider, stable opening, the most commonly performed technique for post-circumcision meatal stenosis in children.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Y-V meatoplasty (Sennert / Hadidi 2025)

A technique specifically designed for ring meatal stenosis after hypospadias repair. The fundamental principle is to interrupt the circumferential scar ring by interposing a V-shaped flap of healthy glans tissue, with the aim of reducing recurrent ring contracture.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

**Steps:**

1. **Inverted V flap design.** A small inverted-V flap is outlined adjacent to the meatus with its apex at the stenotic opening; each limb 3–5 mm.
2. **Flap elevation.** The V flap is raised from underlying glans, preserving the basal blood supply.
3. **Stenotic ring excision.** A wedge of the narrowed meatus underneath the elevated flap is excised, creating a wide patent meatus.
4. **V advancement.** The flap tip is advanced distally into the widened meatus and sutured to the angle, converting the Y-shaped incision into a V-shaped closure.
5. **Scar-line interruption.** The interposed V breaks the circumferential suture or scar line, minimizing further ring contraction.

**Outcomes.** Sennert / Hadidi reported symptom relief in 55/57 patients and two reoperations, with median follow-up four years (range three months to eight years). This single-center retrospective series has no concurrent comparator and does not demonstrate superiority over another meatoplasty technique.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

**Kim & King 1992 precursor.** An earlier related technique described a V-shaped glans flap with apex at the dorsum of the meatus, with the stenosis incised along the back wall of the urethra into non-inflamed tissue and the flap tip sutured to the distal extent of the incision (penile-base tourniquet during the procedure). Anchors healthy tissue across the stenotic area; recurrent stenosis after this maneuver was reported as unusual.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>

### Jordan ventral preputial flap meatoplasty (Fiala 2003)

A pedicled ventral preputial skin flap on a dartos pedicle for meatal and distal urethral strictures in adults; see [Jordan Fasciocutaneous Ventral Penile / Preputial Island Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/fasciocutaneous-penile-flap). Fiala 21-pt series: 100% success at mean 35 mo, no recurrence, no spray during micturition, and cosmetic satisfaction.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

### Extended meatotomy (first-stage Johanson maneuver)

For strictures extending into the fossa navicularis, lay the ventral urethra open from meatus through the entire strictured segment and marsupialize the urethral mucosa to adjacent skin edges, creating a hypospadiac meatus.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> Morey 2007 reports 87% success (14/16) in complex or reoperative fossa-navicularis strictures within a 40-patient series (mean follow-up 52 months). In the same series, onlay reconstruction succeeded in 10/11 short (2.5 cm or less) isolated strictures and 7/13 longer strictures. The AUA 2023 amendment notes that extended meatotomy combined with high-dose topical steroids may reduce recurrence in LS strictures.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

### Malone V-relief technique for LS

Dorsal and ventral meatotomies with an inverted-V relieving incision dorsally to correct puckering, which avoids creating a hypospadiac meatus. Malone 2004 reported no recurrences at mean 3 yr 9 mo with 85% reporting no spraying.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

### BMG meatoplasty / fossa-navicularis urethroplasty

Reserved for recurrent or complex meatal or fossa-navicularis strictures, particularly LS-associated. Multiple approaches:

| Approach | n | Success | Follow-Up | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dorsal inlay BMG (Zumstein) | 32 | 69% | 42 mo (RFS 79% at 12 mo, 74% at 24 mo) | <sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> |
| Sliding-T dorsal inlay (Hoare / Rourke) | 27 | 25/27 (approximately 93%) | 29.6 mo, 96.3% satisfaction; 70.4% LS | <sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| Transurethral ventral inlay (Sterling / Nikolavsky) | 44 | 95% | 36 mo; significant Qmax / PVR / IPSS / QoL improvement | <sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |
| Transurethral dorsal inlay (Farrell) | 16 | 93.8% anatomic / 100% functional | 28.8 mo; avoids glans splitting | <sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> |
| Circumferential buccal graft (Ehlers / Figler) | series | — | Glans-sparing transurethral excision for severe meatal / fossa-navicularis strictures | <sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> |

## Outcomes by Clinical Context

### Pediatric post-circumcision meatal stenosis

In a retrospective series of 22 boys (mean follow-up 12 months), uroflowmetry improved after meatoplasty: Qmax 9.7 to 16.4 mL/s (p = 0.001) and PVR 19.3 to 13.9 mL (p = 0.01). Of the 19 boys with an abnormal preoperative flow pattern, 88% had a bell-shaped pattern afterward.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> In a prospective study of 25 boys after meatotomy (15 fully evaluable, mean follow-up 43 months), the authors concluded that symptom evaluation and physical examination suffice for postoperative assessment and that uroflowmetry does not change management.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

### Post-hypospadias meatal stenosis

In Bashi’s selected follow-up cohort, 63 of 112 eligible patients were analyzed: 45 required one meatoplasty and 18 required repeat surgery, with resolution reported after the final procedure. Of the 18 recurrences, approximately 28% occurred within one year and 39% at five years or later; these percentages do not describe recurrence risk in the entire cohort.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Adult distal urethral strictures

Treatment depends on anatomy and cause: an initial uncomplicated meatal stricture may be treated by meatotomy or dilation, while recurrent disease, LS, or prior hypospadias repair may warrant primary reconstructive assessment. Success percentages from selected technique groups do not establish a required sequence of meatotomy, meatoplasty and graft.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Lichen Sclerosus — Special Considerations

LS-associated meatal stenosis warrants particular attention:

- **Intraurethral steroids.** In the small Potts cohort, 25/28 men did not require escalation of treatment during follow-up. Ongoing self-calibration and steroid use may be necessary; this represents disease stabilization, not proven cure. See the [topical corticosteroid hub](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/dermatologic-topical-urethral/high-potency-topical-corticosteroids).<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
- Extended meatotomy plus topical steroids may decrease recurrence versus meatotomy alone.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- One-stage BMG urethroplasty for LS has a pooled recurrence rate of 10% (95% CI 6–14%), rising to 18% in studies with follow-up of 24 months or more. In long-term studies only, recurrence was higher in patients with LS (OR 1.83, p = 0.05).<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>
- Genital skin is best avoided for flaps or grafts because of the predilection of LS to recur in it; oral mucosa is preferred.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>
- Perineal urethrostomy is a long-term option for extensive LS when consistent with the patient’s goals. Patel reported success in 13/14 selected PU patients; this does not establish the highest success across competing reconstructions.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup> See [Augmented Perineal Urethrostomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/augmented-perineal-urethrostomy).
- Long-term surveillance (and biopsy when indicated) due to potential association of LS with squamous cell carcinoma.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

## Complications

- **Restenosis.** Risk depends on cause, reconstruction and follow-up. Dothan’s 0.58% versus 0% figures measure pediatric reoperation, not all recurrent narrowing. Adult LS-associated disease has a different prognosis.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Bleeding.** Minor, approximately 4%.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Spraying of urinary stream.** Particularly with hypospadiac configurations after ventral meatotomy.
- **Need for manual meatal spreading.** 3.6% of children at the first postoperative visit (meatotomy cohort).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Cosmetic dissatisfaction.** Rare with standard technique.

---

## Prevention

- Petroleum jelly applied to the glans after circumcision was associated with reduced meatal-stenosis risk (RR 0.024; 95% CI 0.005–0.12) in a meta-analysis of three randomized trials.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Pre-circumcision topical steroids** in boys with LS may decrease the rate of subsequent meatal pathology.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
