# Meatotomy

**Meatotomy** is the surgical incision of the urethral meatus performed to relieve **meatal stenosis** — narrowing of the external urethral opening. It is one of the most common minor urological procedures and applies to both pediatric and adult male populations.

For longer fossa-navicularis disease that extends beyond the meatus, see [Sliding-T Dorsal Inlay (Hoare / Rourke)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/sliding-t-dorsal-inlay) and [Transmeatal OMG Ventral Inlay (Nikolavsky)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/transmeatal-omg-ventral-inlay). For lichen-sclerosus-driven distal disease, see [Lichen Sclerosus](/docs/clinical-conditions/03g-genital-scrotal/lichen-sclerosus). For the broader urethral-stricture treatment framework, see the [Master Decision Framework](/docs/surgical-techniques/urethral-reconstruction).

---

## Etiology of Meatal Stenosis

- **Post-circumcision.** The most common cause in boys. Morris 2017 meta-analysis of 27 studies estimated risk **~ 0.66%** (95% CI 0.44–0.91%); some primary-care studies report up to 7.3% in circumcised boys older than 3 years. The traditional chemical / mechanical-trauma-from-diaper hypothesis has been challenged.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Lichen sclerosus (BXO).** Up to **1 in 5 boys** circumcised for LS later require a meatal procedure.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Iatrogenic.** Prior urethral instrumentation, catheterization, transurethral surgery, or failed hypospadias repair.
- **Atopic / inflammatory.** Boys with meatal stenosis have a 61% history of allergic disease vs 13% of controls (atopic dermatitis, allergic rhinitis, asthma).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Idiopathic.**

---

## Indications

Symptomatic meatal stenosis presenting with:<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- **Deviated or deflected stream** — most common indication (~ 67%).
- Thin, high-velocity, or upwardly directed stream.
- Dysuria.
- Frequency or straining.
- Urinary retention in severe cases.
- Recurrent meatitis or urinary tract infections.

Per the **AUA 2023 urethral stricture guideline amendment**, a **first-time presentation of an uncomplicated stricture confined to the meatus or fossa navicularis** can be treated with simple dilation or meatotomy — provided it is not associated with prior hypospadias repair, failed endoscopic manipulation, prior urethroplasty, or lichen sclerosus.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Standard Ventral Meatotomy — Step-by-Step

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1. Anesthesia | Local (EMLA cream applied 1 hr prior under occlusive dressing), local + sedation, dorsal penile nerve block, or general. A prospective RCT showed equivalent intraoperative pain control between EMLA / sedation and general anesthesia (no pain in 92–93%).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| 2. Positioning | Supine; glans and meatus prepped with antiseptic |
| 3. Crush and incise | Place a fine straight hemostat into the meatus along the **ventral midline (6 o'clock)** — one jaw inside the urethra, one on the ventral glans. Close for 30–60 seconds to crush and pre-hemostase the line. Sharp incision with fine scissors along the crushed line opens the meatus ventrally |
| 4. Extent | Carry proximally until adequate caliber — typically 10–14 Fr in children, 16–20 Fr in adults. Depth usually 3–5 mm |
| 5. Hemostasis | Primary hemostasis from the crush; electrocautery or silver nitrate for any persistent bleeders |
| 6. Suture (recommended) | Approximate urethral mucosa to glans epithelium with fine absorbable suture (6-0 or 7-0 chromic / polyglactin) to reduce re-stenosis. Sutureless meatotomy carries a higher restenosis rate |
| 7. Postoperative | Topical antibiotic ointment or petroleum jelly to the meatus 1–2 weeks; gentle meatal spreading 2–3× daily for several weeks to prevent re-adhesion |

## Extended Meatotomy (First-Stage Johanson Maneuver)

For strictures extending into the **fossa navicularis**, an extended meatotomy lays the ventral urethra open from the meatus through the entire strictured segment and marsupializes the urethral mucosa to adjacent skin edges, creating a hypospadiac meatus.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- Morey 2007: **87% success** (14 / 16) in complex / reoperative fossa-navicularis strictures at mean 52 months.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- AUA 2023 notes that extended meatotomy combined with **high-dose topical steroids** may reduce recurrence in LS-related strictures.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

---

## Malone Technique for Lichen Sclerosus

A modified approach uses **dorsal and ventral meatotomies** with an inverted-V relieving incision dorsally to correct puckering. It avoids creating a hypospadiac meatus. Malone 2004 reported **no recurrences at mean 3 yr 9 mo** with 85% reporting no spraying.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Outcomes

| Series | Procedure | Success | Follow-up | Population |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Meeks 2012<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | Simple meatotomy | **87%** | 61 mo | Adults (n = 73) |
| Morey 2007<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | Extended meatotomy (Johanson) | **87%** | 52 mo | Adults (n = 16) |
| Dothan 2023<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | Pediatric meatotomy | **99.4%** (0.58% reoperation) | Variable | Children (n = 1906) |
| Malone 2004<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> | Dorsal + ventral with V-relief (LS) | **100%** (no recurrence) | 45 mo | Adults / children (n = 19) |

Patient satisfaction is high — **84%** satisfied or very satisfied in the multi-institutional Meeks 2012 cohort.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> Uroflowmetry shows significant Qmax and PVR improvement at long-term follow-up, though routine post-op uroflow does not change management.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

---

## Complications

- **Restenosis** — the dominant concern. 0.58% in the largest pediatric series, but higher in adults, particularly with LS (recurrence 20.5% with LS vs 7.5% without, p = 0.04).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Bleeding** — minor, ~ 4%.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Spraying of urinary stream** — particularly with hypospadiac configurations after ventral meatotomy.
- **Need for manual meatal spreading** — in 3.6% of children at the first postoperative visit.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Cosmetic dissatisfaction** — rare with standard technique.

---

## When to Escalate to Urethroplasty

The AUA 2023 guideline recommends offering **urethroplasty** for recurrent meatal or fossa-navicularis strictures (Moderate Recommendation, Grade C).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> Options include meatoplasty, flap urethroplasty, and **buccal mucosa graft urethroplasty** — the latter preferred for LS-related strictures.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

Two contemporary single-stage transmeatal BMG techniques in particular extend meatotomy concepts up the fossa navicularis without an external incision:

- **Sliding-T dorsal inlay** (Hoare / Rourke 2021) — single-stage glans-sparing fossa-navicularis BMG inlay with 92.4% success in 27 patients.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Transmeatal OMG ventral inlay** (Nikolavsky / Daneshvar / Tolbert) — pull-through ventral-inlay technique with multi-institutional 95% success.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
