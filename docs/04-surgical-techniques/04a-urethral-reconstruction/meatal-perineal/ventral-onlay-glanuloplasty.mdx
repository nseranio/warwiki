# Ventral Onlay Glanuloplasty

**Ventral onlay glanuloplasty (VOG)** is a single-stage **buccal mucosal graft** reconstruction for selected fossa-navicularis strictures. Wayne and colleagues described a ventral glans incision with graft onlay and closure without extensive glans-wing mobilization. The reported outcome cohort excluded lichen sclerosus, so its results do not apply to LS-specific repair or split-thickness skin grafting.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Indications

The AUA guideline allows dilation or meatotomy, with or without a guidewire, for a first, uncomplicated stricture confined to the meatus or fossa navicularis when it is not associated with previous hypospadias repair, failed endoscopic treatment, previous urethroplasty or lichen sclerosus. Surgeons should offer urethroplasty for recurrent meatal or fossa-navicularis strictures (Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level Grade C).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> VOG is one of the urethroplasty options for that recurrent or complicated group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Selection

Define distal stricture extent and tissue quality before choosing VOG, transmeatal grafting, meatoplasty or staged reconstruction. The published series addressed fossa-navicularis disease and permitted separately treated proximal skip strictures. Its median stricture length was 2 cm, with some longer strictures included, and causes were mainly iatrogenic (transurethral prostate resection 4/10, GreenLight laser vaporization 2/10, cystolitholapaxy 1/10, traumatic catheterization 3/10). For LS, EAU guidance recommends against genital skin for augmentation penile urethroplasty (strong) and recommends against staged bulbar urethroplasty when single-stage repair is possible (weak).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The ten analyzed patients were drawn from an institutional database of 151 patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Lichen sclerosus affects recurrence: in a two-center series of 93 distal repairs, recurrence was 20.5% with LS and 7.5% without it.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Prior hypospadias repair is another cause; it accounted for 16 of 32 patients (50%) in a single-center series of dorsal inlay BMG repair for meatal and fossa strictures.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Technique Overview

The following summarizes the published technique. It does not prescribe dimensions for every patient.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

1. Open the ventral glans in the midline from the meatus to healthy proximal urethra; place a traction suture on the glans (2-0 silk in the published series) and pass a sensor wire through the stricture if possible.
2. Harvest, defat and tailor buccal mucosa. Anastomose the graft longitudinally to the ventral urethrotomy margins to augment the distal lumen, with interrupted sutures at the apex and a running 5-0 PDS suture along the lateral edges over a 20 Fr red rubber catheter.
3. Cover the graft with dartos tissue and reapproximate the glans without extensive glans-wing undermining. Mature the distal graft edge to the glans to form the neomeatus.
4. Check caliber and tension, then catheterize. The authors discharged patients with a Foley catheter that was removed at the 3-week visit, when patency was assessed with voiding cystourethrography (VCUG).

In the published description, the dartos flap and a two-layer closure of the glans spongiosum provide the vascular support for the graft.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Outcomes and Limits

Of 11 treated patients, one with LS was excluded from the reported analysis. Success required no extravasation on VCUG at 3 weeks and no later dilation or other instrumentation. Nine of ten analyzed patients met this definition at median 30-month follow-up (IQR 24.3–36.8); one recurrence, at 419 days, required dilation and triamcinolone. All ten reported satisfaction with appearance, and no 30-day complications were recorded. This is a small uncontrolled retrospective series. It cannot establish superiority or exclude uncommon harms, and it provides no LS-specific durability data.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

A related single-stage ventral onlay BMG repair for fossa-navicularis strictures, reported in six patients, gave a mean flow rate of 12 mL/s at 3 months, no fistula, and one recurrence at a more proximal site at median 37-month follow-up.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

Functional outcomes and complications are reported in more detail for the glans-sparing transurethral repairs. In the 12-institution ventral inlay series, median Qmax rose from 5 to 18 mL/s and median IPSS fell from 15 to 5, and no patient developed a fistula, glanular dehiscence, graft necrosis or chordee.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> In the transurethral dorsal inlay series, urinary tract infection occurred in 25%, with no de novo erectile dysfunction, chordee or wound infection, and all patients were satisfied or very satisfied.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Comparison With Alternatives

| Technique | Study | n | Follow-up | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VOG | Retrospective series<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 10 | Median 30 months | 9 of 10 successful; no 30-day complications |
| Meatotomy, meatoplasty or substitution urethroplasty | Two-center series<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | 93 | Mean 61 months (distal urethroplasty) | Success 87% meatotomy, 75% meatoplasty, 66% substitution urethroplasty; 84% overall |
| Ventral transverse penile skin island flap (Jordan flap) | Original description<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | 5 | Not stated in abstract | Excellent cosmetic and functional results in all 5 |
| Tailored reconstruction of resistant glanular strictures | Single-center series<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | 19 | Not stated in abstract | Durable functional and cosmetic success in 18 (94.7%) |
| Dorsal inlay BMG through the opened glans | Single-center series<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | 32 | Median 42 months | Success 69%; recurrence-free survival 79% at 12 and 74% at 24 months |
| Transurethral ventral BMG inlay (glans-sparing) | Multi-institutional series<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | Not stated in abstract | Median 17 months | 95% stricture-free (54 patients) |
| Transurethral dorsal inlay BMG (glans-sparing) | Single-center series<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | 16 | Median 28.8 months | Anatomic success 15 of 16 (93.8%); functional success 16 of 16 |

The studies differ in etiology mix, LS inclusion, definition of success and follow-up, and none is randomized; the table does not rank the techniques. The glans-sparing transurethral inlays avoid any external glans incision, whereas VOG opens the ventral glans but avoids extensive glans-wing mobilization.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> Larger distal substitution series report lower success rates (66% and 69%) than the ten-patient VOG series; the cohorts are not comparable.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
