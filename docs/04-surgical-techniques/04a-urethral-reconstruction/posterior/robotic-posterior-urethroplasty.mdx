# Robotic Posterior Urethroplasty

**Robotic-assisted posterior urethroplasty (RPU)** is an alternative to the perineal or transpubic abdominoperineal approach for recalcitrant posterior urethral obstruction. The AUA 2023 urethral stricture guideline amendment states that surgeons may perform robotic or open reconstruction for recalcitrant bladder-neck contracture or post-prostatectomy vesicourethral anastomotic stenosis (VUAS) (Conditional Recommendation; Grade C).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For the canonical PFUI workflow, see [Pelvic Fracture Urethral Injury (PFUI)](/docs/special-populations/05a-trauma-emergencies/pfui). For open anastomotic reconstruction, see [Excision and Primary Anastomosis](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/excision-primary-anastomosis). Core-through urethrotomy is not open urethroplasty and is not a standard repair for obliterative PFUI. For the broader VUAS / BNC framework, see [Bladder Neck Reconstruction & VUAS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction). For the fully transurethral BMG alternative, see [Endoscopic Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/endoscopic-urethroplasty).

---

## Indications

RPU is primarily indicated for posterior obstruction that has failed endoscopic management (median 2–3 prior failed dilations / DVIU / bladder-neck incision before robotic reconstruction).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The following etiology distribution is from Bearrick’s 21-patient series, not Zhang’s 105-patient cohort:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- **Post-prostatectomy VUAS.** Most frequent (approximately 48%).
- **Bladder-neck contracture after endoscopic prostate procedures** (BPH treatment): approximately 24%.
- **Radiation-induced posterior urethral stenosis.** Approximately 24%.
- **Trauma.** One of 21 patients (5%). Zhang’s larger cohort instead reported prior radiation in 57.1%.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Surgical Approaches

The technique is tailored to location and complexity of the stenosis:<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

| Approach | Use |
|---|---|
| **Transabdominal (intra- or extraperitoneal)** | Standard robotic dock for deep pelvic dissection; transvesical access; cystoscopy defines stricture extent |
| Combined robotic transabdominal + open transperineal | Required in 39% of Zhang's 105 patients; Cavallo's series describes the combined approach for complex anastomotic reconstruction |
| Robotic-assisted perineal (Buckley) | Robot used selectively for proximal suture placement during a standard perineal dissection; about 15 min set-up plus 30–45 min suture phase (Unterberg, 10 patients) |
| **Single-port robotic** | Supraumbilical access for intraabdominal or extraperitoneal transvesical reconstruction; described with BMG augmentation by Liu / Shakir / Zhao |

### Reconstructive techniques employed (Zhang 2023 distribution)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

| Technique | Share |
|---|---|
| Excision and primary anastomosis | Commonly used; the primary abstract gives an inconsistent count/percentage, so no numerical share is assigned here |
| Resitting of the bladder neck | 24.8% |
| Y-V plasty | 20% |
| Buccal mucosa graft (BMG) urethroplasty | 13.3% |

### Ancillary procedures

Ancillary procedures include gracilis muscle flap interposition (33.3% in Cavallo's series), rectus abdominis flap, omental flap, and concurrent prostatectomy (66.7% in Cavallo's combined-approach series).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Potential Advantages and Comparative Evidence

Technical visualization benefits and small retrospective comparisons do not establish superiority in patency or continence.

- **Visualization.** Magnified 3D optics improve the view in the deep pelvis, where open visualization is limited.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Ergonomics.** The robot helps with narrow pelvic anatomy and a long distance from perineal skin to the proximal urethra.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Blood loss.** Median blood loss was 100 mL versus 200 mL with open perineal repair (p = 0.001) in Savun's series.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Hospital stay.** 3 versus 4 days in Savun’s retrospective robotic versus open comparison (p = 0.001); Bearrick’s separate robotic-only groups had 1–2-day stays.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Continence.** Savun reported de novo incontinence in 16.6% after robotic versus 100% after open surgery (p = 0.031), but the small retrospective comparison and baseline continence differences limit inference, and a protective effect of the robot is not established.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> A systematic review of ten case series (119 men) reported pooled patency of 80% and pooled de novo incontinence of 17% (8 of 47) after robotic bladder neck reconstruction.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Adjunctive procedures.** Concurrent prostatectomy, flap harvest and tissue transfer can be done in the same operation.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Outcomes

| Series | n | Approach | Success | AUS rate | Complications (endpoint varies) | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zhang 2023<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | 105 | Robotic (39% combined) | 75.2% (no reintervention) | 28.6% | 6.7% (Clavien-Dindo ≥3 within 30 days) | Mean 18.7 mo |
| Bearrick 2022<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | 21 | Robotic | 80–100% anatomic success (by etiology) | 0–80% (by etiology) | 0–40% 30-day complications by etiology | Not reported in abstract |
| Cavallo 2021<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | 12 | Combined robotic + perineal | 83.3% (stenosis recurrence 16.7%) | 75% | Wound abscess 16.7%, leak 8.3%, VTE 8.3% | Mean 596 days |
| Liu 2022<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | 9 | Single-port robotic + BMG | — | — | 0% intraop | 11.7 mo |
| Savun 2025<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | 10 (robotic) vs 18 (open) | Robotic vs open perineal (VUAS) | 80% vs 77.8% patency | — | Comparable | Not reported in abstract |

The AUA 2023 amendment cites robotic-assisted reconstruction patency rates of 72.7–75%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Impact of Etiology — the Radiation Signal

Outcomes differed by etiology in Bearrick's 21-man series, with worse results in the five men with prior radiation (P = 0.004 for reintervention, 0.035 for functional success):<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

| Etiology | Anatomic success | Functional success | Reintervention | AUS placement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Post-BPH treatment | 100% | 100% | 0% | 0% |
| Post-prostatectomy | 90% | 100% | 10% | 30% |
| Radiation-associated | 80% | 60% | 80% | 80% (p = 0.013) |

Prior radiation and preoperative incontinence were associated with worse outcomes in these selected cohorts. They are counseling factors and not a validated prediction model.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

---

## Complications

- **30-day Clavien-Dindo ≥3 complications:** 6.7% in Zhang 2023.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Wound abscess** 16.7%, urinary leak 8.3%, thromboembolism 8.3% in Cavallo 2021.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Stress urinary incontinence** is the main functional concern. AUS placement was required in 28.6% of Zhang's patients and 75% of Cavallo's, and in 0–80% by etiology in Bearrick's.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Erectile dysfunction.** De novo erectile dysfunction occurred in 16.7% in Cavallo's series.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Operative time.** Median operative time was 150 minutes in Savun's robotic group and 377 minutes in Liu's single-port BMG series, in which 5 of 9 patients had ancillary flap harvest.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

---

## Current Evidence Limitations

- The evidence base is retrospective single-center or small multi-institutional series, and no randomized trials compare robotic with open reconstruction.
- Follow-up is short to intermediate (mean or median about 12–19 months in the larger series).
- The AUA 2023 recommendation for robotic or open reconstruction is Conditional, with Grade C evidence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Patient selection is heterogeneous, and experience is concentrated at a few high-volume centers.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
