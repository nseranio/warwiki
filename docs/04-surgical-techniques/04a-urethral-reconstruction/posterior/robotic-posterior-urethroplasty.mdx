# Robotic Posterior Urethroplasty

**Robotic-assisted posterior urethroplasty (RPU)** is an alternative to the perineal or transpubic abdominoperineal approach for recalcitrant posterior urethral obstruction. The AUA 2023 urethral stricture guideline amendment states that surgeons may perform robotic or open reconstruction for recalcitrant bladder-neck contracture or post-prostatectomy vesicourethral anastomotic stenosis (VUAS) (Conditional Recommendation; Grade C).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For the canonical PFUI workflow, see [Pelvic Fracture Urethral Injury (PFUI)](/docs/special-populations/05a-trauma-emergencies/pfui). For open anastomotic reconstruction, see [Excision and Primary Anastomosis](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/excision-primary-anastomosis). Core-through urethrotomy is not open urethroplasty and is not a standard repair for obliterative PFUI. For the broader VUAS / BNC framework, see [Bladder Neck Reconstruction & VUAS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction). For the fully transurethral BMG alternative, see [Endoscopic Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/endoscopic-urethroplasty).

---

## Indications

For nonobliterative posterior stenoses, RPU may follow failed endoscopic treatment; for complete obliteration of VUAS, bulbomembranous stenosis, or bladder-neck stenosis, the EAU 2026 guideline strongly recommends against endoluminal treatment.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> In Bearrick's series, median prior endoscopic treatments were 2–3 by etiology.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Before redo vesicourethral anastomosis, assess bladder function and periurethral necrosis, calcification, or fistulation. The EAU 2026 guideline limits redo VUA to patients with adequate bladder function and no such adverse tissue findings; radiation-related bladder toxicity may make reconstruction futile.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> The following etiology distribution is from Bearrick’s 21-patient series, not Zhang’s 105-patient cohort:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- **Post-prostatectomy VUAS without radiation.** Ten of 21 men (48%).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Bladder-neck contracture after endoscopic prostate procedures** (BPH treatment): approximately 24%.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Post-prostatectomy VUAS with radiation.** Five of 21 men (24%).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Trauma.** One of 21 patients (5%).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Zhang’s larger cohort instead reported prior radiation in 57.1%.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Surgical Approaches

The technique is tailored to location and complexity of the stenosis.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> For a preoperatively continent patient undergoing redo VUA, the EAU 2026 guideline weakly recommends retropubic access and strongly recommends counseling about expected incontinence after transperineal redo.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

| Approach | Use |
|---|---|
| **Transabdominal (intra- or extraperitoneal)** | Standard robotic dock for deep pelvic dissection; transvesical access; cystoscopy defines stricture extent |
| Combined robotic transabdominal + open transperineal | A combined abdominoperineal approach was required in 41/105 (39%) of Zhang's patients<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>; Cavallo's 12-patient series describes robotic transabdominal plus open transperineal reconstruction for complex anastomotic disease<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Robotic-assisted perineal (Buckley) | Robot used for proximal suture placement after standard perineal dissection in ten posterior or proximal bulbar repairs; five of ten strictures were bulbar. Mean robotic setup was 15 min, with 30–45 min for suture placement.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Single-port robotic** | Supraumbilical access for intraabdominal or extraperitoneal transvesical reconstruction; described with BMG augmentation by Liu / Shakir / Zhao |

### Reconstructive techniques employed (Zhang 2023 distribution)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

| Technique | Share |
|---|---|
| Excision and primary anastomosis | For redo VUA, excise the stenosis and mobilize the bladder, bulbar urethra, or both as needed for a tension-free anastomosis.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> The primary abstract gives an inconsistent count/percentage, so no numerical share is assigned here.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| Resitting of the bladder neck | 24.8% |
| Y-V plasty | 20% in Zhang's cohort.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Boswell et al. describe Y-V advancement for nonobliterative stenosis with a viable urethral plate; a scarred epithelialized gap may instead need excision and anastomosis.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Buccal mucosa graft (BMG) urethroplasty | 13.3% |

### Ancillary procedures

Ancillary procedures include gracilis muscle flap interposition (33.3% in Cavallo's series), rectus abdominis flap, omental flap, and concurrent prostatectomy (66.7% in Cavallo's combined-approach series).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Potential Advantages and Comparative Evidence

Technical visualization benefits and small retrospective comparisons cannot establish superiority in patency or continence.

- **Visualization.** Magnified 3D optics improve the view in the deep pelvis, where open visualization is limited.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Ergonomics.** The robot helps with narrow pelvic anatomy and a long distance from perineal skin to the proximal urethra.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Blood loss.** Median blood loss was 100 mL versus 200 mL with open perineal repair (p = 0.001) in Savun's series.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Hospital stay.** 3 versus 4 days in Savun’s retrospective robotic versus open comparison (p = 0.001); Bearrick’s separate robotic-only groups had 1–2-day stays.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Continence.** Savun reported de novo incontinence in 16.6% after robotic versus 100% after open surgery (p = 0.031), but the small retrospective comparison and baseline continence differences limit inference, and a protective effect of the robot is not established.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> A 2024 review of ten robotic case series reported 80% patency (95/119) across differing success definitions; some series counted patency after endoscopic reintervention. De novo incontinence was 17% (8/47).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> A 2026 etiology-stratified synthesis of 33 reconstruction studies (667 patients) estimated robotic patency at 92% for bladder-neck stenosis (n = 95) and 75% for VUAS (n = 96); heterogeneous endpoints and follow-up limit comparison between approaches.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Adjunctive procedures.** Concurrent prostatectomy, flap harvest and tissue transfer can be done in the same operation.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Outcomes

| Series | n | Approach | Success | AUS rate | Complications (endpoint varies) | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zhang 2023<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | 105 | Robotic (39% combined) | 75.2% (no reintervention) | 28.6% | 6.7% (Clavien-Dindo ≥3 within 30 days) | Mean 18.7 mo |
| Bearrick 2022<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | 21 (20 in the reported BPH, prostatectomy, and radiation outcome groups) | Robotic | 80–100% anatomic success (by etiology) | 0–80% (by etiology) | 0–40% 30-day complications by etiology | Not reported in abstract |
| Cavallo 2021<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | 12 | Combined robotic + perineal | 83.3% (stenosis recurrence 16.7%) | 75% | Wound abscess 16.7%, leak 8.3%, VTE 8.3% | Mean 596 days |
| Liu 2022<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | 9 | Single-port robotic + BMG | — | — | No intraoperative events; 30-day UTI, epididymitis, anemia, recurrent stricture, and bowel obstruction requiring surgery (each n = 1) | Median 11.7 mo |
| Savun 2025<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | 10 (robotic) vs 18 (open) | Robotic vs open perineal (VUAS) | 80% vs 77.8% composite patency (16 Fr cystoscope passage or Qmax &gt;15 mL/s) | — | Comparable | Not reported in abstract |

The AUA 2023 amendment cites robotic-assisted reconstruction patency rates of 72.7–75%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Impact of Etiology — the Radiation Signal

Outcomes differed by etiology in Bearrick's 21-man series. Four of five men in the radiation group required reintervention, although four ultimately achieved anatomic success; the latter is not single-operation success. Reintervention and functional success differed by etiology (P = 0.004 and P = 0.035, respectively).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

| Etiology | Anatomic success | Functional success | Reintervention | AUS placement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Post-BPH treatment | 100% | 100% | 0% | 0% |
| Post-prostatectomy without radiation | 90% | 100% | 10% | 30% |
| Post-prostatectomy with radiation | 80% | 60% | 80% | 80% (p = 0.013) |

Prior radiation and preoperative incontinence were associated with worse outcomes in these selected cohorts. They are counseling factors and not a validated prediction model.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Complications

- **30-day Clavien-Dindo ≥3 complications:** 6.7% in Zhang 2023.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Wound abscess, leak, and thromboembolism.** Cavallo reported wound abscess in 16.7%, urinary leak in 8.3%, and thromboembolism in 8.3%.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> In a separate 12-man robotic bladder-neck reconstruction series, one patient developed Clavien IIIb osteitis pubis with pubovesical fistula requiring repair and pubic debridement.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Stress urinary incontinence** is the main functional concern. AUS placement followed surgery in 28.6% of Zhang's patients and 75% of Cavallo's (whose de novo SUI rate was 33.3%), and in 0–80% by etiology in Bearrick's.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> After redo VUA, confirm stable patency before AUS placement; EAU reports that most advise waiting at least three to six months because recurrent stenosis is difficult to treat with a cuff in place.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Erectile dysfunction.** De novo erectile dysfunction occurred in 16.7% in Cavallo's series.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Operative time.** Median operative time was 150 minutes in Savun's robotic group and 377 minutes in Liu's single-port BMG series, in which 5 of 9 patients had ancillary flap harvest.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

---

## Current Evidence Limitations

- The evidence base is retrospective single-center or small multi-institutional series, and no randomized trials compare robotic with open reconstruction.
- Follow-up is short to intermediate: mean 18.7 months in Zhang’s 105-man series and median 11.7 months in Liu’s nine-patient series.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- For recalcitrant bladder-neck or post-prostatectomy vesicourethral anastomotic stenosis, the AUA 2023 recommendation permits robotic or open reconstruction (Conditional Recommendation; Grade C).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Patient selection is heterogeneous, and experience is concentrated at a few high-volume centers.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
