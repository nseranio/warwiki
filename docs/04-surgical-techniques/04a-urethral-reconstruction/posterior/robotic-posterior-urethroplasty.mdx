# Robotic Posterior Urethroplasty

**Robotic posterior urethroplasty** is an evolving alternative to the traditional perineal or transpubic abdominoperineal approach for **vesicourethral anastomotic stenosis (VUAS)** after radical prostatectomy and for **post-radiation bladder neck contracture**. The robotic transabdominal approach offers articulating-instrument access to the deep pelvis and is increasingly combined with an open transperineal stage for extreme defects.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For the canonical PFUI workflow, see [Pelvic Fracture Urethral Injury (PFUI)](/docs/special-populations/05a-trauma-emergencies/pfui). For the open posterior gold-standard repair, see [Excision and Primary Anastomosis](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/excision-primary-anastomosis) and [Core-Through Urethrotomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/core-through-urethrotomy).

---

## Concept

The robotic transabdominal route allows direct visualization of the bladder neck and proximal urethra through an extraperitoneal or transvesical approach. Compared with the open perineal approach, it avoids inferior pubectomy and crural splitting in select cases, and allows precise mucosa-to-mucosa anastomosis under magnified visualization. Most published cohorts are dominated by **post-radiation VUAS**, not classical PFUI.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Indications

- Post-prostatectomy VUAS, especially after radiation
- Salvage / recurrent bladder-neck contracture
- Posterior strictures unsuitable for or failed by the traditional perineal approach
- Surgeon comfort with robotic pelvic dissection

---

## Outcomes

| Series | n | Approach | Success | AUS rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zhang / Zhao 2023 | 105 | Robotic | ~90% | 28.6% (post-radiation subgroup) |
| Cavallo 2021 | smaller | Robotic | Promising | — |

The technique remains **early-adoption**; functional comparison with open perineal and transpubic approaches is limited, and the high concomitant artificial urinary sphincter (AUS) rate in post-radiation VUAS reflects the underlying disease, not the operation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
