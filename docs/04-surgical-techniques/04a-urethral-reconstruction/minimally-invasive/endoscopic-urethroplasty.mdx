# Endoscopic Urethroplasty

**Endoscopic urethroplasty** describes endoluminal buccal mucosa graft (BMG) placement for selected posterior stenoses: Warner's method uses an RD 180 suturing device, whereas Doležel's uses a catheter-mounted tubular graft.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Small cohorts describe endoscopic graft augmentation without an external incision but lack durability comparisons with established reconstruction. Two named approaches are detailed here: the **Warner technique**, first reported in one membranous-stricture case in 2023, and the **Doležel endourethroplasty**, reported in an 11-patient subgroup with PUS or VUAS in 2024.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For the broader VUAS / posterior-urethral-stenosis decision framework, see [Bladder Neck Reconstruction & VUAS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction). For the open perineal benchmark, see [Dorsal Onlay BMG Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/dorsal-onlay-omg). For the standalone graft material, see [Buccal Mucosa Graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa). For the related transmeatal anterior-urethral application, see [Transmeatal OMG Ventral Inlay](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/transmeatal-omg-ventral-inlay).

## Why Endoscopic Posterior BMG

Endoscopic treatment outcomes for posterior stenosis vary substantially with etiology, luminal patency, radiation exposure and prior treatment; no single recurrence percentage applies to all posterior stenoses. Established reconstruction may use excision and anastomosis, a flap, or graft augmentation, with different morbidity and continence risks.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Endoscopic BMG urethroplasty was developed to deliver the augmentation principle through the lumen, sparing the perineum.

## Warner Technique — Membranous Urethral Stricture (Ungerer 2023)

**Index case.** Male with prostate-cancer-related radiation therapy and an 8 mm flow-limiting membranous urethral stricture, a population where open repair carries higher recurrence and morbidity.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Step-by-step.**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1. Stricture dilation | Membranous stricture dilated to allow resectoscope passage; defines proximal / distal extent |
| 2. Graft-bed preparation | In the reported case, a resectoscope removed a 1 cm-wide strip of superficial mucosa from the bladder neck across the strictured segment, creating a vascularized recipient bed for graft imbibition and inosculation. Keep this resection superficial; for DVIU in VUAS or radiation-induced bulbomembranous stenosis, EAU strongly advises against deep six- and 12-o’clock incisions because of rectal injury and urosymphyseal fistula risk.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| 3. BMG harvest | Standard inner-cheek BMG; defatted and trimmed on the back table |
| 4. Pulley delivery | With graft outside the urethra, the **RD 180 endoscopic suturing device** is passed transurethrally; a suture is placed through the proximal graft, then through the bladder neck, then back through the graft. Pulling the suture **advances the graft into the correct intraluminal position** |
| 5. Anchoring | Graft anchored against the prepared bed with SecureStrap fixation, separate from the RD 180 suturing device<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| 6. Catheter | Catheter holds the graft flat against the bed during healing; removed at **4 weeks** |

**Outcome (n = 1).**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Metric | Result |
|---|---|
| Operative time | 2.5 hours |
| EBL | 50 mL |
| Discharge | Same day |
| Catheter | Removed POD 28 |
| Cystoscopy at 10 wk | Good graft viability |
| Qmax | 4 → 20 mL/s |
| 6-month follow-up | No recurrence; no complications |

**Potential advantages.** Entirely transurethral delivery and same-day discharge were feasible in this case. Comparative efficacy and safety, including in irradiated tissue, remain unestablished.

**Limitations.** Single-case-report evidence; requires the RD 180 suturing device and endoscopic-suturing skill; only 6-month follow-up; reproducibility across centers not yet demonstrated; graft fixation relies on suture anchoring and catheter compression alone (no quilting sutures as in the Nikolavsky ventral inlay).

**Instrumentation.** The RD 180 endoscopic suturing device is the enabling technology; its pulley mechanism allows graft delivery into the confined posterior-urethral space without an external incision. The same device is also used in TUITMR mucosal-realignment techniques.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Doležel Endourethroplasty — PUS / VUAS (2024)

**Series.** Single Czech institution, 1999–2022, 38 consecutive patients with posterior urethral stenosis (PUS) or vesicourethral-anastomotic stenosis (VUAS). Twenty-six of 38 (68%) had prior failed procedures (25 endoscopic, one open); 12 of 38 (32%) underwent primary reconstruction.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Twenty-seven patients (71%) had a perineal approach; 11 patients (29%) had endourethroplasty (EUP). Etiologies included post-radical-prostatectomy VUAS and post-TURP or post-radiation membranous stenosis; only 2 of 11 EUP patients had received pelvic radiotherapy, limiting inference for irradiated stenosis.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

**Technique.** The EUP subgroup received a tubularized BMG mounted on a catheter and delivered retrograde to the incised stenosis; onlay grafting was used in the perineal approach:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

1. Endoscopic assessment of stenosis length, location, and degree of obliteration.
2. The reported EUP used a 24 Fr cold-knife urethrotome; tight stenoses were first dilated to 10–12 Fr. For VUAS, the authors made a deep ventral six-o'clock incision through adjacent muscle into prerectal tissue. EAU 2026 strongly recommends against deep six-o'clock DVIU for VUAS or radiation-induced bulbomembranous stenosis because of rectal injury risk.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
3. Standard inner-cheek BMG harvest.
4. A tubularized BMG was fixed to a 20 Fr Tiemann catheter with absorbable 4-0 sutures, then inserted retrograde through the urethra to the incised stenosis; the perineal approach used an onlay graft.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
5. After balloon seating at the bladder neck, a transcutaneous mattress stitch secured the urethral catheter at the penoscrotal angle. The urethral and safety suprapubic catheters remained for 3–4 weeks.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

**Auxiliary cold-knife DVIU.** In the Doležel protocol, a short PUS or VUAS recurrence within the first 12 months is transected by a single cold-knife DVIU. The report presents both recurrence-free survival and survival allowing this additional treatment. An auxiliary DVIU remains a reintervention and must be counted when comparing treatment burden. One patient died on postoperative day two from sepsis-associated multiorgan failure after auxiliary DVIU for complete VUAS reobliteration three months after perineal repair; the authors noted prior salvage radiotherapy and possible urinary extravasation as risk factors.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> EAU 2026 gives a strong recommendation against endoluminal treatment of completely obliterated VUAS.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

**Outcomes.**<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

| Cohort | 3-yr stricture-recurrence-free survival |
|---|---|
| Endourethroplasty (n = 11) | **73%** |
| Perineal approach (n = 27) | 63% |
| Whole cohort | 65% |
| Whole cohort **with permitted auxiliary DVIU** | **81%** |

De novo incontinence occurred in 2 of 18 preoperatively continent patients across both groups. Both events followed perineal repair, with none reported after EUP in this small series; separate robotic series provide no direct continence comparison.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

**What the series adds.** It reports a three-year recurrence endpoint and documents technical feasibility. The small, nonrandomized groups cannot establish superiority over the perineal approach.

**Limitations.** Small, heterogeneous cohort (n = 11 EUP); single-center retrospective design; the published endourethroplasty technique fixes a tubularized graft to a 20 Fr Tiemann catheter with absorbable sutures before retrograde insertion; the auxiliary-DVIU protocol complicates comparison with techniques that count any reintervention as failure.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Published Technique Comparison

| Feature | Warner (Ungerer 2023) | Doležel Endourethroplasty (2024) |
|---|---|---|
| Target anatomy | Membranous urethral stricture | PUS / VUAS (membranous + bladder neck) |
| Graft configuration | Onlay (on resected mucosal bed) | Tubularized BMG mounted on a 20 Fr catheter<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Graft-bed preparation | Resectoscope mucosal-strip resection (1 cm wide) | Cold-knife incision; for VUAS, Doležel reports a deep ventral six-o'clock cut, while EAU 2026 strongly recommends against that incision in VUAS<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Graft delivery | RD 180 suturing device + pulley technique | Endourological (device not specified) |
| Graft fixation | SecureStrap anchors and catheter compression<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | Absorbable 4-0 sutures attach the graft to the catheter; a transcutaneous mattress stitch secures the catheter<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Catheter duration | 4 weeks | Urethral and suprapubic catheters for 3–4 weeks<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Sample size | 1 (case report) | 11 (institutional series) |
| Follow-up | 6 months | Three-year SRFS endpoint; median follow-up was 89 months for the full n = 38 cohort, with no EUP-specific median reported<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Reported outcome | No recurrence reported at 6 months (1 patient)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | EUP three-year SRFS was 73% before and 77% with auxiliary DVIU; 81% with auxiliary DVIU applies to all 38 patients<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| De novo incontinence | Not separately reported in the single-patient case<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 2 / 18 preoperatively continent patients in the combined cohort; both events followed perineal repair<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Auxiliary DVIU | None used | Permitted within 12 months as protocol |
| Primary etiology | Post-radiation | Mixed (post-prostatectomy, post-TURP, post-radiation) |
| Technical-detail published | High (step-by-step + device specifics) | Moderate |

## Open Perineal D-BMGU as Benchmark

For context, these observational series describe open perineal dorsal onlay BMG urethroplasty (D-BMGU) in selected posterior stenosis populations:

- **Angulo 2021** (multi-institutional, n = 107, membranous / bulbomembranous after endoscopic prostate procedures): mean follow-up 59.3 months; 9.35% recurrence; 0.9% de novo SUI. Independent recurrence predictors: postoperative complications (OR 12.5), prior radiation (OR 8.3), and ≥ 2 prior dilations (OR 7.2).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Sterling 2024** (multi-institutional, n = 45, after both prostatectomy and radiation, with VUAS or bulbomembranous stenosis): 7 recurrences (median follow-up 21 months in men without recurrence); no de novo SUI reported (28 men were incontinent before and after repair); 23/27 (85%) reported GRA ≥ +2.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

These are different populations and treatment settings, not a direct comparison. Their results do not establish equivalent success, lower morbidity, or an approach-independent radiation effect.

## Where It Fits

Endoscopic posterior BMG urethroplasty remains an emerging technique with limited clinical evidence. Published applications include:

- Post-radiation membranous stricture in patients unfit for or declining open / robotic perineal reconstruction.
- Post-prostatectomy VUAS where the bladder-neck stenosis is short and amenable to endoluminal graft placement.
- Centers with expertise and equipment for the selected graft-delivery and fixation method; RD 180 is specific to the Warner method.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Selection requires reconstructive assessment of stenosis location, obliteration, tissue quality, radiation injury, bladder function and continence goals. In a single-surgeon retrospective series of 28 men with membranous stricture, 23/28 (82%) passed a 17 Fr cystoscope at four months; median follow-up was eight months. Sixteen later received an artificial urinary sphincter, and three required cystectomy for radiation-related bladder disease, including one delayed urosymphyseal fistula. Four of 28 patients (14%) lost part or all of the graft, and two experienced clot retention requiring operative evacuation.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> The authors favor diversion when severe radionecrosis, calcification, fistula or poor bladder capacity compromises repair; patent four-month cystoscopy in five obliterative cases does not establish durable success or override the EAU guidance below.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Do not treat complete posterior obliteration endoluminally: EAU 2026 gives a strong recommendation against endoluminal treatment of vesicourethral anastomotic stenosis, bulbomembranous stenosis or bladder neck stenosis with complete obliteration (§6.3.5.a) and against endoscopic treatment of an obliterative post-traumatic stenosis (§6.3.5.b).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> The tubular graft configurations reported in this series do not establish routine use; AUA advises against single-stage tubularized graft urethroplasty (Expert Opinion, for anterior urethral reconstruction).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> See [BNC / VUAS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction) for the broader treatment pathway.
