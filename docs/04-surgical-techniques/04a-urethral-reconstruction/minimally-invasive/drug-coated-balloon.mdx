# Drug-Coated Balloon Therapy (Optilume)

The **Optilume® paclitaxel-coated balloon** is the drug-coated-balloon (DCB) device with randomized evidence in recurrent bulbar urethral stricture (ROBUST III). Ureteral use of paclitaxel-coated balloons is off-label and investigational, and the published ureteral series used a different paclitaxel-coated balloon (Lutonix).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The canonical WARWIKI page lives in the cross-cutting Treatment Atlas: [Drug-Coated Balloon Therapy](/docs/surgical-techniques/drug-coated-balloon). This urethral-reconstruction page is a pointer with a short selection summary.

For related urethral options, see [DVIU and Urethral Dilation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/dviu) and [Endoscopic Urethroplasty (Suturing Device)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/endoscopic-urethroplasty). For the ureteral endoscopic counterpart, see [Endoureterotomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/endoureterotomy).

## Urethral Selection Snapshot

| Best supported | Less certain / off-label |
|---|---|
| Recurrent bulbar stricture less than 3 cm after prior dilation or DVIU. AUA: surgeons may offer DCB with dilation or DVIU (Conditional Recommendation, Grade B). EAU: offer DCB after at least two prior endoscopic treatments in patients not fit or unwilling for urethroplasty (strong).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | Penile urethral strictures |
| Patient wants an endoscopic bridge before urethroplasty | Posterior urethral stenosis, BNC, or VUAS |
| Patient understands that durability has not been compared directly with urethroplasty | Treatment-naive strictures outside trials |

## Technique Snapshot

The operative concept is mechanical dilation plus local paclitaxel delivery. The working sequence is as follows. Confirm stricture length and location endoscopically and obtain wire access. Standard dilation or DVIU may precede coated-balloon placement; the urethral device instructions do not require a separate pre-dilation step.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Center the drug-coated segment across the scar and inflate it per the device instructions. Drain briefly with a catheter. Follow with symptom score, uroflow and PVR, and cystoscopy when recurrence is suspected. ROBUST III enrolled anterior strictures of 3 cm or less (about 90% bulbar) with at least two prior dilations; six-month anatomical patency (passage of a 16 Fr flexible cystoscope or 14 Fr catheter) was 50/67 (74.6%) with DCB versus 11/41 (26.8%) with standard dilation or DVIU in the complete-case analysis.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> At 2 years, freedom from repeat intervention in the DCB arm remained higher than in the control arm's last available comparison at 1 year, before crossover (77.8% vs 23.6%, p &lt; 0.001); the control arm itself has no 2-year data because most control patients crossed over to open-label DCB after 6 months.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> ROBUST I, a separate and more heavily pretreated single-arm cohort of 53 men with recurrent bulbar strictures ≤2 cm and 1 to 4 prior endoscopic interventions, reported functional success (at least 50% IPSS reduction without retreatment) in 29/43 (67%) and freedom from retreatment in 33/43 (77%) at 3 years. At 5 years, 31 men had completed all follow-up; functional success was 25/43 (58%), and Kaplan-Meier freedom from repeat intervention was 71.7%.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> The label calls for at least 1 minute of coating hydration before inflation, at least 5 minutes of inflation at the rated burst pressure, and a 12 to 14 Fr Foley catheter for at least 2 days.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Counseling Snapshot

Drug-coated balloon therapy is an endoscopic option for selected recurrent bulbar strictures with better patency than standard dilation or DVIU in ROBUST III; it does not replace substitution urethroplasty. Paclitaxel was detected in semen, and AUA advises contraception for 6 months after treatment when the partner has child-bearing potential, and the label adds 30 days of abstinence or condom use. The label contraindicates paclitaxel hypersensitivity and urologic implants (penile prosthesis or artificial urinary sphincter), and requires resolution of an active urinary tract infection before treatment. AUA does not recommend repeat DCB use; its efficacy has not been ascertained.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> A DCB failure should trigger reassessment of stricture length, spongiofibrosis, radiation history, lichen sclerosus, and candidacy for definitive reconstruction rather than automatic serial repeat dilation.
