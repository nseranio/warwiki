# Drug-Coated Balloon Therapy (Optilume)

The **Optilume® paclitaxel-coated balloon** is the drug-coated-balloon (DCB) device with randomized evidence in recurrent bulbar urethral stricture (ROBUST III). Ureteral paclitaxel-coated-balloon use remains off-label and investigational: a 25-patient series used Lutonix, and a six-patient series used Optilume with median six-month follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

The canonical WARWIKI page lives in the cross-cutting Treatment Atlas: [Drug-Coated Balloon Therapy](/docs/surgical-techniques/drug-coated-balloon). This urethral-reconstruction page is a pointer with a short selection summary.

For related urethral options, see [DVIU and Urethral Dilation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/dviu) and [Endoscopic Urethroplasty (Suturing Device)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/endoscopic-urethroplasty). For the ureteral endoscopic counterpart, see [Endoureterotomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/endoureterotomy).

## Urethral Selection Snapshot

| Best supported | Less certain / off-label |
|---|---|
| Recurrent bulbar stricture less than 3 cm after prior dilation or DVIU. AUA: surgeons may offer DCB with dilation or DVIU (Conditional Recommendation, Grade B). EAU: offer DCB after at least two prior endoscopic treatments in patients not fit or unwilling for urethroplasty (strong).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | Penile strictures of 3 cm or less are within the US anterior-urethral indication, but outside the AUA's bulbar-only recommendation; the ROBUST III penile subgroup was too small for a separate efficacy assessment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| Patient wants an endoscopic bridge before urethroplasty | Posterior urethral stenosis, BNC, or VUAS |
| Patient understands that durability has not been compared directly with urethroplasty | Treatment-naive strictures and patients with lichen sclerosus, prior anterior urethroplasty, or prior hypospadias repair (outside ROBUST III).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |

## Technique Snapshot

The operative concept is mechanical dilation plus local paclitaxel delivery. The working sequence is as follows. Confirm stricture length and location endoscopically and obtain wire access. All 79 ROBUST III DCB recipients underwent pre-dilation with an uncoated balloon or DVIU to at least 20 Fr before coated-balloon placement; the US device instructions do not require a separate pre-dilation step.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Handle the catheter with dry sterile gloves or dry gauze, minimizing contact with the coating; do not wet the balloon before insertion. Center the coated segment across the full stricture, leave it uninflated for at least one minute to hydrate, and then inflate according to the device instructions.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Drain briefly with a catheter. Follow with symptom score, uroflow and PVR, and cystoscopy when recurrence is suspected. ROBUST III enrolled anterior strictures of 3 cm or less (about 90% bulbar) after at least two prior endoscopic treatments, including dilation or DVIU; six-month anatomical patency (passage of a 16 Fr flexible cystoscope or 14 Fr catheter) was 50/67 (74.6%) with DCB versus 11/41 (26.8%) with standard dilation or DVIU in the complete-case analysis.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> At two years, Kaplan–Meier freedom from repeat intervention was 77.8% in the DCB arm versus 23.6% at the control arm's one-year cutoff (p &lt; 0.001); most controls crossed over after six months, so these are not concurrent estimates.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> At three years, freedom from repeat intervention was 71.9% in the original DCB arm, without a concurrent control group.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> ROBUST I, a separate single-arm cohort of 53 men with recurrent bulbar strictures ≤2 cm and 1 to 4 prior endoscopic interventions, had 43 evaluable at 3 years (33 visit completers plus 10 earlier clinical failures); functional success (at least 50% IPSS reduction without retreatment) was 29/43 (67%), and freedom from retreatment was 33/43 (77%).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> At 5 years, 31 men had completed all follow-up; functional success was 25/43 (58%), and Kaplan-Meier freedom from repeat intervention was 71.7%.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> The label calls for at least 1 minute of coating hydration before inflation, at least 5 minutes of inflation at the rated burst pressure, and a 12 to 14 Fr Foley catheter for at least 2 days.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Counseling Snapshot

Drug-coated balloon therapy is an endoscopic option for selected recurrent bulbar strictures with better patency than standard dilation or DVIU in ROBUST III; it does not replace substitution urethroplasty. Paclitaxel was detected in semen. The US label calls for abstinence or condom use for 30 days to limit partner exposure; men with partners of child-bearing potential should use highly effective contraception and avoid fathering children for at least six months.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The label contraindicates paclitaxel hypersensitivity and urologic implants (penile prosthesis or artificial urinary sphincter), and requires resolution of an active urinary tract infection before treatment. AUA does not recommend repeat DCB use; its efficacy has not been ascertained.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> A DCB failure should trigger reassessment of stricture length, spongiofibrosis, radiation history, lichen sclerosus, and candidacy for definitive reconstruction rather than automatic serial repeat dilation.
