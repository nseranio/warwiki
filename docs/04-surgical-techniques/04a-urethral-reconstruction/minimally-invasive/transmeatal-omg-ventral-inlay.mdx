# Transmeatal OMG Ventral Inlay (Nikolavsky)

The **transmeatal oral mucosal graft (OMG) ventral inlay urethroplasty**, developed by [Dmitriy Nikolavsky](/docs/roots/surgeons/h-r/dmitriy-nikolavsky) and colleagues, reconstructs selected distal strictures through the meatus without splitting the glans or a separate penile or perineal incision. In Sterling’s series, 42/44 were patent without additional intervention at mean 36 months<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>; in Daneshvar’s 68-man multicenter cohort, 54/57 men with at least 12 months of follow-up remained stricture-free at median 17 months.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> These uncontrolled, potentially overlapping series establish feasibility rather than superiority or absence of uncommon complications.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For graft material, see [Buccal Mucosa Graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa). For the dorsal-inlay variant in fossa navicularis, see [Sliding-T Dorsal Inlay](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/sliding-t-dorsal-inlay). For the broader Asopa principle, see [Asopa Dorsal Inlay OMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/asopa-dorsal-inlay-omg). For minced-BMG / endoscopic delivery concepts, see [Liquid / Minced Buccal Mucosal Graft](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/liquid-minced-buccal).

## Concept and Rationale

Some open reconstructions for fossa navicularis and distal strictures use glans splitting, circumferential urethral mobilization, or perineal or penile incisions. The Nikolavsky technique performs the entire reconstruction transurethrally through the meatus, using the natural access point of the distal urethra to avoid those incisions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The ventral wall of the fossa navicularis and distal urethra is accessible through the meatus, and a ventral urethrotomy can be performed under direct vision to excise scar tissue and create a graft bed, all without external incisions. The BMG is then delivered through the meatus using a suture-based "pull-through" technique and quilted in place.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Nikolavsky and colleagues first reported the technique in 2016: transurethral ventral wedge resection of the stenosed segment, then transurethral delivery and spread fixation of a buccal mucosal graft inlay into the resulting urethrotomy. The initial series included three men with fossa navicularis or distal strictures (mean length 2.1 cm, range 1–4) and at least two previous failed procedures; at a mean 18 months (range 12–24) there were no recurrences or fistulas, mean preoperative and postoperative uroflow values were 4.3 and 19, and no chordee or change in sexual function was noted.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Step-by-Step Technique

The procedure is accomplished in four steps as described by Sterling, Daneshvar, and Nikolavsky (2023).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Exposure and scar excision

- Patient supine; meatus retracted with stay sutures or a small self-retaining retractor.
- Under direct vision through the meatus, place a guidewire into the bladder and inject methylene blue into the urethra to distinguish healthy mucosa. Make lateral meatal incisions at 3 and 9 o’clock for atraumatic retraction, excise the cicatrice via a transurethral ventral urethrotomy, and inspect the proximal urethra endoscopically.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- The ventral urethrotomy is carried through the strictured segment to a patent proximal lumen, creating a ventral trough that exposes the underlying corpus spongiosum.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

When no suprapubic tract is available to help gauge the proximal extent of a longer stricture, an alternative described in the surgical atlas literature passes a guidewire retrogradely through the meatus, advances a lubricated 5-6 Fr Fogarty catheter to patent bulbar urethra, inflates its balloon to 20-26 Fr, and withdraws it until resistance marks the proximal edge of the stenosis for external marking.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

### Graft delivery (the "pull-through")

- A buccal mucosal graft is harvested from the inner cheek using standard technique and defatted.
- The graft is trimmed to a triangular shape, wider at the meatal end and tapering proximally to match the geometry of the ventral urethrotomy defect. One described sizing approach cuts the graft about 1-1.5 cm longer than the measured defect and 2.5-3 cm at its widest point.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- A double-armed 6-0 polydioxanone (PDS) suture is placed through the proximal (narrow) end of the triangular BMG.
- The suture needles are passed transurethrally to the proximal extent of the urethrotomy and brought out through the ventral penile skin externally.
- By pulling on the suture externally, the graft is delivered through the meatus and advanced to the proximal extent of the urethrotomy (the "pull-through" maneuver).
- The suture is secured externally over a bolster on the ventral penile skin, anchoring the proximal graft in position.

### Graft fixation and quilting

- The distal (wide) end of the graft is secured to the meatal edges with 5-0 polyglactin (Vicryl).
- Additional 6-0 double-armed PDS quilting sutures are placed through the graft and underlying tissue for spread fixation, serving two functions: prevent graft contracture by maintaining the graft in a spread, flat configuration, and eliminate dead space between the graft and underlying bed (promoting imbibition and inosculation).
- The graft sits on the ventral corpus spongiosum, the vascularized graft bed.

### Catheter placement and closure

- A 16 Fr silicone urethral catheter is placed through the reconstructed urethra, serving as a stent to maintain patency and compress the graft against its bed during healing; one described approach inflates its balloon to about 10 cc and secures the catheter to avoid excessive ventral pressure on the graft.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **No external incisions are closed.** The entire procedure is performed through the meatus.
- The originating center planned same-day discharge; 40/44 patients went home on the day of surgery and four stayed overnight.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Postoperative protocol

- Same-day discharge.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Catheter removal and void trial at 1 week** in the originating center’s protocol; individualize timing according to reconstruction and healing. Before removal after urethroplasty, assess for extravasation with validated urethrography (EAU Strong); maintain drainage and repeat assessment after one week if a significant leak persists.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- Follow-up with uroflowmetry, PVR, IPSS, SHIM.
- In Sterling's protocol, cystoscopy was offered for Qmax &lt;13 mL/s, worsening IPSS, or subjective complaints; routine visits used examination, uroflowmetry, PVR, and questionnaires.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Multi-institutional validation (Daneshvar 2020)

Daneshvar’s retrospective 12-institution cohort supports technical reproducibility. Outcomes were available for 57/68 men at at least 12 months, which limits generalization to all treated patients.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- **Qmax** improved from 5 to 18 mL/s (p &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Three of the 57 evaluable patients did not remain stricture-free. The separate Sterling series reported two recurrences with more proximal disease; these should not be attributed to the multicenter cohort.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- In Sterling’s series, both recurrences were treated successfully with dorsal onlay BMG urethroplasty.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Significant improvements in Qmax, PVR, IPSS, and QOL scores.
- No difference in postoperative sexual function scores (SHIM unchanged, p = 0.85).

### TraMUS — extension to penile urethral strictures (Tolbert 2025)

In 2025, Tolbert et al. at the Mayo Clinic described an extension of the Nikolavsky technique to penile urethral strictures, termed TraMUS (Transmeatal Urethral Surgery).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- Same transmeatal ventral inlay principle, with longer grafts (avg 6.5 cm, range 3–15 cm).
- The cohort included 2 men with lichen sclerosus and 3 with hypospadias-related strictures; it did not compare transmeatal repair with staged reconstruction.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Success was 91% at 1 year (10/11 evaluable); the single failure occurred at 3 months.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- One patient developed de novo incontinence (reported as 9%; 11 of 13 treated men had one-year outcome data); the small series does not establish its mechanism.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- No other procedure-related complications.
- Further studies needed to confirm long-term efficacy and compare to traditional open penile urethroplasty.

## Advantages

1. **No external incision.** Entire procedure performed through the meatus; avoids a separate external access wound; graft, oral-donor and catheter complications remain possible.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
2. **Glans-sparing.** Avoids glans splitting, with the aim of limiting disruption; preserved sensation and vascularity are not guaranteed.
3. **No circumferential urethral mobilization.** Limits urethral mobilization; comparative ischemic benefit has not been established.
4. **Same-day discharge**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
5. **One-week catheter protocol.** Patients in the originating-center series returned for a void trial after one week.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
6. **Sexual-function assessment.** In Daneshvar's cohort, median SHIM was 22 before and 21 after repair (p = 0.85).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In Sterling's 44-patient series, four men had a postoperative SHIM decrease greater than three points.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
7. **Multicenter experience.** The retrospective cohort included 68 men at 12 institutions; 54/57 men with at least 12 months of follow-up remained stricture-free at a median of 17 months.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
8. **Complications.** Daneshvar reported no fistula, glanular dehiscence, graft necrosis or chordee; this series does not establish zero risk.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In Sterling’s 44-patient series, no graft loss, fistula or glans dehiscence was reported, but seven men reported de novo urinary stream spraying; among 16 with preoperative splitting or spraying, six reported resolution and ten no change.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
9. **Short operative time.** Median 72 min in the multi-institutional series.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Graft Biology — Why Ventral Inlay Works

- **Graft bed.** The graft is placed as an inlay into the defect created by the ventral urethrotomy and sits on the ventral corpus spongiosum, which is vascularized for imbibition and neovascularization.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Triangular graft shape.** The triangle is intended to match the geometry of the fossa navicularis and to avoid redundant tissue that could cause sacculation or diverticulum formation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Quilting sutures.** Spread fixation is intended to limit graft contracture and to eliminate dead space, where hematoma or seroma could lead to graft failure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Sacculation.** Stable graft support is a technical goal, but small uncontrolled series cannot establish absence of risk or superiority over bulbar ventral onlay reconstruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Patient Selection

### Ideal candidates (original indication)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- **Fossa navicularis or distal penile strictures beginning at the meatus.** Sterling reported a mean length of 2 cm (range 1–5 cm) in the 44-patient series; that observed range is not a 1–3 cm eligibility rule.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Recurrent meatal / distal strictures** that have failed endoscopic management
- **Lichen sclerosus-related distal strictures.** LS accounted for 34% of the multicenter cohort.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In Sterling’s series, 9/44 had LS etiology and both recurrences had biopsy-confirmed LS; these data do not provide a reliable LS-specific recurrence estimate.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Patients desiring minimally invasive, same-day surgery with rapid recovery

### Expanded indications (TraMUS)<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- **Penile urethral strictures** (graft lengths up to 15 cm)
- Iatrogenic, LS, and hypospadias-related penile strictures
- Patients who wish to avoid open penile urethroplasty

### Contraindications / limitations

- **Obliterative strictures** where the ventral urethral plate is completely destroyed: the technique requires a viable ventral graft bed. Before reconstructive surgery, the 2026 EAU guideline recommends RUG to define stricture location and length (strong) and combined RUG/VCUG for nearly obliterative disease (strong).<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Long or proximal strictures.** TraMUS has used grafts up to 15 cm, but proximal visualization and suture reach limit transmeatal access; long-term outcome data remain limited.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Unrecognized proximal extension.** The two recurrences in Sterling's series had more proximal disease than initially appreciated; define the full stricture extent before choosing transmeatal repair.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guideline Context

The AUA Urethral Stricture Disease Guideline Amendment (2023) recommends offering urethroplasty to patients with recurrent meatal or fossa navicularis strictures (Moderate Recommendation, Grade C), and notes that LS-related fossa navicularis strictures are less likely to be reconstructed successfully using genital skin transfer; oral mucosal graft success has been reported in this setting.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> The multi-institutional retrospective data are consistent with offering the Nikolavsky technique within this recommendation. The 2026 EAU guideline recommends offering open meatoplasty or distal urethroplasty for meatal or fossa navicularis/distal strictures (weak).<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> For LS-related penile strictures, it recommends avoiding genital-skin augmentation (strong) and single-stage oral mucosa graft urethroplasty when local conditions permit (weak); it does not establish superiority of this transmeatal technique.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

## Limitations and Future Directions

- **No randomized controlled trials.** All evidence is from retrospective case series. A randomized comparison with open dorsal inlay or staged urethroplasty has not been done.
- **Intermediate-term follow-up.** Sterling reported mean follow-up of 36 months, not a maximum of 36 months. Given that stricture recurrence can occur years after urethroplasty (particularly in LS), longer follow-up is needed to confirm durability.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **TraMUS extension is very early.** Of 13 treated patients, 11 had one-year outcome data after two were lost at 4 and 8 months; median reported follow-up was 14 months, and one patient developed de novo incontinence (reported as 9%). Complex etiologies warrant cautious interpretation.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Surgeon learning curve.** The technique requires comfort with transurethral suturing and graft manipulation; the multi-institutional study reported similar outcomes across 12 centers.
- **Adjacent endoscopic-suturing approaches.** Ungerer 2023 described a fully endoscopic urethroplasty using the RD 180 suturing device for membranous strictures, a conceptually related but technically distinct approach extending minimally invasive urethroplasty to the posterior urethra.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

---

## Practical Role

Transmeatal ventral inlay is an option for selected distal strictures in experienced hands. Counseling covers the observational evidence, oral-graft morbidity, recurrence and the possible need for open revision. Extension to longer penile disease remains supported by a small early cohort rather than comparative trials.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
