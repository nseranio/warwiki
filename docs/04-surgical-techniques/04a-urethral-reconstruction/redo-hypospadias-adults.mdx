# Redo Hypospadias Repair in Adolescents and Adults

**Failed hypospadias repair** in adolescents and adults can present with stricture, fistula, persistent curvature, or incompletely corrected hypospadias after childhood surgery.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Hair-bearing neourethral skin is another documented presentation: 6 of 50 men in a retrospective adult series had hair in the urethra.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> For the full clinical picture of this population (epidemiology, presenting complaints, decision framework, and outcome statistics by series), see [Hypospadias & Epispadias](/docs/special-populations/05f-lifelong-care/hypospadias-epispadias), which this page does not repeat. This page is the operative companion: it summarizes the primary childhood techniques only as background for what was done, then covers evaluation, technique, and graft or flap choice specific to reoperation.

For the staged free-graft technique that dominates adult redo reconstruction, see [Bracka Two-Stage Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/bracka-two-stage). For buccal mucosa graft harvest and biology, see [Buccal Mucosa Graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa). For curvature evaluation and plication or corporoplasty technique, see [Congenital Penile Curvature](/docs/clinical-conditions/03g-genital-scrotal/congenital-penile-curvature) and [Tunica Plication](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease/tunica-plication).

## Background: Why These Patients Return

Childhood hypospadias repair uses one of a small set of techniques selected mainly by meatal location, curvature, and urethral plate quality: meatal advancement and glanuloplasty (MAGPI) for the most distal, minimally curved variants; tubularized incised plate repair (TIP), with or without a dorsal inlay graft (G-TIP), for distal and many proximal cases with a usable plate; an onlay island flap when the plate is preserved but needs lateral augmentation; and staged reconstruction with a free graft (preputial skin or buccal mucosa), placed at a first operation and tubularized at a second, when the plate must be transected for severe curvature or is otherwise unusable.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> These techniques, and the current pediatric selection logic between them, are described on [Hypospadias & Epispadias](/docs/special-populations/05f-lifelong-care/hypospadias-epispadias); they are named here only so the reconstructive surgeon evaluating an adult can read an old operative note.

Each of these primary techniques carries its own recognized mode of later failure:

- **Reoperation after single-stage proximal repair.** In a single-institution 10-year retrospective series of 140 boys with primary proximal hypospadias, reoperation rates were 52.6% after TIP and 52.1% after dorsal-inlay-graft TIP, compared with 28% in the staged preputial group (12 flap and 48 free-graft repairs); staging was favored for severe ventral curvature.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Recurrence of ventral curvature after TIP and dorsal-inlay-graft TIP was described in the same series as a significant, under-recognized complication in its own right, separate from fistula or stricture.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Fistula after TIP versus dorsal-inlay-graft TIP.** A pediatric systematic review and meta-analysis of two randomized and four observational studies (TIP in 350 patients, dorsal-inlay-graft TIP in 267) found urethrocutaneous fistula in 9.4% versus 4.9%, a difference that did not reach statistical significance in pooled analysis; the review also found no significant pooled difference in meatal or urethral stenosis, wound dehiscence, or total complications between the two techniques.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> These are pediatric primary-repair figures, not an adult redo complication rate.
- **Graft contracture after staged repair.** A first-stage free graft can contract before tubularization, narrowing the eventual neourethral plate. In a single-center paired-measurement series spanning seven years, the chapter authors reported graft contraction of 50% or more, sufficient to require revision, in roughly 1 of 7 staged preputial-graft repairs (of 184) versus roughly 1 of 70 repairs (of 114) using a modified three-stage technique with earlier curvature correction and later graft placement; this is the authors' own single-center experience without an independent comparative study, not a validated technique-superiority claim.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

In Barbagli’s two-center series of 1,176 patients treated for failed hypospadias repair (mean age 31; range 1–76 years), 760 (64.6%) underwent single-stage and 416 (35.4%) staged repair. After completion of secondary procedures, 1,036 (88.1%) met a composite endpoint of functional urethra, no fistula, stricture or residual curvature, and acceptable meatal appearance at a mean 60.4 months. This mixed-age, eventual composite outcome is neither adult-only nor single-operation success.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Preoperative Evaluation

The general adult hypospadias evaluation (history of prior operations, penile examination, erection-time curvature assessment, uroflowmetry and PVR, RUG/VCUG, cystourethroscopy, sonourethrography, and urine culture) is covered on [Hypospadias & Epispadias](/docs/special-populations/05f-lifelong-care/hypospadias-epispadias#adult-evaluation) and is not repeated here. In proximal hypospadias, an enlarged prostatic utricle, more common with increasing hypospadias severity, can divert a blindly passed urethral catheter into the utricle rather than the bladder.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> Attempting catheter passage before beginning the repair lets this be identified and addressed before the operative field is committed. When the catheter will not reliably reach the bladder, a guidewire placed cystoscopically into the bladder, with the catheter passed over it, is a described bailout.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Staged Versus Single-Stage Revision

The decision between staged and single-stage reoperation follows the same variables used in adult primary repair (meatal location, curvature severity, plate availability, and tissue quality), but reoperative scarring shifts the balance toward staging more often than in a virgin field.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> For patients with a penile urethral stricture, the EAU 2026 guideline strongly recommends counseling that adverse intraoperative tissue findings may require conversion from a planned single-stage urethroplasty to a staged repair.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> The full comparative adult literature (Morrison, Verla, Aldamanhori, and the risk escalation with each additional prior operation) is summarized on [Hypospadias & Epispadias](/docs/special-populations/05f-lifelong-care/hypospadias-epispadias). In practice:

- **Isolated distal complication.** A single fistula or short distal stricture in an otherwise adequate reconstruction may be amenable to one-stage repair. For distal strictures after failed hypospadias repair, a 2026 systematic review of 20 heterogeneous studies found no statistically significant pooled success difference between single-stage and staged procedures; severe fibrosis or poor tissue quality favored staging. These pooled data do not establish equivalent outcomes in adults.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Longer or more complex reoperative disease** (stricture with poor plate, hair-bearing neourethra, lichen sclerosus, or combined curvature and urethral disease) is usually staged, with buccal mucosa as the preferred graft; technique is detailed on [Bracka Two-Stage Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/bracka-two-stage).
- **Multiply failed repair, severe scarring, or patient preference to avoid further penile reconstruction** is a legitimate indication for [perineal urethrostomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/seven-flap-perineal-urethrostomy) as definitive management rather than another attempt at penile urethral reconstruction.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Persistent or Recurrent Curvature

Curvature can persist after an inadequate first correction or recur years later as the penis undergoes pubertal growth, independent of any urethral problem. In a series of 59 adolescents and young adults (ages 14-21) presenting with recurrent curvature after childhood hypospadias repair (the index repairs were TIP with dorsal plication in 28, Thiersch-Duplay urethroplasty with Nesbit repair in 9, preputial mucosal island onlay with Nesbit repair in 6, and two-stage repair with ventral dermal graft in 3; 13 had been repaired elsewhere with records unavailable), surgical correction was distributed across three approaches by degree of curvature after skin degloving:<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

| Approach used | Patients | Basis for selection |
|---|---|---|
| One-stage dorsal plication (skin detethering in 19/32) | 32 | Recurrent curvature &lt;30° after degloving and artificial erection testing |
| One-stage urethral mobilization and ventral corporotomy with a free dermal graft | 12 | Moderate curvature with a healthy reconstructed urethra and separable urethral-corporal tissue planes |
| First-stage ventral corporal dermal graft and skin coverage, then grafted TIP urethroplasty at 8–12 months | 15 | Curvature &gt;45° from a short scarred neourethra and/or corporal disproportion; fistulas in 11/59 patients were closed during the first repair |

Of the 55 patients with 6-48 months of follow-up (median 30 months), 53 healed well and 2 had wound breakdown with scarring that left mild recurrent curvature, less severe than before surgery. Twenty-eight of the 59 patients reported satisfactory sexual activity. No erectile abnormalities were reported among patients who received corporal or dermal grafts, but sexual function was not assessed in detail.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> The authors attributed recurrence to peri-urethral and skin fibrosis, or to disproportionate growth of a hypoplastic ventral corporal wall or reconstructed urethra, and recommended following boys with proximal hypospadias repair through puberty rather than assuming an initially good childhood result will persist.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> For plication and corporoplasty technique detail, suture material, and outcome series by name, see [Tunica Plication](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease/tunica-plication), which includes congenital-curvature-specific outcome data.

## A Single-Stage Option for Severe, Minimally Treated Adult Disease

Most reoperative literature favors staging once the plate must be transected for severe curvature. A single-stage alternative has been proposed specifically for adults with penoscrotal hypospadias and severe curvature who present with little or no prior surgery and healthy, unscarred prepuce (a population distinct from adults with multiple failed hypospadias repairs, but one the reconstructive urologist occasionally encounters: neglected congenital hypospadias, or a patient who declines a multistage plan). As described by the chapter authors:<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

1. Tethering and scar are released and curvature reassessed intraoperatively.
2. The urethral plate is divided at the corona if curvature cannot otherwise be corrected.
3. A vascularized preputial flap, raised on its dartos pedicle, is rotated to form the urethral tube; the pedicled flap carries the neourethra instead of a free graft, distinguishing this operation from single-stage tubularized free-graft urethroplasty.
4. If curvature persists, a ventral corporotomy is performed and the corporal defect patched with a buccal mucosa graft sized slightly larger than the defect.
5. The native-plate-to-flap anastomosis is kept broad, the tube suture line is placed dorsally toward the corpora, and dorsal shaft manipulation is avoided after plate transection, to protect the glans blood supply.

The chapter authors describe the pedicled flap being tubularized over a 14 Fr catheter to a reported tube width of approximately 25 Fr, with the buccal graft patch deliberately oversized.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> A prospective single-center series of 23 primary penoscrotal repairs in patients aged 16–27 years used a single-stage pedicled preputial tube and corporal augmentation (buccal graft in 21, bovine pericardium in 2); the authors reported a 30.4% complication rate with at least six months of follow-up.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> The study did not include multiply reoperated repairs or a staged comparator.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> It should be presented to patients as a described single-stage option for a specific, narrow scenario, not as an established alternative to staged buccal mucosa graft urethroplasty for the general reoperative population.

## Coverage and Graft Selection

Buccal mucosa is commonly used for reoperative grafting; skin grafts have been associated with higher stricture recurrence in complex cohorts. The EAU 2026 guideline strongly recommends against penile skin grafts or flaps when failed hypospadias repair coexists with lichen sclerosus or scarred skin, and weakly recommends that men with failed hypospadias repair be considered complex patients and referred to specialist centers.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> Well-vascularized coverage of the suture line (dartos, tunica vaginalis, or local fascia) is used when local anatomy allows. These points, along with the comparative adult graft-material data, are covered on [Hypospadias & Epispadias](/docs/special-populations/05f-lifelong-care/hypospadias-epispadias#graft-material-selection) and on [Bracka Two-Stage Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/bracka-two-stage); they are not repeated here.
