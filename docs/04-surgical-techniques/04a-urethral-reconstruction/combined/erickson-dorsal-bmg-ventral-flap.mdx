# Combined Dorsal BMG + Ventral Fasciocutaneous Flap (Erickson)

The **combined dorsal buccal mucosal graft (BMG) with ventral fasciocutaneous flap** is a single-stage tissue-transfer technique for complex, long-segment, or obliterative anterior urethral strictures where neither a graft nor a flap alone provides sufficient tissue for circumferential urethral reconstruction. It was described by [Erickson](/docs/roots/surgeons/a-g/bradley-erickson), Breyer, and McAninch in 2012.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The AUA 2023 urethral stricture guideline amendment permits grafts, flaps, or combinations for long, multi-segment strictures (Moderate Recommendation, Grade C); this is not a technique-specific endorsement of the Erickson operation.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For graft material principles, see [Buccal Mucosa Graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa). For the ventral pedicled-flap principles, see [Penile / Preputial Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/penile-preputial). For the dual-BMG (Palminteri / Gelman) alternative, see [Dorsal Onlay BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/dorsal-onlay-omg) and [Ventral Onlay BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/ventral-onlay-omg). The Karapanos plate-and-spongiosum-preserving modification with the Orandi flap is described in the technique section below.

---

## Concept and Rationale

The technique leverages the complementary strengths of two tissue types:

- **BMG (dorsal).** The graft is free and requires a well-vascularized recipient bed. It is quilted directly onto the tunica albuginea of the corpora cavernosa, which provides imbibition and inosculation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Fasciocutaneous flap (ventral).** The flap is pedicled penile skin that carries its own dartos-fascia blood supply. Placed ventrally, it does not depend on the recipient bed for survival, which suits strictures where ventral spongiofibrosis has compromised local vascularity.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

Together they form a circumferential neourethra (dorsal BMG roof and ventral flap floor), enabling complete segmental urethral replacement in a single stage.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Indications and Patient Selection

- **Long-segment anterior urethral strictures** (mean approximately 9.75 cm in the Erickson / McAninch series).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Obliterative or near-obliterative strictures** not amenable to EPA or simple onlay augmentation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Panurethral or multi-segment strictures** involving both penile and bulbar urethra.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Failed prior urethroplasty** with damaged urethral plates.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Prerequisite:** healthy, non-hair-bearing penile skin available for the fasciocutaneous flap.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

In a GURS survey conducted in 2022 and published in 2024 (134 of 350 invited responded), 90% preferred multiple BMGs over a combined graft/flap for panurethral strictures.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> The survey does not specify dorsal-plus-ventral grafting.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> The combined operation remains an option when single-stage repair is desired and the urethral plate is severely compromised. For patients with penile strictures, EAU 2026 strongly recommends counseling that adverse intraoperative findings may require conversion to staged repair.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

In lichen sclerosus-proven urethral stricture, the AUA amendment states that surgeons should not use genital skin flaps or grafts, so these patients are not candidates for this technique.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Surgical Technique

The steps as described by Erickson, Breyer, and McAninch and refined by Kojovic / Djordjevic:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1. Exposure | Perineal approach; identify the strictured segment; open the urethra ventrally through the stricture |
| 2. Dorsal BMG placement | Mobilize corpus spongiosum off the corpora cavernosa dorsally; harvest BMG from inner cheek(s); **quilt the graft to the tunica albuginea** with interrupted sutures, forming the dorsal wall of the neourethra |
| 3. Ventral fasciocutaneous flap | The original Erickson operation uses a **distal circumferential penile fasciocutaneous flap**, opened into a strip. Kojovic uses a longitudinal dorsal penile-skin flap transposed ventrally. Preserve the vascular pedicle and join flap margins to graft/native urethral margins as appropriate |
| 4. Neourethral closure | Approximate dorsal graft and ventral flap to form a tubular neourethra; when preserved, the corpus spongiosum is wrapped around the repair for additional support<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>. Guidice recommends overlapping the flap's proximal end and the graft's distal end by about 0.5 cm to reduce the risk of an annular, ring-like restenosis at the junction<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| 5. Catheter and closure | Urethral catheter; layered wound closure |

### Karapanos modification

Karapanos 2024 preserves the native urethral plate and corpus spongiosum, uses a narrower Orandi-type ventral pedicled penile-skin flap, and wraps the preserved spongiosum ventrally around the flap for reinforcement, yielding a triangular-cross-section neourethra and 91.7% success after a median 38 mo of follow-up for narrow penile strictures, with transient fistulas in 25% (3 / 12) all healing with prolonged catheterization.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Outcomes

| Series | Evaluated n | Stricture length | Reported outcome | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Erickson 2012<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 14 | Mean 9.75 cm; mean circumferential replacement 5.4 cm | 9/14 without further intervention; 11/14 after allowing one endoscopic treatment | Median 2.5 yr |
| Kojovic / Djordjevic 2019<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | 46 of 51 operated | Mean 5.2 cm | 39/46 patent (84.8%); 36/46 successful voiding (78.3%) when three fistulas also counted | Mean 49 mo |
| Karapanos 2024 (modification)<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | 12 | Median 5 cm | 11/12 successful (91.7%); three transient fistulas | Median 38 mo |
| Anadani 2025 (case report)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | 1 | 5 cm | Early reported success; insufficient for a durability estimate | 3 mo |

Small, uncontrolled series with different operations and endpoints provide no comparison with staged reconstruction. For graft-and-flap substitution repair, EAU 2026 recommends at least one year of follow-up (Strong), three-month flexible cystoscopy (Weak), and a risk-adjusted protocol (Weak); its standard-risk proposal includes uroflowmetry, patient-reported outcomes and anatomic assessment.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> Erickson reported longer strictures among the five initial failures (12.8 versus 8.7 cm, p = 0.04), an exploratory association in a 14-patient cohort.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Complications

- **Stricture recurrence.** Recurrence is the primary concern, at 15–36% in the Erickson and Kojovic series (5/14 initial failures and 7/46, respectively).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Urethrocutaneous fistula.** Approximately 6.5% in Kojovic; transient fistulas in 25% (3 / 12) in Karapanos, all healing with prolonged catheterization.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Post-void dribbling.** Dribbling was reported by 5 of 12 patients (41.7%) in the Karapanos series after spongiosum-preserving repair. The mechanism has not been established.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Sexual outcomes.** Reliable technique-specific ED estimates are unavailable from these small series. Isolated dorsal and ventral BMG onlay studies provide no ED estimate for circumferential graft-plus-flap reconstruction.
- **Donor-site morbidity.** Oral complications from BMG harvest (numbness, tightness) typically mild and transient.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

In Warner 2015, a retrospective multicenter cohort of 466 strictures at least 8 cm long, flap procedures were associated with 32% complications versus 14% without flaps (p = 0.02). Differences in case complexity and procedure selection limit causal comparisons.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Comparison With Alternative Approaches

- **Dual BMG (Palminteri dorsal and ventral; Gelman / Siegel).** Gelman / Siegel reported 94% initial success in 18 patients, with one additional patient successfully treated by DVIU. The ventral graft requires preserved spongiosum; Gelman / Siegel did not compare the technique with a vascularized flap.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Distal variant (Gelman / Sohn).** For obliterative distal strictures with circumferential urethral deficiency, Gelman and Sohn (2011) quilted buccal mucosa to the ventral corpora to rebuild the dorsal urethral plate, usually including the glans and fossa navicularis, and completed a single-stage repair with an onlay penile skin flap. All 12 patients were free of disabling chordee or stricture at a mean 39 months (range 7–96); one urethrocutaneous fistula, and one fistula with medium-caliber fossa narrowing and chordee, were repaired.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Two-stage repair** ([Bracka](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/bracka-two-stage), [Johanson](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/johanson-two-stage)). An option when the plate or local tissues cannot support a single-stage repair. Choice depends on tissue quality, previous repairs, and patient preferences; comparable success of the combined operation has not been established in a controlled trial. Genital skin flaps should not be used for LS-related reconstruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **BMG alone (dorsal or ventral onlay).** Suited to non-obliterative strictures. Hassan 2025 meta found no difference in success between dorsal and ventral onlay BMG (RR 1.00, 95% CI 0.94–1.06), though ventral onlay may reduce transient ED.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Location-based proximal graft, distal flap (Angermeier).** For a panurethral stricture with disease-free penile skin available but too short to bridge the whole defect, Angermeier describes a proximal bulbar ventral BMG onlay combined with a distal penile or penoscrotal island flap based on lateral dartos, starting in exaggerated lithotomy for the perineal graft stage and repositioning to low lithotomy before the distal flap work to limit time with the legs elevated. This location-based sequence is distinct from this page's dorsal-graft/ventral-flap circumferential configuration; the source gives no outcome denominator for this specific sequence.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

---

## Key Considerations

- The flap requires healthy, hairless penile skin. Avoid genital skin for LS-related strictures or if available skin is diseased or hair-bearing. Circumcision alone is not an absolute contraindication; assess the remaining skin and its vascularity.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Hair-bearing skin should not be used for substitution urethroplasty (AUA Clinical Principle) because of the risk of urethral calculi, recurrent UTI and lumen obstruction by hair. The exception is a rare case with no alternative.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- The operation is best performed by reconstructive urologists proficient in both graft and flap techniques.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
