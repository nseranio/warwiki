# Ventral BMG + Gracilis Muscle Flap (Vanni / Zinman)

**Ventral buccal mucosal graft (BMG) urethroplasty with gracilis muscle flap** is a specialized reconstructive technique reserved for high-risk, long-segment urethral strictures with a compromised graft bed and poor local vascular supply, where standard urethroplasty is unlikely to succeed.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The Lahey Hospital group (Zinman, Vanni, and colleagues) reported a selected cohort accumulated over 20 years.

For the foundations gracilis-muscle-flap article (anatomy, harvest principles, multi-organ applications), see [Gracilis Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/gracilis). For graft material principles, see [Buccal Mucosa Graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa). For other combined graft + flap urethroplasties, see [Combined Dorsal BMG + Ventral Fasciocutaneous Flap (Erickson)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/erickson-dorsal-bmg-ventral-flap). For gracilis use in rectourethral fistula repair (Lahey), see the [Rectourethral Fistula](/docs/clinical-conditions/03f-fistulas/in-males/rectourethral) article.

---

## Rationale

A free graft (BMG) requires a well-vascularized recipient bed for imbibition and inosculation. In patients with radiation-damaged tissue, prior failed urethroplasty, or extensive periurethral fibrosis, the native corpus spongiosum cannot provide adequate blood supply. The gracilis muscle flap is harvested and transposed to serve as a new vascularized graft bed for the ventral BMG, replacing the deficient spongiosum.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Beckenstein 1996 documented epithelial migration after gracilis coverage of a partial urethral defect in one patient with Fournier's gangrene. This case does not establish reliable circumferential urethral regeneration or eliminate the need for a mucosal graft.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Indications

The percentages below describe overlapping reports from one center, not independent pooled cohorts. Selection depends on the urethral defect and recipient-bed quality.

- **Radiation-induced strictures.** The most common etiology, 45–60% of cases.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Prior failed urethroplasty:** 33–45% of patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Post-prostatectomy strictures:** 10–23%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Long-segment strictures:** mean length 7.6–8.2 cm (range 3.5–15 cm).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Strictures after transurethral surgery, trauma, or failed hypospadias repair.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Patients with prior UroLume stent placement.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgical Technique

The procedure as described by Palmer, Zinman, and Vanni:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1. Positioning | Lithotomy; perineal incision provides access to the urethra |
| 2. Urethral mobilization | Ventral urethrotomy through the full length of the stricture; debride or excise diseased / fibrotic spongiosum as needed |
| 3. BMG harvest | Inner cheek(s) — both cheeks for long strictures; defat and prepare<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| 4. Ventral BMG inset | Suture the graft as a ventral onlay patch to healthy urethrotomy margins; place its external surface in contact with the vascularized muscle. This differs from quilting an inlay to the urethral plate |
| 5. Gracilis harvest | Mobilize the gracilis while identifying and preserving its **dominant proximal vascular pedicle**; divide distal attachments and secondary pedicles as required for tension-free reach. See the linked flap-anatomy article<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| 6. Tunneling and inset | Tunnel the muscle subcutaneously to the perineum; **wrap the gracilis over the ventral BMG** as a vascularized graft bed |
| 7. Closure | Secure muscle flap; layered perineal closure over a urethral catheter |

## Outcomes

| Series | n | Mean stricture | Success | Mean FU | Time to recurrence | Radiation etiology | Prior urethroplasty | AUS placement |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Palmer 2015<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | 20 | 8.2 cm (3.5–15) | **80%** | 40 mo | 10 mo (2–17) | 45% | 45% | 25% |
| Rozanski / Vanni 2020<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 30 | 7.6 cm (3.5–15) | **76.7%** | 32 mo (4–92) | 8 mo (2–17) | 60% | 33.3% | 23.3% |

Success was defined by absence of recurrent narrowing preventing passage of a 16-Fr cystoscope in the 2015 report and a 17-Fr cystoscope in 2020. The later cohort expands the earlier experience.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Among failures, salvage options included urinary diversion (ileal loop), suprapubic tube placement, endoscopic dilation, or DVIU.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> These selected series support feasibility when reconstructive options are limited; they do not compare the operation directly with diversion or other graft-bed strategies.

## Complications

- **Urinary incontinence** is the dominant complication: 23–25% underwent subsequent AUS placement in these cohorts. The uncontrolled studies cannot separate effects of baseline sphincter damage, radiation, previous procedures, and reconstruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Stricture recurrence:** approximately 20–23%, typically within the first 8–10 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Gracilis donor-site morbidity.** Generally low. Studies in non-urologic contexts report approximately 11% decrease in hip-adduction strength (not clinically noticed by patients), hypoesthesia in the obturator-nerve distribution in approximately 40%, and thigh-contour deformity in up to 59% of myocutaneous flaps. A small myocutaneous-flap study found associations between necrosis and BMI &gt; 25, smoking, and recipient-site radiation; its rates should not be applied directly to isolated gracilis-muscle urethroplasty.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Where It Fits in Urethral Reconstruction

This technique occupies a specific niche. The AUA 2023 urethral stricture guideline amendment recommends oral mucosa as the first-choice graft material and acknowledges that complex, long, multi-segment strictures may require combined tissue-transfer techniques.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> Comparative evidence for standard bulbar dorsal versus ventral BMG onlay does not establish outcomes in these high-risk combined reconstructions. In the 2024 GURS survey, 66% of respondents preferred dorsal and 34% ventral placement; only 134 of 350 invited surgeons responded.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> However, when the local tissue is too compromised for standard graft take, the gracilis muscle flap can provide a vascularized recipient bed.

Other vascularized-flap options in this setting include the rectus abdominis flap and omental flap (used in robotic posterior urethroplasty).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> Gracilis is preferred in the perineal approach due to proximity, reliable pedicle, expendability, and ease of harvest with generally limited, but possible, functional donor morbidity.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
