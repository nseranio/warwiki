# Female Vaginal Flap Urethroplasty (Blandy U-Flap)

The **Blandy U-flap vaginal flap urethroplasty** is the foundational ventral-onlay vaginal-flap technique for female urethral stricture (FUS) repair. A U-shaped anterior-vaginal-wall flap is mobilized on a proximal pedicle, the strictured urethra is opened ventrally, and the flap is inlaid into the urethrotomy defect to augment the urethral lumen.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> It is the female counterpart of the male ventral-onlay urethroplasty.

![Blandy U-flap female vaginal-flap urethroplasty: a U-shaped anterior-vaginal-wall flap with a proximal pedicle is inlaid into a ventral urethrotomy over a Foley, augmenting the ventral circumference](/img/diagrams/blandy-u-flap.svg)
*Blandy U-flap geometry. **(1)** A U-shaped **anterior-vaginal-wall flap** with a **proximal pedicle** is raised over the strictured urethra and a ventral (6 o'clock) urethrotomy is planned. **(2)** The flap is inlaid into the opened urethra as a **ventral onlay** over a 16–18 Fr Foley, sutured to the native plate. The **cross-section** shows the result: native dorsal urethral plate above, vaginal-flap onlay ventrally. Being pedicled, the flap keeps its submucosal vascular plexus — no buccal-graft donor site. (Original WARWIKI schematic)*

For the broader female-stricture treatment ladder, see [Female DVIU and Urethral Dilation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-dviu-dilation), [Distal Urethrectomy with Advancement Meatoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/distal-urethrectomy-advancement), [Lateral-Based Anterior Vaginal Wall Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/lateral-based-anterior-vaginal-wall-flap), [Female Dorsal Onlay BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-dorsal-onlay-urethroplasty), and [Ventral Onlay BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/ventral-onlay-omg).

---

## Eponym and Concept

Attributed to **John Blandy**. The U-flap design uses anterior vaginal wall as a **pedicled** flap (proximal base), preserving the submucosal vascular plexus, and avoids the donor-site morbidity of buccal mucosa.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Indications

- FUS refractory to dilation or DVIU.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Distal and mid-urethral** strictures (most common application).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- Healthy, well-estrogenized anterior vaginal wall (no LS, no severe atrophy).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- Selected longer / pan-urethral strictures in experienced hands.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Contraindications / Limitations

- **Lichen sclerosus** or vaginal atrophy compromising the flap.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- Prior pelvic radiation.
- Prior synthetic midurethral sling with periurethral scarring limiting flap mobilization.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

---

## Technique

Dorsal lithotomy under regional or general anesthesia.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

1. **Cystourethroscopy** — confirm location, length, and proximal urethra / bladder.
2. **Foley placement** — identifies the urethral lumen and serves as a guide.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
3. **U-shaped vaginal incision** — two parallel longitudinal incisions in the anterior vaginal wall flanking the urethra, beginning at the meatus and extending proximally past the stricture, joined distally around the meatus to create a **U-shaped flap with a proximal pedicle**.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
4. **Flap elevation** — vaginal mucosal flap dissected off the periurethral tissue and reflected cephalad to expose the ventral urethra.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
5. **Ventral urethrotomy** — longitudinal **6 o'clock incision** through the full length of the stricture, extending into healthy mucosa proximally and distally.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
6. **Flap inlay** — distal tip of the vaginal flap sutured to the proximal end of the urethrotomy with interrupted 4-0 / 5-0 polyglactin; lateral edges sutured to the lateral edges of the incised urethral mucosa to augment the ventral circumference.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
7. **Tubularization** of the inner portion of the flap over a 16–18 Fr Foley to ensure adequate caliber.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
8. **Second-layer closure** — outer flap layer or native vaginal wall edges closed as a watertight second layer.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
9. **Catheter** — 10–21 days postoperatively (varies by series).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

**Technical principles** — pedicled (not free graft); U-width must allow tension-free augmentation; ventral approach preserves the dorsal urethral surface and pubourethral ligaments.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Important Modifications

### Lateral-based anterior vaginal wall flap (Romero-Maroto / Simonato)

Romero-Maroto 2018 and Simonato 2010 independently described a **lateral-based** rather than midline U-flap, inspired by the Orandi technique. The lateral pedicle better preserves the vascular axis; Simonato's variant adds **partial de-epithelialization** of the flap edges to promote cicatrization at the suture line and reduce fistula risk. Romero-Maroto specifically developed the modification because the **classic Blandy U-flap produced retrusive meatus and inward urinary stream** — a recognized functional limitation of the proximal-pedicled design.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> See [Lateral-Based Anterior Vaginal Wall Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/lateral-based-anterior-vaginal-wall-flap).

### Gormley vaginal inlay flap

Simplified ventral-incision-and-inlay variant. Gormley 2010: 12 patients, follow-up 3 mo–9 yr, 100% subjective symptom relief, all able to catheterize 14 Fr, **0% de novo SUI**.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

### Blaivas vaginal flap reconstruction (urethral loss / neourethra)

For **urethral loss** rather than stricture: vaginal flap rolled into a tube to create a neourethra, covered with a Martius labial fat-pad flap, combined with pubovaginal sling. Blaivas-Heritz 1996: 49 women, continence in 87%.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

## The Retrusive-Meatus / Spraying Problem

The defining functional limitation of the classic Blandy design is the **proximal pedicle pulling the neomeatus posteriorly**, resulting in:

- A retrusive (posteriorly displaced) neomeatus.
- An **inward urinary stream** that sprays the perineum or vagina rather than directing anteriorly.
- **Urinary spraying reported in 2 / 7 (29%)** of VFU patients in Higuchi 2026; not reported with BMG urethroplasty in the same series.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

This is the rationale for the **lateral-based flap modification**, which keeps the neomeatus orthotopic and preserves normal stream direction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Complications

- **Stricture recurrence** — 0–43% across series; **late recurrences at ≥ 5 yr** documented.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- **De novo SUI** — 0–14%; mostly mild and PFPT-responsive; persistent SUI may require TOT or PVS.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Retrusive meatus / inward stream** — specific to the classic U-flap.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Urinary spraying** — VFU-specific; not reported with BMG urethroplasty.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **De novo urgency / urge incontinence** — reported even with technically successful repairs.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Urethrovaginal fistula** — rare; risk reduced by multi-layer closure and Martius interposition.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Need for CIC** — occasional (1 / 6 in Simonato).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Comparison with Alternatives

| Feature | Blandy U-Flap | Lateral-Based Flap | Dorsal Onlay BMG | Ventral Onlay BMG |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tissue source | Anterior vaginal wall | Anterior vaginal wall | Buccal mucosa | Buccal mucosa |
| Blood supply | Pedicled (proximal base) | Pedicled (lateral base) | Free graft | Free graft |
| Meatal position | Risk of retrusive | Orthotopic | Orthotopic | Orthotopic |
| Urinary spraying | Reported | Not reported | Not reported | Not reported |
| Stricture location | Distal / mid | Distal / mid / pan | Any | Any |
| Reported success | 57–100% | 83–100% | 87–92% | 89–98% |
| Donor-site morbidity | None (same field) | None | Oral | Oral |
| Vaginal-tissue health required | Yes | Yes | No | No |

A 2026 meta-analysis (n = 319) found **no significant difference between dorsal and ventral onlay BMG** in success rate (RR 1.00; 95% CI 0.91–1.10), Qmax, PVR, or AUA score.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> AUA 2023 frames urethroplasty success in women as 69–95% depending on technique, with technique selection driven by surgeon experience.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

---

## When to Choose the Blandy U-Flap

- Stricture is **distal or mid-urethral**.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Anterior vaginal wall is **healthy and well-estrogenized** (no LS).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- Surgeon prefers to avoid BMG donor-site morbidity.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- A **pedicled** (vs free-graft) reconstruction is preferred.

Given the retrusive-meatus / spraying signal, many contemporary surgeons prefer the **lateral-based flap modification** or **BMG urethroplasty** when the stricture extends beyond the distal urethra.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

---

## Postoperative Management

- Urethral catheter **10–21 days** (surgeon preference).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- Voiding trial after catheter removal.
- Uroflowmetry, PVR, and cystoscopy at 3–6 mo.
- Topical estrogen in postmenopausal women.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Long-term surveillance is essential** — late recurrences ≥ 5 yr documented.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

---

## Summary

The Blandy U-flap is the foundational ventral-onlay vaginal-flap urethroplasty for FUS — pedicled, donor-site-free, distal / mid-urethral, with reported success of 57–100% across small series. Its defining limitation is **retrusive meatus and inward urinary stream** from the proximal-pedicled design, which has driven adoption of the **lateral-based Romero-Maroto / Simonato modification** and the increasing preference for **BMG urethroplasty** when the stricture extends beyond the distal urethra or when local tissue is unhealthy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
