# Female Vaginal Flap Urethroplasty (Blandy U-Flap)

The **Blandy U-flap vaginal flap urethroplasty** is a ventral-onlay vaginal-flap technique for female urethral stricture (FUS) repair. A U-shaped anterior-vaginal-wall flap is mobilized on a proximal pedicle, the strictured urethra is opened ventrally, and the flap is inlaid into the urethrotomy defect to augment the urethral lumen.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> It is the female counterpart of the male ventral-onlay urethroplasty.

![Blandy U-flap female vaginal-flap urethroplasty: a U-shaped anterior-vaginal-wall flap with a proximal pedicle is inlaid into a ventral urethrotomy over a Foley, augmenting the ventral circumference](/img/diagrams/blandy-u-flap.svg)

*Blandy U-flap geometry. **(1)** A U-shaped **anterior-vaginal-wall flap** with a **proximal pedicle** is raised over the strictured urethra and a ventral (6 o'clock) urethrotomy is planned. **(2)** The flap is inlaid into the opened urethra as a **ventral onlay** over a 16–18 Fr Foley, sutured to the native plate. The **cross-section** shows the result: native dorsal urethral plate above, vaginal-flap onlay ventrally. Being pedicled, the flap keeps its submucosal vascular plexus — no buccal-graft donor site. (Original WARWIKI schematic)*

For the broader female-stricture treatment ladder, see [Female DVIU and Urethral Dilation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-dviu-dilation), [Distal Urethrectomy with Advancement Meatoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/distal-urethrectomy-advancement), [Lateral-Based Anterior Vaginal Wall Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/lateral-based-anterior-vaginal-wall-flap), [Female Dorsal Onlay BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-dorsal-onlay-urethroplasty), and [Ventral Onlay BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-ventral-onlay-urethroplasty).

---

## Eponym and Concept

Attributed to John Blandy. The U-flap design uses anterior vaginal wall as a pedicled flap (proximal base), preserving the submucosal vascular plexus, and avoids the donor-site morbidity of buccal mucosa.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Indications

- FUS refractory to dilation or DVIU.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Distal and mid-urethral** strictures (most common application).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- Healthy, well-estrogenized anterior vaginal wall (no LS, no severe atrophy).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- Selected longer or pan-urethral strictures, which Simonato treated with the lateral-pedicle variant.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Contraindications / Limitations

- **Lichen sclerosus** or vaginal atrophy compromising the flap.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- Prior pelvic radiation.
- Prior synthetic midurethral sling with periurethral scarring limiting flap mobilization.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

---

## Technique

Dorsal lithotomy under regional or general anesthesia.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

1. **Cystourethroscopy.** Confirm location, length, and proximal urethra and bladder.
2. **Foley placement.** Identifies the urethral lumen and serves as a guide.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
3. **U-shaped vaginal incision.** Two parallel longitudinal incisions in the anterior vaginal wall flanking the urethra, beginning at the meatus and extending proximally past the stricture, joined distally around the meatus to create a U-shaped flap with a proximal pedicle.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
4. **Flap elevation.** Vaginal mucosal flap dissected off the periurethral tissue and reflected cephalad to expose the ventral urethra.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
5. **Ventral urethrotomy.** Longitudinal 6 o'clock incision through the full length of the stricture, extending into healthy mucosa proximally and distally.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
6. **Flap inlay.** The distal tip of the vaginal flap is sutured to the proximal end of the urethrotomy with interrupted absorbable sutures. The lateral edges are sutured to the edges of the incised urethral mucosa to augment the ventral circumference.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
7. **Tubularization** of the inner portion of the flap over a large Foley catheter to ensure adequate caliber.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
8. **Second-layer closure.** Outer flap layer or native vaginal wall edges closed as a watertight second layer.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
9. **Catheter.** 10–21 days postoperatively (varies by series).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

Hajebrahimi describes the urethral incision as dorsal, so terminology for the urethrotomy orientation differs among reports.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

**Technical principles.** The flap is pedicled, not a free graft. The U must be wide enough to allow tension-free augmentation. The ventral approach leaves the dorsal urethral surface intact.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Important Modifications

### Lateral-based anterior vaginal wall flap (Romero-Maroto / Simonato)

Romero-Maroto 2018 and Simonato 2010 independently described a lateral-based flap in place of the midline U-flap, and both were inspired by the male Orandi technique. Simonato's lateral pedicle maintains the vascular axis of the flap. His variant also partially de-epithelializes the flap edges to promote cicatrization at the suture line and avoid fistula formation. Romero-Maroto developed the modification because the Blandy technique had produced a retrusive meatus and an inward urinary stream in their patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> See [Lateral-Based Anterior Vaginal Wall Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/lateral-based-anterior-vaginal-wall-flap).

### Gormley vaginal inlay flap

The Gormley inlay is a simplified variant in which the posterior aspect of the stricture is incised and a vaginal flap is advanced into the defect. In Gormley's 12 women (follow-up 3 months to 9 years), all reported relief of most symptoms and could be catheterized with a 14 Fr catheter, and none had stress incontinence. Urge symptoms and difficulty with catheterization persisted in some women despite a technically good repair.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

### Blaivas vaginal flap reconstruction (urethral loss / neourethra)

This technique is for urethral loss, not stricture. A vaginal flap is rolled into a tube to create a neourethra and covered with a Martius labial fat-pad flap, usually with a concomitant anti-incontinence procedure. In Blaivas 1989 (10 women), 6 were continent after a single reconstruction.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> In Blaivas-Heritz 1996 (49 women with urethral or vesical neck damage), a Martius flap was used in 47 and a fascial pubovaginal sling in 41, and 42 (87%) were continent at 1 to 11 years.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

The EAU 2026 update pooled 161 women treated with vaginal flap urethroplasty, always by a ventral approach (96 inverted-U inlay, 47 lateral C flap). Patency was 81.4% (range 67–100%) at 10 to 80.7 months, and new-onset urinary incontinence occurred in 9.3%.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> An earlier review reported a 92.5% success rate for local flaps across 35 studies (488 patients).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## The Retrusive-Meatus / Spraying Problem

The main functional limitation of the classic Blandy design is a retrusive neomeatus, which Romero-Maroto attributes to the proximal pedicle. It results in:

- A retrusive (posteriorly displaced) neomeatus.
- An inward urinary stream that sprays the perineum or vagina instead of directing anteriorly.
- Urinary spraying in 2 of 7 (29%) VFU patients in Higuchi 2026, and in none of the BMG patients in the same series.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Other acute complications in 14 of 161 women (8.7%) in the EAU 2026 pooled data, including UTI and intravaginal direction of the urinary stream.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

This is the rationale for the lateral-based flap modification, which keeps the neomeatus orthotopic and preserves normal stream direction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Complications

- **Stricture recurrence.** Recurrence ranges from 0% to 43% across series, and late recurrences at 5 years or more have been documented.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- **De novo SUI.** Rates range from 0% to 14% (9.3% in the EAU 2026 pooled data). Most cases are mild and respond to pelvic floor therapy. Persistent SUI requires reassessment of urethral healing, obstruction, tissue quality and continence options.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- **Retrusive meatus and inward stream.** These were described with the classic U-flap.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Urinary spraying.** It has been reported in small vaginal-flap cohorts and is not proven exclusive to VFU.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **De novo urgency or urge incontinence.** It has been reported even after technically successful repairs.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Urethrovaginal fistula.** Flap-edge de-epithelialization (Simonato) and Martius interposition (Blaivas) have been used to lower fistula risk.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Need for CIC.** One of 6 women in Simonato's series required CIC.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Comparison with Alternatives

| Feature | Blandy U-Flap | Lateral-Based Flap | Dorsal Onlay BMG | Ventral Onlay BMG |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tissue source | Anterior vaginal wall | Anterior vaginal wall | Buccal mucosa | Buccal mucosa |
| Blood supply | Pedicled (proximal base) | Pedicled (lateral base) | Free graft | Free graft |
| Meatal position | Risk of retrusive | Orthotopic | Orthotopic | Orthotopic |
| Urinary spraying | Reported | Not reported | Not reported | Not reported |
| Stricture location | Distal / mid | Distal / mid / pan | Any | Any |
| Reported success | 57–100% | 83–100% | 69–95% (EAU pooled 83%) | 86–98% (EAU pooled 90.7%) |
| Donor-site morbidity | None (same field) | None | Oral | Oral |
| Vaginal-tissue health required | Yes | Yes | No | No |

A 2026 meta-analysis of 5 studies (n = 319) found no significant difference between dorsal and ventral onlay BMG in success rate (RR 1.00; 95% CI 0.91–1.10), Qmax, PVR or AUA score.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> Katiyar 2021 randomized 24 women to dorsal or ventral onlay and found comparable outcomes, with one recurrence in each arm.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> The AUA 2023 amendment states that surgeons may reconstruct female strictures with oral mucosal grafts, vaginal flaps or a combination (Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level C). It cites success rates of 69–95% across techniques and technique choice based on surgeon experience.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

## When to Choose the Blandy U-Flap

- Stricture is distal or mid-urethral.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Anterior vaginal wall is healthy and well-estrogenized (no LS).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- Surgeon prefers to avoid BMG donor-site morbidity.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- A pedicled (versus free-graft) reconstruction is preferred.

Because of the retrusive-meatus and spraying reports, the lateral-based modification or BMG urethroplasty has been used when the stricture extends beyond the distal urethra.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

---

## Postoperative Management

- Urethral catheter 10–21 days (surgeon preference).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- Voiding trial after catheter removal.
- Uroflowmetry, PVR, and cystoscopy at 3–6 mo.
- Topical estrogen in postmenopausal women.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Long-term surveillance.** Late recurrences ≥ 5 yr documented.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

---

## Summary

The Blandy U-flap is a pedicled, donor-site-free vaginal-flap urethroplasty for distal or mid-urethral FUS, with reported success of 57–100% across small series. Its main limitation is a retrusive meatus and inward urinary stream. The lateral-based Romero-Maroto and Simonato modification addresses this problem. BMG urethroplasty is an alternative when the stricture extends beyond the distal urethra or local tissue is unhealthy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
