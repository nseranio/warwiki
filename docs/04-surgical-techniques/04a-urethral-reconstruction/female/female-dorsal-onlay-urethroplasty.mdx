# Female Dorsal Onlay Urethroplasty

**Female dorsal onlay urethroplasty** places a tissue graft (buccal, lingual, vaginal, or labial mucosa) on the **dorsal (12 o'clock) surface** of the urethra to augment a strictured segment. It is the most evidence-supported definitive treatment for female urethral stricture (FUS), with success rates of **~ 87–95%** — substantially higher than dilation / DVIU at 41–49%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For the broader female-stricture treatment ladder, see [Female DVIU and Urethral Dilation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-dviu-dilation), [Distal Urethrectomy with Advancement Meatoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/distal-urethrectomy-advancement), [Blandy U-Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-vaginal-flap-urethroplasty), [Lateral-Based Anterior Vaginal Wall Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/lateral-based-anterior-vaginal-wall-flap), and [Ventral Onlay BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/ventral-onlay-omg). For graft tissue principles, see the [Buccal Mucosa Graft (foundations)](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa) article.

---

## Epidemiology and Etiology of FUS

FUS accounts for ~ **1% of women presenting with LUTS** and is frequently underdiagnosed.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Most common etiologies:

- **Iatrogenic** — repeated dilations, traumatic catheterization, transurethral procedures.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Idiopathic** — &gt; 50% of cases in some series.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Obstetric** — particularly cephalopelvic disproportion.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Lichen sclerosus / lichen planus**.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Pelvic trauma, radiation, urethral / vaginal atrophy, recurrent infection**.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

Mid-urethral location is most common; mean stricture length ~ 1.5–2 cm.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

---

## Diagnosis

The AUA 2023 guideline includes urethral stricture in the differential for women with decreased stream, incomplete emptying, dysuria, recurrent UTI, or rising PVR.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Workup:

- Uroflowmetry (reduced Qmax), PVR, urethral calibration (inability to pass small catheter), cystourethroscopy (failure of 16 Fr), VCUG / retrograde urethrography, video-urodynamics to differentiate anatomic stricture from functional obstruction.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[9]](#ref9)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

---

## Indications

- Recurrent / refractory FUS after failed dilation or DVIU.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Strictures of any urethral segment (distal / mid / proximal / pan-urethral).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- AUA 2023 recommends urethroplasty over endoscopic re-treatment given low endoscopic durability.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Some experts advocate **upfront urethroplasty** rather than serial dilation.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Graft Materials

| Source | Pros | Cons / Cautions |
|---|---|---|
| **Buccal mucosa** (most common) | Hairless, thick epithelium, infection-resistant, well-suited for wet environment<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | Oral donor-site morbidity (transient discomfort); cheek scarring |
| **Lingual mucosa** | Same biology as buccal; alternative oral source<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | Tongue-tip dysesthesia |
| **Vaginal mucosa (free graft)** | Same operative field; no oral morbidity<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | Avoid in LS / atrophy; vaginal-narrowing risk if harvest site poorly selected |
| **Labial mucosa** | Hairless, wet, elastic, easily accessible; **largest single-surgeon series (Jena n=204)**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | Limited data outside that center |

For the mucosa-tissue principles see [Buccal Mucosa Graft (foundations)](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa).

## Surgical Technique

Dorsal lithotomy.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[2]](#ref2)[[14]](#ref14)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

1. **Setup** — urethral catheter for identification; ± SPC.
2. **Vaginal incision** — suprameatal **inverted-U** or semi-lunar incision over the urethra.
3. **Urethral mobilization** — dissect the urethra dorsally off the underlying vaginal tissue, exposing the dorsal surface.
4. **Dorsal urethrotomy** — longitudinal **12 o'clock** incision through the entire stricture length.
5. **Graft harvest** — buccal / lingual / vaginal / labial; defat / remove fibromuscular tissue from the graft.
6. **Graft placement** — onlay sutured to both urethrotomy edges with running or interrupted absorbable sutures; tension-free anastomosis.
7. **Graft fixation** — quilting sutures to the periurethral tissue / pubic periosteum to prevent contracture and provide vascular contact.
8. **Catheter** — urethral catheter (± SPC) for **~ 3 weeks**.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

**Meatal-sparing modification (Mittal 2021)** — for mid- or proximal strictures, the urethrotomy is centered over the strictured segment, sparing the meatus. Avoids meatal widening and stream spraying.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

**Key technical tenets** — adequate dorsal dissection and mobilization; full-stricture-length incision; defat the graft; tension-free anastomosis.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> Some experts advocate **extensive grafting of the entire urethra** given the difficulty of precisely delineating stricture extent intraoperatively.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Outcomes

| Series | n | Graft | Approach | Success | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jena 2025<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 204 | Labial mucosa | Dorsal onlay | **93.5% at 2 yr** | median 29 mo |
| Richard 2021<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | 19 | Oral (buccal / lingual) | Dorsal onlay | 89.5% overall; 90.9% at 1 yr | median 12 mo |
| Higuchi 2026<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> | 8 | Buccal mucosa | Dorsal onlay | 87.5% (vs 57.1% VFU same series) | median 12 mo |
| Khawaja 2022<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | 25 | Buccal mucosa | Dorsal onlay | &gt; 90% | — |
| Savun 2026<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> | 38 (dorsal arm) | Buccal mucosa | Dorsal onlay | 92.1% patency | variable |
| Gomez 2020<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | pooled review | Buccal mucosa | Dorsal onlay | ~ 86% | mean 21 mo |

Functional improvements consistent across series: **Qmax 7.4 → 15.2 mL/s**; **PVR 161 → 72 mL** (Richard).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Mean OR time ~ 98 min; median LOS ~ 2 d.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Complications

- **De novo SUI** — 9–16% at 1–3 mo, decreasing to ~ 9% at 1 yr; most resolve with PFPT.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Stricture recurrence** — 5–7% at 1–2 yr; salvage with repeat urethroplasty.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Perioperative complications** — Clavien-Dindo II–III in ~ 3%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Urinary spraying** — more common with ventral / VFU approaches; **rare with dorsal onlay**.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Donor-site morbidity** — minimal; transient oral discomfort with BMG; vaginal-narrowing risk if free-graft harvest site is poorly selected.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

---

## Dorsal vs Ventral Onlay

Comparable success rates (~ 89–92%) across approaches.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup> Practical distinctions:

- **Dorsal** — preserves ventro-lateral urethral support important for continence; better suited for **distal strictures requiring meatal reconstruction**.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Ventral** — easier meatus preservation, wider operative field, lower intraoperative bleeding (10 vs 20 mL), vaginal-sparing.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- **No significant difference** in de-novo SUI, sexual function (FSFI), or PROs between approaches.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

---

## Guideline Position

The **AUA 2023 Urethral Stricture Disease Guideline Amendment** recommends offering urethroplasty to female patients given the low efficacy of endoscopic treatment, with success rates of **69–95%** across techniques driven by surgeon experience.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

---

## Summary

Female dorsal onlay urethroplasty — most commonly with buccal mucosa, with growing evidence for labial mucosa (Jena 2025, n = 204, 93.5%) — is the most evidence-supported definitive treatment for FUS. Success is **~ 87–95%** across modern series with low de-novo SUI (resolving to ~ 9% by 1 yr), 5–7% recurrence at 1–2 yr, and very low rates of urinary spraying compared with ventral / vaginal-flap techniques. AUA 2023 endorses offering urethroplasty over repeated endoscopic management for FUS.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
