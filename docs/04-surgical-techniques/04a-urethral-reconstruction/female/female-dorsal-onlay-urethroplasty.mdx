# Female Dorsal Onlay Urethroplasty

**Female dorsal onlay urethroplasty** places a tissue graft (buccal, lingual, vaginal, or labial mucosa) on the dorsal (12 o'clock) surface of the urethra to augment a strictured segment. It is an established reconstructive option for female urethral stricture (FUS). Reported outcomes vary by graft, population and follow-up, and current guidance does not establish superiority over ventral reconstruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For the broader female-stricture treatment ladder, see [Female DVIU and Urethral Dilation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-dviu-dilation), [Distal Urethrectomy with Advancement Meatoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/distal-urethrectomy-advancement), [Blandy U-Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-vaginal-flap-urethroplasty), [Lateral-Based Anterior Vaginal Wall Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/lateral-based-anterior-vaginal-wall-flap), and [Female Ventral Onlay BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-ventral-onlay-urethroplasty). For graft tissue principles, see the [Buccal Mucosa Graft (foundations)](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa) article.

## Epidemiology and Etiology of FUS

FUS accounts for approximately 1% of women presenting with LUTS and is frequently underdiagnosed.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Most common etiologies:

- **Iatrogenic.** Repeated dilations, traumatic catheterization, transurethral procedures.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Idiopathic.** 53.48% of cases in a 35-study review (488 patients) were presumably idiopathic.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Obstetric.** Particularly cephalopelvic disproportion.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Lichen sclerosus / lichen planus**.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Pelvic trauma, radiation, urethral / vaginal atrophy, recurrent infection**.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

Mid-urethral location is most common; mean stricture length approximately 1.5–2 cm.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

---

## Diagnosis

The AUA 2023 guideline includes urethral stricture in the differential for women with decreased stream, incomplete emptying, dysuria, recurrent UTI, or rising PVR.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Workup:

- Pelvic examination for meatal stenosis, prolapse, vulvar dermatoses and local tissue quality; uroflowmetry (reduced Qmax), PVR, urethral calibration (failure to pass a 14 Fr catheter strongly suggests stricture), cystourethroscopy when feasible, VCUG, and video-urodynamics to distinguish anatomic stricture from functional obstruction.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[10]](#ref10)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

---

## Indications

- Recurrent or refractory FUS after failed dilation or DVIU.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Strictures of any urethral segment (distal / mid / proximal / pan-urethral).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- The 2023 AUA amendment states that urethroplasty should be offered to women with stricture because endoscopic treatment has low efficacy.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Some experts advocate upfront urethroplasty over serial dilation.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Graft Materials

The operative principles of dorsal onlay (urethral mobilization, dorsal urethrotomy, graft fixation, quilting onto the periurethral bed) are the same for each tissue source. Donor choice depends on tissue availability, prior harvests and patient preference.

| Source | Pros | Cons / Cautions | Anchor evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Buccal mucosa** (most common default) | Hairless, thick epithelium, infection-resistant, well-suited for wet environment | Oral donor-site morbidity; cheek scarring | Richard 2021 (n=19, 89.5% clinical success, defined as self-reported LUTS improvement);<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Khawaja 2022 (n=25, &gt; 90%);<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Savun 2026 (n=38 dorsal arm, 92.1% by 16 Fr cystoscope passage or Qmax &gt;15 mL/s);<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> Higuchi 2026 (7/8, 87.5%, anatomically stricture-free without reintervention; VFU 4/7, 57.1%, used mainly for distal strictures; median follow-up 12 mo)<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Lingual mucosa** | Same biology as buccal; alternative oral source when buccal sites have been previously harvested | Fewer female reports than buccal; donor-site outcomes come from small series | Sharma 2010 (n=15 women; 1 submeatal stenosis at 3 mo; no neurosensory complications at 1 yr);<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> Simonato 2006 (pilot in 8 men; donor-site data)<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| **Vaginal mucosa (free graft)** | Same operative field; avoids oral harvest; an option when vaginal mucosa is suitable, including when an adequate pedicled flap cannot be mobilized.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> | Avoid in LS / atrophy; vaginal-narrowing risk if harvest site poorly selected; outcomes cannot be ranked reliably across small, heterogeneous graft series | Borchert 2022 (technique video)<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |

For mucosa-tissue principles see [Buccal Mucosa Graft (foundations)](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa), [Labial Mucosa Graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/labial-mucosa), and [Lingual Mucosa Graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/lingual-mucosa).

For free vaginal-graft harvest, Nayyar and colleagues prefer the middle anterior or posterior vaginal wall and avoid the thick, rugose anterior distal one-third, which they preserve as a plane for possible future continence surgery. This is the chapter authors' donor-site preference; head-to-head comparison of vaginal- and oral-graft take is lacking.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

## Surgical Technique

Dorsal lithotomy.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[2]](#ref2)[[16]](#ref16)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

1. **Setup.** Waterloos and Verla advise a three-month interval after dilation or urethrotomy to define a mature stricture and passage of a guidewire into the bladder to avoid a false passage during stricturotomy. Urethral catheter for identification when feasible; ± SPC.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
2. **Suprameatal incision.** A semilunar incision above the meatus exposes the dorsal urethral plane.
3. **Dorsal exposure.** Develop the plane between the dorsal urethra and clitoral bodies, protecting the sphincter and neurovascular tissues; this is distinct from ventral urethrovaginal dissection.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
4. **Dorsal urethrotomy.** Longitudinal 12 o'clock incision through the entire stricture length.
5. **Graft harvest.** Buccal, lingual, vaginal or labial; defat and remove fibromuscular tissue from the graft.
6. **Graft placement.** Place stay sutures at the opened urethral edges. Suture the onlay to both urethrotomy edges with running or interrupted absorbable sutures without tension, incorporating periurethral tissue for fixation; Waterloos and Verla begin bilateral running sutures proximally and attach the distal graft edge to the suprameatal incision.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
7. **Graft fixation.** Secure the graft to the vascular dorsal recipient bed, with mucosa facing the lumen, avoiding folds or dead space.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
8. **Catheter.** Waterloos and Verla describe a 16–20 Fr urethral catheter for two to three weeks after dorsal onlay graft repair and removal after urethrography shows no contrast extravasation. Kuo et al. also left an SPC, with both catheters in place for three weeks in their supraurethral case.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

**Recipient-bed anatomy.** The periurethral and lateral urethropelvic ligaments mark the boundary between the dorsal and ventral dissection planes, and a semicircular perimeatal incision is kept within its intended plane to avoid crossing it; anatomical studies of the female urethral support structures describe them differently.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[22]](#ref22)</sup> The proximal two-thirds of the dorsal urethra has no defined support bed of its own: the recipient bed there is loose areolar or retropubic fibrofatty tissue, while distal clitoral structures provide the bed distally.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> Nayyar and colleagues describe avoiding division of the endopelvic fascia and superior pubourethral ligament to preserve urethral suspension, avoiding far-lateral urethral mobilization, and avoiding saline hydrodissection, which in their experience makes tissue planes edematous and harder to follow.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

**Meatal-sparing modification (Mittal 2021).** For mid- or proximal strictures, the urethrotomy is centered over the strictured segment, sparing the meatus. This avoids meatal widening and stream spraying.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

**Technical tenets.** Adequate dorsal dissection and mobilization; full-stricture-length incision; defat the graft; tension-free anastomosis.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> Some experts advocate extensive grafting of the entire urethra given the difficulty of precisely delineating stricture extent intraoperatively.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

**Graft dimensions.** Reported dorsal free-graft sizes vary by author: Alvarez de Toledo harvests and fenestrates a 4 × 2 cm buccal mucosa graft, which he finds generally sufficient even for panurethral stricture given the relatively constant female urethral length, while Lec and Nitti describe a usual dorsal onlay graft of about 2 cm wide by 3–4 cm long. Neither size is a validated standard; the graft is tailored to the individual defect.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

## Outcomes

| Series | n | Graft | Approach | Success | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Richard 2021<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | 19 | Oral (buccal / lingual) | Dorsal onlay | 89.5% overall; 90.9% at 1 yr | median 12 mo |
| Higuchi 2026<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> | 8 | Buccal mucosa | Dorsal onlay | 87.5% (vs 57.1% VFU same series) | median 12 mo |
| Khawaja 2022<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | 25 | Buccal mucosa | Dorsal onlay | &gt; 90% | — |
| Savun 2026<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | 38 (dorsal arm) | Buccal mucosa | Dorsal onlay | 92.1% (16 Fr scope passage or Qmax &gt;15 mL/s) | variable |
| Gomez 2020<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | pooled review | Buccal mucosa | Dorsal onlay | ~ 86% | mean 21 mo |
| Jena 2025<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 204 | Labial mucosa | Dorsal onlay | 93.5% at 2 yr | median 29 mo |

In the Richard series, Qmax rose from 7.4 to 15.2 mL/s and PVR fell from 161 to 72 mL.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Success definitions differ across series: self-assessed LUTS improvement at 1–3 months (Richard), passage of a 16 Fr cystoscope or Qmax &gt;15 mL/s (Savun), and recurrence assessed by symptoms, Qmax/PVR and failure to pass a 17 Fr cystoscope (Khawaja).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[12]](#ref12)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Complications

- **De novo SUI** was reported in 15.7% at 1–3 months and 9.1% at one year in the Richard dorsal-onlay series.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> A separate review pooling different female-stricture operations reported that most mild-to-moderate postoperative incontinence improved with pelvic floor exercises.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Stricture recurrence.** Richard reported 1/19 (5.3%) at a median 12 months; Jena reported 6.5% at two years, including four repeat urethroplasties. In a multicenter cohort (n = 165; 33 dorsal grafts), two-year stricture-free survival after dorsal graft was 80% (median follow-up 29 months overall), and prior radiotherapy was independently associated with recurrence across all procedures (p = 0.015).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[31]](#ref31)</sup>
- **Perioperative complications** of Clavien-Dindo grade 2–3 occurred in 2.94% of the Jena series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Urinary spraying** occurred in 2 of 7 women after vaginal flap urethroplasty and in none after dorsal onlay in the Higuchi series.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Donor-site morbidity.** Buccal harvest can cause oral pain, numbness or mouth tightness; tightness can persist beyond six months in broader urethroplasty donor-site studies. The 2026 EAU guideline recommends counseling about potential complications of oral graft harvest (Strong).<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup> Free vaginal-graft harvest can narrow the vagina if the donor site is poorly selected.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Bleeding during dorsal dissection.** Purohit and Blaivas advise against blind coagulation or suture ligature for troublesome bleeding and instead pack the retropubic space with gauze between the dorsal urethra and pubis for compression; in their experience this has not required retropubic exploration from above.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

## Dorsal vs Ventral Onlay

The cited studies report success rates of approximately 89–93% across dorsal and ventral approaches, with different success definitions and follow-up periods; they do not establish superiority.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[18]](#ref18)[[19]](#ref19)</sup> In one single-center randomized trial, 19/20 women analyzed in each dorsal-onlay and ventral-inlay arm met the one-year success endpoint (47 randomized; median follow-up 21 months); the ventral-inlay arm had less blood loss, shorter surgery and less pain at six and 24 hours.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup> A 2025 review found no significant success difference in four comparative cohorts (62/69 dorsal versus 93/103 ventral; OR 0.84, 95% CI 0.30–2.36); 21/25 included studies were level 4 evidence.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup> Practical distinctions:

- **Dorsal.** The dorsal approach is thought to leave the ventro-lateral urethral support intact, and it is better suited to distal strictures requiring meatal reconstruction.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Ventral.** The ventral approach allows easier meatus preservation and a wider operative field, with lower intraoperative bleeding in a small trial. Only specific inlay variants are vaginal-sparing.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[18]](#ref18)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- A multicenter retrospective comparison found no significant difference in de novo SUI, sexual function (FSFI) or patient-reported impression of change between approaches.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Guideline Position

The 2023 AUA Urethral Stricture Disease Guideline Amendment (Statement 26; Moderate Recommendation, Evidence Level C) states that surgeons may reconstruct female strictures with oral mucosal grafts, vaginal flaps or a combination. The text adds that urethroplasty should be offered given the low efficacy of endoscopic treatment. It reports success rates of 69–95% across techniques, chosen by surgeon experience.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> The 2026 EAU guideline recommends initial dilation (Weak); at first recurrence, repeat dilation with weekly intermittent self-dilation using a 16–18 Fr catheter or planned repeat dilation (Weak); and at second recurrence, urethroplasty when self-dilation is unsuitable or definitive treatment is desired (Strong).<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup>

---

## Summary

Dorsal onlay is one reconstructive option for FUS. Graft and approach are chosen by local anatomy, donor tissue and patient goals. The small series above do not show superior patency, continence or sexual outcomes over ventral techniques.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
