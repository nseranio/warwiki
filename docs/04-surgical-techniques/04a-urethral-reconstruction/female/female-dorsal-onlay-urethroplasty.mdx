# Female Dorsal Onlay Urethroplasty

**Female dorsal onlay urethroplasty** places a tissue graft (buccal, lingual, vaginal, or labial mucosa) on the dorsal (12 o'clock) surface of the urethra to augment a strictured segment. It is an established reconstructive option for female urethral stricture (FUS). Reported outcomes vary by graft, population and follow-up; current guidance does not establish universal superiority over ventral reconstruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For the broader female-stricture treatment ladder, see [Female DVIU and Urethral Dilation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-dviu-dilation), [Distal Urethrectomy with Advancement Meatoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/distal-urethrectomy-advancement), [Blandy U-Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-vaginal-flap-urethroplasty), [Lateral-Based Anterior Vaginal Wall Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/lateral-based-anterior-vaginal-wall-flap), and [Female Ventral Onlay BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-ventral-onlay-urethroplasty). For graft tissue principles, see the [Buccal Mucosa Graft (foundations)](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa) article.

## Epidemiology and Etiology of FUS

FUS accounts for approximately 1% of women presenting with LUTS and is frequently underdiagnosed.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Most common etiologies:

- **Iatrogenic.** Repeated dilations, traumatic catheterization, transurethral procedures.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Idiopathic.** &gt; 50% of cases in some series.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Obstetric.** Particularly cephalopelvic disproportion.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Lichen sclerosus / lichen planus**.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Pelvic trauma, radiation, urethral / vaginal atrophy, recurrent infection**.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

Mid-urethral location is most common; mean stricture length approximately 1.5–2 cm.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

---

## Diagnosis

The AUA 2023 guideline includes urethral stricture in the differential for women with decreased stream, incomplete emptying, dysuria, recurrent UTI, or rising PVR.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Workup:

- Uroflowmetry (reduced Qmax), PVR, urethral calibration (inability to pass small catheter), cystourethroscopy (failure of 16 Fr), VCUG / retrograde urethrography, video-urodynamics to differentiate anatomic stricture from functional obstruction.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[10]](#ref10)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

---

## Indications

- Recurrent or refractory FUS after failed dilation or DVIU.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Strictures of any urethral segment (distal / mid / proximal / pan-urethral).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- AUA 2023 recommends urethroplasty over endoscopic re-treatment given low endoscopic durability.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Some experts advocate upfront urethroplasty rather than serial dilation.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

---

## Graft Materials

The dorsal onlay technique is donor-source agnostic: the operative principles (urethral mobilization, dorsal urethrotomy, graft fixation, quilting onto the periurethral bed) are identical regardless of the tissue source. The choice of donor is driven by tissue availability, prior harvests, and patient preference.

| Source | Pros | Cons / Cautions | Anchor evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Buccal mucosa** (most common default) | Hairless, thick epithelium, infection-resistant, well-suited for wet environment | Oral donor-site morbidity (transient discomfort); cheek scarring | Richard 2021 (n=19, 89.5%);<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Khawaja 2022 (n=25, > 90%);<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Savun 2026 (n=38 dorsal arm, 92.1%);<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> Higuchi 2026 (n=8, 87.5% vs 57.1% VFU)<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Lingual mucosa** | Same biology as buccal; alternative oral source when buccal sites have been previously harvested | Tongue-tip dysesthesia; smaller series than buccal | Simonato 2006 pilot;<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> Goel 2014 — direct comparison with labial mucosa showed equivalent outcomes<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| **Vaginal mucosa (free graft)** | Same operative field; no oral morbidity; reserved when a vaginal pedicled flap cannot be safely rotated (prior vaginal surgery, compromised lateral pedicle) | Avoid in LS / atrophy; vaginal-narrowing risk if harvest site poorly selected; outcomes cannot be ranked reliably across small, heterogeneous graft series | Borchert 2022 narrative review<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |

For mucosa-tissue principles see [Buccal Mucosa Graft (foundations)](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa), [Labial Mucosa Graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/labial-mucosa), and [Lingual Mucosa Graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/lingual-mucosa).

## Surgical Technique

Dorsal lithotomy.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[2]](#ref2)[[16]](#ref16)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

1. **Setup.** Urethral catheter for identification; ± SPC.
2. **Suprameatal incision.** A semilunar incision above the meatus exposes the dorsal urethral plane.
3. **Dorsal exposure.** Develop the plane between the dorsal urethra and clitoral bodies, protecting the sphincter and neurovascular tissues; this is distinct from ventral urethrovaginal dissection.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
4. **Dorsal urethrotomy.** Longitudinal 12 o'clock incision through the entire stricture length.
5. **Graft harvest.** Buccal, lingual, vaginal or labial; defat and remove fibromuscular tissue from the graft.
6. **Graft placement.** Onlay sutured to both urethrotomy edges with running or interrupted absorbable sutures; tension-free anastomosis.
7. **Graft fixation.** Secure the graft to the vascular dorsal recipient bed, with mucosa facing the lumen, avoiding folds or dead space.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
8. **Catheter.** Urethral catheter (± SPC) for approximately 3 weeks.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

**Meatal-sparing modification (Mittal 2021).** For mid- or proximal strictures, the urethrotomy is centered over the strictured segment, sparing the meatus. Avoids meatal widening and stream spraying.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

**Technical tenets.** Adequate dorsal dissection and mobilization; full-stricture-length incision; defat the graft; tension-free anastomosis.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> Some experts advocate extensive grafting of the entire urethra given the difficulty of precisely delineating stricture extent intraoperatively.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Outcomes

| Series | n | Graft | Approach | Success | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Richard 2021<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | 19 | Oral (buccal / lingual) | Dorsal onlay | 89.5% overall; 90.9% at 1 yr | median 12 mo |
| Higuchi 2026<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> | 8 | Buccal mucosa | Dorsal onlay | 87.5% (vs 57.1% VFU same series) | median 12 mo |
| Khawaja 2022<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | 25 | Buccal mucosa | Dorsal onlay | &gt; 90% | — |
| Savun 2026<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | 38 (dorsal arm) | Buccal mucosa | Dorsal onlay | 92.1% patency | variable |
| Gomez 2020<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | pooled review | Buccal mucosa | Dorsal onlay | ~ 86% | mean 21 mo |

Functional improvements were consistent across series: Qmax rose from 7.4 to 15.2 mL/s and PVR fell from 161 to 72 mL (Richard).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Complications

- **De novo SUI.** 9–16% at 1–3 mo, decreasing to approximately 9% at 1 yr; most resolve with PFPT.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Stricture recurrence.** 5–7% at 1–2 yr; salvage with repeat urethroplasty.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Perioperative complications.** Clavien-Dindo II–III in approximately 3%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Urinary spraying.** More common with ventral or VFU approaches; rare with dorsal onlay.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Donor-site morbidity.** Minimal; transient oral discomfort with BMG; vaginal-narrowing risk if free-graft harvest site is poorly selected.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

---

## Dorsal vs Ventral Onlay

Comparable success rates (approximately 89–92%) across approaches.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[18]](#ref18)[[19]](#ref19)</sup> Practical distinctions:

- **Dorsal.** Preserves ventro-lateral urethral support important for continence; better suited for distal strictures requiring meatal reconstruction.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Ventral.** Easier meatus preservation, wider operative field, lower intraoperative bleeding in a small trial; only specific inlay variants are vaginal-sparing.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[18]](#ref18)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- No significant difference in de-novo SUI, sexual function (FSFI), or PROs between approaches.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

---

## Guideline Position

The AUA 2023 Urethral Stricture Disease Guideline Amendment recommends offering urethroplasty to female patients given the low efficacy of endoscopic treatment, with success rates of 69–95% across techniques driven by surgeon experience.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

---

## Summary

Dorsal onlay is one effective reconstructive option. Select the graft and approach using local anatomy, donor tissue and patient goals. The small series above do not establish superior patency, continence or sexual outcomes over all ventral techniques. AUA guidance supports urethroplasty for FUS because endoscopic durability is limited.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
