# Combined Vaginal Flap + BMG Urethroplasty (Female)

**Combined vaginal flap and BMG urethroplasty** refers to a reconstructive strategy in which a vaginal flap (pedicled, vascularized tissue) and a buccal mucosal graft (free graft) are used together to address complex female urethral strictures that a single tissue source may not manage adequately. The AUA Urethral Stricture Disease Guideline Amendment (2023) states that surgeons may reconstruct female strictures using oral mucosal grafts, vaginal flaps or a combination of these techniques (Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C). The guideline reports success rates of 69–95% across these approaches.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The combined approach is not a single standardized technique. Several distinct configurations are described below.

## Concept and Rationale

Most female urethral strictures are managed with a single tissue source, either a vaginal flap or graft or a BMG alone. A combination has been used when one tissue source seems insufficient because of stricture complexity, tissue quality or anatomical constraints. The rationale is to use the complementary advantages of each tissue.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- **Vaginal flap.** A vaginal flap is well vascularized, is readily available in the same operative field and allows a dorsal or ventral urethroplasty. Coexisting lichen sclerosus or vaginal atrophy may compromise the tissue.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **BMG.** BMG is non-hair-bearing oral donor tissue that avoids diseased genital skin, including tissue affected by lichen sclerosus or vaginal atrophy. It still requires a viable recipient bed and can fail or develop recurrent disease.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Clinical Configurations

### 1. BMG Urethroplasty with Vaginal Flap as Vascular Reinforcement

Ventral BMG with periurethral fascial flaps is a distinct configuration. The flaps are fascia, not vaginal epithelial flaps. In the ventral onlay BMG technique of Berdondini et al. (n = 42, single surgeon), a longitudinal incision is made through the anterior vaginal wall and the periurethral fascia is incised to create two flaps. The ventral urethrotomy runs from the meatus into healthy proximal urethra. The BMG is sutured to the urethral mucosal margins and to the fascial flaps, and the vaginal wall is closed. At a mean follow-up of 38.1 mo, 41 of 42 patients (98%) were stricture-free. The authors consider the periurethral fascia a vascular and mechanical support for the graft.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### 2. Martius Flap–Reinforced Ventral BMG Onlay

Önol et al. described a Martius flap–reinforced ventral BMG onlay in 2 of 17 patients with prior synthetic midurethral slings. The BMG provides the mucosal lining, and a [Martius (labial fat pad) flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/martius) is interposed for a vascular bed and reinforcement.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> See [Female Ventral Onlay Urethroplasty — Martius-Reinforced Variant](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-ventral-onlay-urethroplasty) for the canonical operative description.

### 3. Sequential or Staged Vaginal Flap → BMG for Recurrent Stricture

Blaivas et al. reported 9 women who underwent vaginal flap urethroplasty. There was no recurrence at a minimum of 1 year, but 2 strictures recurred at 5.5 and 6 years. Both women received a BMG and were stricture-free 12–15 months later; the authors cite success of 100% at 1 year and 78% at 5 years.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> This is a combination across two operations, not within one.

### Limits of the Combination Label

A periurethral fascial flap, a vaginal epithelial flap and a Martius labial fat-pad flap are different tissues, so fascial coverage of a BMG or Martius interposition is not a vaginal-flap-plus-BMG augmentation. Salvage BMG after a previous vaginal repair is a sequence of operations, not a single combined procedure.

A two-surface reconstruction has been described with BMG alone. In the transurethral dorsal inlay series of Jefferson et al. (n = 21), 2 patients with obliterative strictures received combined dorsal and ventral inlay grafts.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> The AUA guideline permits combinations of techniques but does not describe a segmental or two-surface vaginal flap plus BMG operation. No source cited here gives a step-by-step operation that combines a dorsal vaginal flap with a ventral BMG. A directly documented technique suited to the defect is the safer basis for operative planning.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Outcomes

No large series has specifically isolated the combined vaginal flap and BMG approach as a distinct cohort. Available evidence comes from series that include combined cases within larger mixed-technique cohorts.

| Study | Configuration | n | Follow-up | Success |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Önol 2011<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | Martius-reinforced ventral BMG | 2 (of 17) | median 24 mo | 100% objective cure (entire cohort) |
| Blaivas 2012<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | Sequential VFU → BMG salvage | 2 (of 17) | 12–15 mo post-BMG | 2/2 stricture-free |
| Berdondini 2024<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | Ventral BMG + periurethral fascia flaps | 42 | mean 38.1 mo | 98% (41/42) |
| AUA 2023<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Oral mucosa, vaginal flap, or combination (pooled) | — | variable | **69–95%** |

## Selecting Tissue and Coverage

The urethral lining and the vascularized coverage are chosen separately. Adequate oral mucosa may reconstruct a long stricture without a second lining tissue. Scarred or deficient periurethral tissue may call for additional vascularized coverage, and unhealthy vaginal tissue can limit a vaginal flap. Extent of disease, recipient viability, prior surgery and continence goals guide the plan. No validated hierarchy assigns a named combination to each scenario.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Comparative Evidence — BMG vs Vaginal Tissue (Single-Tissue)

A 2025 meta-analysis (Kumar et al., 18 studies) found no significant differences between BMG and vaginal-wall graft urethroplasty in success rate (86.2% versus 89.8%), Qmax (23.3 versus 24.9 mL/s), or PVR (14.7 versus 23.0 mL). Heterogeneity was I² = 0% for Qmax and success rate and high (I² > 50%) for PVR. The included studies were retrospective, prospective and comparative cohorts.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> The authors conclude that graft choice should rest on graft availability, patient characteristics and surgeon preference and expertise.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

When vaginal flap (pedicled) is compared directly to dorsal BMG (free graft), Higuchi et al. (retrospective, 15 women) reported success of 87.5% (7/8) for dorsal onlay BMG versus 57.1% (4/7) for vaginal flap at median 12 mo. The vaginal flap was mainly used for distal strictures, whereas BMG was used in any segment, including pan-urethral disease.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Kowalik et al. retrospectively reviewed 10 women. Two of those treated with a vaginal flap had a recurrent stricture requiring dilation, and none of those treated with dorsal BMG did.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Advantages and Limitations

**Advantages**

- Maximizes tissue availability for complex / long strictures.
- Provides vascular support for free-graft take.
- Allows BMG when vaginal tissue is compromised.
- Enables reconstruction of multiple urethral surfaces.
- Salvage option when single-tissue repair fails.

**Limitations**

- Greater surgical complexity and operative time.
- Two tissue harvest sites (oral and vaginal).
- Limited evidence base as a distinct technique.
- Potential donor-site morbidity at both sites.
- Vaginal narrowing if excessive tissue harvested.
- No standardized technique description.

## Summary

The combined vaginal flap and BMG approach is a reconstructive principle and has no single standardized technique. It has been used when one tissue source is insufficient because of stricture length, tissue quality, prior surgery or obliterative disease. The AUA 2023 guideline allows combinations alongside single-tissue approaches, and the pooled female urethroplasty outcomes do not give a distinct success rate for each combination.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The cited reinforcement configurations are BMG augmentation with periurethral fascia or a Martius labial fat pad (Berdondini ventral onlay, 41/42; Önol Martius-reinforced ventral BMG, 2 patients within a cohort of 17 with 100% objective cure).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
