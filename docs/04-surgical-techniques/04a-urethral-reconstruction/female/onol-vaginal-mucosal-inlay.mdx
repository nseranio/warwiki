# Anterior Vaginal Wall Mucosal Inlay (Önol)

The **anterior vaginal wall mucosal inlay urethroplasty** is a **ventral augmentation** technique for female urethral stricture (FUS) that opens the urethra ventrally and inlays a pedicled strip of anterior vaginal wall mucosa into the urethrotomy to widen the lumen. The technique most closely associated with this terminology is the one described by **Önol 2011**, who used it in 10 of 17 women undergoing urethroplasty.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> A simplified variant was described by **Schwender / Gormley 2006**.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

For the family of related anterior-vaginal-wall techniques, see the [Blandy U-Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-vaginal-flap-urethroplasty) and the [Lateral-Based Anterior Vaginal Wall Flap (Romero-Maroto / Simonato)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/lateral-based-anterior-vaginal-wall-flap). For graft alternatives, see [Female Dorsal Onlay BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-dorsal-onlay-urethroplasty) and [Ventral Onlay BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/ventral-onlay-omg).

---

## Where the Önol Inlay Sits in the Family

"Anterior vaginal wall mucosal inlay" is a family of related techniques sharing one principle: **ventral urethrotomy with inlay of anterior vaginal wall tissue to augment the urethral lumen**. Variants:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- **Blandy U-flap** — proximally based U-shaped pedicled flap (classic).
- **Lateral-based vaginal flap (Romero-Maroto / Simonato)** — laterally pedicled modification.
- **Gormley / Schwender vaginal inlay flap** — simplified ventral inlay approach.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Önol anterior vaginal wall mucosal inlay** — ventral pedicled flap inlay; the largest single-technique series using this specific terminology.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Borchert vaginal free graft (dorsal onlay)** — non-pedicled graft placed dorsally; same tissue source, different placement.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

The Önol inlay is distinguished from a dorsal-onlay graft (different urethral surface) and from a tubularized vaginal flap such as the Blaivas neourethra (rolled into a tube rather than inlaid).

---

## Indications

- FUS refractory to dilation or DVIU.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Mid-to-distal urethral strictures** — most common location.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Healthy, well-estrogenized anterior vaginal wall.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- Can be used as **primary urethroplasty** — Önol notably advocated this as a first-line option over repeated dilation, performing primary urethroplasty in 10 of 17 patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Contraindications

- **Lichen sclerosus / vaginal atrophy** — compromised tissue; BMG preferred. Önol used dorsal BMG in 2 patients with atrophic vaginas.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- Prior **synthetic midurethral sling** with periurethral scarring — Önol used Martius-reinforced ventral BMG in this scenario.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Önol Technique

Dorsal lithotomy, regional or general anesthesia.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

1. **Preoperative assessment** — pressure-flow studies confirming bladder-outlet obstruction (urethral pressure &gt; 20 cm H₂O during voiding). Cystourethroscopy to confirm stricture location and length.
2. **Anterior vaginal wall incision** — midline longitudinal incision overlying the urethra; vaginal mucosa dissected laterally off the periurethral tissue to expose the ventral urethral surface.
3. **Ventral urethrotomy** — longitudinal **6 o'clock** incision through the full length of the stricture into healthy mucosa proximally and distally.
4. **Vaginal mucosal flap creation** — flap fashioned from anterior vaginal wall adjacent to the urethrotomy. The flap remains **pedicled** (attached to its submucosal vascular supply) — this is the key distinction from a free graft.
5. **Inlay** — flap inlaid into the ventral urethrotomy defect and sutured to the urethral mucosa with interrupted 4-0 / 5-0 polyglactin, augmenting the lumen ventrally.
6. **Proximal urethral mucosa adjunct** — in one Önol patient, the dilated proximal urethral mucosa was used as an additional tissue source, illustrating the technique's adaptability.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
7. **Second-layer closure** — vaginal wall closed as a second layer over the repair.
8. **Catheter** — postoperative urethral catheter.

**Technical pearls** — keep the inlay pedicled (preserves blood supply); the ventral approach preserves the dorsal urethral surface and pubourethral ligaments.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> Schwender / Gormley specifically emphasized the technique's simplicity and learnability.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Schwender / Gormley Variant (J Urol 2006)

A closely related simplified ventral inlay:<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

- Ventral aspect of the stricture incised longitudinally.
- Vaginal inlay flap advanced into the urethrotomy defect and sutured to urethral edges.
- 8 women, 2 institutions, 1–9 yr follow-up.
- **100% subjective symptom relief**; caliber 9.25 → 16.5 Fr; PVR 130 → 15 cc; **0% de novo SUI**; 1 patient required a single repeat dilation 3 weeks postop without subsequent recurrence.

## Pedicled Inlay vs Vaginal Free Graft

| Feature | Pedicled Vaginal Inlay (Önol / Gormley) | Vaginal Free Graft (Borchert) |
|---|---|---|
| Blood supply | Maintains own pedicle | Relies on recipient bed (imbibition → inosculation) |
| Tissue viability | Higher — independent supply | Recipient-bed dependent |
| Placement | Ventral | Dorsal (Borchert technique) |
| Tissue handling | Less manipulation; stays attached | Harvest, prepare, transplant |
| Vaginal narrowing risk | Minimal (in situ) | Possible if graft large |
| Behavior in scarred beds | Better — own blood supply | May fail in poorly vascularized beds |
| Donor-site morbidity | None | Minimal (vaginal harvest site) |

**Borchert 2022** described the free-graft variant: the vaginal mucosa is harvested separately, the underlying fibromuscular tissue is removed, and the thin mucosal graft is placed dorsally after dorsal urethrotomy. Key tenets are adequate dorsal dissection, full-length stricture incision, and tension-free anastomosis. Harvest-site selection is critical to avoid vaginal narrowing.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Inlay vs Other Urethroplasty Tissue Sources

| Technique | Tissue / approach | Reported success | Key advantage | Key limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Anterior vaginal wall mucosal inlay (pedicled) | Anterior vaginal wall, ventral | 92–100%<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | Own blood supply; no donor-site morbidity; simple | Tissue-quality dependent; spraying risk |
| Vaginal free graft (dorsal onlay) | Anterior vaginal wall, dorsal | variable<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | Avoids BMG harvest; dorsal placement | Recipient-bed dependent; vaginal narrowing risk |
| Dorsal onlay BMG | Buccal mucosa, dorsal | 87–94%<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> | Versatile; any location; LS-resistant | Oral donor-site morbidity |
| Ventral onlay BMG | Buccal mucosa, ventral | 89–98%<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> | Preserves sphincter; spongiosal support | Oral donor-site morbidity |
| Dorsal onlay labial mucosa | Labia minora, dorsal | 93.5% at 2 yr (Jena n = 200+)<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> | Largest single-surgeon series; minimal donor morbidity | Limited data outside that center |

Kumar 2025 SR / meta of 18 BMG-vs-VWG studies found **no significant difference** in success (BMG 86.2% vs VWG 89.8%), Qmax, or PVR.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> AUA 2023 frames urethroplasty success in women as 69–95% across techniques, with selection driven by surgeon experience.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

---

## Advantages

1. **No donor-site morbidity** — same operative field; no oral wound, no cheek / lip numbness, no trismus.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
2. **Pedicled blood supply** — better than free graft in compromised beds.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
3. **Technical simplicity** — Schwender / Gormley specifically emphasized this as an advantage given the rarity of FUS and limited surgical experience.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
4. **Single operative field** — no second team or harvest site.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
5. **Tissue compatibility** — vaginal mucosa is wet, hairless, elastic, histologically similar to urethral mucosa.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
6. **Primary-urethroplasty option** — Önol used it as a first-line operation in patients without prior instrumentation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Limitations and Concerns

- **Tissue-quality dependence** — contraindicated in LS, vaginal atrophy, prior radiation; BMG preferred.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Urinary spraying** — 29% (2 / 7) in Higuchi 2026 VFU vs 0% in DOBMGU (ventral-approach concern).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Retrusive meatus** — classic Blandy variant carries this risk; the lateral-based modification eliminates it.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Lower success in some VFU series** — Higuchi 2026: 57.1% (VFU) vs 87.5% (DOBMGU); Kowalik 2014: 60% (VFU) vs 100% (dorsal BMG). These pooled mixed VFU techniques (not exclusively Önol-style ventral inlay).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- **Limited applicability for proximal / pan-urethral** — most ventral-inlay series focus on distal-to-mid; BMG is more versatile.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Small evidence base** — all series are small (6–17 patients), retrospective, single-center.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

---

## Complications

- **Stricture recurrence** — 0–43% across series and follow-up windows.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- **De novo SUI** — 0–14% across vaginal-flap series; mostly mild and PFPT-responsive.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- **Urinary spraying** — ventral-approach-specific.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **De novo urgency** — reported even with technically successful repairs.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Urethrovaginal fistula** — rare; reduced by multi-layer closure ± Martius interposition.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## When to Choose the Önol Inlay

- Stricture is **distal or mid-urethral**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Anterior vaginal wall is **healthy and well-estrogenized** (no LS).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- Surgeon prefers to avoid BMG donor-site morbidity.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- A **pedicled** flap with own blood supply is preferred over a free graft.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **No prior synthetic MUS** (periurethral scarring limits flap mobilization).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Primary urethroplasty** is being considered as a definitive first-line treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Postoperative Management

- Urethral catheter **10–21 days**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- Voiding trial with uroflowmetry and PVR.
- Cystoscopy at 3–6 mo.
- Topical estrogen in postmenopausal women.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- Long-term surveillance — late recurrences documented.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

---

## Summary

The Önol anterior vaginal wall mucosal inlay is a technically straightforward **ventral augmentation** urethroplasty that uses a pedicled strip of anterior vaginal wall mucosa to widen the lumen, with **100% objective cure** at 24-month median follow-up in the index series and pooled local-flap success of ~ 92%. Its strengths are technical simplicity, no donor-site morbidity, and a pedicled blood supply. Its limitations are dependence on healthy vaginal tissue, the ventral-approach spraying risk, and lower comparative success in some small head-to-head series against dorsal BMG. **Patient selection is critical**; BMG urethroplasty remains the preferred alternative when vaginal tissue quality is compromised.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)[[17]](#ref17)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
