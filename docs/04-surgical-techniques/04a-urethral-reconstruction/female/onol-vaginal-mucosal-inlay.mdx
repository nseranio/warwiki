# Anterior Vaginal Wall Mucosal Inlay (Önol)

The **anterior vaginal wall mucosal inlay urethroplasty** is a ventral augmentation technique for female urethral stricture (FUS) that opens the urethra ventrally and inlays a pedicled strip of anterior vaginal wall mucosa into the urethrotomy to widen the lumen. The technique most closely associated with this terminology is the one described by Önol 2011, who used it in 10 of 17 women undergoing urethroplasty.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> A simplified variant was described by Schwender / Gormley 2006.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

For the family of related anterior-vaginal-wall techniques, see the [Blandy U-Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-vaginal-flap-urethroplasty) and the [Lateral-Based Anterior Vaginal Wall Flap (Romero-Maroto / Simonato)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/lateral-based-anterior-vaginal-wall-flap). For graft alternatives, see [Female Dorsal Onlay BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-dorsal-onlay-urethroplasty) and [Ventral Onlay BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-ventral-onlay-urethroplasty).

---

## Where the Önol Inlay Sits in the Family

"Anterior vaginal wall mucosal inlay" is a family of related techniques sharing one principle: ventral urethrotomy with inlay of anterior vaginal wall tissue to augment the urethral lumen. Variants:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- **Blandy U-flap.** Proximally based U-shaped pedicled flap (classic).
- **Lateral-based vaginal flap (Romero-Maroto / Simonato).** Laterally pedicled modification.
- **Gormley / Schwender vaginal inlay flap.** Simplified ventral inlay approach.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Önol anterior vaginal wall mucosal inlay.** Ventral pedicled flap inlay; the largest single-technique series using this specific terminology.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Borchert vaginal free graft (dorsal onlay).** Non-pedicled graft placed dorsally; same tissue source, different placement.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

The Önol inlay is distinguished from a dorsal-onlay graft (different urethral surface) and from a tubularized vaginal flap such as the Blaivas neourethra (rolled into a tube rather than inlaid).

---

## Indications

- FUS refractory to dilation or DVIU.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Mid-to-distal urethral strictures.** Most common location.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- Healthy, well-estrogenized anterior vaginal wall.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- Can be used as primary urethroplasty; Önol advocated this as a first-line option over repeated dilation, performing primary urethroplasty in 10 of 17 patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Contraindications

- **Lichen sclerosus or vaginal atrophy.** Compromised tissue; BMG preferred. Önol used dorsal BMG in 2 patients with atrophic vaginas.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- Prior synthetic midurethral sling with periurethral scarring. Önol used Martius-reinforced ventral BMG in this scenario.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Önol Technique

Dorsal lithotomy, regional or general anesthesia.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

1. **Preoperative assessment.** Integrated anatomical and functional assessment. The original series used detrusor pressure during voiding &gt; 20 cm H₂O alongside caliber and flow criteria; this is not a urethral-pressure threshold or a universal diagnostic cutoff. Cystourethroscopy to confirm stricture location and length.
2. **Anterior vaginal wall incision.** Midline longitudinal incision overlying the urethra; vaginal mucosa dissected laterally off the periurethral tissue to expose the ventral urethral surface.
3. **Ventral urethrotomy.** Longitudinal 6 o'clock incision through the full length of the stricture into healthy mucosa proximally and distally.
4. **Vaginal mucosal flap creation.** Flap fashioned from anterior vaginal wall adjacent to the urethrotomy. The flap remains pedicled (attached to its submucosal vascular supply), which distinguishes it from a free graft.
5. **Inlay.** Flap inlaid into the ventral urethrotomy defect and sutured to the urethral mucosa with interrupted 4-0 or 5-0 polyglactin, augmenting the lumen ventrally.
6. **Proximal urethral mucosa adjunct.** In one Önol patient, the dilated proximal urethral mucosa was used as an additional tissue source, illustrating the technique's adaptability.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
7. **Second-layer closure.** Vaginal wall closed as a second layer over the repair.
8. **Catheter.** Postoperative urethral catheter.

**Technical pearls.** Keep the inlay pedicled (preserves blood supply); the ventral approach preserves the dorsal urethral surface and pubourethral ligaments.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Schwender / Gormley specifically emphasized the technique's simplicity and learnability.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Schwender / Gormley Variant (J Urol 2006)

A closely related simplified ventral inlay:<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- Ventral aspect of the stricture incised longitudinally.
- Vaginal inlay flap advanced into the urethrotomy defect and sutured to urethral edges.
- 8 women, 2 institutions, 1–9 yr follow-up.
- 100% subjective symptom relief; caliber rose from 9.25 to 16.5 Fr; PVR fell from 130 to 15 cc; 0% de novo SUI; 1 patient required a single repeat dilation 3 weeks postop without subsequent recurrence.

## Pedicled Inlay vs Vaginal Free Graft

| Feature | Pedicled Vaginal Inlay (Önol / Gormley) | Vaginal Free Graft (Borchert) |
|---|---|---|
| Blood supply | Maintains own pedicle | Relies on recipient bed (imbibition → inosculation) |
| Tissue viability | Higher, from independent supply | Recipient-bed dependent |
| Placement | Ventral | Dorsal (Borchert technique) |
| Tissue handling | Less manipulation; stays attached | Harvest, prepare, transplant |
| Vaginal narrowing risk | Minimal (in situ) | Possible if graft large |
| Behavior in scarred beds | Better — own blood supply | May fail in poorly vascularized beds |
| Donor-site morbidity | None | Minimal (vaginal harvest site) |

**Borchert 2022** described the free-graft variant: the vaginal mucosa is harvested separately, the underlying fibromuscular tissue is removed, and the thin mucosal graft is placed dorsally after dorsal urethrotomy. Tenets are adequate dorsal dissection, full-length stricture incision, and tension-free anastomosis. Harvest-site selection avoids excessive vaginal narrowing.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Inlay vs Other Urethroplasty Tissue Sources

| Technique | Tissue / approach | Reported success | Advantage | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Anterior vaginal wall mucosal inlay (pedicled) | Anterior vaginal wall, ventral | 92–100%<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | Own blood supply; no donor-site morbidity; simple | Tissue-quality dependent; spraying risk |
| Vaginal free graft (dorsal onlay) | Anterior vaginal wall, dorsal | variable<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | Avoids BMG harvest; dorsal placement | Recipient-bed dependent; vaginal narrowing risk |
| Dorsal onlay BMG | Buccal mucosa, dorsal | 87–94%<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> | Oral donor tissue avoids genital skin; recipient LS can still recur | Oral donor-site morbidity |
| Ventral onlay BMG | Buccal mucosa, ventral | 89–98%<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> | Preserves sphincter; spongiosal support | Oral donor-site morbidity |

Kumar 2025 SR / meta of 18 BMG-versus-VWG studies found no significant difference in success (BMG 86.2% versus VWG 89.8%), Qmax, or PVR.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> The AUA 2023 amendment states that surgeons may reconstruct female urethral strictures with oral mucosal grafts, vaginal flaps or a combination (Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level Grade C). It reports success rates of 69–95% across these techniques and notes that technique selection rests on surgeon experience.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

## Advantages

1. **No oral donor-site morbidity.** A vaginal donor wound remains; no oral wound, no cheek / lip numbness, no trismus.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
2. **Pedicled blood supply.** The flap keeps its own blood supply, which has been proposed as an advantage over a free graft in compromised beds.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
3. **Technical simplicity.** Schwender / Gormley specifically emphasized this as an advantage given the rarity of FUS and limited surgical experience.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
4. **Single operative field.** No second team or harvest site.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
5. **Tissue compatibility.** Vaginal mucosa is wet, hairless, elastic, histologically similar to urethral mucosa.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
6. **Primary-urethroplasty option.** Önol used it as a first-line operation in patients without prior instrumentation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Limitations and Concerns

- **Tissue-quality dependence.** Contraindicated in LS, vaginal atrophy, prior radiation; BMG preferred.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Urinary spraying.** 29% (2 / 7) in Higuchi 2026 VFU versus 0% in DOBMGU (ventral-approach concern).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Retrusive meatus.** Classic Blandy variant carries this risk; the lateral-based modification aims to reduce it.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Lower success in some VFU series.** Higuchi 2026: 57.1% (VFU) versus 87.5% (DOBMGU); Kowalik 2014: 60% (VFU) versus 100% (dorsal BMG). These pooled mixed VFU techniques (not exclusively Önol-style ventral inlay).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **Limited applicability for proximal or pan-urethral strictures.** Most ventral-inlay series focus on distal-to-mid; BMG is more versatile.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Small evidence base.** All series are small (6–17 patients), retrospective, single-center.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

---

## Complications

- **Stricture recurrence.** 0–43% across series and follow-up windows.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[11]](#ref11)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **De novo SUI.** 0–14% across vaginal-flap series.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> A literature review of female urethroplasty found mild to moderate de novo incontinence in 9.4% of patients, most of whom recovered with pelvic floor exercises.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> One patient in the Hajebrahimi series needed a midurethral sling.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Urinary spraying.** Reported after ventral flap repair; not proven specific to that approach.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **De novo urgency.** Reported even with technically successful repairs.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Urethrovaginal fistula.** Rare; reduced by multi-layer closure with or without Martius interposition.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## When to Choose the Önol Inlay

- Stricture is distal or mid-urethral.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Anterior vaginal wall is healthy and well-estrogenized (no LS).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- Surgeon prefers to avoid BMG donor-site morbidity.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- A pedicled flap with own blood supply is preferred over a free graft.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **No prior synthetic MUS** (periurethral scarring limits flap mobilization).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Primary urethroplasty** is being considered as a definitive first-line treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Postoperative Management

- Urethral catheter 10–21 days.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Voiding trial with uroflowmetry and PVR.
- Cystoscopy at 3–6 mo.
- Topical estrogen in postmenopausal women.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- Long-term surveillance, because recurrence has been reported after the first year.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

---

## Summary

The Önol anterior vaginal wall mucosal inlay is a technically straightforward ventral augmentation urethroplasty that uses a pedicled strip of anterior vaginal wall mucosa to widen the lumen, with 100% objective cure at 24-month median follow-up across the 17 women in the index mixed series (10 of whom had the inlay) and pooled local-flap success of approximately 92% in non-randomized series. Its strengths are technical simplicity, no oral donor wound, and a pedicled blood supply. Its limitations are dependence on healthy vaginal tissue, the ventral-approach spraying risk, and lower comparative success in some small head-to-head series against dorsal BMG. Patient selection turns on vaginal tissue quality: BMG urethroplasty remains the preferred alternative when it is compromised.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)[[16]](#ref16)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
