# Urethral Diverticulectomy

**Urethral diverticulectomy** excises the diverticular sac, closes the urethral communication, and reinforces the repair with multilayer closure (± Martius interposition) to minimize fistula and recurrence. Most cases are female and approached transvaginally; rare male diverticula are typically post-urethroplasty / stricture and approached perineally with reconstruction tailored to the resulting urethral defect length.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For the diagnostic workup of a periurethral mass, see [Female Urethral & Periurethral Masses](/docs/clinical-conditions/03b-voiding-outlet/female-urethral-masses) and [Urethral Diverticulum (Female)](/docs/clinical-conditions/03b-voiding-outlet/urethral-diverticula). For interposition flap principles, see [Martius Flap (foundations)](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/martius).

---

## Epidemiology and Etiology

Urethral diverticula (UD) are epithelium-lined outpouchings of the urethral mucosa, affecting 1–6% of adult women.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> The dominant etiologic theory is chronic infection or abscess of the periurethral (Skene's) glands rupturing into the urethral lumen.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> An identifiable risk factor was present in 45 of 121 women in one series: traumatic vaginal delivery (15%), prior periurethral surgery (14%) and urethral dilation (8%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In men, UD are typically acquired, associated with urethral stricture, prior urethroplasty, trauma, or prolonged catheterization.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

---

## Clinical Presentation

The classic "3 Ds" (dysuria, dyspareunia, dribbling) are present together in only about 5% and are no longer reliable as a diagnostic triad.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> Most common symptoms: recurrent UTI (23–70%), SUI (38–60%), dyspareunia (12–60%), vaginal mass (52–63%), dysuria (18–60%), post-void dribbling, pelvic pain. Up to 21% are asymptomatic.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> In a prospective model, the combination of LUTS, post-micturition dribbling and recurrent UTI had the highest odds of a positive ultrasound finding (OR 13.78).<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> Tender anterior vaginal wall mass is the most common physical finding; expression of purulent or mucoid material through the urethra supports the diagnosis but is not a substitute for anatomical evaluation.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

---

## Imaging and Diagnosis

**MRI (post-void T2-weighted)** is the preferred modality for size, location, configuration (simple, U-shaped, saddle or circumferential), and sphincter relationship.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[10]](#ref10)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> EAU 2026 guidelines recommend MRI for diagnosis and characterization (weak), with urethroscopy, VCUG and ultrasound where necessary.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> A 24.4% MRI-versus-surgical discrepancy (10 of 41 women) has been reported, including 2 cases of intra-diverticular cancer not seen on MRI.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

| Modality | Sensitivity / utility |
|---|---|
| MRI (T2 post-void) | Preferred modality; configuration + sphincter relationship<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Pelvic floor ultrasound | Sensitivity 94%, specificity 100% in one retrospective series; non-invasive<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> |
| VCUG | Sensitivity 72–78% in the series cited; complementary detrusor / sphincter info<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[23]](#ref23)</sup> |
| Double-balloon positive-pressure urethrography | Sensitivity 100% in one series; more sensitive than VCUG |

**Configuration.** The most common location was midurethral 46% (distal 30%, proximal 21%) in one series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Circumferential UD is associated with more difficult repair and recurrence in some female cohorts; the OR 7.97 (95% CI 1.14–55.69) comes from a single-surgeon series of 68 women and is not a universal prediction rule.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

---

## Indications

- **Symptomatic UD.** Recurrent UTIs, pain, dyspareunia, incontinence, voiding dysfunction.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Asymptomatic or minimally symptomatic small UD may be observed. In men, post-void manual decompression has been used for small, unobstructed diverticula.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> EAU guidelines advise warning patients managed conservatively of a small (1–6%) risk of cancer in the diverticulum (weak).<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- **All excised specimens to pathology.** Malignancy (predominantly adenocarcinoma) is reported in about 1–6% of diverticula.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> Individual surgical series found carcinoma in 1 of 89 and 1 of 40 specimens.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

---

## Preoperative Preparation

- **MRI reviewed** to plan approach (supine versus prone), Martius need, and urethral-transection requirement for circumferential disease.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Videourodynamics / VCUG** to assess detrusor function and pre-existing SUI (14–41%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- Treat any active UTI to completion before surgery.
- Consent for de-novo SUI (13–21%), recurrence (2–10%), urethrovaginal fistula, urethral stricture, and the small risk of malignancy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- Perioperative antibiotic prophylaxis per protocol.

---

## Technique 1 — Standard Transvaginal Excision with Three-Layer Closure (84% of cases<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>)

Dorsal lithotomy.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

1. **Setup.** 16–18 Fr urethral Foley as a stent or guide; ± SPC for postop drainage.
2. **Cystourethroscopy.** Identify the diverticular ostium (identified in 60% of one series), assess number and location, exclude intra-urethral pathology.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> A guidewire or ureteric catheter into the diverticulum aids intraoperative identification.
3. **Hydrodissection + vaginal incision.** Dilute vasopressin or 1:200,000 epinephrine for hemostasis; inverted-U incision (preferred) or midline longitudinal over the mass.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
4. **Vaginal flap elevation.** Develop the plane between vaginal wall and periurethral fascia; raise lateral flaps wide enough for a tension-free three-layer closure. Maintain flap thickness for blood supply.
5. **Periurethral fascia incision.** Midline longitudinal; preserve fascial flaps as the second layer.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
6. **Diverticular sac dissection.** Sharp and blunt circumferential mobilization; identify the neck. Keep the urethral catheter as a palpable guide to avoid urethrotomy. Adherent or previously infected sacs require meticulous sharp dissection.
7. **Sac excision.** Amputate at the neck; excise the entire sac (incomplete removal is one recognized cause of recurrence). Send for pathology.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
8. **Layer 1 — urethral mucosa.** Interrupted or running 4-0 polyglactin; transverse closure to avoid lumen narrowing; watertight (test with saline or methylene blue via the Foley).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
9. **Layer 2 — periurethral fascia.** Interrupted 3-0 polyglactin in a direction perpendicular to layer 1 (the cardinal three-layer principle). For UD &gt; 4 cm, use bipedicle double-opposing fascial flaps (Lask), in which left and right fascial flaps are advanced and overlapped for a double-thickness layer.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
10. **Layer 3 — vaginal wall.** Running 2-0 polyglactin in a third orientation; tension-free; trim redundancy as needed.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
11. **Final check.** Retrograde fill ± end-of-case cystourethroscopy for patency and watertightness.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Technique 2 — Modified Prone Jack-Knife (Chapple / Osman, Sheffield)

Offers excellent surgical access, and a more ergonomic position for the surgeon, for large or complex UD.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

1. **Supine phase.** Cystourethroscopy; place urethral Foley, suprapubic catheter, and 5 Fr ureteric stents for proximal or large UD to protect the orifices.
2. **Reposition prone jack-knife.** Hips flexed over a break or bolster; buttocks taped apart; surgeon seated with direct view of the anterior vaginal wall.
3. **Dissection / excision / closure.** Identical principles to Technique 1 (steps 3–11).
4. **Martius flap.** Used in 30% of the Sheffield cohort; ipsilateral labium majus harvest, subcutaneous tunnel, interposed between fascial and vaginal layers.

**Outcomes (n = 121, mean follow-up 10 mo).** 73% continent, 4% symptomatic recurrence, 13% de-novo SUI, 9% Clavien-Dindo I–II.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Technique 3 — Bipedicle Double-Opposing Fascial Flaps (Lask, for UD &gt; 4 cm)

Same setup as Technique 1; the modification is at Layer 2:<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

- After urethral mucosal closure, the periurethral fascia is incised midline-longitudinally and the two fascial flaps (each on its lateral pedicle) are advanced and overlapped in opposite directions with interrupted 3-0 Vicryl, producing a double-thickness fascial layer.
- **Outcomes (n = 17, UD &gt; 4 cm).** No recurrence at mean follow-up of 5 ± 2.4 yr in this retrospective series.

---

## Technique 4 — Circumferential / Dorsal UD: Urethral Transection and Reconstruction

Circumferential UD has initial cure of only 64% versus 100% for simple disease.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> Some complex dorsal components require urethral transection for exposure; it is not mandatory for every circumferential diverticulum.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

**Rovner / Nickles steps:**

1. Standard vaginal incision and flap elevation.
2. **Complete urethral transection** at the level of the diverticulum to access the dorsal wall.
3. Excise the entire mucosalized surface (partial urethrectomy as needed).
4. **Reconstruction.** End-to-end anastomosis when feasible; tubularization of the dorsal diverticular wall to substitute a urethral segment when the dorsal tissue is healthy enough.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
5. **Selective Martius interposition** for tissue reinforcement.
6. **Selective pubovaginal sling.** Rovner and Wein used a sling selectively.

**Outcomes.** In 9 women, all had subjective pain relief; 6 of 8 were pad-free; 1 had a fistula and 1 a stricture.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

**Mehta alternative.** A single-patient video report describes segmental excision without formal transection, incising around or dorsal to the meatus into the retropubic space to access the dorsal component, followed by two-layer urethral repair reinforced with a fibromuscular flap.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

---

## Technique 5 — Marsupialization (Spence-Duckett, distal UD only)

Reserved for distal UD that does not involve the continence mechanism at the midurethra.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

**Classic.** Metzenbaum scissors (one blade in urethra, one in diverticulum); incise through the posterior urethral wall and anterior vaginal wall from ostium to meatus; marsupialize diverticular mucosa to vaginal epithelium.

**Modified (Welch).** A single-patient video report describes the same incision with excision of the sac before marsupialization, intended to reduce urethral shortening and improve cosmesis.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

**Limitation.** Essentially a generous meatotomy; risk of urethral shortening and cosmetic deformity. Ginsburg and Genadry confined marsupialization to UD in the lower third of the urethra; formal excision for the upper two-thirds.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

---

## Technique 6 — Partial Excision / Symptomatic-to-Asymptomatic Conversion

Conservative alternative for complex UD where complete excision carries high morbidity.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> The single-center series (n = 21) included diverticulectomy or partial resection.

- Mean OR time 75.7 ± 28.9 min; mean EBL 25.5 ± 10.7 mL; median catheterization 15 d.
- Symptom cure was 76.2%; complications were mostly Clavien I–II.

---

## Technique 7 — Male Urethral Diverticulectomy

Approach is perineal or penile depending on diverticulum location (most are bulbar or pendulous).<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

- Mobilize the urethra; identify the diverticulum.
- **Excise the sac**; assess resulting urethral defect length.
- **Reconstruction by defect length:**
  - Use primary closure or anastomosis only when tissue quality and location permit a tension-free, non-narrowing repair.
  - Larger defects may require buccal mucosa graft or an appropriate vascularized flap; the 4-cm threshold in a small series is not a general EPA rule, particularly for penile defects.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- Multilayer closure with dartos / spongiosal coverage.
- **Outcomes.** Alphs reported 92% (12/13) success and a 42% complication rate at intermediate follow-up; Cinman reported 91% recurrence-free at a mean of 2.3 yr (22 men, 12 treated with diverticulectomy and urethroplasty).<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

## Outcomes Summary

| Outcome | Reported rate |
|---|---|
| Overall cure (transvaginal excision) | 83–97%<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Initial cure — simple UD | 97–100%<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Initial cure — circumferential UD | 64–83%<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| Cure after reoperation | 92–97%<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Recurrence (overall) | 4–10% (primary up to 22%)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| De-novo SUI | 4–21% (most series 13–16%)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| Resolution of pre-existing SUI | ~ 50%<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Urethrovaginal fistula | 0.9–8.3% (EAU review)<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> |
| Carcinoma in surgical specimens | 1–2.5% in individual series (up to 6% in EAU-cited pooled data)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[8]](#ref8)[[21]](#ref21)</sup> |

## Concomitant Anti-Incontinence Procedure

In the Bradley 2020 multicenter retrospective cohort (n = 485; 96 with a concomitant sling), a concomitant pubovaginal sling was associated with greater SUI resolution (aOR 2.27) and with more long-term retention (aOR 6.98) and recurrent UTI (aOR 3.27).<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> Concomitant versus staged continence surgery is decided after confirming true SUI and discussing retention and infection tradeoffs. Some patients improve after diverticulectomy alone: in the Sheffield series 54% of women with pre-existing SUI were continent after excision, and the authors advocate deferring an incontinence procedure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> EAU 2026 guidelines state that bothersome SUI may be addressed at diverticulectomy with a non-synthetic sling (weak recommendation) and do not recommend a concomitant synthetic midurethral sling, because of the risk of erosion, extrusion and fistula next to a fresh suture line.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

## Postoperative Management

- **Urethral catheter 10–21 days** (median 14–15 d).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- SPC (if placed) clamped at urethral catheter removal; removed once voiding with acceptable PVR confirmed.
- Voiding trial; PVR (median 26 mL in Sheffield).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- 6-week clinical exam; repeat MRI or ultrasound when symptoms or examination suggest residual or recurrent disease; 6–12 mo final assessment; consider autologous fascial PVS for persistent bothersome SUI.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Pelvic-floor muscle training postoperatively. De-novo SUI 13–24% early, resolves to about 12–13% at 12 mo with conservative management.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

---

## Technical Pearls and Pitfalls

- Watertight multilayer closure with no overlapping suture lines is a stated principle of transvaginal diverticulectomy.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- **A Martius flap without meticulous urethral repair** does not prevent recurrence, fistula or SUI, according to a single-patient video report and review.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Circumferential configuration** was an independent predictor of lower cure in one series (OR 7.97, 95% CI 1.14–55.69).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Convert to urethral transection** if the dorsal component cannot be adequately accessed ventrally.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- Avoid excessive cautery near the urethra; thermal injury leads to delayed necrosis or fistula.
- Preserve periurethral fascia as much as possible; it is the second layer and supports the continence mechanism.
- **A previously placed Martius flap can be re-mobilized** and repositioned for recurrent repair, as described in one patient.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **End-of-case cystourethroscopy** confirms patency and watertightness.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
