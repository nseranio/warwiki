# Urethral Diverticulectomy

**Urethral diverticulectomy** excises the diverticular sac, closes the urethral communication, and reinforces the repair with multilayer closure (± Martius interposition) to minimize fistula and recurrence. Most cases are female and approached transvaginally; rare male diverticula are typically post-urethroplasty / stricture and approached perineally with reconstruction tailored to the resulting urethral defect length.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For the diagnostic workup of a periurethral mass, see [Female Urethral & Periurethral Masses](/docs/clinical-conditions/03b-voiding-outlet/female-urethral-masses) and [Urethral Diverticulum (Female)](/docs/clinical-conditions/03b-voiding-outlet/urethral-diverticula). For interposition flap principles, see [Martius Flap (foundations)](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/martius).

---

## Epidemiology and Etiology

Urethral diverticula (UD) are epithelium-lined outpouchings of the urethral mucosa, affecting **1–6% of adult women**.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The dominant etiologic theory is chronic infection / abscess of the **periurethral (Skene's) glands** rupturing into the urethral lumen.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Identifiable risk factors: traumatic vaginal delivery (15%), prior periurethral surgery (14%), urethral dilation (8%).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

In **men**, UD are typically **acquired**, associated with urethral stricture, prior urethroplasty, trauma, or prolonged catheterization.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

---

## Clinical Presentation

The classic "**3 Ds**" (dysuria, dyspareunia, dribbling) are present together in only ~ **5%** and are no longer reliable as a diagnostic triad.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> Most common symptoms: recurrent UTI (23–70%), SUI (38–60%), dyspareunia (12–60%), vaginal mass (52–63%), dysuria (18–60%), post-void dribbling, pelvic pain. Up to **21% are asymptomatic**.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> The strongest predictive symptom cluster is **LUTS + post-micturition dribbling + recurrent UTI** (OR 13.78).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Tender anterior vaginal wall mass is the most common physical finding; expression of purulent / mucoid material from the urethra on compression is pathognomonic.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

---

## Imaging and Diagnosis

**MRI (post-void T2-weighted)** is the gold standard for size, location, configuration (simple / U-shaped / saddle / circumferential), and sphincter relationship.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[10]](#ref10)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> A 24.4% MRI-vs-surgical discrepancy has been reported, including missed intra-diverticular cancer.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

| Modality | Sensitivity / utility |
|---|---|
| MRI (T2 post-void) | Gold standard; configuration + sphincter relationship<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Pelvic floor ultrasound | Sensitivity 94%, specificity 100%; non-invasive first-line<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| VCUG | Sensitivity 44–78%; complementary detrusor / sphincter info<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| Double-balloon positive-pressure urethrography | Sensitivity 100% in one series; more sensitive than VCUG<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |

**Configuration** — most common location is **midurethral 46%** (distal 30%, proximal 21%).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> **Circumferential UD** (&gt; 75% urethral involvement) is the strongest independent predictor of recurrence (OR 7.97) and lower initial cure rates.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

---

## Indications

- **Symptomatic UD** — recurrent UTIs, pain, dyspareunia, incontinence, voiding dysfunction.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Asymptomatic / minimally symptomatic small UD may be observed with post-void manual decompression.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **All excised specimens to pathology** — malignancy (predominantly adenocarcinoma) found in **2–9%**.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

---

## Preoperative Preparation

- **MRI reviewed** to plan approach (supine vs prone), Martius need, and urethral-transection requirement for circumferential disease.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Videourodynamics / VCUG** to assess detrusor function and pre-existing SUI (14–41%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Treat any active UTI to completion before surgery.
- Consent for de-novo SUI (13–21%), recurrence (2–10%), urethrovaginal fistula, urethral stricture, and the **2–9% malignancy** risk.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Perioperative antibiotic prophylaxis per protocol.

## Technique 1 — Standard Transvaginal Excision with Three-Layer Closure (84% of cases<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>)

Dorsal lithotomy.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

1. **Setup** — 16–18 Fr urethral Foley as a stent / guide; ± SPC for postop drainage.
2. **Cystourethroscopy** — identify the diverticular ostium (success ~ 60%), assess number / location, exclude intra-urethral pathology.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> A guidewire / ureteric catheter into the diverticulum aids intraoperative identification.
3. **Hydrodissection + vaginal incision** — dilute vasopressin or 1:200,000 epinephrine for hemostasis; **inverted-U** incision (preferred) or midline longitudinal over the mass.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
4. **Vaginal flap elevation** — develop the plane between vaginal wall and periurethral fascia; raise lateral flaps wide enough for a tension-free three-layer closure. Maintain flap thickness for blood supply.
5. **Periurethral fascia incision** — midline longitudinal; preserve fascial flaps as the critical second layer.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
6. **Diverticular sac dissection** — sharp + blunt circumferential mobilization; identify the **neck**. Keep the urethral catheter as a palpable guide to avoid urethrotomy. Adherent / previously-infected sacs require meticulous sharp dissection.
7. **Sac excision** — amputate at the neck; excise the entire sac (incomplete excision is the most common recurrence cause). Send for pathology.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
8. **Layer 1 — urethral mucosa** — interrupted or running 4-0 polyglactin; **transverse closure** to avoid lumen narrowing; watertight (test with saline / methylene blue via the Foley).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
9. **Layer 2 — periurethral fascia** — interrupted 3-0 polyglactin in a **direction perpendicular to layer 1** (the cardinal three-layer principle). For UD &gt; 4 cm, use **bipedicle double-opposing fascial flaps** (Lask) — left and right fascial flaps advanced and overlapped for a double-thickness layer.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
10. **Layer 3 — vaginal wall** — running 2-0 polyglactin in a **third orientation**; tension-free; trim redundancy as needed.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
11. **Final check** — retrograde fill ± end-of-case cystourethroscopy for patency / watertightness.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

---

## Technique 2 — Modified Prone Jack-Knife (Chapple / Osman, Sheffield)

Provides superior gravity-assisted exposure for large or complex UD.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

1. **Supine phase** — cystourethroscopy; place urethral Foley, suprapubic catheter, and 5 Fr **ureteric stents** for proximal / large UD to protect the orifices.
2. **Reposition prone jack-knife** — hips flexed over a break / bolster; buttocks taped apart; surgeon seated with direct view of the anterior vaginal wall.
3. **Dissection / excision / closure** — identical principles to Technique 1 (steps 3–11).
4. **Martius flap** — used in **30%** of the Sheffield cohort; ipsilateral labium majus harvest, subcutaneous tunnel, interposed between fascial and vaginal layers.

**Outcomes (n = 121)** — **73% continence, 4% recurrence, 13% de-novo SUI, 9% Clavien-Dindo I–II**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Technique 3 — Bipedicle Double-Opposing Fascial Flaps (Lask, for UD &gt; 4 cm)

Same setup as Technique 1; the modification is at Layer 2:<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

- After urethral mucosal closure, the periurethral fascia is incised midline-longitudinally and the two fascial flaps (each on its lateral pedicle) are advanced and **overlapped in opposite directions** with interrupted 3-0 Vicryl, producing a robust double-thickness fascial layer.
- **Outcomes (n = 17, UD &gt; 4 cm)** — **0% recurrence at mean 5 yr**.

---

## Technique 4 — Circumferential / Dorsal UD: Urethral Transection and Reconstruction

Circumferential UD has initial cure of only **64%** vs 100% for simple disease.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> The dorsal component cannot be reached through a standard ventral approach without urethral transection.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

**Rovner / Nickles steps:**

1. Standard vaginal incision and flap elevation.
2. **Complete urethral transection** at the level of the diverticulum to access the dorsal wall.
3. Excise the entire mucosalized surface (partial urethrectomy as needed).
4. **Reconstruction** — end-to-end anastomosis when feasible; **tubularization of the dorsal diverticular wall** to substitute a urethral segment when the dorsal tissue is healthy enough.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
5. **Selective Martius interposition** for tissue reinforcement.
6. **Selective pubovaginal sling** when transection compromises the sphincter.

**Outcomes** — all subjective pain relief; 6 / 8 pad-free; 1 fistula, 1 stricture.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

**Mehta alternative** — segmental excision without formal transection, incising around / dorsal to the meatus into the retropubic space to access the dorsal component, followed by two-layer urethral repair reinforced with a fibromuscular flap.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Technique 5 — Marsupialization (Spence-Duckett, distal UD only)

Reserved for distal UD that does **not** involve the continence mechanism at the midurethra.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

**Classic** — Metzenbaum scissors (one blade in urethra, one in diverticulum); incise through the posterior urethral wall and anterior vaginal wall from ostium to meatus; marsupialize diverticular mucosa to vaginal epithelium.

**Modified (Welch)** — same incision; **excise the sac before marsupialization** to reduce urethral shortening and improve cosmesis.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

**Limitation** — essentially a generous meatotomy; risk of urethral shortening and cosmetic deformity. Ginsburg / Genadry confined marsupialization to UD in the **lower third** of the urethra; formal excision for the upper two-thirds.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

---

## Technique 6 — Partial Excision / Symptomatic-to-Asymptomatic Conversion

Conservative alternative for complex UD where complete excision carries high morbidity, or for poor surgical candidates.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

- Mean OR time 75.7 ± 28.9 min; mean EBL 25.5 ± 10.7 mL; median catheterization 15 d.
- **Symptom cure 76.2%**; complications mostly Clavien I–II.

---

## Technique 7 — Male Urethral Diverticulectomy

Approach is perineal or penile depending on diverticulum location (most are bulbar or pendulous).<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

- Mobilize the urethra; identify the diverticulum.
- **Excise the sac**; assess resulting urethral defect length.
- **Reconstruction by defect length:**
  - **&lt; 4 cm:** primary closure or end-to-end anastomosis.
  - **≥ 4 cm:** substitution urethroplasty with **buccal mucosa graft** or **penile skin flap** (7 / 13 in Alphs).<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- Multilayer closure with dartos / spongiosal coverage.
- **Outcomes** — 92% (12 / 13) success at intermediate follow-up, 42% mostly minor complications (Alphs); 91% recurrence-free at 2.3 yr (Cinman).<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

## Outcomes Summary

| Outcome | Reported rate |
|---|---|
| Overall cure (transvaginal excision) | **83–97%**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Initial cure — simple UD | 97–100%<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Initial cure — circumferential UD | 64–83%<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| Cure after reoperation | 92–97%<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Recurrence (overall) | 4–10% (primary up to 22%)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| De-novo SUI | 4–21% (most series 13–16%)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| Resolution of pre-existing SUI | ~ 50%<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Urethrovaginal fistula | ~ 2%<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Unexpected malignancy in specimen | 2–2.5%<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |

## Concomitant Anti-Incontinence Procedure

Bradley 2020 multicenter retrospective (n = 485): concomitant **pubovaginal sling** improved SUI resolution (aOR 2.27) but increased **long-term retention** (OR 6.98) and **recurrent UTI** (OR 3.27).<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> Most experts **defer** anti-incontinence surgery for ≥ 6 months because pre-existing SUI resolves in ~ 50% after diverticulectomy alone.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Postoperative Management

- **Urethral catheter 10–21 days** (median 14–15 d).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- SPC (if placed) clamped at urethral catheter removal; removed once voiding with acceptable PVR confirmed.
- Voiding trial; PVR (median 26 mL in Sheffield).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **6-week** clinical exam; **3–6 mo** repeat MRI / US to confirm absence of residual / recurrent UD; **6–12 mo** final assessment — consider autologous fascial PVS for persistent bothersome SUI.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Pelvic-floor muscle training postoperatively. De-novo SUI 13–24% early, **resolves to ~ 12–13% at 12 mo** with conservative management.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

---

## Technical Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Three-layer closure with perpendicular suture lines** is the single most important principle — overlapping suture lines drive fistula.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Martius alone does not prevent recurrence** — meticulous urethral repair is the real determinant.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Circumferential configuration** is the strongest independent predictor of recurrence (OR 7.97).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Convert to urethral transection** if the dorsal component cannot be adequately accessed ventrally.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Avoid excessive cautery** near the urethra — thermal injury → delayed necrosis / fistula.
- **Preserve periurethral fascia** as much as possible — it is the second layer and supports the continence mechanism.
- **A previously placed Martius can be re-mobilized** and repositioned for recurrent repair, avoiding contralateral harvest.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **End-of-case cystourethroscopy** confirms patency and watertightness.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
