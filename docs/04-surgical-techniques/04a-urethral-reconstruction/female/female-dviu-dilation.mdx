# Female DVIU and Urethral Dilation

**Urethral dilation** is an option for a confirmed female urethral stricture (FUS). Female DVIU has no reliable dedicated outcomes evidence base, and male-stricture recommendations do not transfer to it.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

For the male counterpart, see [DVIU and Urethral Dilation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/dviu). For paclitaxel-enhanced endoscopic treatment, see [Drug-Coated Balloon Therapy](/docs/surgical-techniques/drug-coated-balloon).

---

## Guideline Position

The 2023 AUA guideline states that surgeons may reconstruct FUS using oral mucosal grafts, vaginal flaps, or both (Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C); its supporting text says urethroplasty should be offered given the low efficacy of endoscopic treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The 2026 EAU urethral-stricture guideline weakly recommends initial dilation and distinguishes maintenance dilation from definitive cure.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> The 2026 EAU Non-neurogenic Female LUTS guideline weakly recommends internal urethrotomy with postoperative self-dilation for stricture-related bladder outlet obstruction, with counseling about limited long-term improvement and postoperative incontinence.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> The EAU urethral-stricture chapter found no outcome study of urethrotomy specifically for anatomical FUS.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> It weakly recommends against dilation or urethrotomy for bladder outlet obstruction after synthetic midurethral tape because of a theoretical risk of urethral mesh extrusion.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

| Scenario | Female-specific guidance |
|---|---|
| Initial confirmed FUS | Dilation to 24–41 Fr (EAU weak recommendation).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| First recurrence | Repeat dilation with planned weekly 16–18 Fr self-dilation, or planned repeat dilation (EAU weak recommendation).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Second recurrence, unable to self-dilate or seeking definitive treatment | Urethroplasty (EAU strong recommendation).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Isolated short meatal stricture | Meatotomy or meatoplasty (EAU weak recommendation; first-line for short meatal strictures).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |

The EAU recurrence pathway is not a requirement to postpone reconstruction in a woman choosing definitive treatment after counseling.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Why Female Strictures Are Different

FUS is rare, affecting roughly 4–20% of women evaluated for refractory LUTS-related bladder outlet obstruction.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Evidence is largely observational, with heterogeneous diagnostic and outcome definitions; small comparative trials do not settle the optimal technique.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

**Etiology** is frequently idiopathic; iatrogenic injury from instrumentation or prior surgery is also important. Other causes include obstetric trauma (especially cephalopelvic disproportion), pelvic trauma, malignancy, radiation, urethral / vaginal atrophy, recurrent infection, and dermatologic disease (lichen planus, lichen sclerosus).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Diagnosis** is challenging given the non-specific presentation. Patients may report obstructive LUTS, recurrent UTI, hesitancy, weak stream, urgency-frequency, urethral pain, elevated PVR, or acute retention. Inability to pass even a small catheter is suggestive. VCUG demonstrates the entire female urethra and helps define stricture location and length; cystourethroscopy can identify the distal stricture edge but often requires preliminary dilation and does not define its full extent.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> For women with refractory LUTS, the EAU strongly recommends flow rate, post-void residual, and VCUG or videourodynamics.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

**Anatomic constraints** unique to the female urethra:

- **Short urethral length (approximately 4 cm).** Less urethra lies between the stricture and the sphincteric complex, so aggressive incision or dilation could threaten continence. New-onset SUI after dilation is reported at 1.4% in the EAU evidence review.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- A short stricture does not by itself establish that female DVIU is preferable to dilation or reconstruction.

## Urethral Dilation

Dilation is the most commonly performed first-line endoscopic intervention for FUS, often attempted at least once before considering urethroplasty.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

**Technique.** Sequential calibrated dilators (sounds or balloon dilators) progressively enlarge the lumen, with the intended caliber chosen from the female-specific guideline range above, performed in clinic or under anesthesia.

**Outcomes.** Dilation success was 47–49% at mean follow-up of 32–43 months in the Osman and Sarin reviews, but their endpoints differed: Osman counted no further intervention, whereas Sarin required symptom improvement without further instrumentation.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> The table reports study-defined outcomes rather than a uniform anatomical patency endpoint.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

| Series / Review | n | Mean follow-up | Pooled / reported success |
|---|---|---|---|
| Osman 2013 SR<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | 107 | 43 mo | ~ 47% |
| Sarin 2021 SR / meta<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | 234 | 32 mo | 49% pooled |
| Chakraborty 2022 review<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | — | — | 41.25% dilation success |
| Blaivas 2012<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | 17 (initial dilation) | Not stated for dilation | 16/17 recurred after initial dilation; follow-up for the remaining patient was not specified |
| Bouchard 2025 review<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | aggregated | — | efficacy decreases with each subsequent dilation |

## Female DVIU

DVIU involves endoscopic scar incision. Published female incision patterns vary, and comparative evidence does not establish a preferred direction or equivalence to dilation.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

**Technical considerations specific to the female urethra:**

- The short urethra means even a "short" incision approaches the sphincter; incise conservatively.
- For isolated meatal disease, consider meatoplasty rather than assuming DVIU is the preferred procedure.
- Standalone female-DVIU outcome data are scarce; most series pool DVIU with dilation under "endoscopic management."<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Endoscopic Management vs Urethroplasty

Observational series generally report higher patency after urethroplasty than dilation. These pooled rates are not randomized comparisons and differ in follow-up and definitions:

| Technique | Pooled success | Mean follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| Urethral dilation | 41–49% across separate reviews | 32–43 mo in Osman and Sarin<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Flap urethroplasty (vaginal or labial/local groups) | 91–93% across reviews | 32–42 mo in Osman and Sarin<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Oral mucosa graft urethroplasty (Sarin: buccal graft) | 89–94% across reviews | 15–19 mo<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Local free graft urethroplasty (Osman: vaginal or labial; Sarin: vaginal) | 80–87% across reviews | 15–22 mo<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |

Several reviews advise that urethroplasty not be delayed after failure or short-interval recurrence following dilation or DVIU, and some authors advise proceeding after one or at most two failed endoscopic attempts.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> The EAU recommends urethroplasty at the second recurrence of female urethral stricture when self-dilation is not feasible or the patient wishes definitive treatment.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Drug-Coated Balloon — Where the Female Evidence Stands

[Optilume drug-coated balloon (DCB)](/docs/surgical-techniques/drug-coated-balloon) is FDA-approved for obstructive symptoms from anterior urethral strictures of ≤3 cm in adult men.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> The female evidence remains limited to two case reports, overlapping single-center reports (a conference abstract and a published series with 12 analyzable women), and a separate nine-woman multicenter retrospective series. In the single-center publication, 11/12 were free from recurrence and repeat intervention at analysis (mean follow-up 12 months, range 6–41); these uncontrolled data do not establish comparative efficacy or long-term durability.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> Use remains exploratory, and patients are counseled accordingly.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

## Complications and Adjuncts

- **Recurrence** is the main limitation, and the chance of success falls with each repeat endoscopic attempt.
- **De novo SUI** is the main continence concern. A review of female stricture interventions reported mild to moderate de novo incontinence in 9.43% across its surgical treatment categories, with most recovering after pelvic floor exercises.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The EAU review reports new-onset SUI in 1.4% after dilation.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- Other complications: urethral bleeding, false passage, worsening peri-urethral fibrosis. Repeat instrumentation itself is a recognized cause of stricture progression.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Self-dilation adjunct.** Ongoing catheterization burden is discussed, and maintained patency is distinguished from cure. The female-specific recurrence pathway above applies.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Intralesional injection.** The AUA guideline discusses mitomycin C and corticosteroid adjuncts to DVIU, but the EAU female-stricture review found no urethrotomy outcome study specific to female FUS. Evidence is insufficient to determine benefit or safety of these injections in women with FUS.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

---

## Summary

Confirm an anatomical stricture before intervention. Counsel about dilation's limited durability, self-dilation as maintenance, and urethroplasty as a definitive option. Male trial results and guideline grades do not apply to female DVIU.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
