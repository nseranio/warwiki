# Vaginal Wall Tubularization Urethroplasty (Female)

**Vaginal wall tubularization urethroplasty** rolls a pedicled flap of anterior vaginal wall into a tube to create a neourethra, replacing a segment of urethra that has been lost or destroyed. This is distinct from vaginal-flap onlay or inlay techniques, which augment an existing urethral lumen; tubularization creates an entirely new urethral conduit from vaginal tissue. Blaivas described tubularized vaginal reconstruction in 10 women with total or partial urethral loss. Later reports of 49 and 74 women included broader urethral and vesical-neck pathology and varied reconstructions, so their outcomes are not tubularization-only results.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For related options, see [Bladder Wall Flap Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/bladder-wall-flap-urethroplasty), [Staged BMG Urethroplasty (Female)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/staged-bmg-female), and [Combined Vaginal Flap + BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/vaginal-flap-bmg-combined).

## Concept and Rationale

Vaginal wall tubularization differs from other vaginal-flap urethroplasty techniques in the degree of urethral replacement.

- **Onlay / inlay techniques.** In Blandy, Orandi-type and dorsal vaginal flap repairs, the strictured urethra is incised longitudinally and a vaginal flap is sutured to the urethral edges to widen the lumen. The native urethral wall forms part of the circumference; the flap provides the remainder. Augmentation procedure.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Tubularization.** The damaged or absent urethral segment is replaced entirely by rolling a vaginal flap into a complete tube (360° circumference), creating a neourethra with no native urethral wall contribution. Substitution or replacement procedure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Blaivas originally proposed this technique as "an alternative to the bladder flap neourethra," arguing that vaginal tissue is more accessible, avoids the morbidity of an abdominal approach, and provides a simpler single-stage reconstruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Historical Development

- **Blaivas 1989.** Initial description in 10 women with total or partial urethral loss and extensive vesical-neck / trigone damage from operative complications. A neourethra was constructed by rolling a vaginal flap into a tube, covered with a Martius labial fat pad graft and vaginal flap. 9/10 had a satisfactory neourethra, but 3 required generous meatotomy and 2 temporary intermittent catheterization; 6/10 were continent after a single reconstruction with anti-incontinence surgery.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Blaivas & Heritz 1996.** Expanded series of 49 women with anatomical damage to the urethra or vesical neck. 48 underwent vaginal-flap reconstruction; 47 had a Martius flap, 41 had a concomitant fascial pubovaginal sling, 5 a modified Pereyra procedure and 1 a Kelly plication. Continence in 42/49 (the abstract labels this 87%, a numerical inconsistency) after the initial procedure; 6 of 7 failures salvaged by secondary procedures.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Flisser & Blaivas 2003.** A broader mixed reconstruction series: 74 women with urethral pathology requiring reconstruction. 72 underwent vaginal-flap repairs (56 with concomitant pubovaginal sling). Successful anatomical repair in 93% by single-procedure vaginal-flap repair. Of 62 patients with preoperative incontinence, 87% considered themselves cured or improved. Follow-up 1–15 yr (median 1.5 yr).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Indications

- **Total or near-total urethral loss.** Iatrogenic injury (sling complications, mesh erosion, repeated urethral surgery), trauma, or malignancy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Extensive vesical-neck and urethral damage.** When any of the bladder neck, proximal urethra and trigone are destroyed and require reconstruction.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Obliterative urethral stricture.** When no functional lumen is identifiable and augmentation is not feasible, replacement of the segment is considered.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Failed prior urethral reconstruction.** When previous repairs have failed and insufficient native urethral tissue remains for augmentation.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The technique is not indicated for standard urethral strictures where the urethral lumen is narrowed but present; these are better managed with augmentation (onlay or inlay) techniques using vaginal flap, BMG, or labial tissue.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Surgical Technique (Blaivas Method)

The tubularized technique was described in the initial 10-patient report. Subsequent publications provide broader vaginal-reconstruction experience and should not be treated as a single standardized tubularization protocol.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

1. **Patient positioning.** Dorsal lithotomy.
2. **Assessment.** Cystoscopy (if possible) and vaginal exam to define the extent of urethral loss and identify the proximal healthy urethra and bladder neck.
3. **Anterior vaginal-wall incision.** Inverted-U or rectangular incision over the area of urethral loss. Flap must be wide enough to be rolled into a tube of adequate caliber (typically ≥ 16–18 Fr).
4. **Flap elevation.** Vaginal-wall flap raised with its vascular pedicle intact (typically based laterally or proximally). Includes the full thickness of the vaginal epithelium and underlying connective tissue.
5. **Tubularization.** Vaginal flap rolled into a tube over a 14–18 Fr urethral catheter, with the epithelial (mucosal) surface facing inward. Edges sutured with running or interrupted 4-0 or 5-0 absorbable sutures (polyglactin).
6. **Proximal anastomosis.** The proximal end of the tubularized neourethra is anastomosed to the healthy proximal urethra or bladder neck with interrupted absorbable sutures (watertight, mucosa-to-mucosa).
7. **Distal anastomosis.** The distal end is brought to the vestibular surface to create a new meatus, or anastomosed to the remaining distal urethral stump.
8. **Martius flap interposition.** A [Martius labial fat pad flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/martius) is harvested from the labium majus and tunneled beneath the vaginal wall to wrap around the tubularized neourethra. Provides vascular support, a tissue barrier between the neourethra and the vaginal closure (intended to reduce fistula risk), and mechanical reinforcement.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In the 1996 series, a Martius flap was used in 47 of 49 patients (96%), a gracilis flap in 1 and an anterior bladder tube in 1.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
9. **Continence planning.** Assess the amount of native outlet preserved, preoperative continence and obstruction risk. Many reported patients received concurrent continence surgery, but this is not mandatory for every reconstruction:
   - **Fascial pubovaginal sling.** Used in 41/49 patients (1996) and 56/72 patients (2003); placed over the Martius flap without tension.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
   - Modified Pereyra procedure: used in a minority; associated with higher failure rates (3/5 had postop SUI in the 1996 series).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
   - Kelly plication: used rarely.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
10. **Vaginal closure.** Remaining vaginal wall closed over the Martius flap as a separate layer.
11. **Catheter management.** Urethral catheter (± SPC) for 2–3 weeks.

## Orandi-Type Vaginal Flap Variant (Onlay, Not Tubularization)

Two groups have described a vaginal-flap technique inspired by the [Orandi technique](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/orandi-flap), adapted for women.

- **Simonato 2010.** 6 women with wide stricture disease (5 with stricture of the entire middle and distal urethra, 1 with proximal involvement and bilateral hydronephrosis) using a vaginal flap with a lateral vascular pedicle. The flap was partially de-epithelialized at the suture margins to promote cicatrix formation and prevent fistula. At mean 70.8 mo, all had normal micturition after catheter removal; 1 required intermittent self-catheterization for significant PVR.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Romero-Maroto 2018.** 9 women with a [lateral-based anterior vaginal-wall flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/lateral-based-anterior-vaginal-wall-flap), developed as an alternative to the Blandy technique (which caused retrusive meatus and inward urinary stream). At mean 80.7 mo (range 12–198): all had symptom relief; caliber increased from 10.8 Fr to ≥ 20 Fr; Qmax from 6.8 to 21 mL/s; there was no recurrence and no de novo SUI.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

These Orandi-type techniques are technically onlay or inlay rather than full tubularization; the vaginal flap replaces one wall of the urethra while the remaining native urethral wall is preserved.

## Hajebrahimi U-Shaped Hybrid Tubularization

Hajebrahimi 2019 described a hybrid technique, in a retrospective series of 14 women, that incorporates elements of both onlay and tubularization. Two parallel anterior vaginal-wall incisions around the meatus, vaginal flaps flipped up, dorsal urethra incised vertically through the stricture, distal flap end sutured to the proximal urethra, and the inner part of the flap is tubularized over a large Foley. The second layer of folded vaginal flap is sutured to native vagina. All patients improved in voiding LUTS; mean Qmax 15.82 mL/s, PVR 27.35 mL. 2 patients (14%) developed SUI (1 improved by 6 months, 1 required TOT at 12 months), and new-onset urgency and worsening urge incontinence were also reported.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Key Technical Considerations

**Flap design and vascularity.** The determinant of success is maintaining the vascular pedicle. Configurations described:

- **Lateral pedicle** (Orandi-type). Preserves the lateral vascular axis; Simonato and Romero-Maroto.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Proximal pedicle.** Base of the flap toward the bladder neck; Blaivas technique.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **U-shaped bilateral pedicle.** Hajebrahimi, with bilateral vaginal-wall flaps maintaining lateral blood supply.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

**De-epithelialization.** Simonato specifically described partial de-epithelialization of the flap at the suture margins to promote tissue cicatrix and prevent fistula.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

**Martius flap reinforcement.** The Blaivas group used a Martius flap in nearly all cases (47/49 in the 1996 cohort), placing the pubovaginal sling over it without tension.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The Blaivas technique is therefore a three-layer construct of neourethra, Martius flap and pubovaginal sling.

**Continence planning.** Extensive outlet loss often requires a continence procedure, but the residual sphincter, bladder function and risk of obstruction or catheter dependence must guide selection. The Blaivas group favored the fascial pubovaginal sling over the modified Pereyra procedure (3/5 Pereyra patients had postop SUI; all subsequently cured by sling).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Tubularization vs Onlay / Inlay Vaginal Flap

| Feature | Tubularization | Onlay / Inlay |
|---|---|---|
| Urethral replacement | Complete (360°) | Partial (one wall) |
| Primary indication | Total urethral loss | Stricture with preserved lumen |
| Native urethral wall | Not required | Required |
| Anti-incontinence procedure | Frequently needed with major outlet loss; individualized | Based on preoperative and postoperative continence |
| Martius flap | Used in nearly all Blaivas cases | Optional |
| Continence outcomes | Reported only for mixed-reconstruction cohorts | Vary with baseline continence, technique and outcome definition |
| Anatomical success | Initial 10-patient report: 9 satisfactory neourethras, with secondary meatotomy in 3 | Small heterogeneous cohorts; not directly comparable |
| Complexity | Higher (tubularization + Martius + sling) | Lower (single-layer augmentation) |
| Urinary spraying | No comparative estimate established | Reported in some small vaginal-flap cohorts |

## Tubularization vs Bladder Wall Flap Neourethra

Blaivas originally proposed vaginal-wall tubularization specifically as an alternative to the Tanagho bladder-flap neourethra.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Feature | Vaginal Wall Tubularization | [Bladder Wall Flap (Tanagho)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/bladder-wall-flap-urethroplasty) |
|---|---|---|
| Approach | Vaginal | Abdominal |
| Tissue source | Anterior vaginal wall | Anterior bladder wall |
| Vascularity | Pedicled (vaginal blood supply) | Pedicled (vesical blood supply) |
| Access-related considerations | Avoids an abdominal incision but adds vaginal and possible labial donor-site morbidity | Requires bladder harvest and often abdominal access; no direct comparative morbidity estimate |
| Bladder capacity | Preserved | Reduced |
| Complexity | Moderate | Higher |
| Best for | Urethral loss with intact bladder | Urethral loss + bladder-neck involvement |

## Advantages and Limitations

**Advantages**

- Vaginal tissue is readily available, well vascularized and in the same operative field. There is no separate donor site apart from the labial Martius flap.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- The procedure is single-stage.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Avoids abdominal approach (unlike bladder flap).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Preserves bladder capacity.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- The Blaivas group's 74-woman series of mixed vaginal-flap reconstructions reported 93% anatomical repair by a single procedure. In 62 women with preoperative incontinence, 54 (87%) considered themselves cured or improved, which is not complete continence.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Vaginal tissue is hairless and suited to urethral lining.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

**Limitations**

- **Requires adequate vaginal tissue.** Atrophy (postmenopausal, hypoestrogenic), prior vaginal surgery, or lichen sclerosus may compromise tissue quality and availability.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Concurrent continence surgery may be needed**, adding obstruction and catheter-dependence risks that must be discussed.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Martius flap harvest.** Required in nearly all cases; adds operative time and labial donor-site morbidity.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Urinary spraying.** Higuchi noted urinary spraying only in the VFU group (2/7), not the BMG group.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Vaginal narrowing.** Harvesting a large vaginal flap may narrow the vaginal canal.
- **Long-term recurrence.** Blaivas 2012 (vaginal flap urethroplasty for stricture, n = 9) found no recurrence at a minimum of 1 yr, but 2 of 9 patients (22%) recurred at 5.5 and 6 yr, so long-term surveillance is needed.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Lower success versus dorsal BMG in some comparative studies.** Higuchi VFU 57.1% versus DOBMGU 87.5% at median 12 mo; Kowalik 2 VFU recurrences versus none after dorsal BMG (10 women). These small retrospective series used onlay or inlay rather than full tubularization.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Position in the Female Reconstructive Algorithm

Vaginal wall tubularization occupies a specific niche for cases of complete or near-complete urethral loss where the native urethral lumen cannot be preserved or augmented. The AUA 2023 guideline amendment states that surgeons may reconstruct female strictures with oral mucosal grafts, vaginal flaps or a combination (Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C), citing success rates of 69–95%. It does not distinguish onlay from tubularization configurations.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> For standard urethral strictures with a preserved lumen, augmentation techniques (dorsal or ventral BMG onlay, vaginal flap onlay) are preferred over tubularization because they preserve native urethral tissue and sphincteric function. Tubularization is used when no native urethra remains to augment.

## Summary

Vaginal wall tubularization is an option for extensive female urethral loss when viable vaginal tissue is available. The initial report contained 10 women. The later 74-woman series involved mixed reconstructions, and its 87% cured-or-improved result is not complete continence after tubularization.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> A pedicled anterior vaginal-wall flap is rolled into a tube to create a neourethra, reinforced with a Martius labial fat pad flap and, in most reported patients, combined with a fascial pubovaginal sling. The technique is indicated for total or near-total urethral loss. Standard stricture disease is treated with augmentation techniques. The Orandi-type lateral-pedicle variants (Simonato, Romero-Maroto) are onlay or inlay repairs, not tubularization. In Romero-Maroto's 9 women, none had recurrence at mean 80.7 mo.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
