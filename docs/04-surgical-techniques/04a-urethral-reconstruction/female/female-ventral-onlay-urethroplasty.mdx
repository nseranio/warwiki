# Female Ventral Onlay Urethroplasty

**Female ventral onlay urethroplasty** places a tissue graft (usually buccal mucosa) or pedicled flap on the **ventral (6 o'clock) surface** of the urethra to augment a strictured segment. Modern series report success rates of **86–98%**, with technical advantages over the dorsal approach: easier meatus preservation, wider operative field, less intraoperative bleeding, and a vaginal-sparing technique when BMG is used.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For the dorsal counterpart see [Female Dorsal Onlay Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-dorsal-onlay-urethroplasty). For the inlay variant (graft placed *into* the urethrotomy without urethral mobilization), see [Female Ventral Inlay BMG (Mandal RCT)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-ventral-inlay-bmg). For pedicled-flap alternatives see the [Blandy U-Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-vaginal-flap-urethroplasty), [Lateral-Based Anterior Vaginal Wall Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/lateral-based-anterior-vaginal-wall-flap), and [Önol Anterior Vaginal Wall Mucosal Inlay](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/onol-vaginal-mucosal-inlay).

---

## Rationale and Indications

Indications mirror those for dorsal onlay — recurrent or refractory FUS after failed dilation or DVIU. AUA 2023 endorses urethroplasty for FUS given low endoscopic durability, with success of 69–95% across techniques driven by surgeon experience.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

The ventral approach is particularly favored when:

- **Meatus preservation** is desired (mid- or proximal strictures).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- A **vaginal-sparing** technique is preferred (BMG avoids vaginal-wall harvest).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Reduced intraoperative bleeding** is a priority (10 vs 20 mL vs dorsal in Savun 2026).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- The surgeon has greater familiarity with the ventral dissection plane.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Graft / Flap Options

| Source | Pros | Cons / Cautions |
|---|---|---|
| **Buccal mucosa graft (BMG)** | Most commonly reported; thick epithelium, hairless, infection-resistant; periurethral fascia provides vascular bed<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | Oral donor-site morbidity (transient) |
| **Vaginal wall pedicled flap** | Own blood supply; useful for distal strictures; lateral-based variant preserves orthotopic meatus<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | Lower success in some series (57–73%); spraying risk with classic Blandy<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Vaginal wall free graft** | No oral morbidity | Avoid in LS / atrophy; vaginal-narrowing risk. Kumar 2025 meta — BMG 86.2% vs VWG 89.8% (not significantly different)<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |

## Surgical Technique — Ventral Onlay BMG

Dorsal lithotomy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[12]](#ref12)[[7]](#ref7)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

1. **Setup** — urethral catheter for identification.
2. **Anterior vaginal wall incision** — longitudinal, over the urethra.
3. **Periurethral fascia dissection** — incise the fascia and elevate **two lateral fascial flaps**, exposing the ventral urethral surface. These flaps are the analog of the male dartos and form the vascular bed for the graft.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
4. **Ventral urethrotomy** — longitudinal **6 o'clock** incision through the entire stricture into healthy urethra proximally and distally.
5. **BMG harvest** — inner cheek; defat / remove fibromuscular tissue from the graft.
6. **Graft placement** — graft sutured to the urethral mucosal margins on both sides (running or interrupted absorbable); tension-free augmentation.
7. **Fascial flap coverage** — periurethral fascial flaps closed over the graft, providing the vascularized bed and mechanical support.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
8. **Vaginal wall closure** — anterior vaginal wall closed over the repair.
9. **Catheter** — urethral ± SPC for **~ 3 weeks**.

### Modified "AZ" technique (Ozlulerden 2020)

BMG sutured to urethral mucosa with periurethral tissue closed in a second layer. **100%** in 7 patients at mean 23 mo with mean OR time of just **64 min**.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

**Key technical tenets:**

- Urethrotomy must extend through the **entire stricture length** into healthy tissue.
- Periurethral fascial flaps provide the **vascular bed** essential for graft take (analog of dartos in male urethroplasty).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- The ventral approach **preserves the dorsal urethral sphincter** mechanism, potentially reducing incontinence risk.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgical Technique — Ventral Vaginal Flap

A pedicled anterior vaginal wall flap rather than a free graft:<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

1. Two parallel incisions on the anterior vaginal wall around the meatus.
2. Raise a U-shaped (Blandy) or **lateral-based** vaginal flap with vascular pedicle intact.
3. Ventral urethrotomy through the stricture.
4. Rotate and suture the flap to the urethral edges.
5. Close the remaining vaginal wall.

The **lateral-based anterior vaginal wall flap (Romero-Maroto)** achieved **100% success** in n = 9 at mean 80.7 mo with no recurrence, no de-novo SUI, and orthotopic meatus.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Meta-Analytic Evidence — Ventral vs Dorsal

Three recent high-level analyses converge on equivalence:

- **Li 2026** — 5 studies, n = 319: **no significant difference** in success (RR 1.00; 95% CI 0.91–1.10; p = 0.95), Qmax, PVR, or AUA score.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Ortac 2025** — 25 studies: pooled success **92.1% (dorsal)** vs **95.5% (ventral)**, OR 0.84, p = 0.74. SUI 4 ventral vs 2 dorsal cases.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Savun 2026** — largest individual comparative cohort (n = 65): patency **92.1% dorsal vs 92.6% ventral** (p = 1.0); no FSFI / PGIC / SUI difference; **significantly less blood loss with ventral** (10 vs 20 mL, p = 0.001).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Advantages

- **Easier meatus preservation** — ventral incision naturally avoids the meatus.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Wider operative field**.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Less intraoperative bleeding** (10 vs 20 mL, Savun).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Vaginal-sparing** when BMG is used.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Sphincter preservation** — dorsal sphincter complex untouched.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Familiar plane** for female pelvic reconstructive surgeons.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

---

## Limitations

- **Less suited to distal strictures requiring meatal reconstruction** — dorsal approach is preferred there.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Urinary spraying** — more common with ventral / VFU than with BMG techniques.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Graft support depends on periurethral fascial flaps** — may be less robust than the dorsal periurethral bed in some patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Vaginal compromise risk** if a vaginal flap (not BMG) is used.

---

## Complications

- **De-novo SUI** — 0–4.5% across ventral BMG series; usually mild, conservatively managed.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Stricture recurrence** — 2–14% across techniques and follow-up windows.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Dyspareunia** — ~ 12.5% in one small series.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Spraying** — primarily VFU rather than BMG.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Donor-site morbidity** — minimal; transient oral discomfort.

---

## Prognostic Factor

The Waterloos / Lumen EAU multicenter study (n = 165) identified **prior pelvic radiotherapy** as the **only independent predictor** of stricture recurrence after female urethroplasty (p = 0.015). Stricture etiology, location, and length were not significant predictors.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

---

## Summary

Female ventral onlay urethroplasty achieves **86–98%** success across modern series, equivalent to dorsal onlay (Li 2026 / Ortac 2025 / Savun 2026 metas) with the practical advantages of meatus preservation, less bleeding, vaginal sparing (BMG), and dorsal sphincter preservation. Berdondini 2024 (n = 42, 98%, Qmax 7.7 → 25.9 mL/s) is the strongest contemporary single-center series; the Mandal 2025 RCT (covered on the [Ventral Inlay BMG page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-ventral-inlay-bmg)) established non-inferiority of the inlay variant. **Prior pelvic radiotherapy** is the strongest negative prognostic factor.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
