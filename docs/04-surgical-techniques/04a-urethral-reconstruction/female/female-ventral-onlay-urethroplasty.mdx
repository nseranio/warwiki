# Female Ventral Onlay Urethroplasty

**Female ventral onlay urethroplasty** places a tissue graft (usually buccal mucosa) or pedicled flap on the ventral (6 o'clock) surface of the urethra to augment a strictured segment. Modern series report success rates of 86–98%. Compared with dorsal onlay, a small randomized trial found easier meatus preservation and a wider operative field with the ventral approach, and it was associated with less intraoperative bleeding. Conventional ventral onlay still requires vaginal dissection; vaginal sparing refers to specific inlay variants.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For the dorsal counterpart see [Female Dorsal Onlay Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-dorsal-onlay-urethroplasty). For the inlay variant (graft placed *into* the urethrotomy without urethral mobilization), see [Female Ventral Inlay BMG (Mandal RCT)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-ventral-inlay-bmg). For pedicled-flap alternatives see the [Blandy U-Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-vaginal-flap-urethroplasty), [Lateral-Based Anterior Vaginal Wall Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/lateral-based-anterior-vaginal-wall-flap), and [Önol Anterior Vaginal Wall Mucosal Inlay](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/onol-vaginal-mucosal-inlay).

## Rationale and Indications

Indications mirror those for dorsal onlay: recurrent or refractory FUS after failed dilation or DVIU. The AUA 2023 amendment states that surgeons may reconstruct female strictures with oral mucosal grafts, vaginal flaps or a combination (Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level C). It cites low efficacy of endoscopic treatment and success of 69–95% across techniques, with technique chosen by surgeon experience.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

The ventral approach is particularly favored when:

- **Meatus preservation** is desired (mid- or proximal strictures).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Avoiding vaginal graft harvest is preferred; BMG onlay still uses a vaginal access incision.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Reduced intraoperative bleeding is a priority (10 versus 20 mL versus dorsal in Savun 2026).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- The surgeon prefers the ventral dissection plane. A step-by-step video demonstrates the operation for female pelvic reconstructive surgeons.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Graft / Flap Options

| Source | Pros | Cons / Cautions |
|---|---|---|
| **Buccal mucosa graft (BMG)** | Most commonly reported; thick epithelium, hairless, infection-resistant; periurethral fascia provides vascular bed<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | Oral donor-site morbidity (transient) |
| **Vaginal wall pedicled flap** | Own blood supply; useful for distal strictures; lateral-based variant preserves orthotopic meatus<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | Lower success in some series (57–73%); spraying reported after vaginal flap urethroplasty<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Vaginal wall free graft** | No oral morbidity | Avoid in LS / atrophy; vaginal-narrowing risk. Kumar 2025 meta — BMG 86.2% vs VWG 89.8% (not significantly different)<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |

## Surgical Technique — Ventral Onlay BMG

Dorsal lithotomy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[12]](#ref12)[[7]](#ref7)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

1. **Setup.** Urethral catheter for identification.
2. **Anterior vaginal wall incision.** Longitudinal, over the urethra.
3. **Periurethral fascia dissection.** Incise the fascia and elevate two lateral fascial flaps, exposing the ventral urethral surface. These flaps are the analog of the male dartos and form the vascular bed for the graft.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
4. **Ventral urethrotomy.** Longitudinal 6 o'clock incision through the entire stricture into healthy urethra proximally and distally.
5. **BMG harvest.** Inner cheek; defat and remove fibromuscular tissue from the graft.
6. **Graft placement.** Graft sutured to the urethral mucosal margins on both sides (running or interrupted absorbable); tension-free augmentation.
7. **Fascial flap coverage.** Periurethral fascial flaps closed over the graft, providing the vascularized bed and mechanical support.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
8. **Vaginal wall closure.** Anterior vaginal wall closed over the repair.
9. **Catheter.** Urethral catheter, with or without an SPC, for approximately 3 weeks (varies with surgeon preference).

### Modified "AZ" technique (Ozlulerden 2020)

The BMG is sutured to the urethral mucosa and the periurethral tissue is closed in a second layer. In the first 7 patients (mean follow-up 23 months), Qmax rose from 5.1 to 31.8 mL/s and PVR fell from 84 to 7 mL at 3 months. The authors saw no infection, vaginal erosion, urinary incontinence or oral discomfort, and mean operating time was 64 minutes.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

### Martius-Reinforced Ventral BMG Onlay

When the periurethral tissue is compromised (typically after synthetic sling or mesh erosion, prior failed urethroplasty, or pelvic radiation), the periurethral fascial bed is unreliable as a vascular supply for the graft. In these cases, a [Martius (labial fat-pad) flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/martius) is mobilized and interposed between the BMG and the vaginal-wall closure to provide a vascularized second layer. Önol et al. described this Martius-reinforced ventral BMG approach in 2 of 17 women with prior synthetic midurethral slings; the broader 17-patient series achieved 100% objective cure at median 24 months.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> In a series of 159 women, Martius interposition was used in 12 women having urethroplasty for stricture. Harvest morbidity was low, with labial hematoma in 1.25% and wound infection in 0.6%.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

**Circumstances in which Martius reinforcement has been used or proposed:**

- Prior synthetic midurethral sling or vaginal mesh erosion.
- Failed prior urethroplasty with scarred / poorly vascularized periurethral bed.
- Pelvic radiotherapy.
- Adjunctive coverage of a tenuous closure layer.

**Technical tenets:**

- Urethrotomy must extend through the entire stricture length into healthy tissue.
- Periurethral fascial flaps provide the vascular bed needed for graft take (analog of dartos in male urethroplasty).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- The ventral approach preserves the dorsal urethral sphincter mechanism, potentially reducing incontinence risk.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgical Technique — Ventral Vaginal Flap

A pedicled anterior vaginal wall flap rather than a free graft:<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

1. Two parallel incisions on the anterior vaginal wall around the meatus.
2. Raise a U-shaped (Blandy) or lateral-based vaginal flap with vascular pedicle intact.
3. Ventral urethrotomy through the stricture.
4. Rotate and suture the flap to the urethral edges.
5. Close the remaining vaginal wall.

The lateral-based anterior vaginal wall flap (Romero-Maroto) achieved 100% success in n = 9 at mean 80.7 mo with no recurrence, no de-novo SUI, and orthotopic meatus.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

Functional improvements were consistent across series. Qmax rose from 7.7 to 25.9 mL/s in Berdondini, and in Mukhtar median Qmax rose from 7 to 18 mL/s while median PVR fell from 100 to 15 mL.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> The EAU 2026 update pooled 247 ventral oral mucosal graft patients (177 onlay, 70 inlay) and reported 90.7% (range 86–98%) stricture-free at 6 to 38.1 months, with new-onset SUI in 3.2%.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

## Meta-Analytic Evidence — Ventral vs Dorsal

Comparative reports show no consistent difference in patency between dorsal and ventral onlay. The Savun report is a retrospective cohort, not a meta-analysis.

- **Li 2026.** 5 studies, n = 319: no significant difference in success (RR 1.00; 95% CI 0.91–1.10; p = 0.95), Qmax, PVR, or AUA score.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Ortac 2025.** In 25 studies, pooled success was 92.1% (dorsal) and 95.5% (ventral). The four comparative cohorts (62/69 dorsal versus 93/103 ventral) showed no significant difference in success (OR 0.84; 95% CI 0.30–2.36; p = 0.74). SUI occurred in 4 ventral and 2 dorsal cases. Most studies (21 of 25) were level 4 evidence.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Savun 2026.** This three-center cohort (n = 65), which the authors describe as the largest individual comparative cohort, found patency of 92.1% dorsal versus 92.6% ventral (p = 1.0). There was no difference in FSFI, PGIC or de novo incontinence. Ventral onlay had less blood loss (10 versus 20 mL, p = 0.001).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Advantages

- **Easier meatus preservation.** Ventral incision naturally avoids the meatus.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Wider operative field**.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Less intraoperative bleeding.** 10 versus 20 mL, Savun.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Avoids vaginal graft harvest** when BMG is used; onlay access still involves the vagina.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Sphincter preservation.** Dorsal sphincter complex untouched.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Teaching resources.** A step-by-step video is available for female pelvic reconstructive surgeons.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

---

## Limitations

- **Less suited to distal strictures requiring meatal reconstruction.** Dorsal approach is preferred there.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Urinary spraying.** In Higuchi 2026, spraying occurred only after vaginal flap urethroplasty (2 of 7), not after BMG.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Graft support depends on the periurethral fascial flaps.** The Martius-reinforced variant has been used when this bed is compromised.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- **Vaginal wound and tissue-quality considerations** apply to ventral onlay access even when the graft is buccal.

---

## Complications

- **De novo SUI.** Rates range from 0% to 4.5% across ventral BMG series and were 3.2% in the EAU pooled data. The cases described were mild.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)[[16]](#ref16)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- **Stricture recurrence.** Recurrence ranges from 2% to 14% across techniques and follow-up windows.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Dyspareunia.** It occurred in 1 of 8 women (12.5%) in one small series.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Spraying.** It was reported after VFU and not after BMG.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Donor-site morbidity.** It is described as minimal, with transient oral discomfort.

---

## Prognostic Factor

In the Waterloos / Lumen EAU multicenter study (n = 165; median follow-up 29 months), prior radiotherapy was an independent predictor of stricture recurrence after female urethroplasty (p = 0.015). Two-year stricture-free survival was 73%, 86% and 80% after ventral onlay flap, ventral onlay graft and dorsal onlay graft, without a significant difference between groups.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

---

## Summary

Female ventral onlay urethroplasty achieves 86–98% success across modern series, and comparative studies show no consistent patency difference from dorsal onlay. Reported practical advantages are meatus preservation, less bleeding, avoidance of vaginal graft harvest (with BMG) and preservation of the dorsal sphincter mechanism. Berdondini 2024 is a single-surgeon series (n = 42; 98% stricture-free at mean 38.1 months; Qmax 7.7 to 25.9 mL/s). The Mandal 2025 RCT (covered on the [Ventral Inlay BMG page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-ventral-inlay-bmg)) reported similar one-year success in the 40 analyzed participants and did not test formal noninferiority. Prior radiotherapy was associated with recurrence in one multicenter cohort.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
