# Staged BMG Urethroplasty (Female)

**Staged BMG urethroplasty in females** is a rarely reported, extrapolated technique adapted from the male two-stage (Johanson-type) BMG urethroplasty, reserved for the most complex female urethral strictures, particularly obliterative disease, lichen sclerosus involvement, failed prior urethroplasty, or severely scarred periurethral tissue where a single-stage repair is deemed unreliable. There are no dedicated female-specific series reporting staged BMG urethroplasty as a distinct technique; the concept is derived from male reconstructive principles and applied case-by-case in women.

For the male template, see [Johanson Two-Stage Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/johanson-two-stage). For single-stage female alternatives, see [Female Dorsal Onlay Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-dorsal-onlay-urethroplasty), [Female Ventral Onlay Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-ventral-onlay-urethroplasty), and [Combined Vaginal Flap + BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/vaginal-flap-bmg-combined).

## Concept and Rationale

In single-stage BMG urethroplasty, the graft is harvested, placed as an onlay or inlay, and the urethra is closed over it in a single operation. The graft relies on the surrounding vascular bed (periurethral fascia, vaginal wall, or [Martius flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/martius)) for imbibition and inosculation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In a staged approach, reconstruction is intentionally divided into two operations separated by a maturation interval:

- **Stage 1.** The strictured urethra is opened (or excised) and a BMG is sutured into the defect as an open plate (not tubularized). The graft matures and vascularizes in situ over an interval that lasts several months in male series.
- **Stage 2.** After graft maturation, the now-vascularized BMG plate is tubularized (rolled and closed) to recreate the urethral lumen.

The two-stage principle is used in male urethroplasty, where it is one option for adverse local tissue, severe scarring or failed reconstruction; LS alone does not mandate staging.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Allowing the graft to mature on a vascular bed before tubularization theoretically improves graft survival and reduces contracture risk versus primary tubularization of a free graft.

## Male Staged BMG: The Template

The male experience provides the technical and outcomes framework from which the female staged approach is derived.

- **Palminteri 2002.** Two-stage technique in 24 men with complex bulbar strictures: BMG sutured to the left urethral mucosal-plate margin at stage 1, perineostomy closed at stage 2. Success was 23/24 (92.8%) at median 18 mo; mean Qmax was 22 mL/s.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Kozinn, Buckley and Zinman 2013 (multistage series, n = 91).** A large male staged BMG cohort of complex anterior strictures (45% hypospadias; 32% LS; 55% panurethral). Urethrectomy with BMG replacement in staged fashion: 51/91 (56%) underwent tubularization (stage 2), 17 were pending closure and 23 had stage 1 only with no plan for completion. Stage 1 required revision in 17/91 (18.7%; 4 with ≥ 2 revisions) and stage 2 in 7/51 (13.7%). The authors report a 90% success rate at a mean follow-up of 15 mo; Qmax rose from 6.7 to 21.5 mL/s.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Patel 2016.** Three approaches for LS strictures in 79 men: single-stage BMG (75% success), 2-stage BMG (9/37, 24%, had stricture recurrence or recurrent LS in the first-stage graft), and perineal urethrostomy (93%). LS can recur in the BMG itself, complicating staged reconstruction.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Warner 2015.** Multi-institutional 466 men with long-segment strictures (≥ 8 cm): second-stage Johanson recurrence 35.7% versus single-stage BMG 17.5% (p < 0.01). In this retrospective series, staging did not improve outcomes, and second-stage Johanson was the only technique with an increased odds ratio of recurrence versus BMG (2.82).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Indications for Staged Approach in Females

Based on extrapolation from male principles and the limited female literature.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

1. **Obliterative urethral stricture.** No urethral lumen identifiable; the entire strictured segment must be excised, leaving no native wall for single-stage graft support.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
2. **Lichen sclerosus.** When LS involves periurethral or vaginal tissue, compromising the vascular bed for single-stage graft take. AUA 2023 notes LS-related strictures are best reconstructed with oral mucosa grafts and that genital skin should be avoided. Whether staging improves outcomes over single-stage BMG in female LS remains unknown. In males, a meta-analysis found one-stage BMG had a pooled recurrence rate of 10% overall (18% at ≥ 24 mo) in LS, which was not significantly different from non-LS overall but higher in long-term studies (OR 1.83, p = 0.05).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
3. **Failed prior urethroplasty with extensive periurethral scarring.** When periurethral tissue is too scarred or devascularized to support a free graft in a single stage.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
4. **Severely compromised local tissue.** Vaginal atrophy, prior radiation, or extensive prior surgery depleting all local tissue options.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
5. **Very long or pan-urethral stricture.** Where reconstruction length exceeds what can be reliably supported by available vascular tissue in a single stage.

## Operative Planning Boundary

The male template, as described by Palminteri and by Kozinn and colleagues, gives the general sequence.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> At stage 1 the strictured segment is opened or excised and a buccal graft is sutured as an open plate onto a vascular bed, with urine diverted by a suprapubic catheter or through the open plate. Before stage 2 the graft is assessed for take, pliability, contracture and LS recurrence, and stage 1 is revised if the graft is unhealthy. At stage 2 the plate is tubularized over a catheter and covered with a second layer of vascularized tissue. For a woman, a vaginal approach with periurethral fascia, vaginal wall or a Martius flap as the graft bed is the logical adaptation, but no series describes it.

The sources on this page do not establish a standardized female staged-BMG operation. Male open-plate dimensions, maturation intervals, fixation to pubic tissue and tubularization instructions should not be transferred into a female operative protocol without a directly documented technique. A complex female defect requires individualized assessment of residual urethra, continence mechanism, vascularized tissue, fistula and drainage options by a reconstructive team.

Staging may be considered when definitive repair cannot safely be completed in one operation, but it still requires a viable graft bed. Waiting does not make an avascular recipient bed suitable. The number and timing of stages depend on tissue healing rather than a fixed four-to-six-month schedule.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[11]](#ref11)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

## Outcomes

There are no published female-specific series reporting outcomes of staged BMG urethroplasty as a distinct technique.

## Comparison — Staged BMG vs Alternatives for Complex Female Strictures

| Approach | Advantages | Disadvantages | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Staged BMG** | Allows graft maturation; applicable when all local tissue compromised | Two operations; prolonged SPC; no female-specific data; ~ 19% stage-1 revision (male data) | Obliterative stricture with no viable local tissue |
| Combined dorsal + ventral BMG (single-stage) | Single operation; augments both surfaces | Requires some native urethral wall laterally | Near-obliterative stricture with residual lumen |
| [Combined vaginal flap + BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/vaginal-flap-bmg-combined) | Single operation; vascularized tissue supports graft | Requires adequate vaginal tissue | Stricture with partial periurethral scarring |
| [Bladder wall flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/bladder-wall-flap-urethroplasty) | Pedicled, well-vascularized; bridges long defects | Abdominal approach; reduces bladder capacity | Complete urethral loss, bladder-neck involvement |
| [Labia minora pedicled flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/labia-minora-flap) (tubularization) | Maintains blood supply; single stage | Limited length; requires healthy labia | Moderate-length stricture with healthy labia |

## Advantages and Limitations

**Advantages**

- Allows graft maturation on a vascular bed before tubularization, theoretically improving survival.
- Applicable when periurethral tissue is too scarred for single-stage graft take.
- Theoretically avoids the circumferential contracture seen with primary tubularized grafts.
- Allows assessment of graft health before committing to tubularization.

**Limitations**

- **No female-specific evidence.** Entirely extrapolated from male data.
- **Two operations**, with prolonged SPC dependence between stages (several months in male series).
- **Stage-1 revision** was needed in about 19% of one male series, adding a further operation.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **LS can recur in the BMG.** Patel found stricture recurrence or recurrent LS in the first-stage graft in 24% of patients planned for two-stage repair.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Selection confounding.** The Warner male cohorts had different disease complexity and treatment selection; observed recurrence differences do not prove single-stage superiority or predict female outcomes.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Female anatomy differs.** Shorter urethra, proximity to the vaginal wall (a readily available vascular bed), and absence of corpus spongiosum mean the conditions necessitating staging in males (long penile strictures, LS-affected genital skin) are less commonly encountered.
- **Alternative tissue sources.** Vaginal and labial pedicled flaps, unique to female anatomy, reduce the need for staged free-graft approaches.

## Position in the Female Reconstructive Algorithm

The following are reasons to seek individualized reconstructive planning, not a validated hierarchy requiring exhaustion of every single-stage option:

1. Single-stage BMG (dorsal or ventral) has failed or is not feasible.
2. Vaginal and labial tissue is unavailable or compromised (LS, atrophy, prior surgery, radiation).
3. Bladder wall flap is not appropriate (no bladder-neck involvement, adequate bladder capacity must be preserved).
4. The periurethral tissue is too scarred to support a free graft in a single stage.

Published female series use single-stage techniques, with success of 83–100% in individual series of mostly short strictures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)</sup> The AUA 2023 amendment states that surgeons may reconstruct female strictures with oral mucosal grafts, vaginal flaps or a combination (Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level Grade C), reports success rates of 69–95%, and does not address staged reconstruction in women.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> The staged approach remains a theoretical option for the rare case that cannot be addressed by any single-stage technique.

## Summary

Staged BMG urethroplasty in females is a conceptual adaptation from male reconstructive urology, and no female-specific technique has been established. Male series report variable success and revision burden; their nonrandomized comparisons cannot establish comparative outcomes for women.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> Single-stage female BMG series report success of 83–100%, and vaginal, labial and bladder flaps offer alternative tissue sources unique to female anatomy. Staged BMG is therefore a salvage option for cases in which single-stage repair cannot be completed safely.
