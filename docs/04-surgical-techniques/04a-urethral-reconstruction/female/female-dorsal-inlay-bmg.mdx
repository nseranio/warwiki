# Female Dorsal Inlay BMG Urethroplasty

**Female dorsal inlay urethroplasty** is a minimally invasive technique for female urethral stricture (FUS) in which a buccal mucosal graft is placed into the opened urethral lumen from the **dorsal (12 o'clock) aspect via a transurethral approach**, **without** extensive urethral mobilization or vaginal-wall dissection. Small early series report high short-term patency and outpatient feasibility. They do not establish zero complication risk or superiority over open graft techniques.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For the traditional dorsal-onlay alternative, see [Female Dorsal Onlay Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-dorsal-onlay-urethroplasty). For the ventral counterpart, see [Female Ventral Inlay BMG (Mandal RCT)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-ventral-inlay-bmg) and [Female Ventral Onlay Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-ventral-onlay-urethroplasty). For the broader ladder, see [Female DVIU and Urethral Dilation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-dviu-dilation).

---

## Inlay vs Onlay — the Defining Distinction

| Feature | Dorsal Inlay | Dorsal Onlay (traditional) |
|---|---|---|
| Access | **Transurethral** (nasal speculum) or small suprameatal | Suprameatal incision between the meatus and clitoral region |
| Vaginal-wall incision | None in the transurethral variant | Not inherent to dorsal urethral access; a separate vaginal donor-site incision is needed when harvesting vaginal graft |
| Urethral mobilization | **Minimal** — superficial dorsal urethrotomy only | Extensive dorsal dissection |
| Graft fixation | Sutures (proximal / distal) + surgical glue or quilting | Running / interrupted sutures + quilting |
| Operative footprint | Same-day discharge reported in a small series | Discharge varies with procedure, patient and local practice |

The technique mirrors the [ventral inlay (Mandal / Gaur)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-ventral-inlay-bmg) approach but applied dorsally — combining the dorsal periurethral tissue bed (favorable for graft take) with the minimally invasive transurethral approach.

---

## Historical Development

- **Tsivian-Sidi 2006** — first described, using a **suprameatal incision** + dorsal inlay (vaginal or buccal graft). 3 patients, all normal micturition / no recurrence at up to 27 mo.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Petrou 2012** — refined Tsivian-Sidi suprameatal technique with autologous **vaginal-epithelium inlay** in 11 women: no repeat surgery reported at mean 22.7 mo (not equivalent to freedom from all recurrence or dilation); Qmax 7.3 → 21.8 mL/s; PVR 187 → 76 mL; 0% de-novo SUI.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Linder / Balzano / Warner 2022** — first **fully transurethral** outpatient approach using a nasal speculum + suture-passing device.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Jefferson 2025** — largest transurethral dorsal-inlay series to date (n = 21).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Indications

- Recurrent / refractory FUS after failed dilation or DVIU.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Strictures of any urethral segment — Jefferson 2025 distribution: distal 33%, mid 19%, proximal 19%, multifocal / pan-urethral 19%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- AUA 2023 endorses urethroplasty for FUS (69–95% success across techniques).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

Particularly advantageous when:

- Minimally invasive, **outpatient / same-day discharge** is desired.
- **Minimal urethral mobilization** is important (prior pelvic surgery, sphincter-disruption concern).
- **Proximal urethral strictures** — the transurethral approach avoids the dorsal periurethral dissection used in traditional dorsal onlay.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Technique — Transurethral Dorsal Inlay (Linder / Jefferson)

Dorsal lithotomy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

1. **Setup** — urethral catheter; lithotomy.
2. **BMG harvest** — inner cheek; avoid Stensen's duct; defat / trim.
3. **Transurethral access** — **nasal speculum** placed transurethrally distal to the stricture for visualization.
4. **Dorsal urethrotomy** — superficial **12 o'clock** incision through the full length of the strictured segment. **No urethral mobilization** off surrounding tissue.
5. **Graft delivery and proximal fixation** — a **suture-passing device** places typically 3 sutures at the **bladder neck** (proximal extent) and through the BMG; ties are tied transurethrally with a **laparoscopic knot pusher**, parachuting the graft into the proximal end.
6. **Distal fixation** — distal end of the BMG secured to the meatus with interrupted sutures.
7. **Mid-graft fixation** — **surgical (tissue) adhesive** secures the midportion of the graft to the underlying dorsal periurethral tissue.
8. **Catheter** — urethral catheter for **3 weeks**.
9. **Discharge** — **same day** in the Jefferson series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Technical principles** — minimal mobilization (the urethra is *not* dissected from surrounding tissue); graft survives on the native dorsal periurethral bed via imbibition / inosculation; suture (proximal / distal) + glue (mid) gives secure fixation without extensive intraurethral suturing.

## Technique — Suprameatal Dorsal Inlay (Tsivian-Sidi / Petrou)

Intermediate between fully transurethral and traditional dorsal onlay:<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

1. **Suprameatal incision** above the meatus between the meatus and clitoral region.
2. **Limited dorsal dissection** of the distal urethra through this incision.
3. **Dorsal urethrotomy** at 12 o'clock through the stricture.
4. **Graft harvest** — vaginal-wall graft (1.5 cm wide) or BMG.
5. **Inlay placement** — mucosal surface against the urethral lumen, sutured with running 5-0 polyglactin to the open urethra.
6. **Catheters** — 18 Fr urethral × 2 wk; 16 Fr SPC × 3 wk.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Combined Dorsal + Ventral Inlay for Obliterative Strictures

Jefferson 2025 reported that **2 of 21 (10%)** required a combined dorsal + ventral inlay through the transurethral approach for **obliterative** strictures — augmentation of both walls, rather than excision and circumferential tubularized free-graft replacement.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Outcomes

| Series | n | Approach | Graft | Success | Follow-up | Key findings |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jefferson 2025<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 21 | Transurethral dorsal inlay | BMG | **95% at 4 mo; 92% at 12 mo** | median 13.2 mo | Same-day discharge; OR median 60 min; 0% complications; 0% SUI |
| Petrou 2012<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | 11 | Suprameatal dorsal inlay | Vaginal graft | No repeat surgery reported; not a complete recurrence-free endpoint | mean 22.7 mo | Qmax 7.3 → 21.8; PVR 187 → 76; 0% SUI |
| Tsivian-Sidi 2006<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | 3 | Suprameatal dorsal inlay | Vaginal / BMG | **100%** | up to 27 mo | First description; all normal micturition |
| Linder 2022<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | 1 | Transurethral dorsal inlay | BMG | **100%** | 3 mo | First transurethral approach; outpatient; proximal stricture |

Functional improvements consistent: Qmax 7.0 → 13.0–13.3 mL/s (Jefferson); 7.3 → 21.8 mL/s (Petrou); PVR 187 → 76 mL; 90% improved on Global Response Assessment; **0% de-novo SUI** across all series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Continence Interpretation

No de novo SUI was observed in the 21-patient Jefferson series. This small uncontrolled experience cannot show that dorsal inlay is safer for continence than dorsal onlay, and pooled complication percentages from different operations should not be compared as though randomized. Minimal mobilization is a technical rationale, not proof of protection from SUI.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

---

## Advantages

- **Minimal urethral mobilization** — defining advantage; preserves periurethral blood supply and sphincter integrity.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Same-day discharge** in all Jefferson 2025 patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Short OR time** — median 60 min (IQR 56–86).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **No vaginal-wall incision** with the transurethral variant.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **0% complications** in the largest series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **0% de-novo SUI** across all reported series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Versatile location** — applied to distal / mid / proximal / pan-urethral disease.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Robust dorsal graft bed** — same biology that supports dorsal onlay success.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Limitations

- **Very limited evidence base** — largest series 21 patients; median follow-up 13.2 mo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Short follow-up** — 92% at 12 mo may evolve with longer follow-up; late recurrences are known with urethroplasty generally.
- **Limited visualization** — nasal-speculum field is narrow.
- **Glue-based mid-graft fixation** — long-term durability of tissue adhesive in this role is uncertain.
- **No comparative data** — no study compares dorsal inlay to dorsal onlay in women. A small female trial compared **ventral inlay vs dorsal onlay** (Mandal 2025); it is not a comparison of dorsal inlay with dorsal onlay.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Single-center experience** — primarily Mayo Clinic (Linder / Jefferson); multicenter validation pending.
- **Obliterative strictures** — 10% required combined dorsal + ventral inlay, suggesting dorsal alone may be insufficient for the most severe disease.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Comparison With Other Graft Approaches

Transurethral dorsal inlay reduces external dissection, while traditional dorsal onlay offers direct access to the dorsal recipient bed. Ventral inlay preserves the vagina through a different urethral access plane. Published cohorts differ in anatomy, tissue source and follow-up; their operating times, catheter durations, patency and continence rates cannot establish a reliable ranking.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

---

## Patient-Selection Pearls

The dorsal inlay approach may be particularly well-suited for:

- Patients prioritizing a **minimally invasive, outpatient procedure**.
- **Proximal urethral strictures** where transurethral access avoids deep vaginal dissection.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Patients in whom minimizing dissection is a goal, with explicit counseling that a continence advantage has not been proven.
- **Multifocal / pan-urethral strictures** — successfully applied in the Jefferson series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Patients with prior pelvic surgery or altered vaginal anatomy.

For **obliterative strictures**, plan for possible combined dorsal + ventral inlay.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Guideline Position

AUA 2023 recommends urethroplasty for FUS with success rates of 69–95% across techniques driven by surgeon experience.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> The dorsal-inlay technique is not specifically named but falls within the umbrella of dorsal oral-mucosa-graft approaches. Given novelty and small evidence base, it is not yet a standard technique but represents a **promising evolution** of the dorsal approach.

---

## Summary

Female dorsal inlay BMG urethroplasty is the **newest and least studied** of the four major female urethroplasty approaches. Its key feature is **transurethral graft placement with minimal urethral mobilization**. Early reports document feasibility; small cohorts and incomplete long-term follow-up limit safety and durability conclusions. Larger multicenter studies and direct comparisons with dorsal onlay are needed before it can be considered standard.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
