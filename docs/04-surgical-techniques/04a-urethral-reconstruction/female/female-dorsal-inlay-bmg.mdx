# Female Dorsal Inlay BMG Urethroplasty

**Female dorsal inlay urethroplasty** is a minimally invasive technique for female urethral stricture (FUS) in which a buccal mucosal graft is placed into the opened urethral lumen from the dorsal (12 o'clock) aspect via a transurethral approach, without extensive urethral mobilization or vaginal-wall dissection. Small early series report high short-term patency and outpatient feasibility. They do not establish zero complication risk or superiority over open graft techniques.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For the traditional dorsal-onlay alternative, see [Female Dorsal Onlay Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-dorsal-onlay-urethroplasty). For the ventral counterpart, see [Female Ventral Inlay BMG (Mandal RCT)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-ventral-inlay-bmg) and [Female Ventral Onlay Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-ventral-onlay-urethroplasty). For the broader ladder, see [Female DVIU and Urethral Dilation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-dviu-dilation).

---

## Inlay vs Onlay — the Defining Distinction

| Feature | Dorsal Inlay | Dorsal Onlay (traditional) |
|---|---|---|
| Access | Transurethral (nasal speculum) or small suprameatal | Suprameatal incision between the meatus and clitoral region |
| Vaginal-wall incision | None in the transurethral variant | Not inherent to dorsal urethral access; a separate vaginal donor-site incision is needed when harvesting vaginal graft |
| Urethral mobilization | Minimal (superficial dorsal urethrotomy only) | Extensive dorsal dissection |
| Graft fixation | Sutures (proximal / distal) + surgical glue or quilting | Running / interrupted sutures + quilting |
| Operative footprint | Same-day discharge reported in a small series | Discharge varies with procedure, patient and local practice |

The technique mirrors the [ventral inlay (Mandal / Gaur)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-ventral-inlay-bmg) approach but is applied dorsally, combining the dorsal periurethral tissue bed (favorable for graft take) with the minimally invasive transurethral approach.

---

## Historical Development

- **Tsivian-Sidi 2006.** First described, using a suprameatal incision and dorsal inlay (vaginal or buccal graft). 3 patients, all with normal micturition and no additional treatment required at 1, 8 and 27 mo of follow-up.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Petrou 2012.** Refined the Tsivian-Sidi suprameatal technique with autologous vaginal-epithelium inlay in 11 women: no repeat surgery reported at mean 22.7 mo (not equivalent to freedom from all recurrence or dilation); Qmax rose from 7.3 to 21.8 mL/s; PVR fell from 187 to 76 mL; 0% de-novo SUI.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Linder / Balzano / Warner 2022.** Case report (1 patient) of a fully transurethral outpatient approach, using a nasal speculum and suture-passing device.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Jefferson 2025.** Retrospective series of 21 women treated with the transurethral dorsal inlay, the largest reported at publication.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Indications

- Recurrent or refractory FUS after failed dilation or DVIU.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Strictures of any urethral segment. Jefferson 2025 distribution: distal 33%, mid 19%, proximal 19%, multifocal or pan-urethral 19%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- The 2023 AUA amendment states that urethroplasty should be offered to women with urethral stricture; it reports success rates of 69–95% across oral mucosa graft and vaginal flap techniques.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

Particularly advantageous when:

- Minimally invasive, outpatient or same-day discharge is desired.
- Minimal urethral mobilization is a goal (prior pelvic surgery, sphincter-disruption concern).
- Proximal urethral strictures, where the transurethral approach avoids the dorsal periurethral dissection used in traditional dorsal onlay.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Technique — Transurethral Dorsal Inlay (Linder / Jefferson)

Dorsal lithotomy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

1. **Setup.** Urethral catheter; lithotomy.
2. **BMG harvest.** Inner cheek; avoid Stensen's duct; defat and trim.
3. **Transurethral access.** A nasal speculum is placed transurethrally distal to the stricture for visualization.
4. **Dorsal urethrotomy.** Superficial 12 o'clock incision through the full length of the strictured segment. No urethral mobilization off surrounding tissue.
5. **Graft delivery and proximal fixation.** A suture-passing device places typically 3 sutures at the bladder neck (proximal extent) and through the BMG; ties are tied transurethrally with a laparoscopic knot pusher, parachuting the graft into the proximal end.
6. **Distal fixation.** Distal end of the BMG secured to the meatus with interrupted sutures.
7. **Mid-graft fixation.** Surgical (tissue) adhesive secures the midportion of the graft to the underlying dorsal periurethral tissue.
8. **Catheter.** Urethral catheter for 3 weeks.
9. **Discharge.** Same day in the Jefferson series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Technical principles.** The urethra is not dissected from surrounding tissue. The graft survives on the native dorsal periurethral bed by imbibition and inosculation. Proximal and distal sutures plus mid-graft glue secure the graft without extensive intraurethral suturing.

## Technique — Suprameatal Dorsal Inlay (Tsivian-Sidi / Petrou)

Intermediate between fully transurethral and traditional dorsal onlay:<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

1. **Suprameatal incision** above the meatus between the meatus and clitoral region.
2. **Limited dorsal dissection** of the distal urethra through this incision.
3. **Dorsal urethrotomy** at 12 o'clock through the stricture.
4. **Graft harvest.** Vaginal-wall graft (1.5 cm wide) or BMG.
5. **Inlay placement.** Mucosal surface against the urethral lumen, sutured with running 5-0 polyglactin to the open urethra.
6. **Catheters.** 18 Fr urethral × 2 wk; 16 Fr SPC × 3 wk.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Combined Dorsal + Ventral Inlay for Obliterative Strictures

Jefferson 2025 reported that 2 of 21 (10%) required a combined dorsal and ventral inlay through the transurethral approach for obliterative strictures. Both walls were augmented, rather than excising the stricture and replacing it with a circumferential tubularized free graft.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Outcomes

| Series | n | Approach | Graft | Success | Follow-up | Key findings |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jefferson 2025<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 21 | Transurethral dorsal inlay | BMG | 95% at 4 mo; 92% at 12 mo (no recurrence on cystourethroscopy) | median 13.2 mo | Same-day discharge; OR median 60 min; 0% complications; 0% de novo SUI; 1 retreatment |
| Petrou 2012<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | 11 | Suprameatal dorsal inlay | Vaginal graft | No repeat surgery reported; not a complete recurrence-free endpoint | mean 22.7 mo | Qmax 7.3 → 21.8; PVR 187 → 76; 0% SUI |
| Tsivian-Sidi 2006<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | 3 | Suprameatal dorsal inlay | Vaginal / BMG | No additional treatment | 1, 8 and 27 mo | First description; all normal micturition |
| Linder 2022<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | 1 | Transurethral dorsal inlay | BMG | Symptomatic improvement, no stress incontinence | 3 mo | First transurethral approach; outpatient; proximal stricture |

In the Jefferson series, median Qmax rose from 7.0 to 13.0 mL/s at 4 months and 13.3 mL/s at 12 months (p = 0.20 and 0.50), and 90% reported improvement on the Global Response Assessment. In the Petrou series, Qmax rose from 7.3 to 21.8 mL/s and PVR fell from 187 to 76 mL. No de novo SUI was reported in any of the series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Continence Interpretation

No de novo SUI was observed in the 21-patient Jefferson series. This small uncontrolled experience cannot show that dorsal inlay is safer for continence than dorsal onlay, and pooled complication percentages from different operations should not be compared as though randomized. Minimal mobilization is a technical rationale, not proof of protection from SUI.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

---

## Advantages

- **Minimal urethral mobilization** is the rationale for preserving periurethral blood supply and sphincter integrity.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- All Jefferson 2025 patients were discharged the same day.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Median operative time was 60 min (IQR 56–86).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- The transurethral variant needs no vaginal-wall incision.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- No complications occurred in the 21-patient series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- No de novo SUI was reported across the series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- The technique has been applied to distal, mid, proximal and pan-urethral disease.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- The dorsal graft bed has the same biology that supports dorsal onlay.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Limitations

- **Very limited evidence base.** Largest series 21 patients; median follow-up 13.2 mo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Short follow-up.** The 92% at 12 mo may evolve with longer follow-up; late recurrences are known with urethroplasty generally.
- **Limited visualization.** The nasal-speculum field is narrow.
- **Glue-based mid-graft fixation.** Long-term durability of tissue adhesive in this role is uncertain.
- **No comparative data.** No study compares dorsal inlay to dorsal onlay in women. A small female trial compared ventral inlay versus dorsal onlay (Mandal 2025); it is not a comparison of dorsal inlay with dorsal onlay.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Single-center experience.** The Linder and Jefferson reports come from one institution, and no multicenter series has been reported.
- **Obliterative strictures.** 10% required combined dorsal and ventral inlay, suggesting dorsal alone may be insufficient for the most severe disease.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Comparison With Other Graft Approaches

Transurethral dorsal inlay reduces external dissection, while traditional dorsal onlay offers direct access to the dorsal recipient bed. Ventral inlay preserves the vagina through a different urethral access plane. Published cohorts differ in anatomy, tissue source and follow-up; their operating times, catheter durations, patency and continence rates cannot establish a reliable ranking.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

---

## Patient-Selection Pearls

The dorsal inlay approach may be particularly well-suited for:

- Patients prioritizing a minimally invasive, outpatient procedure.
- Proximal urethral strictures, where transurethral access avoids deep vaginal dissection.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Patients in whom minimizing dissection is a goal, with explicit counseling that a continence advantage has not been proven.
- Multifocal or pan-urethral strictures, which were treated in the Jefferson series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Patients with prior pelvic surgery or altered vaginal anatomy.

For obliterative strictures, plan for possible combined dorsal and ventral inlay.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Guideline Position

The 2023 AUA amendment (Statement 26, Moderate Recommendation, Evidence Level C) states that surgeons may reconstruct female strictures with oral mucosal grafts, vaginal flaps or a combination. It reports success rates of 69–95%, and technique is chosen by surgeon experience.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Dorsal inlay is not named separately but is a dorsal oral mucosa graft approach. Its evidence base is small, and it is not yet a standard technique.

---

## Summary

Female dorsal inlay BMG urethroplasty places the graft transurethrally with minimal urethral mobilization. Early reports document feasibility. Small cohorts and short follow-up limit conclusions about safety and durability.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
