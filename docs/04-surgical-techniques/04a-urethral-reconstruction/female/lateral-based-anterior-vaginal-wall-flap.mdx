# Lateral-Based Anterior Vaginal Wall Flap (Romero-Maroto / Simonato)

The **lateral-based anterior vaginal wall flap urethroplasty** is a modification of the classic [Blandy U-flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-vaginal-flap-urethroplasty) developed specifically to address the **retrusive-meatus and inward-stream** problem of the proximal-pedicled design. The lateral-based variant was described independently by **Romero-Maroto 2018** and **Simonato 2010**, both adapting the male **Orandi 1968** dartos-pedicled penile flap to the anterior vaginal wall.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For the broader female-stricture treatment ladder, see [Female DVIU and Urethral Dilation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-dviu-dilation), [Distal Urethrectomy with Advancement Meatoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/distal-urethrectomy-advancement), [Female Dorsal Onlay BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-dorsal-onlay-urethroplasty), and [Ventral Onlay BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/ventral-onlay-omg).

---

## Rationale — Why a Lateral Pedicle

The Blandy U-flap pulls the neomeatus posteriorly because its pedicle is proximal; this produces a **retrusive meatus and an inward urinary stream**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> A lateral pedicle:

- **Preserves the natural vascular axis** of the anterior vaginal wall along the lateral pedicle.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Keeps the meatus orthotopic** with normal anterior stream direction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Eliminates urinary spraying** reported with classic VFU (up to 29% in Higuchi 2026).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

---

## Indications

- FUS refractory to dilation or DVIU.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Distal, mid, or pan-urethral** strictures — Simonato extended the technique to long-segment disease.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Healthy, well-estrogenized anterior vaginal wall (no LS, no severe atrophy).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Contraindications

- LS or vaginal atrophy compromising flap quality.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Prior pelvic radiation.
- Prior synthetic midurethral sling with periurethral scarring (Önol used Martius-reinforced ventral BMG instead in this scenario).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

---

## Romero-Maroto Technique (Eur Urol 2018)

Dorsal lithotomy, regional or general anesthesia.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

1. **Cystourethroscopy** — confirm stricture location and length.
2. **Ventral urethrotomy** — longitudinal **6 o'clock** incision through the full stricture into healthy mucosa proximally and distally.
3. **Lateral flap design** — rectangular or trapezoidal anterior-vaginal-wall flap outlined **lateral to the urethra**, with the **vascular pedicle based laterally** (Orandi analog).
4. **Flap elevation** — flap raised off the periurethral tissue, lateral pedicle preserved, then mobilized medially.
5. **Flap inlay** — flap rotated medially into the ventral urethrotomy defect; medial and opposite edges sutured to the urethral mucosa with interrupted 4-0 / 5-0 polyglactin.
6. **Donor-site closure** — primary; the lateral pedicle keeps the meatus in its **orthotopic position** without posterior retraction.
7. **Catheter** — postoperative urethral catheter.

## Simonato Technique (J Urol 2010) — De-Epithelialization Refinement

Same lateral-pedicled design, with one key technical addition:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- **Partial de-epithelialization** of the flap edges where sutures are placed. The exposed submucosal connective tissue promotes **scar (cicatrix) formation at the suture line** and reduces fistula risk by avoiding epithelium-to-epithelium apposition.
- Allows reconstruction of long segments including **pan-urethral** strictures.

---

## Lateral-Based Flap vs Classic Blandy U-Flap

| Feature | Classic Blandy U-Flap | Lateral-Based Flap (Romero-Maroto / Simonato) |
|---|---|---|
| Pedicle orientation | Proximal | Lateral |
| Vascular axis | May be compromised by midline dissection | Preserved along lateral pedicle |
| Meatal position | Risk of retrusive | Orthotopic |
| Urinary stream | Inward / posterior reported | Normal anterior preserved |
| Fistula prevention | Standard suture line | De-epithelialization of suture edges (Simonato) |
| Stricture length | Distal / mid | Distal / mid / pan-urethral |
| Urinary spraying | Reported | Not reported |

## Outcomes

### Romero-Maroto 2018 — Eur Urol<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- **n = 9**, mean age 56 yr (range 41–78); mean follow-up **80.7 mo** (range 12–198 mo).
- 100% symptom relief; urethral caliber **10.8 → ≥ 20 Fr**; **Qmax 6.8 → 21 mL/s** (3-fold).
- **0% recurrence, 0% de novo SUI, 0% fistula, 0% spraying**; all neomeatuses orthotopic.

### Simonato 2010 — J Urol<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- **n = 6** with extensive strictures (5 mid-to-distal, 1 pan-urethral with bilateral hydronephrosis).
- Mean follow-up **70.8 mo**.
- Normal micturition in all after catheter removal; **5/6 (83%)** required no further treatment; 1/6 (17%) required CIC for elevated PVR (impaired detrusor function rather than recurrence).
- **0% reoperation for recurrence, 0% de novo SUI, 0% fistula** (attributed to de-epithelialization).

## Advantages

1. **Orthotopic meatus** — the most clinically significant gain over Blandy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
2. **Preserved vascular axis** along the lateral pedicle.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
3. **Versatility** — Simonato extended the technique to long / pan-urethral disease.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
4. **No spraying** in either reported series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
5. **De-epithelialization (Simonato)** — fistula prevention without donor-site morbidity.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
6. **No donor-site morbidity** — same operative field.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Limitations and Caveats

- **Tiny evidence base** — the two primary series total **15 patients** (Level 4).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Tissue-quality dependence** — like all VFU techniques, requires healthy vaginal tissue.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **VFU vs BMG** — Kumar 2025 SR / meta of 18 studies found **no significant difference** in success between BMG (86.2%) and vaginal-wall graft (89.8%), Qmax, or PVR.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> Counterpoints: Kowalik 2014 reported 0% recurrence with dorsal BMG vs 40% with VFU; Higuchi 2026 reported 87.5% (DOBMGU) vs 57.1% (VFU). These studies pooled mixed VFU techniques (not exclusively the lateral-based modification).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Detrusor function** — 17% (1/6 in Simonato) required CIC for elevated PVR despite a patent urethra.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Martius Adjunct

The Martius labial fat-pad flap can interpose vascularized tissue between the urethral repair and the vaginal closure, particularly when tissue quality is suspect or fistula risk is elevated.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

- Malde 2017 reviewed Martius use in female urology (12 women in the urethroplasty subset).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- Blaivas-Heritz 1996 used Martius in 47/49 vaginal flap reconstructions, achieving 87% continence.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- Flisser-Blaivas 2003 reported 93% anatomic success with single-stage VFU + Martius + pubovaginal sling.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- Önol 2011 used Martius-reinforced ventral BMG specifically when prior synthetic midurethral sling created periurethral scarring.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## When to Choose This Technique

| Scenario | Recommended | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Distal / mid stricture, healthy vaginal tissue | Lateral-based flap | Orthotopic meatus, no donor-site morbidity, durable in long-FU series<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Pan-urethral / long-segment | Lateral-based (Simonato) or dorsal BMG | Both can cover length; BMG is more versatile<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| LS / atrophy | Dorsal onlay BMG | Vaginal tissue unsuitable<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Prior MUS with periurethral scarring | Martius-reinforced ventral BMG | Scarring limits flap mobilization<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Concern for retraction / spraying | Lateral-based flap | Specifically designed to prevent both<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |

AUA 2023 frames urethroplasty success in women as 69–95% across techniques, with selection driven by surgeon experience.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

---

## Postoperative Management

- Urethral catheter **10–21 days**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Voiding trial with uroflowmetry and PVR.
- Cystoscopy at 3–6 months.
- Topical estrogen in postmenopausal women.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Long-term surveillance — late recurrences ≥ 5 yr have been reported with VFU generally.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

---

## Summary

The lateral-based anterior vaginal wall flap is a **refined Blandy modification** that specifically corrects the retrusive-meatus and inward-stream problems of the classic U-flap. With **100% success at ~ 6.7 yr** (Romero-Maroto, n = 9) and **83% success at ~ 5.9 yr** (Simonato, n = 6, with the de-epithelialization fistula-prevention refinement), durability appears excellent. Sample sizes remain small (n = 15 combined across both series), but the technique is a compelling option for women with healthy vaginal tissue who prefer single-stage repair without oral-mucosa donor-site morbidity.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
