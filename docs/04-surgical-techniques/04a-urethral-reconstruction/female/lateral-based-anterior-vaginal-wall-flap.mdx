# Lateral-Based Anterior Vaginal Wall Flap (Romero-Maroto / Simonato)

The **lateral-based anterior vaginal wall flap urethroplasty** is a modification of the classic [Blandy U-flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-vaginal-flap-urethroplasty) developed specifically to address the retrusive-meatus and inward-stream problem of the proximal-pedicled design. The lateral-based variant was described independently by Romero-Maroto 2018 and Simonato 2010, and both were inspired by the male Orandi technique (1968). The EAU 2026 update classifies these repairs as lateral C flaps.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

For the broader female-stricture treatment ladder, see [Female DVIU and Urethral Dilation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-dviu-dilation), [Distal Urethrectomy with Advancement Meatoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/distal-urethrectomy-advancement), [Female Dorsal Onlay BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-dorsal-onlay-urethroplasty), and [Ventral Onlay BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-ventral-onlay-urethroplasty).

---

## Rationale — Why a Lateral Pedicle

The Blandy technique produced a retrusive meatus and an inward urinary stream in the Romero-Maroto patients, and the proximal pedicle is the presumed cause.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> A lateral pedicle has the following aims:

- It preserves the vascular axis of the anterior vaginal wall along the lateral pedicle.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- It keeps the meatus orthotopic, with normal anterior stream direction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- It aims to avoid the urinary spraying reported after vaginal flap urethroplasty (2 of 7 patients in Higuchi 2026, flap type not specified).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

---

## Indications

- FUS refractory to dilation or DVIU.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Distal, mid, or pan-urethral strictures.** Simonato extended the technique to long-segment disease.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Healthy, well-estrogenized anterior vaginal wall (no LS, no severe atrophy).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Contraindications

- LS or vaginal atrophy compromising flap quality.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Prior pelvic radiation.
- Prior synthetic midurethral sling with periurethral scarring (Önol used Martius-reinforced ventral BMG instead in this scenario).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

---

## Romero-Maroto Technique (Eur Urol 2018)

Dorsal lithotomy, regional or general anesthesia.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

1. **Cystourethroscopy.** Confirm stricture location and length.
2. **Ventral urethrotomy.** Longitudinal 6 o'clock incision through the full stricture into healthy mucosa proximally and distally.
3. **Lateral flap design.** Rectangular or trapezoidal anterior-vaginal-wall flap outlined lateral to the urethra, with the vascular pedicle based laterally (Orandi analog).
4. **Flap elevation.** Flap raised off the periurethral tissue, lateral pedicle preserved, then mobilized medially.
5. **Flap inlay.** Flap rotated medially into the ventral urethrotomy defect; medial and opposite edges sutured to the urethral mucosa with interrupted absorbable sutures.
6. **Donor-site closure.** Primary; the lateral pedicle keeps the meatus in its orthotopic position without posterior retraction.
7. **Catheter.** Postoperative urethral catheter.

## Simonato Technique (J Urol 2010) — De-Epithelialization Refinement

Same lateral-pedicled design, with one technical addition:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- **Partial de-epithelialization** of the flap edges where sutures are placed. The exposed submucosal connective tissue promotes scar (cicatrix) formation at the suture line, which Simonato designed to avoid fistula formation.
- Allows reconstruction of long segments including pan-urethral strictures.

## Lateral-Based Flap vs Classic Blandy U-Flap

| Feature | Classic Blandy U-Flap | Lateral-Based Flap (Romero-Maroto / Simonato) |
|---|---|---|
| Pedicle orientation | Proximal | Lateral |
| Vascular axis | Proximal pedicle | Preserved along lateral pedicle |
| Meatal position | Risk of retrusive | Orthotopic |
| Urinary stream | Inward / posterior reported | Normal anterior preserved |
| Fistula prevention | Standard suture line | De-epithelialization of suture edges (Simonato) |
| Stricture length | Distal / mid | Distal / mid / pan-urethral |
| Urinary spraying | Reported | Not reported |

## Outcomes

### Romero-Maroto 2018 — Eur Urol<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- Nine women operated on between 1993 and 2015 were contacted and examined; the study was a cross-sectional observational study with chart review. Mean age was 56 yr (range 41–78); mean follow-up was 80.7 mo (range 12–198 mo).
- 100% symptom relief; urethral caliber rose from 10.8 to ≥ 20 Fr; Qmax rose from 6.8 to 21 mL/s (3-fold).
- **0% recurrence, 0% de novo SUI, 0% fistula, 0% spraying**; all neomeatuses orthotopic.

### Simonato 2010 — J Urol<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- **n = 6** with extensive strictures (5 mid-to-distal, 1 pan-urethral with bilateral hydronephrosis).
- Mean follow-up 70.8 mo.
- Normal micturition in all after catheter removal; 5/6 (83%) required no further treatment; 1/6 (17%) required CIC for significant residual urine.

## Advantages

1. **Orthotopic meatus.** This is the main reported gain over the Blandy flap.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
2. **Preserved vascular axis** along the lateral pedicle.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
3. **Versatility.** Simonato extended the technique to long or pan-urethral disease.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
4. **No spraying** was reported in the Romero-Maroto series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
5. **De-epithelialization (Simonato).** Fistula prevention without donor-site morbidity.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
6. **No oral donor-site morbidity.** The vaginal donor site still has wound and scarring risks.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Limitations and Caveats

- **Small evidence base.** The two primary series total 15 patients (Level 4). The EAU 2026 update lists 47 women treated with a lateral C flap across two studies, and its pooled vaginal flap data (161 women) show 81.4% patency (range 67–100%) and new-onset urinary incontinence in 9.3%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Tissue-quality dependence.** Like all VFU techniques, requires healthy vaginal tissue.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Vaginal graft versus flap distinction.** Kumar 2025 SR / meta of 18 studies found no significant difference in success between BMG (86.2%) and vaginal-wall graft (89.8%), Qmax, or PVR.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> A free-graft comparison is not direct evidence about a pedicled lateral flap. Separately, Kowalik 2014 (10 women) reported two recurrences after VFU and none after dorsal BMG, and Higuchi 2026 reported 87.5% (DOBMGU) versus 57.1% (VFU). The abstracts do not specify the flap subtype, so the results may not apply to the lateral-based modification.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Residual urine.** One of 6 women in Simonato's series required CIC for significant residual urine.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Martius Adjunct

The Martius labial fat-pad flap can interpose vascularized tissue between the urethral repair and the vaginal closure, particularly when tissue quality is suspect or fistula risk is elevated.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

- Malde 2017 reviewed Martius use in female urology (12 women in the urethroplasty subset).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- Blaivas-Heritz 1996 used Martius in 47 of 49 women with urethral or vesical neck damage, with a pubovaginal sling in 41. Continence was achieved in 87% at 1 to 11 years.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- Flisser-Blaivas 2003 reported 93% anatomic success by a single vaginal flap procedure in 72 women (56 with a concomitant pubovaginal sling), median follow-up 1.5 years.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- Önol 2011 used Martius-reinforced ventral BMG specifically when prior synthetic midurethral sling created periurethral scarring.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## When to Choose This Technique

| Scenario | Reconstructive option | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Distal / mid stricture, healthy vaginal tissue | Lateral-based flap | Orthotopic meatus and no oral donor site; mean follow-up 6–7 yr in two small series<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Pan-urethral / long-segment | Lateral-based (Simonato) or dorsal BMG | Both can cover length; BMG is more versatile<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| LS / atrophy | Dorsal onlay BMG | Vaginal tissue unsuitable<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Prior MUS with periurethral scarring | Martius-reinforced ventral BMG | Scarring limits flap mobilization<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Concern for retraction / spraying | Lateral-based flap | Designed to keep the meatus orthotopic<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |

The AUA 2023 amendment states that surgeons may reconstruct female strictures with oral mucosal grafts, vaginal flaps or a combination (Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level C). It cites success of 69–95% across techniques and selection by surgeon experience.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

---

## Postoperative Management

- Urethral catheter 10–21 days.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Voiding trial with uroflowmetry and PVR.
- Cystoscopy at 3–6 months.
- Topical estrogen in postmenopausal women.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Long-term surveillance — late recurrences ≥ 5 yr have been reported with VFU generally.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

---

## Summary

The lateral-based anterior vaginal wall flap is a modification of the Blandy U-flap intended to avoid the retrusive meatus and inward stream of the classic design. Romero-Maroto reported 100% success at a mean of 6.7 yr (n = 9), and Simonato reported 83% success at a mean of 5.9 yr (n = 6, with partial flap de-epithelialization). The series are small (n = 15 combined). The technique is an option for women with healthy vaginal tissue who want single-stage repair without an oral-mucosa donor site.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
