# Distal Urethrectomy with Advancement Meatoplasty (Female)

**Distal urethrectomy with advancement meatoplasty** is a single-stage repair for **short distal (meatal) female urethral strictures** in which the strictured distal segment is excised and a new meatus is created using mobilized anterior vaginal wall mucosa. No graft or oral-mucosa harvest is required.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For the broader female-stricture treatment ladder and endoscopic alternatives, see [Female DVIU and Urethral Dilation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-dviu-dilation). For longer / more proximal strictures requiring augmentation, see [Female Dorsal Onlay BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-dorsal-onlay-urethroplasty), [Ventral Onlay BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/ventral-onlay-omg), and [Blandy U-Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-vaginal-flap-urethroplasty).

---

## Indications

- **Distal (meatal / near-meatal) female urethral stricture** that has failed endoscopic management.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Stricture confined to the **distal one-third** of the urethra.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Meatal stenosis refractory to conservative management.
- LS-associated meatal stricture (some authors prefer BMG in this setting given LS recurrence risk in local tissue).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Oncologic context** — distal urethral resection during vulvectomy for vulvar cancer involving the distal urethra; the same advancement principle is used to construct the neomeatus.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

---

## Contraindications / Relative Limitations

- Strictures extending into the **mid or proximal urethra** — augmentation urethroplasty (vaginal flap or BMG) is required rather than simple excision and advancement.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- LS or atrophy compromising the anterior vaginal wall flap quality.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Stricture involving or approaching the **rhabdosphincter** (mid-to-proximal urethra) — resection risks de novo SUI.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

---

## Surgical Anatomy — the Sphincter Limit

The female urethral sphincter (rhabdosphincter) is concentrated in the **middle and distal thirds** of the urethra; the compressor urethrae lies in the distal third. This is the anatomic basis for the central rule of this operation: **resect no more than the distal 1–1.5 cm**.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- Reid 1990 — **all 4 patients** in whom a portion of the distal urethra was excised during vulvectomy developed stress or total incontinence, with significant decreases in functional urethral length and distal pressure-transmission ratios.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- Hampl 2011 — partial distal-urethral resection of **up to 1.5 cm** preserved urodynamic continence in **18 / 19 (95%)** anterior-vulvar-cancer patients, although 26% reported subjective voiding disturbance.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

The 1.5-cm limit is the practical ceiling carried into FUS practice.

## Technique

Dorsal lithotomy under regional or general anesthesia.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

1. **Cystourethroscopy** to confirm stricture location / extent and rule out proximal / sphincteric involvement.
2. **Circumferential meatal incision** just proximal to the diseased segment; circumferential mobilization of the distal urethra off the periurethral tissue and anterior vaginal wall.
3. **Distal urethrectomy** — sharp excision of the fibrotic distal segment. **Limit resection to the distal 1–1.5 cm** to preserve sphincter function. Send the specimen for pathology to rule out malignancy and confirm spongiofibrosis.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
4. **Spatulation** of the proximal healthy urethral stump (typically ventrally) to widen the new meatus and prevent circular contracture.
5. **Anterior vaginal wall advancement flap** — well-vascularized, tension-free flap of anterior vaginal wall mucosa mobilized and advanced to the spatulated stump.
6. **Neomeatus creation** — interrupted 4-0 or 5-0 absorbable sutures (e.g., polyglactin). A **V-Y advancement** or other non-circular inset is preferred to avoid a circumferential suture line — the dominant mechanism of re-stenosis.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
7. **Catheter** — 14–16 Fr Foley for **7–14 days**; some authors use a suprapubic catheter for urethral rest.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

**Technical pearls** — avoid a circumferential suture line at the neomeatus; keep the vaginal flap well-vascularized and tension-free; send the urethrectomy specimen for pathology.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Outcomes

Outcome data isolating distal urethrectomy with advancement meatoplasty in FUS are limited; most series pool it with other urethroplasty techniques or report it in the post-vulvectomy oncologic setting.

| Setting | Series | n | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| FUS — overall urethroplasty pooled (all techniques) | Osman 2013 SR / Sarin 2021 meta<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | aggregated | 80–94% (vs ~ 47% for dilation) |
| FUS — vaginal flap urethroplasty (includes advancement variants) | Blaivas 2012<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | 17 | **100% at 1 yr; 78% at 5 yr** (vs 6% for dilation) |
| FUS — vaginal flap vs DOBMGU head-to-head | Higuchi 2026<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | 7 vs 8 | VFU **57.1%** (4 / 7) vs DOBMGU 87.5%; spraying 2 / 7 in VFU only. Small sample. |
| Post-vulvectomy neomeatus reconstruction | Lange 2022<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | 42 vaginal-flap advancements / 41 women | 1 neomeatal stenosis (preventable) |
| Post-vulvectomy neomeatus reconstruction | de Lorenzi 2015<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | 47 | **No urethral stenosis** at long-term follow-up; 18.7% urinary incontinence |

## Complications

**Stress urinary incontinence (SUI)** is the dominant procedure-specific concern. The sphincter-anatomy data above (Reid 1990, Hampl 2011) define the resection ceiling. In FUS literature, de novo SUI after urethroplasty (all techniques) is reported at approximately **9.4%**, mostly mild and responsive to PFPT.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

Other reported complications:

- **Neomeatal stenosis / re-stricture** — most common long-term complication; minimized by avoiding circumferential suture lines.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Urinary spraying** — reported with vaginal flap reconstruction of distal strictures (2 / 7 in Higuchi 2026; absent in DOBMGU).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Wound dehiscence** — ~ 4.3% in oncologic series.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Vaginal flap necrosis** — rare (~ 2.1%).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Narrowed introitus** — reported in 25% of oncologic cases with concomitant extensive vulvar resection.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **UTI** — perioperative, related to catheterization.

## Comparison with Alternative Distal-Stricture Techniques

| Technique | Best for | Reported success | Key advantage | Key limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Distal urethrectomy + advancement meatoplasty** | Short distal / meatal stricture | ~ 78–100% (limited data)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | Local tissue; no graft donor site; excises diseased segment | SUI risk if &gt; 1.5 cm resected; spraying |
| Vaginal flap urethroplasty (Blandy / lateral-based) | Distal-to-mid strictures | 57–91%<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | Well-vascularized, readily available | Contraindicated with vaginal atrophy / LS |
| Dorsal onlay BMG urethroplasty | Any location, including pan-urethral | 87–94%<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | Versatile; tissue independent of vaginal health | Oral donor-site morbidity; technically more complex |
| Endoscopic dilation / DVIU | First-line / temporizing | 41–49%<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | Simple, office-based | High recurrence; worsens with repeat |

## Postoperative Management

- Urethral catheter for **7–14 days** (some authors use SPC for urethral rest).<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- Topical estrogen at the neomeatus in postmenopausal women may support mucosal health.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Follow-up uroflowmetry and cystoscopy at 3–6 months to confirm patency.
- Long-term surveillance is essential — late recurrences &gt; 5 years have been reported.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

---

## Summary

Distal urethrectomy with advancement meatoplasty is a straightforward, low-morbidity option for **short distal / meatal female urethral stricture**, leveraging well-vascularized anterior vaginal wall to construct the neomeatus. The two non-negotiable principles are **(1) keep resection ≤ 1.5 cm** to preserve the rhabdosphincter and **(2) avoid a circumferential suture line** at the neomeatus to prevent re-stenosis. Outcomes appear favorable in small series, but the evidence base is limited to retrospective cohorts; **dorsal onlay BMG** offers superior versatility and durability when the stricture extends beyond the distal urethra or local vaginal tissue is unhealthy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[11]](#ref11)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
