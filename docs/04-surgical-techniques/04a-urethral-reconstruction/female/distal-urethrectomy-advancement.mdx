# Distal Urethrectomy with Advancement Meatoplasty (Female)

**Distal urethrectomy with advancement meatoplasty** is a single-stage repair for short distal (meatal) female urethral strictures in which the strictured distal segment is excised and a new meatus is created using mobilized anterior vaginal wall mucosa. No graft or oral-mucosa harvest is required.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For the broader female-stricture treatment ladder and endoscopic alternatives, see [Female DVIU and Urethral Dilation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-dviu-dilation). For longer or more proximal strictures requiring augmentation, see [Female Dorsal Onlay BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-dorsal-onlay-urethroplasty), [Ventral Onlay BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-ventral-onlay-urethroplasty), and [Blandy U-Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/female-vaginal-flap-urethroplasty).

---

## Indications

- Distal (meatal or near-meatal) female urethral stricture that has failed endoscopic management.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Stricture confined to the distal one-third of the urethra.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Meatal stenosis refractory to conservative management.
- LS-associated meatal stricture (some authors prefer BMG in this setting given LS recurrence risk in local tissue).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Oncologic context.** Distal urethral resection during vulvectomy for vulvar cancer involving the distal urethra; the same advancement principle is used to construct the neomeatus.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

---

## Contraindications / Relative Limitations

- Strictures extending into the mid or proximal urethra require augmentation urethroplasty (vaginal flap or BMG) rather than simple excision and advancement.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- LS or atrophy compromising the anterior vaginal wall flap quality.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Stricture involving or approaching the rhabdosphincter (mid-to-proximal urethra); resection risks de novo SUI.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

---

## Surgical Anatomy — the Sphincter Limit

The female urethral sphincter (rhabdosphincter) is concentrated in the middle and distal thirds of the urethra; the compressor urethrae lies in the distal third. Resection must preserve the individual continence mechanism and adequate functional urethral length. The often-cited 1–1.5 cm range derives partly from selected vulvar-cancer series, not a universally safe cutoff for stricture surgery.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- Reid 1990. Of 21 women evaluated prospectively around vulvectomy, all 4 in whom a portion of the urethra was excised developed stress or total incontinence, with significant decreases in functional urethral length and distal pressure-transmission ratios.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- Hampl 2011. Partial distal-urethral resection of up to 1.5 cm was followed by urodynamic continence in 18 of 19 (95%) women with anterior vulvar cancer in a retrospective series, although 5 of 19 (26%) reported urinary symptoms.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- de Mooij 2007. In a retrospective comparison, 4 of 18 women (22%) who underwent radical vulvectomy plus partial urethrectomy (1–1.5 cm) reported incontinence, versus 2 of 17 (12%) treated by vulvectomy alone (p = 0.86), a nonsignificant difference in this small cohort.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- Dimarco 2004. In a 26-patient cohort undergoing partial urethrectomy for primary female urethral carcinoma — a different population from the vulvar-cancer resections above — stress urinary incontinence was reported in 11 (42%), urinary retention in 2 (8%), and urge incontinence in 1 (4%); the follow-up interval for these outcomes was not stated.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

These small oncologic series do not establish a safe resection limit for every woman with a urethral stricture; prior surgery, tissue loss and baseline continence alter risk.

## Technique

Dorsal lithotomy under regional or general anesthesia.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

1. **Cystourethroscopy** to confirm stricture location and extent and rule out proximal or sphincteric involvement.
2. **Circumferential meatal incision** just proximal to the diseased segment; circumferential mobilization of the distal urethra off the periurethral tissue and anterior vaginal wall. Some authors place stay sutures in healthy urethral mucosa proximal to the stricture before excision to limit retraction: Meilika and Sandhu describe circumferential stay sutures placed in identified healthy proximal tissue<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>; Volkin and Nitti describe interrupted absorbable sutures at least 2 mm proximal to the stricture at 6 and 12 o'clock<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>; and Lec and Nitti describe two- or four-quadrant stay sutures (four in their figure), with a nasal speculum and, occasionally, a ventral urethrotomy used to define the proximal stricture limit.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
3. **Distal urethrectomy.** Sharp excision of the fibrotic distal segment, preserving as much healthy mucosa as possible, then attaching healthy urethral mucosa to healthy vaginal mucosa with interrupted absorbable sutures.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> Minimize resection and preserve the sphincter and support complex; do not treat 1.5 cm as a guarantee of continence. Send the specimen for pathology to rule out malignancy and confirm spongiofibrosis.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
4. **Spatulation** of the proximal healthy urethral stump (typically ventrally) to widen the new meatus and prevent circular contracture.
5. **Anterior vaginal wall advancement flap.** Well-vascularized, tension-free flap of anterior vaginal wall mucosa mobilized and advanced to the spatulated stump.
6. **Neomeatus creation.** Interrupted 4-0 or 5-0 absorbable sutures (e.g., polyglactin). A V-Y advancement or other non-circular inset avoids a circumferential suture line. Lange and colleagues describe circular inset of the meatus as a source of urethral stenosis.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
7. **Catheter.** 14–16 Fr Foley for 7–14 days; some authors use a suprapubic catheter for urethral rest.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

**Technical pearls.** Avoid a circumferential suture line at the neomeatus; keep the vaginal flap well-vascularized and tension-free; send the urethrectomy specimen for pathology.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Outcomes

Outcome data isolating distal urethrectomy with advancement meatoplasty in FUS are limited; most series pool it with other urethroplasty techniques or report it in the post-vulvectomy oncologic setting.

| Setting | Series | n | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| FUS — overall urethroplasty pooled (all techniques) | Osman 2013 SR / Sarin 2021 meta<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | 221 / 554 | 80–94% vs 47% for dilation (Osman); 86–93% vs 49% (Sarin) |
| FUS — vaginal flap (9) and BMG (1) urethroplasty, retrospective | Blaivas 2012<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | 17 women with stricture | 100% at 1 yr; 78% at 5 yr (vs 6% for dilation) |
| FUS — vaginal flap vs DOBMGU head-to-head | Higuchi 2026<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | 7 vs 8 | VFU 57.1% (4 / 7) vs DOBMGU 87.5% (7 / 8) at median 12 mo; spraying 2 / 7 in VFU only. Small sample. |
| Post-vulvectomy neomeatus reconstruction | Lange 2022<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | 42 vaginal-flap advancements / 41 women | 1 neomeatal stenosis, 1 neomeatal dehiscence with occlusion, 1 partial flap necrosis; the authors judged the first two preventable |
| Post-vulvectomy neomeatus reconstruction | de Lorenzi 2015<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | 47 | No urethral stenosis at long-term follow-up (68.1% evaluated); 18.7% urinary incontinence |

## Complications

**Stress urinary incontinence (SUI)** is the dominant procedure-specific concern. The small oncologic reports above illustrate risk but do not define a universally safe resection ceiling. In FUS literature, de novo SUI after urethroplasty (all techniques) is reported at approximately 9.4%, mostly mild and responsive to PFPT.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> The Osman systematic review reported no de novo stress incontinence in its 221 women.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

Other reported complications:

- **Neomeatal stenosis or re-stricture.** Reported after both vulvectomy reconstruction and stricture surgery; noncircular insets are used to avoid it.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Urinary spraying.** Reported with vaginal flap reconstruction of distal strictures (2 / 7 in Higuchi 2026; absent in DOBMGU).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Wound dehiscence.** 4.3% in the de Lorenzi series.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Partial vaginal flap necrosis.** 2.1% in the de Lorenzi series.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Narrowed introitus.** Reported in 25% of women evaluated long-term after oncologic vulvectomy.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **UTI.** Perioperative, related to catheterization.

## Comparison with Alternative Distal-Stricture Techniques

| Technique | Best for | Reported success | Key advantage | Key limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Distal urethrectomy + advancement meatoplasty** | Short distal / meatal stricture | ~ 78–100% (limited data)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | Local tissue; no graft donor site; excises diseased segment | SUI risk depends on resection, anatomy and baseline function; spraying |
| Vaginal flap urethroplasty (Blandy / lateral-based) | Distal-to-mid strictures | 57–91%<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | Well-vascularized, readily available | Contraindicated with vaginal atrophy / LS |
| Dorsal onlay BMG urethroplasty | Any location, including pan-urethral | 87–94%<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | Versatile; tissue independent of vaginal health | Oral donor-site morbidity; technically more complex |
| Urethral dilation | Initial option for confirmed nonmeatal FUS; see the endoscopic pathway | Limited durability | Office-based in selected patients | DVIU evidence in true FUS is insufficient; isolated meatal disease may be treated with meatoplasty |

## Postoperative Management

- Urethral catheter for 7–14 days (some authors use SPC for urethral rest).<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> Other authors describe substantially shorter drainage: Volkin and Nitti, and separately Lec and Nitti, report leaving a catheter for as little as 1–3 days after limited distal reconstruction, given that postoperative swelling can itself cause retention. Duration should match the extent of reconstruction; no comparative study has established an optimal length.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- Topical estrogen at the neomeatus in postmenopausal women may support mucosal health.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Follow-up uroflowmetry and cystoscopy at 3–6 months to confirm patency.
- Long-term surveillance is needed; late recurrences &gt; 5 years have been reported.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

---

## Summary

Distal urethrectomy with advancement meatoplasty is an option for short distal or meatal female urethral stricture. Well-vascularized anterior vaginal wall is used to construct the neomeatus. Functional urethral length and the continence mechanism are preserved, and the neomeatus is made wide and well supported. A fixed 1.5 cm limit does not guarantee continence. Outcomes in small retrospective series are favorable. Oral mucosal augmentation is an alternative when the stricture extends beyond the distal urethra or local tissue is unhealthy. The available series do not show that one graft orientation is superior.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[11]](#ref11)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
