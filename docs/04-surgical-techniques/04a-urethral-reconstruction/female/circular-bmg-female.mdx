# Circular (Circumferential) BMG Urethroplasty (Female)

**Circular (circumferential) BMG urethroplasty** is a specialized technique for severe distal female urethral strictures in which the strictured segment is excised and replaced with a tubularized buccal mucosal graft that reconstructs the full circumference of the urethra. It is among the least commonly reported female urethroplasty techniques; the primary description comes from Önol et al. (2011), who used it in 2 of 17 patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The evidence base is extremely limited. Guidelines do not recommend the technique as a distinct entity, and the EAU 2026 review found only this two-patient report.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Concept and Rationale

Most BMG urethroplasty techniques in women use the graft as a single-surface augmentation (dorsal onlay, ventral onlay, or ventral inlay), where the graft replaces only one wall of the urethra and the remaining native wall provides structural support and a vascular bed for graft take.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> In contrast, the circular / circumferential approach involves complete excision of the diseased urethral segment and replacement with a BMG that is tubularized to form the entire urethral circumference. This is conceptually analogous to tubularized graft reconstruction in male urethroplasty, which is generally discouraged due to high failure rates from poor graft take in the absence of a native vascular bed.

## Reported Use and Limitations

This is a technical description, not a routine treatment recommendation. The AUA 2023 amendment states that surgeons should not perform a single-stage tubularized graft urethroplasty because of restenosis risk (Expert Opinion). It excepts a tubularized graft supported in its entirety by a suitable graft bed. A vascularized wrap has not been shown to give comparable outcomes.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> Published or proposed applications include:

- **Severe distal urethral stricture** with near-complete or complete luminal obliteration where the urethral mucosa is too damaged for augmentation alone.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Circumferential spongiofibrosis** where no viable native urethral wall remains to serve as a graft bed.
- **Failed prior augmentation urethroplasty** with circumferential scarring.
- Situations where local tissue (vaginal, labial) is unavailable (atrophy, lichen sclerosus, prior surgery) and the stricture is too short to justify a bladder-flap reconstruction.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Surgical Technique (Önol)

The steps below combine the description in the Önol series with standard tubularized-graft principles. Dimensions are approximate.

1. **Patient positioning.** Lithotomy; vaginal approach.
2. **Exposure.** Anterior vaginal-wall incision; circumferential dissection of the strictured distal urethral segment.
3. **Excision.** The fibrotic / strictured segment is excised completely, leaving healthy proximal and distal urethral margins.
4. **Graft harvest.** Buccal mucosa from the inner cheek, sized to match the excised length and wide enough to create a tube of adequate caliber (approximately 2–2.5 cm width for an approximately 18 Fr tube).
5. **Tubularization.** The BMG is tubularized over a catheter (mucosal surface inward) using continuous or interrupted absorbable sutures.
6. **Anastomosis.** End-to-end to the proximal healthy urethra and distally to the meatal remnant or vestibular mucosa.
7. **Vascular support.** A [Martius flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/martius) or periurethral tissue is wrapped around the tubularized graft to provide a vascular bed for take and mechanical reinforcement.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
8. **Closure.** Layered vaginal-wall closure over the reconstruction.
9. **Catheter management.** Urethral catheter (± SPC) for approximately 3 weeks.

### Aybek-Zumrutbas (AZ) Technique

A distinct technique that should not be confused with circular BMG. Gülpınar et al. described the AZ technique as a novel BMG approach that achieved 100% success in 14 patients at 24.6-month median follow-up, with numerically different rates from ventral onlay (83.3%) and vaginal-sparing inlay (87.5%) in small nonrandomized groups; superiority was not established.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Combined Dorsal + Ventral Inlay

Jefferson et al. described a transurethral approach where 2 of 21 patients with obliterative strictures required a combined dorsal and ventral inlay, augmenting both surfaces of the urethra without full circumferential excision; 92% success at 12 months across the entire cohort.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Outcomes

The evidence for circular BMG urethroplasty specifically is extremely limited:

- **Önol 2011.** 2 patients with severe distal strictures underwent circular BMG reconstruction within a 17-patient series. Overall series achieved 100% objective cure and 88% subjective cure at median 24-month follow-up (range 6–78 mo). Mean Qmax 10.8 to 28.9 mL/s. Individual outcomes for the 2 circular-BMG patients were not reported separately.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

No other published series has reported outcomes for circumferential BMG urethroplasty in women as a distinct cohort. The EAU 2026 review lists both circumferential patients as stricture-free at a mean of 21 months and notes that flow-rate and residual-volume changes are not reported for this technique.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Theoretical Concerns and Limitations

- **Graft take.** A free graft requires imbibition and inosculation from a vascular bed. When the graft forms the entire circumference, there is no native urethral wall to provide blood supply; the graft depends entirely on the surrounding wrap (Martius flap or periurethral tissue). This is the same concern that limits tubularized graft use in male urethroplasty, where failure rates are significantly higher than for onlay techniques.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Contracture risk.** Circumferential grafts are prone to cicatricial contracture during healing, with potential restricture.
- **Continence risk.** Excision of the distal urethral segment may compromise the intrinsic sphincteric mechanism, though the distal female urethra contributes less to continence than the mid-urethral high-pressure zone.
- **Limited tissue.** Buccal harvest is constrained (typically approximately 6–7 cm from one cheek), limiting the length of tubularized reconstruction possible.

## Position in the Reconstructive Algorithm

Circular BMG urethroplasty occupies a very narrow niche. Most reported series use single-surface augmentation techniques (dorsal or ventral onlay or inlay) for female urethral strictures, with pooled studies showing high reported success for dorsal and ventral augmentation (92.1% and 95.5% in a 25-study meta-analysis, 21 of which were Level 4 evidence). Meta-analyses found no significant difference between dorsal and ventral onlay.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> The AUA 2023 guideline recommends urethroplasty using oral mucosa grafts, vaginal flaps, or a combination, without specifically endorsing circumferential reconstruction.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

When circumferential disease precludes straightforward augmentation, several options exist. Reported options are not an evidence-based ranking:

1. **Combined dorsal and ventral graft.** Augments both surfaces while preserving lateral urethral walls.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
2. **Pedicled labial or vaginal flap tubularization.** A pedicled flap maintains its own vascular supply, unlike a free graft.
3. **Circular BMG with Martius flap wrap.** Historical salvage description, subject to the tubularized free-graft concerns above.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
4. **[Bladder wall flap urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/bladder-wall-flap-urethroplasty).** For longer defects or bladder-neck involvement.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Summary

Circular BMG urethroplasty is a rarely used salvage technique for severe circumferential female urethral strictures where augmentation alone is insufficient and local pedicled tissue is unavailable. The evidence base is limited to 2 patients in a single series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The technique requires meticulous vascular support (typically a Martius flap) to overcome the inherent disadvantage of a free graft lacking a native urethral vascular bed. For most female urethral strictures, established augmentation techniques have a much broader evidence base; the two circular-graft cases cannot establish comparative safety or efficacy.
