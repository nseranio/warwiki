# Penile Traction Therapy (PTT)

Penile traction therapy applies **longitudinal mechanical force** to the penile shaft to stimulate **mechanotransduction-driven collagen remodeling**, fibroblast realignment, and tissue regeneration. Its principal evidence base is in **Peyronie's disease** (curvature, length preservation, and as an adjunct to intralesional therapy), with secondary applications as a **pre-prosthesis length-restoration adjunct** and in selected post-prostatectomy / post-radical-cystectomy length-preservation programs. The contemporary anchor is the **RestoreX device**, which is the only PTT modality with **RCT-level evidence** of efficacy for curvature, length, and erectile-function improvement.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Devices

| Device | Mechanism | Daily Use | Best Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RestoreX** | Counter-bending traction with controlled force-distribution; pivot point at the suspensory ligament | **30–90 min/day** | Ziegelmann 2019 *J Urol* RCT (n = 110) — only PTT device with RCT-level outcomes<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Penimaster PRO** | Longitudinal traction via vacuum-locked glans cap | 3–6 hr/day | Small uncontrolled series; Cochrane found high uncertainty<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Andropenis** | Longitudinal traction via dynamic spring tension | 3–8 hr/day | Underpowered studies; daily-use compliance is the major barrier<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Vacuum erection device (no constriction ring)** | Repeated negative-pressure stretching | Variable | Small studies; limited evidence for curvature; some benefit for length<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |

The defining advantage of RestoreX is the **short daily-use window (30–90 min)** vs the 3–8 hr requirement of older traction devices, which has been the dominant adherence ceiling.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Peyronie's Disease — Evidence

### Monotherapy

**Ziegelmann 2019 *J Urol* RCT** (n = 110) — RestoreX vs control over 3 months:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- **Curvature:** −11.7° vs +1.3° in controls
- **Length:** +1.5 cm in treatment arm
- **Erectile function:** improvement in men with concurrent ED
- **Adverse events:** none more than minor

**Joseph 2020 *J Sex Med* open-label and follow-up** — at 6 months:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- **95%** of men experienced length gains (mean 2.0–2.2 cm)
- **61%** had curvature improvements (mean 16.8–21.4°)

### Combined with intralesional CCH

The most evidence-based PTT combination. **Alom 2019 *J Sex Med*** — CCH + RestoreX (n = 56) vs CCH alone (n = 53):<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- Curvature improvement **49% vs 31%** (p &lt; 0.05)
- Length gain **+1.9 cm** in combination arm
- Combined-therapy patients **6.9× more likely** to achieve ≥20° curvature improvement

This combination has emerged as the most evidence-based **non-surgical regimen** in modern Peyronie's care.

### Acute vs stable phase

- **Acute phase (≤18 months from onset, pain present, evolving curvature):** PTT is **first-line conservative therapy** with intralesional CCH; goals are pain reduction, length preservation, and curvature attenuation.
- **Stable phase (no progression ≥3 months, painless):** PTT remains useful as an **adjunct** during CCH cycles or as **length-preservation** therapy. PTT alone in stable disease produces modest curvature reduction; when surgical correction is offered, PTT is usually combined with surgery rather than substituted for it.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Other Reconstructive Applications

### Pre-IPP length restoration

In men with significant pre-implantation shortening (Peyronie's, post-RP, post-priapism), 1–3 months of preoperative RestoreX traction may **restore corporal length** before prosthesis sizing. Levine 2011 reported pilot data in men prior to IPP placement.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

### Post-radical-prostatectomy / post-cystectomy

Anecdotal use as part of penile-rehabilitation programs to mitigate post-RP shortening. Evidence base is limited; not yet guideline-recommended for this indication.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Adverse Effects and Contraindications

Generally well-tolerated. Most-reported events: skin irritation, transient discomfort, glans hypoesthesia (especially with prolonged-wear devices). **Contraindications:** active phase with intolerable pain, severe vascular disease of the penis, active genital infection, anticoagulation with bruising risk, penile prosthesis in situ (without specific surgeon clearance).

---

## Bottom Line for the Reconstructive Urologist

PTT — especially **RestoreX with its 30–90 min/day protocol** — has moved from "experimental adjunct" to **evidence-based component of contemporary Peyronie's-disease care**, particularly in combination with CCH. It is the only mechanical PTT modality with **RCT-level data** showing meaningful curvature, length, and erectile-function improvement. It does **not replace** surgical correction in stable PD with functionally significant deformity, but it preserves length and reduces curvature meaningfully when used appropriately — and it is the single most useful adjunct when CCH is being deployed.
