# Stem Cell Therapy for Erectile Dysfunction

**Stem cell therapy (SCT)** for erectile dysfunction is an investigational regenerative approach intended to regenerate smooth muscle, endothelium and cavernous-nerve function. The AUA 2018 guideline classifies intracavernosal stem cell therapy as investigational (Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level Grade C). The guideline panel found that the studies do not show that benefits reliably outweigh risks or burdens. Restoration of normal erectile function has not been convincingly demonstrated, and the best cell source, dose and duration of effect are not established. No stem cell product is currently FDA-approved for ED.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**AUA 2018, investigational only; commercial-clinic risk.** "For men with ED, intracavernosal stem cell therapy should be considered investigational (Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level Grade C)."<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Counsel patients that SCT for ED should be pursued only within approved clinical trials. Unregulated clinics market stem-cell injections for ED without adequate evidence, which raises patient-safety and ethical concerns.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Rationale and target populations

Conventional ED treatments (PDE5i, ICI, VED) relieve symptoms but do not reverse the underlying pathology of smooth-muscle degeneration, endothelial dysfunction, cavernous-nerve injury and corporal fibrosis. SCT is intended to be restorative.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

The populations with the greatest unmet need and most active research are:<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- **Post-radical-prostatectomy ED** (cavernous nerve injury)
- **Diabetic ED** (vasculogenic and neuropathic)
- **PDE5i-refractory ED** of any etiology

---

## Cell sources

Several cell sources have been investigated, each with different trade-offs<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>:

| Source | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Adipose-derived stem cells (ADSCs)** | Most commonly used in clinical trials; harvested via liposuction; abundant yield |
| **Bone-marrow-derived MSCs (BMSCs)** | Studied in phase I trials; require bone-marrow aspiration |
| **Bone-marrow mononuclear cells / concentrate** | Minimally manipulated; point-of-care processing |
| **Umbilical-cord-blood MSCs (CBMSCs)** | Allogeneic, low immunogenicity; preclinical superior neurotrophic-factor secretion vs ADSCs<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| **Placenta-derived MSCs** | Recent clinical-trial data (Ji 2025)<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **Urine-derived stem cells (UDSCs)** | Non-invasive harvest; preclinical only<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Dental pulp stem-cell conditioned medium (SHED-CM)** | Acellular; uses paracrine factors (Koga 2022)<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |

---

## Mechanism — predominantly paracrine

The therapeutic effect is thought to be mainly paracrine rather than due to direct cellular differentiation<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[6]](#ref6)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>:

1. **Paracrine signaling.** VEGF, BDNF, NGF, and IGF-1 secretion leading to angiogenesis, neuroregeneration, and smooth-muscle repair.
2. **Anti-fibrotic effects.** Reduced collagen deposition; improved smooth-muscle-to-collagen ratio in corpora cavernosa.
3. **Anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory effects.** Reduced oxidative stress and inflammatory cytokines.
4. **Endothelial repair.** Restoration of eNOS and nNOS expression; improved NO–cGMP signaling.
5. **Recruitment of endogenous progenitor cells.** Host-driven repair.
6. **Extracellular vesicles (EVs or exosomes).** Bioactive cargo (mRNA, miRNA, proteins) mediating tissue repair even without intact-cell transplantation.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Routes of administration

| Route | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Intracavernosal injection (ICI)** | The predominant route in clinical trials; direct delivery to the corpus cavernosum<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Intravenous (systemic)** | Used in Nguyen Thanh 2021 ADSC trial for sexual dysfunction<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Transendocardial** | Secondary ED outcomes from cardiac stem-cell trials (Ory 2020)<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **Periprostatic / peri-neural with scaffold** | Preclinical models loading scaffold-bound stem cells onto injured cavernous nerves<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |

## Clinical trial evidence

Published human data are limited, mainly phase I safety trials with small samples. A review published in 2021 identified 27 registered trials, of which 9 had published results, covering fewer than 100 patients in total.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

| Study | Cell type | n | Population | Route | Key outcomes | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Haahr 2018**<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> | Autologous ADRCs | 21 | Post-RP ED | ICI | 53% (8/15) continent men achieved erections sufficient for intercourse; IIEF-5 median 6 to 8 at 12 mo; no improvement in the 6 incontinent men or in men with ED before RP | 12 mo |
| **You 2021**<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | Autologous BMSCs | 10 (9 completed) | Post-RP (5) and DM (5) ED | ICI | IIEF 18.1 to 24.9 at 1 mo (p = 0.02); no adverse event judged related to the cells | 6 mo |
| **Bieri 2020 (Caverstem)**<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> | Autologous BM concentrate | 140 (40 trial + 100 registry) | PDE5i-refractory | ICI | IIEF-5 +2 to +9 (protocol-dependent); peak at 3 mo, maintained at 6 mo; industry-funded | Up to 12 mo |
| **Nguyen Thanh 2021**<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | Autologous ADSCs | 31 (15 men, 16 women) | Reduced sexual function | IV | Testosterone rose and stayed high to 6 mo, then fell by 12 mo; satisfaction reported by all; no SAEs | 12 mo |
| **Koga 2022 (acellular)**<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | SHED-CM | 38 | Mixed ED | ICI | 97.4% improved; mean IIEF-5 13.1 to 19.3 after three injections; 47.4% reached IIEF-5 ≥ 21; open-label, no control | Not stated |
| **Ory 2020 post-hoc**<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | Allogeneic / autologous MSCs / BM-MNCs | 36 (28 SCT, 8 placebo) | Ischemic CMP + ED | Transendocardial | No significant change overall (SCT vs placebo); 200M-cell dose IIEF-EF 14 to 20 (p = 0.014); autologous source 14 to 20 (p = 0.030) | 12 mo |
| **Ji 2025 RCT**<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | Placenta-derived MSCs, LI-ESWT, or both | 33 (three arms) | Refractory diabetic ED | ICI + ESWT | Combined therapy: 70% reached EHS &gt; 2 at 6 mo (p = 0.045); better than either monotherapy | 6 mo |

## Network meta-analysis — SCT vs other regenerative therapies

**Hinojosa-Gonzalez 2024 Bayesian network meta-analysis** of 16 RCTs / 907 patients comparing SCT, PRP, and LI-ESWT<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>:

| Therapy | SMD vs control | 95% CrI | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stem cell therapy** | 0.92 | −0.49 to 2.3 | Not significant (wide CrI; few RCTs; high heterogeneity) |
| **PRP** | 0.83 | 0.15 to 1.5 | Significant |
| **LI-ESWT** | 0.84 | 0.49 to 1.2 | Significant |

The non-significant result for SCT is attributed to the small number of RCTs and high heterogeneity. Only PRP and LI-ESWT had credible intervals excluding zero. The authors note heterogeneity in control groups and follow-up durations.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

## Safety

Published clinical trials of SCT for ED report a favorable short-term safety profile:<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[11]](#ref11)[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

- No serious adverse events were attributed to SCT.
- Minor adverse events include bruising at the harvest or injection site, transient pyrexia, back pain after bone-marrow aspiration, and mild local pain.
- No tumor formation, ectopic tissue growth or systemic complications have been reported in these small trials.
- Long-term safety beyond 12 months is not established.

---

## Combination therapies — emerging signal

Combining SCT with other regenerative modalities has been proposed to be synergistic<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>:

- **SCT and LI-ESWT.** In the Ji 2025 RCT (33 patients across three arms), placenta-derived MSCs plus LI-ESWT gave EHS &gt; 2 in 70% at 6 months, better than either monotherapy.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **SCT and PRP.** Overlapping but complementary mechanisms; PRP provides concentrated growth factors that may enhance stem-cell survival and engraftment.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **SCT and hydrogen sulfide donors (NaHS).** Preclinical synergistic improvement in cavernous-nerve-injury models (Asker 2025).<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

---

## Current barriers

Barriers to SCT becoming standard of care<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[7]](#ref7)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>:

1. **Lack of large randomized placebo-controlled trials.** Most published data are Phase I with small samples and short follow-up.
2. **Heterogeneity** in cell source, dose, processing, delivery route, and outcome measures across studies.
3. **Optimal cell source undetermined.** Head-to-head comparisons of cell types in humans are lacking.
4. **Optimal dosing and re-treatment interval unknown.**
5. **Long-term durability and safety not established.**
6. **Cost-effectiveness unstudied.**
7. **Commercial exploitation.** Unregulated clinics market SCT for ED without adequate evidence.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guideline position

| Body | Position |
|---|---|
| **AUA 2018**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Investigational (Conditional Recommendation; Grade C); use only within an IRB-approved clinical trial. The guideline also classes LI-ESWT as investigational (Conditional; Grade C) and PRP as experimental (Expert Opinion) |

---

## When to refer / when not to use

- **Refer to a clinical trial**: PDE5i-refractory ED in motivated patients with realistic expectations; post-RP ED with intact cavernous-nerve anatomy; diabetic ED with adequate metabolic control.
- **Do not offer outside trials**: routine clinical use is not supported by the evidence; commercial-clinic SCT is not equivalent to trial-grade therapy and should be discouraged.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **LI-ESWT or PRP** show significant pooled SMDs versus control in the network meta-analysis, which SCT does not. The AUA 2018 guideline nonetheless classes LI-ESWT as investigational and PRP as experimental, so a patient interested in regenerative therapy is counseled on all three.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

---

## Postoperative / post-procedural management

- **Activity restriction.** Standard ICI restrictions apply; no post-SCT activity guidelines have been validated.
- **PDE5i bridging** is used in some trial protocols.
- **PRO assessment.** IIEF / IIEF-5 / EHS at baseline, 1 mo, 3 mo, 6 mo, 12 mo per the major trial protocols.
- **Counseling.** Patients are told before treatment that SCT is investigational and not FDA-approved, and that long-term safety and durability are undefined.
