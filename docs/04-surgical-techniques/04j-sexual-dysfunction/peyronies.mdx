# Peyronie's Disease

This page hosts the **operative decision support** for Peyronie's disease (PD): phase determination, intralesional and mechanical therapies, and the surgical algorithm (plication, plaque incision/grafting or prosthesis with adjunctive straightening). For pathophysiology, evaluation, and natural history, see [Peyronie's Disease in Clinical Conditions](/docs/clinical-conditions/03g-genital-scrotal/peyronies-disease). For technique-specific operative deep-dives, see the [Peyronie's Disease subsection](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease).

---

## Decision Framework

PD management depends on phase determination (non-surgical therapy in the active phase, surgery reserved for stable disease). Treatment selection also depends on erectile-function status, curvature severity, deformity complexity and length concerns. The current guideline sources are the AUA 2015 Peyronie's Disease Guideline, the EAU 2025 Male Sexual and Reproductive Health Update (carried forward with strength ratings in the March 2026 limited update) and the 2026 BJU guideline-of-guidelines synthesis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Among non-surgical options, CCH (Xiaflex) is the only FDA-licensed intralesional therapy. A pooled analysis of prospective studies reported about 35% curvature improvement, and each of the 4 treatment cycles added responders.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> The Cochrane review found a small mean difference from placebo in curvature (6.9° at long-term follow-up, moderate certainty).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The RestoreX traction device has randomized evidence (n = 110, 3 months) of improved curvature and length, and of erectile function in men with ED, at 30–90 min/day.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Surgery is reserved for stable disease with deformity that prevents intercourse. In retrospective plication series, Reddy reported immediate postoperative curvature &lt;20° in 93/102 patients later reached by survey; 62.7% of those respondents perceived shortening at median 59.5 months. Hudak reported perceived curvature improvement in 96% of 132 survey respondents at mean 14 months.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> Plaque incision or excision and grafting (PEG) can limit shortening when length or complex deformity drives the choice, but long-term length gain is uncommon and erectile function can worsen.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> For PD with refractory ED, IPP with adjunctive straightening treats both problems. The observational PROPPER registry reported acceptable satisfaction; self-reported depression was 36/187 (19.3%) at baseline and 6/57 (10.5%) among one-year respondents. A separate multicenter series described adjunctive correction for residual curvature after IPP placement.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

### Determine Disease Phase

| Finding | Phase | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Penile pain present, curvature changing/worsening, symptom duration ≤18 months | **Acute (active)** | Non-surgical therapy only; **surgery not recommended (outcomes unknown; AUA)** |
| Curvature stable ≥3 months; pain may persist with erection | **Chronic (stable)** | Consider surgery when stable deformity compromises intercourse, after assessing pain and rigidity<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[23]](#ref23)</sup> |

The AUA guideline defines stable disease as symptoms clinically quiescent or unchanged for at least 3 months (pain, if present, is less common and usually occurs with erection). Surgical series most often required at least 12 months of symptoms and 3–6 months of stable curvature, and outcomes of surgery in active disease are unknown.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Non-surgical Treatment by Disease Phase

CCH plus clinician and patient modeling is an AUA Moderate Recommendation (Grade B) for stable PD with curvature &gt;30° and &lt;90° and intact erectile function. Acute-phase injection studies have low-quality, heterogeneous evidence; the options below require phase-specific selection.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

| Clinical Scenario | First-Line | Alternative(s) | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stable PD, curvature &gt;30° and &lt;90°, intact EF** | Intralesional [CCH (Xiaflex)](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/peyronies-disease-agents) plus clinician/patient modeling (up to four cycles/eight injections); counsel penile hematoma and corporal rupture and avoid intercourse for four weeks after injection<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | [Penile traction therapy (RestoreX)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/penile-traction-therapy) as monotherapy | Surgery during active disease |
| **Prefers maximum non-surgical effect** | **CCH + RestoreX combination** (retrospective cohort, 113 completers: 49% curvature reduction vs 31% with CCH alone; +1.9 cm length; 6.9× more likely to achieve ≥20° improvement)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | CCH alone or RestoreX alone | Vitamin E, tamoxifen (AUA: clinicians should not offer; Moderate Recommendation) |
| **Intralesional alternative to CCH** | [Interferon α-2b](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/peyronies-disease-agents) (AUA 2015: clinicians may administer; Moderate Recommendation, Grade C; EAU 2026 no longer recommends it because evidence is limited and the drug was withdrawn from the European and US markets in 2021) | [Verapamil](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/peyronies-disease-agents) (AUA: clinicians may offer; Conditional Recommendation, Grade C; EAU 2026 finds the evidence insufficient and makes no recommendation) | Corticosteroids (poor evidence); intralesional hyaluronic acid (EAU 2026 weak, acute phase, limited evidence and no placebo-controlled trials) |
| **Oral therapy preferred** | **No oral monotherapy has robust evidence.** Pentoxifylline (off-label) and coenzyme Q10 have modest or uncertain benefit | Tadalafil daily (mechanism-based) | **Vitamin E and tamoxifen** (AUA: clinicians should not offer; Moderate Recommendation, Grade B for vitamin E and C for tamoxifen) |
| **Pain predominant** | [Li-ESWT](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/li-eswt) for **pain only** (no curvature benefit); penile traction; tadalafil | NSAIDs / pain-directed pharmacotherapy | **ESWT for curvature reduction** (Cochrane: no curvature benefit;<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> AUA: clinicians should not use it for curvature or plaque size; Moderate Recommendation, Grade B, while ESWT for pain is Conditional, Grade B) |

### Stable-Phase Surgical Decision Algorithm

The branch point is erectile-function status. The matrix below summarizes the AUA, EAU, ISSM and CUA guidelines together with the cited series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

| Clinical Scenario | First-Line | Alternative(s) | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Adequate EF + curvature ≤60° + simple deformity + adequate length** | [Tunical plication](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease/tunica-plication) (Nesbit, 16-dot, 8-dot, parallel rows) (mixed-deformity retrospective series: 91% reported straight erections at mean 4.5 months after 8-dot plication; 96% of 132/154 respondents reported curvature improvement at mean 14 months in Hudak)<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> | PEG (length-preserving but higher de novo ED risk) | Plication in patient unwilling to accept perceived shortening |
| **Adequate EF + complex deformity** (biplanar curvature, hourglass or hinge) | For a true hourglass or hinge deformity, consider tunical lengthening or extra-tunical grafting; plication alone does not restore girth at the narrowed segment<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | Selected biplanar curvature may be plicated if length and rigidity are adequate; Reddy 2018 studied severe curvature defined by ≥60° or biplanar ≥35°, with 102/327 survey responders<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> | Applying the Reddy result to hinge deformity |
| **Adequate EF + curvature &gt;60° or hourglass / indentation deformity** | [PEG (plaque incision/excision + grafting)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease/plaque-incision-grafting) (buccal mucosa pooled success 98.6% and de novo ED 1.7%; in a separate PEG cohort, mean stretched length gained 0.9 cm, while a buccal mucosa meta-analysis found +1.99 cm affected-side length)<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[16]](#ref16)[[19]](#ref19)</sup> | Plication with patient counseled on additional shortening | PEG in patient unwilling to accept possible erectile-function worsening |
| **Adequate EF + severe shortening as primary concern** | **PEG**, the non-prosthetic option that provides length gain | Plication only if shortening is acceptable | Plication when length preservation is the explicit goal |
| **ED refractory to PDE5i/ICI + any curvature** | [IPP with adjunctive straightening](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease/prosthesis-with-straightening) — treats both problems; acceptable satisfaction in PROPPER; self-reported depression was 36/187 (19.3%) among baseline respondents and 6/57 (10.5%) among one-year respondents<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> | (No alternative with both EF and curvature reliably addressed) | Leaving functionally significant residual curvature after prosthesis inflation |
| **Residual curvature &gt;15–20° after IPP inflation** | **Adjunct options in a retrospective IPP cohort**: [manual modeling](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease/manual-modeling) with or without scratch (~75% of patients; median 26° correction), tunical plication (~5%; median 40°), or plaque incision/grafting (~2%; median 55°); these are separate patient groups<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> | [Scratch technique + postoperative VED](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease/scratch-technique) (Antonini 2018; mean residual curvature 14.1–21.5° after surgery by plaque location, falling to 7.7–9.1° after 24 weeks of VED)<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> | Skipping adjunctive straightening when residual curvature is functionally significant |
| **PD without ED but prefers prosthesis (emerging indication)** | IPP in selected men (Moncada 2025: 87.9% satisfied at 6 months in the 33 men without ED; revision for mechanical failure was less common than in the ED group)<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | Plication or PEG (standard non-ED algorithm) | Routine IPP in PD without ED; the authors state this concept warrants further research |
| **Recalcitrant complex PD with ED + severe shortening** | In a single-surgeon series of 43 men, penile prosthesis plus multiple corporeal incisions and collagen-fleece grafting was associated with mean 2.5 cm postsurgical length gain at median 21-month follow-up; among 39 questionnaire respondents, 94.9% were satisfied with straightness and 89.7% would recommend the operation.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> | Standard IPP + manual modeling | Acceptance of major shortening as inevitable |

A 2023 randomized comparison of CCH plus RestoreX plus sildenafil versus surgery plus RestoreX plus sildenafil found greater curve improvement after surgery (84% versus 54%) at three months among 38 completers, but less measured length (-0.5 versus +1.0 cm) and more adverse events (50 versus 13 events); 18/19 surgery patients had plication and one had incision/grafting.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

### Plication vs Grafting Sub-Comparison

| Factor | Favors Plication | Favors PEG |
|---|---|---|
| **Curvature severity** | Any (retrospective data include ≥60°) | Traditionally &gt;60–70%; meaningful overlap |
| **Deformity complexity** | Simple, biplanar | Hourglass, severe focal indentation |
| **Penile length concern** | Adequate length, accepts modest shortening | Length preservation / gain is priority |
| **Operative complexity** | Simpler, shorter OR time, can be done without degloving | More complex, graft harvest required |
| **De novo ED risk tolerance** | Minimal (0–5%) | Accepts 5–15.7% risk |
| **Surgeon experience** | Widely reproducible | Requires grafting expertise |
| **Yafi 2018 nonrandomized prospective comparison** (n = 14 plication, 25 PEG)<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> | Plication +92.2% curvature correction; −1.0 cm length; +4.9 IIEF-5 | PEG +94.9% curvature correction; +0.9 cm length; +2.2 IIEF-5 |

### Graft-Material Selection (When PEG Is Chosen)

| Graft | Type | Curvature Success | De Novo ED | Length Change | Best Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Buccal mucosa graft** | Autologous | 98.6% pooled reported success | 1.7% pooled reported de novo ED | +1.99 cm on the affected side | Badr 2026 pooled 17 reports (nine full texts, eight abstracts) and found comparable outcomes in its head-to-head analysis.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> Natsos 2024 found mostly single-arm studies at high risk of bias, so these figures do not establish graft superiority.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Collagen fleece (TachoSil)** | Xenograft | 93.7% immediately after surgery; 91.2% at median 47.2 mo | De novo ED not separately reported; 15.7% worsened EF | +1.1 cm | Hatzichristodoulou 2021 — 319/367 attended long-term evaluation; median 47.2-mo follow-up, suture-free sealing, median 79.8-min OR, 87.8% satisfaction<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> |
| **Tunica vaginalis** | Autologous | ~90% at 24 mo | Rare | Significant gain | Same operative field; readily available |
| **Small intestinal submucosa (SIS)** | Xenograft | &gt;80% | 5–15% | Variable | Off-the-shelf; no donor-site morbidity |
| **Pericardium (bovine / human cadaveric)** | Xeno- / allograft | &gt;80% | 5–15% | Variable | Off-the-shelf; good handling |
| **Saphenous vein** | Autologous | &gt;80% | 5–20% | Variable | Traditional graft |
| **Dermis** | Auto- / allograft | &gt;80% | Variable | Variable | Less commonly used today |
