# PDE5 Inhibitors (Procedures)

**Phosphodiesterase type 5 (PDE5) inhibitors** are the standard oral treatment for erectile dysfunction, and the AUA guideline advises informing every man with ED of this option unless it is contraindicated.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> By inhibiting PDE5, they prevent the degradation of cyclic GMP, enhance nitric oxide–mediated smooth muscle relaxation in the corpus cavernosum and facilitate erection in response to sexual stimulation. Available agents include sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil, and avanafil, with differences in onset, duration, and food or alcohol interactions.

This atlas page is the procedural / clinic-facing companion to the full [PDE5 inhibitor pharmacology hub](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/pde5-inhibitors). Use this page for counseling, escalation, and sexual-medicine workflow; use the pharmacology page for drug-class depth, off-label indications, safety ceilings, and the full reference set.

## Indications

| Indication | Practical role |
|---|---|
| Erectile dysfunction | Standard oral pharmacotherapy when sexual stimulation is possible and nitrates are not required<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| LUTS / BPH | Tadalafil 5 mg daily only; the 2026 AUA BPH guideline advises offering it with or without ED (Moderate Recommendation, Grade B)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Penile rehabilitation after radical prostatectomy | A network meta-analysis found regular sildenafil 100 mg associated with higher erectile-function recovery than placebo after nerve-sparing surgery, while on-demand PDE5i were not shown better than placebo; the AUA guideline advises that early PDE5i use may not improve spontaneous erections<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Peyronie's disease with ED | Treats concomitant ED; disease-modifying antifibrotic benefit remains exploratory |
| Recurrent ischemic priapism | Daily PDE5i has been described as a preventive option in case series with conflicting results<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |

PDE5 inhibitors are not a definitive answer for every ED patient. Poor responders include men with severe diabetes, pelvic radiation injury, major neurogenic injury, severe veno-occlusive dysfunction, untreated hypogonadism, or incorrect use. Escalation should move deliberately to [intracavernosal injections](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/intracavernosal-injections), [intraurethral alprostadil](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/muse-intraurethral), vacuum erection device, or penile prosthesis rather than repeating ineffective oral therapy indefinitely.

## Mechanism & Agents

| Agent | Usual use pattern | Practical differentiator |
|---|---|---|
| Sildenafil | On-demand | A high-fat meal delays onset and lowers peak levels<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Tadalafil | On-demand or daily | Longest duration (label: effect up to 36 hours); the PDE5i with a US label for BPH<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Vardenafil | On-demand | Label advises avoiding it with congenital QT prolongation and class IA or III antiarrhythmics<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Avanafil | On-demand | The 100- or 200-mg dose may be taken as early as about 15 minutes before sexual activity; the 50-mg dose is taken about 30 minutes before.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |

PDE5 inhibitors do not create an erection without sexual stimulation. They preserve NO-driven cGMP signaling. If the upstream neural stimulus is absent or severely impaired, response falls, and therapies that act directly on the corpora, such as ICI, are used.

## Key Steps

1. Confirm that sexual activity is safe. Screen for organic nitrates or nitrites and guanylate-cyclase stimulators such as riociguat. Before selecting an agent and starting dose, review alpha-blockers, other hypotensive drugs, CYP3A4 inhibitors, and renal and hepatic function; the STENDRA label directs against avanafil use with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
2. Clarify the goal: on-demand intercourse support, daily LUTS/ED therapy, or post-prostatectomy rehabilitation.
3. Start with a sensible agent and dose, then titrate to effect rather than declaring early failure.
4. Teach correct use: timing, food effect, need for stimulation, and the expected number of attempts before judging efficacy. Counsel patients to seek emergency care for an erection lasting more than four hours and to stop the drug and seek prompt assessment for sudden visual or hearing loss.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
5. Screen for reversible contributors: hypogonadism, medication effects, poorly controlled diabetes, untreated sleep apnea, depression, pelvic pain, and relationship barriers.
6. If response is inadequate, switch agents or escalate to MUSE, ICI, VED or implant according to the patient's goals and manual comfort. MUSE is discontinued in the United States (see [intraurethral alprostadil](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/intraurethral-alprostadil)).

**Inadequate response.** Confirm adequate dose, timing, food instructions, and sexual stimulation over more than one attempt before judging response. Trying another agent is an option, not a prerequisite to injections, devices, or prosthesis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> For rehabilitation after radical prostatectomy, scheduled dosing has a different purpose than on-demand intercourse support, so the regimen should match the indication.

## Outcomes & Evidence

The AUA ED guideline recommends that every man be informed of PDE5 inhibitors unless contraindicated (Strong Recommendation, Grade B), and that the dose be titrated for efficacy (Strong Recommendation, Grade B).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The 2018 AUA review reported mean IIEF-EF gains of about 8–9 points with sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil and avanafil versus about 2 with placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> A 2026 dose-specific network meta-analysis of on-demand therapy (83 trials; 6,029 participants) ranked sildenafil highest for achieving IIEF-EF &gt;26, but its between-agent comparisons were indirect.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> Men taking nitrates regularly should not use PDE5 inhibitors.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Tadalafil 5 mg daily improves LUTS/BPH symptom scores at 12 weeks in product-label trials and is the only PDE5 inhibitor with a US BPH indication.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> In a Cochrane review, PDE5 inhibitors improved IPSS by a mean 1.89 points more than placebo (95% CI 1.50–2.27 points; 4,293 participants; low-certainty evidence over six to 12 weeks).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For penile rehabilitation, the evidence is mixed. A network meta-analysis of 22 studies (2,711 patients) found that regular sildenafil 100 mg was associated with higher erectile function recovery than placebo (OR 4.00, 95% CrI 1.40–13.4; moderate certainty), while on-demand PDE5i were not shown to be better than placebo.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The AUA guideline advises telling men who want to preserve erectile function after radical prostatectomy or radiotherapy that early PDE5i use may not improve spontaneous, unassisted erections (Moderate Recommendation, Grade C).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The regimen should therefore match the goal: assisted erections in the early recovery period, or attempted recovery of unassisted function.

For recurrent ischemic priapism, daily PDE5i is not acute treatment. The AUA/SMSNA guideline states that optimal strategies to prevent recurrence are unknown (Conditional Recommendation, Grade C).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> It lists PDE5 inhibitors among agents used in case series, reports conflicting efficacy, and notes that regimented PDE5i therapy may reduce the frequency and duration of episodes.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> A 2025 randomized phase 2 feasibility trial in 64 men aged 18–40 with sickle cell anemia and recurrent priapism reported 3.02 versus 2.5 episodes per participant-month with hydroxyurea plus tadalafil versus hydroxyurea plus placebo (median follow-up 10 months; placebo-to-tadalafil rate ratio 0.8, 95% CI 0.3–1.9).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> The 2026 EAU guideline recommends starting preventive PDE5 inhibitors only when the penis is flaccid (Weak).<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

**Nitrate interaction.** Nitrates in any form are contraindicated with all four agents, whether used regularly or intermittently.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> The tadalafil label states that in a life-threatening situation at least 48 hours should pass after the last dose before nitrates are considered.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> The sildenafil label states that the interval after which nitrates can be given safely is unknown.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> In a study of 18 healthy subjects, the vardenafil label reports no additional blood-pressure or heart-rate effects when 0.4 mg sublingual nitroglycerin was given 24 hours after a 20 mg LEVITRA dose; it says a safe interval for nitrate administration has not been determined.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> Princeton IV advises avoiding nitrates for at least 24 hours after sildenafil, vardenafil or avanafil and 48 hours after tadalafil.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> Avanafil's US label permits consideration after 12 hours only in a life-threatening situation with close hemodynamic monitoring; this is distinct from routine nitrate co-use.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
