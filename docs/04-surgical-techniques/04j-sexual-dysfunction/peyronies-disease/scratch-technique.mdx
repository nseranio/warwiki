# The Scratch Technique and Endocavernosal Plaque Disruption

The **scratch technique** is an endocavernosal plaque disruption method performed from inside the corpora cavernosa, through the corporotomy incisions made for inflatable penile prosthesis (IPP) placement, without the need for penile degloving, circumferential skin incision, or neurovascular bundle mobilization.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Scratch is an endocavernosal adjunct to modeling performed before IPP cylinder placement. Shaeer's related punch technique reached septal plaques; Antonini did not evaluate ventral curvature and cautioned against ventral scratching except in experienced hands.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The related **Shaeer punch technique** extends the concept by using a punch forceps to formally excise plaque from inside the corpus.

---

## Anatomic Rationale — Why Endocavernosal?

The defining feature: plaque disruption is performed from within the corpus cavernosum, through the same corporotomy used for cylinder placement. Endocavernosal plaque access has these reported features relative to external plaque surgery:

1. **No NVB mobilization.** The dorsal neurovascular bundle is not mobilized in the described approach; postoperative sensory loss was not observed in the 26-patient punch series, but its risk is not established as zero.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
2. **Incision approach.** Antonini used an infrapubic approach without degloving<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>; Shaeer used subcoronal degloving in 12/26 punch cases<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>.
3. **Septal access; ventral caution.** Shaeer's punch cohort included ventral and septal plaques, with the spongiosum protected near the urethra; septal plaques were not removed in its external comparator. Antonini did not evaluate ventral scratch and cautioned against it except in experienced hands.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## The Antonini Scratch Technique

Described by Antonini and colleagues in a 145-patient prospective series published in *Journal of Urology* in 2018.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Step-by-step

1. **Map deformity and perform corporotomy.** Antonini induced a saline artificial erection, marked the pathology externally, and used an infrapubic IPP approach in all 145 patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
2. **Use the external plaque mark.** After corporotomy and dilation, guide internal disruption by the plaque marked during the saline artificial erection, before cylinder placement.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
3. **Endocavernosal disruption.** In Antonini's protocol, an 80-mm nasal speculum is opened transversely across the plaque, followed by longitudinal internal scratching with a No. 12 scalpel blade before cylinder placement.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
4. **IPP insertion.** Standard cylinder placement, rear-tip extender selection, and deployment
5. **Full inflation and assessment.** Device inflated to maximum; residual curvature measured by goniometer
6. **Further correction if needed.** Antonini added Wilson modeling after implant placement when needed; the cohort did not evaluate plication or grafting as part of this protocol.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Clinical outcomes (Antonini 145 patients)

**Baseline:**
- Mean preoperative IIEF-5: 9.8 ± 2.3
- Plaques distributed across proximal third, middle third, and subcoronal regions

**Immediate postoperative residual curvature:**

| Plaque location | After scratch, IPP, and modeling when needed |
|---|---|
| Proximal third | 21.5° ± 4.5° |
| Middle third | 17.3° ± 4.8° |
| Subcoronal | 14.1° ± 3.1° |

**Combined protocol: vacuum therapy for 3 minutes twice daily for 12 weeks; curvature assessed at 24 weeks:**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Plaque location | Residual curvature at 24-week follow-up |
|---|---|
| Proximal third | **8.7° ± 2.5°** |
| Middle third | **9.1° ± 2.9°** |
| Subcoronal | **7.7° ± 0.9°** |

**Functional outcomes:**
- Mean IIEF-5 at 6 months: 18.9 ± 3.1 (P&lt;0.001 improvement); 24.1 ± 3.6 at 1 year
- The authors described complications as minimal, although Table 1 lists 33/145 (22.7%) early postoperative events and 14/145 (9.6%) longer-term events.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### The 12-Week Vacuum Therapy Co-Protocol

At discharge after scratch, IPP placement, and modeling when needed, mean residual curvature was 14.1–21.5° by plaque location; the study did not isolate scratching's effect.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> At 24-week follow-up after a prescribed 12-week VED course, mean residual curvature was 7.7–9.1° by plaque location; the study cannot isolate VED's effect.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Protocol:**
- **Vacuum erection device** applied twice daily
- **3 minutes per session**
- **12 weeks total**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Begin 2 weeks after discharge, without a constriction ring.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Rationale:** Antonini proposed that postoperative VED stretches the tunica during healing; the uncontrolled study did not test the mechanism or isolate its benefit.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Antonini prescribed twice-daily vacuum therapy after scratch plus IPP, but did not report an adherence analysis or establish whether VED was necessary for correction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## The Shaeer Punch Technique

A more aggressive endocavernosal variant: instead of simply scratching the plaque, punch forceps are used to formally excise tissue from within the corpus.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Technique

1. Corporotomy and dilation
2. **Small punch forceps** (typically 3–4 mm) introduced through the corporotomy
3. **Punches of plaque tissue excised** from inside the corpus; the surgeon works along the tunical surface corresponding to the plaque, taking multiple small bites
4. IPP cylinders placed
5. Inflation and assessment

### Outcomes — Shaeer series

**26 patients treated with punch plus a penile prosthesis versus 18 retrospective excision-grafting plus prosthesis comparators:**<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

| Parameter | Punch technique | Excision-grafting |
|---|---|---|
| Preoperative curvature | 58.1° ± 11.7° | 58° ± 14.8° |
| **Straight after surgery** | **26/26** | **18/18** |
| **Additional operative time** | **7.3 ± 1.7 min** | **50.8 ± 11.1 min** |
| Operative time reduction vs. excision-grafting | **85%** | |
| **Glans hyposthesia** | **0%** | **39%** |

In this retrospective matched comparison, all 26 punch patients and all 18 grafting patients were straight postoperatively. Mean additional plaque-surgery time was 7.3 versus 50.8 minutes, and glans hyposthesia was reported in 0/26 versus 7/18; these small groups cannot establish equivalent efficacy or absence of sensory risk.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Advantages of Endocavernosal Techniques

Compared with external plaque incision or grafting (PEG):<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

| Advantage | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Minimally invasive** | No degloving; no circumferential skin incision |
| **NVB preservation** | Dorsal bundle not mobilized in the described punch approach; no sensory loss observed in 26 patients, but risk is not established as zero<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Urethral exposure** | External urethral dissection was avoided; no urethral injury was observed in 26 punch patients, but risk is not established as zero.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Reduced operative time** | 85% less additional plaque-surgery time versus excision-grafting in the 26-versus-18-patient comparison<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Preserved sensation** | 0/26 with sensory loss after punch plus prosthesis vs. 7/18 (39%) reporting glans hyposthesia after excision-grafting plus prosthesis<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Infection** | Infection occurred in 1/26 punch and 2/18 excision-grafting patients.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Septal access** | Punch reached septal plaques that were not removed in Shaeer's external excision-grafting comparator.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **No graft material required** | Avoids graft placement and its material cost.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

## Limitations and Caveats

### Residual curvature without vacuum adjunct

In Antonini's combined scratch, IPP, and modeling-as-needed protocol, mean curvature at discharge was 14.1–21.5° by plaque location; this does not measure scratch alone.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Options for residual deformity include:

- Discuss Antonini's 12-week postoperative VED adjunct; its independent benefit remains untested.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Additional intraoperative maneuvers (modeling, plication) are added, or
- Residual curvature is judged acceptable for intercourse

### Patient selection

- **Studied selection and timing.** Antonini performed scratch before cylinder insertion for stable curvature &gt;30° or indentation/hourglass deformity in men with refractory ED; mean baseline curvature was 65.8°.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Severe baseline curvature (&gt;60°)** does not alone determine a grafting adjunct; reassess residual deformity after IPP placement and modeling.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Calcified plaques.** Shaeer reported calcification in 11/26 punch patients, but neither series compared punch with scratch in this subgroup.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Heavily fibrotic corpora.** Scratch disrupts a localized Peyronie plaque before cylinder insertion; its effect on diffuse corporal fibrosis or cylinder space was not evaluated in Antonini's cohort.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Technique variability and terminology

The literature uses overlapping terms ("scratch technique," "manual modeling with scratching," "endocavernosal plaque disruption," "transcorporeal plaque surgery"), sometimes interchangeably. The distinction is between:

- **Manual modeling.** External bending of the inflated device to fracture the plaque (see [manual modeling](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease/manual-modeling))
- **Scratch technique.** Internal disruption of the plaque with an instrument through the corporotomy
- **Punch technique.** Internal formal excision of plaque tissue with punch forceps through the corporotomy

Many surgeons combine modeling and scratching, applying scratches inside the corpus and then modeling externally after cylinder placement.

## Integration into the Adjunctive Ladder

In the retrospective Hammad 2025 multicenter series, manual modeling with or without the scratch technique was used in 74.7% of 499 PD-IPP cases, making it the most common adjunct.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

**Proposed escalation sequence.** The ICSM 2024 consensus lists modeling, plication, plaque incision or excision, and grafting for deformity persisting after prosthesis placement. Scratching is described in a combined-treatment series and is not a separately endorsed step in that consensus.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

1. **Initial maneuver:** Manual modeling for persistent curvature after IPP insertion; transcorporeal scratching is a separately described adjunct performed before cylinder insertion, not an established first-line partner.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
2. **Residual curvature after modeling.** For curvature &gt;30°, choose a shortening or lengthening procedure according to deformity, penile length, and defect size; plication is one option.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
3. **Plaque incision, excision, or grafting.** These are options for persistent deformity after prosthesis placement in the ICSM consensus; it gives no fixed 40–60° third-line threshold.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

See [prosthesis with adjunctive straightening](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease/prosthesis-with-straightening) for the complete algorithm.

## Postoperative Adjuncts

### Vacuum therapy (Antonini protocol)

- 3 minutes twice daily
- 12 weeks total, beginning 2 weeks after discharge, without a constriction ring.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- In the combined scratch, IPP, and VED cohort, mean curvature was 14.1–21.5° at discharge and 7.7–9.1° at 24-week follow-up; VED's separate effect was not measured.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Home modeling (Moncada protocol)

- Begin 4 weeks postoperatively
- Manual bending of the inflated device opposite the residual curvature
- Daily sessions for 6 months
- In an uncontrolled series of 76 men with &lt;45° residual curvature, 94.7% reached ≤10° at 6 months<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

See [manual modeling](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease/manual-modeling) for detailed home-modeling protocol.

### Early prosthesis activation

Bream's general IPP instructions begin device cycling 2–3 weeks after surgery if the incision has healed; this practice has not been compared with Antonini's VED protocol after scratch.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### PDE5 inhibitors

A randomized trial of 123 men receiving a three-piece IPP (11 with Peyronie disease) found that sildenafil 50 mg daily for one month produced higher erectile-function and sexual-life scores at 2–3 months, with no between-group difference at 6 or 12 months; scratch-specific benefit was not tested.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Complications

In Antonini's combined scratch, IPP, optional modeling, and VED cohort, 33/145 (22.7%) had early complications<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>; Shaeer reported infection in 1/26 punch-plus-prosthesis patients<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>.

| Complication | Rate |
|---|---|
| Glans hyposthesia | 0/26 after punch plus prosthesis versus 7/18 (39%) after excision-grafting plus prosthesis in one comparison<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Infection | 1/26 after punch plus penile prosthesis versus 2/18 after excision-grafting plus prosthesis in one retrospective comparison; risk relative to prosthesis placement alone is unknown.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Scrotal hematoma or swelling | 24/145 (16.6%) in Antonini's combined-treatment cohort (18 mild, 6 severe).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Cylinder damage during scratching | Theoretical; cylinders are placed after scratching in most protocols |
| Prosthesis-related complications | Antonini reported 3/145 (2.1%) impending lateral cylinder extrusions and 3/145 (2.1%) mechanical failures; comparative risk versus standard IPP is unknown.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Urethral injury | None in the 26-patient punch series.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Neurological or sensory injury | None observed in Antonini's 145-patient combined cohort or Shaeer's 26-patient punch cohort.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

## Clinical Implications

Scratch and punch are described endocavernosal approaches during prosthesis surgery; operative-time comparison is available for punch versus excision-grafting, not for scratch. Reported implications and limits include:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- Avoid planned NVB mobilization; Shaeer observed no glans hyposthesia in 26 punch patients, but sensory risk is not established as zero.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- In Shaeer's retrospective comparison, punch added 7.3 ± 1.7 minutes versus 50.8 ± 11.1 minutes for excision-grafting; scratch-specific comparative time was not measured.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- In Antonini's uncontrolled combined-treatment series, 136/145 had residual curvature ≤15° at one year; the separate contributions of scratch and VED are unknown.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Have been described as adjunctive maneuvers in selected PD-IPP reports; the ICSM 2024 moderate recommendation lists modeling, plication, plaque incision/excision and grafting for persistent deformity after prosthesis placement.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- Shaeer's punch reached septal plaques that were not removed in that study's external excision-grafting comparator.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

**Patient counseling should emphasize:**

- Antonini's combined-treatment cohort had mean residual curvature of 14.1–21.5° at discharge by plaque location; individual results vary.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Discuss the 12-week VED schedule used by Antonini and the separate home-modeling protocol; neither has been shown necessary for correction after scratch plus IPP.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- Gradual improvement over 6 months
- In Antonini's combined cohort, mean IIEF-5 rose from 9.8 before surgery to 18.9 at 6 months, while 33/145 (22.7%) had early postoperative complications.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
