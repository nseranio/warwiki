# Plaque Incision / Excision with Grafting

Plaque incision or excision with grafting (**PIG / PEG**) is the tunical lengthening arm of the Peyronie's disease (PD) surgical algorithm. It corrects curvature by releasing or partially removing the contracted plaque on the concave side and patching the tunical defect with a graft. Unlike plication, PIG/PEG releases the concave side and aims to limit shortening while correcting severe or complex deformity. Long-term length gain is uncommon, supplemental plication may shorten the penis, and postoperative erectile dysfunction is a substantial risk.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

Tunical lengthening is an option for men with normal erectile function, inadequate penile length, severe curvature or complex deformity (EAU weak recommendation).<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> Curvature &gt;60° is a proposed, unvalidated threshold; erectile rigidity, deformity and length guide selection.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

### Terminology — incision vs excision

The terms PIG and PEG are used interchangeably in the literature but describe distinct plaque-management approaches:

| Term | Definition | Current role |
|---|---|---|
| **Plaque incision (PIG)** | Plaque is **incised** (cut through) but not removed; resulting tunical defect is grafted | **Most commonly performed today** — preserves native tissue, smaller defects |
| **Partial plaque excision (PEG)** | Most fibrotic / calcified portion of plaque excised; surrounding tunica preserved | Levine's preferred approach — removes the dominant scar without sacrificing peripheral tunica |
| **Complete plaque excision** | Entire plaque removed | **Rarely performed today** — larger defects, higher ED rates, no demonstrated superiority |

The AUA Guideline uses the inclusive phrase "plaque incision or excision and/or grafting" to encompass all variants.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Indications

The AUA 2015 PD Guideline (Moderate Recommendation; Grade C) allows plaque incision or excision and/or grafting for men whose rigidity is adequate for coitus, with or without pharmacotherapy, vacuum or ICI, and who have stable disease. The specific thresholds below (such as curvature above 60°) come from the Levine surgical algorithm and later series, not from the AUA statement. PIG/PEG is appropriate for:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

- **Curvature &gt; 60°** where plication would produce unacceptable shortening
- **Complex deformity.** Hourglass, hinge effect, compound or biplanar curvature. For selected hourglass or hinge indentation, consider extratunical grafting with or without plication as a tunica-preserving alternative; supporting series are small and do not establish superiority.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)[[36]](#ref36)</sup>
- **Short penis** where length preservation is the dominant goal
- **Adequate erectile rigidity** for coitus (with or without PDE5i / VED / ICI)
- **Stable disease phase.** The AUA requires symptoms unchanged for at least 3 months; surgical series typically required at least 12 months from onset and 3–6 months of stable curvature

The 2026 BJU Guideline-of-Guidelines reports that grafting is generally reserved for preserved erectile function and prosthesis for refractory ED.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> The Fifth International Consultation on Sexual Medicine also recommends considering a prosthesis for patients at high risk of postoperative ED (strong recommendation; good-quality evidence).<sup>[[37]](#ref37)</sup> EAU 2026 uses lengthening (incision or excision with grafting) for men with normal erectile function who have inadequate length, severe curvature or complex deformity (weak), warns of de novo ED up to 50% in men without preoperative ED, notes that complete plaque removal may raise venous-leak ED (partial excision may be acceptable with florid calcification), and identifies no superior graft; it states that grafting without a concomitant prosthesis in complex deformity raises complication risk including ED, so primary prosthesis is a limited-evidence alternative.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

**Patients to counsel against.** In the Langbo / Levine series of 251 PEG patients, 22% were initially advised to undergo IPP but elected PEG; these patients had significantly lower postoperative ability to engage in intercourse (51% versus 76%) and a higher rate of eventual IPP conversion (13% versus 4%), reinforcing the importance of the algorithm.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Technique — Plaque Incision vs. Partial Excision

### Partial plaque excision with grafting (PEG)

**Partial excision for selected plaques.** Plaque incision with grafting is a tunical-lengthening approach; partial excision may be used for florid calcification while avoiding the potential erectile-function cost of complete plaque removal.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

**Steps:**

1. **Degloving or subcoronal incision** with full penile mobilization
2. **Approach-specific exposure.** For a dorsal plaque, lift the dorsal neurovascular bundle from the tunica (Lue technique); a lateral plaque may be exposed laterally without full dorsal mobilization. For a ventral plaque, dissect and protect the urethra and corpus spongiosum as needed before tunical incision.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
3. **Artificial erection and relaxing incision.** After approach-specific exposure, induce a saline artificial erection to identify the point of maximum curvature, then make an H-shaped or Y-shaped relaxing incision through the plaque on the concave side.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
4. **Partial excision** of the densest portion of the plaque (if calcified). For a selected bothersome calcified plaque where preserving the overlying tunica is desired, a tunica-sparing technique has been described: a lateral longitudinal opening slightly larger than the plaque is made, the calcification is separated from the underlying erectile tissue, and the overlying tunica is carefully dissected free from the plaque (about 3–4 mm) with a fresh blade under magnification before the tunical opening is closed with interrupted sutures starting at the corners to avoid trapping the neurovascular bundle. This dissection is slow and technically demanding. **This is a separate, graft-free operation rather than a comparative recommendation:** the original 12-man series selected &gt;1.5 mm of overlying tunica on ultrasound, used primary tunical closure, and added plication for straightening; graft harvest and inlay in steps 5–6 do not apply.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)[[33]](#ref33)</sup>
5. **Graft harvest and sizing.** Measure the opened defect under penile stretch and tailor the graft to its shape and material; in the described sutured pericardial PEG technique, the patch is cut 2–3 mm longer than the measured defect, whereas collagen fleece sealing requires at least 5 mm of overlap on each side.<sup>[[38]](#ref38)</sup>
6. **Graft suturing** into the tunical defect with running non-absorbable monofilament suture (Prolene 4-0 or 5-0) or absorbable suture (PDS)
7. **Repeat saline artificial erection testing** after graft inlay to confirm straightening and check the graft closure for leaks before closing Buck's fascia.<sup>[[39]](#ref39)</sup>
8. Closure of Buck's fascia and redraping

### Plaque incision without excision (relaxing incisions + grafting)

Incisions through the plaque without excision: the plaque is divided to allow the concave side to open up, and the resulting defect is grafted. Preferred when the plaque is densely adherent to the neurovascular bundle or when excision would risk NVB injury.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

In a retrospective multicenter post-CCH series of 90 men (25 grafting with IPP, 18 grafting alone and 47 Nesbit plication), graft-material details below concern the 43 grafted men, whereas the complication and recurrent-curvature counts concern the full cohort at three months:<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- Among the 43 grafted men, the graft was bovine pericardium in 22 (51.2%) and collagen fleece in 21 (48.8%)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Median postoperative penile length: 13.0 cm (IQR 12.0–15.0)
- At three months, 86 of 90 (95.6%) men in the mixed-procedure cohort reported no surgical complication.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Persistent/recurrent curvature &gt;20° at three months in the mixed-procedure cohort: 4 of 90 (4.4%).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### Incision patterns

Several incision configurations have been described:

| Pattern | Description | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **H-incision (Lue 1998)**<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | Two parallel longitudinal incisions connected by a transverse incision through the plaque | Most widely used; rectangular defect; controlled expansion. |
| **Double-Y / stellate** | Y-shaped incisions at each end of the plaque (or cruciate) | Diamond-shaped defect; useful for focal plaques |
| **Single relaxing incision** | One transverse incision across the plaque | Simplest; for mild contractures |
| **Egydio geometric** | One geometrically planned incomplete circumferential double-Y relaxing incision, with grafting | **Sansalone 2011** European multicenter series (n=157): mean intraoperative stretched-length gain **2.5 cm** (range 1.7–4.1 cm); 19/157 (12%) had mild residual curvature &lt;15° at three months (88% without residual curvature); median follow-up was 20 months<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **Egydio Tunica Expansion Procedure (TEP)** | Multiple staggered small cuts on the tunica without graft, used with penile prosthesis implantation | In 416 implant recipients with or without PD, penile gain was **3.3 cm** (range 2–6 cm) intraoperatively across the full cohort; curvature was corrected during surgery in the 287 PD cases<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |

### The Hatzichristodoulou "sealing" technique

The **TachoSil sealing technique** has been described with an equine-collagen patch coated with human fibrinogen and thrombin after partial plaque excision.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Evarrest has a different carrier: oxidized regenerated cellulose and polyglactin 910 fibers, not collagen. Its results and handling instructions should not be substituted for TachoSil data.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

**Steps:**

1. Penile degloving; NVB mobilization
2. **Partial plaque excision** on the concave side
3. **Collagen fleece applied** over the tunical defect; the fibrin-coated surface activates on contact with bleeding tissue and forms a watertight seal
4. Gentle pressure for 3–5 minutes to ensure adherence
5. No suture fixation required for the graft

**Outcomes (319 of 367 operated patients returned for long-term evaluation; 86.9% follow-up):**<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- Median operative time: 79.8 minutes (range 50–130)
- **Intraoperative complete straightness: 299/319 (93.7%)**<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Mean penile length increase: 1.1 ± 0.6 cm (P=.017)
- At median 47.2-month follow-up, 291/319 (91.2%) maintained complete straightness.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Glans sensation returned to baseline in 300/319 (94%) at long-term evaluation.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Patient satisfaction: 87.8%; partner satisfaction: 84.3% among 319 long-term participants.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Erectile function worsened in 50/319 (15.7%)**, improved in 78/319 (24.5%) and was unchanged in 191/319 (59.8%) at long-term evaluation.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Eleven treatment-related Clavien-Dindo grade 1 complications were reported; this does not replace separate counseling about erectile-function deterioration

These are outcomes among followed patients in a prospective cohort, not randomized evidence of material superiority. A separate four-center retrospective collagen-fleece cohort (n=88) reported postoperative ED in 38.6% and residual curvature in 19.3% at median 31 months; the populations and endpoint definitions differ.<sup>[[34]](#ref34)</sup>

## Graft Materials

### Buccal mucosa graft (BMG) — autologous

A 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis of 17 reports (9 full texts and 8 abstracts) found:<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

| Outcome | Value |
|---|---|
| Success rate | **98.6%** |
| Satisfaction | **92.1%** |
| De novo ED rate | **1.7%** |
| Penile shortening rate | 1.1% |
| IIEF-5 change | +1.69 points |
| Length of affected side | +1.99 cm |

BMG is a thin, flexible autologous graft option with favorable results in selected series, but cheek harvest adds donor-site morbidity. The 2026 review included eight abstract-only reports, direct comparisons have not established superiority over other grafts, and EAU recommends no single graft material.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[24]](#ref24)</sup> For harvest and handling, see [buccal mucosa graft — foundations](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa). For BMG harvest and handling, see [buccal mucosa graft — foundations](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/buccal-mucosa).

### Hemostatic patches (TachoSil, Evarrest)

- **Different carriers:** TachoSil uses equine collagen; Evarrest uses oxidized regenerated cellulose plus polyglactin 910.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
- Suture-free tunical coverage has been reported, but product-specific evidence and technique matter.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Farrell 2019 was a small retrospective IPP-plus-grafting comparison: 18 mixed-patch cases (10 Evarrest, 6 Nu-Knit, 2 TachoSil) versus 15 pericardium cases. Median follow-up was 6.6 months for patches and 34.6 months for pericardium. The shorter operative time does not prove equivalence, cost-effectiveness, or applicability to grafting without an IPP.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- For product labels and handling distinctions, see the [fibrin sealant patch hub](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/intraoperative-adjuncts/tissue-sealants/tachosil).

### Bovine / cadaveric pericardium (Veritas, Tutoplast, CorMatrix)

- Veritas is bovine pericardium, Tutoplast is processed cadaveric (human) pericardium, and CorMatrix is a porcine extracellular-matrix product
- Pericardium has a long track record in PD grafting, with reliable handling and suture retention
- Cadaveric pericardium was associated with satisfaction with erectile function in one retrospective analysis (OR 61.4, 95% CI 7.7–491; a wide interval from a small subgroup)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- In the Farrell comparison, outcomes were similar to hemostatic patches, with longer operative time (166 versus 122 minutes)<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Porcine small intestinal submucosa (SIS; Cook Biodesign)

- Remodels to host tissue over 6–12 months
- Good handling; acceptable outcomes
- Higher cost than some alternatives

### Dermal graft — autologous

- Historical standard (Devine). [Devine](/docs/roots/surgeons/a-g/charles-devine) and Horton (1974) described surgical treatment of Peyronie's disease with an autologous dermal graft placed into the tunical defect<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>
- Meaningful donor-site morbidity (abdominal or inguinal harvest)
- Now less commonly used given superior alternatives

### Saphenous vein — autologous

- Lown ED rate in some series
- Donor-site consideration (saphenous vein harvest)
- Longer operative time
- Harvest has been described through several small incisions with tunneling between them, rather than one long skin incision, with tributaries ligated and their sites opened as the vein is segmented to reduce leakage; the segments are then joined into a single patch before inlay.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>
- The deep dorsal vein has also been used as a local autologous graft. In a 1993 series of 44 operated men with Peyronie's disease, 18 underwent plaque incision with a deep dorsal vein graft through a single incision; the authors reported rapid return of shaft suppleness, but the report gave no comparative curvature or erectile-function outcomes.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup>

### Tunica vaginalis — autologous

- Adjacent tissue, easily harvested
- Thin and flexible
- Useful in revision settings where other materials have been used
- **Ainayev 2022** compared TV and BMG in 20 men per group. Both groups had 90% technical success (residual curvature ≤15°) at 24 months, with no statistically significant between-group erectile-function or length difference; this small study did not establish equivalence.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

### Comparative graft data

**Natsos 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis** of grafts in PD surgery without prosthesis:<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

- **BMG.** Highest penile straightening rates and least de novo ED
- **TachoSil collagen fleece.** Best overall performance when preoperative curvature was taken into account (used for more severe curvatures, still with favorable results)
- **Tutoplast (cadaveric pericardium).** Higher incidence of postoperative ED
- Most included studies were single-arm with a high risk of bias, and no randomized trial establishes graft superiority

**Saphenous vein versus BMG** (Danacıoğlu 2021, n=41): no significant differences in IIEF, length, or partner satisfaction; residual curvature 13.1% (SV) versus 11.2% (BMG).<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

**SIS versus collagen fleece** (matched-pair analysis, Rosenhammer 2019, n=43 each, median curvature 80°): CF significantly faster (80 versus 104 min, p &lt; 0.001); SIS had significantly more shortening (28% versus 5%, p = 0.007).<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

## Special Scenario: Compound / Biplanar Curvature

For severe compound curvature (primary and secondary plane), PEG alone is often insufficient, and supplemental plication in a second plane is typically required. The Levine group's 240-patient series with 161 compound-curvature patients:<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- Average primary curvature: 79° (range 35–140°)
- Average secondary curvature: 36° (range 20–80°)
- After PEG, average residual curvature: 30°, requiring 1–6 additional plication sutures
- At 61-month follow-up:
  - Recurrent curvature after PEG plus supplemental plication: 12.4% at average 61-month follow-up.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
  - Net change in penile length: +0.36 cm
  - Decreased penile sensation after PEG plus supplemental plication: 13% at average 61-month follow-up.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
  - Able to engage in penetrative sex after PEG plus supplemental plication, with or without pharmacotherapy: 79%.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For the most complex cases, the operation is PEG on the primary curve plus plication in the second plane, and the patient should be counseled to expect a multi-element reconstruction.

## Post-CCH Failure Surgery

A growing patient population: men who attempted intralesional CCH (Xiaflex) first and either did not achieve sufficient correction or developed complications (corporal rupture). In Cocci's 90-patient mixed-procedure series, 86/90 (95.6%) reported no recorded surgical complication and 4/90 (4.4%) had persistent or recurrent curvature &gt;20° at three months; the authors also reported paresthesia in all 43 graft recipients without grading its severity.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The series documents feasible surgery after CCH, but its mixed procedures, three-month assessment and lack of a non-CCH comparator cannot determine whether prior CCH changes grafting difficulty or longer-term outcomes.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Microscopic Electric Rotary Grinding

A novel technique using fine electric rotary grinding (a modified dental-grade burr or equivalent) to precisely remove fibrotic plaque tissue under microscopic visualization, followed by grafting with tunica vaginalis or bovine pericardium.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Reported to offer more precise plaque removal with preservation of adjacent tunica; adopted in selected Chinese and European centers; not widely adopted in U.S. practice.

## Outcomes

### Straightening

- Successful straightening: 80.0–96.4% among the four PIG studies reporting this outcome in a 12-study review; success definitions differed.<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup>
- Modern large series (collagen fleece sealing, Hatzichristodoulou): among 319/367 patients evaluated long term, 93.7% were completely straight intraoperatively and 91.2% at median 47.2-month follow-up<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- BMG systematic review: 98.6% success<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

### Length

Grafting aims to limit shortening relative to plication, but durable length gain is uncommon and supplemental plication may still shorten the penis.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> The 319-patient cohort reported a mean gain of 1.1 ± 0.6 cm.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### Erectile function

The main downside is erectile dysfunction. De novo ED was reported in 1.7% in a BMG meta-analysis<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> and 21% in a Levine PEG series<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>. In a different PIG series, 46% had a ≥6-point fall in the IIEF erectile-function domain at six months, a different endpoint from de novo ED<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>; results vary with patient selection, graft material and outcome definition. Mechanism is thought to involve veno-occlusive dysfunction at the graft–native-tunica interface.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

**Mulhall predictors of postoperative ED** (Flores / Mulhall 2011, multivariable analysis; n=56): degree of preoperative curvature, Egydio incision technique (larger tunical defects), patient age, and baseline venous leak. Mulhall's group discourages PIG in older men, those with venous leak, and those with profound curvature.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

**Levine predictors** (Taylor / Abern / Levine 2012; n=109): neither preoperative cardiovascular risk factors (HTN, DM, smoking) nor duplex parameters (PSV, EDV, RI) significantly predicted postoperative ED. Rate was 10% TAP, 21% PEG.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

**Montorsi 2000** (n=50, vein graft, highly selected RigiScan and Doppler-normal cohort): only 6% ED, illustrating the patient-selection range across series.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

### Comparative head-to-head — Yafi 2018 prospective multi-institutional

CCH versus plication versus PEG (n = 57):<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

| Outcome | CCH (n=18) | Plication (n=14) | PEG (n=25) | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mean curvature correction | 23.3° (34.4%) | 72.0° (92.2%) | **71.8° (94.9%)** | 0.001 |
| Mean IIEF-5 change | +5.7 | +4.9 | +2.2 | NS |
| Mean SPL change | −0.2 cm | −1.0 cm | **+0.9 cm** | 0.001 |

In Yafi et al.’s prospective nonrandomized comparison, mean curvature fell by 94.9% after PEG and 92.2% after plication; these are mean changes, not patient-level straightening rates. Mean stretched length changed by +0.9 cm after PEG and −1.0 cm after plication.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

### Sensation

In a prospective 63-patient PIG series, sensory loss was reported by 21% at one week, 8% at six months and 3% at one year; one patient had extensive persistent loss at two years.<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup> In a separate 319-patient partial-excision series, 300 (94%) returned to baseline glans sensation at median 47.2-month follow-up.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Complications

| Complication | Rate |
|---|---|
| Erectile-function outcomes | 1.7% de novo ED in a BMG meta-analysis; 21% postoperative ED after PEG in one series; 46% had a ≥6-point IIEF-EF decline at six months in a separate PIG series.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[20]](#ref20)[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |
| Decreased sensation | 21% at one week, 8% at six months and 3% at one year in a 63-patient PIG series.<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup> |
| Hematoma | 0–4.0% in four reporting PIG studies.<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup> |
| Persistent/recurrent curvature | 4.4% at three months across mixed post-CCH procedures; 12.4% at average 61 months after PEG plus supplemental plication.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Infection | 0–2.7% in five reporting PIG studies.<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup> |
| Graft-related (rupture, migration) | Rate not quantified in the reviewed PIG studies.<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup> |
| Urethral injury | Very rare |

Interrupted septal sutures placed during graft inlay, in addition to meticulous hemostasis, have been described as reducing subgraft hematoma at one center; no denominator or comparative effect size is reported for this maneuver.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

No PIG/PEG-specific VTE recommendation exists in the AUA or EAU guidelines. Apply the general risk framework (age, obesity, prior VTE, malignancy, prolonged operation or immobility) on the [VTE Prophylaxis](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/perioperative-eras/vte-prophylaxis) page rather than a procedure-specific rule.

## The Langbo Lesson — Patient Selection Matters

A Levine-group analysis examined patients who were counseled toward prosthesis (based on suboptimal erectile rigidity) but elected for PEG instead:<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- **Lower postoperative ability to engage in intercourse:** 51% among men advised to receive IPP but choosing PEG versus 76% among men initially advised to receive PEG.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Higher rate of eventual penile prosthesis implantation:** 7/54 (13%) among men initially advised IPP versus 7/197 (4%) among men initially advised PEG.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

PEG should be offered only to patients whose erectile function supports it. Patients with borderline ED who choose PEG over prosthesis have worse long-term outcomes and higher crossover-to-prosthesis rates.

## Counseling

- **Length preservation is a goal, not a guarantee.** In a 137-patient PEG cohort, 75.2% had stable or greater measured stretched length, but 46.0% perceived postoperative shortening. Counsel that grafting aims for functional straightness and may not restore predisease length.<sup>[[35]](#ref35)</sup>
- **ED risk is real.** De novo ED was 1.7% in a BMG meta-analysis and postoperative ED 21% in one PEG series; a different PIG series found a ≥6-point IIEF-EF decline in 46% at six months. Estimates vary by graft, technique, baseline function and endpoint.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[20]](#ref20)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- **Recovery is longer** than plication: typically 6–8 weeks before return to sexual activity. Postoperative traction may be discussed; in Rybak et al.'s retrospective PEG comparison, mean measured length change was +1.48 cm with traction (n=36) versus +0.24 cm without (n=23), but nonrandomized allocation cannot establish a causal effect.<sup>[[40]](#ref40)</sup>
- **Complex deformities may require combined PEG and plication.** Multi-element operations are expected for compound curvatures
- **Patient selection.** Patients counseled toward prosthesis who insist on PEG have materially worse outcomes

## PIG/PEG vs Plication — Head-to-Head

| Feature | PIG / PEG | Plication (TAP / 16-dot / PSP) |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | Lengthens concave side | Shortens convex side |
| Typical selection | Severe or complex deformity when plication would cause unacceptable shortening; the proposed &gt;60° threshold is unvalidated<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> | Uncomplicated curvature with adequate length; curvature degree alone does not mandate grafting<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> |
| Penile length effect | In Yafi’s selected PEG group (n=25), mean stretched length changed **+0.9 cm** | In Yafi’s tunical-plication group (n=14), mean stretched length changed −1.0 cm; whole-cohort median follow-up was 12 months<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> |
| Erectile-function outcomes | 1.7% de novo ED in a BMG meta-analysis; 21% postoperative ED after PEG in Taylor et al.; 46% had a ≥6-point IIEF-EF decline at six months in Flores et al.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[20]](#ref20)[[19]](#ref19)</sup> | 10% postoperative ED after TAP in Taylor et al.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> |
| Operative time | Longer (80–165 min) | Shorter (48–80 min) |
| Curvature outcome in separate cohorts | 91.2% completely straight at median 47.2 months after collagen-fleece partial excision.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | 93% reported curvature &lt;30° at survey after TAP (mean follow-up 72 months).<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup> |
| Complexity | Higher (NVB mobilization, graft handling) | Lower (suture-only) |
| Sensory changes | More common (NVB mobilization) | Less common |
| Best long-term satisfaction series | 87.8% among 319/367 long-term respondents (Hatzichristodoulou, median 47.2 mo)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | 82% satisfied or very satisfied (Taylor–Levine TAP, n=61, mean 72 mo)<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup> |
