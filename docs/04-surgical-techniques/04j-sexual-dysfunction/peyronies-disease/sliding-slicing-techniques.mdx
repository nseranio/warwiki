# Sliding & Slicing (Penile-Disassembly) Techniques for Peyronie's Disease

The **sliding and slicing techniques** are a family of penile-lengthening operations. They release the tunica albuginea, often with partial disassembly of the penis, and bridge the resulting tunical defect with interposition grafts. They are performed almost exclusively for Peyronie's disease (PD) with severe deformity (hourglass, hinge or complex curvature) or marked length loss, typically with simultaneous inflatable penile prosthesis (IPP) implantation. They are among the most extensive operations in the PD reconstructive armamentarium. Glans hypoesthesia and, in the worst cases, glans necrosis are the complications tied to mobilizing the neurovascular bundle and glans.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

This page belongs to the [Peyronie's Disease subsection](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease). These techniques have been moved out of the cosmetic atlas because the evidence base, society positioning and patient population are PD-driven. For the broader PD framework see the [Peyronie's Disease overview](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease) and the AUA guideline.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> For the standard plaque-incision and grafting alternative see [Plaque Incision / Excision with Grafting](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease/plaque-incision-grafting).

**Investigational positioning.** The SMSNA 2024 position statement on cosmetic penile enhancement addresses sliding and slicing techniques. This page reports its position as investigational, to be performed in the context of clinical research and at specialized centers.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Counseling addresses glans hypoesthesia, partial necrosis and, in the worst case, glans loss.

## Conceptual Framework — What "Sliding" and "Slicing" Mean

Both techniques release the tunica albuginea so that the corpora can expand, then bridge the resulting tunical defect with an interposition graft. The "sliding" and "slicing" labels refer to two different ways of releasing the tunica.

- **Sliding (Rolle 2012).** The tunica is incised on the ventral and dorsal aspects, the corpora are "slid" apart to gain length, and the dual dorsal-ventral defect is bridged with two patch grafts (porcine small intestinal submucosa in the original report). An IPP is implanted simultaneously to splint the reconstruction. The original report described three patients (mean length gain 3.2 cm, follow-up 13 months).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Slicing, multiple-slit technique (MUST; Egydio 2018).** The tunica is incised through multiple parallel transverse slits along its convex side without complete corporal-glans disassembly. The slits open under intracorporal expansion or IPP cylinder placement, and the multiple small defects are bridged with a single sheet graft. The intent is to avoid the glans ischemia risk of full disassembly. The originating series reported 138 patients (60% with PD; 103 malleable and 35 inflatable devices), a mean length gain of 3.1 cm at a median follow-up of 15.2 months, and one glans necrosis.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Penile-disassembly technique (Perovic 2001).** This is the historical antecedent. The corpora cavernosa are separated from the glans, neurovascular bundle and urethra, the deformity is repaired on the free corpora, and the structures are then reassembled. The original series (46 patients with PD, mean follow-up 27 months) reported complete straightening in 87%, with recurrence in six, and used incisional grafts with penile skin or saphenous vein. It was described for severe deviation under the glans cap, hourglass deformity and multiple plaques, not specifically for length gain.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Egydio geometrical-principle relaxing incision (2004).** This related single-incision approach uses geometric principles to determine incision shape. It is less extensive than full disassembly and lies between standard plaque incision and grafting and the sliding family.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Indications

Sliding and slicing techniques are used for the subset of PD patients in whom the following apply, based on the originating series.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- **Severe penile shortening** is a primary patient concern, in addition to curvature.
- **Complex deformity** is present (hourglass, hinge, biplanar curvature) that is not adequately addressed by simple plication or single-axis grafting.
- **Severe curvature** (typically &gt; 60°) is combined with significant shortening.
- **Concurrent erectile dysfunction** refractory to medical therapy is present, because IPP implantation is performed concurrently to splint the reconstruction. Sliding / slicing as standalone non-prosthesis operations are uncommon in current practice.
- The patient understands and accepts the higher complication risk, particularly glans hypoesthesia or ischemia, relative to standard plication or plaque incision and grafting.

These techniques are not used for cosmetic length augmentation in men with normal anatomy and normal erectile function, the earlier cosmetic-atlas framing. Such patients are managed with the [cosmetic male decision framework](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/male-cosmetic) (penile traction therapy first, then SLD ± suprapubic lipectomy if anatomically indicated).

## Surgical Components — Common Steps Across Variants

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1. Approach | Subcoronal (degloving) incision allows full exposure of all aspects of the penile shaft and is the preferred approach when significant tunical work is anticipated. |
| 2. Mobilization | The dorsal neurovascular bundle and urethra are mobilized off the tunica albuginea to the extent the chosen variant requires. Perovic disassembly is the most extensive; MUST is less extensive. |
| 3. Tunical opening | Horizontal slits (Rolle), multiple parallel slits (MUST), or geometric relaxing incision (Egydio 2004). |
| 4. Length gain | Achieved by allowing the corpora to "slide" apart longitudinally under stretch / IPP cylinder expansion. |
| 5. Graft interposition | Bovine pericardium, porcine SIS, collagen matrix, dermis, saphenous vein, or tunica vaginalis bridge the resulting defect(s). |
| 6. IPP implantation | Performed concurrently to splint the reconstruction and treat coexisting ED. |
| 7. Closure | Reconstitution of Buck's fascia and dartos; subcoronal skin closure. |

## Outcomes — Honest Framing

The published literature consists of single-center case series from the surgeons who developed each variant. No randomized trials compare sliding or slicing techniques with plaque incision and grafting alone or with grafting plus IPP. Reported outcomes include:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- **Length restoration.** Mean gains of about 3 cm were reported in the originating series (3.2 cm, n = 3; 3.1 cm, n = 138), with a range of 2–5 cm in the MUST series. Selection bias and the absence of independent replication limit these figures.
- **Straightening.** Originating series report straightening when combined with IPP.
- **Patient satisfaction** is reported as high in the originating series. Outcomes were assessed mainly with the IIEF and the Erectile Dysfunction Inventory of Treatment Satisfaction rather than a PD-specific instrument.

A 2020 systematic review of aesthetic augmentation phalloplasty in men without penile deformity (16 studies, 1192 patients, poor methodological quality) reported a pooled complication rate of 14.6% across all augmentation techniques. Combined length and girth procedures had the highest rate. The review included penile disassembly among other techniques, and its population was cosmetic rather than PD.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Complications

The complication profile supports performing these operations at specialized centers.

| Complication | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Glans hypoesthesia / sensory loss** | Associated with dorsal neurovascular bundle mobilization. Usually partial and transient, but persistent hypoesthesia has been described. |
| **Glans ischemia / partial necrosis** | The most serious complication, tied to the disassembly step. Risk is expected to rise with the extent of neurovascular bundle and urethral mobilization. One glans necrosis occurred in the 138-patient MUST series.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Vascular compromise is a surgical emergency. |
| **Wound dehiscence and graft loss** | Reported in case series and managed with revision or staged reconstruction. |
| **Erectile dysfunction (de novo or worsening)** | Graft-related veno-occlusive risk applies, as with standard plaque incision and grafting. When an IPP is implanted concurrently, the ED is treated at the same operation. |
| **Recurrence of curvature** | The IPP cylinders splint the reconstruction, which is expected to lower recurrence relative to grafting without IPP. |
| **Length loss over time** | Long-term follow-up may show partial loss of restored length. Published follow-up is short (median 15.2 months in the MUST series). |
| **Standard IPP-related complications** | Mechanical malfunction, infection, erosion and fibrosis. Published series do not compare these rates with PD-IPP without disassembly. |
| **Investigational positioning (SMSNA 2024)** | Sliding and slicing techniques are reported as investigational by SMSNA 2024, and counseling reflects this.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |

## Where Sliding / Slicing Sit in the PD Surgical Algorithm

| Scenario | Standard pathway | Where sliding / slicing fit |
|---|---|---|
| Curvature ≤ 60°, intact EF, no shortening concern | [Tunica plication](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease/tunica-plication) | Not indicated |
| Curvature &gt; 60° or complex deformity, intact EF | [Plaque incision / excision + grafting](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease/plaque-incision-grafting) | Not indicated |
| PD + ED refractory to medical therapy, mild–moderate residual curvature | IPP + [manual modeling](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease/manual-modeling) ± plication | Not indicated |
| PD + ED refractory + severe complex deformity + significant shortening | IPP + plaque-incision-and-grafting (PICS) **or** sliding / slicing technique at a specialized center | **Considered**: Rolle sliding or Egydio MUST when shortening is the dominant patient complaint and standard PICS is judged inadequate |
| Cosmetic length augmentation in a man with normal anatomy and normal EF | [Cosmetic male framework](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/male-cosmetic): PTT first, then SLD ± suprapubic lipectomy | **Not indicated**: these techniques are not for the cosmetic patient |

## Counseling Essentials

- **Expectations.** Sliding and slicing operations have restored length in selected PD patients with severe shortening. They are not cosmetic operations.
- **Glans ischemia** is the principal safety concern and is discussed explicitly.
- **No randomized comparative data** exist. The published evidence is single-center case series from the originating surgeons.
- **Specialized-center care.** SMSNA 2024 is reported here as limiting these procedures to clinical research and specialized centers.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Concurrent IPP** is the usual pairing. It splints the reconstruction and treats the ED that typically accompanies the indication.
