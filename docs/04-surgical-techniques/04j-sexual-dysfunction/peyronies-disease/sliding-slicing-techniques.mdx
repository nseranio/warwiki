# Sliding & Slicing (Penile-Disassembly) Techniques for Peyronie's Disease

The **sliding and slicing techniques** are a family of penile-lengthening operations. They release the tunica albuginea, often with partial disassembly of the penis, to permit corporal lengthening. Original sliding bridges dorsal and ventral tunical defects with grafts; MuST distributes smaller incisions without interposition grafts.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> Published prosthesis-associated series include men with severe erectile dysfunction and marked penile shortening, with or without Peyronie's disease (PD); complex deformity may be present, and malleable or inflatable prostheses have been used. In the original MuST cohort, 83/138 (60.1%) had PD.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> They are among the most extensive operations in the PD reconstructive armamentarium. Glans hypoesthesia and, in the worst cases, glans necrosis are the complications tied to mobilizing the neurovascular bundle and glans.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

This page belongs to the [Peyronie's Disease subsection](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease). These techniques have been moved out of the cosmetic atlas because the evidence base, society positioning and patient population are PD-driven. For the broader PD framework see the [Peyronie's Disease overview](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease) and the AUA guideline.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> For the standard plaque-incision and grafting alternative see [Plaque Incision / Excision with Grafting](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease/plaque-incision-grafting).

**Investigational positioning.** The SMSNA 2024 position statement on cosmetic penile enhancement addresses sliding and slicing techniques. The SMSNA considers sliding and slicing investigational and recommends IRB oversight, experienced prosthetic surgeons, and counseling about potentially severe complications.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Counseling addresses glans hypoesthesia, partial necrosis and, in the worst case, glans loss. The EAU 2026 guideline goes further: it recommends against the sliding technique because of reported glans necrosis from concomitant release of the neurovascular bundle and urethra (strong), and says that newer variants (MoST, MuST, multiple-incision technique) should be used only by experienced high-volume surgeons after full counseling. It also recommends against penile prosthesis, penile disassembly or sliding for men who simply seek lengthening (strong).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Conceptual Framework — What "Sliding" and "Slicing" Mean

Both techniques release the tunica albuginea for corporal expansion. Original sliding covers its larger defects with grafts; MuST distributes the incisions and avoids interposition grafting.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> The "sliding" and "slicing" labels refer to two different ways of releasing the tunica.

- **Sliding (Rolle 2012).** The tunica is incised on the ventral and dorsal aspects, the corpora are "slid" apart to gain length, and the dual dorsal-ventral defect is bridged with two patch grafts (porcine small intestinal submucosa in the original report). An IPP is implanted simultaneously to splint the reconstruction. The original report described three patients (mean length gain 3.2 cm, follow-up 13 months).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Slicing, multiple-slit technique (MUST; Egydio 2018).** MuST uses multiple paired dorsal and ventral hemicircumferential tunical incisions, with small longitudinal slits where girth restoration is needed. Its distributed small defects avoid the interposition graft used in original sliding.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> The intent is to avoid the glans ischemia risk of full disassembly. The originating series reported 138 patients (60% with PD; 103 malleable and 35 inflatable devices), a mean length gain of 3.1 cm at a median follow-up of 15.2 months, and one glans necrosis.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Penile-disassembly technique (Perovic 2001).** This is the historical antecedent. The corpora cavernosa are separated from the glans, neurovascular bundle and urethra, the deformity is repaired on the free corpora, and the structures are then reassembled. The original series (46 patients with PD, mean follow-up 27 months) reported complete straightening in 40/46 (87%), mild recurrent deviation in six, and postoperative shortening in 9%; incisional grafts used penile skin or saphenous vein.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> It was described for severe deviation under the glans cap, hourglass deformity and multiple plaques, not specifically for length gain.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Egydio geometrical-principle relaxing incision (2004).** This related single-incision approach uses geometric principles to determine incision shape. It is less extensive than full disassembly and lies between standard plaque incision and grafting and the sliding family.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Indications

Sliding and slicing techniques are used for the subset of PD patients in whom the following apply, based on the originating series.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- **Severe penile shortening** is a primary patient concern, in addition to curvature.
- **Complex deformity** is present (hourglass, hinge, biplanar curvature) that is not adequately addressed by simple plication or single-axis grafting.
- **Curvature with significant shortening** was represented in the originating series; the EAU notes that the &gt;60° definition of severe curvature has not been validated, and the MuST PD subgroup had a mean curvature of 55° (range 0–90°).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Concurrent erectile dysfunction** refractory to medical therapy is present, because IPP implantation is performed concurrently to splint the reconstruction. Sliding / slicing as standalone non-prosthesis operations are uncommon in current practice.
- The patient understands and accepts the higher complication risk, particularly glans hypoesthesia or ischemia, relative to standard plication or plaque incision and grafting.

These techniques are not used for cosmetic length augmentation in men with normal anatomy and normal erectile function, the earlier cosmetic-atlas framing. Such patients are managed with the [cosmetic male decision framework](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/male-cosmetic) (penile traction therapy first, then SLD ± suprapubic lipectomy if anatomically indicated).

## Surgical Components — Common Steps Across Variants

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1. Approach | Subcoronal (degloving) incision allows full exposure of all aspects of the penile shaft and is the preferred approach when significant tunical work is anticipated. A trans-scrotal degloving route (a 4–5 cm ventral transverse scrotal incision, with distal dissection beneath superficial Buck's fascia to complete shaft degloving) has also been described and used for corporoplasty, including sliding/slicing techniques; in an 89-patient mixed corporoplasty series assessed through 3 months, no circumcision was required and investigators reported no preputial edema, skin necrosis or loss of glans sensitivity; six men had self-resolving sub-dartos hematomas. The single-center, developer-reported series did not compare the two routes head to head or establish either as superior.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| 2. Mobilization | The dorsal neurovascular bundle and urethra are mobilized off the tunica albuginea to the extent the chosen variant requires. Perovic disassembly is the most extensive; MUST is less extensive. |
| 3. Tunical opening | Horizontal slits (Rolle), multiple parallel slits (MUST), or geometric relaxing incision (Egydio 2004). A described MuST variant uses a meshed-graft pattern: transverse hemicircumferential tunical incisions connected by two longitudinal incisions, with interspersed cuts about 0.8 cm long made while a dilator applies tension from within the corpora, preserving the lateral neurovascular plexus during distal dissection; longitudinal incisions or a double-Y pattern have been added for hourglass deformity, and for severe calcified curvature a single larger incision at the point of maximal curvature, with additional smaller incisions added only if neurovascular-bundle length permits, has been described. This is an operative variant without its own comparative outcome data.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| 4. Length gain | Achieved by allowing the corpora to "slide" apart longitudinally under stretch / IPP cylinder expansion. |
| 5. Defect coverage | Original sliding bridges its dorsal and ventral defects with interposition grafts; reported materials include bovine pericardium, porcine SIS, collagen matrix, dermis, saphenous vein, and tunica vaginalis. Graft-free MoST and MuST variants use fascial coverage or smaller dispersed tunical defects, respectively.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| 6. Prosthesis implantation | A penile prosthesis is placed concurrently for erectile dysfunction and corporal support; published series used both malleable and inflatable devices.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| 7. Closure | Reconstitution of Buck's fascia and dartos; subcoronal skin closure. |

## Outcomes — Honest Framing

Primary outcomes are mainly reported in small, developer-involved series. A 2024 systematic review of 15 penile-length-preservation studies (1,186 recipients across several techniques) reported an overall postoperative complication incidence of 21.7% among 1,161 cases with complication data and functional outcomes in only five studies; the complication figure combines techniques and is not a technique-specific risk estimate.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> No randomized trials compare sliding or slicing techniques with plaque incision and grafting alone or with grafting plus IPP. Reported outcomes include:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- **Length restoration.** Mean gains of about 3 cm were reported in the original sliding series (3.2 cm, n = 3 with PD)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> and the mixed-etiology MUST series (3.1 cm, n = 138, 60.1% with PD; range 2–5 cm)<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>. Selection bias and the absence of independent replication limit these figures. A prospective multicenter international series of the sliding technique in 28 men with end-stage PD and severe shortening (three European tertiary referral centers, mean follow-up 37 months, range 9–60) reported a mean penile lengthening of 3.2 cm (range 2.5–4) with no late recurrence of the shaft deformity; postoperative complications were a transfusion-requiring bleed in one anticoagulated patient (3.5%, Clavien-Dindo II) and prosthesis infection requiring removal in 2 (7%, Clavien-Dindo III). This is a larger, developer-involved multicenter extension of the original series, without a comparison against plaque incision and grafting.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Straightening.** Originating series report straightening when combined with IPP.
- **Patient satisfaction** is reported as high in the originating series. Outcomes were assessed mainly with the IIEF and the Erectile Dysfunction Inventory of Treatment Satisfaction rather than a PD-specific instrument.

A 2020 systematic review of aesthetic augmentation phalloplasty in men without penile deformity (16 studies, 1192 patients, poor methodological quality) reported a pooled complication rate of 14.6% across all augmentation techniques. Combined length and girth procedures had the highest rate. The review included penile disassembly among other techniques, and its population was cosmetic rather than PD.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Complications

The complication profile supports performing these operations at specialized centers.

| Complication | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Glans hypoesthesia / sensory loss** | Associated with dorsal neurovascular bundle mobilization. Usually partial and transient, but persistent hypoesthesia has been described. |
| **Glans ischemia / partial necrosis** | The most serious complication, tied to the disassembly step. Neurovascular bundle and urethral mobilization may compromise glans perfusion. In a 21-case series of glans necrosis after penile prosthesis implantation, arteriosclerotic cardiovascular disease, diabetes, smoking, prior prosthesis explantation or radiation, subcoronal incision, and occlusive wrapping were common; without unaffected controls, the series cannot estimate independent risk.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> One glans necrosis occurred in the 138-patient MUST series.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> A dusky glans after prosthesis placement warrants urgent assessment. In a 21-case series of glans necrosis after prosthesis implantation, 17 men managed expectantly with the implant retained sustained significant glans loss, while four managed with immediate removal healed without sequelae; these observational outcomes do not establish a universal explant rule.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **Wound dehiscence and graft loss** | Reported in case series and managed with revision or staged reconstruction. |
| **Erectile dysfunction (de novo or worsening)** | Graft-related veno-occlusive risk applies, as with standard plaque incision and grafting. When an IPP is implanted concurrently, the ED is treated at the same operation. |
| **Recurrence of curvature** | The IPP cylinders support the reconstruction; comparative evidence has not established whether this lowers recurrence relative to grafting without IPP.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| **Durability of restored length** | Durability remains uncertain; median follow-up was 15.2 months (range 6–36) in the mixed-etiology MUST series<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>, and mean follow-up was 37 months (range 9–60) in the 28-patient sliding series<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>. |
| **Standard IPP-related complications** | Mechanical malfunction, infection, erosion and fibrosis. Published series do not compare these rates with PD-IPP without disassembly. |
| **Investigational positioning (SMSNA 2024)** | Sliding and slicing techniques are reported as investigational by SMSNA 2024, and counseling reflects this.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |

## Where Sliding / Slicing Sit in the PD Surgical Algorithm

| Scenario | Standard pathway | Where sliding / slicing fit |
|---|---|---|
| Curvature ≤ 60°, intact EF, no shortening concern | [Tunica plication](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease/tunica-plication) | Not indicated |
| Curvature &gt; 60° or complex deformity, intact EF | [Plaque incision / excision + grafting](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease/plaque-incision-grafting) | Not indicated |
| PD + ED refractory to medical therapy, mild–moderate residual curvature | IPP + [manual modeling](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease/manual-modeling) ± plication | Not indicated |
| PD + ED refractory + severe complex deformity + significant shortening | Prosthesis with established adjunctive straightening, including plaque incision and grafting when needed | EAU 2026 gives a strong recommendation not to use the original sliding technique because of life-changing complications such as glans necrosis. MuST/MoST are limited to experienced high-volume surgeons after full counseling; SMSNA calls sliding/slicing investigational under research oversight.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Cosmetic length augmentation in a man with normal anatomy and normal EF | [Cosmetic male framework](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/male-cosmetic): PTT first, then SLD ± suprapubic lipectomy | **Not indicated**: these techniques are not for the cosmetic patient |

## Counseling Essentials

- **Expectations.** Sliding and slicing operations have restored length in selected PD patients with severe shortening. They are not cosmetic operations.
- **Glans ischemia** is the principal safety concern and is discussed explicitly.
- **No randomized comparative data** exist. The primary evidence consists largely of small developer-led series, including one prospective three-center sliding study of 28 men; there are no randomized comparisons.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Specialized-center care.** SMSNA 2024 is reported here as limiting these procedures to clinical research and specialized centers.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Concurrent IPP** is the usual pairing. It splints the reconstruction and treats the ED that typically accompanies the indication.
