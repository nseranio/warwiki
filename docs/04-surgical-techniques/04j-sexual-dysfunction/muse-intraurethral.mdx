# Intraurethral Alprostadil (MUSE)

The **Medicated Urethral System for Erection (MUSE)** delivers a small alprostadil (prostaglandin E1) pellet into the urethra via a prefilled applicator. The drug is absorbed through the urethral mucosa into the corpus spongiosum and diffuses into the corpora cavernosa, producing smooth muscle relaxation and erection independent of neurogenic stimulation. In the trials the AUA guideline reviewed, 29.5% to 78.1% of men had successful intercourse, depending on how success was defined. Only men with a positive in-office test proceeded to home treatment in most of those trials. Intraurethral alprostadil is generally less effective than intracavernosal injection but is less invasive.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

This atlas page is the administration and counseling companion to the full [Intraurethral Alprostadil](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/intraurethral-alprostadil) pharmacology hub.

## Indications

| Indication | Practical role |
|---|---|
| PDE5i contraindication, failure or intolerance | Needle-free ED option; the AUA advises informing men with ED of it (Conditional Recommendation, Grade C) and performing an in-office test first (Clinical Principle)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Needle aversion or limited willingness for ICI | Lower efficacy than ICI but easier to accept for some patients |
| Post-prostatectomy ED rehabilitation | Alternative when PDE5i are contraindicated or poorly tolerated |
| Combination with PDE5i | Combined use with an oral PDE5i has been described in selected non-responders |

Suitable candidates are motivated patients who can manipulate the applicator, understand the lower efficacy compared with ICI, and value avoiding a needle enough to accept that trade-off.

## Technique Overview

MUSE delivers alprostadil through urethral mucosa and not directly into the corpus cavernosum, so the doses are much higher than ICI doses. The US label reports priapism (a rigid erection of 4 hours or longer) in 2 of 1,511 men (0.13%) in the placebo-controlled trials, and the AUA guideline advises teaching patients what to do about a prolonged erection.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The label contraindicates MUSE with urethral stricture, balanitis, severe hypospadias or curvature, and acute or chronic urethritis, so check the urethra and glans before prescribing to a reconstructive patient. Sickle cell disease or trait, thrombocythemia, polycythemia and multiple myeloma are also contraindications.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

The AUA guideline lists 100, 250, 500 and 1,000 microgram doses; the last US label (revised May 2024) lists 250, 500 and 1,000 micrograms, recommends 250 micrograms for the first supervised dose and allows no more than two systems in 24 hours.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> MUSE is no longer marketed in the United States. The final DailyMed listing gives marketing end dates of December 31, 2022 (250 micrograms) and April 30, 2023 (500 and 1,000 micrograms), and Drugs@FDA and the Orange Book list all strengths under NDA 020700 as discontinued. Confirm supply before offering an in-office test.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> The AUA guideline advises choosing the lowest dose expected to produce an erection sufficient for intercourse for the in-office test, because adverse events increase with dose.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Some clinics begin at 500 micrograms during supervised titration to avoid an early false impression of failure; that is a practice choice, not the label's 250 microgram starting dose or a guideline recommendation.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Key Steps

1. Have the patient urinate immediately before administration. Residual urine aids dissolution and dispersal of the pellet.
2. Hold the penis straight and pointing up, and insert the applicator stem about 3 cm into the urethra.
3. Depress the button to release the pellet, move the applicator slightly to separate the pellet from the tip, and remove it.
4. Keep the penis upright and roll it between the hands for 30-60 seconds.
5. Have the patient walk or stand for about 10 minutes to aid blood flow. Onset is expected within 5-10 minutes, and the effect usually lasts 30-60 minutes.
6. Perform the first dose in the office. Hypotension occurred during in-clinic testing in 3.3% of men in the pivotal trial, and an optional constriction band was allowed in the comparative ICI trial.
7. Use a condom with a pregnant partner and counsel female partners about possible vaginal burning from drug transfer.

## Outcomes & Evidence

In the randomized placebo-controlled trial, 299 of 461 men (64.9%) randomized to alprostadil had intercourse at least once at home, compared with 93 of 500 (18.6%) on placebo. Only men with a positive in-office response (996 of 1,511, 65.9%) were randomized. Mild penile pain followed 10.8% of treatments, and the trial report described no priapism or penile fibrosis (the label reports 2 priapism cases across the two pivotal studies).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> A positive in-office test does not guarantee home success.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In a randomized crossover trial of 111 men, at least one erection sufficient for intercourse occurred in 92.6% of men using intracavernosal alprostadil alfadex and 61.8% of men using MUSE with optional constriction band (p &lt; 0.0001). More patients and partners preferred the injection.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The AUA guideline advises counseling men that the intraurethral route is generally less effective than ICI.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
