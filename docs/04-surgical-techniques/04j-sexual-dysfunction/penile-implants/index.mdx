# Penile Implants

**Penile prosthesis implantation** is the definitive surgical treatment for erectile dysfunction (ED) refractory to medical management or when other treatments are contraindicated or unacceptable.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Patient satisfaction is high, roughly 80–95% across series, and a meta-analysis of 83 studies pooled about 83%.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)[[15]](#ref15)[[26]](#ref26)</sup> The procedure irreversibly alters corporal tissue and eliminates natural erections — a critical informed consent point — but does not affect urination, ejaculation, penile sensation, or orgasm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

## Indications

Penile implants are indicated for men with **moderate-to-severe ED who have failed or cannot use** less invasive treatments (PDE5 inhibitors, VED, ICI), or who prefer a permanent solution. Expanded indications include:

- Organic ED refractory to PDE5 inhibitors, VED, and intracavernosal injection therapy
- Peyronie's disease with concurrent ED (EAU 2025 recommends offering implantation when ED accompanies a deformity that needs surgery)<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>
- Corporal fibrosis from priapism, infection, or prior surgery
- Post-prostatectomy or radiation-induced ED with failed medical management
- Prior prosthesis explantation requiring replacement
- Neophallus construction (phalloplasty in gender-affirming surgery)
- Refractory ischemic priapism (as definitive treatment)

**Contraindications:** Systemic, cutaneous or urinary tract infection (AUA 2018),<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup> inadequate medical optimization, unrealistic patient expectations, or significant psychiatric comorbidity.

## Preoperative Assessment

### Medical Optimization

Risk factors for infection and device failure should be optimized before surgery:

| Factor | Target |
|---|---|
| Diabetes | HbA1c &lt;8.5% (ideally &lt;7%) |
| Obesity | BMI &lt;30 |
| Active UTI | Treat and clear before proceeding |
| Immunosuppression | Optimize where possible |
| Testosterone | Correct hypogonadism if present |

### Psychological Screening — "CURSED" Traits

The **CURSED** mnemonic identifies patients at elevated risk for post-implant dissatisfaction:

| Letter | Trait |
|---|---|
| **C** | Compulsive personality |
| **U** | Unrealistic expectations |
| **R** | Revision — previously operated elsewhere |
| **S** | Surgeon shopping |
| **E** | Entitled / demanding |
| **D** | Denial / Psychiatric disorder |

### Informed Consent Essentials

Patients must understand:
- Natural erections will no longer be possible after implantation
- The procedure is considered **permanent and irreversible**
- Device survival is about 77% at 10 years and about 53% at 20 years, so a mechanical revision over a lifetime is common<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>
- Most patients report satisfaction despite the revision burden<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[15]](#ref15)[[26]](#ref26)</sup>
- Risk of infection, erosion, and device failure requiring explantation
- Postoperative expectations, including differences between inflatable and malleable devices in operation, appearance and concealability; the AUA advises this counseling for every man who chooses implantation, and notes that implant surgery is unlikely to restore the penile dimensions present before ED developed<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup>

## Device Types

### Three-Piece Inflatable Penile Prosthesis (3-piece IPP)

The gold standard in most health systems.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Consists of:
- **Two intracavernosal cylinders** providing rigidity and expansion
- **Intrascrotal pump** with valve mechanism for inflation/deflation
- **Retropubic fluid reservoir** (AMS 700 reservoirs are 65 or 100 mL) placed in the space of Retzius or ectopically

![Three-piece penile implant: Male pelvis and penile section, conceptual device view](/img/diagrams/ipp-components.svg)

*Two corporal cylinders connect to a scrotal pump and a fluid reservoir. Inflation transfers fluid to the cylinders; deflation returns it to the reservoir. Use the current model-specific IFU for sizing and implantation details. (Original WARWIKI schematic.)*

**Advantages:** Best flaccidity and erect rigidity; most natural appearance and function; highest patient satisfaction among device types.

**Disadvantages:** Most mechanically complex; requires reservoir placement; demands adequate manual dexterity; longer operative time.

### Two-Piece Inflatable Penile Prosthesis (2-piece IPP)

Self-contained system integrating the reservoir into the pump mechanism. No separate abdominal reservoir required.

**Advantages:** Avoids reservoir placement (relevant in prior pelvic surgery, transplant recipients); simpler implantation; good option for standard anatomy.

**Disadvantages:** Less rigidity and less complete flaccidity than 3-piece; limited cylinder expansion.

### Malleable / Semi-Rigid Prosthesis

Two firm, bendable rods implanted into the corpora cavernosa. Device is manipulated (bent down to conceal, bent upward to use).

**Advantages:** Simplest implantation and operation; most durable (no fluid components to fail); suitable for limited dexterity; good length conservation.

**Disadvantages:** Permanent semi-erect state that can be difficult to conceal; less natural appearance; lower satisfaction scores than inflatable devices.

## Device Operation — Inflation and Deflation

Patients are taught to cycle the device at the activation visit (~4–6 weeks); the maneuver differs by manufacturer pump. Adequate manual dexterity and key-pinch strength are prerequisites — assess them preoperatively, because a patient who cannot reliably work the pump is better served by a malleable device.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

**Inflation (achieving erection) — all three-piece devices:**

- Locate the scrotal pump by palpation and steady it between thumb and forefinger.
- **Squeeze and release the pump bulb repeatedly** (typically **6–15 squeezes**); each cycle transfers fluid from the reservoir into the cylinders.
- Rigidity builds progressively with each squeeze until the erection is full — stop when it is satisfactory.

**Deflation (returning to flaccidity) — differs by pump:**

- **AMS 700 with MS pump (Boston Scientific):** press the **deflation button for 2–4 seconds**; fluid returns to the reservoir. Squeezing the shaft afterward makes the penis more flaccid. The manufacturer's operating instructions warn against squeezing the deflation button and the pump bulb at the same time.
- **Coloplast Titan (One-Touch Release, OTR):** squeeze the opposing touch-pads on the pump **once** ("one-touch release") to open the valve, then gently compress the cylinders to empty them. In a 100-patient comparison, the OTR pump needed fewer teaching visits than the earlier Genesis pump (mean **1.19 vs 1.87**).<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

:::warning[OTR pseudo-malfunction]
In roughly **7.8%** of Coloplast Titan OTR patients in one 550-patient series (5.3% needed a visit; the rest corrected it themselves), the inflate/deflate valve disc sticks in the deflate position: the pump bulb feels **hard** and will not draw fluid, mimicking device failure. It is corrected at the bedside by applying **very firm pressure to the pump bulb** to snap the disc back to the inflate position, after which the device functions normally — recognizing this prevents a needless return to the OR.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
:::

**Deflation is the harder skill.** Patients pick up inflation quickly but commonly struggle with deflation — the more frequent early complaint — and scrotal edema in the first weeks can make the pump hard to locate. Deflation is therefore the focus of teaching and is routinely reinforced at the follow-up visit.

**Auto-inflation.** The IPP analog of an unwanted change of state is *auto-inflation* — spontaneous partial filling of the cylinders between uses from reservoir back-pressure. Modern three-piece reservoirs incorporate a **lockout valve** that largely eliminates it; a patient reporting the device inflating on its own warrants evaluation of reservoir position and valve function rather than reassurance.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

**Cycling after activation.** Patients inflate and deflate daily; one Titan protocol used daily inflation for 6 months, then maximal inflation 1–2 hours daily; in a 40-patient uncontrolled series, penile measurements were better at 12 months than at 6 months or immediately after surgery.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> Note that the IPP has **no "deactivation" lock** — unlike the [artificial urinary sphincter](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter#system-operation), it is simply left deflated between uses.

## Surgical Technique

### Approaches

Side-by-side trade-offs across the four approaches are reviewed in [Otero 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32488210).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

| Approach | Incision | Best For | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Penoscrotal (PS)** | Transverse at penoscrotal junction | Primary implants; most common | Direct pump placement; excellent corporal access; slightly longer activation wait<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Infrapubic (IP)** | Lower abdominal above pubic bone | Surgeon preference; earlier activation | Direct reservoir visualization; higher dorsal nerve injury risk; faster operative time<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **Subcoronal (SC)** | Circumcising / degloving incision | Complex reconstructive cases | Full corporal access for fibrosis, Peyronie's correction, concurrent work<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |

### Key Operative Steps

1. **Corporotomy** — longitudinal incision into corpora cavernosa bilaterally
2. **Corporal dilation** — sequential dilation to measured length; cavernotomes for fibrosis
3. **Sizing** — measure corporal length; select cylinder ± rear-tip extenders to fill space
4. **Cylinder placement** — cylinders deployed with Keith needle; proximal cap to crus, distal tip to glans
5. **Reservoir placement** (3-piece) — space of Retzius via created defect in transversalis fascia; ectopic subfascial if prior pelvic surgery
6. **Pump placement** — dependent scrotal position between dartos layers; bilateral testicular placement avoidance
7. **Connection and cycle test** — tubing connected, device inflated/deflated to confirm function before closure
8. **Closure** — layered; device left partially inflated in many protocols

**"No-touch" technique:** Device handled only with instrument tips, not hands, to minimize contamination.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Wound irrigation with antibiotic/antifungal solution before closure. Many surgeons avoid drains; some high-volume infrapubic surgeons use closed suction.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> In the PHOENIX registry, drain placement did not change hematoma rates.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

### Operative Time
Approximately **70–95 minutes** for primary cases; infrapubic approaches are generally faster than penoscrotal.

## Complications

### Early postoperative complications

Early complications are uncommon in the large prospective PHOENIX registry (30 centers, inflatable and malleable devices), and early hematoma was more frequent in low-volume than in high-volume centers. The authors issued a July 2026 correction to the cohort (1,072 to 1,034 patients), so take exact percentages from the corrected tables. See the [complication-page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/penile-implants/complications) discussion.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

### Late<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[14]](#ref14)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

| Complication | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Infection** | 1–3% (primary); up to 10%+ (revision/radiation) | Most feared; often requires complete explantation |
| **Mechanical failure** | Highly variable; above 15% in about half of studies with 5–11 years of follow-up | Fluid leak, tubing kink, pump/cylinder failure; improved with modern devices |
| **Erosion** | 0.02–5% (up to 32.5% in high-risk) | Cylinder through urethra or glans; pump/reservoir through skin |
| **Urethral injury** | Uncommon intraoperatively | Abort procedure; repair and delay reimplantation |
| **Penile shortening** | Variable | Preoperative counseling essential; Peyronie's patients at highest risk |
| **Glans ischemia** | Rare | Distal cylinder oversizing; crossover injury |
| **Sensory change** | Variable | Infrapubic approach carries slightly higher dorsal nerve risk |

### Infection Management
Infection typically mandates **complete device explantation**. The **salvage protocol** (Mulcahy technique) — immediate device replacement with antibiotic wound irrigation — can be attempted in select cases without tissue erosion, with salvage rates of ~80–90% in experienced hands.

## Outcomes

### Functional Success and Satisfaction

| Metric | Rate | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Overall functional success | &gt;90% | <sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| Prosthesis functional at ~2 years | 88% overall; 94% after primary implantation (single-center series) | <sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| Patient satisfaction | ~80–93% (pooled 83% in a 2025 meta-analysis) | <sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)[[15]](#ref15)[[17]](#ref17)[[26]](#ref26)</sup> |
| Partner satisfaction | Generally somewhat lower than patient satisfaction; higher with inflatable devices | <sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup> |

### Predictors of Satisfaction<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Surgeon experience** is the only independent predictor of higher satisfaction; satisfaction scores plateau at about 15 procedures/year<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- Hydraulic devices report higher satisfaction scores than malleable prostheses<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- Absence of complications correlates with satisfaction<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- Middle-aged men may report lower satisfaction than younger and older patients<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- Comprehensive preoperative counseling and realistic expectation-setting improves outcomes

### Long-Term Durability
- **Device survival (inflatable):** about 87% at 5 years, 77% at 10 years, 64% at 15 years and 53% at 20 years in a meta-analysis of 12 studies (20,161 patients; manufacturer-affiliated authors); newer series show better 5-year survival (91% vs 82%)<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>
- Modern coating technologies and device redesign have progressively reduced failure rates<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Median device survival is approximately **20 years** in that analysis<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

## Special Populations

**Peyronie's disease with ED:** IPP implantation straightens most curvatures mechanically; severe residual curvature requires concurrent modeling (gentle manual bending), plication, or grafting at time of implant.

**Corporal fibrosis (priapism, prior infection, revision):** Specialized dilation tools (cavernotomes, Rossello dilators, Carter-Trost technique) create space in scarred corpora. Malleable implants may be preferred when fibrosis is severe.

**Post-radiation:** Higher infection and erosion risk; antibiotic coating and meticulous technique critical; patients counseled on reduced durability.

**Neophallus (phalloplasty patients):** No native corpora; implant placed in surgically created neocorpora or directly in the neophallus tissue. Technically demanding; malleable or narrow-profile devices preferred. Erosion rates higher than in cis men.

**Concurrent penile prosthesis + AUS:** Staged or simultaneous implantation safely performed in experienced centers; pump positioning planned to avoid device-on-device conflict in the scrotum.

---

## Postoperative Management

- **Immediate:** Partial device inflation for 24 hours to 2 weeks to reduce hematoma and promote tissue molding around cylinders
- **Activation visit:** At **4–6 weeks** — wound assessment, pump cycling instruction, patient education on device use
- **Pain management:** Multimodal protocols (nerve block, NSAIDs, non-narcotic analgesics); narcotic-free pathways increasingly adopted<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- **Sexual activity:** Typically cleared at the activation visit (~4–6 weeks)
- **Device ID card:** Patients carry identification for TSA screening and MRI clearance purposes

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Natural erections after surgery | Not possible — irreversible change |
| Patient satisfaction | ~80–93% across series |
| Device survival | ~77% at 10 years, ~53% at 20 years |
| Infection risk | 1–3% primary; higher with radiation/revision |
| Effect on sensation / orgasm | None — preserved |
| Effect on ejaculation | None — preserved |
