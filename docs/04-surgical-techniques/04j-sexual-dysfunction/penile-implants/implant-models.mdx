# Penile Implant Models

The two dominant manufacturers of penile implants are Boston Scientific (AMS) and Coloplast, each with a flagship 3-piece inflatable, secondary device lines and a malleable option. Devices from Rigicon, ZSI and Promedon are used internationally. Of these, only Rigicon's Rigi10 malleable prosthesis has FDA 510(k) clearance.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> This page is a reference to the devices in clinical use in 2026, their distinguishing features and the patient types where each is used.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

See [preoperative evaluation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/penile-implants/preoperative-evaluation) for device-selection framework, and [infection](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/penile-implants/infection) for coating-specific antibiotic handling.

---

## Three-Piece Inflatable Penile Prostheses (3-Piece IPP)

The standard choice for most primary implants. It has two corporal cylinders, a scrotal pump and an abdominal reservoir, and generally gives better flaccidity and rigidity than two-piece and malleable devices.

### Boston Scientific AMS 700 Series

The longest-standing 3-piece platform in clinical use. It originated as the American Medical Systems 700 in the 1980s and has been iterated continuously. **InhibiZone** is a factory-applied impregnation of rifampin and minocycline hydrochloride. Configurations are available with and without this treatment, and InhibiZone is not available in all markets, so confirm the component label.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

| Variant | Expansion | Cuff / cylinder | Key features |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AMS 700 CX** | Controlled expansion, girth only | Standard (12 mm deflated, 18 mm inflated per the IFU) | Widely used 3-piece; MS Pump |
| **AMS 700 LGX** | Length and girth | Standard | Length expansion in addition to girth; the IFU cautions that LGX cylinders in Peyronie's disease may not give a satisfactory result |
| **AMS 700 CXR** | Controlled expansion, girth only | Narrower and shorter (9.5 mm deflated, 14.5 mm inflated; 12–18 cm) | Per the IFU, suits anatomy needing shorter and narrower cylinders and may be useful in reimplantation |

**Pump:** The MS Pump has a deflation button that is squeezed for 2–4 seconds to return fluid to the reservoir, and an internal lock-out valve designed to resist autoinflation (IFU).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

**Reservoir:** Available in 65 mL (spherical) and 100 mL capacities. The 100 mL **Conceal Low Profile Reservoir** is available in some markets.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

**Coating handling:** Do not soak InhibiZone-treated components before implantation; the manufacturer permits a brief rinse or dip in sterile solution immediately before placement. Follow the instructions supplied with the specific component.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> In Im and colleagues' in-vitro study, two-minute exposure of treated reservoir discs to 0.05% chlorhexidine gluconate did not reduce measured antimicrobial activity compared with saline or vancomycin/gentamicin. This experiment did not establish a clinical infection benefit or a preferred irrigation solution.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> See the [infection article](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/penile-implants/infection) for clinical evidence and the broader prevention protocol.

### Coloplast Titan Series

The principal competitor to the AMS 700. Its hydrophilic polyvinylpyrrolidone coating absorbs the antibiotic solution the surgeon dips it in immediately before placement, so the surgeon chooses the antibiotic, which the factory-coated AMS does not allow.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

| Variant | Expansion | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| **Titan OTR (One Touch Release)** | Girth expansion | Hydrophilic coating; one-touch release pump that deflates with one hand using release pads<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| **Titan Touch** | Girth expansion | Hydrophilic coating; the manufacturer describes a touch pump in which one firm squeeze of the release pads opens the deflate valve, with the pads serving as a landmark for the patient |
| **Titan with zero-degree tubing** | Girth expansion | The manufacturer describes proximal tubing that connects to the cylinder at zero degrees to ease insertion |

**Pump:** The one-touch release pads are the main difference between the Titan pump options.

**Reservoir:** The manufacturer lists a **Cloverleaf reservoir** with a lock-out valve (75 mL is a listed size). It is a low-profile design used for ectopic reservoir placement, including high submuscular (HSM) placement in patients with altered abdominal anatomy. Confirm sizes in the current catalog.

**Coating handling:** The device is dipped in the chosen antibiotic solution immediately before placement, commonly for at least 30 seconds. Vancomycin plus gentamicin was associated with the lowest infection rate in the Towe 2020 retrospective multicenter cohort of 468 diabetic men with a Titan (1.4% vs 6.4% for all other dips; commonly vancomycin 1 g/L plus gentamicin 80–160 mg/L).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> In an in-vitro study, adding fluconazole or amphotericin B to the vancomycin-gentamicin dip did not reduce antibacterial activity on the hydrophilic surface.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> In a retrospective cohort of 2,150 hydrophilic implants, intraoperative 0.05% CHG (Irrisept) was associated with a higher infection rate (4.6% vs 2.1%, OR 2.07), and infection was 12% in revisions with CHG. The authors advise caution with 0.05% CHG on hydrophilic implants pending prospective data.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### Rigicon Infla10 AX / X

A newer 3-piece platform from Rigicon (Ronkonkoma, NY), marketed as the Infla10 Pulse series (Pulse, Pulse X and Pulse AX). The AX version adds length expansion to girth expansion.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> The surface carries a **HydroShield hydrophilic coating** that absorbs a surgeon-selected aqueous or antibiotic dip solution; the manufacturer does not describe a factory-impregnated antibiotic. Reservoirs come in 65, 70 and 110 mL volumes, and cylinder lengths run 10–24 cm. The manufacturer's site lists the device as not available for sale in the United States, while its Rigi10 malleable prosthesis has FDA 510(k) clearance (K181673, 2019). Adoption is growing outside the US; long-term comparative data are accumulating.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Two-Piece Inflatable Penile Prostheses (2-Piece IPP)

For patients whose anatomy precludes safe abdominal reservoir placement (extensive pelvic surgery, solid-organ transplant, hernia mesh, prior reservoir complications) and for surgeons seeking to avoid the reservoir-associated complication profile. The reservoir is integrated into the rear-tip of the cylinders or the pump housing.

| Model | Manufacturer | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **AMS Ambicor** | Boston Scientific | Prefilled and preconnected; reservoirs at the proximal end of the cylinders; simple pump; the IFU describes no antibiotic surface treatment<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>; used after radical prostatectomy, cystectomy or transplant when a reservoir is undesirable |
| **Rigicon Infla2P** | Rigicon | Two-piece platform with the reservoir integrated in the scrotal pump; prefilled and preconnected; HydroShield hydrophilic coating; listed by the manufacturer as not available for sale in the United States<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |

**Trade-offs:** Less rigidity than 3-piece; less complete flaccidity; shorter cylinder expansion range. The trade-off is favorable only when 3-piece anatomy is truly not feasible; the 2-piece is not a routine first-line choice for the standard primary patient.

## Malleable / Semi-Rigid Prostheses

Two firm, bendable rods implanted in the corpora cavernosa. The device is manually bent down for concealment and bent up for use. No fluid, no pump, no reservoir.

| Model | Manufacturer | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| **AMS Spectra** | Boston Scientific | Spring-and-cable design with snap-fit rear-tip extenders (0.5–7.5 cm); 9.5, 12 or 14 mm diameters and 12, 16 or 20 cm lengths; the sell sheet lists no antibiotic treatment<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Coloplast Genesis** | Coloplast | Silver wire coil and helix core; hydrophilic coating that takes the surgeon's choice of aqueous solution; 9.5, 11 or 13 mm diameters with trimmable cylinders and tail caps (standard, +0.5 cm, +1.0 cm)<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **Boston Scientific Tactra** | Boston Scientific | Dual-layer silicone shell over a nitinol (nickel-titanium) core; external etchings for trimming to corporal size; insertion-fit rear-tip extenders; FDA approval in April 2019<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| **Rigicon Rigi10** | Rigicon | Stainless-steel interior core with a hydrophilic coating; FDA 510(k) clearance in April 2019<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| **Promedon Tube** | Promedon | Malleable: twisted silver wire core with a PTFE cover inside a tapered silicone body of graded (low, medium, high) hardness; trimmable proximal segment with separate radiopaque rear tip extenders<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| **ZSI 100** | Zephyr Surgical Implants | Compact malleable prosthesis used mainly in Europe |

**Where malleables shine:**

- Limited manual dexterity (arthritis, neuromuscular disease, intellectual disability, stroke)
- Severe corporal fibrosis where inflatable cylinders cannot be safely placed
- Neophallus (phalloplasty) implants, where inflatable device erosion risk is prohibitive
- Revision after multiple inflatable device failures
- Patient preference for simplicity / cost / durability
- Spinal cord injury patients with impaired dexterity and concerns about intermittent self-catheterization with an inflated cylinder

**Where malleables fall short:**

- Aesthetic appearance: always semi-erect, difficult to fully conceal
- Patient satisfaction scores are generally lower than with inflatable devices
- Not ideal for men with an active sex life who value a flaccid/erect distinction

## Specialty / Niche Devices

### ZSI 475 (3-piece IPP, Zephyr Surgical Implants)

European 3-piece IPP with notable design features:

- **Preconnected reservoir** (not surgeon-connected); reduces tubing connection time
- **Intrascrotal pump with dedicated FTM (female-to-male GAS) variants.** The only IPP explicitly designed and sized for neophallus placement
- Used in European gender-affirming surgery centers; a review lists the ZSI 475 as not yet FDA approved<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

The ZSI 475 FTM is the contemporary IPP with a variant specifically designed for phalloplasty, and early outcomes have been reported.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> Many centers use standard AMS or Coloplast devices in that population.

### ZSI 100 / 475 FTM — dedicated GAS variants

Specific neophallus-sized devices with modified anchoring points and shorter cylinder profiles to suit neocorporal anatomy. See the GAS section for additional detail on phalloplasty prosthesis considerations.

## Coating Technology Comparison

| Technology | Manufacturer | Mechanism | Practical implications |
|---|---|---|---|
| **InhibiZone** | Boston Scientific | Factory-applied rifampin + minocycline hydrochloride impregnation | **Do not soak**; a brief sterile rinse/dip immediately before implantation is permitted by the cited instructions. Does not replace the usual antibiotic protocol.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **Hydrophilic PVP** | Coloplast (Titan) | Polyvinylpyrrolidone surface absorbs surgeon-applied antibiotic dip | Surgeon-customized antibiotic choice; dip for at least 30 sec; 0.05% CHG was associated with more infections in one cohort and reduced antimicrobial activity in vitro<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| **HydroShield hydrophilic** | Rigicon | Hydrophilic surface that absorbs a surgeon-selected dip solution | Per-manufacturer instructions for the dip solution; comparative CHG data exist only for Titan<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Parylene microcoating** | Multiple | Ultra-thin polymer barrier reducing protein adsorption and biofilm formation | Used as a substrate beneath active antibiotics; not an independent infection-prevention layer |

The coating decision is central to the broader antibiotic strategy; see the [infection article](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/penile-implants/infection) for the complete device-specific framework including the 2026 Irrisept pivot.

## Device Selection — a Practical Framework

### Primary, virgin anatomy, no special considerations

- **Default: 3-piece AMS 700 CX/LGX or Coloplast Titan OTR/Touch.**
- Choice between AMS and Coloplast is largely surgeon preference and institutional experience.
- Patients with manual dexterity concerns (arthritis, tremor) benefit from the Titan Touch tactile ridge.
- AMS 700 LGX adds length expansion; the IFU cautions against LGX in Peyronie's disease.

### Prior pelvic surgery (radical prostatectomy, cystectomy, transplant)

- **3-piece with HSM reservoir** first choice; see [reservoir placement](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/penile-implants/reservoir-placement).
- **AMS Ambicor 2-piece** when HSM is not feasible (extensive mesh, multiple reservoir complications).

### Severe corporal fibrosis

- **AMS 700 CXR** (narrower, shorter cylinders) for inflatable placement.
- **Malleable (Tactra, Rigi10)** when fibrosis precludes safe inflatable placement.

### Peyronie's disease + ED

- **3-piece.** Peyronie's modeling is compatible with any 3-piece device, although the AMS 700 IFU states that LGX cylinders in Peyronie's disease may not give a satisfactory result.
- Subcoronal approach typically preferred for severe curvature.

### Limited manual dexterity, neuromuscular disease, intellectual disability

- **Malleable.** Tactra, Genesis, Rigi10, Spectra. Simplicity and durability outweigh aesthetic considerations.

### Neophallus (phalloplasty)

- **ZSI 475 FTM** or **malleable**: standard IPPs can be used but erosion rates are higher; dedicated neophallus devices or malleables often preferred.

### Revision after prior infection

- **3-piece with antibiotic coating** (both AMS and Coloplast are acceptable).
- Some surgeons favor switching manufacturer at revision (AMS to Coloplast or vice versa) to alter the surface-microbe interaction, though evidence supporting this is anecdotal.
- Consider malleable in the highest-risk reinfection-prone patient.

## Device Sizing

### Cylinder length

- Measured from proximal crus to distal glans with the Coloplast or Boston Scientific corporal measuring device
- Available cylinder lengths typically 12, 15, 18, 21, and 24 cm (device- and manufacturer-specific)
- **Rear-tip extenders (RTEs)** in 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, and 3 cm sizes are used to fine-tune total cylinder length to the measured corpus
- **Total length (cylinder plus RTE)** should precisely match measured corporal length. Undersize: SST deformity (the cylinder tip doesn't fill the distal corpus, and the glans droops forward); oversize: distal pain, glans aneurysm, or erosion.

### Reservoir volume

- **65 mL.** AMS spherical reservoir.
- **75 mL.** Coloplast Cloverleaf reservoir (manufacturer listing).
- **100 mL.** AMS spherical or Conceal reservoir. The AMS IFU states that the Conceal can be filled up to 100 mL to accommodate all cylinder sizes.

The AMS IFU fills the reservoir to its nominal volume. Some surgeons under-fill by approximately 5–10% of total capacity to lower the risk of autoinflation.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Manufacturer Support and Device ID

All current devices come with:

- **Patient identification card.** For TSA screening, MRI compatibility information, and future revision reference
- **MRI compatibility.** Contemporary IPPs are MR conditional. The AMS 700 IFU specifies a static magnetic field of 3 T or less, and the Ambicor IFU gives conditions for 1.5 T and 3 T. Patients should present the ID card at imaging.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Warranty.** Manufacturers state a limited warranty on device components, and the AMS IFU notes that penile prostheses are not considered lifetime implants.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Clinician support line.** Both AMS and Coloplast provide one for intraoperative device questions.
