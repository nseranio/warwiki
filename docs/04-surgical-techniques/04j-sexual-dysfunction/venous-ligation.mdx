# Penile Venous Ligation Surgery

**Penile venous ligation surgery** treats corporeal veno-occlusive dysfunction (CVOD), also called venous leak, in which inadequate trapping of blood within the corpora cavernosa prevents the achievement or maintenance of erection. The classic operation resects the deep dorsal vein and ligates pathologic drainage pathways. The AUA 2018 guideline does not recommend penile venous surgery for ED (Moderate Recommendation; Grade C) because long-term results are inconsistent.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> A 2020 review discussed a surgical niche; later selected cohorts reported combined ligation and embolization or standalone embolization, with limited long-term comparative evidence.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

The AUA 2018 guideline states: "For men with ED, penile venous surgery is not recommended (Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C)." The guideline adds that venous ligation is unlikely to result in long-term success for the overwhelming majority of men and delays treatment with more reliable options such as penile prosthesis surgery.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

A 1999 series restricted surgery to 23 men meeting at least three study-specific criteria: age &lt;50 years, mild leak on cavernosometry, &gt;30% cavernosal smooth muscle, normal cavernous electromyography, or cavernosal oxygen tension &gt;65 mm Hg.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> At long-term follow-up, 6/12 assessed men had spontaneous erections.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Prospectively collected series describe combined ligation plus embolization in selected men with drug-resistant ED and CVL confirmed on imaging; standalone embolization has also been reported. The Allaire and Hoppe reports had no randomized comparator.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> A 2024 PiVET-ED conference abstract reported interim six-month sham-controlled embolization results for 31 participants; these preliminary data do not establish long-term benefit.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

---

## Pathophysiology and rationale

Normal erection requires arterial inflow, sinusoidal smooth-muscle relaxation, and compression of subtunical venules against the tunica albuginea (the veno-occlusive mechanism). In CVOD, blood drains prematurely through incompetent veins, most commonly the deep dorsal vein, cavernous veins, and crural veins. Surgery aims to eliminate these pathologic drainage pathways.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

**Conceptual caveat.** Contemporary understanding suggests that CVOD is often an effect rather than a primary cause of ED, secondary to smooth-muscle degeneration, fibrosis, or arterial insufficiency. This may partly explain the variable surgical outcomes.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Preoperative diagnosis and patient selection

Diagnosis of venous leak requires hemodynamic confirmation:

| Test | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Pharmacologically challenged penile duplex sonography (PC-PDS)** | EDV ≥5 cm/s can support suspected veno-occlusive dysfunction after adequate pharmacologic stimulation and rigidity-based assessment; redose or reassess when the erectile response is inadequate. Assess arterial inflow separately: Flores et al. classify PSV 30–35 cm/s as a gray zone and ≥35 cm/s as normal in their protocol.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[21]](#ref21)</sup> |
| **Dynamic infusion cavernosometry / cavernosography (DICC)** | Maintenance flow rates and identification of leak sites<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **CT cavernosography** | Anatomic mapping of leak sites, used in contemporary series<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |

Selection features proposed in individual series, without a validated universal rule<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>:

- Young age and primary venous leak were favored in Cayan’s selected cohort, whereas Stief found no age association and poor results in his small primary-ED subgroup.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- Normal arterial inflow on PC-PDS. Selective internal pudendal arteriography (SIPA) was used in some older studies but is not routine work-up for venous ligation.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- Mild-to-moderate leak on cavernosometry.
- Normal cavernous electromyography (SPACE). Abnormal results predict poor outcomes (full spontaneous erections in 28 of 41 men with normal single-potential analysis of cavernous electrical activity, SPACE, versus 3 of 36 with abnormal results).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- No significant atherosclerosis, diabetes, or smoking.
- Smooth-muscle content preserved on biopsy where assessed.

## Surgical technique

Historical open procedures varied; the steps below combine reported approaches and were not all performed in each series.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| **1. Incision** | Infrapubic or inguinoscrotal approach. |
| **2. Dorsal-vein treatment** | Hwang resected the deep dorsal vein from the coronal sulcus to the pubic arch; Cayan also resected the superficial dorsal vein, while Stief ligated and divided superficial dorsal and lateral veins before deep dorsal-vein resection. Wespes reported slightly better results with resection than simple ligation.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **3. Ligation of cavernous veins** | At the penile hilum. |
| **4. Crural ligation** | Banding of the crura proximal to the entrance of the cavernous artery using umbilical tapes (in more extensive procedures, e.g. Cayan 2008). |
| **5. Preservation** | Preserve dorsal arteries and dorsal nerves bilaterally. |

**Technique and safety pearls.** These are described operative and safety practices, not evidence that the dorsal or ventral approach is superior or that venous surgery should be performed routinely:<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

- **Protect the urethra before passing crural tapes.** A Foley catheter is placed before the umbilical tape is passed between the crus and the bulbar corpus spongiosum, to help identify and avoid injury to the urethra.
- **Identify the cavernous artery before ligating.** The cavernous artery's entrance into the corpus cavernosum is traced (with intraoperative Doppler ultrasound to confirm it), and crural ligation is performed proximal to that point to avoid ligating the artery itself.
- **Use polyester, not cotton, umbilical tapes,** with at least two tapes placed separately around each crus, to reduce the foreign-body and infection reaction associated with cotton tape.
- **Reattach the dorsal tunica to the pubic periosteum** after the fundiform and suspensory ligaments are divided, to substitute for the severed ligament and limit penile shortening.

## Outcomes — variable response and uncertain long-term durability

Results are highly variable and depend heavily on patient selection, technique, and follow-up duration.

| Series | n | Follow-up | Success | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Wespes 1985**<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | 20 | Not stated | 16/20 (80%) improved enough for satisfactory intercourse | The 4 failures had serious arterial lesions |
| **Wespes 1992**<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> | 67 | Long-term | 31/67 (46%) potent | 7 relapses from deeper vein leakage; 11 failures from arterial disease |
| **Freedman 1993**<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | 46 | &gt; 12 mo | 11/46 (24%) sustained potency | 74% initial improvement with high relapse; sustained function in 43% of distal leaks and 16% of proximal leaks |
| **Hwang 1994**<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | 35 (30 followed) | 27.5 mo (mean) | 12/30 (40%) sustained spontaneous erection | 93% initial success; inadequate elimination of leaking veins, especially crural veins, was the likely cause of recurrence |
| **Stief 1994**<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | 77 | 6 mo (77/77); later mean 21 mo (70/77 reviewed) | 31/77 (40%) full spontaneous erections at 6 mo | Four of 31 initial responders later required intracavernosal pharmacotherapy; normal SPACE predicted six-month success (28/41 vs 3/36). |
| **Kim & McVary 1995**<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> | 15 | 29 mo (mean) | 60% potent | Shorter ED duration predicted success; 40% penile contracture |
| **Cayan 2008**<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | 26 | ≥ 12 mo | Complete erectile improvement in 11/26 (42%); partial in 8/26 (31%) | Mean IIEF-5 6.7 → 16.3; complete or partial satisfaction in 23/26 (88%) with or without PDE5i |
| **Rahman 2005**<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> | 11 (highly selected congenital crural leak, median age 28) | 34 mo (mean, range 6–58) | Marked improvement in 9/11 (82%) | IIEF-5 8.9 → 17.5 (mean); items 2, 3, and 5 improved (p &lt; 0.05); the paper attributes lasting results to careful selection for an isolated congenital crural abnormality rather than systemic disease or smooth-muscle atrophy |
| **Allaire 2021**<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> (combined ligation + embolization) | 45 | 14 mo | 73.3% primary and 82.2% secondary success | Prospective; combined open and endovascular. Primary success was clinical EHS ≥ 3 with penetrative intercourse possible, no vascular reoperation, and no prosthesis; secondary success was penetrative intercourse after allowing reoperation |
| **Allaire 2025**<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> (mixed venous procedures) | 171 | PC-PDS at 3 mo; clinical follow-up mean 23 mo | 138/171 (80.7%) met the study’s composite: three-month pharmacologically challenged EHS &gt;3 with a gain ≥0.5, or postoperative erection stability if preoperative challenged EHS was 4 | 81.9% received ligation plus embolization, 12.9% ligation alone, and 5.3% embolization alone; 24/171 (14.0%) required vascular reoperation |

**Pattern.** Freedman reported intercourse-capable erections in 34/46 (74%) within 6 months; Hwang reported excellent erections in 28/30 (93%) within 3 months.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> At later follow-up, Freedman reported unaided intercourse in 11/46 (24%) after &gt;12 months, Hwang spontaneous erections in 12/30 (40%) after a mean 27.5 months, and Wespes intercourse-capable potency in 31/67 (46%) at long-term follow-up.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> The 2021 Allaire combined-procedure report found 73.3% primary success at a mean 14 months.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Its 45 patients were included in the 2025 cohort of 171, in which 138/171 (80.7%) met a composite of three-month pharmacologically challenged EHS &gt;3 with a gain ≥0.5 or postoperative erection stability when preoperative challenged EHS was 4; mean clinical follow-up was 23 months, and 81.9% received both ligation and embolization.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> These overlapping reports came from one group and neither included a randomized comparator; longer-term durability remains uncertain.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Complications

| Complication | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Penile shortening** | Hwang: 3 cases (35 operated; 30 long-term followed); Freedman: 20/46 (43%) | Counsel explicitly.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **Penile hypoesthesia / glans numbness** | Wespes: 4/67 (6%); Hwang: 4 cases (complication denominator unstated); Freedman: 9/46 (20%) | <sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **Penile deviation / curvature** | 3/30 (Hwang) | <sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **Wound infection** | 1/30 (Hwang) | <sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **Early reoperation for penile or groin complication (Allaire 2025)** | 11/171 (6.4%) | The mixed-procedure cohort also reported two symptomatic pulmonary emboli.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Recurrent ED after an initial response** | 7/67 relapsed several months after surgery in Wespes’ series.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> | Overall late favorable outcomes in older series ranged from 24% to 46%, but those rates include primary nonresponse and measure different endpoints.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> Incomplete treatment of leak sites, especially crural veins, was proposed as a cause; cavernosography after failed stripping has shown a persistent cavernosal vein.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| **Penile contracture** | 40% (Kim & McVary 1995) | <sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| **Hematoma with skin necrosis** | 1 case in Stief’s 77-patient series | Required multiple plastic revisions and left significant glans paresthesia.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Persistent preputial edema** | 14 cases in Stief’s series | Reported as a postoperative side effect; the report does not assign a complication denominator.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |

## Emerging — venous-leak embolization

Endovascular embolization of venous-leak sites has emerged as a less invasive alternative or adjunct:

- **Hoppe 2026 multicenter registry** (n = 175): 82/138 (59.4%) achieved an IIEF-15 improvement of at least 4 points at a mean 9.2 months.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> Venous leak recurred or persisted in 10.2% at a mean 16 months.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> Non-target pulmonary embolism occurred in 3/175 (1.7%).<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> A partly overlapping SwissPower analysis found a ≥4-point IIEF-6 improvement in 62/140 (44.3%) at nine months and patient-reported improvement in 102/142 (72%) at six weeks after venous embolization.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- **Allaire 2021 and 2025.** The 2021 combined-procedure cohort (n = 45) reported 37/45 (82.2%) secondary success after allowing reoperation, with 32% of successful men achieving penetration without medication.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The overlapping 2025 cohort (n = 171) found that 138/171 (80.7%) met a composite of three-month pharmacologically challenged EHS &gt;3 with a gain ≥0.5 or postoperative erection stability when preoperative challenged EHS was 4; 81.9% received both modalities.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **PiVET-ED trial.** The published protocol describes a prospective, randomized, single-blinded, sham-controlled study of pelvic vein embolization for venogenic ED (planned n = 80), designed to provide randomized evidence.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

## Guideline position

| Body | Position |
|---|---|
| **AUA 2018**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Not recommended (Moderate Recommendation; Grade C). Unlikely to result in long-term success; delays prosthesis treatment |
| **EAU 2025 and March 2026 limited update**<sup>[[17]](#ref17)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | Venous ligation surgery for veno-occlusive dysfunction is "no longer recommended because of poor long-term results" (guideline text, without a strength rating; section 5.6.11.b of the March 2026 update, which also questions the concept of venogenic ED and reports 60–70% long-term success for revascularization in young men with post-traumatic arterial stenosis) |

## When to refer / when not to operate

- **Select cautiously.** Assess venous dysfunction, arterial inflow, and competing ED causes; counsel about high recurrence and the AUA and EAU non-recommendations.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> Young age, primary leak, nonsmoking status, and absence of major vascular comorbidity characterize some older favorable cohorts. SIPA, SPACE, DICC leak grading, and smooth-muscle biopsy were study-specific selection tools, not prerequisites for every contemporary patient.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Guideline caution.** The AUA advises against penile venous surgery for ED (Moderate Recommendation; Grade C) and describes penile prosthesis as a more reliable surgical option.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Evaluate arterial disease and other untreated organic causes before considering a venous procedure; failed medical therapy alone is not a contraindication in the published venous-surgery cohorts.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Embolization.** Embolization or combined ligation plus embolization has been reported from experienced centers.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> In Allaire's 2025 series, patients at high thromboembolic risk did not undergo embolization; Hoppe's registry reported non-target pulmonary embolism in 3/175 treated men (1.7%).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

## Postoperative management

- **Activity restriction.** Avoid intercourse, masturbation, cycling, and strenuous exercise for 4–6 weeks.
- **Postoperative imaging.** Cayan used duplex ultrasound or cavernosography as needed; the Allaire 2025 cohort repeated pharmacologically challenged duplex at three months.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **PDE5 inhibitor response.** In Cayan's 26-man series, 12 men achieved satisfactory erections with PDE5 inhibitors after surgery despite preoperative nonresponse.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **PRO assessment.** IIEF / IIEF-5 at baseline, 3 mo, 6 mo, 12 mo, with annual follow-up given known late attrition.
- **Counseling.** Counsel preoperatively that penile shortening was reported in 3 patients in Hwang's 35-man series (30 had long-term follow-up) and in 20/46 (43%) men in Freedman's series<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>, and discuss the high long-term recurrence rate; document the AUA 2018 non-recommendation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
