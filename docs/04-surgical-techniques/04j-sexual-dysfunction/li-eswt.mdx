# Low-Intensity Extracorporeal Shockwave Therapy (Li-ESWT)

**Low-intensity extracorporeal shockwave therapy (Li-ESWT)** is a noninvasive modality studied in several urologic and urogynecologic conditions. Evidence varies substantially by indication. The AUA 2018 guideline calls it investigational for ED. Trials report pain benefit in CP/CPPS and in Peyronie's disease, although the Peyronie's evidence is inconsistent. Applications in female SUI, OAB and female sexual dysfunction are early-stage or preclinical.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Mechanism of Action

Li-ESWT uses energy flux densities &lt; 0.25 mJ/mm². Preclinical studies propose that it acts through two physical forces: direct mechanical stress from the high peak pressure wave, and cavitation bubble formation or collapse that causes local microtrauma and is thought to trigger regenerative cascades.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The proposed effects are:

- **Neovascularization.** VEGF, eNOS, and VEGF-receptor sensitization promote new blood vessel formation
- **Tissue regeneration.** Chemoattractant recruitment or activation of stem or progenitor cells; increased PCNA expression
- **Anti-inflammatory effects.** Downregulation of COX-2, IL-6, and NGF; modulation of inflammatory cascades
- **Nerve repair.** Increased neurotrophic factors, Schwann-cell activation
- **Increased tissue permeability.** May facilitate intravesical drug delivery

---

**Focused versus radial devices.** Most ED efficacy trials used focused shockwave devices (piezoelectric, electromagnetic or electrohydraulic sources derived from lithotripsy technology), which concentrate energy at a defined focal depth. The CP/CPPS evidence below includes both focused and radial devices.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[42]](#ref42)</sup> **Radial wave devices** generate a pressure wave by a ballistic projectile striking an endplate; the wave disperses radially from the applicator with rapid energy attenuation rather than converging on a focal point, producing lower peak energy and a less-defined treatment zone. Radial devices are less stringently regulated than the focused devices studied in ED trials and are often marketed directly to patients. A patient reporting he has "already tried shockwave therapy" for ED may have used a radial-wave device rather than one of the focused devices in the trials cited below, which is relevant both to interpreting a prior non-response and to whether referral for focused Li-ESWT represents a meaningfully different option. The same focused-versus-radial distinction affects reported outcomes in CP/CPPS below.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## 1. Erectile Dysfunction

### Guideline positions

- **AUA 2018.** Conditional Recommendation, Grade C; investigational: "findings from randomized sham-controlled trials that have evaluated low-intensity ESWT do not clearly indicate that benefits reliably outweigh risks/burdens for men with ED."<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **EAU 2026.** Use Li-SWT, with or without PDE5 inhibitors, in men with mild vasculogenic ED, as an alternative in well-informed men who do not want or are not suitable for oral therapy, and in vasculogenic ED with poor PDE5i response (weak).<sup>[[42]](#ref42)</sup> An earlier review of guideline recommendations reached a similar conclusion.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **ESSM 2019.** Efficacy "doubtful and deserves more investigation."<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Cochrane 2025 (21 RCTs, 1,357 men)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Outcome | Effect | Certainty |
|---|---|---|
| Short-term IIEF | MD **3.89** (95% CI 2.89–4.89; I² = 62%; 15 studies) | Low |
| IIEF-EF at &gt;3 months | MD **5.25** (95% CI 2.47–8.04; I² = 87%; 5 studies, 276 men) | Low |
| Short-term EHS | MD 1.06 (95% CI 0.83–1.28) | Low |
| AEs vs sham | RD 0.00 (95% CI -0.01 to 0.02; I² = 0%) | Low |
| Discontinuation vs sham | RR 0.77 (95% CI 0.47–1.27; I² = 0%) | Low |

The Cochrane authors note that the short-term IIEF-EF difference may not reach the 4-point MCID, and that all included trials had treatment periods of 3 months or less.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> A 2026 meta-analysis of 19 RCTs (You) found statistically significant pooled IIEF improvements at 1, 3 and 6 months, but most studies failed to reach the MCID, so clinical benefit remains unclear.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### Best responders

In a 2017 meta-analysis, men with mild-to-moderate ED had better responses than men with more severe ED or comorbidities.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> A 2022 double-blind, sham-controlled RCT in moderate ED (n = 70) reported that 27 of 34 evaluated men (79%) reached the MCID at 3 months versus none in the sham group (p &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> In a retrospective series of 126 PDE5i-refractory men, the severe-ED group, which had a higher prevalence of diabetes, showed no significant improvement.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

### Treatment parameters

Protocols are heterogeneous. A 2018 meta-analysis of 9 RCTs found larger IIEF gains with lower energy density (0.09 mJ/mm²), 3,000 pulses per treatment and courses shorter than 6 weeks.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> The 2026 meta-regression associated treatment frequency and pulse number per course with response and could not identify a superior regimen.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> The sham-controlled trial above used 12 sessions twice weekly, 5,000 impulses, 0.096 mJ/mm² and 5 Hz.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

### Post-prostatectomy ED

The Cochrane 2025 review excluded men after radical prostatectomy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> A meta-analysis of 5 studies (n = 460) reported an IIEF benefit at 3–4 months (WMD −2.04, p = 0.02) but no significant difference at 9–12 months, with a low level of evidence.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> A sham-controlled RCT (Pedersen 2025, n = 75) found no significant improvement in IIEF or EHS at 24 weeks, with a median IIEF of 4 in both groups.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> A prospective comparative study of 80 completers (Jang 2023) reported that early Li-ESWT plus daily tadalafil was more often associated with EHS ≥ 3 at 6 months than tadalafil alone (OR 3.62, p = 0.041).<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

## 2. Peyronie's Disease — pain only

The strongest signal for Li-ESWT in Peyronie's disease is for pain. The EAU 2026 guideline states that ESWT may be offered to treat penile pain but does not improve curvature or plaque size, and recommends it for pain in the acute phase (weak).<sup>[[42]](#ref42)</sup> ESSM: "patients reporting pain associated with PD may benefit from LISWT, although no effect is expected on disease progression."<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Sokolakis 2022, sham-controlled RCT in stable PD (n = 102 randomized; 63 evaluated at 3 years)<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

- Pain improvement at 4 wk: 16/34 versus 7/29 (p = 0.005); VAS MD 2.2 points (p = 0.002)<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- Pain improvement at 3 yr: 15/34 versus 7/29 (p = 0.031); VAS MD 2.5 points (p = 0.002)<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- No significant difference in curvature or sexual function at any time point
- No treatment-related complications

### Cochrane 2023 PD (3 studies, 151 participants)

Li-ESWT may improve penile pain (MD −1.09; 95% CI −2.22 to 0.04; I² = 59%; low certainty), but the CI crosses the one-point MCID threshold.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> The review rated the effect on curvature as little to no difference.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

### Spirito 2024 (n = 194 acute PD; 10-yr retrospective)<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

Mean follow-up was 125.6 months. Pain fell from 6.5 to 3.1 at 3 months and 1.0 at 12 months and stayed stable. Mean curvature worsened from 18.3° to 28.6° at 12 months and then stabilized. Satisfaction was &gt; 90%.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> There was no control group.

### Bakr 2021 meta (3 RCTs, n = 238)

The pooled analysis found no significant reduction in penile deviation and no effect on erectile function or pain scale. ESWT was associated with plaque-size reduction (OR 2.59, p = 0.02), of questionable clinical significance.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

**Bottom line.** One sham-controlled RCT and one retrospective series report durable pain relief, but the pooled analyses are inconsistent on pain (Bakr 2021 found no effect; Cochrane 2023 rates the estimate low certainty). Controlled studies show no curvature or erectile function benefit. Li-ESWT is used as an adjunct in the acute, painful phase.

## 3. Chronic Prostatitis / Chronic Pelvic Pain Syndrome (CP/CPPS)

The AUA 2025 male chronic pelvic pain guideline advises discussing low-intensity ESWT with patients who have CP/CPPS (Statement 26; Moderate Recommendation, Grade A). This recommendation concerns CP/CPPS; the AUA's investigational classification for ED applies to a different indication.<sup>[[43]](#ref43)</sup>

Results are most consistently positive in this indication.

| Source | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Mykoniatis 2021** meta of 5 sham RCTs<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> | NIH-CPSI pain WMD 3.2 (post-tx) → 4.4 (1 mo) → 3.61 (3 mo); total + QoL also improved; long-term LUTS / EF effects clinically insignificant |
| **Wu 2026** meta of 8 RCTs (n = 455)<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> | At follow-up closest to 12 wk, NIH-CPSI WMD −8.46 (I² = 94.8%); exploratory device subgroups: **focused** WMD −6.59 (I² = 0%); **radial** imprecise, extreme heterogeneity (I² = 98.2%) |
| **Zheng 2026** SR of 11 studies<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> | All demonstrated significant NIH-CPSI total + pain improvement; minor AEs (1 first-degree burn, 4 transient hematuria/hematospermia) |
| **Labetov 2024** meta<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> | Effect long-lasting through 6-mo follow-up (p &lt; 0.05) |
| **Cochrane 2018** (Franco)<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> | Short-term NIH-CPSI MD −6.18 (95% CI −7.46 to −4.89; 3 studies, 157 participants; high certainty); may not be sustained at medium-term follow-up (low certainty) |

**Protocol comparison.** Mykoniatis 2021 RCT of once-weekly versus twice-weekly (6 sessions, 5,000 pulses, 0.096 mJ/mm²) showed no significant difference; both were equally effective and safe.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

**ESSM 2019:** "LISWT is not a primary treatment for CP/CPPS, but it may be considered as an option to relieve pain."<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## 4. Interstitial Cystitis / Bladder Pain Syndrome (IC/BPS)

Early; not in the AUA IC/BPS guideline.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

- **Chuang 2020 double-blind placebo-controlled RCT (54 screened; 49 randomized, 24 ESWT and 25 placebo)**<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>: primary endpoint (OSS) not met (both arms improved); however, more ESWT patients achieved VAS ≥ 3 improvement at 4 wk post-treatment (p = 0.035) and at 12 wk post-treatment among 21 evaluated per arm (12/21 [57.1%] versus 4/21 [19.0%], p = 0.011).
- **Shen 2021 RCT (n = 25)**<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>: significant OSS and VAS reduction at 4 wk post-treatment (p &lt; 0.05).
- **Jhang 2023 prospective (n = 30)**<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup>: ICSI 12.87 to 7.87, sustained to 1 yr.
- Preclinical (rat cystitis models): Li-ESWT suppressed bladder overactivity, reduced COX-2/IL-6/NGF, improved inflammation.<sup>[[30]](#ref30)[[31]](#ref31)</sup>

## 5. Female Stress Urinary Incontinence

**Investigational.** A single-blind, sham-controlled RCT randomized 60 women (45 active, 15 sham); four weeks of Li-ESWT reduced pad-test leakage versus sham. Longer follow-up was principally within the active arm, so comparative durability remains uncertain.<sup>[[45]](#ref45)</sup>

- **Long 2020** single-arm multicenter (n = 50 women): 8 wk Li-ESWT improved pad-test leakage, Qmax, functional bladder capacity, frequency, urgency, and nocturia at 1-mo follow-up.<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup>
- **Zhang 2020** irreversible rat SUI model<sup>[[33]](#ref33)</sup>: increased leak-point pressure, increased urethral or vaginal smooth and striated muscle, restored urothelial integrity, increased progenitor cells.
- **Zambon 2019** narrative review: minimally invasive approach based on animal data; no controlled human trials.<sup>[[34]](#ref34)</sup>

---

## 6. Overactive Bladder

**Investigational.** A single-blind, sham-controlled RCT randomized 65 women (50 active, 15 sham) to eight weekly treatments. The report describes symptom and diary improvement through six months, but longer-term outcomes were largely compared with baseline rather than sham.<sup>[[46]](#ref46)</sup>

- **Lee 2020** prospective pilot (n = 82 women): 8 wk Li-ESWT improved OAB questionnaires, functional bladder capacity, daytime frequency, urgency, nocturia and Qmax; all listed improvements except functional bladder capacity remained significant at 3-mo follow-up.<sup>[[35]](#ref35)</sup>
- **Kimura 2022** ischemic OAB rat model: restored voiding intervals (14.9 versus 5.1 min), increased bladder blood flow, upregulated sGC-cGMP pathway with VEGF / CD31 induction.<sup>[[36]](#ref36)</sup>

---

## 7. Vulvodynia / Provoked Vestibulodynia

Two small sham-controlled RCTs are the strongest controlled evidence among the female applications.

- **Gruenwald 2021** double-blind sham-controlled RCT (n = 32 provoked vestibulodynia; 500 pulses, 0.09 mJ/mm², 2×/wk × 6 wk)<sup>[[37]](#ref37)</sup>: dyspareunia VAS fell from 8.0 to 4.4 at 3 months (p &lt; 0.005 versus baseline).
- **Hurt 2020** double-blind sham-controlled RCT (n = 62 vulvodynia; 3,000 pulses, 0.25 mJ/mm², weekly × 4)<sup>[[38]](#ref38)</sup>: VAS and cotton-swab reductions at 1, 4 and 12 weeks in the treatment group (p &lt; 0.01), with no significant change in the placebo group.
- **Siegal & Chubak 2021** cautioned: "enthusiasm with which energy-based treatments for sexual dysfunction have been adopted is disproportionate to the amount of data currently available."<sup>[[39]](#ref39)</sup>

---

## 8. Premature Ejaculation — experimental

**No dedicated sham-controlled RCTs of Li-ESWT monotherapy.** A SR found dapoxetine plus non-pharmacologic therapies (including shockwave) significantly improved IELT versus dapoxetine alone (SDM 1.6, p = 0.01).<sup>[[40]](#ref40)[[41]](#ref41)</sup>

The AUA/SMSNA 2020 guideline advises counseling that evidence is insufficient to support alternative therapies for PE (Statement 14; Expert Opinion).<sup>[[44]](#ref44)</sup>

## Comprehensive summary

| Indication | Evidence | Key finding | Guideline |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ED** | 21 RCTs (Cochrane 2025); low certainty | IIEF +3.89 short-term; MCID uncertain | AUA 2018: investigational; EAU 2026: weak |
| **PD pain** | Sham RCT plus pooled analyses; low certainty | Pain relief to 3 yr in one RCT; pooled pain estimate inconsistent; no curvature benefit | EAU 2026: pain, acute phase (weak); ESSM: pain only |
| **CP/CPPS** | 5 sham RCTs in Mykoniatis 2021; 8 RCTs with sham or standard-therapy controls in Wu 2026<sup>[[20]](#ref20)[[21]](#ref21)</sup> | NIH-CPSI improved; effects ≥ 3 mo | AUA 2025: discuss (Moderate, Grade A)<sup>[[43]](#ref43)</sup>; ESSM 2019: option for pain; Cochrane 2018: high certainty short-term |
| **IC/BPS** | 2 RCTs; low certainty | VAS ≥ 3 improvement; primary OSS not met | Not in AUA IC/BPS |
| **Female SUI** | Small sham RCTs plus uncontrolled and preclinical studies | One trial reported four-week pad-test improvement versus sham; the pooled sham comparison was not significant and comparative durability is uncertain | Investigational<sup>[[32]](#ref32)[[33]](#ref33)[[47]](#ref47)</sup> |
| **OAB** | Small sham RCTs with overlapping cohorts plus pilot studies | Short-term symptom improvement versus sham; Qmax gains and longer follow-up are largely within-arm | Investigational<sup>[[35]](#ref35)[[47]](#ref47)</sup> |
| **Vulvodynia / vestibulodynia** | 2 sham RCTs (small) | Significant pain + sexual function improvement | Emerging |
| **Premature ejaculation** | No dedicated RCTs | Adjunct to dapoxetine | Experimental |

## Safety profile (all indications)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[16]](#ref16)[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

- Most common: minor bruising / ecchymosis, transient discomfort
- Rare: hematuria, hematospermia (CP/CPPS), one reported first-degree burn
- Serious adverse events were not reported in the sources reviewed here
- **No difference versus sham** in Cochrane ED review (RD 0.00)
- Long-term safety data are limited

---

## Key unresolved issues

1. **Protocol heterogeneity.** Energy density, pulse number, frequency, sessions, and focused, radial or multifocal devices vary across studies, limiting comparisons and conclusions about a preferred regimen.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
2. **Statistical versus clinical significance.** Many ED studies show statistically significant changes that fall below MCID.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
3. **Durability.** Data beyond 6–12 months are sparse; one Peyronie's pain RCT evaluated 63 men at 3 years, while a CP/CPPS meta-analysis reported effects through 6 months.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
4. **Device subgroups.** In exploratory CP/CPPS subgroup analyses at follow-up closest to 12 weeks, focused-device trials had I² = 0%, while radial-device trials had I² = 98.2%; head-to-head data are lacking.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

---

## Bottom Line for the Reconstructive Urologist

Li-ESWT is investigational for ED in the AUA guideline, with statistically significant but clinically uncertain benefit and most trials falling short of the MCID.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> The EAU gives a weak recommendation for mild vasculogenic ED.<sup>[[42]](#ref42)</sup> In acute-phase Peyronie's disease it is used for pain and does not change curvature.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[18]](#ref18)[[42]](#ref42)</sup> Results are most consistently positive in CP/CPPS. In Wu 2026, an exploratory focused-device subgroup showed NIH-CPSI improvement with I² = 0% at follow-up closest to 12 weeks; separate reviews reported improvement at 3 and 6 months across study protocols.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)[[21]](#ref21)[[23]](#ref23)</sup> Small sham-controlled trials are positive in IC/BPS (for a secondary pain endpoint) and in vulvodynia or vestibulodynia.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)[[37]](#ref37)[[38]](#ref38)</sup> Female SUI and OAB have small sham-controlled trials, but remain investigational. A review of non-prostatic urinary indications found heterogeneous protocols, short follow-up and uncertain clinical utility.<sup>[[47]](#ref47)</sup> Patients are counseled about heterogeneous protocols, uncertain durability and out-of-pocket cost.
