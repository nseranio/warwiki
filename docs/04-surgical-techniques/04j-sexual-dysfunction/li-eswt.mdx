# Low-Intensity Extracorporeal Shockwave Therapy (Li-ESWT)

Low-intensity extracorporeal shockwave therapy delivers focused acoustic pulses to the cavernosal tissue (for ED) or to the penile shaft over the plaque (for Peyronie's-related pain) with the proposed mechanisms of **neovascularization, recruitment of endogenous progenitor cells, and tissue remodeling**. It occupies the **Tier 2.5 / regenerative** position in the contemporary ED algorithm and is acknowledged in the 2025 EAU update and the 2025 Cochrane systematic review as a **promising but not-yet-guideline-endorsed** standalone option. It is best understood as an **adjunct** to PDE5 inhibitors in mild-to-moderate vasculogenic ED, or as a **pain-directed-only** therapy in Peyronie's disease.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

> For the broader Tier-2.5 framework (PRP, stem-cell, combination regenerative therapies) see the [Erectile Dysfunction Decision Framework](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction). For Peyronie's pain-phase management see [Peyronie's Disease](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease).

---

## Mechanism of Action

Acoustic energy applied at low intensity (energy flux density ~0.05–0.20 mJ/mm²) appears to upregulate VEGF, eNOS, and endothelial-progenitor-cell recruitment, drive cavernosal neovascularization, and reduce fibrotic burden. The dose-response relationship is non-linear — higher energy (0.15 mJ/mm²) produces larger effect sizes than lower energy in network meta-analysis.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

---

## Erectile Dysfunction

### Evidence base

- **Lu 2017 *Eur Urol* meta-analysis** (14 studies, n = 833) — Li-ESWT improved IIEF (mean difference 2.00; 95% CI 0.99–3.00; p &lt; 0.001) over sham and pre-treatment baseline.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Hinojosa-Gonzalez 2024 *J Sex Med* Bayesian network meta** of regenerative therapies — Li-ESWT SMD vs control 0.84 (95% CrI 0.49–1.2). High-energy protocol (0.15 mJ/mm²) SMD 1.1.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Cochrane 2025 (Ergun)** — concluded evidence is still evolving; protocols are heterogeneous; long-term durability and standardized dosing remain unsettled.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Capogrosso 2025 *Eur Urol* update** — Li-ESWT acknowledged as the most evidence-supported regenerative option but **not yet recommended as standalone therapy** for routine practice.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Patient selection

**Best candidates:** mild-to-moderate vasculogenic ED, partial PDE5i responders, men preferring non-pharmacologic options, men with cardiovascular contraindications to PDE5i. **Poor candidates:** severe ED unresponsive to ICI, neurogenic ED (no vascular substrate to remodel), patients expecting prosthesis-level reliability.

### Typical protocol

| Parameter | Range |
|---|---|
| **Energy flux density** | 0.05–0.25 mJ/mm² (higher energy = larger effect, but more nociception) |
| **Pulses per session** | 1500–3000 |
| **Sessions** | 6–12 over 3–9 weeks |
| **Anatomic targets** | Distal, mid, and proximal corpora cavernosa bilaterally, plus crura |
| **Frequency** | 1–2 sessions/week |

### Outcomes summary

- IIEF-EF improvement of **2–6 points** in most positive trials
- Effect attenuates by **6–12 months** post-treatment in many series — durability is not yet established
- Combination with **PDE5i** appears to outperform either alone in mild–moderate vasculogenic ED<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Peyronie's Disease

A specific evidence message: **ESWT improves penile pain in acute-phase Peyronie's disease but does NOT improve curvature or plaque size**.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> Per AUA 2015, ESWT **should not be used for the curvature or plaque-burden component** of PD management. It is acceptable as an analgesic-only intervention during the acute phase if pain is the dominant symptom and other interventions are not desired.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

The Cochrane 2023 PD non-surgical review found **no evidence of curvature reduction** with ESWT.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

---

## Adverse Effects and Contraindications

Generally well-tolerated. Most-reported events: transient penile pain or paresthesia during treatment, ecchymosis, mild hematuria. **Contraindications:** acute infection, bleeding diathesis, anticoagulation requiring interruption, penile prosthesis in situ.

---

## Bottom Line for the Reconstructive Urologist

Li-ESWT is a **reasonable adjunct** to optimized PDE5i therapy in mild-to-moderate vasculogenic ED, and an **acceptable pain-directed therapy** in acute-phase PD when pain is dominant. It is **not a standalone replacement** for guideline-recommended first-line ED therapy, and it is **not a curvature-modifying therapy** in PD. Counsel patients on heterogeneous protocols, uncertain durability, and out-of-pocket cost (rarely covered by insurance).
