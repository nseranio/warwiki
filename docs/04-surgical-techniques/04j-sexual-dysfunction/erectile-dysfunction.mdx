# Erectile Dysfunction (Procedures)

This page hosts the operative decision support for erectile dysfunction (ED): the stepwise framework from lifestyle modification through PDE5 inhibitors, intracavernosal injection, vacuum erection device, intraurethral alprostadil, regenerative therapy, and penile-prosthesis implantation, including infection salvage. For pathophysiology, evaluation, and natural history, see [Erectile Dysfunction in Clinical Conditions](/docs/clinical-conditions/03g-genital-scrotal/erectile-dysfunction). For implant-specific operative deep-dives, see the [Penile Implants subsection](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/penile-implants).

---

## Decision Framework

ED management is patient-centered and shared-decision based. The AUA 2018 Erectile Dysfunction Guideline asks clinicians to inform every man of all treatment options that are not medically contraindicated, and holds that beginning with any option is valid regardless of invasiveness or reversibility; the EAU 2025 Male Sexual & Reproductive Health Update, carried into the March 2026 limited update, likewise replaces the traditional three-tier concept with a personalized algorithm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> The EAU 2026 recommendations rate PDE5 inhibitors first-line (strong), intracavernosal injection an alternative first-line or second-line option (strong), topical or intraurethral alprostadil, vacuum devices and low-intensity shockwave weak, and prosthesis for failure of other treatment or by preference (strong).<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> In practice most men move from least to most invasive, with selection guided by severity, etiology, comorbidities, and patient/partner preference; the ladder below reflects that usual sequence. The EAU 2025 update emphasizes personalized treatment: patients should be fully counseled on every available modality and choose based on invasiveness, tolerability, effectiveness, and personal expectations.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Combination therapy (PDE5i plus a second agent or device) improves IIEF modestly over monotherapy (mean +1.8 points; larger in monotherapy-resistant and post-prostatectomy ED) and may be considered for refractory, complex, or difficult-to-treat ED; most individual add-ons rest on single studies and heterogeneity is high.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> In one 40-patient observational cohort, successful immediate salvage was associated with a mean 0.6 cm reduction in total corporeal length versus 3.7 cm after delayed reimplantation.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

### Treatment Selection by Clinical Scenario

| Clinical Scenario | First-Line | Alternative(s) | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Newly diagnosed ED, no contraindications** | Lifestyle modification + [PDE5 inhibitor](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/pde5-inhibitors), titrated to maximum dose with ≥6 properly executed attempts | Daily tadalafil 2.5–5 mg if concurrent BPH/LUTS or preference for spontaneity | Skipping cardiovascular risk assessment; declaring failure before maximum-dose trial |
| **Symptomatic hypogonadism confirmed by two low morning fasting total-testosterone measurements + ED<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>** | PDE5i plus [testosterone replacement](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/testosterone-replacement) when indicated, after assessing fertility intent (EAU 2026 uses total T below 12 nmol/L and treats testosterone alone as first-line for mild ED, strong, with a PDE5 inhibitor added for more severe ED, weak)<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | PDE5i alone (AUA: may work better combined with testosterone when deficiency is confirmed, defined as total T &lt;300 ng/dL plus symptoms/signs) | Testosterone alone without confirmed hypogonadism, or as sole treatment for moderate/severe hypogonadal ED (AUA advises against testosterone as ED monotherapy; EAU recommends it first-line for mild hypogonadal ED, strong)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| **PDE5i non-responder after an adequately instructed, individually titrated trial<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>** | Combination therapy: switch agent → PDE5i + [VED](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/ved) → daily tadalafil → PDE5i + intraurethral alprostadil | [Intracavernosal injection (ICI)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/intracavernosal-injections) | Prosthesis implantation without counseling on benefits, harms and other suitable options<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| **PDE5i failure / intolerance, prefers pharmacotherapy** | [ICI](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/intracavernosal-injections) (alprostadil or a compounded combination selected by response and tolerability), with an in-office injection test and technique training followed by instructed home dose titration<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | Intraurethral alprostadil where available ([MUSE](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/muse-intraurethral) is discontinued in the US) | Self-titration without supervised first dose (priapism risk) |
| **Prefers non-pharmacologic option** | [Vacuum erection device](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/ved) | MUSE | Restrictive ring &gt;30 minutes (ischemia/skin-necrosis risk); VED use with bleeding disorders or anticoagulant therapy (EAU contraindications)<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| **Concurrent nitrate use (any formulation)** | VED, ICI (alprostadil monotherapy) | MUSE | **All PDE5 inhibitors (absolute contraindication)** |
| **Post-radical prostatectomy ED (early, &lt;12–24 months)** | Penile rehabilitation: PDE5i ± VED ± ICI | Daily tadalafil | Promising unassisted recovery from early PDE5i (AUA: may not improve spontaneous, unassisted function)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Mild–moderate vasculogenic ED, partial PDE5i response, prefers regenerative adjunct** | Consider [Li-ESWT](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/li-eswt) with or without PDE5i after counseling on uncertain clinical benefit (EAU weak; AUA investigational)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[1]](#ref1)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | Intracavernous PRP only in a clinical trial (EAU 2026; AUA experimental)<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Stem-cell therapy (AUA: investigational)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Refractory to optimized conservative + medical therapy, or patient preference** | [3-piece IPP](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/penile-implants/implant-models) (AMS 700 series or Coloplast Titan) | 3-piece IPP with an [ectopic reservoir](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/penile-implants/reservoir-placement) when feasible after prior pelvic surgery; 2-piece IPP if safe reservoir placement is not feasible<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>; malleable (limited dexterity, neophallus, salvage, cost-constrained) | Continued failed pharmacotherapy without device counseling |
| **Peyronie's disease + ED unresponsive to medical therapy** | [IPP with adjunctive straightening](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease/prosthesis-with-straightening) (Wilson manual modeling → tunical plication → plaque incision/grafting as needed) | (See [Peyronie's algorithm](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies)) | IPP alone without addressing curvature |
| **Severe corporal fibrosis (post-priapism, post-explant)** | Malleable prosthesis at high-volume center | Staged dilation → 3-piece IPP | Standard 3-piece in densely fibrosed corpora without dilation strategy |
| **Acute prosthesis infection** | Assess medical stability and local tissue viability; discuss immediate complete explantation, washout and reimplantation (IPP or malleable) with suitable patients<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | [Extracapsular reimplantation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/penile-implants/infection) (mature capsule) | **Explant with delayed reimplantation** when the patient is suitable for salvage (mean 3.7 cm corporal length loss vs 0.6 cm with successful immediate salvage)<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| **Young man with traumatic arterial injury (no atherosclerosis)** | [Penile arterial revascularization](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/penile-arterial-revascularization) at high-volume center (IEA → dorsal penile artery, Michal II); AUA: conditional recommendation for young men with focal arterial occlusion and no generalized vascular disease or veno-occlusive dysfunction, with long-term success not well established<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | ICI / PDE5i bridge during workup | Revascularization for atherosclerotic ED (poor outcomes) |

Timing of rehabilitation after prostatectomy has been compared in one nonrandomized cohort. Among men with fully functional preoperative erections who had bilateral nerve-sparing radical prostatectomy and committed to pharmacological rehabilitation (sildenafil, then intracavernosal injection if needed), 48 who started before 6 months (mean 2 months) had a higher IIEF erectile-function domain score at 2 years than 36 who started at 6 months or later (mean 7 months; 22 vs 16, p &lt; 0.001), with more unassisted (58% vs 30%) and sildenafil-assisted (86% vs 45%) functional erections. The groups were matched for age, comorbidity and baseline erectile function, but treatment timing was not randomized.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

### Stepwise Treatment Ladder

| Step | Tier | Expected Efficacy | When to Advance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | Lifestyle modification + risk-factor optimization (weight loss, exercise, smoking cessation, medication review, treat hypogonadism) | Small average improvement; individual lifestyle trials in the AUA review report IIEF gains of roughly 1–4 points, larger in a subset of men<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Insufficient response, or patient desires faster improvement |
| **1** | **PDE5 inhibitor** monotherapy at maximum tolerated dose | **60–70%** successful intercourse<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | Failure after dose titration + counseling |
| **1B** | **Combination therapy** — PDE5i + testosterone (if hypogonadal), PDE5i + VED, agent switch, daily tadalafil | Mean IIEF +1.8 over monotherapy in a *JAMA Netw Open* meta-analysis of 44 studies (3,853 men); add-on VED, Li-ESWT and daily tadalafil each rest on single studies<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | Failure of optimized combination |
| **2A** | **Intracavernosal injection** (alprostadil, bimix, or trimix) | Intracavernosal alprostadil produced an optimal erectile response for sexual satisfaction in &gt;70% and increased rigidity scores in 90%<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>; one randomized comparison in men with ED for &gt;6 months reported 50% efficacy with trimix versus 22% with alprostadil monotherapy<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>. A 2025 meta-analysis found no consistent adverse-effect advantage for combined over single-agent injections<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | Failure, intolerance, &gt;50% dropout, or patient preference for definitive solution |
| **2B** | **Intraurethral alprostadil (MUSE)** or **vacuum erection device** | MUSE: successful intercourse at home in 64.9% of 1,511 men and 69% of 249 men with organic ED<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>; VED: published efficacy for erections satisfactory for intercourse was as high as 90%<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | Insufficient response or dissatisfaction |
| **2.5** | **Regenerative therapy** — [Li-ESWT](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/li-eswt) (best evidence), PRP (emerging) | Li-ESWT mean IIEF +2.0 (95% CI 0.99–3.00) in a 2017 meta-analysis of 14 studies<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>; the 2025 Cochrane review (21 sham-controlled RCTs) found a short-term IIEF-EF gain of about 3.9 points, below the 4-point minimal important difference, with low-certainty evidence and no adverse-event signal<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>. AUA 2018 classes Li-ESWT as investigational (Conditional Recommendation, Grade C), stem-cell therapy as investigational and PRP as experimental<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | Insufficient response → Step 3 |
| **3** | **Penile prosthesis implantation** (3-piece IPP preferred) | An ICSM-cited AMS 700 CX series reported 97% patient and 92% partner satisfaction; EAU 2026 cites 92–100% of patients and 91–95% of partners across prosthesis implantation<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | Definitive endpoint; irreversible |

MUSE remains a guideline option, but FDA now lists every MUSE strength as discontinued in the United States; see [intraurethral alprostadil](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/intraurethral-alprostadil) before planning it.

### IPP Device-Selection Sub-Comparison

| Feature | 3-Piece IPP | 2-Piece IPP | Malleable (Semi-Rigid) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Components** | Two cylinders + scrotal pump + retropubic (or ectopic) reservoir | Two corporal cylinders + scrotal pump; fluid storage varies by model (see [implant models](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/penile-implants/implant-models))<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | Two bendable rods |
| **Naturalness** | Most natural erection / flaccidity — gold standard | Intermediate rigidity; limited fluid volume | Always semi-rigid — concealment difficult |
| **Mechanical survival** | Five-year mechanical survival varied by three-piece model in ICSM-cited series (AMS 700 CX 85%, Ultrex 74%, Mentor Alpha-1 90%, Alpha NB 94%)<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>; EAU 2026 cites &lt;5% failure at five years for modified three-piece models, while pooled implant survival across models was 87.2% at five years and 76.8% at ten years<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | Limited Ambicor data: 94–100% mechanical survival in three older series (mean follow-up 29–43 months)<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | Highest — no hydraulic components |
| **Surgical complexity** | Most complex (reservoir placement) | Avoids retropubic reservoir | Simplest |
| **Cost** | Highest | Intermediate | Lowest |
| **Ideal candidate** | Standard candidate with adequate dexterity, no reservoir contraindication | Prior pelvic / abdominal surgery making reservoir placement risky | Limited dexterity; neophallus; cost constraints; salvage after infection |

### Prosthesis Infection Salvage — Length-Preservation Hierarchy

| Strategy | Technique | Infection-Free Rate | Length Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Immediate IPP salvage (Mulcahy)** | Historical Mulcahy seven-solution washout after complete explantation, followed by same-session IPP reimplantation; contemporary irrigants and sequences vary<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | **82%** long-term | Successful immediate salvage cohort (not IPP-specific): mean **0.6 cm** total corporeal length loss<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| **Immediate malleable salvage** | Mulcahy washout + malleable prosthesis; 17/54 infection-free patients (31%) later underwent conversion to IPP at a mean of 6.7 mo<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | **93%** multi-institution (54/58); 70.5% infection-free overall in 76 VA salvage patients (61 malleable, 15 IPP); malleable-specific rate not reported (reinfection 45.5% with diabetes versus 20.5% without)<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> | Preserves length |
| **Extracapsular salvage** | Explant infected or eroded device, wash capsule, reimplant in extracapsular sinusoidal space<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> | **94.4%** infection-free (17/18 with a developable plane, from 20 cases; follow-up 7–38 months) | Bypasses contaminated capsule |
| **Delayed reimplantation** | Explant, then reimplant after infection resolution; a 3–6-month delay has been described<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> | Standard | **Mean 3.7 cm corporeal length loss** in the delayed-reimplantation cohort, irreversible<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |

The Lopategui 2018 finding is the operational anchor: delayed reimplantation costs about 3.7 cm of corporeal length, regardless of time-to-reimplant interval, while immediate salvage costs about 0.6 cm. In medically suitable patients, discuss immediate salvage and its alternatives.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> For coating-specific irrigation evidence, see the [implant-infection article](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/penile-implants/infection).
