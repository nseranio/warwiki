# Intracavernosal Injections (ICI)

**Intracavernosal injection (ICI)** of vasoactive agents into the corpus cavernosum produces erection independent of neurogenic stimulation. It is an option for men with contraindications to PDE5 inhibitors, men who prefer not to take an oral drug and men in whom PDE5 inhibitors are ineffective, including men after prostatectomy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Alprostadil (prostaglandin E1) monotherapy and TriMix (papaverine, phentolamine, alprostadil) are the most commonly used formulations. Alprostadil is the only agent FDA-approved for ICI in the US; combinations are compounded.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

This atlas page is the practical self-injection / sexual-medicine workflow companion to the full [Intracavernosal Injection Agents](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/intracavernosal-injection-agents) pharmacology hub.

## Indications

| Indication | Why ICI is useful |
|---|---|
| PDE5i failure or contraindication | Direct corporal smooth-muscle pharmacology bypasses impaired NO signaling<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Post-prostatectomy ED | Acts directly on the corpora, so it does not depend on intact cavernous nerve signaling<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Severe diabetes / vascular ED | Response rates were lower in diabetic and pelvic-radiation patients in a large tertiary series, but most TriMix users still achieved intercourse<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Diagnostic penile duplex Doppler ultrasound | Produces pharmacologic erection for arterial inflow and veno-occlusive assessment |
| Prolonged erection after ICI | Intracavernosal phenylephrine is the rescue drug and not an ED induction agent<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

ICI differs from oral therapy in its risks. Needle technique, priapism rescue planning, fibrosis surveillance and adherence support matter as much as the prescription.

## Agents & Formulations

| Formulation | Components | Practical role |
|---|---|---|
| Alprostadil | PGE1 | FDA-approved monotherapy; penile pain with erection can limit use<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| BiMix | Papaverine + phentolamine | Compounded combination |
| TriMix | Alprostadil + papaverine + phentolamine | Most patients in a 1,412-man tertiary cohort used it; 89% of TriMix users could have intercourse<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| QuadMix | TriMix + atropine | Used after failure of three-drug combinations in a stepped-care program<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Phenylephrine | Alpha-1 agonist | Priapism / prolonged-erection rescue, dosed and monitored separately<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

Combination therapy was developed to use lower doses of each component, aiming to improve efficacy and reduce adverse effects.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> No combination is FDA-approved, concentrations vary widely, and some components (alprostadil) have limited shelf life. A commonly used compounded mixture is 30 mg papaverine, 10 µg alprostadil and 1 mg phentolamine per mL, with a starting dose of 0.1–0.5 mL.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Technique Overview

1. Before alprostadil use, screen for labeled contraindications: hypersensitivity; sickle cell disease or trait, leukemia or multiple myeloma; penile deformity, cavernosal fibrosis or Peyronie’s disease; and a penile implant. Assess whether cardiovascular status permits sexual activity. Perform the first injection in the office with dose titration and detumescence monitoring.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
2. Use the specified device and needle for the formulation. For Caverject, use a ½-inch, 29- or 30-gauge needle and inject laterally into the proximal third of the corpus cavernosum; avoid visible veins and do not bend or straighten the needle.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
3. Avoid the dorsal neurovascular bundle and ventral urethra.
4. Compress the puncture site with an alcohol swab or sterile gauze for five minutes or until bleeding stops, especially in anticoagulated patients.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
5. Alternate sides and injection sites with each dose. Document any pre-existing fibrosis, plaque or deformity before starting, and monitor for progression.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
6. Limit frequency to no more than 3 injections per week with at least 24 hours between injections, as in alprostadil labeling.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
7. Give written priapism instructions. Patients who receive ICI teaching or an in-office test are told to return to the office or emergency department for an erection lasting more than 4 hours (AUA/SMSNA Statement 22, Expert Opinion).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The AUA ED guideline describes commonly used self-measures (attempting ejaculation, oral pseudoephedrine, ice) for which no evidence was retrieved, and advises presenting within 2–4 hours of injection if a painful, non-bendable erection persists.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> AUA/SMSNA permits clinician-selected conservative measures for prolonged erections of 1–4 hours, but these must not delay treatment of a fully rigid erection beyond four hours. Pain alone does not determine the need for intervention. When a post-ICI erection of four hours or less requires treatment, intracavernosal phenylephrine is the initial option (Statement 20, Expert Opinion); it is also advised when conservative management fails (Statement 21, Moderate Recommendation, Grade C).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Outcomes & Evidence

Persistence is the limiting outcome. The AUA ED guideline advises informing men with ED of ICI as a treatment option (Moderate Recommendation, Grade C), does not make oral-drug failure a prerequisite, and advises an in-office injection test before home self-injection (Clinical Principle).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The 2026 EAU guideline recommends ICI as an alternative first-line therapy for well-informed patients or as second-line therapy (Strong).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> More than 90% of the study arms behind the guideline were observational. In a tertiary-center series of 1,412 men (2002-2006 enrollment), 89% of TriMix users could have intercourse, priapism occurred in 0.5%, and discontinuation was significant by 3 years, in part because some men after prostatectomy recovered natural or PDE5i-assisted erections.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Across studies the guideline reports the lowest mean priapism rate (1.8%) with single-agent alprostadil, though prolonged or painful erections occurred in a mean 6.3% of those studies.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The complications to actively prevent are:

- prolonged erection or ischemic priapism,
- penile pain, especially with alprostadil,
- corporal fibrosis / curvature,
- hematoma, especially with anticoagulation,
- dropout from anxiety, cost, or inadequate coaching.

Education before the first home injection, partner involvement when appropriate and early follow-up are practical measures to limit dropout; the AUA guideline notes that the office visit builds confidence with the technique and supports adherence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
