# Erectile Dysfunction

This section covers the operative half of the male sexual-dysfunction toolkit. The landing defaults to **erectile dysfunction (ED)** — the stepwise framework from lifestyle modification through PDE5 inhibitors, intracavernosal injection, vacuum erection device, intraurethral alprostadil, low-intensity shockwave / regenerative therapy, and penile prosthesis implantation, including infection salvage. **Peyronie's disease** lives in its own subsection because the reconstructive question (straighten, lengthen, or prosthetically support the penis) is anatomically distinct from the medical-management question that drives ED treatment selection.

The medical-therapy hubs (PDE5 inhibitors, ICI agents, MUSE, testosterone replacement, androgen adjuncts, Peyronie's plaque-modifying agents, priapism management) live in [Pharmacology — Sexual Medicine & Andrology](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology). Penile-revascularization technique lives at [Foundations — Saphenous Vein Graft](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/saphenous-vein).

## Master Decision Framework

ED management follows a **stepwise, patient-centered algorithm** endorsed by the **AUA 2018 Erectile Dysfunction Guideline** and the **EAU 2025 Male Sexual & Reproductive Health Update**, progressing from least to most invasive with treatment selection guided by severity, etiology, comorbidities, and patient/partner preference.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> The EAU 2025 update emphasizes **personalized treatment** — patients should be fully counseled on every available modality and choose based on invasiveness, tolerability, effectiveness, and personal expectations.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> **Combination therapy** is superior to monotherapy across multiple PDE5i-based combinations and should be attempted before advancing to prosthesis.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> For prosthesis infection, **immediate Mulcahy salvage preserves penile length** (mean 0.6 cm loss vs 3.7 cm with delayed reimplantation).<sup>[[32]](#ref32)[[35]](#ref35)</sup>

ED is a clinical diagnosis. Minimum evaluation: complete sexual / medical / psychosocial history, validated questionnaires (SHIM/IIEF-5, EHS), focused physical examination, and selective laboratory testing including a **morning serum total testosterone in every man with ED** (Moderate Recommendation; Grade C).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> ED is an **independent risk marker for cardiovascular disease** — over two-thirds of men with coronary artery disease report ED before any coronary symptoms — so cardiovascular risk stratification (Princeton III) is part of every initial encounter.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### Treatment Selection by Clinical Scenario

| Clinical Scenario | First-Line | Alternative(s) | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Newly diagnosed ED, no contraindications** | Lifestyle modification + [PDE5 inhibitor](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/pde5-inhibitors) (sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil, or avanafil), titrated to maximum dose with ≥6 properly executed attempts | Daily tadalafil 2.5–5 mg if concurrent BPH/LUTS or preference for spontaneity | Skipping cardiovascular risk assessment; declaring failure before maximum-dose trial |
| **Confirmed hypogonadism (morning total T &lt;300 ng/dL) + ED** | PDE5i + [testosterone replacement](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/testosterone-replacement) | PDE5i monotherapy (often suboptimal in hypogonadal men) | Testosterone monotherapy without PDE5i |
| **PDE5i non-responder after ≥6 max-dose attempts** | Combination therapy: switch PDE5i agent → PDE5i + [VED](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/ved) → daily tadalafil → PDE5i + intraurethral alprostadil | [Intracavernosal injection (ICI)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/intracavernosal-injections) | Premature progression to prosthesis without optimization |
| **PDE5i failure / intolerance, prefers pharmacotherapy** | [ICI](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/intracavernosal-injections) (alprostadil → bimix → trimix), in-office training and home dose titration | [MUSE](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/muse-intraurethral) (intraurethral alprostadil) | Self-titration without supervised first dose (priapism risk) |
| **Prefers non-pharmacologic option** | [Vacuum erection device](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/ved) | MUSE | Restrictive ring &gt;30 minutes (ischemia risk) |
| **Concurrent nitrate use (any formulation)** | VED, ICI (alprostadil monotherapy) | MUSE | **All PDE5 inhibitors (absolute contraindication)** |
| **Post-radical prostatectomy ED (early, &lt;12–24 months)** | Penile rehabilitation: PDE5i ± VED ± ICI | Daily tadalafil | "Watch and wait" without rehabilitation |
| **Refractory to optimized conservative + medical therapy, or patient preference** | [3-piece IPP](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/penile-implants/implant-models) (AMS 700 series or Coloplast Titan) | 2-piece IPP (prior pelvic surgery, reservoir contraindication); malleable (limited dexterity, neophallus, salvage, cost-constrained) | Continued failed pharmacotherapy without device counseling |
| **Peyronie's disease + ED unresponsive to medical therapy** | [IPP with adjunctive straightening](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease/prosthesis-with-straightening) (Wilson manual modeling → tunical plication → plaque incision/grafting as needed) | (See [Peyronie's algorithm](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease)) | IPP alone without addressing curvature |
| **Severe corporal fibrosis (post-priapism, post-explant)** | Malleable prosthesis at high-volume center | Staged dilation → 3-piece IPP | Standard 3-piece in densely fibrosed corpora without dilation strategy |
| **Acute prosthesis infection** | **Immediate Mulcahy salvage** (washout + same-session reimplant — IPP or malleable) | [Extracapsular reimplantation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/penile-implants/infection) (mature capsule) | **Delayed reimplantation** (mean 3.7 cm corporal length loss vs 0.6 cm with immediate salvage) |
| **Young man with traumatic arterial injury (no atherosclerosis)** | Penile arterial revascularization at high-volume center | ICI / PDE5i bridge during workup | Revascularization for atherosclerotic ED (poor outcomes) |

### Stepwise Treatment Ladder

| Step | Tier | Expected Efficacy | When to Advance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | Lifestyle modification + risk-factor optimization (weight loss, exercise, smoking cessation, medication review, treat hypogonadism) | IIEF improvement of 3–5 points with structured programs<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Insufficient response after adequate trial, or patient desires faster improvement |
| **1** | **PDE5 inhibitor** monotherapy at maximum tolerated dose | **60–70%** successful intercourse<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup> | Failure after dose titration + counseling on proper use |
| **1B** | **Combination therapy** — PDE5i + testosterone (if hypogonadal), PDE5i + VED, PDE5i agent switch, daily tadalafil | Superior to monotherapy across IIEF in JAMA Network Open meta of 59 RCTs<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | Failure of optimized combination |
| **2A** | **Intracavernosal injection** (alprostadil, bimix, or trimix) | Up to **70%** with alprostadil; up to **90%** with trimix<sup>[[20]](#ref20)[[21]](#ref21)</sup> | Failure, intolerance, &gt;50% dropout, or patient preference for definitive solution |
| **2B** | **Intraurethral alprostadil (MUSE)** or **vacuum erection device** | MUSE 43–69%; VED 60–80%<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)</sup> | Insufficient response or dissatisfaction |
| **2.5** | **Regenerative therapy** — Li-ESWT (best evidence), PRP (emerging) — as adjunct or in PDE5i partial responders | Li-ESWT mean IIEF +2.0 (95% CI 0.99–3.00, p &lt; 0.001) in meta of 14 studies<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>; investigational, not yet guideline-recommended as standalone<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[22]](#ref22)</sup> | Insufficient response → Step 3 |
| **3** | **Penile prosthesis implantation** (3-piece IPP preferred) | **70–90%** patient and partner satisfaction — among the highest of any prosthetic device in medicine<sup>[[28]](#ref28)[[29]](#ref29)</sup> | Definitive endpoint; irreversible |

### IPP Device-Selection Sub-Comparison

| Feature | 3-Piece IPP | 2-Piece IPP | Malleable (Semi-Rigid) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Components** | Two cylinders + scrotal pump + retropubic (or ectopic) reservoir | Two cylinders + combined pump-reservoir in scrotum | Two bendable rods |
| **Naturalness** | Most natural erection / flaccidity — gold standard | Intermediate rigidity; limited fluid volume | Always semi-rigid — concealment difficult |
| **Mechanical survival** | &gt;90% at 5 years with modern devices<sup>[[28]](#ref28)[[30]](#ref30)</sup> | &gt;90% at 5 years | Highest — no hydraulic components |
| **Surgical complexity** | Most complex (reservoir placement) | Avoids retropubic reservoir | Simplest |
| **Cost** | Highest | Intermediate | Lowest |
| **Ideal candidate** | Standard candidate with adequate dexterity and no reservoir contraindication | Prior pelvic / abdominal surgery making reservoir placement risky | Limited dexterity; neophallus; cost constraints; salvage after infection |

### Prosthesis Infection Salvage — Length Preservation Hierarchy

| Strategy | Technique | Infection-Free Rate | Length Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Immediate IPP salvage (Mulcahy)** | 7-solution antiseptic / antibiotic washout + same-session IPP reimplantation<sup>[[32]](#ref32)[[33]](#ref33)</sup> | **82%** long-term | Mean **0.6 cm** loss<sup>[[35]](#ref35)</sup> |
| **Immediate malleable salvage** | Mulcahy washout + malleable prosthesis; later elective conversion to IPP at ~6.7 months in ~31%<sup>[[36]](#ref36)</sup> | **93%** in single-center series; **70.5%** in 2026 VA national n = 76 (45.5% reinfection in diabetics)<sup>[[37]](#ref37)</sup> | Preserves length |
| **Extracapsular salvage** | Explant infected device, wash capsule, implant in extracapsular sinusoidal space<sup>[[34]](#ref34)</sup> | **94.4%** (n = 18) | Bypasses contaminated capsule |
| **Delayed reimplantation** | Explant, wait 3–6 months, reimplant | Standard | **Mean 3.7 cm corporeal length loss**, irreversible<sup>[[35]](#ref35)</sup> |

The Lopategui 2018 finding is the operational anchor: **delayed reimplantation costs ~3.7 cm of corporeal length, regardless of time-to-reimplant interval, while immediate salvage costs ~0.6 cm**. When the patient is medically suitable, salvage should be offered at presentation rather than deferred.<sup>[[35]](#ref35)</sup>

## Epidemiology

ED is defined as the **persistent inability to attain and maintain an erection sufficient to permit satisfactory sexual performance**. It affects an estimated **52% of men aged 40–70** at all severities, with prevalence rising sharply from ~12% at 40 to ~50% at 70.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> ED reduces quality of life for the patient and partner and is an **independent risk marker for cardiovascular disease** — more than two-thirds of patients with coronary artery disease report ED symptoms before any coronary syndrome.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Pathophysiology and Etiologic Classification

Normal erection requires coordinated function of **psychogenic, endocrine, vascular, and neurogenic systems**. Nitric oxide (NO) released from parasympathetic non-adrenergic non-cholinergic neurons and corporal endothelium activates guanylyl cyclase, raising cGMP, which relaxes cavernosal smooth muscle. Sinusoidal expansion increases arterial inflow and compresses subtunical venules against the tunica albuginea (the corporal veno-occlusive mechanism), producing rigidity. ED arises from disruption at any node in this pathway.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

| Etiology | Mechanism | Common Causes | Prevalence Among ED Patients |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vasculogenic** | Impaired arterial inflow (atherosclerosis) and/or veno-occlusive failure | Atherosclerosis, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, diabetes, smoking, metabolic syndrome | Most common — ~70% of organic ED<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Neurogenic** | Disrupted central or peripheral neural pathways | Spinal cord injury, multiple sclerosis, Parkinson's disease, radical pelvic surgery (especially radical prostatectomy), diabetic neuropathy | 10–19%<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Endocrine / hormonal** | Testosterone deficiency, hyperprolactinemia, thyroid dysfunction | Hypogonadism (morning total T &lt;300 ng/dL), pituitary adenoma, diabetes mellitus | Hypogonadism in 5–35%<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Psychogenic** | Performance anxiety, depression, relationship conflict, stress | Primary psychogenic or secondary to organic ED | 10–25% as primary; mixed in the majority<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Drug-induced** | Medication side effect | Antihypertensives (β-blockers, thiazides), SSRIs/SNRIs, antiandrogens, opioids, alcohol, recreational drugs | Up to 25%<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Mixed** | Combination of organic + psychogenic | Most common clinical presentation | Majority of cases<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

## Comprehensive Evaluation

The **AUA 2018 Guideline** establishes the evaluation framework, refined by the **2018 Process of Care Model** and the **EAU 2025 Update**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

### Step 1 — Core evaluation (every patient)

Per AUA, every man with ED warrants a thorough medical, sexual, and psychosocial history, focused physical examination, and selective laboratory testing (Clinical Principle).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- **Sexual history** — onset (gradual vs sudden), severity, situational vs global, attain vs maintain, presence of nocturnal / morning / masturbatory erections (preserved nocturnal erections favor a psychogenic component), prior erectogenic-therapy use, partner factors.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Medical history** — cardiovascular risk factors (hypertension, diabetes, hyperlipidemia, smoking, obesity), prior pelvic surgery / radiation, neurological disease, medications (β-blockers, thiazides, SSRIs, antiandrogens), substance use, mood disorders.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Validated questionnaires** — **Sexual Health Inventory for Men (SHIM / IIEF-5)** and **Erection Hardness Score (EHS)** for severity, longitudinal tracking, and treatment-response measurement (Expert Opinion).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Physical examination** — vitals, genital exam (penile skin, urethral meatus, palpation for Peyronie's plaque from pubic bone to coronal sulcus), testicular size and consistency, secondary sexual characteristics, focused cardiovascular and neurological examination.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Laboratory testing** — **morning serum total testosterone in every man with ED** (Moderate Recommendation; Grade C); confirm low values with repeat measurement, free / bioavailable testosterone, LH, prolactin. Add fasting glucose / HbA1c, fasting lipid panel; PSA when age- and clinically appropriate.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

### Step 2 — Specialized testing (select patients)

| Test | What It Measures | Normal Values | Indications |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Penile Duplex Doppler Ultrasound (PDUS)** | Arterial inflow (PSV) and veno-occlusive function (EDV) after intracavernosal injection | PSV &gt;30 cm/s (normal arterial); EDV &lt;5 cm/s (normal venous) | Young men considering vascular surgery; Peyronie's-disease assessment; medicolegal cases; PDE5i non-responders to inform prognosis<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **Nocturnal penile tumescence (NPT)** | Erections during REM sleep | ≥3 episodes/night, ≥60% rigidity, ≥10 min duration | Differentiate organic vs psychogenic ED; medicolegal evaluation<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Intracavernosal injection test** | Erectile response to vasoactive agent | Full rigid erection within 10 min | Vascular-integrity screen; in-office training for ICI therapy<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Dynamic infusion cavernosometry / cavernosography (DICC)** | Corporal veno-occlusive function | Maintenance flow &lt;5 mL/min; pressure drop &lt;45 mmHg/30 sec | Young men with suspected congenital venous leak; pre-vascular-surgery workup<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Selective internal pudendal angiography** | Penile arterial anatomy | Patent pudendal and penile arteries | Young men with traumatic arterial injury being considered for revascularization<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

A key Memorial Sloan Kettering operating principle: PDUS accuracy is **entirely predicated on achieving complete cavernosal smooth-muscle relaxation**. A redosing protocol (titrate up to maximum dose of vasoactive agent) optimizes reliability; without full relaxation, false-positive arterial-insufficiency and venous-leak diagnoses are common.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

### Step 3 — Cardiovascular risk stratification

ED is a **risk marker for underlying CVD** and patients should be counseled accordingly (Clinical Principle).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The **Princeton III Consensus** stratifies patients into low-, intermediate-, and high-cardiac-risk categories before initiating sexual activity or pharmacotherapy:

- **Low risk** — asymptomatic, &lt;3 major CVD risk factors, controlled hypertension, mild stable angina, post-revascularization without ischemia, mild valvular disease, or NYHA Class I heart failure → can proceed with sexual activity and ED treatment.
- **Intermediate risk** — ≥3 CVD risk factors, moderate stable angina, recent MI (2–8 weeks ago without intervention), NYHA Class II heart failure, or non-cardiac atherosclerotic disease → defer sexual activity and ED treatment until cardiology evaluation.
- **High risk** — unstable or refractory angina, uncontrolled hypertension, NYHA Class III–IV heart failure, recent MI (&lt;2 weeks), high-risk arrhythmias, hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy, or moderate-to-severe valvular disease → cardiology stabilization before any sexual activity or ED pharmacotherapy.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### Step 4 — Psychosexual assessment

Referral to a **mental health professional** should be considered to promote treatment adherence, reduce performance anxiety, and integrate treatments into a sexual relationship (Moderate Recommendation; Grade C). Psychological factors may be primary drivers or secondary contributors and are present to some degree in the majority of patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Treatment Algorithm

### Tier 0 — Lifestyle modification and risk-factor optimization

Per AUA, men with ED who have comorbidities known to negatively affect erectile function should be counseled that **lifestyle modifications, including diet and increased physical activity, improve overall health and may improve erectile function** (Moderate Recommendation; Grade C).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Intervention | Evidence | Expected Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| **Weight loss** | RCTs of intensive lifestyle change show IIEF improvement in obese men with ED<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | Modest EF improvement; concurrent CVD risk reduction |
| **Aerobic exercise** | Meta-analyses show IIEF improvement of 3–5 points<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | Comparable to PDE5i in mild ED; augments pharmacotherapy |
| **Smoking cessation** | ED prevalence ~2× higher in smokers; cessation improves endothelial function<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | Gradual improvement over months |
| **Alcohol moderation** | Heavy use impairs EF; moderate use neutral<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Variable |
| **Medication review** | Switch β-blockers, thiazides, SSRIs to alternatives where possible<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | May resolve drug-induced ED |
| **Treat underlying conditions** | Optimize HbA1c, blood pressure, lipid panel<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | Reduces progression; augments treatment response |

### Tier 1 — First-line pharmacotherapy: PDE5 inhibitors

Per AUA, men with ED should be informed about **FDA-approved oral PDE5 inhibitors** unless contraindicated (Strong Recommendation; Grade B); instructions should be provided to maximize efficacy (Strong Recommendation; Grade C); and the dose should be titrated for optimal efficacy (Strong Recommendation; Grade B).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Agent | Typical Dose | Onset | Duration | Unique Features |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sildenafil (Viagra)** | 25–100 mg | 30–60 min | 4–6 hr | Most clinical experience; take on empty stomach (food delays absorption)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Tadalafil (Cialis)** | 10–20 mg on-demand; 2.5–5 mg daily | 30–60 min | Up to 36 hr | Daily dosing option; FDA-approved for BPH/LUTS; food-independent; longest duration<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Vardenafil (Levitra)** | 5–20 mg | 30–60 min | 4–6 hr | ODT formulation available (no water needed)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Avanafil (Stendra)** | 50–200 mg | 15–30 min | 6–12 hr | Fastest onset; most PDE5-selective; fewest visual side effects; can be taken with food<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

**Overall efficacy: 60–70%** — including diabetic, hypertensive, and SCI populations. Level 1 evidence shows **no significant differences in efficacy, safety, or tolerability** among the four agents.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

**Contraindications.** Concurrent nitrate use (absolute — risk of severe hypotension); inadequate cardiac reserve for sexual activity. Caution with α-blockers (orthostatic hypotension).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

**Counseling points to maximize efficacy.**

1. Adequate sexual stimulation is required — PDE5i do not cause spontaneous erections.
2. Sildenafil and vardenafil are taken on an empty stomach.
3. Allow adequate onset time (30–60 min for most; 15 min for avanafil).
4. Trial of **≥6 attempts at maximum tolerated dose** before declaring failure.
5. Switch between PDE5i agents before declaring class failure.
6. Daily tadalafil 2.5–5 mg may benefit men who prefer spontaneity or have concurrent BPH/LUTS.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

**Non-responders.** 30–35% of patients fail PDE5i; **30–50% of these are salvageable** with detailed counseling on proper use and physician follow-up to confirm an adequate clinical trial.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

### Tier 1B — Combination therapy for PDE5i suboptimal responders

A **JAMA Network Open systematic review and meta-analysis** of 59 RCTs and 8,000+ patients showed combination therapies were superior to monotherapy across multiple regimens.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

| Combination | Evidence | Clinical Utility |
|---|---|---|
| **PDE5i + testosterone** | Superior to PDE5i alone in hypogonadal men; TRT may restore PDE5i responsiveness<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[19]](#ref19)</sup> | First combination to try in men with confirmed hypogonadism (morning total T &lt;300 ng/dL) |
| **PDE5i + VED** | Additive effect — VED provides mechanical engorgement; PDE5i enhances vascular response<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | Useful in post-prostatectomy rehabilitation |
| **PDE5i + Li-ESWT** | Emerging evidence of synergistic benefit<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | May improve PDE5i responsiveness in mild–moderate vasculogenic ED |
| **PDE5i + intraurethral alprostadil** | Dual cGMP + cAMP pathway activation<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | Salvage in PDE5i non-responders |
| **PDE5i + psychosexual therapy** | Addresses both organic and psychogenic components<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | Recommended whenever psychological factors contribute |

### Tier 2 — Second-line therapies

#### 2A. Intracavernosal injection (ICI) therapy

The most effective non-surgical treatment. Per ICSM 2015, ICI with alprostadil is effective and well-tolerated and should be offered as **second-line therapy** (Level 1 evidence; Grade A for efficacy; Grade C for positioning).<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

| Agent | Composition | Efficacy | Key Adverse Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Alprostadil (Caverject)** | PGE1 | Up to 70% | Penile pain (37%); prolonged erection (4%); priapism (0.4%); penile fibrosis (3%)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[21]](#ref21)</sup> |
| **Trimix** | Alprostadil + papaverine + phentolamine | Up to 90% | Lower pain than alprostadil alone; combined-agent priapism risk higher (OR 1.84 vs single agent)<sup>[[20]](#ref20)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Bimix** | Papaverine + phentolamine | 60–80% | No penile pain; higher priapism risk than alprostadil alone<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |

Per the FDA Caverject label, the initial alprostadil dose is **2.5 mcg for vasculogenic / psychogenic / mixed etiology** and **1.25 mcg for pure neurogenic etiology**, titrated upward — no more than 3 injections per week with ≥24 hours between, and doses &gt;60 mcg are not recommended.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

**Dropout exceeds 50%** — primarily inconvenience, needle anxiety, and partner reluctance — making in-office training and structured patient education essential.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

#### 2B. Intraurethral alprostadil (MUSE)

Alprostadil 125–1000 mcg pellet inserted into the urethra via applicator. Success rates **43–69%** — lower than ICI but less invasive. Side effects: penile pain, urethral burning, hypotension, syncope (rare), priapism (rare).<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

#### 2C. Vacuum erection device (VED)

Negative pressure draws blood into the penis; a constriction ring at the base maintains the erection. Effective in **60–80%**, no systemic side effects. Limitations: cold / numb sensation, impaired ejaculation, petechiae, partner dissatisfaction with appearance. Particularly useful in post-prostatectomy rehabilitation and for patients preferring non-pharmacologic options.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

### Tier 2.5 — Emerging / regenerative therapies

**Low-intensity extracorporeal shockwave therapy (Li-ESWT).** A **2025 Cochrane systematic review** and the **2025 EAU update** both recognize growing evidence but note that high-quality long-term data are still lacking.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[22]](#ref22)</sup> A meta-analysis of 14 studies (n = 833) showed Li-ESWT significantly improved IIEF (mean difference 2.00, 95% CI 0.99–3.00, p &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> A Bayesian network meta-analysis of regenerative therapies estimated SMD vs control of **0.84 (95% CrI 0.49–1.2)**, with higher-energy protocols (0.15 mJ/mm²) producing larger effect (SMD 1.1).<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

**Platelet-rich plasma (PRP).** A meta-analysis of 12 controlled trials (n = 991) showed PRP improved IIEF vs placebo (SMD 0.59, 95% CI 0.34–0.84) with higher rates of minimal clinically important difference (RR 1.94). The EAU 2025 update concludes that **more conclusive evidence is needed** before routine clinical use.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)[[25]](#ref25)[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

**Stem-cell therapy.** The least mature of the regenerative therapies — network-meta SMD vs control 0.92 (95% CrI −0.49 to 2.3, not statistically significant). **Investigational only**.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

### Tier 3 — Penile prosthesis implantation

Per AUA, men with ED should be informed about **penile prosthesis implantation**, including benefits and risks (Strong Recommendation; Grade C). Prosthesis surgery should not be performed in the presence of systemic, cutaneous, or urinary tract infection.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Outcomes.**

- **Satisfaction 70–90%** for both patient and partner — among the highest of any prosthetic device in medicine.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[29]](#ref29)[[30]](#ref30)</sup>
- **Mechanical survival &gt;90% at 5 years** with modern devices. A tertiary-center series reported 88.4% functional at median 26 months (94.4% primary vs 57.1% secondary).<sup>[[30]](#ref30)[[31]](#ref31)</sup>
- **Infection rate 2–4%** — reduced by antibiotic-impregnated (InhibiZone) and hydrophilic (Titan) coatings.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[29]](#ref29)</sup>
- Prosthesis implantation does not interfere with urination, ejaculation, sensation, or orgasm.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup>

**Special populations.**

| Population | Key Considerations |
|---|---|
| **Post-radical prostatectomy** | Most common indication; altered pelvic anatomy; consider ectopic reservoir placement; concurrent AUS may be placed for dual ED + SUI; corporal fibrosis uncommon unless &gt;2 yr post-RP<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Peyronie's disease** | IPP addresses ED + curvature; adjunctive ladder = manual modeling → tunical plication → plaque incision/grafting; PROPPER &gt;80% satisfaction at 1–2 yr; depression decreased 19.3% → 10.5%<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Diabetes mellitus** | Infection risk 2–3× baseline; antibiotic / hydrophilic coatings reduce risk; optimize HbA1c preoperatively<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Corporal fibrosis** (post-priapism, post-explant) | Most technically demanding; may require cavernotomy, cylinder downsizing, or staged procedures; high-volume surgeon essential<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Neophallus** (post-phalloplasty) | No tunica albuginea; higher infection, erosion, and mechanical-failure rates; specialized expertise required; rear-tip extender often needed<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Spinal cord injury** | Higher erosion risk due to absent sensation; malleable may be preferred; meticulous sizing<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |

For the operative deep-dive, see [Penile Implants](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/penile-implants) (preoperative evaluation, intraoperative setup, surgical approaches, reservoir placement, models database, infection management, revision scenarios, complications).

### Tier 4 — Penile vascular surgery (highly selected patients)

| Procedure | Indication | Outcomes |
|---|---|---|
| **Penile arterial revascularization** | **Traumatic** penile arterial injury in men &lt;40 yr with documented arterial occlusion on angiography; **no atherosclerotic disease** | Potentially curative — 60–70% long-term success in properly selected patients<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |

## ED in Special Clinical Contexts

### Post-radical prostatectomy ED

ED after radical prostatectomy reflects **cavernous-nerve injury** at surgery. Even with bilateral nerve-sparing technique, recovery of spontaneous erections may take 12–24 months. Penile-rehabilitation protocols (early PDE5i ± VED ± ICI) aim to preserve corporal smooth muscle and prevent fibrosis during this window, though the rehabilitation evidence base remains limited.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[38]](#ref38)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### Diabetic ED

Diabetes drives ED through **multiple converging pathways** — macro- and microvascular disease, autonomic and peripheral neuropathy, hypogonadism, and local factors (Peyronie's, balanitis). PDE5i efficacy is lower (50–60% vs 60–70% overall). Optimization of HbA1c, treatment of hypogonadism, and combination therapy improve outcomes.<sup>[[39]](#ref39)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### Neurogenic ED (spinal cord injury, multiple sclerosis)

ICI with alprostadil achieves **80–93%** success in neurogenic ED. PDE5i are effective in many men with incomplete lesions. Initial ICI dose is **lower (1.25 mcg alprostadil)** because of heightened sensitivity and higher priapism risk; VED is also effective and well-tolerated.<sup>[[40]](#ref40)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

## Key Takeaways

ED management follows a **stepwise algorithm** endorsed by the AUA 2018 and EAU 2025 guidelines, progressing from lifestyle modification through PDE5 inhibitors (60–70% efficacy), intracavernosal injection (up to 90% with trimix), and ultimately penile prosthesis implantation (70–90% satisfaction).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[16]](#ref16)[[2]](#ref2)[[29]](#ref29)</sup> The **EAU 2025 update** emphasizes personalized treatment with full counseling on every available modality.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> **Combination therapy** is superior to monotherapy and should be optimized before advancing to prosthesis.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> Among regenerative therapies, **Li-ESWT has the strongest evidence** but still lacks high-quality long-term data and guideline endorsement as standalone therapy.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[22]](#ref22)[[23]](#ref23)[[24]](#ref24)</sup> For prosthesis infection, **immediate Mulcahy salvage preserves penile length** (0.6 cm loss vs 3.7 cm with delayed reimplantation) and should be offered whenever the patient is medically suitable.<sup>[[35]](#ref35)[[33]](#ref33)</sup> Cardiovascular risk stratification belongs in every ED encounter — the symptom may be the **first manifestation of systemic vascular disease**.
