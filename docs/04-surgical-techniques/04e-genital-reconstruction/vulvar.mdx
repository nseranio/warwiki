# Vulvar Reconstruction

This page hosts the Decision Framework and Treatment Database for **non-gender-affirming vulvar reconstruction** — vulvar cancer, extramammary Paget disease (EMPD), VIN, lichen sclerosus / lichen planus sequelae, FGM/C reconstruction, trauma, vulvar hidradenitis suppurativa, and GSM-related fat-grafting. **Feminizing gender-affirming vulvoplasty / vaginoplasty** lives at [04k Gender-Affirming Surgery](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery).

---

## Decision Framework

Vulvar reconstruction restores form and function of a **composite three-dimensional structure** — labia majora and minora, clitoral hood / clitoris, vestibule, posterior fourchette and perineal body — while protecting pudendal blood supply and sensation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Indications include defects after vulvar cancer, extramammary Paget disease or VIN excision; selected refractory sequelae of lichen sclerosus or lichen planus; FGM/C; trauma; infection; and hidradenitis suppurativa. WHO estimates that **more than 230 million** girls and women alive today have undergone FGM/C.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> Höckel and Dornhöfer describe neoplastic defect classes; Ricotta proposes a location-based Toulouse algorithm; Han and Caretto describe other selected reconstructive pathways. These are complementary series and author algorithms, not universal flap assignments.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)[[7]](#ref7)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> Kwong's prospective **69-woman/136-flap mixed-condition cohort** reports postoperative wound and patient-reported outcomes, but cannot establish flap superiority to primary closure.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> FGM/C operations and other indications require their own counseling and evidence appraisal.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

### Assess Clinical Context

| Variable | Sub-Categories |
|---|---|
| **Etiology** | Vulvar cancer / EMPD / VIN (occult invasion in 4/13 of one selected skinning-vulvectomy series<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>) / lichen sclerosus or lichen planus sequelae / FGM/C / trauma / Fournier's gangrene / vulvar HS. Fat grafting for GSM-related symptoms is investigational, not an established reconstructive first-line indication. |
| **Defect characteristics** | Location (anterior commissure / lateral hemivulva / posterior / total) — depth (superficial vs deep) — size — adjacent-structure involvement (vagina / urethra / anus / groin / mons pubis) |
| **Primary vs secondary reconstruction** | Prior surgery / flaps used; prior radiation; recurrent disease |
| **Patient factors** | Age, BMI, smoking, diabetes, radiation history, comorbidities, fertility goals (preservation if oncologic) |
| **Functional goals** | Sexual function, continence, ambulation and body image; in Kwong's selected questionnaire subset, **40/45** respondents said reconstruction helped acceptance of their diagnosis and surgery<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |

### Determine If Reconstruction Is Needed

Consider reconstruction when primary approximation would impose excessive tension, distort the urethra or introitus, leave dead space or compromise function. Coordinate cancer extent and treatment with the disease-specific pathway; the listed NCCN resource does not itself validate a universal flap ranking.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> These are individualized surgical judgments, not a guarantee of better cosmesis or function. Kwong's prospective cohort reported none-to-mild complications for **126/136 flaps at day 7** and **113/136 at days 8–30**; it did **not** compare flaps with primary closure or show that every flap was healed by day 30.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### Technique Selection by Defect Type

The options below summarize published techniques by defect pattern; **they are not a ranked guideline or an automatic indication**. Match available vascular territory, depth, radiation, oncologic margins and patient goals in a reconstructive team.

| Defect Type | Reported options to consider | Other options | Key constraint |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Small, anterior or posterior commissure** | **Limberg (rhomboid) flap** or primary closure when laxity allows | Anterior- or posterior-based labial flap | Closure under tension at the posterior fourchette (introital stenosis) |
| **Moderate, lateral (hemivulvectomy)** | Consider **V-Y medial-thigh**, **lotus petal (LPF)** or another nearby flap according to reach and vascularity | [Internal pudendal-artery perforator (IPAP)](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/ipap) flap in selected defects; Han's approach is a cohort-specific algorithm | A flap whose pedicle or donor area is compromised by resection or prior treatment |
| **Moderate, posterior** | **Gluteal V-Y flap** or **lotus petal (lower-petal variant)** with scar in gluteal fold | Genito-crural island perforator flap in selected defects (Commenge 2025 n = 27 / 46 flaps)<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> | Posterior closure under tension |
| **Total vulvectomy** | **Bilateral V-Y + bilateral LPF** combinations; **pubolabial V-Y flap** can reconstruct the entire vulva with a single flap | Combined flap algorithms per Höckel-Dornhöfer 11-procedure taxonomy | Pubolabial V-Y when groin dissection has extended medially |
| **Extended with inguinal involvement** | **Pedicled ALT flap** in selected extended defects, as described by Gentileschi, especially when inguinal or previously treated tissue is involved<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> | Tensor fasciae latae (TFL) flap when donor territory and reach fit | Mons pubis flap when groin dissection has compromised the superficial external pudendal artery |
| **Extended, posterior (perianal / gluteal)** | **Gluteal-thigh flap** (inferior gluteal artery; ideal for posterior orientation) | Gracilis myocutaneous flap | VRAM when posterior orientation is dominant |
| **Extended, anterior (suprapubic)** | **VRAM** (large pelvic dead-space filling) or **pedicled DIEP** (rectus-sparing alternative) | ALT flap | VRAM in patient at high abdominal-hernia risk; consider DIEP |
| **Vulvar + vaginal (Negosanti Type II)** | **Pedicled DIEP flap** (Negosanti 2015 algorithm — Type II requires tissue volume) | VRAM; tubularized gracilis or ALT for neovagina | Skin grafts alone for combined vulvovaginal defects |
| **Superficial (VIN / Paget — skinning vulvectomy)** | **STSG** (scalp / thigh / buttock donor) permits histologic examination; occult invasion was 4/13 in Lavoué's selected cohort<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | FTSG from groin crease in small selected defects; historical skinning-vulvectomy experience provides context<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | STSG when deep defect requires bulk |
| **Secondary reconstruction (recurrence)** | Choice driven by available unused flaps + radiation field — Caretto 2023 algorithm: ALT, DIEP, V-Y, free flap as needed | Free flap when all pedicled options exhausted | Re-using already-radiated flap territory |
| **FGM/C clitoral / vulvovestibular reconstruction** | Offer individualized multidisciplinary assessment; selected patients may consider clitoral reconstruction after informed counseling<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> | Foldès-style reconstruction, selected aOAP/hood variants or Mañero vaginal-mucosal graft, each with different source populations<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[21]](#ref21)</sup> | Automatic surgery or promised sensory/sexual outcomes |
| **Lichen sclerosus with introital stenosis** | Optimize disease control; consider **perineoplasty / de-adhesion** for persistent functional stenosis despite adequate topical treatment<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | Selected local flap or aOAP for complex refractory sequelae; fat grafting remains investigational | Operating on uncontrolled active disease; applying one cohort's relapse percentage to all procedures |
| **GSM / vulvar atrophy** | Evidence-based nonsurgical management and individualized assessment | Fat grafting or PRP only as an investigational adjunct in selected settings | Presenting regenerative surgery as established first-line care |

### Perforator vs Musculocutaneous Flap Sub-Comparison

| Feature | Perforator / Fasciocutaneous (LPF / V-Y / IPAP / ALT-perforator / Genito-Crural) | Musculocutaneous (VRAM / Gracilis) |
|---|---|---|
| **Donor-site morbidity** | Varies by flap and harvest | Eseme's proportional meta-analysis reported 57.6% for VRAM versus 16.0% for gracilis across different oncologic series; this is not a randomized head-to-head comparison<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> |
| **Tissue bulk for pelvic dead space** | Insufficient for large pelvic defects | Excellent — VRAM, gracilis with muscle |
| **Irradiated recipient bed** | Depends on the pedicle, radiation field and remaining tissue | Vascularized bulk may help selected deep defects; no universal superiority is established |
| **Sensation preservation** | Depends on flap design and nerve preservation; not automatically sensate | Depends on design and nerve preservation; not automatically insensate |
| **Aesthetic outcome** | Often thinner and locally matched, with case-specific tradeoffs | May provide necessary volume but require contour adjustment |
| **Surgical complexity** | Depends on perforator dissection and defect | Depends on harvest, transfer and donor closure |
| **Evidence context** | Small, heterogeneous location-specific series and author algorithms; do not infer a universal perforator-first rule<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[7]](#ref7)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> | Proportional meta-analysis and selected observational cohorts; different defects and treatment histories limit cross-flap rankings<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[20]](#ref20)</sup> |

### Special Decision Branch — Clitoral / Vulvovestibular Reconstruction Post-FGM/C

The extent of clitoral injury varies by FGM/C type and individual anatomy. A residual clitoral body may be accessible beneath scar, but its presence, neurovascular integrity and potential function must be assessed rather than assumed.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

| Step | Operation | Anchor |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Assess scar and remnant anatomy; a Foldès-style approach releases scar and exposes the clitoral remnant while protecting dorsal neurovascular structures | Foldès original technique; 38/40 studies in Almadori's scoping review involved clitoral reconstruction, mostly Foldès-style variants — not 95% of patients<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[24]](#ref24)</sup> |
| **2** | Selected suspensory-ligament release and downward mobilization, avoiding neurovascular injury | Foldès and variant-specific technique descriptions<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[24]](#ref24)</sup> |
| **3** | Fixation and any projection target depend on the chosen variant; **bulbocavernosus anchoring and ≥ 5-mm projection are O'Dey-specific teaching, not Foldès requirements** | O'Dey 2024 PRS<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **4** | Optional **Omega-Domed (OD) preputial flap** for clitoral hood reconstruction (85% of O'Dey series) | O'Dey 2024 PRS<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **5** | Optional **aOAP flap** in selected complex cases (used in 36% of O'Dey's 119-person mixed-procedure FGM/C cohort; cohort-wide outcomes are not aOAP-specific) | O'Dey 2024 PRS<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **6** | Offer sexual-health counseling before or alongside surgery and multidisciplinary support, with individual follow-up needs | WHO 2025 conditional recommendation<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> |
| **Alternative** | Vaginal mucosal graft for clitoral / labial reconstruction; Mañero's 32-person series reported mean FSFI 16 → 29 without a control group | Mañero 2018 *Obstet Gynecol*<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> — see database |

### Special Decision Branch — Surgical Management of Lichen Sclerosus

LS surgery addresses selected **functional sequelae despite adequate medical treatment**; it does not cure the inflammatory disease. Continue individualized dermatologic follow-up and topical therapy according to activity and current guidance, not a fixed postoperative dose for every patient.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

| Indication | First-Line | Alternative | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Introital stenosis with dyspareunia / apareunia after adequate topical therapy** | Perineoplasty / de-adhesion (Lauber 2021: 41 respondents from 70 invited; mean follow-up 2.3 years) | Local skin flap in selected defects | Continue individualized disease control; residual dyspareunia was reported by 10/27 Lauber respondents who answered that item<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Severe refractory LS with sexual dysfunction** | Selected aOAP flap after appropriate disease control (O'Dey 2024 series n = 61)<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> | Skinning vulvectomy + aOAP flap in selected disease patterns | Relapse risk requires ongoing dermatologic surveillance |
| **LS sequelae after multiple failed interventions** | Selected local skin flaps; Rangatchew's series reported benefit among women with baseline dyspareunia and an 8.4-year mean clinical follow-up in those followed | Topical disease management and counseling remain necessary | Its **38% severe relapse** is from a small followed subgroup, not an all-procedure rate |

### Concurrent Procedures

| Procedure | When |
|---|---|
| **Inguinofemoral lymphadenectomy** | Through separate incisions from vulvectomy; may limit availability of groin-based flaps |
| **Sentinel lymph-node biopsy** | Oncologic eligibility depends on full staging, groin assessment and current specialist guidelines; coordinate with the vulvar-cancer pathway rather than using this reconstructive table alone |
| **Vaginal reconstruction** | If vaginal resection is included, evaluate site-specific options such as bowel or tubularized vascularized tissue; no universal sigmoid preference is established here |
| **Urethral reconstruction** | If urethral meatus is involved — careful repositioning to prevent stenosis or spraying |
| **Psychosexual counseling** | Offer according to patient goals and indication, particularly before or alongside FGM/C reconstruction<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> |
| **Adjuvant radiotherapy** | Coordinate timely treatment with oncology; a flap must not be assumed to prevent or cause a delay from an uncontrolled cohort |

### Postoperative Management & Surveillance

- **Wound dehiscence** is common, but pooled percentages across unlike flap designs and indications should not be used as an individual prognosis.
- **Sexual rehabilitation** — in Kwong's respondent subsets, sexual activity was reported by 5/54 preoperatively and 11/45 at 12 months (p = 0.041); this is not a comparative treatment effect.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Urinary continence** — in the same cohort, 26/54 reported no incontinence before surgery versus 37/46 at 12 months (p = 0.0038); follow-up denominators differed.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Oncologic surveillance** — local recurrence 14.9% at 2 yr across flap types (no significant difference by flap type); 58.3% recurrence at median 5 mo for advanced / recurrent disease (Zhang 2015).
- **LS-specific** — monitor ongoing inflammation and functional recurrence; tailor topical maintenance to activity and guideline-based follow-up rather than applying one series' relapse percentage to all repairs.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[25]](#ref25)</sup>
- **FGM/C-specific** — offer multidisciplinary follow-up responsive to sexual-health, psychological and urogenital needs; no single patient-reported scale is mandatory for every person.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
