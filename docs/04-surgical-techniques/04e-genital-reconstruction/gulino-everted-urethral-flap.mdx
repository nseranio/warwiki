# Distal Urethral Reconstruction Glanuloplasty (Gulino Technique)

The Gulino technique is a reported neoglans reconstruction in which distal urethral tissue is everted over the corporal stump after selected glans or distal penile resection. Its initial report was a small, short-follow-up case series; it should be understood as a specialized reconstructive option rather than an established standard or a predictable source of sensory recovery.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For the broader context of penile-cancer surgery and reconstruction, see [Glanuloplasty With Flaps](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/glanuloplasty-flaps), [Glansectomy With STSG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/glansectomy-stsg), and [Penile Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile).

---

## Evidence-Bounded Role

The original report described the technique after glansectomy or distal penile amputation for penile carcinoma, using retained urethral tissue to cover the distal corporal surface.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Its findings cannot establish a preferred urethral-flap orientation, operative sequence, margin strategy, comparative functional benefit, or long-term cancer control.

Current EAU penile-cancer guidance identifies glansectomy with reconstruction as one option for appropriately selected glans-confined disease, but does not recommend the Gulino technique or a urethral flap by name. The essential treatment priority remains a stage-appropriate oncologic resection, followed by reconstruction chosen for the individual defect and care setting.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Planning and Follow-up

Consideration of any urethral-flap reconstruction requires assessment of disease extent, urethral involvement, the availability and quality of local tissue, prior treatment, ability to maintain oncologic control, and the person’s urinary, sexual, appearance, and revision priorities. The responsible reconstructive and oncologic team determines whether this option is feasible.

After cancer surgery, follow-up should include examination for local recurrence and assessment of healing, meatal/urethral symptoms, urinary and sexual function, appearance, and need for further treatment. The follow-up plan depends on pathological risk and the treating cancer team.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## What Is Not Established

- A reported small-series sensory result is not proof that sensation will be preserved or superior to another reconstruction.
- The literature reviewed here does not establish specific urethral length, technical steps, catheter duration, dressing regimen, or margin threshold.
- Direct comparisons with inverted urethral flaps, skin-graft reconstruction, or more radical surgery have not been recovered.
- The early case-series follow-up is insufficient to establish durable recurrence, functional, psychosexual, or quality-of-life outcomes.
