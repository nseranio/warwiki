# Distal Urethral Reconstruction Glanuloplasty (Gulino Technique)

The **Gulino technique** is a urethral-mucosa neoglans reconstruction first reported by **Gulino, Sasso, Falabella, and Bassi (2007)** after glansectomy or distal-shaft amputation. In the original 14-patient report, every participant had subjective and objective thermal and tactile sensibility in the neoglans area. This is a small uncontrolled cohort, not proof of superiority over other reconstructions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For the related inversion variant that has the largest evidence base, see [Inverted Urethral Flap (IUF) Glanuloplasty (Belinky / Chavarriaga)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/inverted-urethral-flap). For the comparative overview of all four flap-based glanuloplasty options (IUF, Gulino, scrotal, rectus), see [Glanuloplasty With Flaps](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/glanuloplasty-flaps). For the STSG-based standard, see [Glansectomy With STSG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/glansectomy-stsg). Decision framework: [Penile Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile).

---

## Historical Context and Innovation

The authors developed distal-urethral reconstruction to provide a mucosal neoglans after cancer surgery. Comparisons with skin-graft sensation or surface properties were not tested head-to-head in the 14-patient report.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The authors used the **distal penile urethra** as a mucosal, spongiosal-supplied autologous neoglans donor. They described the operation as **"innovative, easy and rapid"** with **"appreciable functional and esthetic results"**; these are author conclusions from an uncontrolled series.

Belinky 2011 and Chavarriaga 2022 later reported a related inverted-urethral-flap approach. The accessible sources do not establish a direct lineage or comparative advantage.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Anatomical Basis

### Vascular supply

- **Urethral / bulbar artery** (internal pudendal branch) supplies the corpus spongiosum; broader penile-anatomy sources describe anastomotic pathways, but they do not substitute for technique-specific perfusion data.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Cavernospongious anastomoses are background anatomy, not a guarantee of flap viability in an individual operation.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- Dorsal arteries principally perfuse glans and spongiosum with perforators to the distal corpora (4 / 5 specimens)

### Neural supply

- Urethral mucosa is sensate; the original cohort documented thermal and tactile sensibility but did not prove the mechanism or a comparative sensory advantage.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Dorsal nerves (somatic) lie in Buck's fascia (absent at 12 o'clock); perineal nerves to ventral skin / frenulum; cavernosal nerves (autonomic) for erection

### Tissue properties

- **Non-keratinized mucosa** — provides a mucosal surface rather than a skin-graft surface; clinical equivalence to native glans epithelium was not tested
- Buck's fascia envelops corpora + spongiosum, providing structural support

## Indications

| Indication (Gulino 2007) | Cohort |
|---|---|
| **Simple glansectomy** for penile SCC confined to the glans | 8 / 14 |
| **Amputation of the distal third of the shaft** for more extensive disease | 6 / 14 |

Applicable when:
- Sufficient distal urethral length can be preserved beyond the corporal transection
- Urethral preservation oncologically acceptable with negative margins; intraoperative frozen-section use follows the treating team's judgment and guideline context
- Patient desires functional + cosmetic reconstruction

## Surgical Technique

### Reported distal-urethral configurations

| Feature | **Gulino distal-urethral reconstruction** | Belinky / Chavarriaga inversion |
|---|---|---|
| **Manipulation** | Penile urethra mobilized, ventrally opened and spread over the corporal apices in the accessible follow-up description<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> | Urethral segment described as inverted after partial penectomy |
| **Mucosal surface orientation** | **Outward** (same) | **Outward** (same) |
| **Tissue distribution** | Symmetric over corporal tips | Differential ventral/dorsal elasticity |
| **Resulting ventral curvature** | Not reported in the original abstract | 1/10 in Belinky's abstract |

### Step-by-step from the open follow-up description<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

1. **Resection and margin assessment** — remove the glans en bloc from the cavernosal apices and distal urethra; the reported series checked cavernosal-apex and distal-urethral margins intraoperatively. Current EAU guidance supports frozen section when completeness is in doubt rather than as a universal rule for every organ-sparing case.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
2. **Urethral mobilization** — dissect the penile urethra from the ventral corpora from the apices toward the shaft base to obtain a urethral stump at least 3 cm longer than the corporal bodies in this published protocol.
3. **Spatulation and coverage** — open the ventral urethra longitudinally for 3 cm, spread it over the cavernosal apices and secure the mucosal edges over the dorsal albuginea.
4. **Fixation and skin inset** — the authors used 3-0 absorbable sutures and placed penile skin 1 cm from the neoglans margin, anticipating epithelial migration.
5. **Neomeatus and drainage** — suture the urethral opening ventrally to penile skin; the reported protocol left a bladder catheter for five days.

These measurements and postoperative choices describe one center's technique, not a universal protocol. The actual figure panel was not independently inspected in this review.

## Open Follow-up Cohort (2013)<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

The open follow-up paper described 42 patients treated for early penile carcinoma with three different procedures: diathermocoagulation (6), glandulectomy with urethral-mucosa glanduloplasty (25), and glandulectomy with limited apical resection (11). Across the mixed cohort, 31/42 reported erections; 25/42 were able to have intercourse. Among the 25 glanduloplasty patients, 19 reported normal ejaculation and orgasm at six months. These procedure-specific and mixed-cohort denominators should not be merged with the original 14-patient series.

---

## Key Findings

### Sensibility in the original cohort

All 14 participants had subjective and objective thermal and tactile epicritic sensibility in the neoglans area. The cross-series figures below use different methods and do not establish superiority:

| Technique | Sensation preservation | n |
|---|---|---|
| **Gulino distal-urethral reconstruction** | **100%** | 14 |
| Glansectomy + STSG (Pang SR) | 83.7% (63.6–91.2%) | 327 |
| Glansectomy + STSG (Falcone 2022) | 91.2% | 34 |
| Partial penectomy without reconstruction | Variable, often absent | — |

The authors attributed this to urethral mucosa, but the study did not directly compare neurophysiology or sensation with STSG.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

### Ejaculation and orgasm findings

IIEF ejaculation and orgasm domain scores did **not significantly change** post-operatively, contrasting with broader partial-penectomy literature:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

- Whyte SR — partial penectomy generally decreases all IIEF domains including orgasm
- Kieffer (n = 90) — significantly more problems with orgasm (effect size 0.54, p = 0.031) vs penile-sparing surgery
- Falcone 2024 (n = 99) — IIEF-15 fell after glansectomy (− 12.955, p = 0.002) and WLE (− 14.1, p = 0.025)

These within-cohort observations do not isolate the effect of reconstruction or establish uniqueness.

### No local recurrence reported at mean 13 months

This short follow-up cannot establish long-term oncologic equivalence. Comparative data below come from different cohorts:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[16]](#ref16)[[10]](#ref10)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- Parnham (STSG, n = 177): 9.3% LR at median 41.4 mo
- Chavarriaga IUF (n = 74): 6-y RFS 90.5%
- EAU-ASCO SR: cumulative 5-y RFR of 82% for organ-sparing

### Retraction and curvature

No penile retraction was reported in the original abstract. Curvature was not reported there, so Belinky's 1/10 ventral curvature cannot prove an advantage of one configuration.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guideline Context

Neither NCCN nor EAU-ASCO mention Gulino or any urethral-flap reconstruction by name, but the technique aligns with their principles:<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- **NCCN** — the inherited page reports neoglans creation with STSG / FTSG in selected instances; the full current NCCN text was not accessible in this review and no universal frozen-section mandate is inferred
- **EAU-ASCO** — strong recommendation for organ-sparing surgery with reconstruction for confined disease (PeIN, Ta, T1–T2); frozen section in cases of doubt (weak)
- EAU-ASCO notes sexual and erectile functions vary; penile-preserving surgery generally preserves erection but glans sensation and orgasm can be affected

## Psychosexual Considerations

The Gulino technique addresses several documented psychosexual concerns:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[14]](#ref14)[[17]](#ref17)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

- **Body image** — Romero 2005: 50% of sexually abstinent post-partial-penectomy patients cited shame from absent glans as the main reason for not resuming intercourse; Gulino creates a natural-appearing neoglans
- **Orgasm** — Kieffer 2014 effect size 0.54 worse with (partial) penectomy vs penile-sparing; Gulino's preserved IIEF orgasm domain is clinically meaningful
- **Overall QoL** — Roumieux 2025 narrative review emphasized organ-sparing surgery preserves sexual function but more invasive treatment compounds declines; rigorous prospective PROMs needed
- **Multidisciplinary support** — Torres Irizarry 2024: individualized sexual therapy, support groups, family counseling are essential regardless of surgical technique

---

## Advantages

1. **Thermal and tactile sensibility in 14/14** in the original report, without a direct comparator
2. **No significant within-cohort change** in IIEF ejaculation and orgasm domains in that report
3. **No local recurrence or penile retraction reported** at mean 13 months; this is insufficient for long-term oncologic conclusions
4. Meatal stenosis, flap necrosis and curvature were **not reported in the accessible abstract** and must not be converted to zero-event rates
5. **Single-stage** — performed simultaneously with tumor resection
6. **No separate donor site** — eliminates thigh / oral donor morbidity
7. **Moist mucosal surface** — biologically mimics native glans
8. **"Easy and rapid"** by authors' description

---

## Limitations

1. **Small sample size** (n = 14) — single published series
2. **Short follow-up** (mean 13 mo) — insufficient for definitive oncologic conclusions
3. **Single-center single-surgeon** — no external validation
4. **No validated QoL instruments** — satisfaction qualitative ("appreciable")
5. **No long-term oncologic data** — 0% LR at 13 mo cannot compare to 9.1% at 40.7 mo in Pang SR
6. **No standardized cosmetic-satisfaction data**
7. **Not specifically referenced in NCCN / EAU-ASCO**
8. **Rigid erections reported in 10/14** — not directly comparable with pooled STSG definitions or populations
9. **No direct comparison with the Belinky / Chavarriaga inversion technique**
10. **Requires sufficient urethral length** — not feasible for proximal amputations or urethra-involving tumors

---

## Future Directions

- **Larger multicenter validation** with standardized PROMs (IIEF-15, IPSS, EQ-5D, EORTC QLQ-C30)
- **Long-term oncologic follow-up (≥ 5 y)** confirming the safety of preserving longer urethral stumps
- **Direct prospective or matched comparison** of the Gulino configuration vs inversion (Belinky / Chavarriaga)
- **Histological characterization** of the reconstructed urethral mucosa over time — keratinization, metaplasia, or retention of native mucosal character
- **Objective neurophysiological assessment** (somatosensory evoked potentials, biothesiometry) to quantify the sensory advantage over STSG
- **Hybrid approaches** — BMG meatoplasty + urethral flap for neoglans

---

## Key Takeaways

- A reported distal-urethral neoglans technique from 2007 that predates the Belinky / Chavarriaga IUF literature; direct influence was not established here
- The accessible follow-up describes mobilizing and ventrally opening the penile urethra, then spreading mucosa over the cavernosal apices; it is not simply a sleeve rolled over the tip
- Thermal and tactile sensibility was present in **14/14** in the original short series, without a direct comparator
- IIEF ejaculation and orgasm domains did not change significantly within that cohort
- No local recurrence or penile retraction was reported at mean 13 months; stenosis, necrosis and curvature were not reported in the abstract
- **Small single-center series (n = 14, 13-mo follow-up)** — limits definitive long-term oncologic conclusions; the Belinky / Chavarriaga IUF remains the larger-evidence sister technique
