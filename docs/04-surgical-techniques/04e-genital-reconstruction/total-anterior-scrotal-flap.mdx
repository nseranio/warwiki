# Total Anterior Scrotal Flap (Zhao Technique)

The **total anterior scrotal flap (Zhao)** is a single-stage axial-pattern scrotal flap that recruits anterior scrotal skin supplied by both anterior and posterior scrotal arterial systems to cover a penile-shaft defect after elongation via suspensory-ligament division. Zhao's retrospective series included 18 patients, of whom 12 received the total flap and six a bilateral flap. In the mixed 18-patient cohort, length increased in flaccid and erect states (p &lt; 0.05); the abstract reports recovery of deep and superficial sensation and retained erectile function, while 15/18 reported satisfactory intercourse. These are not total-flap-only outcome estimates, and there was no nonoperative or graft comparator.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For single-stage skin-only scrotal flaps, see [Bipedicled Anterior Scrotal Flap (Fakin)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/bipedicled-anterior-scrotal-flap), [Modified Bipedicle Scrotal Tunnel Flap (Murányi)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/muranyi-scrotal-tunnel-flap), and [Modified Bilateral Butterfly Scrotal Flap (Yao)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/yao-butterfly-scrotal-flap). For staged or outpatient variants, see [Pribaz / McLaughlin staged](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/staged-scrotal-flap) and [Ventral Slit Scrotal Flap (VSSF)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/ventral-slit-scrotal-flap). For the graft alternative, see [Penile Skin Grafting](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile-skin-grafting). Full framework: [Penile Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile).

## Rationale

The Zhao technique was developed at a Chinese center over a 15-year period (1992–2007) for partial penile defects caused by trauma (animal bite), tumor excision, or circumcision complications, where the remaining stump is too short for functional intercourse.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The defining innovation is combining penile elongation (suspensory ligament division) with maximal-area scrotal coverage in a single operation, addressing the length deficit and the skin deficit simultaneously.

The authors used bilateral scrotal flaps in 6/18 and the total anterior design in 12/18. The abstract does not provide a patient-level threshold proving that a bilateral design was insufficient in all 12; design selection should follow the actual defect and available donor skin.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Related penile-lengthening and scrotal-coverage approaches, including Shirong's 52-case report, predated the Zhao cohort; the sources do not establish one exclusive inventor of the combination.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Vascular Anatomy — The Triple Territory

The flap exploits all three Carrera scrotal vascular territories simultaneously:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

| Source artery | System | Territory |
|---|---|---|
| **Anterior scrotal arteries** (terminal branches of inferior external pudendal) | External pudendal / femoral | Two lateral territories — their inferior external pudendal arteries enter at the midpoint of the scrotal root and fan across the corresponding hemiscrotum <sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Posterior scrotal arteries** (branches of perineal arteries) | Internal pudendal / perineal | Central territory — enter via the posterior scrotal surface, run alongside the scrotal septum |

These territories are widely inter-anastomosed in Carrera's cadaveric microvascular study. Zhao describes total anterior coverage drawing on both anterior and posterior scrotal arterial systems, but the original abstract does not test superiority of its perfusion against other designs or report a total-flap-specific loss rate.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Indications

| Indication | Notes (Zhao series) |
|---|---|
| **Partial penile defect with stump too short for intercourse** | The prototype indication |
| **Animal bite injury** | Most common etiology — 9 / 18 (50%); manage the wound according to tissue destruction, prescribe antibiotics according to local resistance, and assess rabies risk from geography, animal, wound and attack details.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| **Penile tumor excision** | 6 / 18 (33%) <sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Circumcision complications** with significant tissue loss | 3 / 18 (17%) <sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Patient needs both elongation and skin coverage** | Defining indication |
| **Flap selected in Zhao's cohort** | 12 / 18 (67%) received the total design; the abstract does not establish the exact selection threshold <sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |

### Prerequisites

- Intact uninvolved anterior and posterior scrotal skin
- Intact suspensory ligament (for elongation)
- Viable corpora cavernosa and corpus spongiosum
- Functional urethra

## Operative Technique

The steps below combine reconstructive anatomy with general scrotal-flap technique. Zhao's abstract does not specify the elevation plane, pedicle arrangement, inset sequence or drainage. These details reflect standard practice and are not a step-by-step description by Zhao.

### 1. Penile elongation — suspensory ligament division

- Suprapubic or penoscrotal incision to expose the suspensory ligaments
- **Divide the fundiform (superficial) ligament,** the Scarpa's-fascia sling from the linea alba
- **Divide the triangular (deep) suspensory ligament,** partially or completely. It is the true ligament from the pubic symphysis to Buck's fascia; identify and protect the dorsal penile neurovascular bundle near its base and the external pudendal vessels near the fundiform base, accounting for variable attachment anatomy.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- Releases tethering at the penile base; a separate cadaveric study measured an average apparent length gain of 26.4 mm (range 4–60 mm), which must not be projected as a clinical gain for Zhao's patients.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- V-Y advancement plasty at the dorsal penile base has been described to reduce skin traction; it does not guarantee prevention of retraction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### 2. Assess the combined defect

- Original defect and newly exposed proximal corpora after elongation
- If small enough for bilateral hemiscrotum flaps, bilateral approach (6 / 18 in Zhao)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- If a larger continuous flap is judged necessary, consider the total anterior scrotal flap (used in 12 / 18 in Zhao; individual choice criteria not reported in the abstract)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### 3. Flap design

- The entire anterior scrotum is used as a single continuous sheet
- Boundaries:
  - Superior: penoscrotal junction (scrotal root)
  - Inferior: approximately the inferior pole of the testes
  - Lateral: lateral scrotal creases bilaterally
  - Includes the midline raphe and skin from both hemiscrotums
- Broad-based pedicle incorporating both anterior scrotal arteries (lateral roots) and posterior scrotal arteries (central / posterior)

### 4. Flap elevation

- Elevate inferior to superior
- Plane above external spermatic fascia, preserving dartos with the flap
- Tunica vaginalis kept intact; testes transiently exposed
- Carefully preserve lateral pedicles (at lateral roots) and central / posterior pedicle

### 5. Flap rotation and wrapping

- Position the denuded shaft (including newly elongated proximal segment) centrally
- Wrap the flap as a single continuous sheet around the shaft
- Close with a single longitudinal dorsal-midline suture line (versus the two longitudinal scars of bilateral techniques)

### 6. Inset

- Distal circumferential suture line to subcoronal margin / glans
- Proximal suture line to penile base / pubic skin (incorporates the V-Y closure)
- Dorsal midline longitudinal closure of flap free edges
- All interrupted absorbable suture

### 7. Donor closure

- Remaining posterior and inferior scrotal skin advanced anteriorly and closed primarily
- Drain if dead-space concern
- Testes re-covered by the residual scrotal envelope

### 8. Anti-retraction considerations

- Some penile-lengthening protocols describe postoperative traction or stay-suture strategies; the available Zhao abstract does not establish a required glans-to-thigh device or prove prevention of re-attachment. Follow the specific operative protocol and monitor tissue perfusion.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## How It Differs From Other Scrotal-Flap Techniques

| Feature | Total anterior scrotal (Zhao) | Bilateral scrotal (Jeong / Yao) | Fakin bipedicled | Murányi tunnel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Flap composition** | Entire anterior scrotum, single sheet | Two hemiscrotum flaps divided at raphe | Single midline flap with central window | Single midline flap with tunnel |
| **Blood supply** | Anterior + posterior scrotal (triple territory) | Anterior scrotal only (bilateral) | Bilateral anterior scrotal | Bilateral external pudendal |
| **Skin surface area** | Entire anterior scrotum used | Defect-dependent | Defect-dependent | Defect-dependent <sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Shaft suture lines** | Single dorsal longitudinal | Dorsal + ventral longitudinal | Circumferential at base + coronal | Dorsal + ventral longitudinal, plus subcoronal circumferential <sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **Combined with elongation** | Yes (integral) | Not typically | No | No |
| **Staging** | Single | Single | Single | Single |
| **Primary indication** | Partial penile defect with length deficit | Jeong: paraffinoma; Yao: skin or foreskin shortage after trauma or tumor treatment <sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> | Circumferential skin defect (siliconoma) | Circumferential skin defect (paraffinoma) |

| Parameter | Result |
|---|---|
| Study period | 1992–2007 (15 y) |
| Etiologies | Animal bite 9, tumor excision 6, circumcision 3 |
| Bilateral scrotal flap | 6 / 18 (anterior scrotal artery only) |
| **Total anterior scrotal flap** | 12 / 18 (anterior + posterior scrotal arteries) |
| Combined suspensory ligament division | Described as part of the combined procedure; patient-level division count not separately reported in the abstract <sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Penile length increase, flaccid + erect** | Significant (p < 0.05) |
| **Deep/superficial sensation** | Reported recovered across the mixed 18-patient cohort; no total-flap-only estimate in abstract |
| Erectile function | Reported retained across the mixed cohort; no total-flap-only estimate in abstract |
| Satisfactory sexual intercourse | 15 / 18 (83.3%) during 0.5–5 years of follow-up |
| **Flap loss** | Not quantified in the abstract; do not infer a zero-event subgroup rate |
| Mean follow-up | 2.3 y (range 1–9) for the mixed cohort |

## Suspensory Ligament Division — Integral Component

The elongation step is integral to the Zhao technique, not adjunctive.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### Anatomy

- **Fundiform ligament.** Superficial sling arising from the linea alba or Scarpa's fascia in a 34-cadaver series; its bundles split around the penile root and reunite inferiorly with the scrotal septum.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- **Triangular (deep) suspensory ligament.** True ligament from pubic symphysis to Buck's fascia on the dorsal shaft
- The suspensory ligament anchors the penile root to the pubic symphysis; its division can increase visible projection without increasing the absolute length of penile tissue.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### Length gain

- Cadaveric average 26.4 mm (range 4–60 mm)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- In the 16-cadaver study, shorter pre-dissection length correlated with greater immediate apparent gain (r = −0.601, p = 0.014); clinical prediction has not been validated.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Shirong n = 52. Its reported length results come from a separate procedure or cohort and cannot quantify Zhao's gain<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Deskoulidi and Caminer describe 2–4 cm postoperative flaccid length gain after combined inverted V-Y plasty, suspensory-ligament division, distally based fat-flap interposition and fat injection in a 75-patient report.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### Risk of scar-mediated re-attachment

Scar re-attachment and altered erection angle are recognized counseling concerns after ligamentolysis. The measures listed here are technical considerations; their ability to prevent re-attachment in Zhao's cohort is unproven:

1. Fat-flap interposition between the penile base and pubis has been described in other suspensory-ligament division procedures.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
2. V-Y advancement plasty at the dorsal base
3. Stay sutures / traction to maintain stretch during healing<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Advantages

1. The entire anterior scrotum provides the maximal skin surface area as a single sheet.
2. The total flap is described as supplied by both anterior and posterior scrotal arteries. Comparative perfusion superiority and a total-flap-specific zero-loss rate are not established.
3. The single continuous sheet leaves one dorsal suture line, versus the two longitudinal scars of bilateral techniques.
4. Elongation and coverage are combined in one operation. This is a defining feature of the Zhao approach and is not unique among historical methods.
5. Sensation recovery and retained erectile function were reported in the mixed 18-patient cohort. Total-flap subgroup outcomes and standardized sensory testing are unavailable.
6. Follow-up averaged 2.3 years (range 1–9) in the mixed cohort. It is not established as the longest scrotal-flap follow-up.
7. The operation is single-stage.
8. The technique has been reported for animal-bite, tumor-excision and circumcision etiologies in a small uncontrolled series.
9. Scrotal skin quality is comparable to native shaft skin.

---

## Limitations and Disadvantages

1. Harvesting the entire anterior scrotum leaves a large donor defect, with potential scrotal volume reduction, discomfort and testicular ascension (22.7% in Mendel's bilateral-flap cohort).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
2. Suspensory ligament division can alter the erection angle and allow scar re-attachment with loss of the length gained.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Penile instability during thrusting is also a described concern.
3. The operation applies to partial penile defects and not to total or near-total penile loss.
4. The series is small (n = 18), which limits the evidence base.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
5. The series reports no standardized PROMs such as the IIEF or POSAS.
6. Hair-bearing scrotal skin varies between patients; assess the donor area and counsel about hair on the reconstructed shaft. Laser depilation before or after surgery has been described.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
7. Late skin retraction (approximately 27%) was reported in Mendel's distinct bilateral-flap cohort. It is not a Zhao or universal scrotal-flap rate.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
8. Altered shaft shape is a possible donor or design issue. Mendel's approximately 4.6% pyramidal shape is not a Zhao-specific risk estimate.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
9. The Zhao publication has no comparison group. In separate cohorts, Mendel reported suture dehiscence in 7/22 and infection in 3/22 bilateral-flap patients; Fakin reported partial wound disruption in 8/43 and donor-site hematoma in 5/43 bipedicled-flap patients.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

| Flap design | Single continuous sheet | Two separate hemiscrotum flaps |

| Shaft suture lines | Single dorsal | Dorsal + ventral |
| Donor-site morbidity | Larger donor area; comparative morbidity not reported | Smaller donor area; comparative morbidity not reported |

Zhao reported both designs after penile elongation but did not specify an intraoperative choice rule or defect-size threshold in the abstract.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Patient Selection — When Total Anterior Coverage May Be Considered

| Choose Zhao total anterior scrotal flap | Consider alternative |
|---|---|
| **Partial penile defect with length deficit needing both elongation and coverage** | No length deficit (skin-only) → full-thickness skin graft or a defect-matched scrotal flap (Fakin / Murányi / Yao)<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Large circumferential defect** exceeding bilateral hemiscrotum capacity | Bilateral defect manageable with hemiscrotum flaps → Yao butterfly |
| Animal-bite injury or tumor-excision shortening | Paraffinoma / siliconoma (no length deficit) → Fakin / Murányi |
| Penile length restoration is part of a shared reconstructive goal after counseling on ligamentolysis | Wound bed not ready for single-stage coverage, such as uncontrolled infection → debridement, then a staged bipedicled scrotal flap (Pribaz / McLaughlin)<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| Adequate anterior + posterior scrotal skin available | Scrotal skin insufficient / involved → STSG / FTSG or regional flap<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| No total-flap-specific sensory advantage established<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Small ventral-only deficit → VSSF |
| | Total penile loss → assess acute amputation urgently for reimplantation if the part is available and suitable; when reimplantation is not possible, individualize delayed reconstruction, with RFFF phalloplasty among the options.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |

## Key Takeaways

- The Zhao approach integrates suspensory ligament division with scrotal-flap coverage in a single operation. Separate historical lengthening and coverage methods exist.
- The anterior and posterior scrotal arterial systems supply the total-flap design. Comparative robustness and total-flap-specific loss rates are unverified.
- The total flap provides the maximal skin surface area as a single continuous sheet with one dorsal suture line.
- Sensation recovery was reported in Zhao's mixed 18-patient cohort at mean 2.3-year follow-up; the abstract gives no sensory-recovery count.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> No total-flap-only or standardized sensory estimate exists.
- The trade-off is a larger donor area and ligamentolysis-related angle and re-attachment concerns. The Zhao abstract does not establish a routine stay-suture regimen.
- The technique is reserved for partial penile defects with a length deficit. It is not first-line for circumferential paraffinoma or simple skin defects.
