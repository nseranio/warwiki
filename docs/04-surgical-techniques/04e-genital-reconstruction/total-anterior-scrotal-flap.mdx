# Total Anterior Scrotal Flap (Zhao Technique)

Zhao and colleagues reported scrotal-skin flap reconstruction for partial penile defects.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The available bibliographic record does not provide enough source detail to support the inherited operative protocol, vascular claims, selection algorithm, or outcome comparisons. This page therefore records the named approach as a historical, specialist reconstructive option rather than a standardized technique.

For the broader reconstructive framework and other reported coverage approaches, see [Penile Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile), [Bipedicled Anterior Scrotal Flap (Fakin)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/bipedicled-anterior-scrotal-flap), [Modified Bipedicle Scrotal Tunnel Flap (Murányi)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/muranyi-scrotal-tunnel-flap), [Staged Bipedicled Scrotal Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/staged-scrotal-flap), and [Penile Skin Grafting](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile-skin-grafting).

## Evidence boundary

- The full Zhao source package was not recovered in this audit.
- The available record does not establish an operative sequence, flap vascular territory, suspensory-ligament role, patient-selection rule, comparative benefit, complication rate, functional outcome, or durability.
- A named scrotal-flap report should not be interpreted as a default approach to any penile defect.
- Reconstruction selection requires specialist assessment of the cause and extent of tissue loss, urethral/corporal involvement, tissue quality, infection/oncologic context, available donor tissue, patient goals, and local expertise.
