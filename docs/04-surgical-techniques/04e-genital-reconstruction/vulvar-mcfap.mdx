# Medial Circumflex Femoral Artery Perforator (MCFAP) Flap for Vulvar Reconstruction

The **MCFAP flap** is a fasciocutaneous perforator flap based on **musculocutaneous perforators from the medial circumflex femoral artery** that traverse the **gracilis muscle** to supply the superior medial-thigh skin. First described as a perforator flap by **Hallock (2003)**, it represents the **perforator-based evolution of the gracilis musculocutaneous flap** — preserving the gracilis while harvesting only the overlying skin and subcutaneous tissue. In vulvar reconstruction, MCFAP was used in **8 of 27 flaps** (the second most common perforator type) in the Huang 2015 series, with 100% flap survival and no donor-site morbidities.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Anatomic Basis — Medial Circumflex Femoral Artery

The MCFA is one of two major branches of the deep femoral (profunda) artery, arising from the DFA in ~65% and directly from the common femoral artery in ~32%.

| Study | n | DFA origin | CFA origin | Distance from inguinal point |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tomaszewski 2016 (meta-analysis)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | 38 studies / 4,351 lower limbs | **64.6%** (95% CI 58.0–71.5) | **32.2%** (95% CI 25.9–39.1) | When CFA — single branch in 81.1% at 50.14 mm |
| Vazquez 2007<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | 442 lower limbs | 78.8% (Pattern I — both MCFA / LCFA from DFA) | 20.5% (Pattern II — MCFA from femoral) | Pattern I more frequent in females (p = 0.01) |
| Zlotorowicz 2018 CTA<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | 100 patients | ~69% | ~31% | 41.7 mm to MCFA |
| Patel 2022<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | 155 femoral systems | — | — | 41.7 mm to MCFA |

### Relationship to gracilis muscle

The MCFA gives off branches that supply the gracilis — these provide the musculocutaneous perforators forming the basis of the MCFAP flap.

| Source | Detail |
|---|---|
| Juricic 1993 (n = 84 gracilis muscles)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | Main neurovascular pedicle from artery of the adductors (73%), MCFA (19.2%), or both (7.7%) |
| Magden 2010 (n = 30)<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | Most proximal pedicle = deep branch of MCFA (60 mm from pubic tubercle, 0.9 mm); MCFA itself dominant in 13% (1.2 mm, 98 mm from pubic tubercle); deep femoral a. dominant in 87% (1.6 mm, length 54 mm) |
| Macchi 2008 CTA (n = 40)<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | Principal pedicle enters gracilis at 10 ± 1 cm from ischiopubic attachment; caliber 2.5 ± 0.5 mm — statistically larger when arising directly from DFA (45%) than from artery of adductors (46%) (p &lt; 0.001) |

## Perforator Anatomy — The MCFAP Territory

### Musculocutaneous perforators (Peek 2009, 43 cadaver dissections + 14 clinical)<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

- Musculocutaneous perforators in **100%** of dissections; **≥ 0.5 mm in 93%**.
- Septocutaneous perforators in 84%; ≥ 0.5 mm in 63%.
- Most musculocutaneous perforators in the **anterior quarter of the muscle** where the pedicle enters.
- Constant intramuscular anastomosis between main and second vascular pedicles — enables an **extended gracilis perforator flap up to 27 cm**.

### Skin territory

| Series | Skin paddle |
|---|---|
| Peek 2009<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> | Up to **18 × 15 cm** |
| Coquerel-Beghin 2006 (32 cadaveric thighs)<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | Mean 110 × 91.4 mm (length × width); surface 79.5 cm² (42.4–139.3); 1–6 perforators (mean 3.78); 80% exit opposite the principal pedicle within 60 mm of muscle |
| Eom 2011 (n = 40 clinical)<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | Average 71.6 cm² (vertical 6.4 cm × transverse 10.9 cm); **90% of perforators from MCFA**; one-third musculocutaneous / two-thirds septocutaneous; pedicle length 6 cm; artery diameter 0.8 mm |

### Vascular-territory head-to-head (Zaussinger 2019, 26 lower limbs)<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

| Parameter | TMG (MCFA-based) | PAP (profunda-based) | FCI (infragluteal) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pedicle diameter | 2.9 ± 0.6 mm | **3.6 ± 0.7 mm** | 2.9 ± 0.7 mm |
| Pedicle length | 6.7 ± 1.0 cm | 8.8 ± 1.0 cm | **12.5 ± 1.5 cm** |
| Angiosome area | 74.1 ± 32.1 cm² | **98.5 ± 26.7 cm²** | 77.2 ± 9.0 cm² |

MCFA-based (TMG) territory has the **smallest pedicle diameter and angiosome area** of the three but is comparable in clinical utility.

### Sensory innervation

- **Obturator nerve** — motor to gracilis; sensory to overlying medial-thigh skin.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Anterior branch of the obturator nerve** — cutaneous territory corresponds to the MCFAP territory; hypesthesia in 40.5% of patients after gracilis flap harvest.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

## Nomenclature — Related MCFA-Based Flaps

| Flap | Muscle included | Design |
|---|---|---|
| **Gracilis musculocutaneous** | Yes (entire) | Workhorse for perineal / vaginal reconstruction; bulky; unreliable skin paddle |
| **Transverse musculocutaneous gracilis (TMG)** | Yes (proximal) | Transverse musculocutaneous; breast reconstruction |
| **Short gracilis** | Yes (distal only — obturator-based) | Less bulky; sacrifices MCFA pedicle |
| **MCFAP / Gracilis perforator** | **No** | Thin, pliable; preserves gracilis; concealed scar |
| **Extended gracilis perforator** | No | Up to 27 cm using intramuscular anastomosis<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |

The key innovation: MCFAP **preserves the gracilis muscle** while harvesting only the overlying skin and subcutaneous tissue on musculocutaneous perforators.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## The MCFAP Flap in Vulvar Reconstruction — Huang 2015<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The only published series specifically reporting MCFAP for vulvar reconstruction:

- 27 flaps in 16 patients (island pedicled perforator flaps after vulvar cancer surgery).
- Perforator-flap types: DFAP / PAP (11), **MCFAP (8)**, IPAP (4), EPAP (2), medial-thigh free-style (2).
- **All 27 flaps survived (100%)**, including all 8 MCFAP flaps.
- 3 patients required small-wound debridement; 1 required flap debulking.
- All donor sites closed primarily; no donor-site morbidities.
- The MCFAP was the **second most commonly used** perforator type (8/27, 29.6%).

MCFAP was selected when the perforator location and defect geometry favored the MCFA territory over DFAP, IPAP, or EPAP.

## Surgical Technique

Based on Hallock 2003, Huang 2015, Eom 2011:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

1. **Position** — lithotomy for vulvar reconstruction (or supine with hip abducted / externally rotated).
2. **Perforator identification** — handheld Doppler over the proximal medial thigh along the anterior border of the gracilis; perforators 8–10 cm from the pubic tubercle.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
3. **Flap design** — island skin paddle centered on the perforator(s):
   - **Transverse** (medial-groin-crease orientation — most common for vulvar)
   - **Longitudinal** (along the gracilis axis — for larger flaps)
   - **Free-style** (centered on the strongest Doppler signal).<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
4. **Incision and suprafascial elevation** — perforator(s) identified and carefully dissected through the gracilis muscle substance, preserving the muscle, motor nerve, and remaining vasculature.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
5. **Intramuscular perforator dissection** — trace the perforator through gracilis to its origin from the MCFA pedicle.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
6. **Transfer** — transposition / rotation / advancement into the vulvar defect; arc of rotation extends throughout the groin region and potentially to the lateral thigh.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
7. **Donor closure** — primary, with the scar concealed in the medial groin crease.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Key technical points:

- Gracilis muscular branches can be independently dissected from musculocutaneous perforators → the muscle can be separately included as a **conjoint flap** (e.g., to wrap exposed vessels) while the cutaneous component closes skin.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- The free-style approach (Eom) does not require preoperative imaging.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- Flap thinning is feasible per reconstructive need.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

## The MCFAP Flap as a Local vs Free Flap (Hallock 2003)

### Local flap<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- 4 clinical cases for adjacent groin wounds (post-vascular interventions).
- Uncomplicated healing with no vascular compromise.
- Arc of rotation extends throughout groin and potentially the lateral thigh.
- Conjoint-flap option (gracilis included separately) allows the muscle to wrap and protect exposed vascular structures.
- Described as ideal local perforator flap — consistent source pedicle, reliable skin territory, concealed medial-groin donor scar.

### Free flap<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- First clinical free MCFAP transfer.
- **No muscle included** — muscle bulk avoided, function preserved.
- Consistent pedicle location familiar to most reconstructive surgeons.

## MCFAP Flap vs Gracilis Musculocutaneous Flap

| Feature | **MCFAP (perforator)** | Gracilis musculocutaneous |
|---|---|---|
| Muscle sacrifice | **None — gracilis preserved**<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | Yes — gracilis sacrificed |
| Tissue thickness | Thin, pliable | Bulky (muscle + skin) |
| Skin-paddle reliability | High (perforator-based) | **Unreliable** — moderately high partial-necrosis rates<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| Donor-site morbidity | Minimal — no muscle loss, concealed scar | 11% adduction-strength loss; 40.5% hypesthesia; contour deformity<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| Volume for dead-space filling | Limited (fasciocutaneous only) | High — muscle bulk fills dead space |
| Pedicle length | ~6–7 cm | ~6–7 cm (extendable by tunneling under adductor longus) |
| Operative complexity | Higher (perforator dissection through muscle)<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> | Lower (standard musculocutaneous harvest) |
| Best indication | Thin skin coverage (vulvar, scrotal, groin) | Volume reconstruction (vaginal, pelvic dead space) |

The traditional gracilis musculocutaneous flap has historically been plagued by unreliable skin perfusion with moderately high partial-necrosis rates. Whetzel & Lechtman (1997) addressed this with a myofasciocutaneous design, improving skin viability.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> The MCFAP flap avoids this problem entirely by designing the flap directly around identified perforators.

## Donor-Site Morbidity

### Gracilis muscle harvest (Deutinger 1995, n = 42)<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

- Adduction strength decreased by 11% (not noticed by patients).
- Hypesthesia in the obturator-nerve cutaneous territory in 40.5%.
- Contour deformity of the thigh when a musculocutaneous flap is elevated.
- Many patients dissatisfied with aesthetic appearance of the donor site.

### MCFAP (perforator-based) — expected advantages<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- No adduction strength loss — gracilis preserved.
- No contour deformity — no muscle removed.
- Concealed scar in the medial groin crease.
- No donor-site morbidities reported in the Huang vulvar series.

### TMG donor data (Siegwart 2021, n = 38)<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

- Normal-to-slightly-diminished superficial skin sensation in 98.4% of thighs.
- Strength and mobility without impairment in &gt; 80%.
- All patients would choose the operation again at 4-y follow-up.

## Position in Reconstructive Algorithms

| Algorithm | Position |
|---|---|
| **Höckel 2008**<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> | MCFA specifically named among arterial systems (with EPA, obturator, gluteal aa.) enabling variation of donor sites from gluteal fold to genitocrural sulcus; gracilis MC listed among 11 vulvovaginal procedures; short gracilis flap (terminal obturator branches, MCFA sacrificed) noted as less-bulky alternative |
| **Huang 2015**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | MCFAP is one of five perforator flap types; selected when perforator location / defect geometry favor MCFA territory; medial / inner thigh = perforator-rich region |
| **Toulouse 2025**<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> | Perforator flaps first-line over musculocutaneous; location-based; MCFAP territory overlaps with broader medial-thigh perforator territory |
| **Han 2023**<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> | Vulvo-thigh junctional crease as decision point — lateral → PAP / TUG (TUG = MCFA-based); medial → IPAP. MCFAP not specifically named but falls within PAP / TUG for lateral defects |

## Advantages

| Advantage | Detail |
|---|---|
| Gracilis muscle preservation | Key advantage over musculocutaneous gracilis — no adduction-strength loss or contour deformity<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Thin, pliable tissue | Ideal for vulvar skin replacement<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |
| Consistent vascular anatomy | MCFA pedicle location familiar; musculocutaneous perforators in 100% of dissections<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| 100% flap survival | Huang vulvar series (8/8 MCFAP)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Concealed donor scar | Medial groin crease<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Primary donor closure | All donor sites closed primarily<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |
| No donor-site morbidities | None reported in vulvar series<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Versatile design | Local pedicled (transposition / V-Y / propeller) or free microsurgical<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |
| Conjoint-flap option | Gracilis can be independently included as a chimeric component if volume / muscle coverage needed<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Large potential skin territory | Up to 18 × 15 cm (standard) or 27 cm (extended via intramuscular anastomosis)<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| Compatible with inguinofemoral LND | MCFA territory not disrupted by standard groin dissection<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> |
| Familiar anatomy | Gracilis pedicle is among the most familiar in reconstructive surgery<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |

## Limitations

| Limitation | Detail |
|---|---|
| Limited published vulvar data | Only 8 MCFAP flaps for vulvar reconstruction (Huang 2015); no dedicated MCFAP vulvar series<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Shorter pedicle | 6.7 cm vs PAP 8.8 cm and infragluteal 12.5 cm — limits arc of rotation for distant defects<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| Smaller angiosome | 74.1 cm² vs PAP 98.5 cm² — limits flap dimensions for large defects<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| Intramuscular perforator dissection | Technically demanding; risks damaging the perforator<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| Variable dominant pedicle origin | Artery of adductors 73% / MCFA 19.2% / both 7.7% — anatomic variability<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Skin-paddle reliability historically variable | Juricic: cutaneous vascularization from the main pedicle satisfactory 55% / poor 25% / absent 20% — though perforator-based approach mitigates this by directly identifying viable perforators<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Not suitable for large defects with pelvic dead space | Thin flap cannot fill dead space — gracilis MC or VRAM preferred<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> |
| Potential overlap with PAP / TUG territory | Territories adjacent and may overlap — limits the use of one if the other is harvested |
| Obturator-territory hypesthesia | Even without muscle sacrifice, perforator dissection through gracilis may cause sensory disturbance<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| Hair-bearing skin | Medial thigh skin may be hair-bearing — undesirable for certain vulvar subunits |

## Key Takeaways

1. The MCFAP flap is a fasciocutaneous perforator flap on musculocutaneous MCFA perforators traversing the gracilis muscle, first described by Hallock 2003 as both local and free flap.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
2. **Perforator-based evolution of the gracilis MC** — preserves the gracilis, eliminating the 11% adduction-strength loss, 40.5% hypesthesia rate, and contour deformity associated with gracilis sacrifice.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
3. In Huang 2015, MCFAP was the **second most commonly used perforator flap** for vulvar reconstruction (8/27, 29.6%), with 100% survival and no donor-site morbidities.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
4. Perforator anatomy is highly reliable — musculocutaneous perforators present in 100% of dissections, ≥ 0.5 mm in 93%.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
5. Provides thin, pliable tissue with a concealed medial-groin scar; flap dimensions up to 18 × 15 cm (standard) or 27 cm (extended).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
6. Vs the PAP flap, MCFAP has a shorter pedicle (6.7 cm) and smaller angiosome (74.1 cm²), but uniquely offers the **conjoint flap option** — gracilis can be independently included for volume.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
7. **Critical limitation** — paucity of published vulvar-specific data; MCFAP remains a niche option vs IPAP, GCIPF, and PAP.
