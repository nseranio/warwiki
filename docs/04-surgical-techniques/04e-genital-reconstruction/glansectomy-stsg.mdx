# Glansectomy With Split-Thickness Skin Graft (STSG)

Glansectomy removes part or all of the glans for selected penile cancers and can be followed by split-thickness skin graft reconstruction of a neoglans. It is one organ-preserving option for appropriately staged disease confined to the glans when wide local excision or glans resurfacing is not suitable.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The treatment objective is oncologically safe resection with an explicit discussion of local-recurrence risk, function, and the possible need for more extensive surgery. This page is not an operative protocol or a substitute for multidisciplinary penile-cancer assessment.

---

## Role in Treatment Selection

For glans-confined invasive disease, the EAU guideline includes wide local excision, partial glansectomy, and total glansectomy with reconstruction among the surgical options. The choice depends on the extent and depth of disease, ability to achieve appropriate oncologic control, pathological assessment, and the person’s priorities.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Issue | Evidence-bounded consideration |
|---|---|
| Disease extent | Glansectomy is considered when lesion characteristics make less extensive local treatment unsuitable; clinically suspected corporal involvement requires a different oncologic approach.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Reconstruction | A split-thickness skin graft is commonly used to reconstruct a neoglans after glansectomy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Local recurrence | Organ preservation may involve a higher local-recurrence risk than more radical surgery, which should be discussed before treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Functional goals | Urinary, sexual, sensory, appearance, and quality-of-life goals require individual counselling; small heterogeneous studies cannot provide a reliable personal forecast.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

## Reconstruction and Follow-up

Graft selection, graft fixation, meatal management, catheter use, wound care, and timing of mobilization are center- and patient-specific technical decisions. The published evidence reviewed here does not establish one operative sequence, margin rule, dressing, or postoperative protocol as universally best.

After organ-sparing penile-cancer surgery, follow-up should include examination for local recurrence and assessment of healing, graft complications, meatal/urethral symptoms, urinary and sexual function, appearance, and need for further treatment. Surveillance intensity is guided by stage, pathology, and the treating oncology team.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Evidence Limits

The 2022 systematic review of glansectomy and reconstruction synthesized mostly small, non-comparative series. Reported local recurrence, graft loss, meatal stenosis, erectile outcomes, and cosmesis varied substantially across studies. These findings support informed counselling and outcome collection, not superiority claims, fixed complication rates, or a standard technique.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
