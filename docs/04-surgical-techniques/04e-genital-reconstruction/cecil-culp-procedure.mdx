# Cecil-Culp Procedure

The **Cecil-Culp procedure** describes a historical staged reconstructive concept in which penile tissue is temporarily positioned against scrotal tissue before later separation and urethral/skin reconstruction. Published reports use related terminology inconsistently, and the available directly reviewed evidence is chiefly from selected hypospadias-fistula repair and a single pediatric trauma case. This page is a historical evidence overview, not a contemporary procedural protocol.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

See [Urethral Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/urethral-reconstruction) for the current atlas and [Principles of Urethral Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/urethral-reconstruction-principles) for general reconstructive framing.

## Directly described contexts

| Context | What was reported | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Complex proximal hypospadias | A 1979 report describes a Cecil urethroplasty modification with concurrent scrotoplasty in a subset of a 13-year hypospadias experience. The abstract identifies difficult reconstruction when the urethral groove is inadequate and scrotal folds are high; it describes caudal scrotal rotation to extend the urethra at the second stage.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | It does not provide a current comparative treatment recommendation, a universal operative sequence, or a reproducible complication rate. |
| Recurrent urethrocutaneous fistula after hypospadias repair | A retrospective series reported 15 boys treated with a modified two-stage Cecil technique from 1994–1999. No recurrent fistula was reported at mean 21-month follow-up (range 1–62 months).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | This selected, small, uncontrolled series cannot establish a general cure rate, superiority over other coverage methods, or long-term outcomes. |
| Severe pediatric penile trauma | One 12-year-old with a close-range shotgun injury underwent repeated debridement, suprapubic marsupialization for eight months, a graft-delay procedure, later graft harvest, and penoplasty tubularization.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | This is a single modified trauma case, not evidence for a standard scrotal technique, timing rule, or trauma algorithm. |

## Contemporary boundary

The 2023 AUA urethral-stricture guideline states that surgeons should not use genital skin for reconstruction in lichen-sclerosus-proven urethral stricture and should monitor for symptomatic recurrence after urethroplasty.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> That guidance does not itself evaluate Cecil-Culp for every historical indication, but it is an important limit on extending a scrotal-skin concept to lichen sclerosus.

No directly reviewed source in this recovery establishes that Cecil-Culp is interchangeable with Johanson, Turner-Warwick, or other staged techniques; that it should precede or replace buccal-mucosa graft reconstruction; or that it is indicated for a defined adult stricture, fistula, or trauma population.

## Evidence limits

- The principal directly reviewed reports are old, small, and noncomparative; the trauma literature recovered here is one case report.
- Exact stage timing, catheter strategy, flap dimensions, tissue planes, complication prevention, and aftercare protocols were removed because they were not supported by the recoverable sources.
- Historical reports from the inherited bibliography on posterior urethral injury, scrotal flaps, and staged stricture reconstruction were not recovered as complete sources in this pass; their numerical outcomes and recommendations are not retained.

## Key takeaways

1. Cecil-Culp is a historical staged tissue-coverage concept rather than a single standardized modern operation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
2. The clearest directly reviewed clinical report is a 15-boy recurrent-fistula series with no recurrence reported over limited follow-up.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
3. It should not be generalized to lichen-sclerosus urethral stricture, where the current AUA guideline advises against genital skin reconstruction.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
