# Penile Skin / Shaft Reconstruction

Penile shaft coverage after infection, inflammatory disease, tumor resection or injury may use primary closure, a skin graft or a flap. Choose according to the viable tissue remaining, recipient-bed vascularity, depth of loss and the need to accommodate erection.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Planning

| Setting | Priority |
|---|---|
| Fournier's or other destructive infection | Control infection and complete debridement before definitive coverage |
| Lichen sclerosus | Define skin and urethral involvement separately; preserve suitable unaffected tissue and select healthy donor skin |
| Avulsion or degloving | Assess urethral, glans and corporal injury in addition to the skin defect |
| Foreign-body granuloma | Define material and disease extent; decide whether wound readiness permits immediate coverage |
| Oncologic resection | Establish appropriate resection margins and match reconstruction to the resulting defect |

These principles do not make every defect a graft indication. Some can close without tension; others need vascularized tissue. The EAU guideline strongly recommends against genital skin for LS-related penile urethral reconstruction, a different application from external shaft coverage.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Reported shaft coverage by setting: Fournier's gangrene defects are mostly covered with split-thickness grafts, and the authors of one 10-year review advise full-thickness grafts on the penis where possible to limit contracture.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Scrotal or regional flaps are used when the graft bed is poor; a review supports full-thickness grafts and pedicled scrotal flaps for penile shaft skin defects.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Graft thickness

STSG covers large areas, and its donor site re-epithelializes. FTSG carries more dermis, which limits secondary contraction, but it needs a better recipient bed and donor size is limited. In one retrospective series of 22 buried-penis graft recipients (9 FTSG, 13 STSG), no significant difference in complications or recurrence was detected; the study cannot establish equivalence.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

Remove nonviable tissue while preserving useful healthy skin and dartos when appropriate. Obtain hemostasis, maintain graft contact and avoid constriction at the corona or base. Skin grafting can improve coverage and function, but sensory changes and contracture remain possible.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Outcomes to assess

Record graft-area take, wound complications, sensation, erection-related tethering or curvature, recurrence, revisions and patient-reported function and appearance. Donor-site morbidity and longer-term results matter alongside early healing. Small observational studies do not establish one graft or flap as best for every shaft defect.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
