# Penile Primary Closure ± Z-Plasty

**Primary closure** covers a penile skin defect by direct approximation when healthy, mobile skin can close it without restricting penile expansion. A local scar release or Z-plasty can redistribute available skin but cannot replace a large missing skin envelope. The underlying problem (skin loss, a superficial contracture, concealment, or deeper penile curvature) is assessed before the operation is selected.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For the wider reconstructive plan, see [Penile Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile), [Penile Skin Grafting](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile-skin-grafting), and [Buried Penis Repair](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/buried-penis-repair).

## Selecting closure

| Finding | Implication |
|---|---|
| Small defect with viable, sufficiently mobile surrounding skin | Direct approximation may be suitable; assess tension with the shaft extended. |
| Superficial linear scar or penoscrotal web with adjacent tissue available | Consider local rearrangement, including Z-plasty. Determine whether the deformity is confined to skin. |
| Healthy dorsal skin with a ventral shortage | Selected rotational or advancement flaps may redistribute skin. |
| Extensive or circumferential nonviable skin | Graft or flap coverage may be needed; forcing direct closure risks tethering and breakdown. |
| Active infection, uncertain viability, or substantial inflammatory edema | Treat the underlying process and individualize the timing of definitive coverage. |

These are tissue-based decisions, not a mandatory sequence of operations. Buried-penis series describe different combinations of release, fixation, local flaps and grafting; their results do not establish one closure for all defects.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

In Tausch's 56-patient referral-center series, every operation began with ventral penile release. When the uncovered shaft skin was viable, penoscrotal skin was anchored to the proximal shaft and the ventral defect was closed with scrotal flaps. Circumferential nonviable skin was excised and replaced with a split-thickness graft. Success, defined as resolution of symptoms without additional procedures, was 49/56 (88%).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### Typical indications

| Scenario | Notes |
|---|---|
| Stage 1 buried penis (Santucci staging) | Only a phimotic band is involved; stages 2 to 5 add skin excision with graft, scrotal excision, escutcheonectomy and panniculectomy<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Concealed penis (pediatric or adult) | Degloving and release of tethering dartos bands with fixation of the penile base, when adequate shaft skin remains<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| Penoscrotal webbing | Z-plasty at the penoscrotal junction reconstructs the penoscrotal angle<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Chordee or hypospadias revision with ventral skin shortage | Z-plasty addresses ventral skin shortage or scar contracture<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Post-circumcision traumatic short penis | Penoscrotal Z-plasty has been combined with suspensory ligament release and suprapubic liposuction<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |

Preserve available skin during planning. In an already circumcised patient with a trapped or buried penis, another circumcision can worsen shaft-skin deficiency. A phimotic appearance alone does not establish that further circumferential skin removal is appropriate.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Operative considerations

- **Expose only as required.** A focal skin defect does not automatically require complete penile degloving. When release or degloving is indicated, preserve viable coverage and protect the urethra and neurovascular structures.
- **Assess skin requirements with the shaft extended.** Check mobility, perfusion, asymmetry and tension before final closure. Stretch helps reveal a shortage that is obscured when the penis is retracted; it does not prove that every closure performed in a flaccid state will fail. Skin sized to the flaccid penis is undersized in erection: in Iblher's four grafted patients, the non-erect to erect area ratio was 50% to 72%.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Closure under tension risks tethering, curvature with erection and wound breakdown.
- **Keep fixation separate from skin closure.** Selected concealed-penis repairs address deficient skin attachments, but anchoring is not obligatory for every primary closure. The described fixation planes vary. Frenkl fixes the subcutaneous skin at the base of the degloved penis to Buck's fascia at 3 and 9 o'clock.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Alter and Ehrlich tack the subdermis of the ventral penoscrotal junction to the tunica albuginea, because hypermobile ventral skin and dartos that let the corpora telescope proximally were a major cause of failure in their experience (13 patients).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> These are different operations, not interchangeable anatomical instructions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Change the coverage plan when necessary.** A wound that can be sutured closed but blanches, distorts the shaft, or cannot accommodate extension needs reassessment. Tighter sutures do not correct the shortage; the reconstruction has to address it.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Routine pharmacologic erection or postoperative tadalafil is not established for simple primary closure. Iblher's report combined intraoperative prostaglandin, postoperative tadalafil and negative-pressure dressings in only four skin-graft patients. It cannot isolate a drug effect or establish a routine drug regimen for this procedure.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### Sequence for primary closure

A general sequence assembled from the buried and concealed penis series follows. Each step varies with the operation chosen.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

1. Release the tethering fibrous dartos bands. Deglove only as far as the deformity requires.
2. Excise diseased tissue (phimotic ring, LS-affected skin, scar) and assess the viability of the remaining skin.
3. Fix the penile base, by one of the methods above, so that the shaft does not retract.
4. Place the penis on stretch, by assistant traction or a pharmacologic erection.
5. Approximate the skin with interrupted absorbable sutures, commonly 4-0 to 5-0, and confirm that the closure is tension-free at full stretch.
6. If dorsal skin is in excess and ventral skin is deficient, redistribute it (Byars-type flaps below).

### Byars-type skin redistribution

Dorsal skin can be divided and rotated ventrally in selected reconstructions. Availability, vascularity, previous operations and the underlying diagnosis determine suitability. A published midline-incision rotation-flap series involved 18 young boys and combined several operative steps; it does not establish the best technique for adult acquired defects.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Z-plasty

A basic Z-plasty transposes two triangular skin flaps of equal dimension to lengthen and redirect a scar. A symmetric 60-degree design has a theoretical 75% gain along the original central limb and approximately 90-degree reorientation; actual tissue gain is smaller and depends on local laxity and tension. This is geometric scar lengthening, not a predicted increase in penile length.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Z-plasty may help a selected superficial contracture or web. It does not correct intrinsic corporal curvature or supply enough tissue for every ventral deficit. Preserve flap perfusion and avoid closure under tension; necrosis, hematoma, wound separation and recurrent tethering remain possible.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

| Variant | Use | Reported experience |
|---|---|---|
| Penoscrotal Z-plasty | Reconstructs the penoscrotal angle and removes webbing | Xu (41 concealed-penis procedures): degloving with penoscrotal angle reconstruction through a longitudinal incision or Z-plasty. Correction was successful in all, median flaccid length gain 2.1 cm, follow-up 6 months to 2 years, no erectile discomfort<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| Lateral shaft Z-plasty | Lateral contracture band with curvature | Used in some of Alter and Ehrlich's patients alongside suprapubic dermatolipectomy and ventral tacking sutures<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Serial or multiple Z-plasties | Longer contracture bands | One of the configurations evaluated in the pediatric series below<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Double-opposing Z-plasty | Bifid scrotum and complex penoscrotal anomalies | One of the configurations evaluated in the pediatric series below<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Penoscrotal Z-plasty with lengthening | Post-circumcision traumatic short penis in adolescents | Combined with suspensory ligament release and suprapubic liposuction: 9 patients, mean erect length gain 23 ± 4 mm at 3 months, no complications, none sexually active<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |

A 2025 retrospective pediatric series reported 98% primary healing among 100 Z-plasty procedures performed for mixed indications, including primary and revision hypospadias. Follow-up was available for 97%, over one to two years. One case (1%) healed by secondary intention after suture-line separation and one (1%) developed a hypertrophic scar. This supports feasibility in that setting; it is not a controlled comparison or an adult durability estimate.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Complications and pitfalls

| Problem | Cause and response |
|---|---|
| Flap necrosis | High wound tension or compromised perfusion; flap sloughing from high tension is a described complication<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Hematoma under the flap | Threatens flap viability; meticulous hemostasis<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Trapdoor effect | Elevation of central tissue as the surrounding scar contracts<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Recurrent concealment | Untreated ventral skin and dartos hypermobility was a major cause of failure in Alter and Ehrlich's report<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>. In Frenkl's 79 patients, repeat buried-penis repair was needed in 3/26 (11.5%) who were circumcised at the time of repair, and 7/49 (14.3%) with circumcision revision had mild recurrence that needed no further treatment<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| Insufficient lengthening | The defect is too large for Z-plasty; convert to a graft or flap<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Acquired curvature or tethering with erection | Closure not assessed on stretch or under tension; reassess early |
| Wound dehiscence | Tension at full stretch; one case in the pediatric Z-plasty series healed by secondary intention<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |

### Ventral slit scrotal flap

For a trapped penis from LS or phimosis with adequate scrotal skin, a ventral slit in the phimotic ring is combined with rotated scrotal flaps to resurface the ventral shaft without skin grafting. In Westerman's 15 consecutive outpatients (13 with LS), 11/15 (73.3%) remained satisfied and needed no further intervention. Three (20.0%) recurred because of LS, panniculus migration or concealment by edematous groin tissue, and 2 of them had subsequent skin grafting.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Follow-up

Individualize dressing, catheter need and activity restrictions to the actual repair and associated urethral work. Check perfusion, wound healing, voiding and emerging tethering. Sexual activity resumes after adequate healing and the operating team's assessment; no validated universal schedule exists.

Painful curvature or recurrent concealment requires assessment of its cause. Neither finding automatically calls for immediate reoperation: distinguish superficial tethering, recurrent inflammation, deeper deformity and wound complications. Continue disease-specific care when lichen sclerosus or another inflammatory condition is present.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
