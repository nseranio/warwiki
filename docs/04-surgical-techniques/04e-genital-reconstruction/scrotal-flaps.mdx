# Scrotal Flap Reconstruction

A **flap** supplies vascularized tissue when the recipient surface cannot support a graft, when substantial dead space needs filling, or when a combined scrotal and perineal defect needs durable coverage. Skin-loss percentage and testicular exposure alone do not dictate a flap. Some extensive or total superficial defects can be grafted, and smaller deep defects may need a flap.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Tissue is chosen by the defect, perfusion, prior surgery, donor availability and patient goals. The [scrotal overview](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/scrotal-reconstruction) compares closure, graft and pouch options.

## Planning principles

- Obtain infection source control and a viable wound before definitive reconstruction. A flap does not cover untreated necrosis.
- Assess the skin area, depth, dead space, reach, thickness and future access to the testes and urethra.
- Preserve the planned arterial inflow and venous drainage. Prior debridement, scars, radiation or inguinal surgery can alter otherwise favorable anatomy.
- Plan positioning, donor closure and graft requirements together. Not every donor site closes primarily, and no single flap gives normal sensation and thermal function.
- Discuss staged contour revision, pain, loss, wound problems and donor morbidity alongside the expected coverage benefit.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Carrera's 15-cadaver study described interconnected lateral territories supplied by the inferior external pudendal arteries and a central territory supplied by continuations of the perineal arteries, which run deeply on both sides of the septum. This anatomy supports median island scrotal flaps. Flap design follows the actual blood supply, and wide undermining is not assumed to be safe for every scrotal flap.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Flap families

| Option | Features and reported experience |
|---|---|
| [Pudendal-thigh / Singapore](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/singapore-pudendal-thigh) | Nearby pliable tissue. Protective sensation was retained in Mopuri's five patients with the modified flap. Reach and donor condition determine suitability.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| [Medial thigh](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/medial-thigh) | Local tissue for advancement or a planned vascular flap. Thickness, pedicle design and bilateral requirements vary with the defect.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| [MCFAP / gracilis perforator](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/mcfap) | A muscle-sparing perforator option. Coskunfirat used it in seven post-Fournier reconstructions, by transposition or V-Y advancement, with direct donor closure. Two wounds dehisced and healed by secondary intention or secondary suturing. The title's claim of superiority rests on this uncontrolled series.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| [Island groin](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/island-groin) | Regional option with adjustable thickness and reach. Sahai's 29 patients (25 Fournier, 4 avulsion) were treated over about 30 years, and all had good aesthetic results.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| [SCIP](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/scip) | Relatively thin skin with a concealed donor. Three post-Fournier patients had surviving flaps without significant complications. Anatomy, reach and donor closure need individual planning.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| [ALT](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/anterolateral-thigh) | Covers larger defects. Thickness, tunnel geometry and donor morbidity matter. Yao's scrotal series had three patients.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| [Posteromedial thigh / vPMT](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/pmtp-propeller) | Perforator-based advancement or propeller designs. Wishart used 21 flaps in 12 patients with perianal, vulvar and scrotal defects (3 scrotal patients). Flaps were 5–9 cm wide and 18–35 cm long, all survived and all donor sites closed primarily.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| [Gracilis](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/gracilis) | Muscle fills dead space. Skin components and perfusion need appropriate design. Singh's 40 perineal reconstructions after abdominoperineal resection or exenteration had donor-site complications in 12.5% and recipient-site complications in 40%.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| [VRAM](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/vram) | Abdominal tissue may suit a larger combined pelvic and perineal defect. Abdominal-wall morbidity, previous operations and stoma plans are considered.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| [IGAP / gluteal fold](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/igap-gluteal-fold) or [posterior thigh](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/posterior-thigh) | Additional regional options for combined defects. Positioning, sitting comfort and prior donor treatment are considered. Unal used SGAP and IGAP flaps in 12 patients with gluteal and perianal hidradenitis, which is not an isolated scrotal population.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |

No comparative data give maximum flap dimensions, a best tissue match or a ranking of complication rates across these techniques.

## Outcome literature

The Alammar 2026 systematic review included 107 reports, 619 patients and 625 flaps after Fournier's gangrene. Flap loss was reported in 1.6% of cases. That endpoint does not capture all wound problems or every partial necrosis, and a flap-only review cannot compare flaps with grafting. Standardized functional and cosmetic outcomes were uncommon.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Perineal oncology cohorts differ from scrotal populations. Singh's gracilis cohort followed abdominoperineal resection or pelvic exenteration. Obesity (odds ratio 7.5) and active smoking (9.3) were associated with minor complications, and neoadjuvant chemoradiation (21.4) with any complication. The authors judged the overall complication rate comparable to VRAM, with less donor-site morbidity. The series does not rank gracilis or VRAM for scrotal reconstruction.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

**Fertility and sexual function.** Yao reported no significant change in sperm count or IIEF in three ALT recipients, without a graft comparator. Demir's rat experiment found testicular function after flap reconstruction comparable to controls and worse function after grafting. Erectile scores are not fertility measures, and no human study shows that flaps preserve spermatogenesis better than grafts or that flap bulk restores scrotal thermoregulation.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Distinct clinical applications

### Extramammary Paget's disease

Kim reported 46 patients with margin-controlled excision. Scrotal defects were reconstructed with internal pudendal artery perforator (IPAP) and scrotal flaps, suprapubic defects with SEPAP flaps and penile defects with skin grafts, often in combination. All but four were reconstructed successfully with these combinations, and one had marginal flap necrosis. This is an institutional algorithm. The oncological margin and surveillance plan take priority.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

### Penile coverage using scrotal tissue

The **sensate EPAP hemi-scrotal flap** transfers healthy scrotal tissue to a penile defect. Tsukuura described one 40-year-old man after penile replantation, using a 9 × 14 cm flap planned with Doppler ultrasound. The external pudendal artery perforator and the anterior scrotal nerve were isolated, and the donor site closed primarily. At seven months the flap had good color match and sensation, and the patient had no pain or stretching.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> See [EPAP hemi-scrotal flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/epap-hemi-scrotal-flap).

### Genital lymphedema

Coverage and lymphatic reconstruction are separate objectives. Abdelfattah used a functional lymphatic SCIP-based transfer in 26 patients after excision of fibrotic tissue. Flap survival was 100%, cellulitis fell and all reported quality-of-life improvement. Ehrl's nine-patient series included eight giant cases and one with pitting edema. Five received VLNT to the groin or scrotum, and no recurrence was seen in the whole cohort at median 49 months. These uncontrolled series do not isolate the added benefit of VLNT.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

The International Society of Lymphology consensus supports individualized treatment, continuing skin care and decongestive management, and counseling about donor-site lymphedema and other morbidity when lymphatic transfer is considered.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

### Combined muscle and skin transfer

TUGPAP combines upper gracilis and profunda perforator tissue. Ciudad's 28-patient report covered breast, head and neck, and pelviperineal defects. The flaps used two vascular pedicles with recipient anastomoses, so TUGPAP is a microsurgical option and not a routine single-pedicle local scrotal operation.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
