# Reverse Bilateral Anterior Scrotal Artery Flap (Gao / Qu)

This is a **single published case report**, not an established standard technique for penile resurfacing. Gao and Qu described use of a reverse bilateral anterior scrotal flap after debridement for complete penile-skin loss following a complicated circumcision.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The report describes an intact result in that individual at long follow-up, but it cannot establish comparative effectiveness, reproducibility, or a general indication.

For the broader reconstructive framework and alternatives, see [Penile Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile), [Bipedicled Anterior Scrotal Flap (Fakin)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/bipedicled-anterior-scrotal-flap), [Modified Bipedicle Scrotal Tunnel Flap (Murányi)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/muranyi-scrotal-tunnel-flap), and [Ventral Slit Scrotal Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/ventral-slit-scrotal-flap).

## Reported case

The authors reported a 31-year-old man presenting 20 days after circumcision with infection, necrosis, and extensive penile-skin loss. After debridement, they used bilateral anterior scrotal tissue, reversed and wrapped around the shaft, with primary donor-site closure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The article reports uncomplicated healing, intercourse at two months, and favorable aesthetic and functional photographs at two and ten years.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Interpretation limits

- This is evidence from **one patient**; the published outcome is not an expected result for another patient.
- The case report does not compare this flap with grafting, antegrade scrotal flaps, staged reconstruction, or other options.
- The report does not support a standardized operative protocol, vascular-selection rule, complication rate, or universal salvage indication.
- Choice of reconstruction requires individualized assessment of tissue loss, infection control, local vascularity, donor tissue, patient goals, and surgeon expertise.
