# Reverse Bilateral Anterior Scrotal Artery Flap (Gao / Qu)

The **reverse bilateral anterior scrotal artery flap** is Gao and Qu's single-stage bilateral scrotal-skin resurfacing method. In their open case report, the anterior scrotal flaps were elevated, reversed in orientation and wrapped from the dorsal to ventral shaft; the schematic calls them pedicled on the bilateral anterior scrotal arteries. The report does not describe proximal arterial ligation, demonstrate retrograde arterial flow, or establish that the flap can bypass a damaged scrotal-root pedicle. One patient with post-circumcision skin loss healed without reported complications through ten years and reported normal erections and intercourse.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For antegrade single-stage scrotal flaps, see [Fakin](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/bipedicled-anterior-scrotal-flap), [Murányi](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/muranyi-scrotal-tunnel-flap), [Yao butterfly](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/yao-butterfly-scrotal-flap), and [Total Anterior Scrotal Flap (Zhao)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/total-anterior-scrotal-flap). For staged or outpatient variants, see [Pribaz / McLaughlin staged](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/staged-scrotal-flap) and [VSSF](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/ventral-slit-scrotal-flap). For full framework, see [Penile Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile).

---

## Rationale

Gao uses “reverse” to describe flap reversal and wrapping. The reported schematic still depicts an anterior-scrotal-artery pedicle. No angiography, arterial clamp test or vessel-division sequence in the report establishes a reverse-flow circuit. Preserve the useful scrotal vascular-anatomy context below, but do not use it to infer that an injured proximal pedicle can be safely sacrificed.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Reverse-flow flaps elsewhere depend on an adequate arterial and venous circuit; in Torii's series of 22 extremity reverse-flow island flaps, 16 survived completely, 4 had partial necrosis and 2 had total necrosis.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Carrera and Giraldo describe interconnections in scrotal and perineogenital vasculature, but neither the density of those connections relative to extremities nor patient-level reverse perfusion for the Gao design was tested in this case.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Vascular Anatomy Enabling Reverse Flow

### Three Carrera scrotal territories<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

| Source artery | Origin | Territory |
|---|---|---|
| **Anterior scrotal arteries** | Inferior external pudendal → femoral | Two lateral territories (each enters at the midpoint of the scrotal root and fans out over its hemiscrotum) |
| **Posterior scrotal arteries** | Perineal → internal pudendal | Central territory (enter posteriorly along the scrotal septum) |

### Inter-territorial anastomoses — anatomical context, not a measured Gao flow path

- Boundaries between the three territories carry **choke anastomoses** (Taylor angiosome concept)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Giraldo identified three major perineogenital anastomotic circuits at the penile base, the scrotal septum, and the perineal fat / lateral spermatic-scrotal fascia<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Interconnections may support alternative flap designs, but the Gao case did not quantify their density or demonstrate a retrograde route

### Reported design versus a theoretical reverse-flow mechanism

- **Antegrade (normal):** external pudendal, then inferior external pudendal, then anterior scrotal artery, then scrotal skin
- **Gao's reported design:** anterior scrotal skin, reversed in orientation and wrapped onto the shaft, with the schematic labeling bilateral anterior scrotal artery pedicles<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Hypothetical salvage circuit, not established by Gao:** posterior scrotal, then inter-territorial connections, then anterior scrotal territory. Do not equate the flap's reversed orientation with measured retrograde flow or recommend proximal arterial division from this case.

## Indications

| Indication | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Complete circumferential penile skin loss** | The Gao case |
| **Post-circumcision skin necrosis after debridement** | Reported in one patient; not a frequency estimate |
| **Viable anterior scrotal skin and pedicles** | Required for the reported design; assess each patient |

### Contraindications

- **Compromised donor-skin perfusion or unverified pedicle.** Assess vascular anatomy before selecting the flap
- **Scrotal skin disease:** LS, lymphedema
- **Prior scrotal surgery** when it may have damaged the intended donor skin or pedicle; not an automatic exclusion without assessment
- **Insufficient scrotal skin**

## Operative Technique

The open case text and schematic support the 6 × 6 cm anterior-scrotal flap, reversed dorsal-to-ventral wrapping, ventral Z-suture and anti-retraction stay sutures. They do not verify the exact elevation plane, bilateral pedicle dissection, Foley/dressing protocol or deliberate arterial division. Confirm figure-dependent steps before operative use.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### 1. Debridement of the penile defect

- Excise all infected, necrotic or non-viable penile skin to Buck's fascia
- Irrigate; hemostasis
- Gao case: 20 days post-circumcision, infection, necrosis and complete skin loss; debrided before flap design<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### 2. Reversed-flap design

- Two bilateral scrotal flaps, one on each hemiscrotum
- Anterior-scrotal donor flaps; the published schematic labels bilateral anterior scrotal arterial pedicles
- The reported flap measured 6 × 6 cm for a 5 cm-wide circumferential defect; adapt dimensions to the actual defect and available donor tissue

### 3. Preserve and assess the vascular pedicles

- Identify the intended supply and maintain perfusion during elevation and transfer
- Do not ligate or divide the anterior scrotal arteries based on Gao's report. Neither the case text nor the schematic documents that step, and the schematic instead labels anterior-artery pedicles<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### 4. Flap elevation

- The case describes anterior-scrotal flap dissection but does not specify a reproducible superior-to-inferior sequence or confirm the precise fascial plane in accessible text
- Preserve viable scrotal tissue and vascular connections; consult the original operative images and individual anatomy before adopting an elevation plane

### 5. Flap rotation and wrapping

- Reverse the elevated flap orientation and wrap around the penile shaft from dorsal to ventral, as described in the case<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Confirm that the flap lies without tension or vascular compromise; the precise bilateral skin arrangement is figure-dependent

### 6. Inset

- A ventral Z-suture was used to limit contracture or ventral curvature, and stay sutures into the tunica albuginea were used to limit scarring or retraction in this case<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Distal and proximal edges and suture material should follow the actual operative plan; these particulars were not specified in the accessible case text

### 7. Donor closure

- The schematic depicts primary scrotal donor-site closure; confirm available tissue and tension in the individual patient<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### 8. Dressing and monitoring

- Standard flap monitoring, including venous congestion, is prudent; Gao does not report a Foley/dressing regimen or establish a scrotal reverse-flow complication distribution

## Reverse-Flow Physiology — General Background, Not Demonstrated in Gao

### Arterial inflow

- In Tanaka and Tajima's rat island-flap experiments, viable flap area diminished in proportion to the decrease in blood flow when arterial inflow was retrograde (correlation between blood flow and survival r = 0.885)<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Adequate retrograde inflow depends on the caliber and density of inter-territorial anastomoses
- Scrotal vascular interconnections are anatomically described, but the Gao case did not measure retrograde arterial inflow<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Venous drainage — the Achilles heel

- In the same rat experiments, the relationship between venous outflow and viability was "all or nothing": retrograde venous outflow caused either no effect or complete congestive necrosis<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- In Torii's cadaver and amputated-limb studies of extremity reverse-flow flaps, venous blood drained by:
  1. Valve reflux at pressures of 90–105 cmH₂O
  2. Communicating branches between venae comitantes
  3. Bypass vessels around valves<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Scrotal venous return has anterior and posterior routes; the actual venous drainage route in Gao was not measured. Extremity reverse-flow failure rates must not be presented as this flap's risk.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Choke-vessel conversion

- Choke anastomoses convert to true anastomoses through surgical delay or acute hemodynamic stress<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- In rabbit and dog delay studies, early vasoconstriction resolved within about 3 hours and was followed by progressive choke-vessel dilation that peaked at 48–72 h. Long-term studies showed permanent dilation<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- In a rat multi-territory flap model, HIF-1α, iNOS and VEGF expression rose after flap transfer, and hypoxia-induced VEGF and iNOS upregulation was HIF-1α dependent in cultured endothelial cells<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- These experimental timing and mechanism data do not establish a postoperative retrograde-perfusion course or safe pedicle-division interval for Gao's flap<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

|---|---|
| Patient | 31-year-old male |
| Etiology | Complete penile skin loss 20 d after circumcision (infection + necrosis) |

| **Flap survival** | Complete in this **one** patient |
| Complications | **None** |

| Cosmesis | Satisfactory |

## Advantages

1. **Reversed flap orientation.** Provided coverage in one post-circumcision case; bypass of a compromised proximal pedicle was not demonstrated
2. **Single-stage**
3. **No skin graft required**
4. **Observed long-term result.** Ten-year complication-free follow-up in one patient with reported erection and intercourse
5. **Scrotal vascular connections.** Useful anatomical context, not proof that this design has superior perfusion to other flaps
6. **Scrotal skin quality** matches native shaft skin

---

## Limitations and Disadvantages

1. **n = 1 evidence base.** Every conclusion about safety and efficacy is from a single patient
2. **Vascular mechanism not measured.** Extremity reverse-flow necrosis rates and experimental venous behavior cannot quantify this scrotal flap's risk<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
3. **Congestion or ischemia remains a flap-safety concern.** Monitor perfusion; avoid inferred arterial division
4. **Technical detail incomplete.** Exact vessel handling and tissue plane require original operative-image interpretation and patient-specific vascular assessment
5. **Prior perineal surgery, radiation or trauma** may affect donor-site vascularity but are not automatic contraindications established by this one case
6. **No standardized PROMs**
7. **Unknown complication profile** at the population level
8. **Donor-site and late morbidity.** Testicular ascension and retraction are worth monitoring; Mendel's percentages come from a different bilateral-flap cohort and are not Gao-specific or universal class-effect rates<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
9. **No comparison with antegrade techniques.** Whether reverse-flow offers any advantage in patients with intact proximal pedicles is unclear

---

## Patient Selection — Interpreting the Single Case

The report supports consideration of a scrotal flap when:

- Debridement has produced a suitable wound bed and viable scrotal donor tissue or pedicles remain
- **Circumferential penile coverage** is required
- The surgeon can assess actual pedicle anatomy and flap perfusion; the published case does not authorize sacrificing anterior scrotal inflow

Compare other local flap or graft options by defect, donor skin and vascularity. Gao does not establish that this method is a salvage option when the proximal anterior-scrotal pedicle is unavailable.

## Key Takeaways

- Gao's reversed scrotal-flap orientation achieved complete coverage in one case; retrograde arterial flow and proximal-pedicle bypass were not demonstrated
- The original schematic labels bilateral anterior scrotal artery pedicles; do not infer or perform proximal arterial division from this report
- Scrotal interconnections are anatomical context, not evidence of a measured reverse-flow/venous-drainage circuit in this patient
- **Level V evidence (single case).** Every recommendation should be interpreted accordingly; broader adoption requires case-series validation
- Choose among local flaps and grafts according to the actual defect, donor tissue, perfusion and patient goals; no comparative ranking follows from the Gao case
