# Modified Bipedicle Scrotal Tunnel Flap (Murányi Technique)

The Murányi technique is a reported single-stage bipedicle scrotal flap for shaft coverage after excision of paraffin-induced sclerosing lipogranuloma. It uses a scrotal tunnel to transfer coverage to the penile shaft. Its evidence comes from a single-institution cohort and does not establish a standard procedure, expected outcome, or applicability to other causes of penile skin loss.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For related approaches, see [Bipedicled Anterior Scrotal Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/bipedicled-anterior-scrotal-flap), [Penile Skin Grafting](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile-skin-grafting), and [Penile Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile).

---

## Reported Context

The source report describes a scrotal-tunnel flap after treatment of paraffin-induced penile lipogranuloma.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Its technical details, selection criteria, full complication definitions, and follow-up instruments were not recovered in this audit. This page therefore does not prescribe a dissection plane, flap dimensions, closure pattern, perioperative pathway, or postoperative regimen.

## Clinical Boundary

Paraffin-induced or other foreign-body penile injury requires individualized assessment of tissue involvement, infection or inflammation, viable coverage options, scrotal-tissue availability, and the person’s priorities. A result in a selected paraffinoma cohort should not be generalized to silicone injury, trauma, Fournier gangrene, oncologic defects, or circumcision-related skin loss without direct evidence.

Scrotal-flap reconstruction also has donor- and recipient-site trade-offs, including hair-bearing tissue, wound healing, contour, and possible need for further treatment. The appropriate approach is determined by the treating reconstructive team and the underlying pathology.

## What Is Not Established

- A preferred tunnel, flap, vessel-preservation, closure, or postoperative technique.
- Superiority to another scrotal-flap, staged reconstruction, or graft pathway.
- A reliable rate of complications, intercourse, erectile function, pain, satisfaction, or revision.
- Effectiveness for indications beyond the reported paraffinoma cohort.
