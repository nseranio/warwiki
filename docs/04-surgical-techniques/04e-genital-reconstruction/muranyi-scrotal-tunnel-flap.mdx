# Modified Bipedicle Scrotal Tunnel Flap (Murányi Technique)

The **Murányi modified bipedicle scrotal tunnel flap** is a single-stage penile skin reconstruction developed for paraffin-induced sclerosing lipogranuloma. It uses a subcutaneous scrotal tunnel for penile pull-through and an inverted-V ventral closure to avoid the historical T-junction. In the 49-patient single-surgeon series, 13/49 (26.5%) had a postoperative complication. Among the 30 of 49 patients who completed the questionnaire, 27 (90%) were satisfied or very satisfied and all reported intercourse ability. The 19 nonrespondents are not represented.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For the related single-stage flap relying on a central window rather than a tunnel, see [Bipedicled Anterior Scrotal Flap (Fakin)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/bipedicled-anterior-scrotal-flap). For the graft alternative, see [Penile Skin Grafting](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile-skin-grafting). For the full decision framework, see [Penile Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile).

---

## Rationale

Penile paraffinoma / sclerosing lipogranuloma requires complete excision of all infiltrated skin and subcutaneous tissue, often leaving a circumferential shaft defect.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The Murányi modification was designed to address limitations of prior techniques:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Prior approach | Limitation |
|---|---|
| Jeong 1996 bilateral scrotal flaps | Complex flap design; T-style ventral-coronal anastomosis prone to necrosis<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| Pribaz two-stage scrotal flap | Second operation, prolonged recovery<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| STSG / FTSG | Suboptimal when residual foreign material on the corpora prevents graft take<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Fakin bipedicled anterior scrotal flap | Requires central window in the elevated flap + circumferential wrap<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |

The tunnel lets scrotal skin envelop the shaft without the separate bilateral-flap rotation used in some other techniques. Operative simplicity relative to those techniques has not been measured.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Indications

- **Paraffin-induced sclerosing lipogranuloma.** The prototype indication (all 49 patients in the Murányi series)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Requires adequate remaining scrotal skin and blood supply. The series included selected patients with penoscrotal disease (stage 4a) and excluded anterior or whole-scrotal infiltration (stage 4b), which precluded this flap.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Circumferential penile shaft defect after radical excision
- Other causes of circumferential loss (Fournier's gangrene, circumcision injury, oncologic excision) are conceivable indications, but the published evidence is limited to paraffinoma

## Operative Technique

### 1. Circumferential incisions and lipogranuloma excision

- Two circumferential incisions: subcoronal and at the penile base proximal to the affected penile skin.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Fully denude the shaft by excising all lipogranuloma-involved penile skin and subcutaneous tissue in the plane between Buck's fascia and involved dartos; use a dorsal longitudinal incision for firm, thickened skin. Remove involved mons pubis or scrotal lipogranulomas in stage 3–4a disease.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Meticulous hemostasis

### 2. Horizontal scrotal incision

- Transverse scrotal incision positioned and sized from the measured denuded-shaft length and circumferences instead of a fixed 2–3 cm offset. The author manuscript gives the incision length as half the denuded-shaft circumference and locates it by a geometry calculation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### 3. Subcutaneous tunnel creation

- Tunnel dissected between the horizontal scrotal incision and the proximal penile circumferential incision
- Plane is between scrotal skin / dartos and external spermatic fascia / tunica vaginalis
- Tunnel must be wide enough to accommodate the shaft without compression
- **Both lateral pedicles preserved** within the scrotal skin; bilateral external-pudendal / anterior-scrotal-artery blood supply maintained

### 4. Penile pull-through

- The denuded shaft is pulled through the tunnel from the penoscrotal junction, emerging through the horizontal scrotal incision
- Scrotal skin naturally drapes circumferentially around the shaft

### 5. Subcoronal and dorsal suture lines

- Distal scrotal-skin edge sutured to the subcoronal skin circumferentially
- Longitudinal dorsal suture line where the scrotal-skin edges meet on the dorsal shaft
- Interrupted absorbable sutures

### 6. Ventral inverted-V incision and longitudinal closure

- On the ventral scrotal skin, place the inverted-V apex 1 cm from the subcoronal suture; position its parallel limbs half the measured denuded-shaft circumference from the dorsal midline on each side. Deepen to the tunica vaginalis and free the testes from the flap as described by Murányi.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Inverted V serves two purposes:
  - Defines the lateral border of the scrotal skin flap before longitudinal ventral closure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
  - The horizontal scrotal incision forms the subcoronal anastomosis; the separate inverted-V incision closes longitudinally as the ventral midline suture.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- This step adapts the Shin inverted-V principle to avoid the T-junction associated with necrosis in some bilateral-flap reports. Necrosis prevention has not been shown.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### 7. Donor-site closure

- Remaining scrotal skin closed primarily
- Testes covered by posterior and lateral scrotal skin

## How It Differs From Other Scrotal-Flap Techniques

| Feature | Murányi tunnel | Fakin bipedicled<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | Jeong bilateral<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | Shin inverted-V<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flap design | **Tunnel pull-through** | Central window in flap | Two separate hemiscrotum flaps | Two hemiscrotum flaps |
| Staging | Single | Single | Single | Single |
| Dorsal suture line | Longitudinal dorsal | Circumferential at base + coronal | T-style at corona | Dorsal only |
| Ventral closure | **Inverted V → longitudinal** | Circumferential | T-style (necrosis-prone) | Inverted V (end-to-end) |
| Tunnel creation | **Yes (defining feature)** | No | No | No |
| Series size | 49 | 43 | 17 | 34 (14 inverted-V) |

## Outcomes — Murányi Series (n = 49)

### Perioperative (2017–2020)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- All paraffin-induced sclerosing lipogranuloma; all single-stage

### Complications (Clavien-Dindo)

- **Patients with a complication: 26.5%** (13 / 49); there were 14 graded events among these patients<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Grade 1 (no intervention): 5 events
- Grade 2: 0
- **Grade 3a (intervention without general anesthesia): 8 events.** The manuscript also reports marginal flap necrosis in 8/49 patients (7 subcoronally, 1 near the midscrotal suture line), managed with debridement and local wound care before secondary healing.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Grade 3b (intervention under general anesthesia): 1 scrotal hematoma evacuation on postoperative day 2<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Grade 4–5: 0 (no life-threatening complications, no deaths)

### Patient-Reported Outcomes (n = 30 questionnaire respondents)

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Satisfied or very satisfied | **90% (27 / 30)**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Intercourse ability | **30 / 30 respondents**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Erectile dysfunction | 6.7% (2 / 30)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Pain / tension on erection | 10% (3 / 30)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Premature ejaculation | 3.3% (1 / 30)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Penile lymphedema | 3.3% (1 / 30)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |

## Comparative Context

| Outcome | Murányi (n = 49) | Fakin bipedicled (n = 43) | Mendel bilateral (n = 22) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Patients with any complication | 13/49 (26.5%) | Not reported as a comparable patient-level composite<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | Early and late events cannot be added into a patient-level composite<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Wound disruption / dehiscence | Two short wound-disruption events were reported (one penoscrotal, one subcoronal); a separate patient count was not given, so these cannot be compared directly with patient rates<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 19% partial wound disruption<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | 31.8% suture dehiscence<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Erectile dysfunction | 2/30 respondents<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | All in Fakin reported erection ability; standardized ED comparison unavailable<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | n/r |
| Pain / tension on erection | 3/30 respondents<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | n/r | n/r |
| Intercourse ability | 30/30 respondents<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | All in Fakin reported ability; different follow-up<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | n/r |
| Patient satisfaction | 27/30 respondents satisfied/very satisfied<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 4.37 / 5 mean<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | 8 / 10 median global satisfaction among 12/22 respondents<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |

### Single-stage vs two-stage data (Lumbiganon: 35 flap patients compared among 42 treated)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

- Postoperative fever: single-stage 56.5% versus two-stage 8.3% (RR 6.78, 95% CI 1.01–43.83), in a retrospective comparison of 23 single-stage and 12 two-stage patients<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- Without Clavien-Dindo surgical complications: single-stage 43.5% versus two-stage 83.3% (RR 0.53, 95% CI 0.31–0.89)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- Hospital stay: single-stage 7.4 ± 3.2 d versus two-stage 10.9 ± 1.6 d

These columns do not rank the techniques. The Murányi, Fakin, Mendel and Lumbiganon cohorts differ in patient selection, procedures, outcome definitions and follow-up. Lumbiganon's 56.5% is a postoperative fever rate in one arm and not an overall complication rate.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> The Murányi authors caution that cross-technique complication comparisons are difficult.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Advantages

1. **Single-stage.** Avoids second operation and psychological burden of a buried penis
2. **Simplified flap design.** No central window or complex bilateral rotation
3. **Avoids the T-junction.** Inverted-V ventral closure separates suture intersections. A reduction in complications has not been shown in comparative data
4. **Preserved bilateral blood supply.** Both external-pudendal pedicles intact within the scrotal tunnel
5. **Natural skin draping.** Scrotal skin conforms to the shaft as it passes through the tunnel
6. **Vascularized coverage** after excision of the involved material. Residual foreign substance on the corpora is not a reason to leave diseased tissue behind

---

## Disadvantages and Limitations

- **Requires adequate scrotal skin and perfusion.** The authors excluded anterior or whole-scrotal infiltration (stage 4b), but included selected stage 4a penoscrotal involvement<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Pain or tension on erection (3/30 questionnaire respondents, 10%).**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Tethering at the penoscrotal junction is a possible mechanism
- **Erectile dysfunction (2/30 questionnaire respondents).**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Mechanism and risk relative to the Fakin flap are unknown
- **Grade 3a events.** Eight interventions without general anesthesia among the 49 operated patients; the manuscript reports events and patients separately
- **Hair-bearing skin.** Scrotal hair density varies; Jeong noted that a hirsute scrotum may be a contraindication to bilateral scrotal flaps<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Testicular position.** Two of 30 Murányi questionnaire respondents reported that their testes sometimes slid up beside the penile shaft; scrotal volume change was not quantified. Testicular ascension occurred in 22.7% after a different bilateral-flap technique in the Mendel series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Single-etiology evidence.** All 49 patients were paraffinoma; applicability to other etiologies is extrapolated

## Adjunctive Refinements

### Kim Y-V advancement for length preservation

Y-V advancement at the dorsal penile base and partial suspensory ligament resection were added to a bipedicled scrotal flap. In 5 patients there was no penile shortening and all were satisfied with the appearance.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

### Shin inverted-V anastomosis

Shin compared T-style with inverted-V ventral closure in 34 patients (14 in the inverted-V group). All 14 inverted-V flaps survived without ventral skin necrosis. The Murányi technique incorporates this principle in step 6.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### Kang single-stage NPWT + dermal substitute + STSG

For penile foreign-body granuloma when scrotal-flap coverage is unsuitable or a graft is preferred, Kang described single-stage NPWT with MatriDerm Flex and STSG in a mixed-material cohort; paraffin-only outcomes were not reported. In 11 patients, near-complete graft take at the first dressing change on postoperative day 5 was 90.9% (10 of 11), and median satisfaction was 37 of 45.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Patient Selection — When a Murányi Tunnel May Be Considered

| Choose Murányi tunnel | Choose two-stage scrotal flap | Choose Fakin bipedicled |
|---|---|---|
| Circumferential paraffinoma defect with adequate remaining scrotal skin/perfusion | Wound requiring further debridement or infection control before definitive coverage | Surgeon experience with central-window technique |
| Single-stage preferred after individualized wound assessment | After bulky excision in stage 3–4a disease, residual scrotal blood supply may preclude the one-stage tunnel; the authors used Cecil-type staging in selected cases<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Alternative one-stage design judged better for the defect |
| Extent of foreign-body infiltration and diseased tissue mapped for excision | Staged embedding chosen for patient-specific reasons | Graft or other flap considered on its own wound-bed merits |
| Surgeon experienced with tunnel dissection | Patient accepts a second operation | No reliable comparative superiority established |

---

## Key Takeaways

- The subcutaneous tunnel and inverted-V ventral closure define the technique.
- It is a single-stage tunnel approach with distinct suture geometry; ease relative to other flaps is unproven.
- Among 30 of 49 questionnaire respondents, all reported intercourse ability, two reported ED and three reported pain or tension on erection. Nonresponders are unreported.
- Eight grade 3a events occurred among 13 patients with complications. Wound morbidity remains clinically relevant.
- This is a 49-patient single-surgeon paraffinoma cohort. Eight had no visit after discharge; the other 41 had mean clinical follow-up of 20.0 months. The 30/49 questionnaire respondents answered at a mean of 26.9 months, and nonresponse limits interpretation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
