# Mañero Vaginal Mucosal Graft — Clitorolabial Reconstruction After FGM/C

The Mañero technique is a reported clitorolabial reconstruction after FGM/C that uses a vaginal mucosal graft. It has been described in a single prospective cohort and should be understood as an investigational, specialized reconstructive approach—not a standardized alternative with established comparative benefit.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For broader context, see [Foldès Clitoral Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/foldes-clitoral-reconstruction), [aOAP Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/aoap-flap), and the [Vulvar Reconstruction Atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar).

---

## Reported Approach

The published report describes use of a vaginal graft in conjunction with clitoral and labial reconstruction after FGM/C.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The full operative report, graft-harvest details, healing data, complications, and patient-level outcomes were not recovered in this audit. This page therefore does not specify tissue dimensions, dissection, fixation, donor-site management, or postoperative protocol.

## Evidence and Uncertainty

The initial cohort reported changes in sexual-function and quality-of-life measures, but a single uncontrolled cohort cannot determine whether changes result from the graft, other surgical components, counselling, selection, natural recovery, or measurement effects.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

A 2026 systematic review of clitoral reconstructive surgery identified very-low-certainty evidence across reported outcomes. The predominance of observational work, incomplete follow-up, bias, and limited comparative evidence preclude a technique ranking or a reliable estimate of individual benefit or harm.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Care Boundary

Decisions about reconstructive surgery after FGM/C require person-centred counselling, informed consent, and assessment within an experienced multidisciplinary setting. Surgery should not be presented as the only response to FGM/C-related physical, sexual, or psychological concerns. Suitability, potential benefit, and the risks of both recipient and donor sites are individual clinical decisions.

## What Is Not Established

- That a vaginal graft is the only or best alternative to another reconstructive approach.
- A preferred graft-harvest method, operative sequence, postoperative regimen, or donor-site risk profile.
- Superiority over Foldès-style surgery, local flaps, non-surgical support, or another intervention.
- A predictable effect on sexual function, appearance, sensation, quality of life, or revision need.
