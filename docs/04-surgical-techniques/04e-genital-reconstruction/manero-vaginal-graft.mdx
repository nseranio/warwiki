# Mañero Vaginal Mucosal Graft — Clitorolabial Reconstruction After FGM/C

The **Mañero vaginal mucosal graft technique** is a described approach to clitorolabial reconstruction after FGM/C by Iván Mañero and Trinidad Labanca (Barcelona, Spain; 2018). It uses a **free vaginal mucosal graft** to cover the repositioned clitoris and adjacent labial region. It was the **one vaginal-graft study among 13** included in the 2026 Meremikwu clitoral-reconstruction review—not the only published alternative operation worldwide.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In an uncontrolled series of 32 consecutive women, the original abstract reports FSFI **16 → 29 (P &lt; .05)** and Female Genital Self-Image Scale **11 → 23 (P &lt; .05)**. It does not report a separate quality-of-life endpoint or demonstrate a comparative treatment effect.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For the broader treatment menu see the [Vulvar Reconstruction Atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar). For the workhorse FGM/C reconstruction technique see [Foldès Clitoral Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/foldes-clitoral-reconstruction); for the aOAP-flap variant used for FGM/C vulvovestibular and LSA reconstruction see [aOAP Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/aoap-flap).

The **2025 WHO guideline** conditionally suggests clitoral reconstruction for selected women (very-low-certainty evidence), with sexual-health counseling and multidisciplinary support; it does not preferentially recommend this graft over Foldès-style or other coverage methods.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

---

## Background and Rationale

A limitation of exposing the residual clitoris without dedicated coverage is prolonged re-epithelialization or discomfort in some patients. A free graft offers a different tissue source when local coverage is limited, but its benefit over other approaches has not been directly compared.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> A detailed contemporary technical review describes the Mañero graft as harvested from the **posterior vaginal wall** (approximately 3–4 × 2 cm), then placed over the neoclitoris toward the labia majora; a **lateral** vaginal graft described elsewhere in that review is the Swedish authors' separate modification, not Mañero's donor site.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

Rationale for using vaginal mucosa specifically:

| Property | Detail |
|---|---|
| Tissue choice | Vaginal mucosa is a supple coverage tissue; whether it gives better sensation, comfort or appearance than other coverage methods is untested<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| Concealed donor site | Harvest avoids a visible external donor scar, but the original abstract does not specify donor-wound closure or quantify donor-site morbidity<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Availability | The vaginal canal can provide mucosa when external local tissue is limited; suitability depends on individual anatomy and donor-site risks<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |

## Surgical Technique

The original abstract confirms a vaginal graft but does not publish the operative details in accessible abstract text. The sequence below retains **technical-review descriptions** of Mañero's operation; graft thickness, exact sutures, donor-wound closure and postoperative protocol require the original full paper before they can be taught as source-verified specifics.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

### Step 1 — Clitoral stump exposure and mobilization

- Vulvar scar tissue overlying the clitoral stump is excised; the reviewed technical summary does not establish a universal anesthesia regimen.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- Residual clitoral body identified and dissected free from surrounding fibrosis.
- Suspensory ligament transected to allow downward mobilization of the clitoral stump (as in Foldès).
- Dorsal neurovascular bundle carefully preserved throughout.

### Step 2 — Neoglans fashioning

- Distal end of the mobilized clitoral body shaped into a neoglans.
- Neoclitoris sutured into anatomical position with anti-retraction fixation sutures.

### Step 3 — Vaginal mucosal graft harvest

- A technical review describes a **posterior vaginal mucosal graft approximately 3–4 × 2 cm**. Confirm the harvest plane, graft thickness and donor-wound closure in the original operative paper before using those details as a protocol.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

### Step 4 — Graft placement for clitoral and labial reconstruction

- Vaginal mucosal graft trimmed and tailored to the recipient site.
- Used to cover the **neoclitoris and extend toward the labia majora**, with clitorolabial reconstruction described in the original abstract. This does not prove recreation of an innervated native prepuce or labium minus.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- Graft fixation is required for take, but the exact suture, dressing and immobilization regimen must be checked against the original full text before use.

### Step 5 — Closure and postoperative care

- The review describes a urinary catheter and bed rest for five days in that cohort; these are **reported study aftercare**, not a validated standard for every vaginal-graft case.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- Donor-site closure, analgesia details and longer-term graft sensation remain insufficiently verified from the accessible original abstract.

## Published Outcomes — Mañero & Labanca 2018 (n = 32)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Original series — 32 consecutive women treated at the Iván Mañero Clinic in Barcelona, Spain. The authors describe prospective follow-up in the abstract; a technical review reports **27/32 assessed at six months** and two partial graft necroses that healed secondarily. The original operative full text and tables remain needed to check these details directly.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| **FSFI** | **16 → 29** (P &lt; .05), before versus after among reported participants<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Female Genital Self-Image Scale** | **11 → 23** (P &lt; .05); this is self-image, not a standalone quality-of-life measure<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Follow-up / graft events** | 27/32 assessed at six months; 2 partial graft necroses healed by secondary intention, as summarized in a contemporary review—not independently checked in the inaccessible full original paper<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |

## Comparison with Other Coverage Techniques

| Technique | Tissue source | Potential use | Key limitation | Reported outcome (not a head-to-head comparison) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mañero vaginal mucosal graft** | Posterior vaginal wall (free graft) | Alternative coverage tissue; concealed donor site | Free graft requires recipient-bed vascularization; single-center evidence (n = 32) | 16 → 29, P &lt; .05<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **Wilson & Zaki sensate labial flaps** | Labia minora remnant (pedicled flap) | Sensate local coverage when adequate tissue remains | Feasibility depends on labial remnant | FSFI 11.6 → 29.1 in its separate series<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **O'Dey OD flap** | Local vulvar tissue (pedicled flap) | Preputial reconstruction, often combined with NMCS/aOAP | Combined-program outcomes cannot isolate this flap | Improvements reported for combined procedures<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Foldès (exposed neoglans)** | Mobilized residual clitoral tissue; no free graft in original | Largest reported cohort (n = 2,938) | Exposed tissue re-epithelializes; no dedicated graft coverage | 430/841 respondents reported orgasm at one year<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |

## Position in the Literature

The Mañero vaginal mucosal graft technique was the **one vaginal-graft technique** among the 13 studies included in the **2026 Meremikwu review** (the other 12 used Foldès-style reconstruction).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The **2024 Almadori scoping review** found clitoral reconstruction, mostly Foldès or variants, in **38/40 included studies**; this is not a worldwide patient share, and other flap/coverage variants are also described.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

The technique has been recognized in multiple reviews as an important innovation that addresses the soft-tissue-coverage gap after clitoral mobilization, particularly in women with extensive mutilation where local tissue is insufficient.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> However, it remains supported by only a **single-center, single-cohort study** (Level of Evidence IV); no comparative trials exist.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Limitations and Considerations

| Limitation | Detail |
|---|---|
| Limited evidence | One published cohort (n = 32) from a single center; no randomized or comparative studies<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Free-graft biology | Unlike pedicled flaps (Wilson & Zaki sensate labial; O'Dey OD), a free mucosal graft depends entirely on revascularization from the recipient bed — inherent risk of partial / complete graft loss<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Donor-site morbidity | Vaginal scarring, stenosis or discomfort are plausible concerns, but rates and closure method are not established by the accessible original abstract<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Sensation | The free graft has no transferred neurovascular pedicle; postoperative sensation and any reinnervation were not established by this cohort's accessible abstract<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Multidisciplinary care | WHO suggests sexual-health counseling before or with surgery. Counseling was **reported in 15/40 studies** in the 2024 scoping review, not necessarily absent from all others.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |

---

## Key Takeaways

1. The Mañero vaginal mucosal graft was the sole vaginal-graft series in the 2026 13-study review, not the only alternative reconstructive operation worldwide.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
2. A technical review describes a **posterior** vaginal donor graft for neoclitoral coverage; the lateral-wall graft in that review is a different team's modification.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
3. Outcomes — original abstract: n = 32, FSFI 16 → 29 and genital self-image 11 → 23 (both P &lt; .05); no separate quality-of-life outcome or comparison group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
4. Evidence base is single-center, single-cohort (Level IV); no comparative trials.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
5. Trade-off versus pedicled labial flaps — the free graft offers coverage when local tissue is limited but depends on recipient-bed revascularization; comparative function and sensation remain unknown.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
