# Mañero Vaginal Mucosal Graft — Clitorolabial Reconstruction After FGM/C

The **Mañero vaginal mucosal graft** is a technique for clitorolabial reconstruction after FGM/C, described by Iván Mañero and Trinidad Labanca (Barcelona, Spain; 2018). A free vaginal mucosal graft covers the repositioned clitoris and the adjacent labial region. It was the only vaginal-graft study among the 13 included in the 2026 Meremikwu clitoral-reconstruction review; the other 12 used Foldès-style reconstruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The original series followed 32 consecutive women prospectively. Female Sexual Function Index (FSFI) scores rose from 16 to 29 (P &lt; .05) and Female Genital Self-Image Scale scores from 11 to 23 (P &lt; .05). The series had no comparison group and no separate quality-of-life endpoint.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For the broader treatment menu see the [Vulvar Reconstruction Atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar). For the FGM/C reconstruction technique see [Foldès Clitoral Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/foldes-clitoral-reconstruction). For the aOAP-flap variant used for FGM/C vulvovestibular and LSA reconstruction see [aOAP Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/aoap-flap).

The 2025 WHO guideline conditionally suggests clitoral reconstruction for selected women, on very-low-certainty evidence, with sexual-health counseling and multidisciplinary support. It does not prefer this graft over Foldès-style or other coverage methods.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Background and Rationale

Exposing the residual clitoris without dedicated coverage can lead to prolonged re-epithelialization or discomfort in some patients. A free graft supplies a different tissue source when local coverage is limited. Its benefit over other approaches has not been compared directly.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> A technical review describes the Mañero graft as harvested from the posterior vaginal wall (about 3–4 × 2 cm) and placed over the neoclitoris toward the labia majora. A lateral vaginal graft described in the same review is a separate modification by the Swedish authors and not Mañero's donor site.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

Rationale for using vaginal mucosa:

| Property | Detail |
|---|---|
| Tissue choice | Vaginal mucosa is a supple coverage tissue; whether it gives better sensation, comfort or appearance than other coverage methods is untested<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| Concealed donor site | Harvest avoids a visible external donor scar; the original abstract does not specify donor-wound closure or quantify donor-site morbidity<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Availability | The vaginal canal can provide mucosa when external local tissue is limited; suitability depends on individual anatomy and donor-site risks<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |

## Surgical Technique

The original abstract confirms a vaginal graft but gives no operative detail. The sequence below follows the technical-review description of Mañero's operation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Graft thickness, suture choice, donor-wound closure and dressing are not given in the sources reviewed here.

### Step 1 — Clitoral stump exposure and mobilization

- Vulvar scar tissue overlying the clitoral stump is excised.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- Identify the residual clitoral body and dissect it free from surrounding fibrosis.
- Transect the suspensory ligament to allow downward mobilization of the clitoral stump, as in Foldès.
- Preserve the dorsal neurovascular bundle throughout.

### Step 2 — Neoglans fashioning

- Shape the distal end of the mobilized clitoral body into a neoglans.
- Suture the neoclitoris into anatomical position with anti-retraction fixation sutures.

### Step 3 — Vaginal mucosal graft harvest

- A posterior vaginal mucosal graft of about 3–4 × 2 cm is harvested, as described in the technical review. The harvest plane, graft thickness and donor-wound closure are not specified.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

### Step 4 — Graft placement for clitoral and labial reconstruction

- Trim and tailor the vaginal mucosal graft to the recipient site.
- The graft covers the neoclitoris and extends toward the labia majora. The original abstract describes this as clitorolabial reconstruction. It does not show recreation of an innervated native prepuce or labium minus.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- The graft must be fixed for take. The suture, dressing and immobilization regimen is not specified in the sources reviewed.

### Step 5 — Closure and postoperative care

- In the reviewed cohort a urinary catheter and bed rest were used for five days. This is the reported study aftercare and not a validated standard for every vaginal-graft case.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- Donor-site closure, analgesia and longer-term graft sensation are not described in the accessible sources.

## Published Outcomes — Mañero & Labanca 2018 (n = 32)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The series comprised 32 consecutive women treated at the Iván Mañero Clinic in Barcelona, Spain, and followed prospectively. The technical review reports that five patients were lost to follow-up, that 27 were assessed at six months, and that two had partial graft necrosis that healed by secondary intention.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| **FSFI** | **16 → 29** (P &lt; .05), before versus after among reported participants<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Female Genital Self-Image Scale** | **11 → 23** (P &lt; .05); this is self-image, not a standalone quality-of-life measure<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Follow-up / graft events** | 27/32 assessed at six months; 2 partial graft necroses healed by secondary intention, as summarized in a contemporary review<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |

## Comparison with Other Coverage Techniques

| Technique | Tissue source | Potential use | Key limitation | Reported outcome (not a head-to-head comparison) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mañero vaginal mucosal graft** | Posterior vaginal wall (free graft) | Alternative coverage tissue; concealed donor site | Free graft requires recipient-bed vascularization; single-center evidence (n = 32) | 16 → 29, P &lt; .05<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **Wilson & Zaki sensate labial flaps** | Labia minora remnant (pedicled flap) | Sensate local coverage when adequate tissue remains | Feasibility depends on labial remnant | FSFI 11.6 → 29.1 in its separate series<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **O'Dey OD flap** | Local vulvar tissue (pedicled flap) | Preputial reconstruction, often combined with NMCS/aOAP | Combined-program outcomes cannot isolate this flap | Improvements reported for combined procedures<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Foldès (exposed neoglans)** | Mobilized residual clitoral tissue; no free graft in original | Largest reported cohort (n = 2,938) | Exposed tissue re-epithelializes; no dedicated graft coverage | 430/841 respondents reported orgasm at one year<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |

## Position in the Literature

The Mañero graft was the one vaginal-graft technique among the 13 studies in the 2026 Meremikwu review.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The 2024 Almadori scoping review found Foldès reconstruction in 38 of 40 included studies. Other flap and coverage variants are also described.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

Reviews describe the technique as addressing the soft-tissue coverage gap after clitoral mobilization, particularly in women with extensive mutilation and insufficient local tissue.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> It rests on a single-center, single-cohort study (level of evidence IV), and no comparative trials exist.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Limitations and Considerations

| Limitation | Detail |
|---|---|
| Limited evidence | One published cohort (n = 32) from a single center; no randomized or comparative studies<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Free-graft biology | Unlike pedicled flaps (Wilson & Zaki sensate labial; O'Dey OD), a free mucosal graft depends entirely on revascularization from the recipient bed, so partial or complete graft loss is possible<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Donor-site morbidity | Vaginal scarring, stenosis or discomfort are plausible concerns; rates and closure method are not given in the original abstract<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Sensation | The free graft has no transferred neurovascular pedicle; postoperative sensation and reinnervation were not reported in the original abstract<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Multidisciplinary care | WHO suggests sexual-health counseling before or with surgery. The 2024 scoping review found counseling reported in 15 of 40 studies<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |

---

## Key Takeaways

1. The Mañero vaginal mucosal graft was the sole vaginal-graft series in the 2026 13-study review. Other reconstructive operations exist.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
2. A technical review describes a posterior vaginal donor graft for neoclitoral coverage. The lateral-wall graft in that review is a different team's modification.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
3. In the original series (n = 32), FSFI rose from 16 to 29 and genital self-image from 11 to 23 (both P &lt; .05). There was no quality-of-life outcome and no comparison group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
4. The evidence is a single-center, single-cohort series (level IV).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
5. Compared with pedicled labial flaps, the free graft offers coverage when local tissue is limited but depends on recipient-bed revascularization. Comparative function and sensation are unknown.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
