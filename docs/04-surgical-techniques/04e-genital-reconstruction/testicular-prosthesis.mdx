# Testicular Prosthesis

Testicular prosthesis placement restores the scrotal envelope after orchiectomy or for congenital / acquired anorchia, with consistently high satisfaction (77–96%) when offered — yet only ~⅔ of orchiectomy patients are offered an implant and ~⅓ of those proceed.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)[[19]](#ref19)</sup> The **offer rate is the dominant counseling gap**, not patient acceptance.

For the broader scrotal-reconstruction ladder see [Scrotal Reconstruction Techniques](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/scrotal-reconstruction).

---

## History and Materials

| Era | Material |
|---|---|
| **1941** | First implant — Vitallium ball.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **1950s–60s** | Glass, polyethylene, acrylic. |
| **1970s–90s** | Silicone gel-filled (Dow Corning) — worldwide standard.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **1995** | Silicone gel manufacture **discontinued in the US** (breast-implant fallout) despite no evidence of systemic disease attributable to testicular silicone.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **2002** | FDA approves the **Torosa** saline-filled prosthesis (Coloplast).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **2020s** | **Rigicon Testi10™** saline — Kaplan-Meier survival **99.8% at 54 mo** (n = 427).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |

Silicone gel remains the international standard (Nagor, Promedon, Prometel, Sebbin, Polytech, Eurosilicone).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### Available devices

- **Saline (US).** Torosa (Coloplast) — silicone-elastomer shell, intraoperatively filled with sterile saline; four sizes (XS / S / M / L); volume adjustable to match the contralateral testis.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Rigicon Testi10™ — silicone shell saline; 0.2% revision rate over 54 mo (only 1 revision from iatrogenic puncture during filling).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Silicone gel (international).** More natural feel than saline; cohesive (no "sloshing"); 25–44% of patients still rate it "too firm" (texture is the unmet need regardless of material).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Other / experimental.** Elliptical silastic shapes that better approximate the native ovoid testis (Ning 2011, n = 20; 100% satisfied with body image and position).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Indications

| Indication | Detail |
|---|---|
| Post-orchiectomy for **testicular cancer** | Most common indication.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Testicular torsion** | Non-salvageable testis — intravaginal exchange at orchiectomy is feasible (Bush 2012 n = 12 adolescents: no infection / extrusion, 91.7% symmetric).<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **Congenital anorchia / vanishing testis** | Pediatric indication.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| **Cryptorchidism** with no testis found at inguinal / abdominal exploration | Pediatric indication.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| **Trauma** | Post-orchiectomy for unreconstructable injury.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Atrophy** (post-orchitis, post-torsion) | Selected.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Gender-affirming masculinizing surgery** | Neoscrotal augmentation as part of phalloplasty / scrotoplasty.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |

**Contraindications.** Active local infection, active malignancy at the surgical site, active rheumatological disease (per original FDA protocol).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Preoperative Counseling

| Topic | Counseling point |
|---|---|
| **Offer rate** | 91% of orchiectomy patients consider being offered the prosthesis "extremely important" regardless of whether they accept. 42% of men without a prosthesis report never having been offered one.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[11]](#ref11)[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |
| **Firmness** | Will feel firmer than a native testis — the **most common complaint** (25–70% across studies); 59.3% satisfied with "feel" vs 89.8% satisfied with "look".<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Position** | High-riding (20–39%) is the second most common complaint.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Weight** | Generally lighter than a native testis.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **Size** | Measure the contralateral testis (ultrasound or calipers); have sample sizes in clinic; intraoperative comparison.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Timing** | Simultaneous with orchiectomy is safe even when chemo / radiotherapy will follow.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Academic-center effect** | Patients at academic institutions are more likely to be offered and to receive a prosthesis (OR 2.87).<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |

## Timing

### Simultaneous (at orchiectomy)

- **Testicular cancer.** Musi 2020 (n = 393) — no significant difference in complication rate between adjuvant ChT (4.34%), RT (6.20%), and no further treatment (6.03%).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Torsion (adolescent).** Bush 2012 (n = 12) — intravaginal prosthesis at orchiectomy: no infection, no extrusion, 91.7% symmetric scrotum.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

### Delayed

- Active infection at orchiectomy.
- Patient undecided.
- Concerns about wound healing.
- Pediatric: implantation **within 1 year** of orchiectomy is associated with fewer complications than later placement (p &lt; 0.05).<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

## Surgical Approaches

| Approach | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Inguinal** | Standard when simultaneous with radical inguinal orchiectomy. After cord ligation and orchiectomy, prosthesis inserted through the inguinal incision into a dartos pouch, secured to dartos or distal gubernacular remnant.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| **Scrotal** | Direct scrotal incision into a dartos pouch. Higher theoretical infection / extrusion risk because of direct prosthesis–suture-line contact and thin scrotal skin.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| **Suprascrotal ("Wink", Libman)** | 2-cm semilunar incision 2–3 cm lateral to the penis, just above the scrotum; blunt dissection into the scrotum. Pediatric series (Osemlak n = 290) — **lowest complication rate** (p = 0.01); incision hidden by pubic hair; avoids prosthesis–suture-line contact.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| **Intravaginal** | Prosthesis placed under the tunica vaginalis with preplaced sutures; native tunica becomes an extra barrier layer. Animal model favors this over the tunica-albuginea position (better mobility, less chronic inflammation).<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[17]](#ref17)</sup> |

### Technical pearls<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

- Dartos pouch sized adequately — too small produces high-riding prosthesis.
- Secure to dartos / gubernacular remnant with absorbable suture to prevent migration.
- For saline devices: fill intraoperatively to the matched volume; protect against inadvertent puncture.
- Antibiotic prophylaxis; some surgeons irrigate the pocket with antibiotic solution.
- Outpatient procedure.

### The fixation question

Besombes 2024 (n = 169; 146 respondents) found that fixation **did not improve positioning** but **significantly worsened pain / discomfort** (30.8% vs 9.78%, p &lt; 0.05) — challenges the routine practice of suturing the prosthesis to dartos / gubernacular remnant. Retrospective and non-randomized.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

## Outcomes — Satisfaction

| Series | n | Overall satisfaction | "Too firm" | High-riding | Would repeat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clifford 2018 | 40 | **82.5%** good / excellent | 44% | 20% inappropriate | 87.5%<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Araújo 2024 | 51 with prosthesis | **96.1%** | 25.5% | — | —<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Yossepowitch 2011 | 86 | **77%** good / excellent | 70% | 39% | —<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| Adshead 2001 | 71 | **73%** | — | — | —<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| Incrocci 1999 | 22 | 68% improved body image | — | — | —<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| Nichols 2019 | 59 with prosthesis | 89.8% satisfied with **look**; 59.3% with **feel** | — | — | —<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |

Receiving a prosthesis is an **independent positive predictor of overall genital satisfaction** (OR 3.29).<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> Sexual function is not impaired — recipients more often report normal sexual activity (74% vs 50%) and none report severe ED (0% vs 16.7%; differences non-significant).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Complications

Saline FDA pivotal trial (n = 149) — 19% adverse-event rate.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

| Complication | Rate |
|---|---|
| Device-related discomfort / pain | 3% |
| Scrotal edema | 1.3% |
| Infection | 1.3% (may require explantation) |
| Extrusion | 2.6% (requires explantation) |
| Deflation / leakage (saline only) | 0.7% |
| Pulmonary embolism | 0.7% (rare) |
| Migration / high-riding | 20–39% patient-reported |
| "Too firm" | 25–70% patient-reported |

**Population-specific complication signals:**

- **Transgender men (neoscrotal augmentation).** Pigot 2019 — overall explantation **20.8%** (infection, extrusion, discomfort, leakage, urethral problems); modern technique (Hoebeke scrotoplasty) reduces this to **11.5%**. Smoking is a significant risk for infection / explantation. Trend toward smaller / lighter prostheses and delayed implantation has improved outcomes.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Pediatric** (Osemlak n = 290). 92.1% good outcomes; complications fewer when implantation occurred within the first 3 years of life (p = 0.002) or within 1 year of the primary operation (p &lt; 0.05). Suprascrotal access — lowest complication rate (p = 0.01).<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Post-chemo / RT.** No increased complication rate.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Rigicon Testi10™.** 0.2% revision rate at 54 mo (n = 427; 100% pediatric / adolescent survival in 45).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Capsular contracture.** Unlike breast implants, clinically significant capsular contracture is rarely reported; a fibrous capsule does form.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

## Saline vs Silicone Gel

| Parameter | Saline (US) | Silicone gel (international) |
|---|---|---|
| Overall satisfaction | 77–87.5% good / excellent | 73–96.1% |
| "Too firm" | 44–70% | 25.5–70% |
| Position high | 20–39% | 20–39% |
| Satisfied with **look** | 89.8% | ~90% |
| Satisfied with **feel** | 59.3% | ~58–80% |
| Body-image improvement | 68% | 68% |
| Deflation / leakage | 0.7% (unique to saline) | N/A |
| Intraoperative volume adjustment | **Yes** (advantage) | No (preselected sizes) |
| Feel | "Sloshes" slightly; perceived firmer | More natural / cohesive |
| Systemic disease link | None reported | None reported with up to 5+ yr follow-up |

No head-to-head RCT exists; the wide ranges reflect questionnaire / cohort heterogeneity rather than a clear material-specific signal.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Pediatric Considerations<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

- **Timing.** Congenital absence — implantation between **1–3 years of age**; post-orchiectomy — within **1 year**.
- **Approach.** Suprascrotal (lowest complication rate).
- **Sizing.** Infant-sized prostheses available; Elder 1989 reported good outcomes in 41 boys.
- **Adolescent torsion.** Bush 2012 — intravaginal exchange at orchiectomy; 100% survival, 91.7% symmetric scrotum.
- **Adolescent Rigicon Testi10™.** 100% survival in 45 patients, no revisions.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Gender-Affirming Use<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

For the dedicated transmasculine-specific page (delayed-implantation timing, Hoebeke vs bilateral-rotational scrotoplasty selection, ZSI 475 FtM testicle-shaped-pump overlap, staging within the masculinizing reconstruction sequence), see **[Testicular Implants (Gender-Affirming)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/testicular-implants)** in the 04k gender-affirming-surgery section.

- Largest series (n = 206; mean follow-up 11.5 yr) — overall explantation 20.8%; modern technique 11.5%.
- Multiple brands used (Nagor, Promedon, Sebbin).
- Current trend — **smaller / lighter prostheses** and **delayed implantation** (rather than at scrotoplasty) improves outcomes.
- Smoking is a significant risk for infection / explantation.

## Emerging — Hormone-Eluting and Tissue-Engineered Devices

Future-direction concepts shift from passive cosmetic replacement toward **endocrinologically active implants** that deliver testosterone, eliminating separate hormone replacement.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

| Concept | Stage | Key feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Poly(HEMA) testosterone-releasing prosthesis (Yoshida / Imai)** | Clinical (n = 12 hypogonadal men) | Constant T release ≥ 200 ng/dL serum for 48 mo; in vitro release 5.5 ± 1.5 mg/day × 900 days; available in Japan, not FDA-approved.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)[[23]](#ref23)</sup> |
| **Double-layer silastic STPT (Chen / Ning)** | Preclinical (castrated rats) | Controlled testosterone-undecanoate release via semi-permeable membranes; normalized T / LH, preserved ICPmax.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> |
| **Tissue-engineered cartilage prosthesis (Raya-Rivera / Atala)** | Preclinical (castrated athymic mice) | Bioengineered cartilage shell with hollow refillable center; physiologic T sustained through 14 wk in vivo; ex vivo release &gt; 500 ng/dL for 16 wk.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> |
| **Improved biomimetic designs** | Conceptual / patent | Softer, more natural tactile properties.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |

## Key Recommendations<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)[[11]](#ref11)[[14]](#ref14)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

1. **Offer to all patients** undergoing orchiectomy regardless of age.
2. **Simultaneous placement** at orchiectomy is safe — preferred when feasible, including before chemo / RT.
3. **Size selection** guided by contralateral measurement; sample implants in clinic.
4. **Counsel** about firmness (the most common complaint) and high-riding risk.
5. **Suprascrotal approach** has the lowest complication rate.
6. **Pediatric** implantation ideally within the first 3 years of life or within 1 year of orchiectomy.
7. Satisfaction is 77–96%; sexual function is not impaired.
