# Testicular Prosthesis

Testicular prosthesis placement is an elective reconstructive option after testicular loss or absence. It restores scrotal contour but does **not** restore spermatogenesis or endocrine testicular function. A 2020 review emphasizes that the decision is personal and that clear counseling about benefits, limitations, alternatives, and possible later placement matters.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For the broader reconstructive context, see [Scrotal Reconstruction Techniques](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/scrotal-reconstruction). Gender-affirming implant considerations are addressed separately in [Testicular Implants (Gender-Affirming)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery/testicular-implants).

## Evidence context

In a single-center retrospective survey, 51 of 59 respondents who had undergone orchiectomy chose a silicone prosthesis; 49 of the 51 recipients reported overall satisfaction, while 13 described the implant as too firm.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The study found no statistically significant difference in measured sexual-function outcomes between recipients and nonrecipients, but its observational design and small comparison group do not establish a causal effect.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

A pediatric single-center cohort reported 290 primary implants from 2000–2014. Its authors observed fewer complications with earlier implantation and with a suprascrotal approach, but this is observational experience from one setting rather than a universal timing or approach standard.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Counseling and planning boundaries

- Discuss whether a prosthesis aligns with the patient's goals; declining or delaying placement is a valid option.
- Explain that satisfaction, size/weight/position, firmness, pain, infection, migration, extrusion, and need for revision may vary by device, patient, indication, operation, and follow-up.
- Address fertility and hormone questions separately; a cosmetic prosthesis does not replace testicular function.
- Timing and approach should be individualized to the clinical setting, local tissue condition, patient age and growth, prior surgery, infection risk, and available expertise.
- The reviewed observational sources do not establish a single best device, fixation method, timing, access route, or expected individual outcome.
