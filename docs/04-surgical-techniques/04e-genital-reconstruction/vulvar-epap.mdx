# External Pudendal Artery Perforator (EPAP) Flap for Vulvar Reconstruction

The **EPAP flap** is a fasciocutaneous perforator flap based on **skin perforators from the external pudendal artery system** (superficial and / or deep branches), which arises from the **femoral artery** in the groin. It encompasses a family of related flap designs — including the **mons pubis flap**, **supermedial thigh flap**, and **STEPA flap** — all sharing the same source vessel but differing in donor site, design, and application. In vulvar reconstruction, EPAP has been used as a pedicled island perforator flap with **100% flap survival** in the Huang et al. (2015) series, though it remains one of the **less commonly utilized** perforator flaps compared with IPAP and DFAP / PAP.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

For the broader treatment menu see the [Vulvar Reconstruction Atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar). ## Anatomic Basis — The External Pudendal Artery System

The EPA system consists of two branches — **SEPA** and **DEPA** — both arising from the femoral artery (occasionally the deep femoral artery) in the femoral triangle.

### SEPA — La Falce 2006 (n = 50 cadaveric sides)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Present | 92% (46/50 sides) |
| Origin | Femoral a. 45/46; deep femoral a. 1/46 |
| Diameter | 1.2–3.8 mm (highly variable) |
| Distance from inguinal ligament | 0.8–8.5 cm |
| Duplication | 46% duplicated, 24% common trunk, 30% single |
| Course | Superficial to the spermatic cord / round ligament; crosses the femoral vein superficially |
| Skin territory | Suprapubic, infraumbilical, mons pubis, labial / scrotal skin<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

### DEPA<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

- Passes deep to the spermatic cord / round ligament and through fascia lata to reach the perineal skin.
- The **superficial branch of DEPA** is the largest and most consistent artery supplying the supermedial thigh flap territory.
- Supplies the medial thigh, labial, and scrotal skin.

### EPA microsurgical caliber — Allepot 2023 (n = 10 cadavers)<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- EPA system: common trunk or duplicated.
- **83% of EPAs** arose between a horizontal reference line through the pubic tubercles and 3 cm below it, at the level of a vertical axis centered on the femoral artery.
- Adequate caliber for microsurgical anastomosis — suitable as a recipient vessel in phalloplasty.

### Perineal perforators — Jin 2009 (n = 11 cadavers / 22 sides)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

Two of the four constant perineal perforators arise from the SEPA:

1. Inguinal perforating branch of SEPA.
2. Perineal perforating branch of SEPA.

Both are direct perforating branches forming upper, middle, and lower deep-fascial anastomoses with obturator and IPA perforators.

### Anteromedial thigh territory — El-Shennawy 2022 (30 cadaveric limbs)<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

- Upper medial thigh skin (squares 1 and 4) supplied primarily by SEPA, DEPA, and superficial circumflex femoral a.
- Segmental, long, large-diameter perforators grant the anteromedial thigh flap its versatility.

### Sensory innervation

- **Ilioinguinal nerve** — mons pubis and supermedial-thigh flap territories.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Genitofemoral nerve** — anterior perineal contribution.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Anterior scrotal / labial nerve** — incorporated for sensate reconstruction.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## The EPAP Flap in Vulvar Reconstruction — Huang 2015<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The only published series specifically reporting EPAP for vulvar reconstruction after cancer surgery:

- 27 flaps in 16 patients using island pedicled perforator flaps.
- Perforator flap types: DFAP / PAP (n = 11), MCFAP (n = 8), **EPAP (n = 2)**, medial thigh free-style (n = 2), IPAP (n = 4).
- **All 27 flaps survived (100%)**, including both EPAP flaps.
- 3 patients developed small wounds requiring debridement; 1 required flap debulking.
- All donor sites closed primarily; no donor-site morbidities.
- All patients satisfied with cosmetic and functional results.

EPAP was used in only 2/27 flaps — reflecting its niche role vs the more commonly used DFAP and MCFAP for vulvar reconstruction.

## The Mons Pubis Flap — The Original EPA-Based Vulvar Flap

### Mayer & Rodriguez 1991<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- Pedicled skin flap based on SEPA designed in the suprapubic area and rotated into place for vulvar reconstruction.
- SEPA supplies suprapubic and infraumbilical skin.
- Pliable, thin tissue with inconspicuous donor scar — considered superior to skin grafts or bulkier musculocutaneous flaps.

### Spear 1994 — cadaveric injection study + clinical series<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- Cadaver injection demonstrated primary and secondary vascular territory.
- n = 4 patients — excellent cosmetic results.
- Provides pliable, hair-bearing skin resembling the normal vulva; donor defect inconspicuous within pubic hair.

### Potkul 1994 — hemivulvectomy series<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

- n = 4 radical hemivulvectomy patients reconstructed with mons pubis pedicle flap.
- Excellent results — pliable, hair-bearing skin authentically mimics the normal side, providing good sexual function.
- Recommended where excellent cosmesis is desirable.

### Höckel 2008 caveat<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

The mons pubis flap and groin flap **necessitate a modified groin-dissection technique** to preserve nourishing vessels — standard inguinofemoral lymphadenectomy may sacrifice the SEPA, rendering the flap unavailable.

## The Supermedial Thigh Flap — DEPA-Based Variant (Har-Shai 1984)<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

- Arterial and sensate flap used for one-stage reconstruction of the scrotum and vulva.
- **Primary supply**: superficial branch of DEPA — largest and most consistent artery.
- **Secondary supply**: musculocutaneous perforators of the medial circumflex femoral a.
- **Additional supply**: subcutaneous branches of the superficial femoral a. (when present).
- **Sensory innervation**: branches of the ilioinguinal nerve.

---

## The STEPA Flap — Free Microsurgical Variant (Phoon 2014)<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Cadaveric anatomic study (6 fresh cadavers) characterizing the super-thin EPA flap as the **thinnest myocutaneous free flap** available:

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Flaps raised | 8 hemiscrotal + 2 bipedicled scrotal |
| EPA diameter at origin | 2.81 mm |
| Vein diameter | 4.44 mm |
| Pedicle length | 11 cm (range 10–12) |
| **Flap thickness** | **Mean 1.1 mm** (&gt; 80% of flap thickness &lt; 1.1 mm) |
| Donor-site morbidity | Favorable |

Demonstrates the remarkable thinness achievable with EPA-based flaps and the reliable vascular anatomy of the EPA system.

---

## The Sensate EPAP Hemi-Scrotal Flap (Tsukuura 2025)<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

Most recent EPAP innovation — described for penile shaft reconstruction with principles applicable to perineal reconstruction:

- EPA perforator isolated to elevate a perforator flap with a wider arc of rotation than traditional scrotal flaps.
- **Anterior scrotal nerve** incorporated for sensate reconstruction.
- Elevated **above the deep fascia** (external spermatic fascia).
- Perforator isolation allows complete coverage with a single flap while maintaining sensory function.
- Tunica vaginalis freed from the flap pedicle — eliminates pain and stretching complications.
- Primary donor closure with a more inconspicuous lateral scar.

## Surgical Technique — EPAP Flap for Vulvar Reconstruction

Based on principles across the EPA-based literature:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

1. **Position** — lithotomy.
2. **Perforator identification** — handheld Doppler in the inguinal / genitocrural region; perforators within ~3 cm below a horizontal line through the pubic tubercles.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
3. **Flap design** — island skin paddle centered on the identified perforator(s). Possible donor variants:
   - **Mons pubis / suprapubic** (SEPA-based) — for anterior vulvar defects.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
   - **Upper medial thigh** (DEPA-based) — for lateral vulvar defects.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
   - **Labiocrural / genitocrural region** — for medial vulvar defects.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
4. **Incision and elevation** — circumferential incision; raised as a true island flap on its perforator pedicle in a suprafascial plane.
5. **Transfer** — transposed, rotated, or advanced into the vulvar defect.
6. **Donor closure** — primary; scar concealed in the mons pubis, inguinal crease, or genitocrural fold.

## Position in Reconstructive Algorithms

| Algorithm | Position |
|---|---|
| **Negosanti 2015**<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | Two primary flaps (LPF + pedicled DIEP); EPAP not specifically named, but EPA territory contributes to the broader perineal perforator network |
| **Toulouse 2025**<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> | Perforator flaps first-line over musculocutaneous; location-based rather than size-based; EPAP not specifically named but encompassed in the perforator family alongside IPAP / GCIPF / DFAP |
| **Höckel 2008**<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> | Mons pubis flap (SEPA) and anterior labial flaps (SEPA + DEPA) listed among recognized axial-pattern flaps; **pudenda externa** named as one of the arterial systems contributing to the perineal anastomotic network. **Critical caveat — mons pubis flap requires modified groin dissection** to preserve the SEPA |
| **Huang 2015**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | EPAP is one of five perforator flap types used; selected when perforator location and defect geometry favor the EPA territory over DFAP / MCFAP / IPAP |

## Comparison with Other Perforator Flaps for Vulvar Reconstruction

| Feature | **EPAP** | IPAP | DFAP / PAP | GCIPF |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Source artery | External pudendal a. (femoral) | Internal pudendal a. (internal iliac) | Profunda femoris a. | Anterior obturator a. |
| Donor site | Mons pubis / upper medial thigh / genitocrural | Ischiorectal fossa / gluteal fold | Posteromedial thigh | Genitocrural sulcus |
| Best for | **Anterior / mons pubis defects** | Posterior / lateral defects | Lateral / extended defects | Lateral / anterior defects |
| Tissue thickness | Very thin (STEPA 1.1 mm) | Thin (can be thinned) | Moderate | Very thin |
| Pedicle length | Long (STEPA 11 cm) | Variable | Variable | Variable |
| Compatibility with LND | **May be compromised** by standard groin dissection | Compatible | Compatible | Compatible |
| Published vulvar cases | Limited (2 in Huang) | Large (71+ flaps) | Moderate (11+) | Moderate (46) |
| Flap survival | 100% (limited data) | 94.4–100% | 100% | 100% |

## Advantages

| Advantage | Detail |
|---|---|
| Ultra-thin tissue | STEPA mean 1.1 mm — thinnest available flap; pedicled EPAP also thin and pliable<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Excellent anterior-vulvar tissue match | Mons-pubis variant provides pliable hair-bearing skin authentically mimicking labia majora<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Long pedicle | 11 cm (STEPA) — wide arc of rotation or free transfer<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Adequate vessel caliber | EPA 2.81 mm artery / 4.44 mm vein — suitable for microsurgery<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Sensate | Ilioinguinal (mons pubis / supermedial thigh) or anterior scrotal / labial nerve (EPAP hemi-scrotal)<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| Concealed donor scar | Hidden in mons pubis (pubic hair), inguinal crease, or genitocrural fold<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| 100% flap survival | Limited vulvar reconstruction data — all reported cases survived<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| No muscle sacrifice | Fasciocutaneous / perforator design<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Primary donor closure | All donor sites closed primarily<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Favorable donor-site morbidity | No functional deficits<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Versatile design | Pedicled island / propeller / V-Y / free microsurgical transfer<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Rich perforator network | SEPA + DEPA + superficial circumflex femoral a. — vascular redundancy<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |

## Limitations

| Limitation | Detail |
|---|---|
| Very limited published vulvar data | Only 2 EPAP flaps for vulvar reconstruction (Huang 2015) — niche option vs IPAP / DFAP / PAP<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Vulnerability to standard inguinofemoral LND | Standard groin dissection may sacrifice SEPA / DEPA — a **modified groin-dissection technique** is required<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| Highly variable anatomy | SEPA diameter 1.2–3.8 mm; distance from inguinal ligament 0.8–8.5 cm; duplication 46% / common trunk 24% / single 30%<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Absent in 8% | SEPA not found in 4/50 cadaveric sides — flap not universally available<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Hair-bearing skin | Advantageous for labia majora but undesirable for other vulvar subunits<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| Pigmentation differences | Scrotal / labial skin may differ from recipient site for distant reconstruction<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Not suitable for posterior vulvar defects | EPA territory is anterior / superior — posterior defects → IPAP or gluteal-fold<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| Not suitable for large defects with pelvic dead space | Thin flap cannot provide adequate volume<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| Proximity to groin incision | Donor site may overlap the groin-dissection field, increasing wound-complication risk |
| No dedicated large clinical series | Unlike IPAP (71+ flaps), GCIPF (46), or gluteal-fold (127), EPAP lacks a dedicated vulvar series demonstrating reproducibility |

## Key Takeaways

1. The EPAP flap is based on perforators from the EPA system (superficial and / or deep branches), arising from the femoral artery; supplies the mons pubis, upper medial thigh, and labial / scrotal skin.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
2. The **mons pubis flap** (Mayer & Rodriguez 1991, Spear 1994) is the best-characterized EPA-based flap for vulvar reconstruction — pliable hair-bearing skin authentically mimicking labia majora; ideal for **anterior vulvar / hemivulvectomy defects**.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
3. The **STEPA flap** (Phoon 2014) achieves a mean thickness of 1.1 mm with an 11 cm pedicle and adequate vessel caliber (2.81 mm artery) — the thinnest available flap for microsurgical transfer.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
4. In the only vulvar reconstruction perforator series (Huang 2015), 2 EPAP flaps were used among 27 total perforator flaps, with 100% survival — but EPAP remains a niche option vs IPAP and DFAP / PAP.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
5. **Critical limitation** — standard inguinofemoral lymphadenectomy may sacrifice the EPA, requiring a modified groin-dissection technique to preserve the flap's blood supply.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
6. Best suited for **anterior vulvar defects** (mons pubis, anterior commissure, labia majora); posterior and lateral defects → IPAP or gluteal-fold flaps.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
