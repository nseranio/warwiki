# Pubolabial V-Y Amplified Advancement Flap for Vulvar Reconstruction

The **pubolabial V-Y amplified advancement flap** (the "V-Y amplified sliding flap from the pubis") is a cranially based fasciocutaneous island flap harvested from the mons pubis and advanced caudally into the vulvar defect. First described by **Moschella and Cordova (2000)**, its defining feature is that it is the **only single-flap technique capable of reconstructing the entire vulva after total vulvectomy**, eliminating the need for bilateral flaps.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For the broader treatment menu see the [Vulvar Reconstruction Atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar). For underlying V-Y geometry see the foundations [V-Y Advancement Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/v-y-advancement) page. For adjacent vulvar atlas pages see [Medial-Thigh V-Y](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar-medial-thigh-vy), [Gluteal V-Y](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar-gluteal-vy), [Limberg / Rhomboid](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar-limberg-flap), [Lotus Petal](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/lotus-petal), and [Singapore (pudendal-thigh)](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/singapore-pudendal-thigh) flaps.

---

## Historical Context and Nomenclature

The pubolabial V-Y flap was introduced by Moschella and Cordova in their 2000 publication on innervated island flaps for morphofunctional vulvar reconstruction. They described two complementary techniques — the V-Y amplified sliding flap from the pubis for symmetric / anterior defects, and a fasciocutaneous island flap from the gluteal fold for posterior defects. The pubolabial flap was the more frequently used of the two, applied in 14 of 22 patients in their original series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The term **"amplified"** refers to the unique biomechanical design — combining **downward (caudal) advancement** with **bilateral medial rotation**, effectively amplifying the tissue reach beyond what a simple V-Y advancement could achieve.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Vascular Anatomy and Blood Supply

| Source | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Deep pubic arterial network** | Dense subfascial plexus in the mons pubis; the flap's defining pedicle<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Superficial / deep external pudendal aa.** | Femoral branches supplying pubic and labial skin. SEPA present in 92% of specimens; diameter 1.2–3.8 mm<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Mons pubis axial-pattern flap** | A related but distinct flap based specifically on the SEPA; primary + secondary vascular territory demonstrated on cadaver injection<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Inguinal / perineal perforators** | Among the four relatively constant perineal perforators identified in microdissection studies<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **DEPAP perforators** | From the deep external pudendal artery; basis for standalone perforator flaps in perineal reconstruction<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |

### Sensory innervation

- **Ilioinguinal nerve branches** provide sensation, enabling a sensate reconstruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- The ilioinguinal nerve terminates on average **2.7 cm lateral to the midline and 1.7 cm superior to the pubic symphysis**, coursing through the mons pubis territory.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- In ~28% of specimens the ilioinguinal is the dominant sensory nerve to the mons pubis / inguinal crease (type B); in other patterns the genitofemoral nerve shares this territory.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Indications

| Indication | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Total vulvectomy defects** | The flap's defining advantage — only **one** flap is required for restoration of the whole vulva, unlike bilateral medial-thigh V-Y or bilateral pudendal-thigh flaps<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Symmetric anterior defects | Specifically indicated when the defect is symmetric and anteriorly oriented<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Anterior vulvar field resection (VFR) | Designated reconstruction after anterior VFR in the Höckel VFR trial<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |

### Critical contraindication

- **Medially extended groin dissection** — the pubolabial V-Y flap cannot be applied if the inguinofemoral lymphadenectomy has been extended medially, as this disrupts the vascular pedicle entering from the inguinal region. In such cases use bilateral medial-thigh V-Y or bilateral pudendal-thigh flaps.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgical Technique

1. **Position** — lithotomy.
2. **Design** — large V-shaped (triangular) skin island marked on the mons pubis, apex pointing cranially (toward the umbilicus), base oriented caudally toward the vulvar defect. Wider than a standard V-Y to accommodate the amplified movement.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
3. **Incision** through full-thickness skin and subcutaneous tissue.
4. **Island mobilization** — flap raised as a true island, completely circumscribed and mobilized on its deep subcutaneous pedicle. Island-flap mobilization is possible because of the rich perineal blood supply.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
5. **Amplified advancement** — combined movement:
   - **Caudal (downward) advancement** — triangular island slides inferiorly toward the defect.
   - **Bilateral medial rotation** — lateral edges rotate medially, wrapping around the defect to reconstruct both labial folds simultaneously.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
6. **Inset** — flap sutured into the total vulvectomy defect, reconstructing labial folds, vestibulum, and anterior commissure in a single stage.
7. **Donor-site closure** — cranial donor site on the mons pubis is closed primarily in V-to-Y.

### Key technical points

- The amplified movement is the distinguishing feature — advancement + bilateral rotation covers a much larger area than simple V-Y advancement from the same donor site.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- True **island** (not peninsula) maximizes mobility and reach.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- The deep pubic vascular network provides the pedicle — careful subfascial elevation preserves the supply.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- All scars hidden in natural folds; donor scar concealed in the suprapubic region.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Position in Reconstructive Algorithms

| Algorithm | Position |
|---|---|
| **Höckel 2008**<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | "Preferable alternative" for total vulvar defects — only one flap needed for full vulva restoration. Cannot be used if groin dissection extended medially. |
| **Höckel VFR 2018**<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | Designated reconstruction for **anterior VFR**. Posterior VFR → bilateral Limberg; interior VFR → labial flaps; total / extended VFR → bilateral pudendal-thigh, medial-thigh V-Y, or gluteal-fold V-Y. |
| **Höckel anatomical reconstruction 2004**<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | Random and axial-pattern skin flaps on deep external / internal pudendal aa. for deep anterior vulvectomy; advocates reconstruction of the anterior commissure with clitoral protrusion after superficial / deep vulvectomy. |

## Outcomes

### Moschella and Cordova 2000 — original series<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- 14 patients underwent the pubolabial V-Y amplified sliding flap.
- Compatible with inguinofemoral lymphadenectomy.
- All flaps allowed correct morphofunctional reconstruction with good local sensibility.
- Aesthetically satisfactory results; scars hidden in natural folds.
- No major complications reported.

### Höckel and Dornhöfer 2004<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- Anatomical reconstruction after vulvectomy using random and axial-pattern skin flaps on deep external / internal pudendal aa.; good postoperative results; successful reconstruction of labial folds, vestibulum, posterior commissure, and perineum.

### General V-Y flap outcomes (applicable to the pubolabial variant)

| Parameter | V-Y flaps (general) |
|---|---|
| Overall complication rate | 21%<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| Reoperation rate | **25%** — lowest among compared vulvar flap types<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Median hospital stay | **14 d** — shortest among compared flap types<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Wound dehiscence (tumors &gt; 4 cm) | 11% (vs 40% direct closure)<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| Flap survival | Approaches 100%<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| Sensation | Good local sensibility; fully restored by 24 mo<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Sexual function | Significant postoperative improvement<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |

## Related Flap — Mons Pubis Axial Flap

A closely related but distinct flap shares the same donor site:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

| Feature | Mons pubis axial flap |
|---|---|
| Vascular basis | Superficial external pudendal a. (axial, not random) |
| Design | Pedicled rotation flap (not V-Y advancement) |
| Tissue quality | Pliable, hair-bearing skin closely resembling normal vulva<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Donor site | Inconspicuous; hidden in pubic hair |
| Critical limitation | Requires a modified groin-dissection technique to preserve the SEPA — disrupted by standard inguinofemoral lymphadenectomy<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Series | Spear 1994 (n = 4) — excellent cosmetic results;<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Mayer & Rodriguez 1991 — superior to skin grafts or musculocutaneous flaps for vulvar reconstruction.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |

The pubolabial V-Y flap differs in that it relies on the deep pubic arterial network (rather than the SEPA specifically), uses a V-Y advancement with amplified rotation (rather than simple rotation), and is designed as an **island** (rather than a pedicled peninsula).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Advantages

| Advantage | Detail |
|---|---|
| Single-flap total vulvar reconstruction | The only technique that reconstructs the entire vulva with one flap, avoiding bilateral-flap morbidity / complexity<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Sensate | Ilioinguinal-nerve innervation; good local sensibility<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Amplified reach | Combined advancement + bilateral medial rotation covers more area than standard V-Y<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Excellent tissue match | Pliable, hair-bearing mons-pubis skin resembles the normal vulva<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Concealed scars | Donor scar suprapubic; all final scars in natural folds<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Compatible with inguinofemoral LND | Provided dissection is not extended medially<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| No muscle sacrifice | Fasciocutaneous design preserves underlying musculature<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Single-stage | Performed simultaneously with the oncologic resection<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Low complication rate | Consistent with general V-Y profile<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |

---

## Limitations

| Limitation | Detail |
|---|---|
| Cannot be used with medially extended groin dissection | The single most important contraindication — bilateral flaps from other donor sites required instead<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Best suited for symmetric / anterior defects | Asymmetric or predominantly posterior defects → [gluteal V-Y](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar-gluteal-vy) or pudendal-thigh flaps<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Limited published series | Moschella & Cordova (n = 14) remains the primary dedicated report; no large comparative studies specific to this variant |
| Hair-bearing skin | Mons-pubis donor provides hair-bearing tissue, which may be undesirable for some patients<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Potential bulkiness | Mons-pubis tissue may be excessively thick in obese patients |
| Not suitable for extended defects | Inguinal / gluteal / perianal extension → musculocutaneous flaps (TFL, gluteal-thigh, VRAM)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |

---

## Key Takeaways

1. The pubolabial V-Y amplified advancement flap is a unique single-flap solution for total vulvar reconstruction (Moschella & Cordova 2000).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
2. Its "amplified" design combines caudal advancement with bilateral medial rotation, enabling coverage of the entire vulvar defect from a single cranial donor site.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
3. Vascularized by the deep pubic arterial network and innervated by ilioinguinal nerve branches — a sensate, well-matched reconstruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
4. Höckel & Dornhöfer consider it a "preferable alternative" to bilateral flaps for total vulvar defects, with the critical caveat that it cannot be used after medially extended groin dissection.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
5. In the Höckel VFR trial it was the designated reconstruction specifically for anterior VFR.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
