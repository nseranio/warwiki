# Glanuloplasty With Flaps

**Glanuloplasty with flaps** covers reconstructive techniques that use pedicled tissue flaps rather than free skin grafts to create a neoglans after partial penectomy, glansectomy, or traumatic glans loss. The four principal flap-based approaches are the inverted urethral flap (IUF, Belinky and Chavarriaga), distal urethral reconstruction (Gulino), scrotal flap (Mazza and Cheliz), and rectus abdominis myofascial flap (Shaeer).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

For STSG-based neoglans creation after invasive disease, see [Glansectomy With STSG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/glansectomy-stsg). For superficial or organ-sparing disease, see [Glans Resurfacing](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/glans-resurfacing). For the broader decision framework, see [Penile Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile).

## 1. Inverted Urethral Flap (IUF) — Belinky / Chavarriaga

The largest dedicated IUF cohort is Chavarriaga n=74 with median follow-up of 72 months.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> The Belinky and Chavarriaga abstracts do not give complete operative figures or complication tables. See [Inverted Urethral Flap (IUF) Glanuloplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/inverted-urethral-flap) for the source-scoped detail.

### Principle

After partial penectomy a segment of distal urethra longer than needed for voiding is preserved and inverted over the closed corporal tips, providing a moist mucosal neoglans that mimics native tissue.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### Surgical technique

Fixed measurements, suture choices and catheter duration vary between surgeons; the values quoted are those cited for this technique.

1. **Partial penectomy** with negative corporal and urethral margins; current EAU guidance uses frozen section when complete removal is in doubt rather than as an automatic rule
2. **Urethral preservation.** Preserve sufficient length for the flap without tension; 2–3 cm has been cited
3. **Corporal closure** with absorbable suture
4. **Urethral flap creation.** Invert the preserved distal urethral segment over the closed corporal tips with mucosa facing outward
5. **Neoglans shaping.** Suture the inverted flap circumferentially to the shaft skin at the corporal-transection level, creating a corona-like ridge
6. **Neomeatus** fashioned at the tip of the neoglans
7. **Urethral catheter** according to the operative protocol; 7–10 days has been cited

### Oncologic outcomes — Chavarriaga 2022 (n = 74, median follow-up 72 mo)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| **6-year overall survival** | **86.5%** |
| **6-year RFS** | **90.5%** |
| 6-year PFS | 85.1% |
| ILND performed | 39.2% (29 / 74) |
| DSNB performed | 83.8% (62 / 74) |

### Functional outcomes — Chavarriaga<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

| Domain | Result |
|---|---|
| Mean EQ-5D-3L-VAS global health | **84.6 ± 10.4** |
| Mean **IIEF-5** | **17.3 ± 7** (mild-moderate ED) |
| Mean **ICIQ-MLUTS** | **1.7 ± 3.2** (minimal voiding symptoms) |

### Original Belinky series (n = 10, mean follow-up 11 mo)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- 0% flap necrosis; 0% neomeatal stenosis
- 10% local recurrence (1 / 10); 10% ventral penile curvature (without penetration difficulty)

### Advantages

- Single-stage with the partial penectomy
- No separate donor site
- Mucosal surface mimics native glans
- Six-year RFS 90.5% and mean ICIQ-MLUTS 1.7 in the selected Chavarriaga cohort, without a technique comparator

### Limitations

- Requires sufficient urethral length beyond the corporal transection
- Ventral curvature reported in 1/10 in Belinky; not reported in the larger abstract and no mechanism established
- Requires negative margins despite preserving a longer urethral segment; the cohort does not prove comparative oncologic equivalence

## 2. Distal Urethral Reconstruction — Gulino

Related but distinct distal-urethral technique (n=14, mean follow-up 13 months).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> See [Gulino Distal Urethral Glanuloplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/gulino-everted-urethral-flap) for the source-scoped method and outcomes.

### Technique

1. After glansectomy or distal shaft amputation, mobilize the penile urethra from the ventral corpora toward the base
2. In the accessible 2013 method, obtain at least 3 cm beyond the corporal bodies, open the urethra ventrally for 3 cm and spread it over the corporal apices
3. Secure the mucosal edges over the dorsal albuginea and create the neomeatus ventrally; these are one-center protocol details, not a universal standard

### Functional outcomes

| Domain | Result |
|---|---|
| **Thermal + tactile sensation** | **100% (14 / 14)** |
| Rigid erections (spontaneous / induced) | 71% (10 / 14) |
| IIEF ejaculation domain change | Not significant |
| IIEF orgasm domain change | Not significant |
| Local recurrence | 0/14 at mean 13 months |
| Penile retraction | None reported in the abstract |

Thermal and tactile sensibility was reported in 14/14, and ejaculation and orgasm domains did not change significantly within the cohort. There was no direct sensory or sexual-function comparison with STSG or IUF.

## 3. Scrotal Flap Glanuloplasty — Mazza / Cheliz

Two-stage pedicled scrotal flap (n=34, mean follow-up 73.2 months).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> See [Scrotal Flap Glanuloplasty (Mazza / Cheliz)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/mazza-scrotal-flap-glanuloplasty) for source-scoped detail.

### Surgical technique — two-stage

**Stage 1:**

1. After partial penectomy, design a pedicled scrotal flap from the anterior scrotum, based on the anterior scrotal artery<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
2. Transfer the distal end of the flap to the penile stump
3. Suture the urethral end through a hole created in the scrotal flap; this establishes the neomeatus
4. Suture flap borders to the adjacent tunica albuginea, securing the neoglans
5. Pedicle remains attached to the scrotum for the initial healing phase

**Stage 2 (4–6 weeks later):**

6. **Divide the flap pedicle**, completing separation from the scrotum
7. Trim the pedicle stump and close

### Outcomes (n = 34, mean follow-up 73.2 mo)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Normal-appearing penis | All patients |
| Unobstructed urinary flow | All patients |
| Sexual potency preserved | **20.5% (7 / 34)** |
| Definitive depilation required | **17.6% (6 / 34)** |
| Partial flap necrosis | 5.8% (2 / 34, required grafting) |
| Meatal stenosis | 2.9% (1 / 34, minor procedure) |

### Advantages

- Well-vascularized tissue (anterior scrotal artery)
- Prevents penile stump retraction into the scrotum
- Adequate bulk for a natural-appearing neoglans
- Long mean follow-up within the reported series (73.2 mo)
- Scrotal skin elasticity and thickness similar to penile shaft

### Limitations

- **Two-stage.** Second operation 4–6 wk later
- **Hair-bearing donor.** Depilation in approximately 18%
- Potency reported in 7/34; baseline function and causal explanation require the full paper
- Partial flap necrosis approximately 6%

### Related scrotal-flap applications (penile shaft)

For penile shaft skin coverage rather than glanuloplasty:
- Zhao n = 18, 2.3-y follow-up: 100% sensation recovery, 83% satisfactory intercourse<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- McLaughlin n = 8 bipedicled: all satisfactory, only minor complications<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- Fakin n = 43 bipedicled anterior scrotal for siliconoma: satisfaction 4.37 / 5, all patients with postoperative erection and intercourse<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

See [Bipedicled Anterior Scrotal Flap (Fakin)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/bipedicled-anterior-scrotal-flap) and related shaft-flap pages for these applications.

## 4. Rectus Abdominis Myofascial Flap Neoglans — Shaeer

Single-case-report technique for glans loss after circumcision, with six-month appearance findings.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> See [Rectus Abdominis Myofascial Neoglans (Shaeer)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/shaeer-rectus-myofascial-neoglans) for the abstract-supported sequence, later modified case and unresolved full-source details.

### Technique

1. Harvest 12 × 4 cm infraumbilical rectus abdominis muscle as a pedicled flap on the inferior epigastric vessels via paramedian incision
2. Partially deglove the penis through a circumferential incision 1 cm below the summit
3. Use the distal penile skin to elongate the urethra so the meatus sits at the neoglans tip
4. **Reflect and tunnel** the flap subcutaneously beneath the mons veneris and alongside the penis to emerge distal to the penile summit
5. **Sculpt the flap into glans shape** and secure around the neourethra
6. Create a corona by tucking the proximal edge of the flap to its undersurface

### Outcomes (6-month follow-up, n = 1)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- Neoglans with similar consistency, color, and shape to the native glans
- Functional urethral meatus at the tip
- Satisfactory cosmetic result

### Advantages

- The authors reported consistency, color and shape similar to a normal glans in one patient
- Single-stage
- Can be combined with phalloplasty for total penile reconstruction<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

### Limitations

- Abdominal donor morbidity is plausible, but rates from other VRAM contexts cannot be assigned to this one-patient operation<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- More technically complex than urethral or scrotal options
- **Single case report.** No long-term oncologic or functional data

## Comparison With STSG-Based Neoglans

| Feature | Flap-based (IUF / Gulino / scrotal / rectus) | STSG neoglans |
|---|---|---|
| **Sensation** | 14/14 thermal/tactile in the distinct Gulino cohort; not reported in the accessible IUF abstract | 83.7% (range 63.6–91.2%) in the Pang SR<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Donor site | None (urethral) or scrotum / abdomen | Thigh |
| Tissue quality | Mucosal flap = moist; rectus = spongy / muscle | Keratinized skin |
| Sexual function | IUF mean IIEF-5 17.3 | 91.1% preserved erection (Pang SR) |
| Evidence base | Smaller cohorts (largest n = 74 IUF) | Largest evidence (327 procedures across 14 studies in Pang SR) — referenced in NCCN / EAU-ASCO |

STSG has a larger published outcome base in the cited review. The available reports do not establish a universal graft-versus-flap hierarchy; selection depends on disease, defect, preservable tissue, donor considerations and expertise.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

## Guideline Context

Current EAU guidance does not mandate a specific neoglans flap hierarchy.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

- NCCN guidance addresses neoglans reconstruction with STSG or FTSG after glansectomy<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- EAU supports selected organ-sparing surgery with reconstruction but no specific flap or graft type

Choice is left to surgeon expertise and patient anatomy.

---

## Postoperative Surveillance

Follow-up depends on the cancer treatment category and nodal status, not the flap alone. Current EAU guidance lists three-monthly primary-site review for years 1–2 and annual review for years 3–5 after partial penectomy or amputation; penile-preserving pathways use three-monthly then six-monthly review.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

- Clinical examination on the treatment-specific schedule
- Patient self-examination education
- Biopsy of any suspicious lesion
- Salvage organ-sparing surgery for small recurrences not involving the corpora cavernosa

## Key Takeaways

- The IUF cohort of Chavarriaga (n = 74) supplies longer-term selected-cohort oncologic and PROM data, but its abstract does not report stenosis, necrosis, curvature or sensation
- The Gulino technique mobilizes and spatulates the distal urethra; thermal and tactile sensibility was reported in 14/14 without a comparator
- The Mazza/Cheliz series (n = 34) is two-stage, with depilation in 6/34, partial necrosis in 2/34 and stenosis in 1/34
- The original Shaeer case reported similar consistency, color and shape at six months; general effectiveness and donor risk are not established
- Cross-series percentages use different populations and definitions and cannot rank flap or graft choices
