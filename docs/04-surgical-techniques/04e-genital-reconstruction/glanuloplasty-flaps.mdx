# Glanuloplasty With Flaps

Flap glanuloplasty uses pedicled local or regional tissue to create a neoglans after partial penectomy, glansectomy, or selected traumatic tissue loss. Reported methods include urethral, scrotal, and abdominal-tissue flaps, but the published evidence consists largely of small case series and case reports with non-uniform indications and outcomes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

This page is an overview, not a recommendation for a particular flap. In penile-cancer care, oncologic resection and surveillance determine whether and how reconstruction is offered; current EAU guidance does not prescribe a particular neoglans flap.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

---

## Reported Flap Families

| Flap family | Published context | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Urethral flaps | Urethral tissue has been used to cover the distal corporal stump after partial penectomy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | Small oncologic series; no comparative evidence establishes a preferred orientation, length, or expected sensory/sexual outcome. |
| Scrotal flaps | Pedicled scrotal tissue has been reported for neoglans reconstruction and for selected shaft-coverage defects.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | The evidence is limited, and donor-site characteristics, staged procedures, and hair-bearing tissue require individualized consideration. |
| Abdominal myofascial flaps | A rectus-abdominis myofascial neoglans has been described in a single traumatic reconstruction report.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | A case report cannot establish safety, durability, or comparative aesthetic and functional benefit. |

## Treatment Boundary

For glans-confined penile cancer, the EAU guideline lists local excision, partial glansectomy, and total glansectomy with reconstruction among options depending on disease extent and the feasibility of oncologic control. A graft is commonly used after glansectomy; no guideline-supported hierarchy of flap reconstruction was recovered in this audit.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

The selection of a flap depends on the defect, availability and quality of local tissue, urethral involvement, prior treatment, donor-site consequences, cancer-control requirements where relevant, and the person’s urinary, sexual, appearance, and revision priorities. These decisions belong to the treating reconstructive and oncologic team.

## Outcomes and Follow-up

Outcome reports should clearly separate cancer control from reconstruction-specific healing and function. Relevant domains include flap survival, wound and meatal/urethral complications, urinary stream, sexual and sensory function, appearance, quality of life, and further intervention. Existing publications do not support a universal success rate, a “most natural” flap, or a standard postoperative regimen.

After oncologic reconstruction, scheduled examination for local recurrence remains essential. The intensity and duration of follow-up are based on stage, pathology, and the responsible cancer team.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
