# Lotus Petal Flap for Vulvar Reconstruction

The **lotus petal flap** is a fasciocutaneous transposition flap based on **perforators of the internal pudendal artery**, first described by Yii and Niranjan in 1996. It is now a workhorse in perineal and vulvar soft-tissue reconstruction — reliable, thin, sensate, with a concealed donor scar, and **superior functional / cosmetic results compared with V-Y advancement for primary vulvar malignancies**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For the broader treatment menu see the [Vulvar Reconstruction Atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar). For the underlying flap design see the foundations [Lotus Petal Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/lotus-petal) page. For adjacent vulvar atlas pages see [Medial-Thigh V-Y](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar-medial-thigh-vy), [Gluteal V-Y](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar-gluteal-vy), [Pubolabial V-Y](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar-pubolabial-vy), [Limberg / Rhomboid](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar-limberg-flap), and the foundations [Singapore (pudendal-thigh)](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/singapore-pudendal-thigh) and [IGAP / Gluteal-Fold](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/igap-gluteal-fold) pages.

## Nomenclature and Synonyms

The lotus petal flap is known by several interchangeable names — clarifying nomenclature is important.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

| Term | Origin / scope |
|---|---|
| Lotus petal flap (LPF) | Named because cumulative perineal flap designs resemble petals of a lotus<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Singapore flap | Wee & Joseph 1989 — original pudendal-thigh flap for vaginal reconstruction<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Pudendal thigh flap | Same as Singapore — axial-pattern variant<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Gluteal-fold flap | When harvested from the gluteal sulcus territory<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| IPAP flap | Modern perforator-based evolution (Hashimoto 2014)<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |

All share the same vascular territory — terminal branches of the internal pudendal artery — but differ in design (peninsula vs island, fascial vs supra-fascial, transposition vs propeller vs V-Y).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Vascular Anatomy and Blood Supply

### Primary supply

- **Internal pudendal artery perforators** — the dominant supply via the IPA's terminal branches (superficial perineal / posterior labial artery) which give direct cutaneous perforators to the perineal skin.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **3–5 perforators** consistently present in the perineal anogenital triangle; mean **27.3 mm from the ischial tuberosity** to the IPA perforator.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Giroux et al. (2021) identified 24 perforators &gt; 5 mm in 15 subjects on color-Doppler — confirming routine preoperative imaging is not required for safe harvest.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### Contributing arteries — the "petals"

The perineal vascular territory has contributions from multiple arterial systems, each corresponding to a different "petal":<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

| Petal | Source |
|---|---|
| Upper / anterior | External pudendal aa. (superficial and deep) |
| Lateral | Obturator a. perforators |
| Medial (thigh) | Medial circumflex femoral a. |
| Lower / posterior (gluteal fold) | Gluteal aa. |

These form a dense network of epifascial and subfascial anastomoses, with four relatively constant perineal perforators identified in microdissection studies.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Sensory innervation

- **Perineal branches of the pudendal nerve** provide flap sensation.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Posterior cutaneous nerve of the thigh** contributes to the gluteal-fold territory.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- The flap retains sensory innervation of the corresponding perineal region — a sensate reconstruction.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Indications

| Application | Detail |
|---|---|
| Hemivulvectomy | Unilateral LPF<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Total vulvectomy | Bilateral LPF<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Vulvar carcinoma / melanoma | 66.1% of the Argenta indication mix<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| Vulvar dysplasia / VIN | 20.3% of Argenta<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| Extramammary Paget disease | Reliable coverage |
| Hidradenitis suppurativa | 5.1% of Argenta<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| Pelvic-floor reconstruction after ELAPE | Bilateral LPF with possible neovagina creation<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| Posterior perineal defects | Chronic presacral cavities, perineal resurfacing<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| Vaginal reconstruction | Bilateral flaps for partial / complete neovagina<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Rectovaginal fistula repair | Interposition<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Colorectal disease | 8.5% of Argenta<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |

### Defect classification — Negosanti algorithm

| Defect type | Reconstruction |
|---|---|
| **IA** — half-vulvar resection, vagina preserved | Monolateral LPF |
| **IB** — total vulvar resection, vagina preserved | Bilateral LPF |
| **II** — vulvar + vaginal resection with pelvic dead space | **Pedicled DIEP preferred over LPF** |

## Surgical Technique

1. **Position** — lithotomy.
2. **Design** — elliptical / petal-shaped skin island marked along the gluteal fold / genitocrural sulcus, long axis aligned along the crease. Base positioned over the dense perforator zone near the ischial tuberosity.
3. **Perforator identification** — ischial tuberosity is the key landmark; perforators within ~27 mm medial to it. Handheld Doppler optional, not mandatory.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
4. **Incision and elevation** — original technique is subfascial; supra-fascial elevation is also feasible.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
5. **Transposition** — flap rotated medially on its pedicle into the vulvar defect; arc centered on the perforator cluster near the ischial tuberosity.
6. **Inset** — sutured into the defect; folded / tubularized for vaginal reconstruction.
7. **Donor closure** — primary, with scar concealed in the gluteal fold.

Key technical points:

- Raise unilaterally (hemivulvectomy) or bilaterally (total vulvectomy).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- Mean defect surface area covered: **29 cm²** (Argenta) to **157.9 cm²** (Sawada bilateral).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- Median operative time **118 min** (supra-fascial variant).<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

## Variants and Modifications

| Variant | Author / year | Concept |
|---|---|---|
| **Tunneled LPF**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Confalonieri 2017 | Flap passed through a subcutaneous tunnel — avoids visible skin bridge; superior functional / cosmetic results vs V-Y for primary malignancies |
| **Supra-fascial LPF**<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | Bodin 2012 | Supra-fascial elevation — faster, easier, less invasive; n = 5 elderly (mean 72 y), no flap failure / partial necrosis; OR 118 min |
| **Thinned cutaneous LPF**<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> | Warrier 2004 | Thinned flap without identifying the blood supply; sited over the dense perforator zone; n = 8 / 12 flaps, no flap failure |
| **IPAP flap**<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | Hashimoto 2014 | Perforator-based, free-style pedicle perforator flap; can be propeller, transposition, or V-Y; 71 flaps in 45 cases — 94.4% complete survival, 4 partial necrosis, 0% total loss |
| **Supra-fascial LPF for posterior perineal defects**<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> | Bodin 2015 | Extension of supra-fascial technique to presacral cavities / posterior perineal resurfacing; n = 6, no wound complications or flap necrosis at mean 20.5 mo |

## Comparison with Other Flaps

### LPF vs V-Y advancement (Confalonieri 2017, n = 284)

| Parameter | LPF (n = 106) | V-Y (n = 128) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overall complication rate | **13%** | 21% | 0.588 (NS) |
| Primary-tumor complications | Lower | Higher | 0.202 (NS) |
| Recurrent-tumor complications | Comparable | Comparable | 0.281 (NS) |
| Functional / cosmetic results | **Superior** (tunneled variant) | Inferior | — |

<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Transposition vs advancement flaps (Di Donato SR 2017)<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

| Group | Series | n | Overall complication rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Advancement (V-Y gluteal fold, medial thigh) | 11 | 165 | **26.7%** |
| Transposition (LPF, gluteal-thigh, ALT) | 13 | 278 | **22.3%** |

Complication rates were comparable between the two groups.

### Perforator vs non-perforator flaps (Wendelspiess meta-analysis 2024, n = 1,840)<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

- Overall short-term surgical complication rate comparable between perforator (n = 276) and non-perforator (n = 1,564) flaps (p &gt; 0.05).
- Tendency toward fewer complications with perforator flaps.
- QOL assessment scarce across studies.

---

## Risk Factors for Complications

Hellinga 2018 (n = 93 patients, 137 flaps) — largest dedicated LPF complication study:<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

- **Younger age** (&lt; 60 y) associated with higher complication risk (p = 0.015).
- **Bilateral LPF** vs unilateral — bilateral has higher complication risk.
- **Donor-site morbidity** in 14% of patients.
- **Major complications (grade ≥ IIIb)** in 18.3% of patients.

Larger consecutive series reveal higher complication rates than smaller selected series — Argenta noted that "previous reports of smaller series likely underestimate the risk of complications through case selection."<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

---

## Quality of Life and Functional Outcomes

### Kwong 2025 prospective study (n = 69 women, 136 flaps)<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

- 92.6% of flaps had none-to-mild complications at 7 d.
- At 12 mo — significant improvements in genital symptoms (p &lt; 0.05), urinary continence (48.1% → 80.4%, p = 0.004), sexual activity (9.3% → 24.4%, p = 0.04), body image, and cancer-acceptance scores.

### Hellinga 2020 QOL / sexual / physical functioning study<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

- No significant QOL differences between LPF and control (primary closure) groups.
- 33% of patients sexually active postoperatively vs 87% preoperatively.
- No perineal herniation.
- Bilateral hip-joint range of motion smaller than accepted values postoperatively.

### Sawada 2004<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- No pain or abnormal sensation at flap or donor site.
- No severe damage to excretion, hip mobility, or perineal sensation.
- Concluded LPF is "one of the most ideal reconstructive procedures for the vulvoperineal region from various viewpoints of oncology, function, wound healing, and cosmetic surgery."

## Position in Reconstructive Algorithms

| Algorithm | Position |
|---|---|
| **Höckel 2008**<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | Pudendal-thigh / lotus-petal for lateral and hemivulvectomy defects; bilateral for total vulvar defects and distal circumferential vaginal defects; lower (gluteal-sulcus) petal preferred for concealed donor scar |
| **Negosanti 2015**<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | Type IA → monolateral LPF; Type IB → bilateral LPF; Type II → pedicled DIEP. LPF + DIEP together can repair all kinds of wide vulvar defects |
| **Toulouse 2025**<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> | Perforator flaps (IPAP, DFAP) first-line over traditional non-perforator flaps; IPAP preferred for posterior and lateral defects |
| **Salgarello 2005**<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | Island V-Y is the workhorse; pedicled pudendal-thigh / lotus-petal listed alongside for small-to-medium defects |

## Advantages

| Advantage | Detail |
|---|---|
| Superior functional / cosmetic results | Tunneled variant better than V-Y for primary vulvar malignancies<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Thin and pliable | Inherently thin profile matches vulvar tissue; can be further thinned<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[17]](#ref17)</sup> |
| Sensate | Retains perineal sensation; no pain or abnormal sensation postoperatively<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| Concealed donor scar | Hidden in the natural gluteal fold<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| No muscle sacrifice | Fasciocutaneous design preserves underlying musculature<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| Reliable vascular supply | 3–5 consistent perforators; no total flap loss reported across major series<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| Versatile | Unilateral or bilateral; vulvar, vaginal, perineal, and pelvic-floor reconstruction<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[12]](#ref12)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| Fast and technically simple | Supra-fascial variant ~118 min; no microsurgery required<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| Compatible with inguinofemoral LND | Donor pedicle preserved<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| No preoperative imaging required | Perforators reliably harvested without Doppler or CTA<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Significant QOL gain at 12 mo | Genital symptoms, urinary continence, sexual function, body image, cancer acceptance<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> |

## Limitations

| Limitation | Detail |
|---|---|
| Higher complication rates in larger unselected series | Argenta 36% overall in 59 consecutive patients — smaller series underestimate risk through case selection<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| High minor wound complication rate | Superficial wound separation most common (~15%); in irradiated patients, minor complications reach 46.4%<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| Younger patients (&lt; 60 y) at higher risk | p = 0.015<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> |
| Not suitable for very large defects with pelvic dead space | Type II → DIEP or VRAM for adequate volume<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Limited advancement distance | Transposition arc limited by pedicle length; very anterior defects out of reach |
| Prior radiotherapy | Increased complication risk, particularly for pelvic-floor reconstruction<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| Partial flap loss ~5–9% | Occasionally requires skin grafting or revision<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| Donor-site morbidity 14% | Lange / Hellinga<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> |
| Reduced hip ROM postoperatively | Hellinga 2020<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> |

## Key Takeaways

1. Lotus petal flap = thin, sensate, reliable fasciocutaneous transposition flap on IPA perforators (Yii & Niranjan 1996); now a vulvoperineal workhorse.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
2. **Tunneled variant superior to V-Y** in functional and cosmetic results for primary vulvar malignancies; comparable complication rate (~13% vs 21%, p = 0.588).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
3. **No total flap loss** reported across all major series — exceptional vascular reliability.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
4. **Supra-fascial modification** simplifies harvest without compromising outcomes; the **IPAP perforator evolution** is the modern Toulouse-algorithm standard.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[18]](#ref18)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
5. Prospective data confirm significant 12-month improvements in genital symptoms, urinary continence, sexual function, body image, and psychological coping.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
6. Best suited for lateral, posterior, and hemivulvectomy defects; bilateral for total vulvar and neovagina creation; Type II defects with pelvic dead space → DIEP or VRAM.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
