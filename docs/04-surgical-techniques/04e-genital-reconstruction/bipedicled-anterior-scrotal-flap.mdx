# Bipedicled Anterior Scrotal Flap

A **bipedicled anterior scrotal flap** is a reported local-tissue option for acquired penile shaft skin deficits. Published descriptions use scrotal tissue with bilateral pedicles to resurface selected defects. The available clinical evidence consists of retrospective series with varied diagnoses, designs, and follow-up; it does not establish a universal reconstructive sequence, superiority over grafting or other flaps, or an individual outcome prediction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

See [Penile Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile) for the broader reconstruction framework and [Penile Skin Grafting](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile-skin-grafting) for a distinct coverage approach.

## Reported anatomy and use

A cadaveric microvascular study described lateral scrotal territories supplied by the inferior external pudendal system and a central territory supplied through perineal arteries, with anastomoses between territories.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> This supports the anatomical rationale for described scrotal flaps, but does not validate a fixed pedicle, dissection plane, flap dimension, or safety margin for every patient.

The published cohorts include patients with foreign-material injection complications, buried penis, and other acquired shaft skin deficits.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Their heterogeneous diagnoses and local practice patterns mean that a reported indication is not a stand-alone selection recommendation.

## What the clinical reports show

| Report | Design and population | Source-specific result | Important boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fakin et al., 2017 | Retrospective 43-person silicone-injection cohort; mean follow-up 10.69 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | The abstract reports partial necrosis in 9%, donor-site hematoma in 12%, partial wound disruption in 19%, mean satisfaction 4.37/5, and self-reported erection/intercourse ability after reconstruction. | One diagnosis, no comparator, short mean follow-up, and self-reported outcomes; it cannot establish comparative benefit or long-term durability. |
| Mendel et al., 2023 | Retrospective 22-person mixed-indication series (2009–2017); only 12 completed the functional questionnaire.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | The abstract reports early and late wound/shape complications and late revision in 27.3%; questionnaire respondents reported median global satisfaction 8/10. | Small mixed cohort with incomplete questionnaire response; the results should not be generalized by diagnosis or used as a personal risk estimate. |
| Lumbiganon et al., 2023 | Retrospective 42-person foreign-body-granuloma series; 23 single-stage and 12 two-stage flap reconstructions, with other procedures in the remainder.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | Fever and the proportion without Clavien-Dindo complications differed statistically; the abstract did not find statistically significant differences in infection, dehiscence, or reoperation. Hospital stay was shorter with single-stage reconstruction. | Nonrandomized treatment assignment, small comparison groups, and one diagnosis preclude a general single- versus two-stage rule. |
| McLaughlin et al., 2024 | Eight-person staged bipedicle-flap case series for varied acquired defects.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | The abstract reports satisfactory postoperative outcomes and two minor complications. | Level-V, small, uncontrolled series; it supports feasibility only. |

## Interpretation and limits

- Neither the cited series nor the cadaveric study establishes that a bipedicled anterior scrotal flap is preferable to split- or full-thickness grafting, another local flap, or other reconstruction for a particular defect.
- The reports do not support fixed defect dimensions, a mandatory single- or two-stage approach, a contamination threshold, a timing rule, or expected sensory/erectile outcomes for an individual.
- Reported late skin retraction, testicular ascension, penile-shape change, shortening, and revision in the 2023 mixed series are relevant counseling observations, but arose in a small retrospective cohort and cannot be assumed across techniques or populations.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Technical execution, disease-specific workup, wound management, and postoperative follow-up require a reconstructive team’s individualized assessment.

## Key takeaways

1. Bipedicled anterior scrotal flaps are reported local-tissue options for selected acquired penile shaft skin deficits.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
2. Current evidence is limited to small, retrospective, heterogeneous series; direct comparative evidence is lacking.
3. The one available retrospective stage comparison does not establish a general stage-selection rule.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
