# Modified Bilateral "Butterfly" Scrotal Flap (Yao Technique)

The **Yao butterfly flap** is a reported single-stage bilateral scrotal flap in which a flap from each side of the midline raphe is pedicled on an anterior scrotal artery and used for penile-skin shortage. The original abstract describes seven selected patients, satisfactory outcomes, a statistically significant postoperative increase in measured flaccid and erect length, and small necrotic areas in 2/7 that healed with wound care. The abstract omits the length-change magnitude; full-text Table 1 gives paired flaccid measurements, with a flaccid-only test (p &lt; 0.001), but no erect-length measurements or comparator. Follow-up visits were scheduled through postoperative day 30.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For the related single-flap, central-window variant, see [Bipedicled Anterior Scrotal Flap (Fakin)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/bipedicled-anterior-scrotal-flap). For the tunnel variant, see [Modified Bipedicle Scrotal Tunnel Flap (Murányi)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/muranyi-scrotal-tunnel-flap). For the graft alternative, see [Penile Skin Grafting](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile-skin-grafting). For the full decision framework, see [Penile Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile).

## Rationale

The authors positioned the modification against limitations they attributed to prior approaches. These are design rationales, not head-to-head comparative findings:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Prior approach | Limitation |
|---|---|
| Skin grafts (STSG / FTSG) | Scarring / contracture; depend on a well-vascularized recipient bed and fail when residual foreign material on the corpora prevents take<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Pribaz staged scrotal flap | Two-stage; prolonged recovery<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Fakin bipedicled flap | Single midline flap with a central window and bilateral pedicles<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| Jeong (1996) bilateral flaps | T-style ventral junction; later authors proposed modifications to reduce junctional wound concerns, without a statistical comparative analysis<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |

Reported design feature: two flaps, each on an anterior-scrotal-artery pedicle, avoiding the older T-style junction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Vascular Anatomy

The butterfly design exploits the three Carrera scrotal territories:<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- **Two lateral territories.** Each supplied by an inferior external pudendal artery entering at the midpoint of the scrotal root, fanning over the corresponding hemiscrotum
- **One central territory.** Supplied by branches of the perineal arteries (internal pudendal system) via the posterior scrotum
- **Wide inter-anastomoses** connect the three scrotal arterial territories.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

Each butterfly wing is centered over an anterior scrotal artery (terminal branch of the external pudendal). Dividing the design along the midline raphe is intended to leave each lateral flap on its own reported pedicle.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

---

## Indications

- **Penile skin shortages** following trauma (avulsion, bite injuries)
- **Scar contracture** with pain or erectile dysfunction after prior penile surgery
- **Circumferential or near-circumferential shaft defects** after tumor excision
- **Foreskin / penile-shaft deficiency**
- **Foreign body granuloma (paraffinoma, siliconoma; extrapolated to Yao).** Jeong used a different bilateral flap in 17 paraffinoma cases where residual material could preclude skin grafting; Fakin reported a bipedicled flap in 43 siliconoma cases. Neither etiology appears in Yao's seven-patient table.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

**Prerequisite:** intact uninvolved scrotal skin with adequate laxity on both sides of the midline raphe.

## Operative Technique

The JoVE publication is a visual protocol. The sequence below is a general outline of the technique. Flap dimensions, dissection plane, catheter duration and activity restrictions vary by surgeon and defect.

### 1. Penile degloving and defect creation

- Penis on stretch
- Set excision margins by the involved penile skin and scar; the Yao protocol does not specify routine coronal and penoscrotal circumferential incisions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Remove involved scar and penile skin until the shaft is released.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Measure circumference and length of the resulting defect

### 2. Flap design — the "butterfly wings"

- Two symmetric flaps on the anterior scrotum, one on each side of the midline raphe
- Yao describes a midscrotal incision approximately half the penile circumference, then a transverse incision that creates two long trapezoidal flaps, each approximately the length of the stretched penis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Width** of each flap ≈ half the shaft circumference (so the two flaps meet at dorsal and ventral midlines)
- **Length.** Match the measured defect on a fully stretched penis; Yao used each flap's length equal to stretched penile length.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Pedicle.** At the lateral scrotal root (anterior scrotal artery entry); free edge along the midline raphe

### 3. Flap elevation

- Elevate from medial to lateral, beginning at the midline raphe
- Elevate the flaps by blunt dissection along the tunica vaginalis, preserving as much superficial dartos on each flap as possible.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Carefully preserve the anterior-scrotal-artery pedicle at the lateral root
- Tunica vaginalis kept intact; testes transiently exposed but covered

### 4. Flap rotation and wrapping

- Position the denuded shaft between the two elevated wings
- Each flap rotated medially and superiorly from its lateral pedicle
- Right flap covers right hemi-circumference; left flap covers left hemi-circumference
- Flaps meet at dorsal midline and ventral midline of the shaft

### 5. Inset and suturing

- **Distal (coronal) circumferential suture line.** Distal flap edges to the subcoronal margin
- **Proximal (base) circumferential suture line.** Proximal flap edges to penile base / pubic skin
- **Dorsal midline longitudinal suture line.** Free edges of the two flaps together
- **Ventral midline longitudinal suture line.** Free edges together (end-to-end, not T-junction)
- The Yao protocol does not specify suture material or pattern for these penile inset lines.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### 6. Scrotal donor-site closure

- Advance remaining posterior and lateral scrotal skin medially; close primarily
- In Yao's protocol, place a drain at the scrotal base and secure it to the skin with 2-0 silk.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Testes re-covered by the remaining scrotal envelope

### 7. Dressing and postoperative care

- **Dressing and review.** Apply a light compressive dressing. In Yao's protocol, the drain is removed and the dressing changed on day 3; the dressing is changed again on day 5, with examinations on days 7, 14 and 30.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Place a urethral catheter intraoperatively for drainage and urethral protection, as in Yao's protocol; postoperative catheter duration was not specified.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Yao's postoperative protocol does not state a fixed interval for resuming sexual activity or strenuous activity.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Number of flaps** | **2 (independent bilateral)** | 1 midline | 1 tunnel | 2 bilateral | 2 bilateral | 1 midline |
| **Pedicle** | Bilateral, independent | Bilateral, shared | Bilateral via tunnel | Bilateral | Bilateral | Neovascularization |
| **Staging** | Single | Single | Single | Single | Single | Two-stage |

| **Window / tunnel** | None | Window | Tunnel | None | None | None |
| **Series size** | 7 | 43 | 49 | 17 | 34 (14 inverted-V) | 8 |

## Outcomes — Yao Series (n = 7)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- Etiologies: trauma (5/7) and post-infectious defects (2/7); no post-tumor case was included.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Authors described overall outcomes as satisfactory;** no individual patient satisfaction scores or validated satisfaction instrument were reported.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Partial flap necrosis 2 / 7 (28.6%).** Small areas only, resolved with conservative wound care; no reoperation<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **No complete flap loss reported**
- **Measured flaccid penile length increased** from 3.2–4.3 cm preoperatively to 7.8–9.2 cm postoperatively in 7/7 patients (t-test, p &lt; 0.001). The abstract also claims increased erect length, but the full text gives no erect measurements or untreated comparator.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- The full text reports postoperative IIEF-5 for all seven patients (median 23, range 5–25); baseline IIEF-5 and a validated satisfaction measure were not reported, and one patient had no intercourse during follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Advantages

1. **Single-stage.** Avoids second operation and temporary penile burial
2. **Separate bilateral pedicles.** Each wing has its own reported anterior-scrotal-artery pedicle; any outcome advantage over a shared design remains theoretical
3. **No tunnel or central window.** Direct rotation avoids those design elements, but comparative pedicle-compression risk has not been established
4. **No T-junction.** The ventral end-to-end closure avoids the older junction geometry; a lower necrosis rate has not been demonstrated
5. **Potential asymmetric sizing.** The separate wings might be sized differently for an eccentric defect when both donor areas are adequate; Yao reported symmetric flaps and no asymmetric-case outcome.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
6. **Measured flaccid-length change.** In seven patients, tabulated lengths were 3.2–4.3 cm before and 7.8–9.2 cm after surgery (p &lt; 0.001); there was no comparator, and follow-up visits were scheduled through day 30.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
7. **Skin match.** Thin, elastic scrotal skin can resurface the shaft, but donor hair may remain visible after transfer.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
8. **No distant donor site.** Same operative field
9. **Conceptually straightforward.** No microsurgical expertise required

---

## Limitations and Disadvantages

1. **Very small published series (n = 7).** Complication profile and long-term outcomes remain imprecise<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
2. **Partial necrosis in 2/7.** A small denominator; cross-series percentages cannot rank this design against Fakin or Mendel<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
3. **Suture-line and contracture trade-off.** Yao creates dorsal and ventral longitudinal junctions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Murányi also creates dorsal and ventral longitudinal lines, the latter after an inverted-V incision; Fakin described a single bipedicled anterior scrotal flap.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> In broader scrotal-flap experience, ventral scar contracture can tether the shaft and require revision.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
4. **Hair-bearing variability.** Inspect hair distribution in the planned anterior-scrotal donor skin and counsel about possible shaft hair after transfer.
5. **Donor-site and late-shape concerns.** Testicular ascension and skin retraction were reported in a different bilateral-scrotal-flap cohort and are counseling context, not Yao-specific rates<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
6. **Restricted case mix.** The seven patients had traumatic (5/7) or post-infectious (2/7) defects; use after tumor surgery or for paraffinoma remains extrapolation beyond this cohort.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
7. **Follow-up scheduled only through day 30.** Late flap durability, contracture, donor morbidity, and sexual outcomes remain unreported for this cohort.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Comparison With the Original Jeong (1996) Bilateral Flap

The Yao butterfly is a direct modification of Jeong's bilateral scrotal flap:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

| Feature | Jeong (1996) | **Yao butterfly (2022)** |
|---|---|---|
| Ventral anastomosis | T-style coronal-scrotal junction | **End-to-end midline** |
| Ventral junction | T-style, later modified because of wound concerns | End-to-end; comparative risk reduction unproven |
| Indication | Paraffinoma only | Trauma (5/7) and post-infectious defects (2/7)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Penile length assessment | Not reported | Measured flaccid length increased (p &lt; 0.001); erect values not tabulated<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Format | Text description | Video article (JoVE) |
| Series size | 17 | 7 |

The Shin inverted-V modification (2013, n = 14) also changed the ventral junction. Shin reported that all 14 inverted-V flaps survived without ventral skin necrosis but did not provide a statistical comparison; Yao's end-to-end geometry likewise has no proven comparative anti-necrosis effect.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Adjunctive Refinements

### Kim Y-V dorsal-base advancement (length preservation)

Y-V advancement at the dorsal penile base plus partial suspensory-ligament resection was reported with a different bipedicled scrotal flap in five patients.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

### Zhao suspensory-ligament division

Combining a scrotal flap with suspensory-ligament division was reported in a separate 18-patient series. It should not be used to explain Yao's length measurements without the Yao full methods.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Patient Selection

| Choose Yao butterfly | Consider alternative |
|---|---|
| Circumferential shaft defect after trauma or infection, including scar contracture; use after tumor surgery extrapolates beyond Yao's seven cases<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Skin graft / regional flap if scrotal skin insufficient or involved |
| Adequate bilateral scrotal skin | A staged or non-scrotal option when wound-bed or donor considerations favor it; the 2025 review suggests staging for larger resurfacing areas or higher dehiscence risk (smoking, diabetes, vascular disease), without proven comparative benefit.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| A bilateral single-stage design fits the defect and donor tissue | Alternative flap or graft when its verified anatomy and wound requirements fit better |
| Avoiding a T-style junction is desired | Another design when scars, pedicle reach or donor morbidity are more favorable |
| Surgeon preference for two independent flaps | |

## Key Takeaways

- **Two independent bilateral flaps.** Each flap is on its own anterior scrotal artery, which distinguishes Yao from single-flap variants.
- **End-to-end midline ventral closure** avoids a T-style junction; comparative necrosis reduction is unproven
- The seven-patient report tabulates flaccid length before (3.2–4.3 cm) and after surgery (7.8–9.2 cm; p &lt; 0.001); its abstract also reports an erect-length increase, but erect measurements and a comparator are not provided.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Minor flap necrosis** occurred in 2 of 7 patients (28.6%) and was managed conservatively in both.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Yao has dorsal and ventral longitudinal junctions; Murányi also has dorsal and ventral longitudinal lines after a ventral inverted-V incision. Fakin described a single bipedicled anterior scrotal flap.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
