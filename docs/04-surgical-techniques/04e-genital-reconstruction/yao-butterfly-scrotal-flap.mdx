# Modified Bilateral "Butterfly" Scrotal Flap (Yao Technique)

The **Yao butterfly flap** is a reported **single-stage bilateral scrotal flap** in which a flap from each side of the midline raphe is pedicled on an **anterior scrotal artery** and used for penile-skin shortage. The original abstract describes seven selected patients, satisfactory outcomes, a statistically significant postoperative increase in measured flaccid and erect length, and small necrotic areas in **2/7** that healed with wound care. It does not provide a comparative estimate or the magnitude of length change.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For the related single-flap, central-window variant, see [Bipedicled Anterior Scrotal Flap (Fakin)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/bipedicled-anterior-scrotal-flap). For the tunnel variant, see [Modified Bipedicle Scrotal Tunnel Flap (Murányi)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/muranyi-scrotal-tunnel-flap). For the graft alternative, see [Penile Skin Grafting](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile-skin-grafting). For the full decision framework, see [Penile Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile).

## Rationale

The authors positioned the modification against limitations they attributed to prior approaches. These are design rationales, not head-to-head comparative findings:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Prior approach | Limitation |
|---|---|
| Skin grafts (STSG / FTSG) | Scarring / contracture; depend on a well-vascularized recipient bed — fail when residual foreign material on corpora prevents take<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Pribaz staged scrotal flap | Two-stage; prolonged recovery<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Fakin bipedicled flap | Single midline flap with central window — both pedicles must function simultaneously<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| Jeong (1996) bilateral flaps | T-style ventral junction; later authors proposed modifications to reduce junctional wound concerns, without a statistical comparative analysis<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |

Key reported design feature: **two flaps**, each on an anterior-scrotal-artery pedicle, avoiding the older T-style junction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Vascular Anatomy

The butterfly design exploits the three Carrera scrotal territories:<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- **Two lateral territories** — each supplied by an **inferior external pudendal artery** entering at the midpoint of the scrotal root, fanning over the corresponding hemiscrotum
- **One central territory** — supplied by branches of the **perineal arteries** (internal pudendal system) via the posterior scrotum
- **Wide inter-anastomoses** between the three territories make scrotal skin one of the most reliably perfused tissues in the perineum

Each butterfly wing is centered over an **anterior scrotal artery** (terminal branch of the external pudendal). Dividing the design along the midline raphe is intended to leave each lateral flap on its own reported pedicle. This is a defining design feature, not a demonstrated outcome advantage over single-flap variants.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

---

## Indications

- **Penile skin shortages** following trauma (avulsion, bite injuries)
- **Scar contracture** with pain or erectile dysfunction after prior penile surgery
- **Circumferential or near-circumferential shaft defects** after tumor excision
- **Foreskin / penile-shaft deficiency**
- **Foreign body granuloma** (paraffinoma, siliconoma) — flap survives even with residual material on corpora<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

**Prerequisite:** intact uninvolved scrotal skin with adequate laxity on **both** sides of the midline raphe.

## Operative Technique

The JoVE publication is a visual protocol, but its full methods and operative video were not accessible in this source pass. The inherited sequence below is retained as a practical outline pending direct comparison with that package; dimensions, dissection plane, catheter and activity restrictions should not be treated as a universal protocol.

### 1. Penile degloving and defect creation

- Penis on stretch
- Circumferential incision at the **coronal sulcus** and a second at the **penoscrotal junction** (or at the proximal margin of diseased tissue)
- Excise all involved skin / scar / granuloma to **Buck's fascia**; fully denude the shaft
- Measure circumference and length of the resulting defect

### 2. Flap design — the "butterfly wings"

- Two **symmetric flaps** on the anterior scrotum, one on each side of the midline raphe
- Each flap is a **rectangular or trapezoidal island** with its long axis along the raphe
- **Width** of each flap ≈ half the shaft circumference (so the two flaps meet at dorsal and ventral midlines)
- **Length** matches the defect length (coronal sulcus → penile base)
- **Pedicle** at the lateral scrotal root (anterior scrotal artery entry); **free edge** along the midline raphe

### 3. Flap elevation

- Elevate from medial → lateral, beginning at the midline raphe
- Plane **above the external spermatic fascia**, preserving dartos within the flap (maintains the subdermal plexus)
- Carefully preserve the anterior-scrotal-artery pedicle at the lateral root
- Tunica vaginalis kept intact; testes transiently exposed but covered

### 4. Flap rotation and wrapping

- Position the denuded shaft between the two elevated wings
- Each flap rotated **medially and superiorly** from its lateral pedicle
- Right flap covers right hemi-circumference; left flap covers left hemi-circumference
- Flaps meet at **dorsal midline** and **ventral midline** of the shaft

### 5. Inset and suturing

- **Distal (coronal) circumferential suture line** — distal flap edges to the subcoronal margin
- **Proximal (base) circumferential suture line** — proximal flap edges to penile base / pubic skin
- **Dorsal midline longitudinal suture line** — free edges of the two flaps together
- **Ventral midline longitudinal suture line** — free edges together (**end-to-end, not T-junction**)
- All with interrupted absorbable sutures

### 6. Scrotal donor-site closure

- Advance remaining posterior and lateral scrotal skin medially; close primarily
- Drain may be placed if dead-space concern
- Testes re-covered by the remaining scrotal envelope

### 7. Dressing and postoperative care

- Light compressive dressing
- Foley catheter for early postoperative urinary drainage
- Avoid sexual activity and strenuous activity 4–6 weeks

## How It Differs From Other Scrotal-Flap Techniques

| Feature | **Yao butterfly** | Fakin bipedicled<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | Murányi tunnel<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | Jeong bilateral<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | Shin inverted-V<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | Pribaz staged<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Number of flaps** | **2 (independent bilateral)** | 1 midline | 1 tunnel | 2 bilateral | 2 bilateral | 1 midline |
| **Pedicle** | Bilateral, independent | Bilateral, shared | Bilateral via tunnel | Bilateral | Bilateral | Neovascularization |
| **Staging** | Single | Single | Single | Single | Single | Two-stage |
| **Shaft suture lines** | Dorsal + ventral longitudinal | Circumferential at base + coronal | Dorsal longitudinal + ventral inverted-V | Dorsal + ventral T-style | Dorsal + ventral inverted-V | Circumferential |
| **Ventral junction** | **End-to-end midline** | Circumferential | Inverted-V → longitudinal | T-style (necrosis-prone) | Inverted-V end-to-end | N/A (staged) |
| **Window / tunnel** | None | Window | Tunnel | None | None | None |
| **Series size** | 7 | 43 | 49 | 17 | 34 (14 inverted-V) | 8 |

## Outcomes — Yao Series (n = 7)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- Etiologies: penile trauma and post-tumor surgery
- **Satisfactory outcomes in all 7**
- **Partial flap necrosis 2 / 7 (28.6%)** — small areas only, resolved with conservative wound care; no reoperation
- **No complete flap loss reported**
- **Penile length significantly increased** (flaccid + erect, p < 0.05); the abstract does not report the magnitude or a comparator
- The abstract describes satisfactory outcomes but does not supply a validated satisfaction instrument or complete erectile-function table

## Advantages

1. **Single-stage** — avoids second operation and temporary penile burial
2. **Separate bilateral pedicles** — each wing has its own reported anterior-scrotal-artery pedicle; any outcome advantage over a shared design remains theoretical
3. **No tunnel or central window** — direct rotation avoids those design elements, but comparative pedicle-compression risk has not been established
4. **No T-junction** — the ventral end-to-end closure avoids the older junction geometry; a lower necrosis rate has not been demonstrated
5. **Flexible asymmetric design** — wings can be sized independently if one hemiscrotum has less skin or the defect is eccentric
6. **Measured penile-length change** — the seven-patient report found a significant increase, without a reported magnitude or comparator in the abstract
7. **Comparable skin quality** — thin, elastic, predominantly non-hair-bearing scrotal skin
8. **No distant donor site** — same operative field
9. **Conceptually straightforward** — no microsurgical expertise required

---

## Limitations and Disadvantages

1. **Very small published series (n = 7)** — complication profile and long-term outcomes remain imprecise
2. **Partial necrosis in 2/7** — a small denominator; cross-series percentages cannot rank this design against Fakin or Mendel
3. **Two longitudinal shaft scars** (dorsal + ventral) vs single dorsal scar of Fakin / Murányi — potential cosmesis tradeoff
4. **Hair-bearing variability** — anterior scrotal skin is usually non-hair-bearing but varies by ethnicity / individual
5. **Donor-site and late-shape concerns** — testicular ascension and skin retraction were reported in a different bilateral-scrotal-flap cohort and are counseling context, not Yao-specific rates<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
6. **Single-etiology evidence (trauma / tumor / scar)** — paraffinoma applicability is extrapolated
7. **No long-term follow-up data** in the original publication

## Comparison With the Original Jeong (1996) Bilateral Flap

The Yao butterfly is a direct modification of Jeong's bilateral scrotal flap:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

| Feature | Jeong (1996) | **Yao butterfly (2022)** |
|---|---|---|
| Ventral anastomosis | T-style coronal-scrotal junction | **End-to-end midline** |
| Ventral junction | T-style, later modified because of wound concerns | End-to-end; comparative risk reduction unproven |
| Indication | Paraffinoma only | Trauma, tumor, scar contracture |
| Penile length assessment | Not reported | Significant increase (p < 0.05) |
| Format | Text description | Video article (JoVE) |
| Series size | 17 | 7 |

The **Shin inverted-V** modification (2013, n = 14) also changed the ventral junction. Shin reported fewer complications but did not provide a statistical comparison; Yao's end-to-end geometry likewise has no proven comparative anti-necrosis effect.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

---

## Adjunctive Refinements

### Kim Y-V dorsal-base advancement (length preservation)

Y-V advancement at the dorsal penile base plus partial suspensory-ligament resection was reported with a different bipedicled scrotal flap in five patients. That small series does not establish benefit or compatibility with the Yao design.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

### Zhao suspensory-ligament division

Combining a scrotal flap with suspensory-ligament division was reported in a separate 18-patient series. It should not be used to explain Yao's length measurements without the Yao full methods.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Patient Selection

| Choose Yao butterfly | Consider alternative |
|---|---|
| Circumferential shaft defect (trauma / tumor / scar) | Skin graft / regional flap if scrotal skin insufficient or involved |
| Adequate bilateral scrotal skin | A staged or non-scrotal option when wound-bed or donor considerations favor it |
| A bilateral single-stage design fits the defect and donor tissue | Alternative flap or graft when its verified anatomy and wound requirements fit better |
| Avoiding a T-style junction is desired | Another design when scars, pedicle reach or donor morbidity are more favorable |
| Surgeon preference for two independent flaps | |

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Two independent bilateral flaps** each on its own anterior scrotal artery — distinguishes Yao from single-flap variants
- **End-to-end midline ventral closure** avoids a T-style junction; comparative necrosis reduction is unproven
- The seven-patient abstract reports significant flaccid and erect length increases, without magnitude or comparator
- **28.6% minor flap necrosis** — counsel patients; managed conservatively in all cases
- Tradeoff: two longitudinal shaft scars vs one in Fakin / Murányi
