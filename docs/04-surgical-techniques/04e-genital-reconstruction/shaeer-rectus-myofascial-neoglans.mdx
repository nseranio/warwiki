# Rectus Abdominis Myofascial Neoglans (Shaeer Technique)

Shaeer and El-Sebaie described a rectus abdominis myofascial flap for neoglans construction in **one patient** with glans amputation after circumcision.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The article's accessible abstract describes a pedicled infraumbilical flap tunneled to the penile tip and sculpted around an elongated neourethra. This is an early feasibility report, not an established standard for glans reconstruction.

For the broader reconstructive framework and other named approaches, see [Glanuloplasty With Flaps](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/glanuloplasty-flaps), [Inverted Urethral Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/inverted-urethral-flap), [Gulino Everted Urethral Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/gulino-everted-urethral-flap), [Scrotal Flap Glanuloplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/mazza-scrotal-flap-glanuloplasty), and [Glansectomy With STSG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/glansectomy-stsg).

## What the report describes

The abstract reports a 12 × 4 cm infraumbilical rectus abdominis myofascial segment based on inferior epigastric vessels. It describes partial penile degloving, use of distal penile skin to elongate the urethra, subcutaneous tunneling of the flap, and sculpting around the neourethra to create a coronal impression.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> At six months, the authors reported a neoglans with a corona and terminal meatus, with appearance and consistency they considered similar to a normal glans.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Interpretation limits

- The evidence is a **single case report**, with six months of reported follow-up.
- The accessible record does not establish long-term tissue behavior, sensory, sexual, voiding, donor-site, or patient-reported outcomes.
- It does not compare this operation with graft, urethral-flap, scrotal-flap, or other neoglans approaches.
- The report does not establish a standardized protocol, selection rule, oncologic role, or expected complication rate.
- Reconstruction planning requires specialist assessment of the defect, urethral and corporal status, available tissue, donor-site risk, patient priorities, and local expertise.
