# Perineoplasty + De-Adhesion for Vulvar Lichen Sclerosus

Perineoplasty and division of adhesions are reported reconstructive treatments for selected structural sequelae of vulvar lichen sclerosus (LS), such as introital stenosis or symptomatic adhesions. They are adjuncts to—not replacements for—ongoing LS assessment, medical management, and surveillance.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For condition-level information, see [Lichen Sclerosus](/docs/clinical-conditions/03g-genital-scrotal/lichen-sclerosus). For the broader surgical map, see [Vulvar Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar).

---

## Evidence-Bounded Role

Published reports describe perineoplasty, adhesiolysis, and other procedures for selected people with function-limiting scarring after LS. These studies are mainly retrospective case series with varied indications, technique components, follow-up, and outcome measures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Surgery may be considered only after an individualized assessment of symptoms, anatomy, disease activity, medical treatment, malignancy concerns, and the person’s goals. It does not alter the underlying chronic disease biology, and recurrence or new scarring can occur.

## Care Boundary

Procedure selection, extent of tissue release or reconstruction, pathology sampling, postoperative care, and ongoing topical treatment are decisions for the responsible vulvar specialist and treating team. The available sources in this audit do not establish a universal surgical ladder, a fixed operative technique, a routine use of laser or grafting, a steroid regimen, or a predictable improvement in sexual or urinary function.

Long-term follow-up should address symptom control, recurrent adhesions or stenosis, wound healing, sexual function, urinary symptoms, and surveillance for malignant change as clinically indicated.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## What Is Not Established

- That any named procedure is a default or preferred operation for LS sequelae.
- A standard incision, flap/graft, laser method, adhesiolysis technique, postoperative dilator plan, or medication protocol.
- A reliable individual probability of symptom improvement, satisfaction, relapse, complication, or revision.
- Generalization from selected surgical series to all people with LS.
