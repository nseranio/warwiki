# Perineoplasty + De-Adhesion for Vulvar Lichen Sclerosus

**Perineoplasty and targeted de-adhesion** are options for symptomatic vulvar lichen sclerosus (LS) sequelae (persistent introital stenosis, labial or periclitoral adhesions or clitoral phimosis) despite guideline-conform topical treatment. They address scarred anatomy and do not treat the underlying inflammatory disease. The 2026 German-language S3 guideline strongly recommends that women agree to topical corticosteroid treatment before and after surgery, usually clobetasol propionate 0.05%; it does not specify a perioperative duration for vulvar procedures.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> Longer-term maintenance is tailored to disease activity rather than a fixed indefinite clobetasol dose.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> For broader LS context see [Lichen Sclerosus](/docs/clinical-conditions/03g-genital-scrotal/lichen-sclerosus); for the surgical algorithm placement see the [Vulvar Reconstruction database](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar).

---

## Indications

Surgery is reserved for established, function-limiting architectural sequelae of LS despite appropriate topical therapy; the 2026 guideline gives a weak recommendation ("we suggest") for de-adhesion, synechiolysis, perineoplasty or anatomic vulvoplasty in women with persistent introital stenosis that causes mechanical problems with voiding or intercourse despite guideline-conform topical corticosteroid treatment. It gives a weak recommendation against surgery for girls, although surgery may be indicated in individual cases to improve functional impairment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

- **Refractory dyspareunia or apareunia** from introital stenosis despite adequate clobetasol
- **Clitoral phimosis** with pain or sexual dysfunction
- **Labial / periclitoral adhesions** restricting introital access or burying the meatus
- **Complete introital obliteration** causing urinary retention is rare but reported<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Patient-stated desire to resume sexual activity is one relevant goal in Lauber's selected cohort.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Document the patient's functional goal, including relief of mechanical voiding or intercourse difficulty after topical treatment.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

Maximally treat LS topically before surgery; surgery does not replace medical therapy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> ACOG reserves surgery, which is not curative, for malignancy and postinflammatory sequelae such as labial adhesions and introital stenosis.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Procedures — simplest to most complex

Selection is driven by anatomic distribution of scar (posterior band versus anterior vestibule versus clitoral hood versus circumferential) and prior surgical history. In Lauber's respondent cohort, 18/41 underwent de-adhesion in addition to perineoplasty; Burger and Obdeijn performed posterior vestibuloplasty in 23 women, with de-hooding in four and anterior grafting in five.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> No randomized trial compares techniques head-to-head; the procedural detail below is consolidated from the available retrospective series.

### 1. Fenton's procedure (median perineotomy)

Simplest, least invasive option; it is one of the two most commonly performed procedures in Gurumurthy's 10-year series.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

- **Technique.** A single midline incision through the scarred posterior fourchette or perineum divides the constricting fibrotic band. The incision is closed transversely to widen the introitus (Heineke–Mikulicz principle).
- **Best for.** It suits mild-to-moderate posterior introital stenosis with a discrete constricting band.
- **Advantages.** It is quick, needs minimal excision and can be done under local anesthesia.
- **Limitations.** It does not address circumferential scarring, anterior vestibular disease or clitoral phimosis, and it widens less than full vestibuloplasty.

### 2. CO₂ laser adhesiolysis

The other commonly performed Gurumurthy technique.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

- **Technique.** The CO₂ laser divides labial and periclitoral adhesions. Adjacent tissue is protected and an appropriate laser setting is used. One described approach releases a pocket formed by fused labia minora by placing a probe inside it as a backstop, incising along the probe with either a CO₂ laser or scalpel, and closing the divided edges with a running delayed-absorbable suture; this is an author-described technical example, not a pediatric indication or routine protocol, and the page's guideline-based caution about surgery in girls still applies.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Best for.** It suits isolated labial or periclitoral adhesions without significant posterior stenosis. Assess prepuce-to-glans tethering and hood retractability separately.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Potential advantages.** Division is controlled and hemostasis is good. Comparative benefit over careful sharp release has not been established.
- **Re-adhesion prophylaxis.** After sharp lysis in three adolescents with recurrent adhesions, Surgicel (oxidized regenerated cellulose) was sutured to exposed surfaces; none had recurrence at one year.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

### 3. Posterior vestibuloplasty (perineoplasty) with vaginal mucosal advancement

The operation with the largest evidence base for LS-related introital stenosis (Rouzier, Lauber).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

Operative steps:

1. Transverse or inverted-U incision at the posterior fourchette / perineal body.
2. En-bloc excision of scarred, fibrotic perineal skin and underlying tissue.
3. Mobilization of posterior-vaginal-wall mucosa inferiorly to cover the defect without excessive closure tension.
4. Suture of vaginal mucosa to perineal skin edges, replacing scarred introital tissue with supple vaginal epithelium.

- **Best for.** It suits moderate-to-severe posterior introital stenosis.
- **Outcomes.** Rouzier reported dyspareunia improvement in 45/50 evaluable women and improved sexual quality in 43/50 (64 operated).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Lauber reported less dyspareunia (p = 0.02) and 37 of 40 satisfied or very satisfied (70 invited; 41 responded). These selected retrospective cohorts are not comparative efficacy estimates.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Histology.** Suspicious new, hyperkeratotic, eroded or ulcerated lesions are biopsied to exclude differentiated VIN or SCC.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> Lauber submitted all excised specimens.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### 4. Clitoral de-hooding (clitoral unroofing)

- **Technique.** Entrapped keratin debris ("smegma pearls") and hair are cleared after opening the fused prepuce. Burger's excisional variant preserves 1–2 mm of the inner preputial base and sutures the inner to outer edge after hood excision.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Goldstein and Burrows described probe lysis and an approximately 5-mm dorsal slit without tissue excision.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Best for.** It suits clitoral phimosis with pain, sexual dysfunction or retained keratinaceous material.
- **Outcomes.** Burger reported no complications among four de-hooding patients.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> In Lauber's separate cohort, 2/18 patients who underwent additional de-adhesion reported recurrent prepuce agglutination; median follow-up for the cohort was 2.3 years.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Caution.** Cadaveric dissections place the dorsal clitoral nerve deep to dense connective tissue; deep dissection or suture placement may injure it.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> Counsel about possible sensory loss or troublesome hypersensitivity after release; their frequency is not established.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

### 5. Anterior vestibuloplasty with free full-thickness vaginal mucosal graft

Burger and Obdeijn described a novel, investigational variant.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- **Technique.** In Burger and Obdeijn's five-patient graft subgroup, agglutinated anterior vestibular tissue and adjacent epithelium were excised, and full-thickness mucosa was harvested from the anterior or posterior vaginal wall. The donor wound was closed anterior to posterior to prevent narrowing; the inset graft had a tie-over pack for five days, with immobilization and transurethral catheter drainage.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Best for.** Burger and Obdeijn used this graft for left- and right-sided anterior vestibular agglutination that narrowed the vaginal entrance.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Complications.** 1/5 patients in the index series required reoperation for graft contraction and keratinization; the role of postoperative estrogen in maintaining graft viability is undefined.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Status.** The technique is investigational and needs further study.

### 6. Local skin-flap advancement

Plastic-surgery approach for extensive sequelae, using local Y-V, V-Y or pubolabial advancement or rhomboid, lotus-petal or gluteal-fold pivoting designs after excision of scarred tissue. In Rangatchew's selected 38-person flap series, dyspareunia was reported by 33 before surgery and five at short-term follow-up (seven outcomes unknown); 74% of survey respondents were satisfied or very satisfied, but recurrent LS-related pain was common over a mean 8.4-year clinical follow-up. Full long-term data and the Brauer 2016 qualitative findings live on the dedicated page: see [Local Skin Flaps for LS Sequelae](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/local-flaps-ls-sequelae).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### 7. aOAP flap (last-resort reconstruction)

Highly specialized option for selected refractory cases: skinning vulvectomy with bilateral perforator-flap reconstruction. This replaces tissue but does not cure LS or remove the need for disease surveillance. Detailed on the dedicated atlas page: see [aOAP Flap (O'Dey)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/aoap-flap).

## Choosing the appropriate technique

No randomized data; selection follows anatomic distribution and surgeon expertise.

| Clinical scenario | Described option | Evidence and anatomic rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Isolated posterior constricting band, mild stenosis | **Fenton's procedure** | Simplest, quickest, lowest morbidity |
| Isolated labial / periclitoral adhesions | **Targeted sharp or laser adhesiolysis** | Choose the method for the anatomy; Surgicel evidence is a three-person report |
| Moderate-severe posterior introital stenosis | **Posterior vestibuloplasty + vaginal advancement** | Reported in the larger available retrospective cohorts |
| Clitoral phimosis | **Clitoral de-hooding** | Targeted release; the four de-hooding patients in one series had no reported complications<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Anterior vestibular involvement | **Anterior vestibuloplasty + mucosal graft** | Novel; addresses anterior scarring |
| Multi-site involvement | **Combined session** (perineoplasty + de-hooding + adhesiolysis) | One-stage anatomic correction |
| Extensive scarring / failed prior surgery | **Local skin flaps** (Rangatchew) | Maximum tissue recruitment |
| Severe refractory disabling scarring | **Specialist assessment for aOAP or other flap** | Selected salvage reconstruction, not definitive LS cure |

| Study | n | Procedure | Dyspareunia / sexual outcome | Satisfaction | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Rouzier 2002**<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | 64 (50 evaluated) | Perineoplasty | 45/50 improved dyspareunia; 43/50 improved quality of sexual intercourse | Not reported | Not reported |
| **Gurumurthy 2012**<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | 25 | Fenton's / laser adhesiolysis | Dyspareunia outcome not reported; 80% had symptom improvement after initial surgery | — | Ten-year study period, not ten-year follow-up |
| **Burger 2016**<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | 23 | Posterior vestibuloplasty ± de-hooding ± grafting | Complications-focused report | — | Median observation 32 months; 2008–2012 surgery period |
| **Rangatchew 2017**<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | 38 | Local skin flaps | 33 reported dyspareunia before, five at short-term follow-up (seven unknown); 75% of surveyed patients with preoperative dyspareunia reported benefit | 74% of survey respondents satisfied / very satisfied | Mean 8.4 yr clinical follow-up; 78% follow-up rate |
| **Lauber 2021**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 70 invited; 41 responded | Perineoplasty ± de-adhesion (18/41) | General complaints decreased (p &lt; 0.001); dyspareunia decreased (p = 0.02), but 10/27 respondents to that question still had intercourse pain | 37/40 satisfied or very satisfied; 39/41 would recommend | Median 2.3 yr |
| **Krapf 2026**<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> | 86 respondents (148 surgical-list entries, including 3 duplicates) | Adhesion lysis for introital stenosis or clitoral phimosis | Paired FSFI mean 15.0 to 22.7 (n=71); paired FSDS-R mean 30.24 to 16.61 (n=74) | 80/86 satisfied; 82/86 would recommend | Mean surgery-to-survey interval 4.35 yr (SD 3.17) |

These small, selected, uncontrolled series suggest that many patients benefit, but differing procedures, denominators, follow-up and nonresponse limit any pooled success claim. Rouzier, but not Lauber, reported no association between the baseline factors it tested and failure; that does not exclude clinically important predictors.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Complications

Reported complications include:<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- **Wound dehiscence and flap retraction.** Rouzier reported dehiscence in 4/64, vaginal flap retraction without dyspareunia improvement in 2/64, and later splitting of residual perineal skin causing dyspareunia in 3/64.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Burger reported postoperative infection with subtotal vaginal advancement dehiscence in 1/23.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **LS reactivation with bullae** was reported in one Burger patient after postoperative steroid interruption; this single event is a caution, not a quantified risk estimate
- **Localized pain** (about 13% in one series; causality uncertain)<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Graft contraction / keratinization** when anterior vestibuloplasty with mucosal graft is performed

## Long-Term Recurrence — the Defining Caveat

Surgery does not modify LS biology. Among Rangatchew's long-term followed patients, recurrent dyspareunia was attributed to minor LS relapse in 50% (coitus preserved) and more severe relapse in 38% (apareunia); incomplete long-term follow-up limits generalization of these recurrence estimates.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> Counsel about recurrent scarring and possible revision even after technically successful repair. In a 2026 pre–post cohort from three specialty clinics, respondents surveyed a mean 4.35 years after surgery (SD 3.17) reported recurrent scarring in 29/86 and revision in 9/86; the mean interval to revision was 3.9 years.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> Only 86 of 148 surgical-list entries yielded follow-up responses, and three entries were duplicates.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> In Lauber's different cohort, 23/41 required intensified topical treatment for LS activity; that is not the same endpoint as repeat stenosis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Perioperative Principles

- **Preoperative.** Maximize topical-steroid disease control, document LS extent and consent for relapse risk. Assess the mechanical scar and discuss coexisting pelvic-floor spasm or vulvodynia as potential contributors to pain or sexual dysfunction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Arrange interdisciplinary counseling, including pelvic floor physiotherapy and sex therapy advice, as the 2026 S3 guideline strongly recommends.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Histology.** Biopsy any suspicious new or changing lesion, and submit excised tissue when needed for diagnosis or to exclude neoplasia.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Postop topical steroids.** Agree on perioperative treatment and individualized maintenance. Lauber used daily clobetasol or mometasone before and for four weeks after surgery, then encouraged twice-weekly maintenance. Lauber's regimen was series-specific.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Vaginal dilators.** Dilators can be considered after healing when appropriate. Lauber encouraged use from four weeks postoperatively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **After clitoral adhesion release.** Published protocols have included manual preputial retraction for postlysis care; its efficacy has not been investigated.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **Long-term follow-up.** Follow-up covers symptom control, treatment adjustment and surveillance for suspicious lesions. The 2026 guideline strongly recommends regular review, for example every 3 to 6 months initially and every 12 months once the disease is stable.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Positioning in the LS Surgical Ladder

| Clinical situation | Option and evidence status |
|---|---|
| Refractory introital stenosis / focal adhesions | **Perineoplasty + de-adhesion** (this page) |
| Extensive sequelae with sexual dysfunction unresponsive to conservative care | [Skinning vulvectomy + aOAP flap (O'Dey)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/aoap-flap) |
| Investigational adjunct for LS symptoms | [Microfat / nanofat ± PRP / SVF](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/microfat-nanofat-grafting) (see VLS section). The 2026 guideline suggests anatomic release for persistent mechanical narrowing.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> A randomized nanofat-plus-PRP pilot (19 assessed) reported improvement in some symptoms and signs, including stenosis, but no significant skin-elasticity gain at one year.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| Clitoral burying without broader sequelae | Targeted de-adhesion/de-hooding for LS; [Foldès reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/foldes-clitoral-reconstruction) is a separate FGM/C procedure, not a routine LS adjunct |
