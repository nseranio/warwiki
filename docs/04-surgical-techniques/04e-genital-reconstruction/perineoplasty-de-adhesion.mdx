# Perineoplasty + De-Adhesion for Vulvar Lichen Sclerosus

**Perineoplasty and targeted de-adhesion** are options for symptomatic vulvar lichen sclerosus (LS) sequelae (persistent introital stenosis, labial or periclitoral adhesions or clitoral phimosis) despite guideline-conform topical treatment. They address scarred anatomy and do not treat the underlying inflammatory disease. The 2026 German-language S3 guideline strongly recommends topical corticosteroid ointment before and after surgery (usually clobetasol propionate 0.05%, for example 4 weeks before and 4 to 12 weeks after the procedure). Longer-term maintenance is tailored to disease activity rather than a fixed indefinite clobetasol dose.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> For broader LS context see [Lichen Sclerosus](/docs/clinical-conditions/03g-genital-scrotal/lichen-sclerosus); for the surgical algorithm placement see the [Vulvar Reconstruction database](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar).

---

## Indications

Surgery is reserved for established, function-limiting architectural sequelae of LS despite appropriate topical therapy; the 2026 guideline gives a weak recommendation ("we suggest") for de-adhesion, synechiolysis, perineoplasty or anatomic vulvoplasty in women with persistent introital stenosis that causes mechanical problems with voiding or intercourse despite guideline-conform topical corticosteroid treatment. It gives a weak recommendation against surgery for girls, although surgery may be indicated in individual cases to improve functional impairment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

- **Refractory dyspareunia or apareunia** from introital stenosis despite adequate clobetasol
- **Clitoral phimosis** with pain or sexual dysfunction
- **Labial / periclitoral adhesions** restricting introital access or burying the meatus
- **Complete introital obliteration** causing urinary retention is rare but reported<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Patient-stated desire to resume sexual activity is a core selection criterion<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Maximally treat LS topically before surgery; surgery does not replace medical therapy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Procedures — simplest to most complex

Selection is driven by anatomic distribution of scar (posterior band versus anterior vestibule versus clitoral hood versus circumferential) and prior surgical history. Components are routinely combined in a single operative session. No randomized trial compares techniques head-to-head; the procedural detail below is consolidated from the available retrospective series.

### 1. Fenton's procedure (median perineotomy)

Simplest, least invasive option; it is one of the two most commonly performed procedures in Gurumurthy's 10-year series.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

- **Technique.** A single midline incision through the scarred posterior fourchette or perineum divides the constricting fibrotic band. The incision is closed transversely to widen the introitus (Heineke–Mikulicz principle).
- **Best for.** It suits mild-to-moderate posterior introital stenosis with a discrete constricting band.
- **Advantages.** It is quick, needs minimal excision, can be done under local anesthesia and has low morbidity.
- **Limitations.** It does not address circumferential scarring, anterior vestibular disease or clitoral phimosis, and it widens less than full vestibuloplasty.

### 2. CO₂ laser adhesiolysis

The other commonly performed Gurumurthy technique.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

- **Technique.** The CO₂ laser divides labial and periclitoral adhesions. Adjacent tissue is protected and an appropriate laser setting is used.
- **Best for.** It suits isolated labial or periclitoral adhesions (clitoral phimosis) without significant posterior stenosis.
- **Potential advantages.** Division is controlled and hemostasis is good. Comparative benefit over careful sharp release has not been established.
- **Re-adhesion prophylaxis.** After sharp lysis in three adolescents with recurrent adhesions, Surgicel (oxidized regenerated cellulose) was sutured to exposed surfaces; none had recurrence at one year. This case report does not establish a routine prophylactic standard.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

### 3. Posterior vestibuloplasty (perineoplasty) with vaginal mucosal advancement

The operation with the largest evidence base for LS-related introital stenosis (Rouzier, Lauber).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

Operative steps:

1. Transverse or inverted-U incision at the posterior fourchette / perineal body.
2. En-bloc excision of scarred, fibrotic perineal skin and underlying tissue.
3. Mobilization of posterior-vaginal-wall mucosa inferiorly to cover the defect without excessive closure tension.
4. Suture of vaginal mucosa to perineal skin edges, replacing scarred introital tissue with supple vaginal epithelium.

- **Best for.** It suits moderate-to-severe posterior introital stenosis.
- **Outcomes.** Rouzier reported dyspareunia improvement in 45/50 evaluable women and improved sexual quality in 43/50 (64 operated). Lauber reported less dyspareunia (p = 0.02) and 37 of 40 satisfied or very satisfied (70 invited; 41 responded). These selected retrospective cohorts are not comparative efficacy estimates.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Histology.** Suspicious new, hyperkeratotic, eroded or ulcerated lesions are biopsied to exclude differentiated VIN or SCC. Lauber submitted all excised specimens; this series protocol does not show that all otherwise benign excisions must be sent in every setting.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

### 4. Clitoral de-hooding (clitoral unroofing)

- **Technique.** The scarred, fused prepuce overlying the glans clitoridis is excised sharply, and entrapped keratin debris ("smegma pearls") and hair are expressed.
- **Best for.** It suits clitoral phimosis with pain, sexual dysfunction or retained keratinaceous material.
- **Outcomes.** Four patients in the Burger series had de-hooding, all without complications.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Caution.** The dorsal nerve of the clitoris runs immediately deep, and meticulous dissection is needed to preserve sensation.

### 5. Anterior vestibuloplasty with free full-thickness vaginal mucosal graft

Burger and Obdeijn described a novel, investigational variant.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- **Technique.** Excise scarred anterior vestibular tissue, harvest full-thickness vaginal mucosa from the anterior or posterior vaginal wall as described in the series, and inset the graft into the anterior vestibular defect.
- **Best for.** It suits anterior vestibular stenosis or scarring that posterior vestibuloplasty alone cannot address, typically with circumferential introital involvement.
- **Complications.** 1/5 patients in the index series required reoperation for graft contraction and keratinization; the role of postoperative estrogen in maintaining graft viability is undefined.
- **Status.** The technique is investigational and needs further study.

### 6. Local skin-flap advancement

Plastic-surgery approach for extensive sequelae, using local Y-V, V-Y or pubolabial advancement or rhomboid, lotus-petal or gluteal-fold pivoting designs after excision of scarred tissue. In Rangatchew's selected 38-person flap series, dyspareunia was reported by 33 before surgery and five at short-term follow-up (seven outcomes unknown); 74% of survey respondents were satisfied or very satisfied, but recurrent LS-related pain was common over a mean 8.4-year clinical follow-up. Full long-term data and the Brauer 2016 qualitative findings live on the dedicated page: see [Local Skin Flaps for LS Sequelae](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/local-flaps-ls-sequelae).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### 7. aOAP flap (last-resort reconstruction)

Highly specialized option for selected refractory cases: skinning vulvectomy with bilateral perforator-flap reconstruction. This replaces tissue but does not cure LS or remove the need for disease surveillance. Detailed on the dedicated atlas page: see [aOAP Flap (O'Dey)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/aoap-flap).

## Choosing the appropriate technique

No randomized data; selection follows anatomic distribution and surgeon expertise.

| Clinical scenario | Recommended technique | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Isolated posterior constricting band, mild stenosis | **Fenton's procedure** | Simplest, quickest, lowest morbidity |
| Isolated labial / periclitoral adhesions | **Targeted sharp or laser adhesiolysis** | Choose the method for the anatomy; Surgicel evidence is a three-person report |
| Moderate-severe posterior introital stenosis | **Posterior vestibuloplasty + vaginal advancement** | Reported in the larger available retrospective cohorts |
| Clitoral phimosis | **Clitoral de-hooding** | Targeted, low complication rate |
| Anterior vestibular involvement | **Anterior vestibuloplasty + mucosal graft** | Novel; addresses anterior scarring |
| Multi-site involvement | **Combined session** (perineoplasty + de-hooding + adhesiolysis) | One-stage anatomic correction |
| Extensive scarring / failed prior surgery | **Local skin flaps** (Rangatchew) | Maximum tissue recruitment |
| Severe refractory disabling scarring | **Specialist assessment for aOAP or other flap** | Selected salvage reconstruction, not definitive LS cure |

## Outcomes

| Study | n | Procedure | Dyspareunia / sexual outcome | Satisfaction | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Rouzier 2002**<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | 64 (50 evaluated) | Perineoplasty | 45/50 improved dyspareunia | 43/50 improved sexual quality | Not reported |
| **Gurumurthy 2012**<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | 25 | Fenton's / laser adhesiolysis | 80% improved after initial surgery | — | Ten-year study period, not ten-year follow-up |
| **Burger 2016**<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | 23 | Posterior vestibuloplasty ± de-hooding ± grafting | Complications-focused report | — | Median observation 32 months; 2008–2012 surgery period |
| **Rangatchew 2017**<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | 38 | Local skin flaps | 33 reported dyspareunia before, five at short-term follow-up (seven unknown); 75% of those with preoperative dyspareunia reported benefit | 74% satisfied / very satisfied | Mean 8.4 yr clinical follow-up; 78% follow-up rate |
| **Lauber 2021**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 70 invited; 41 responded | Perineoplasty ± de-adhesion (18/41) | General complaints decreased (p < 0.001); dyspareunia decreased (p = 0.02), but 10/27 respondents to that question still had intercourse pain | 37/40 satisfied or very satisfied; 39/41 would recommend | Median 2.3 yr |

These small, selected, uncontrolled series suggest that many patients benefit, but differing procedures, denominators, follow-up and nonresponse limit any pooled success claim. Rouzier, but not Lauber, reported no association between the baseline factors it tested and failure; that does not exclude clinically important predictors.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Complications

Reported complications include:<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- **Wound dehiscence** / infection of advanced vaginal epithelium
- **LS reactivation with bullae** was reported in one Burger patient after postoperative steroid interruption; this single event is a caution, not a quantified risk estimate
- **Localized pain** (about 13% in one series; causality uncertain)
- **Graft contraction / keratinization** when anterior vestibuloplasty with mucosal graft is performed

---

## Long-Term Recurrence — the Defining Caveat

Surgery does not modify LS biology. Among Rangatchew's long-term followed patients, recurrent dyspareunia was attributed to minor LS relapse in 50% (coitus preserved) and more severe relapse in 38% (apareunia); the series had incomplete long-term follow-up and should not be read as a universal recurrence probability.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> Counsel about recurrence even after technically successful repair. In Lauber's different cohort, 23/41 required intensified topical treatment for LS activity; that is not the same endpoint as repeat stenosis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Perioperative Principles

- **Preoperative.** Maximize topical-steroid disease control, document LS extent and consent for relapse risk.
- **Histology.** Biopsy any suspicious new or changing lesion, and submit excised tissue when needed for diagnosis or to exclude neoplasia. Do not infer an individual cancer risk from a single broad historical percentage.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Postop topical steroids.** Agree on perioperative treatment and individualized maintenance. Lauber used daily clobetasol or mometasone before and for four weeks after surgery, then encouraged twice-weekly maintenance. This is a series protocol and not a universal dose.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Vaginal dilators.** Dilators can be considered after healing when appropriate. Lauber encouraged use from four weeks postoperatively, but its observational data did not establish a benefit from dilation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Long-term follow-up.** Follow-up covers symptom control, treatment adjustment and surveillance for suspicious lesions. The 2026 guideline strongly recommends regular review, for example every 3 to 6 months initially and every 12 months once the disease is stable.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

|---|---|
| Refractory introital stenosis / focal adhesions | **Perineoplasty + de-adhesion** (this page) |
| Extensive sequelae with sexual dysfunction unresponsive to conservative care | [Skinning vulvectomy + aOAP flap (O'Dey)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/aoap-flap) |
