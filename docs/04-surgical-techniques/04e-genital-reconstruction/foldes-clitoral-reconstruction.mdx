# Foldès Clitoral Reconstruction (± OD Preputial Flap)

The **Foldès clitoral reconstruction** is a widely reported technique for exposing and repositioning residual clitoral tissue after female genital mutilation/cutting (FGM/C). In a 2024 scoping review, 38 of 40 included studies (95%) used Foldès' technique. The figure is a proportion of studies, not of patients or procedures worldwide.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Thabet and Thabet first described clitoral reconstruction in Egypt, and Pierre Foldès described it in France in 2004. The operation relies on a residual clitoral body and a preserved dorsal neurovascular supply. The remaining anatomy varies between patients.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

For the broader treatment menu see the [Vulvar Reconstruction Atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar). For the overarching genital reconstructive framework see [Genital Reconstruction Principles](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/genital-reconstruction-principles).

The 2025 WHO guideline conditionally suggests clitoral reconstruction for selected women living with FGM/C (very-low-certainty evidence). It does not endorse a specific technique. WHO emphasizes a skilled multidisciplinary service and sexual-health counseling before or alongside surgery.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

## Anatomical Basis

The clitoris is a largely internal organ. The visible glans represents only a small portion; the clitoral body extends 3–4 cm deep, anchored by the suspensory ligament to the pubic symphysis, with paired crura extending posterolaterally. After many forms of FGM/C, a clitoral stump persists beneath the scar, although residual tissue and scar patterns vary. The dorsal neurovascular bundle (branches of the pudendal nerve and dorsal clitoral artery) runs along the dorsal surface of the body and is the structure to preserve during reconstruction.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

The WHO classification of FGM/C types guides surgical planning.

## Surgical Technique (Foldès Method)

Foldès' 2012 cohort used general anesthesia via laryngeal mask. The operative report describes the following sequence; later variants are not interchangeable with its steps.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

1. **Scar excision and exposure.** Circular "buttonhole" skin incision over the clitoral shaft stump; the skin over its distal portion is sharply resected. Defibulation or pseudocyst removal was performed first when needed.
2. **Suspensory ligament transection.** Gradually transected close to the pubic bone, as deeply as needed to allow sufficient downward mobilization of the clitoral stump. It allows the retracted clitoris to be brought to its anatomical position.
3. **Neurovascular bundle preservation.** Dorsal neurovascular bundle carefully identified and preserved throughout dissection. It is needed to maintain sensation in the reconstructed neoglans.
4. **Neoglans exposure.** Residual scar is removed from the mobilized distal stump and the exposed clitoral tissue brought toward its anatomical position. The 2012 report does not specify wedge plasty or a ≥5-mm projection target as universal requirements.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
5. **Anti-retraction fixation.** An initial layer holds the neoclitoral shaft; running or interrupted Monocryl sutures inferiorly pass from the residual fibrous layer surrounding the tunica to vestibular mucosa and skin. The report does not describe bilateral bulbocavernosus anchoring.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
6. **Closure and analgesia.** Superior vestibular skin is closed with interrupted polyglactin through bilateral subcutaneous connective tissue and midline periosteum; dissected spaces receive local anesthetic. Drug and dose choices follow local protocol.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## The O'Dey Preputial Flap (OD Preputial Flap) Modification

O'Dey developed three complementary techniques for clitoral and vulvovestibular reconstruction and reported their outcomes in a retrospective cohort.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

| Technique | Frequency in O'Dey cohort | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Omega-domed (OD) flap** | 85% | Preputial reconstruction — recreating the clitoral hood (typically destroyed in FGM/C) |
| **Neurotizing and molding of the clitoral stump (NMCS)** | 82% | Refinement of the clitoral stump itself |
| **Anterior obturator artery perforator flap** | 36% | More extensive vulvovestibular reconstruction (labia minora, vestibule) |

In O'Dey's cohort of 119 women (94.1% attended the 1-year follow-up), patients reported significant improvement in clitoral sensation and ability to achieve orgasm and reduction in dysmenorrhea, dysuria and dyspareunia (all p &lt; 0.001). There was one major complication (flap loss), and 10 patients (8.4%) had secondary ambulatory interventions. The series had no comparator and reports the first author's own techniques.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Indications and Patient Selection

- **Anatomy and goals, not type alone**, guide suitability. The 2012 Foldès cohort specifically included type II or type III with clitoral excision and excluded type III without clitoral excision; reports of other types do not expand that cohort's eligibility.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Primary indications: chronic clitoral or vulvar pain, sexual dysfunction (anorgasmia, reduced desire or arousal), negative body image, desire for anatomical restoration or identity recovery.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Discuss nonsurgical care, including sexual-health counseling, as part of shared decision-making; WHO does not require a failed counseling trial as a universal prerequisite.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- Preoperative assessment includes psychosexual evaluation, anatomical education and discussion of expectations.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- Foldès' landmark cohort reported patient expectations as: identity recovery 99%, improved sex life 81%, pain reduction 29%.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Outcomes

### Foldès prospective cohort (n = 2,938)<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Only 866 of 2,938 patients (29%) attended the 1-year follow-up, and there was no comparator. The following outcomes describe respondents, not all 2,938 women.

| Anatomic result | Frequency |
|---|---|
| Hoodless visible glans | **42%** |
| Normal-appearing clitoris | **28%** |
| Visible projection | 24% |
| Palpable projection | 6% |
| No change | 0.4% |

- **430 of 841 respondents (51%) reported orgasms** at 1 year; this is not 51% of the 2,938 operated women, nor proof of a surgery-attributable gain.
- Most patients reported improvement or no worsening in pain (821/840) and clitoral pleasure (815/834).

### Meremikwu 2026 SR / meta-analysis (13 studies, nearly all Foldès)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

- A before–after pooled pain comparison (six studies, 1,116 participants) reported OR 79.67 (95% CI 41.67–152.33; I² = 82%) for reduced vulval, clitoral or intercourse pain. It is not an odds ratio against no surgery or another technique.
- The review also reported improvement in body image, self-esteem and clitoral sexual function. Certainty of evidence was very low (GRADE) across all outcomes. All but one included study used the Foldès technique.

### Almadori 2024 scoping review (40 studies, 7,274 women)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

These heterogeneous aggregate findings span the included FGM/C reconstructive studies; they are not a Foldès-specific probability of improvement or complications. More than half of the reported improvements lacked a specified outcome domain, and only 14 of 40 studies reported complications.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- **6,858/7,274 (94%) reported as improved** across differing definitions and follow-up completeness.
- **207 reported complications** across the review's surgical cohorts (about 3% by its summary); incomplete adverse-event reporting makes this an unreliable individual risk estimate.

## Complications

Published series report the following adverse events; incomplete follow-up and very-low-certainty evidence limit precise estimates of benefit and harm:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

| Type | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Immediate** (5% in Foldès cohort) | Hematoma, suture failure, moderate fever; 4% required brief re-admission |
| Other reported | Infection, edema, prolonged postoperative pain, mild inflammation, necrosis |
| **Chronic pain** | 4 of 87 women in one retrospective series; all 4 had a history of physical abuse<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **Clitoral burial** (retraction beneath the skin) | Recognized complication; patients with burial were younger in the same series (median 29.6 vs 33.8 years)<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |

## Adjuncts and Modifications

| Modification | Author / year | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **Sensate labial flaps** | Wilson & Zaki 2022<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | Innervated flaps from the labia minora to cover the neoclitoris; 40 women followed prospectively, FSFI 11.6 → 29.1 at 24 weeks |
| **Vaginal mucosal graft** | Mañero & Labanca 2018<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> | Alternative coverage technique; 32 women followed prospectively, FSFI 16 → 29 |
| **Autologous PRP (A-PRP)** | Manin 2022<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | Applied intraoperatively; a single case report suggests it may reduce pain and speed healing |

## Ethical and Counseling Considerations

Clitoral reconstruction is offered within a multidisciplinary framework with sexual-health counseling before or alongside surgery. The 2024 scoping review found psychosexual counseling offered in 15 of 40 studies (37.5%). The figure is a reporting proportion, not proof that counseling was absent in the others.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

- Patients must be informed about the limited certainty of evidence regarding benefits, the realistic range of outcomes, and the risks.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- The American Academy of Pediatrics notes that data on outcomes in adolescents are inadequate.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- Reconstruction complements, and is not the sole component of, comprehensive care for the physical, psychological and sexual health needs of FGM/C survivors.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Key Takeaways

1. Foldès-style reconstruction was used in 38 of 40 studies (95%) in a 2024 scoping review. The figure is a share of studies, not of patients worldwide.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
2. Anatomic premise: after many forms of FGM/C the clitoral body and dorsal neurovascular bundle remain beneath the scar and can be liberated and repositioned. Residual tissue varies between patients.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
3. The 2012 operative sequence is scar exposure, graded suspensory-ligament release while protecting the dorsal neurovasculature, repositioning and fixation, then closure. The report does not establish fixed projection measurements or muscle anchoring.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
4. O'Dey's OD, NMCS and aOAP procedures form a separate combined reconstructive program. Its reported improvements cannot be assigned to the Foldès operation or to one component alone.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
5. Outcomes: Foldès enrolled 2,938, but only 866 (29%) returned at 1 year; 430/841 respondents reported orgasm. Meremikwu's large pain OR is a heterogeneous before–after comparison, not treatment versus control. Review-wide improvement and complication percentages have inconsistent definitions and reporting; certainty is very low.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
6. WHO 2025 conditionally suggests surgery for selected women, with very-low-certainty evidence and counseling and multidisciplinary care; adolescents have insufficient outcome data.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
