# Foldès Clitoral Reconstruction

Foldès-style clitoral reconstruction is the most frequently reported reconstructive approach in published FGM/C surgery literature. It is a family of techniques that mobilizes residual clitoral tissue; named modifications and accompanying procedures should not be treated as interchangeable or standardized. Available outcome evidence is predominantly observational and has very low certainty.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For the broader care context, see [Female Genital Mutilation / Cutting](/docs/clinical-conditions/03g-genital-scrotal/female-genital-mutilation-cutting) and the [Vulvar Reconstruction Atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar).

## Evidence base

The pivotal prospective cohort included 2,938 adults with Type II or III FGM treated in one French center between 1998 and 2009. At one year, 866 participants (29%) returned for follow-up. Among respondents with available outcome data, the authors reported anatomic findings, pain/pleasure responses, and orgasm outcomes; immediate complications were reported in 5% of the full surgical cohort and brief readmission in 4%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Because follow-up was incomplete, outcomes were self-reported in part, and there was no untreated or alternative-procedure comparison, the cohort cannot establish a general success rate, a causal sexual-function benefit, long-term durability, or comparative superiority.

The 2026 systematic review identified 13 studies, with all but one using Foldès-style surgery. It reported improvement in pain, body image, self-esteem, and clitoral sexual function across the included studies, but graded certainty as **very low for all outcomes** and noted adverse events including hematoma, infection, suture failure, edema, prolonged pain, inflammation, and necrosis.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Technique and clinical boundary

Anatomical and technical reviews describe liberation and downward mobilization of residual clitoral tissue, with variations in scar management, glans/prepuce reconstruction, and fixation.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The recoverable sources do not support a universal incision, ligament-release depth, projection target, suture plan, anaesthesia approach, aftercare pathway, or indication threshold. The approach is not a substitute for comprehensive, trauma-informed assessment and support.

Combined O'Dey procedures, including Omega Domed, NMCS, and aOAP techniques, are reported as a multi-procedure program. Its cohort-wide outcomes cannot be attributed to a Foldès procedure or any single component.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Evidence limits

- Published reconstruction cohorts have substantial risk of bias, heterogeneous interventions, incomplete follow-up, and limited validated outcome assessment.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- No recovered study establishes which FGM/C subtype, symptom, age group, or patient preference predicts benefit from a particular technique.
- The 2024 scoping review shows that Foldès-style procedures predominate in published reports; it does not establish a procedure-specific improvement or complication rate for counselling.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Surgery should be considered within a multidisciplinary care pathway that includes clear counselling about uncertain benefits and risks.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Key takeaways

1. Foldès-style reconstruction is the predominant published technique family, but not a single standardized operation.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
2. The large prospective cohort has important follow-up and comparison limits.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
3. Current systematic-review certainty for measured outcomes is very low; informed counselling should make that uncertainty explicit.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
