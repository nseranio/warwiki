# Anterior Obturator Artery Perforator (aOAP) Flap

The **anterior obturator artery perforator (aOAP) flap** is a described local perforator-flap option for selected vulvovestibular defects. Its reported vascular basis is the anterior branch of the obturator vessels. The directly identified clinical literature is limited to retrospective experience from one center; it should not be read as a comparative treatment standard or a procedural protocol.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For the broader treatment menu, see the [Vulvar Reconstruction Atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar). The related [GCIPF (vulvar)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar-gcipf) page covers a different published reconstruction context.

## Anatomy evidence

In the original cadaveric report, 20 specimens from 10 female cadavers were assessed. The reported perforator arose from the anterior obturator vessels, was musculocutaneous in 16 specimens and septocutaneous in four, and lay a mean 1.3 ± 0.3 cm lateral to the inferior pubic ramus. The authors described a skin territory of approximately 7 × 15 cm centered on the genitofemoral sulcus.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

These measurements describe a cadaveric series. They do not establish a universal dissection route, skin-paddle size, Doppler rule, donor-site closure method, or patient-specific flap safety.

## Published clinical contexts

| Context | Directly reported evidence | What the report can and cannot show |
|---|---|---|
| Female genital mutilation/cutting reconstruction | A retrospective single-center cohort of 119 women treated from 2014–2021 reported aOAP use in 36% alongside other reconstructive procedures; one-year follow-up was available for 94.1%.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | The reported symptom and sexual-function changes are outcomes of a combined reconstructive program, not flap-specific effects. |
| Lichen sclerosus-associated sexual dysfunction | A retrospective 61-person series (2014–2022) reported skinning vulvectomy with reconstruction; bilateral aOAP flaps were used in 53 people, with one-year follow-up and reduced dyspareunia/inability to have intercourse reported by the authors.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | It has no untreated or alternative-flap comparison and cannot establish durable disease control, relative benefit, or an indication threshold. |
| Vulvoperineal oncologic reconstruction literature | A 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis of 49 studies (1,840 patients) compared perforator with non-perforator flaps across vulvoperineal cancer reconstruction. Short-term complications were comparable; quality-of-life reporting was sparse.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | This is not an aOAP-specific analysis and should not be transferred to benign vulvar disease or FGM/C reconstruction. |

## Interpreting the reported outcomes

The FGM/C cohort included Omega Domed and neurotizing/molding clitoral procedures as well as aOAP reconstruction. It reported statistically significant changes in several symptoms and sexual-function measures, one major flap loss, and 10 secondary ambulatory interventions among 119 participants.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Because procedures were combined and flap use was not randomized, those results cannot quantify the independent contribution, complication rate, sensory outcome, or durability of the aOAP flap.

The lichen-sclerosus report described several reversible minor postoperative complications and noted that altered tissue quality and chronic topical-corticosteroid exposure may contribute to minor postoperative complications.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Its one-year observation window does not establish longer-term symptom, recurrence, malignancy-surveillance, or revision outcomes.

## Evidence limits and clinical boundary

- No identified randomized or head-to-head study compares aOAP with pudendal-thigh, lotus-petal, gracilis, graft, or other reconstructive options.
- The directly identified aOAP clinical reports are retrospective, from a single center, and include concomitant procedures; patient selection and operative details should therefore remain local specialist decisions.
- Cadaveric anatomy supports a described vascular territory, not a guarantee of flap presence, tissue match, sensation, donor-site appearance, or safety in an individual patient.
- The broader perforator-flap meta-analysis is indirect and has sparse long-term patient-reported evidence.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

This page does not replace disease-specific assessment, exclusion of malignancy when clinically indicated, counseling, or longitudinal care by the treating vulvar/reconstructive team.

## Key takeaways

1. aOAP is a reported local perforator-flap option with a cadaverically described anterior-obturator vascular basis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
2. The available clinical reports describe selected, combined reconstructions rather than isolated flap comparisons.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
3. Current evidence cannot establish flap-specific functional benefit, superiority, long-term durability, or a universal operative approach.
