# Autologous Fat Grafting for FGM/C Vulvar Scars

Autologous fat grafting has been reported as an emerging approach for vulvar scars after female genital mutilation/cutting (FGM/C). The directly identified FGM/C evidence is one small uncontrolled cohort, so it should not be presented as an established regenerative treatment, replacement for other reconstruction, protocol, or outcome guarantee.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For broader context, see the [Vulvar Reconstruction Atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar) and [Female Genital Mutilation / Cutting](/docs/clinical-conditions/03g-genital-scrotal/female-genital-mutilation-cutting).

## Direct FGM/C evidence

Almadori and colleagues reported 13 FGM survivors with vulvar scars who underwent autologous fat grafting. At mean follow-up of 12.23 ± 3.03 months, the report described statistically significant changes in the Vulvar Architecture Severity Scale, Female Genital Self-Image Scale, Female Sexual Function Index, and Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

This is a small, uncontrolled cohort. Its results cannot establish causality, long-term durability, comparative effectiveness, selection criteria, optimal fat processing/injection, repeat-treatment needs, complication rates, or outcomes for a particular person. The VASS was developed and validated for vulvar lichen sclerosus; use of a scale in this FGM/C cohort does not itself validate the scale for every FGM/C scar context.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Indirect scar literature

A systematic review/meta-analysis of autologous fat transfer for varied fibrosis and scar-related conditions included 45 studies and 3,033 patients, reporting improvements in satisfaction and selected scar measures. It also emphasized the need for research on long-term effects and on mechanical/regenerative properties.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Those studies span other tissues and scar causes, so they cannot establish the mechanism, safety, efficacy, or treatment schedule for FGM/C-related vulvar scars.

## Evidence limits and clinical boundary

- No recovered randomized, sham-controlled, or comparative study evaluates fat grafting for FGM/C-related vulvar scars.
- The reported FGM/C cohort does not show that fat grafting restores removed anatomy, treats every FGM/C-related symptom, or improves outcomes versus counselling, other procedures, or no procedure.
- Molecular, cell-based, and scar-remodelling explanations from laboratory or non-vulvar scar research remain indirect for this clinical setting.
- Procedure planning, consent, follow-up, and any relationship to other reconstructive work require individualized specialist assessment and trauma-informed care.

## Key takeaways

1. Fat grafting for FGM/C-related vulvar scars has one identified small uncontrolled clinical cohort with approximately one-year follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
2. Reported changes in scar and patient-reported scales are promising but cannot establish comparative benefit or predict an individual outcome.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
3. Broader scar-fat-grafting evidence is indirect and should not be transferred as FGM/C-specific proof.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
