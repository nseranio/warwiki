# Autologous Fat Grafting (Lipofilling) for FGM/C-Related Vulvar Scars

**Autologous fat grafting (lipofilling)** is an emerging approach to symptomatic vulvar scarring and volume loss after FGM/C. Its proposed tissue-remodeling effects come largely from non-FGM scar research and have not been shown by tissue sampling in FGM/C. The Almadori, Hansen, Butler and Salgarello (2025) pilot is an uncontrolled 13-patient series. At a mean follow-up of 12.23 months it reported improved clinician-rated vulvar architecture (VASS), sexual function (FSFI), genital self-image (FGSIS) and psychological scores (HADS). These before-and-after findings do not establish comparative efficacy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For the broader treatment menu see the [Vulvar Reconstruction Atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar). For the FGM/C clitoral technique see [Foldès Clitoral Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/foldes-clitoral-reconstruction); for the published non-Foldès coverage option see [Mañero Vaginal Mucosal Graft](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/manero-vaginal-graft); for the vulvovestibular perforator-flap variant see [aOAP Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/aoap-flap).

## Rationale and Unmet Need

Vulvar scarring is a common long-term consequence of FGM/C, though prevalence and severity vary by cutting type and population.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Clitoral reconstruction excises scar to expose the stump. Its studies do not address diffuse surrounding vulvar fibrosis, which can cause pain, dyspareunia and distress. The AAP notes that FGM/C-related scarring can include unintended labial fusions, inclusion cysts, and obstructive infibulation scars.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Fat grafting has been studied for scarring and fibrosis in other body regions (3,033 patients across 45 studies in a 2020 meta-analysis). That literature gives indirect rationale. It does not establish a standard of care or a treatment effect for FGM/C scars.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Mechanism of Action

The following mechanical and biological mechanisms are proposed from general scar, cell and animal research. The FGM/C pilot measured clinical scores, not tissue histology or pathway activity; none of the molecular effects below was established in its vulvar grafts.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

### Mechanical (rigottomy effect)

- Physical injection of fat beneath and within scar tissue creates micro-tunnels that mechanically disrupt fibrotic bands and adhesions.
- May release scar tethering and improve pliability; the relative contribution of release versus graft biology is unknown.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Biological / regenerative (ADSC-mediated)

Adipose tissue contains stromal cells that show anti-fibrotic, pro-angiogenic and immunomodulatory activity in experimental settings:<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

| Mechanism | Detail |
|---|---|
| **ECM remodeling** | ADSCs upregulate MMP-1 and MMP-2 and increase the MMP/TIMP ratio, which promotes degradation of excess collagen in hypertrophic scars<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Anti-fibrotic cytokines** | ADSCs secrete HGF and TGF-β3, which inhibit fibroblast proliferation and reduce collagen I, collagen III, fibronectin, α-SMA, and CTGF<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Decorin-mediated TGF-β1 trapping** | Recent transcriptomic / proteomic analysis identified **decorin (DCN)** as the principal anti-fibrotic component of the ADSC secretome. It binds and sequesters TGF-β1 and prevents activation of the TGF-β / Smad2 signaling pathway in fibroblasts<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Pro-regenerative immune response** | Lipofilling of dermal scars was followed by infiltration of M2 macrophages, T lymphocytes and mast cells, which the authors interpret as a shift from a pro-fibrotic to a pro-regenerative immune environment<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Neoangiogenesis** | ADSCs promote new blood-vessel formation within the scar, and vascularization increased after lipofilling in biopsies<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **Epidermal normalization** | After lipofilling, epidermal cell proliferation increases to levels similar to normal skin<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Neuropathic pain reduction** | Expression of PGP 9.5 (a neuropathic-pain marker) was lower after fat grafting in vaginal biopsies in a retrospective non-FGM series<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |

## The Almadori 2025 Study<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Study design.** Prospective uncontrolled cohort, Level of Evidence IV. Improvement from baseline cannot distinguish the procedure’s effect from other care, selection, or time.

**Population.** 13 FGM/C survivors with vulvar scars; types I (2), II (5) and III (6). Eligibility included ages 18–70, no pregnancy, active infection or cancer.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Technique.** Centrifuged macro-fat was placed subcutaneously for volume, and micro-fat more superficially at the dermal junction or in scars. The mean injected volume was 9.8 mL. Patients received sedation or general anesthesia and left the same day.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Follow-up.** Mean 12.23 ± 3.03 months (range 6–18). One patient had self-resolving donor-site bruising; the authors reported no other complications in this small series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Outcome measures and results

| Outcome | Description | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **VASS** (Vulvar Architecture Severity Scale) | Clinician-assessed 6-region 4-point grading of vulvar scar severity | Significant improvement in all vulvar aesthetic units (p &lt; 0.001) |
| **FGSIS** (Female Genital Self-Image Scale) | Patient-reported genital self-image | Significant improvement (p = 0.001) |
| **FSFI** (Female Sexual Function Index) | Patient-reported sexual function across 6 domains | Significant improvement (p = 0.019) |
| **HADS** (Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale) | Patient-reported psychological well-being | Significant improvement (p = 0.002) |

## The Vulvar Architecture Severity Scale (VASS)

Almadori et al. (2020) developed the VASS as a 6-region, 4-point clinical grading system based on the aesthetic-unit principle. It was designed for vulvar lichen sclerosus and later applied to FGM/C scar assessment.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> Validation used 14 observers scoring photographs of 25 patients with lichen sclerosus.

- **Six vulvar aesthetic units** are scored independently, for example the clitoral hood, labia minora, labia majora and perineum.
- Each region graded on a 4-point severity scale: none, mild, moderate or severe, taking into account both vulvar architecture and skin involvement.
- **Reliability.** Intra-observer Pearson's r was &gt; 0.9 (mean 0.994) and inter-observer ICC was 0.928–0.944.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

VASS was used as a clinician-rated outcome in the FGM/C study. Its validation in lichen sclerosus does not itself establish validity or a meaningful-change threshold in FGM/C.

## Surgical Technique

The steps distinguish what the FGM/C pilot used from variants in the general scar literature. They are technical descriptions and do not show that one preparation or injection plane is superior.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[7]](#ref7)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

### Step 1 — Fat harvest

- The pilot's illustrated donor sites were abdomen or thigh. Macro-fat was harvested with a 3-mm cannula with two 1-mm ports; micro-fat with a 2-mm, eight-port cannula.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Other donor sites and low-pressure harvest systems are general fat-grafting options and were not compared in this series.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Step 2 — Fat processing

- Lipoaspirate processed to separate viable fat from blood, oil, and tumescent fluid.
- The FGM/C series used centrifugation for both macro- and micro-fat; it did not compare processing methods or use nanofat or lipoconcentrate. The report does not provide a centrifuge speed or time protocol.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Decanting, filtration, nanofat and lipoconcentrate are other described scar-treatment preparations, not studied FGM/C regimens here. Emulsification or filtration does not by itself establish cell enrichment or superior clinical effect.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

### Step 3 — Fat injection

- In the pilot, macro-fat was placed subcutaneously with a 1.5-mm cannula; micro-fat was placed more superficially at the dermal junction or in scar with a 21-gauge cannula.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Small aliquots and multiple passes are general graft-handling principles; the paper does not establish a specific fan pattern or compare these choices in FGM/C.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

### Step 4 — Postoperative care

- The 13 patients were discharged the same day after sedation or general anesthesia.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- The pilot does not establish a standard return-to-activity interval. Postoperative instructions are individualized.

## Supporting Evidence from General Scar Literature

| Study | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Krastev 2020 SR / meta-analysis**<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | 45 studies, 3,033 patients. Significant improvement in patient and surgeon satisfaction (p = 0.001); most notable improvement in scar **stiffness** (p &lt; 0.001) and pliability (p = 0.004); minor complications in 4.8% of procedures |
| **Al Qurashi 2022 SR / meta-analysis**<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> | 9 studies, n = 179. Most prominent POSAS improvements: color (2.4 pts), stiffness (2.9 pts), irregularity (2.2 pts), thickness (1.8 pts); pain 1.3 pts; itch 0.6 pts |
| **Spiekman 2022 prospective clinical**<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | 27 enrolled, 20 completed; 2 lipofilling treatments at 3-month intervals; no placebo control. Total POSAS 73.2 → 46.1 → 32.3 after the first and second treatments. Histology: M2 macrophage, T-lymphocyte and mast-cell infiltration, increased vascularization, epidermal proliferation similar to normal skin, and ECM organization moving toward normal skin |
| **Huang 2015 neuropathic scar pain**<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | Neuropathic scar pain, n = 13, uncontrolled. VAS pain fell 5.62 points at 24 weeks (from 7.54); NPSI 49.38 → 14.62; 10 of 13 (77%) had a VAS improvement of 5 points or more |

## Fat Grafting in the Female Genital Area — Related Evidence

**Lai 2023, micro-autologous fat transplantation (MAFT) in the vulvovaginal area (retrospective, n = 20, non-FGM)**:<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

- Mean 21.9 mL injected vaginally, 20.8 mL in vulva / mons pubis.
- Mean total FSFI rose from 43.8 to 68.6 at 6 months (retrospective, no comparator). The non-FGM population gives indirect context only for FGM/C counseling.
- Vaginal-tissue biopsies showed increased neocollagenesis, neoangiogenesis and estrogen-receptor expression.

**Menkes 2021 microfat / nanofat in genital rejuvenation**:<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

- In 50 women with genitourinary syndrome of menopause, microfat in the labia majora and nanofat in the vagina were followed by improved Vaginal Health Index and Female Sexual Distress scores at 1 and 3 months (uncontrolled; not an FGM/C population).

## Fat Grafting vs Other FGM/C Reconstructive Approaches

| Approach | Nature | Target | Invasiveness | Anesthesia | Advantage | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Fat grafting** | Scar/volume treatment | Vulvar scar tissue | Smaller incisions | Sedation or general in pilot | Targets scarring and contour; may be repeated | Single uncontrolled study (n = 13); no comparative cost or access data; does not reconstruct clitoris<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| [Foldès clitoral reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/foldes-clitoral-reconstruction) | Surgical | Clitoral stump/scar | Moderate | General in original cohort | Largest reported cohort (n = 2,938, 29% seen at 1 year); exposes and repositions residual clitoral tissue | Does not study diffuse surrounding scar remodeling<sup>[[19]](#ref19)[[20]](#ref20)[[21]](#ref21)</sup> |

## Potential as Adjunct or Standalone Therapy

Almadori et al. propose several potential advantages. Their pilot did not compare cost, access, anesthesia options or combination regimens with other reconstructions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Advantage | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Less extensive tissue rearrangement** | Donor harvest and vulvar injection rather than a large local flap; the pilot used sedation or general anesthesia and same-day discharge |
| **Possible lower-resource use** | Syringes/cannulas and processing equipment may be less complex than microsurgical transfer, but no comparative cost or implementation study was performed |
| **Addresses a distinct problem** | Vulvar scarring/volume loss may coexist with clitoral or anatomic concerns; treatment choice depends on the patient's findings and goals |
| **Repeatable** | Multiple sessions are possible (mean 1.4 procedures per scar treatment in the general literature)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Combinable** | Could be used as an adjunct to clitoral reconstruction, treating scar tissue while the Foldès operation addresses the clitoral stump. This combination has not been studied |

---

## Limitations

| Limitation | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Very limited evidence** | One study (n = 13) from a single center; Level of Evidence IV<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **No control group** | Study lacks a comparator arm (sham injection / saline / no treatment) |
| **Fat-graft survival** | Long-term volumetric retention in the vulvar region is unknown. Partial resorption in other body regions often necessitates repeat procedures (mean 1.4 procedures per scar treatment in a meta-analysis)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **No histological confirmation** | Unlike Lai et al., the Almadori FGM/C study did not include tissue biopsies confirming the biological mechanisms in the vulvar context<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **Patient selection** | Included types I, II and III; the small cohort does not identify which patients benefit most, and individual cases included prior reconstruction or defibulation |
| **Standardization** | The report describes centrifuged macro-/micro-fat and injection volumes but provides no comparative processing protocol or validated repeat-session schedule |
| **Future trials** | The authors state that the results warrant further testing in clinical trials<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

## Emerging Adjuncts and Combinations

| Approach | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Hybrid fractional laser + lipofilling** | In a prospective comparative study of cutaneous scars of mixed etiology, Delia 2025 found greater improvement with laser plus lipofilling than with laser alone. Any combination benefit for FGM/C vulvar scars remains untested.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> |
| **SVF enrichment** | Isolating and concentrating the stromal vascular fraction may enhance regenerative potency. Preclinical data show that SVF and purified ADSCs both reduce hypertrophic-scar parameters, and ADSCs may be more efficient<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[26]](#ref26)</sup> |
| **MSC therapy** | Hansen 2025 SR (11 preclinical and clinical studies) found improved scar appearance and collagen remodeling through anti-fibrotic and immunomodulatory effects. Methods were heterogeneous, and standardized protocols and RCTs are still needed<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup> |
| **Nanofat** | Emulsified or filtered fat has been described in other scar and genital-aesthetic contexts. It was not used in the FGM/C pilot, and its efficacy in thin vulvar scars is unestablished.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |

---

## Key Takeaways

1. Autologous fat grafting is an emerging option directed at symptomatic vulvar scarring and volume loss after FGM/C.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
2. Mechanical scar release and adipose-cell signaling are plausible mechanisms drawn largely from other scar settings; the FGM/C pilot did not test molecular mechanisms.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
3. Almadori 2025: 13 patients had improved VASS, FGSIS, FSFI and HADS scores at a mean of 12 months (all p ≤ 0.019), without a comparator.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
4. Same-day discharge followed sedation or general anesthesia in this pilot. Lower cost, scalability and low-resource feasibility remain hypotheses, not measured outcomes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
5. Fat grafting could be combined with [Foldès clitoral reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/foldes-clitoral-reconstruction), which targets the clitoral stump and a different anatomic problem. This combination has not been studied.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
6. The evidence base is a single-center series of 13 patients (Level IV). The authors call for further clinical trials.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
