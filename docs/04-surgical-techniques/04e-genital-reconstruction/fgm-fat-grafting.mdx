# Autologous Fat Grafting (Lipofilling) for FGM/C-Related Vulvar Scars

Autologous fat grafting for FGM/C-related vulvar scars is an emerging approach to symptomatic scarring and volume loss. Its proposed tissue-remodeling effects are drawn largely from non-FGM scar research, not demonstrated by tissue sampling in FGM/C. The Almadori, Hansen, Butler & Salgarello (2025) pilot is an uncontrolled 13-patient series reporting improved clinician-rated vulvar architecture (VASS), sexual function (FSFI), genital self-image (FGSIS), and psychological scores (HADS) at a mean 12.23-month follow-up. These before–after findings do not establish comparative efficacy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For the broader treatment menu see the [Vulvar Reconstruction Atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar). For the FGM/C clitoral technique see [Foldès Clitoral Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/foldes-clitoral-reconstruction); for the published non-Foldès coverage option see [Mañero Vaginal Mucosal Graft](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/manero-vaginal-graft); for the vulvovestibular perforator-flap variant see [aOAP Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/aoap-flap).

## Rationale and Unmet Need

Vulvar scarring is an important long-term consequence of FGM/C, though prevalence and severity vary by cutting type and population.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Clitoral reconstruction excises scar to expose the stump but does not specifically study treatment of diffuse surrounding vulvar fibrosis, which can cause pain, dyspareunia and distress. The AAP notes that FGM/C-related scarring can include unintended labial fusions, inclusion cysts, and obstructive infibulation scars.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Fat grafting has been studied for scarring and fibrosis in other body regions (3,033 patients across 45 studies in a 2020 meta-analysis). That literature supplies indirect rationale, not proof of a standard of care or treatment effect for FGM/C scars.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Mechanism of Action

The following mechanical and biological mechanisms are proposed from general scar, cell and animal research. The FGM/C pilot measured clinical scores, not tissue histology or pathway activity; none of the molecular effects below was established in its vulvar grafts.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

### Mechanical (rigottomy effect)

- Physical injection of fat beneath and within scar tissue creates micro-tunnels that mechanically disrupt fibrotic bands and adhesions.
- May release scar tethering and improve pliability; the relative contribution of release versus graft biology is unknown.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Biological / regenerative (ADSC-mediated)

Adipose tissue contains stromal cells that show anti-fibrotic, pro-angiogenic and immunomodulatory activity in experimental settings:<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

| Mechanism | Detail |
|---|---|
| **ECM remodeling** | ADSCs upregulate MMP-1 and MMP-2 and increase the MMP/TIMP ratio — promoting degradation of excess collagen in hypertrophic scars<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Anti-fibrotic cytokines** | ADSCs secrete HGF and TGF-β3, inhibiting fibroblast proliferation and reducing collagen I, collagen III, fibronectin, α-SMA, and CTGF<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Decorin-mediated TGF-β1 trapping** | Recent transcriptomic / proteomic analysis identified **decorin (DCN)** as the principal anti-fibrotic component of the ADSC secretome — binds and sequesters TGF-β1, preventing activation of the TGF-β / Smad2 signaling pathway in fibroblasts<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Pro-regenerative immune response** | Lipofilling induces infiltration of M2 macrophages, T lymphocytes, and mast cells — shifts the local immune environment from pro-fibrotic to pro-regenerative<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Neoangiogenesis** | ADSCs promote new blood-vessel formation within the scar — improving perfusion and oxygenation<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **Epidermal normalization** | After lipofilling, epidermal cell proliferation increases to levels similar to normal skin<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Neuropathic pain reduction** | Fat grafting reduces expression of PGP 9.5 (neuropathic-pain marker) in treated tissues<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |

## The Almadori 2025 Study<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Study design**: Prospective uncontrolled cohort, Level of Evidence IV. Improvement from baseline cannot distinguish the procedure’s effect from other care, selection, or time.

**Population**: 13 FGM/C survivors with vulvar scars; types I (2), II (5) and III (6). Eligibility included ages 18–70, no pregnancy, active infection or cancer.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Technique.** Centrifuged macro-fat was placed subcutaneously for volume, and micro-fat more superficially at the dermal junction or in scars. The mean injected volume was 9.8 mL. Patients received sedation or general anesthesia and left the same day.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Follow-up.** Mean 12.23 ± 3.03 months (range 6–18). One patient had self-resolving donor-site bruising; the authors reported no other complications in this small series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Outcome measures and results

| Outcome | Description | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **VASS** (Vulvar Architecture Severity Scale) | Clinician-assessed 6-region 4-point grading of vulvar scar severity | Significant improvement in all vulvar aesthetic units (p &lt; 0.001) |
| **FGSIS** (Female Genital Self-Image Scale) | Patient-reported genital self-image | Significant improvement (p = 0.001) |
| **FSFI** (Female Sexual Function Index) | Patient-reported sexual function across 6 domains | Significant improvement (p = 0.019) |
| **HADS** (Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale) | Patient-reported psychological well-being | Significant improvement (p = 0.002) |

## The Vulvar Architecture Severity Scale (VASS)

Developed and validated by Almadori et al. (2020) as a 6-region 4-point clinical grading system based on the aesthetic-unit principle to evaluate vulvar disease severity.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> Originally designed for vulvar lichen sclerosus, subsequently applied to FGM/C scar assessment:

- **6 vulvar aesthetic units** independently scored: clitoral hood, labia minora, labia majora, perineum, and surrounding areas.
- Each region graded on a 4-point severity scale: none, mild, moderate or severe, taking into account both vulvar architecture and skin involvement.
- **Validation.** intra-observer reliability Pearson's r &gt; 0.9 (mean 0.994); inter-observer ICC 0.928–0.944, high reliability.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

VASS was used as a clinician-rated outcome in the FGM/C study. Its validation in lichen sclerosus does not itself establish validity or a meaningful-change threshold in FGM/C.

## Surgical Technique

The steps distinguish what the FGM/C pilot used from variants in general scar literature. They are technical descriptions, not proof that one preparation or injection plane is superior.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[7]](#ref7)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

### Step 1 — Fat harvest

- The pilot's illustrated donor sites were abdomen or thigh. Macro-fat was harvested with a 3-mm cannula with two 1-mm ports; micro-fat with a 2-mm, eight-port cannula.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Other donor sites and low-pressure harvest systems are general fat-grafting options, not tested comparisons in this series.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Step 2 — Fat processing

- Lipoaspirate processed to separate viable fat from blood, oil, and tumescent fluid.
- The FGM/C series used centrifugation for both macro- and micro-fat; it did not compare processing methods or use nanofat or lipoconcentrate. The report does not provide a centrifuge speed or time protocol.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Decanting, filtration, nanofat and lipoconcentrate are other described scar-treatment preparations, not studied FGM/C regimens here. Emulsification or filtration does not by itself establish cell enrichment or superior clinical effect.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

### Step 3 — Fat injection

- In the pilot, macro-fat was placed subcutaneously with a 1.5-mm cannula; micro-fat was placed more superficially at the dermal junction or in scar with a 21-gauge cannula.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Small aliquots and multiple passes are general graft-handling principles; the paper does not establish a specific fan pattern or compare these choices in FGM/C.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

### Step 4 — Postoperative care

- The 13 patients were discharged the same day after sedation or general anesthesia.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- The pilot does not establish a standard return-to-activity interval; use individualized postoperative instructions.

## Supporting Evidence from General Scar Literature

| Study | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Krastev 2020 SR / meta-analysis**<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | 45 studies, 3,033 patients. Significant improvement in patient and surgeon satisfaction (p = 0.001); most notable improvement in scar **stiffness** (p &lt; 0.001) |
| **Al Qurashi 2022 SR / meta-analysis**<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> | Most prominent POSAS improvements: color (2.4 pts), stiffness (2.9 pts), irregularity (2.2 pts), thickness (1.8 pts); pain 1.3 pts; itch 0.6 pts |
| **Spiekman 2022 prospective clinical**<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | n = 27, 2 lipofilling treatments. Total POSAS 73.2 → 32.3. Histology: M2 macrophage infiltration, ↑ vascularization, epidermal proliferation normalization, ECM remodeling toward normal skin architecture |
| **Huang 2015 neuropathic scar pain**<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | n = 13. VAS pain ↓ 5.62 pts at 24 wk (7.54 → 1.92); NPSI 49.38 → 14.62; 77% achieved ≥ 5-pt VAS improvement |

## Fat Grafting in the Female Genital Area — Related Evidence

**Lai 2023, MAFT in the vulvovaginal area (n = 20, non-FGM)**:<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

- Mean 21.9 mL injected vaginally, 20.8 mL in vulva / mons pubis.
- The study reported improved sexual-function scores; its non-FGM population provides indirect context for FGM/C counseling.
- Histological evidence of ↑ collagenesis, angiogenesis, and estrogen-receptor expression in vulvar tissue.

**Menkes 2021 microfat / nanofat in genital rejuvenation**:<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

- VHI and FSD scores significantly improved at 1 and 3 mo (p &lt; 0.05).

## Fat Grafting vs Other FGM/C Reconstructive Approaches

| Approach | Nature | Target | Invasiveness | Anesthesia | Key advantage | Key limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Fat grafting** | Scar/volume treatment | Vulvar scar tissue | Smaller incisions | Sedation or general in pilot | Targets scarring and contour; may be repeated | Single uncontrolled study (n = 13); no comparative cost or access data; does not reconstruct clitoris<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| [Foldès clitoral reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/foldes-clitoral-reconstruction) | Surgical | Clitoral stump/scar | Moderate | General in original cohort | Largest reported cohort (n = 2,938); exposes and repositions residual clitoral tissue | Does not study diffuse surrounding scar remodeling<sup>[[19]](#ref19)[[20]](#ref20)[[21]](#ref21)</sup> |
| [aOAP combined techniques (O'Dey)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/aoap-flap) | Surgical (flaps) | Clitoris + prepuce + vulvovestibular | Moderate-high | General | Comprehensive anatomic reconstruction | Single-surgeon experience; complex<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> |
| A-PRP | Proposed adjunct | Neoclitoral surface | Additional intraoperative step | Applied intraoperatively | Healing/pain benefit hypothesized | Adjunct only; 5-patient pilot cannot establish comparative benefit<sup>[[23]](#ref23)[[24]](#ref24)</sup> |

## Potential as Adjunct or Standalone Therapy

Almadori et al. propose several potential advantages. Their 13-patient pilot did not compare cost, access, anesthesia options or combination regimens with other reconstructions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Advantage | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Less extensive tissue rearrangement** | Donor harvest and vulvar injection rather than a large local flap; the pilot used sedation or general anesthesia and same-day discharge |
| **Possible lower-resource use** | Syringes/cannulas and processing equipment may be less complex than microsurgical transfer, but no comparative cost or implementation study was performed |
| **Addresses a distinct problem** | Vulvar scarring/volume loss may coexist with clitoral or anatomic concerns; treatment choice depends on the patient's findings and goals |
| **Repeatable** | Multiple sessions possible (average 1.4 sessions for scar treatment in general literature)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Combinable** | Could potentially be used as an adjunct to clitoral reconstruction — addressing scar tissue while Foldès addresses the clitoral stump — though this combination has not yet been studied |

---

## Limitations

| Limitation | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Very limited evidence** | Only one study (n = 13) from a single center; Level of Evidence IV<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **No control group** | Study lacks a comparator arm (sham injection / saline / no treatment) |
| **Fat-graft survival** | Long-term volumetric retention in the vulvar region is unknown; 30–70% resorption rates in other body regions often necessitate repeat procedures<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **No histological confirmation** | Unlike Lai et al., the Almadori FGM/C study did not include tissue biopsies confirming the biological mechanisms in the vulvar context<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **Patient selection** | Included types I, II and III; the small cohort does not identify which patients benefit most, and individual cases included prior reconstruction or defibulation |
| **Standardization** | The report describes centrifuged macro-/micro-fat and injection volumes but provides no comparative processing protocol or validated repeat-session schedule |
| **Future trials warranted** | Authors explicitly state results warrant further testing in clinical trials<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

## Emerging Adjuncts and Combinations

| Approach | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Hybrid fractional laser + lipofilling** | Delia 2025 addresses scars outside this FGM/C cohort; any combination benefit for FGM/C vulvar scars remains untested.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> |
| **SVF enrichment** | Isolating and concentrating the stromal vascular fraction may enhance regenerative potency; preclinical data show SVF and purified ADSCs both reduce hypertrophic-scar parameters, though ADSCs may be more efficient<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[26]](#ref26)</sup> |
| **MSC therapy** | Hansen 2025 SR — beneficial effects on scar remodeling through anti-fibrotic / immunomodulatory mechanisms; standardized protocols and RCTs still needed<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup> |
| **Nanofat** | Emulsified/filtered fat described in other scar and genital-aesthetic contexts; not used or compared in the FGM/C pilot, and superior efficacy in thin vulvar scars is unestablished.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |

---

## Key Takeaways

1. Autologous fat grafting is an emerging option directed at symptomatic vulvar scarring and volume loss after FGM/C.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
2. Mechanical scar release and adipose-cell signaling are plausible mechanisms drawn largely from other scar settings; the FGM/C pilot did not test molecular mechanisms.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
3. Almadori 2025: n = 13 with improved mean 12-month VASS, FGSIS, FSFI and HADS scores (all p ≤ 0.019), without a comparator.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
4. Same-day discharge followed sedation or general anesthesia in this pilot. Lower cost, scalability and low-resource feasibility remain hypotheses, not measured outcomes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
5. Potentially combinable with [Foldès clitoral reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/foldes-clitoral-reconstruction) (clitoral-stump-targeted), which addresses different anatomic problems, though this combination has not yet been studied.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
6. Evidence base is very limited (single-center n = 13, Level IV); RCTs and multi-center series are warranted.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
