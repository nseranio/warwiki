# Inverted Urethral Flap (IUF) Glanuloplasty

The **inverted urethral flap (IUF)** is a single-stage neoglans reconstruction reported with partial penectomy. Chavarriaga's retrospective 74-patient cohort had median follow-up of 72 months, which its authors describe as the largest cohort reported. Its outcomes do not establish superiority over skin grafts or other flap methods.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For the comparative overview of all four flap-based glanuloplasty options (IUF, Gulino distal-urethral, Mazza scrotal, Shaeer rectus) see [Glanuloplasty With Flaps](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/glanuloplasty-flaps). For the STSG-based standard, see [Glansectomy With STSG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/glansectomy-stsg). For the broader decision framework, see [Penile Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile).

---

## Historical Development and Rationale

First described by Belinky, Cheliz, Graziano, and Rey (2011, n = 10) and later reported by Chavarriaga et al. at Instituto Nacional de Cancerología (Bogotá), Colombia in a 2022 retrospective cohort of 74 patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The rationale is to preserve a longer urethral stump and use its mucosal surface for the neoglans. Descriptions of the technique commonly cite 2–3 cm of urethra beyond the corporal transection, but published series do not establish that measurement as a fixed rule, and the length that can be preserved depends on the tumor margin.

---

## Anatomical Basis

- The corpus spongiosum surrounds the penile urethra. The urethra receives blood supply from both proximal and distal directions, which supports the use of a distally based urethral segment; this anatomy does not guarantee flap viability in an individual operation.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Paired cavernosal, dorsal and bulbourethral arteries have extensive anastomotic connections. The deep dorsal artery supplies the glans.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Buck's fascia envelops the corpora cavernosa and spongiosum circumferentially.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Urethral mucosa is sensate, but IUF-specific objective sensory outcomes have not been reported. The thermal and tactile sensation described by Gulino followed a different technique and does not apply to IUF.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Indications

| Indication | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Partial penectomy for penile SCC** | Primary and most-studied indication<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Traumatic partial amputation** | When distal urethra is preserved |
| Partial penectomy with enough urethral length to form a flap without compromising oncologic margins | Commonly cited length is 2–3 cm beyond the corporal transection; not standardized |

### Contraindications

- Tumor involving or extending into the urethra (preserving a longer stump risks a positive margin)
- Proximal penectomy with insufficient residual urethra
- Total penectomy (no residual urethra)

---

## Surgical Technique

The steps below outline the technique. Measurements, suture sizes, catheter duration and dressings vary by surgeon and are not standardized in the published series.

### Step 1 — Partial penectomy

- Penile-base tourniquet
- Circumferential skin incision at the planned amputation level
- Sharp transection of the corpora
- Confirm negative corporal and urethral margins. The technique reports used intraoperative assessment, and EAU guidance suggests frozen section when complete removal is in doubt, not for every organ-sparing operation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

### Step 2 — Urethral preservation (defining step)

- Preserve a longer urethral segment than needed for voiding alone; 2–3 cm distal to the corporal transection is commonly cited
- Send the urethral stump for frozen section to confirm negative margin at the true urethral transection

### Step 3 — Corporal closure

- Close each corpus cavernosum tip individually with 3-0 or 4-0 absorbable interrupted suture
- Approximate tunica albuginea for hemostasis and a smooth rounded corporal tip surface

### Step 4 — Urethral flap inversion

- The preserved distal urethral segment, still on its spongiosal blood supply, is inverted (folded back proximally) over the closed corporal tips
- Mucosal (luminal) surface faces outward, creating the moist pink glans-like surface
- Serosal / adventitial surface faces inward against the corpora

### Step 5 — Neoglans shaping and fixation

- Suture the inverted flap circumferentially to the penile shaft skin at the level of the corporal transection with fine absorbable suture
- Junction creates a corona-like ridge simulating the coronal sulcus
- Sculpt / trim to approximate native-glans shape and contour

### Step 6 — Neomeatus creation

- Fashion a new urethral meatus at the tip of the neoglans by creating a small opening in the inverted flap
- Evert the meatal edges and suture them to prevent stenosis
- Position to allow directed urinary stream for standing voiding

### Step 7 — Catheterization and dressing

- 14–16 Fr Foley through the neomeatus for 7–10 days
- Light compressive dressing

## Published Series

### Belinky 2011 — Original Description (n = 10)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Mean age | 61 y (18–71) |
| Indication | Penile SCC — all partial penectomy |
| Mean follow-up | 11 mo (5–17) |
| **Flap necrosis** | 0% |
| **Neomeatal stenosis** | 0% |
| Local tumor recurrence | 10% (1 / 10) |
| Ventral penile curvature | 10% (1 / 10); no penetration difficulty |

Authors' conclusion: "simple, reproducible" with "satisfactory functional and cosmetic results, and an acceptable complication rate."

### Chavarriaga 2022 — Retrospective Cohort (n = 74)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The authors describe it as the largest IUF cohort reported.

#### Demographics

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| n | 74 |
| Enrollment | May 2007 – December 2019 |
| Median age | 62 y (IQR 52–76) |
| **Median follow-up** | 72 mo (IQR 38–121) |
| DSNB | 83.8% (62 / 74) |
| ILND | 39.2% (29 / 74) |

#### Oncologic outcomes (6-year)

| Outcome | Estimate |
|---|---|
| **Overall survival** | 86.5% |
| **Recurrence-free survival** | 90.5% |
| **Progression-free survival** | 85.1% |

#### Functional outcomes (validated PROMs)

| Domain | Instrument | Result | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Global health status | EQ-5D-3L-VAS | 84.6 ± 10.4 | Cohort mean, not a pre/post effect |
| Erectile function | IIEF-5 | 17.3 ± 7 | Cohort mean with wide variation |
| Voiding symptoms | ICIQ-MLUTS | 1.7 ± 3.2 | Cohort mean, no technique comparator |

IIEF-5 scores, percentages of preserved function and ICIQ scores are different constructs from different populations. These figures provide counseling context but are not comparative-effect estimates.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

**Comparative IIEF-5 context:**

| Cohort | IIEF-5 |
|---|---|
| **Chavarriaga IUF (n = 74)** | mean 17.3 |
| Pérez / Chavarriaga multicenter OSS with STSG (n = 57; 32 assessed) | median 19 (IQR 10.75–25) |
| Croghan partial glansectomy, sexually active patients<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> | mean 14.9 |
| Croghan radical glansectomy, sexually active patients<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> | mean 15.8 |

The mean ICIQ-MLUTS voiding score of 1.7 and the Pérez multicenter median of 4 (IQR 1–15) use different summaries in different cohorts and should not be interpreted as a comparative effect.

A distinct distal-urethral mucosal reconstruction, sharing the general principle of using urethral tissue for a neoglans:

Thermal and tactile sensibility was reported in all 14 participants. This was a separate uncontrolled cohort with no IUF or graft comparator.

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| n | 57 (20 glans resurfacing, 23 partial penectomy, 14 glansectomy); all with STSG |
| Median age | 55.1 y |
| Median follow-up | 55.7 mo |

This cohort used split-thickness skin graft reconstruction, not IUF, and pooled three different organ-sparing operations. Functional data were assessed in 32 patients. Its median IIEF-5 is context and cannot validate an IUF-specific effect.

## Complications

| Complication | Belinky (n = 10) | Chavarriaga (n = 74) | Gulino (n = 14) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flap necrosis | 0% | not reported | not reported |
| Neomeatal stenosis | 0% | not reported | not reported |
| Local tumor recurrence | 10% (1 / 10) | 9.5% (6-yr RFS 90.5%) | 0% |
| Ventral penile curvature | 10% (1 / 10) | not reported | not reported |
| Penile retraction | n/r | n/r | None reported |
| Urethral fistula | not reported | n/r | not reported |

Belinky reported ventral penile curvature in 1/10, without penetration difficulty. Its mechanism is not described, and no larger-series rate is available.

Belinky reported no meatal stenosis in 10 patients, but Chavarriaga does not report the event, so zero stenosis cannot be stated across IUF series or as an advantage over STSG. Pang's review of glansectomy series gives meatal stenosis in 8.1% (range 0–14.3%). Wang's seven-patient series describes a spiral embedded flap for meatal stenosis after penile cancer surgery and does not show how often salvage is needed after IUF.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Oncologic Safety

The main oncologic question is whether preserving a longer urethral stump risks residual disease. The evidence and constraints are:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

1. Preserve a longer urethral stump only when negative urethral and corporal margins remain achievable; use intraoperative pathology according to the operative plan and current guideline context.
2. Chavarriaga reported 6-year RFS of 90.5% in selected patients. Comparison with a different STSG cohort does not show equivalence or superiority.
3. Urethral involvement and the required resection depend on individual tumor extent. AJCC 8 staging does not make urethral preservation automatically safe.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
4. The EAU-ASCO guideline recommends offering organ-preserving surgery for the primary tumor when feasible; it does not name IUF as a preferred technique.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Comparison With Scrotal-Flap Glanuloplasty (Mazza)

| Parameter | IUF (Chavarriaga) | Mazza scrotal flap |
|---|---|---|
| n | 74 | 34 |
| Follow-up | 72 mo median | 73.2 mo mean |
| **Stages** | 1 | 2 |
| Donor site | None | Scrotum |
| Flap necrosis | not reported in Chavarriaga; 0/10 in Belinky | 5.8% |
| Meatal stenosis | not reported in Chavarriaga; 0/10 in Belinky | 2.9% |
| Erectile function | IIEF-5 17.3 | 20.5% potency |
| **Depilation needed** | No | 17.6% |

IUF is single-stage and avoids a scrotal donor site. Mazza's technique is two-stage (the flap pedicle is divided 4–6 weeks later), and 17.6% needed depilation of the neoglans. The two have not been compared directly, so superiority is unproven.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

## Surveillance — Current EAU Framework<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

Follow-up depends on whether treatment is classified as penile-preserving or amputation and on nodal status. The current EAU table lists three-monthly primary-site review for years 1–2 and annual review for years 3–5 after amputation; penile-preserving treatment uses three-monthly then six-monthly review. Groin examination, ultrasound, FNAC or cross-sectional imaging are risk- and nodal-status dependent. Capable patients are taught self-examination and given rapid re-access. The schedule follows the treating cancer team's classification of the operation and nodal status.

## Psychosexual Impact and Quality of Life

Partial penectomy has a substantial psychosexual impact that IUF aims to mitigate:<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- **Whyte 2022 SR.** Three of four eligible studies reported a decrease across all IIEF domains after partial penectomy, and many patients still maintained satisfactory sex lives. In one study, greater residual penile length was associated with higher postoperative function, and older age and anxiety with lower function.
- **Roumieux 2025 qualitative study.** Nine men described effects of penile-cancer diagnosis and treatment on intimacy, including a "voyage of sexual re-discovery". The authors call for pre-surgical information and post-surgical psychosexual support.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **Yu 2016 (n = 43).** IIEF-15 scores fell and SAS and SDS scores changed significantly. Flaccid penile length was positively associated with intercourse satisfaction, and anxiety was negatively associated with erectile, orgasmic, desire and intercourse-satisfaction domains.
- **Romero 2005 (n = 18).** 55.6% had erectile function sufficient for intercourse after partial penectomy. Among sexually abstinent patients, 50% cited shame about small penis size and absence of the glans as the main reason for not resuming.
- **Kieffer 2014 (n = 90).** Men who had (partial) penectomy reported significantly more problems than those with penile-sparing surgery with orgasm (effect size 0.54), appearance concerns (0.61), life interference (0.49) and urinary function (83% vs 43%).
- **EAU-ASCO.** The guideline recommends offering organ-preserving surgery for the primary tumor when feasible.

IUF is intended to address these concerns by giving a glans-like mucosal surface. IUF-specific patient-reported evidence is limited to Chavarriaga's cohort mean EQ-5D-3L-VAS of 84.6 and mean IIEF-5 of 17.3, without a preoperative or technique comparator. Gulino's 14-patient cohort reported thermal and tactile sensibility in all participants, but that was a different distal-urethral technique.

## Advantages

1. **Single-stage.** It is performed at the same operation as partial penectomy.
2. **No separate donor site.** It avoids thigh (STSG) or oral (BMG) donor morbidity.
3. **Moist mucosal surface.** The surface resembles native glans.
4. **Possible sensory preservation.** IUF-specific objective sensory results are not available.
5. **Complications.** Belinky reported no stenosis or necrosis in 10 patients; Chavarriaga does not report those events.
6. **Follow-up.** Chavarriaga's selected cohort reported 6-year RFS of 90.5%, mean ICIQ-MLUTS voiding score 1.7 and EQ-5D-3L-VAS 84.6.

---

## Limitations

1. Enough preservable urethral length is required without compromising margins; the commonly cited 2–3 cm is not a validated threshold.
2. Ventral curvature occurred in 1 of 10 in Belinky; Chavarriaga does not report a rate.
3. **Limited published data.** There are two dedicated IUF series (Belinky n = 10, Chavarriaga n = 74) and a distinct Gulino distal-urethral series (n = 14). No randomized comparison with STSG exists.
4. **Not a named technique** in the EAU-ASCO guideline recommendations.
5. **Retrospective design.** Chavarriaga's series is a chart review that needs external validation.
6. **No standardized cosmetic-satisfaction data**, unlike the Pang STSG review (86.3% satisfaction).

---

## Postoperative Surveillance

Follow-up after partial penectomy, per EAU-ASCO 2023 and the EAU chapter:<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

- Follow the EAU schedule for the treatment and nodal status; amputation and penile-preserving pathways differ.
- Patient self-examination covers the neoglans and penile stump.
- Biopsy any suspicious lesion.
- Salvage organ-sparing surgery is an option for small recurrences not involving the corpora.
- Imaging and groin surveillance follow nodal risk or status.

---

## Future Directions

- Multicenter validation across institutions and populations is needed.
- A randomized comparison of IUF and STSG neoglans would address functional and oncologic outcomes.
- Standardized functional-outcome reporting was called for in the Pang review.
- Hybrid IUF and BMG meatoplasty combinations have not been studied.
- Long-term histologic studies could characterize keratinization or metaplastic change in inverted urethral mucosa.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The largest reported dedicated IUF cohort is Chavarriaga's n = 74, with 6-year OS 86.5%, RFS 90.5% and mean IIEF-5 17.3 in selected patients.
- The technique preserves a longer urethral stump and uses its mucosa for the neoglans; the commonly cited 2–3 cm is not a validated threshold.
- It is single-stage with no separate donor site. Belinky reported no stenosis or necrosis in 10 patients; Chavarriaga does not report those events.
- Negative urethral and corporal margins are required. EAU guidance suggests frozen section when completeness is in doubt, not automatically.
- Belinky reported ventral curvature in 1 of 10 without penetration difficulty; Chavarriaga does not report a curvature rate.
- The operation suits partial penectomy with enough preservable urethra, and is not used for proximal amputations or urethra-involving tumors.
