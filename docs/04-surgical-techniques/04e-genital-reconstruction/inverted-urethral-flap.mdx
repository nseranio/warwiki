# Inverted Urethral Flap (IUF) Glanuloplasty

The **inverted urethral flap (IUF)** is a single-stage neoglans reconstruction reported with partial penectomy. Chavarriaga's retrospective 74-patient cohort had median follow-up of 72 months, making it the largest dedicated IUF cohort identified here. Its outcomes do not establish superiority over skin grafts or other flap methods.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For the comparative overview of all four flap-based glanuloplasty options (IUF, Gulino distal-urethral, Mazza scrotal, Shaeer rectus) see [Glanuloplasty With Flaps](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/glanuloplasty-flaps). For the STSG-based standard, see [Glansectomy With STSG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/glansectomy-stsg). For the broader decision framework, see [Penile Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile).

---

## Historical Development and Rationale

First described by Belinky, Cheliz, Graziano, and Rey (2011, n = 10) and later reported by Chavarriaga et al. at Instituto Nacional de Cancerología (Bogotá), Colombia in a 2022 retrospective cohort of 74 patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The reported rationale is to preserve a longer urethral stump and use its mucosal surface for the neoglans. The inherited technical description specifies 2–3 cm beyond the corporal transection, but the accessible Belinky and Chavarriaga abstracts do not give that measurement; the original full methods remain required before treating it as a fixed rule.

---

## Anatomical Basis

- The corpus spongiosum surrounds the penile urethra and receives urethral or bulbar arterial supply. This background anatomy supports planning but does not guarantee flap viability in an individual operation.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Dorsal arteries** principally perfuse glans and spongiosum with perforators to the distal corpora; additional collateral flow to the urethral flap
- The urethra has dual blood supply (proximal and distal); enhances flap reliability
- Urethral mucosa is sensate, but IUF-specific objective sensory outcomes were not reported in the accessible Chavarriaga abstract; Gulino's different technique must not be substituted as an IUF sensory result.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Buck's fascia** circumferentially envelops the corpora and spongiosum and helps maintain structural integrity of the flap

---

## Indications

| Indication | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Partial penectomy for penile SCC** | Primary and most-studied indication<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Traumatic partial amputation** | When distal urethra is preserved |
| Partial penectomy with enough urethral length to form a flap without compromising oncologic margins | The inherited 2–3-cm measurement awaits full-method verification |

### Contraindications

- Tumor involving or extending into the urethra (preserving a longer stump risks a positive margin)
- Proximal penectomy with insufficient residual urethra
- Total penectomy (no residual urethra)

## Surgical Technique

The complete Belinky and Chavarriaga methods and operative figures were not accessible in this review. The inherited outline below preserves useful technique concepts, but its measurements, suture sizes, catheter duration and dressing are not independently verified as a universal protocol.

### Step 1 — Partial penectomy

- Penile-base tourniquet
- Circumferential skin incision at the planned amputation level
- Sharp transection of the corpora
- Confirm negative corporal and urethral margins. The technique reports used intraoperative assessment; current EAU guidance suggests frozen section when complete removal is in doubt rather than mandating it for every organ-sparing operation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

### Step 2 — Urethral preservation (defining step)

- Preserve a longer urethral segment than needed for voiding alone; the inherited protocol states 2–3 cm distal to the corporal transection, pending confirmation in the original full methods
- Send the urethral stump for frozen section to confirm negative margin at the true urethral transection

### Step 3 — Corporal closure

- Close each corpus cavernosum tip individually with 3-0 or 4-0 absorbable interrupted suture
- Approximate tunica albuginea for hemostasis and a smooth rounded corporal tip surface

### Step 4 — Urethral flap inversion

- The preserved distal urethral segment, still on its spongiosal blood supply, is inverted (folded back proximally) over the closed corporal tips
- Mucosal (luminal) surface faces outward, creating the moist pink glans-like surface
- Serosal / adventitial surface faces inward against the corpora

### Step 5 — Neoglans shaping and fixation

- Suture the inverted flap circumferentially to the penile shaft skin at the level of the corporal transection with fine absorbable suture
- Junction creates a corona-like ridge simulating the coronal sulcus
- Sculpt / trim to approximate native-glans shape and contour

### Step 6 — Neomeatus creation

- Fashion a new urethral meatus at the tip of the neoglans by creating a small opening in the inverted flap
- **Evert the meatal edges and suture** to prevent stenosis
- Position to allow directed urinary stream for standing voiding

### Step 7 — Catheterization and dressing

- 14–16 Fr Foley through the neomeatus for 7–10 days
- Light compressive dressing

## Published Series

### Belinky 2011 — Original Description (n = 10)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Mean age | 61 y (18–71) |
| Indication | Penile SCC — all partial penectomy |
| Mean follow-up | 11 mo (5–17) |
| **Flap necrosis** | **0%** |
| **Neomeatal stenosis** | **0%** |
| Local tumor recurrence | 10% (1 / 10) |
| Ventral penile curvature | 10% (1 / 10) — no penetration difficulty |

Authors' conclusion: "simple, reproducible" with "satisfactory functional and cosmetic results, and an acceptable complication rate."

### Chavarriaga 2022 — Retrospective Cohort (n = 74)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

This was the largest dedicated IUF cohort identified in this review.

#### Demographics

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| n | 74 |
| Enrollment | May 2007 – December 2019 |
| Median age | 62 y (IQR 52–76) |
| **Median follow-up** | **72 mo (IQR 38–121)** |
| DSNB | 83.8% (62 / 74) |
| ILND | 39.2% (29 / 74) |

#### Oncologic outcomes (6-year)

| Outcome | Estimate |
|---|---|
| **Overall survival** | **86.5%** |
| **Recurrence-free survival** | **90.5%** |
| **Progression-free survival** | **85.1%** |

#### Functional outcomes (validated PROMs)

| Domain | Instrument | Result | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Global health status | EQ-5D-3L-VAS | **84.6 ± 10.4** | Cohort mean, not a pre/post effect |
| Erectile function | **IIEF-5** | **17.3 ± 7** | Cohort mean with wide variation |
| Voiding symptoms | ICIQ-MLUTS | **1.7 ± 3.2** | Cohort mean, no technique comparator |

IIEF-5, percentages of preserved function and IPSS and ICIQ scores are different constructs from different populations; these figures provide counseling context but are not valid comparative-effect estimates.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

**Comparative IIEF-5 context:**

| Cohort | IIEF-5 |
|---|---|
| **Chavarriaga IUF (n = 74)** | **mean 17.3** |
| Pérez / Chavarriaga multicenter OSS (n = 57) | median 19 (IQR 10.75–25) |
| Croghan partial glansectomy<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | mean 14.9 |
| Croghan radical glansectomy<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | mean 15.8 |

The mean ICIQ-MLUTS voiding score of 1.7 and the Pérez multicenter median of 4 (IQR 1–15) use different summaries in different cohorts and should not be interpreted as a comparative effect.

## Complications

| Complication | Belinky (n = 10) | Chavarriaga (n = 74) | Gulino (n = 14) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flap necrosis | 0% | n/r in abstract | n/r in Gulino abstract |
| Neomeatal stenosis | 0% | n/r in abstract | n/r in Gulino abstract |
| Local tumor recurrence | 10% (1 / 10) | 9.5% (6-yr RFS 90.5%) | 0% |
| Ventral penile curvature | 10% (1 / 10) | n/r in abstract | n/r in Gulino abstract |
| Penile retraction | n/r | n/r | None reported in Gulino abstract |
| Urethral fistula | n/r in Belinky abstract | n/r | n/r in Gulino abstract |

Belinky reported ventral penile curvature in 1/10, without penetration difficulty. The abstract does not establish its mechanism or a larger-series rate.

Belinky reported no meatal stenosis in 10 patients, but the accessible Chavarriaga abstract does not report the event. This cannot be presented as zero across all IUF series or as a comparative advantage over STSG. Wang's separate seven-patient salvage series does not establish how often salvage is needed after IUF.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Oncologic Safety

The central oncologic concern — whether preserving a longer urethral stump risks residual disease — is addressed by:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

1. Preserve a longer urethral stump only when negative urethral and corporal margins remain achievable; use intraoperative pathology according to the operative plan and current guideline context.
2. Chavarriaga reported 6-year RFS of 90.5% in selected patients; comparison with a different STSG cohort does not prove equivalence or superiority.
3. Urethral involvement and required resection are determined by individual tumor extent; AJCC 8 staging does not make urethral preservation automatically safe.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
4. Current EAU guidance supports selected organ-sparing reconstruction and frozen section when complete removal is in doubt, but does not name IUF as preferred.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
5. The full current NCCN text was not accessible in this review; inherited NCCN wording is not used to impose a universal IUF protocol.

## Surveillance — Current EAU Framework<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

Follow-up depends on whether treatment is classified as penile-preserving or amputation and on nodal status. The current EAU table lists three-monthly primary-site review for years 1–2 and annual review for years 3–5 after amputation; penile-preserving treatment uses three-monthly then six-monthly review. Groin examination, ultrasound, FNAC or cross-sectional imaging are risk- and nodal-status dependent. Educate capable patients in self-examination and ensure rapid re-access; do not apply one schedule to every IUF patient without the treating cancer team's classification.

## Psychosexual Impact and Quality of Life

Partial penectomy has a substantial psychosexual impact that IUF aims to mitigate:<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

- **Whyte 2022 SR.** Partial penectomy decreases all IIEF domains; many patients still maintain satisfactory sex lives; greater residual penile length is associated with higher postoperative function; older age and anxiety with lower function
- **Roumieux 2025 qualitative study**<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>. Penile-cancer diagnosis and treatment profoundly impact intimacy; patients describe a "voyage of sexual re-discovery"; emphasizes need for comprehensive pre-surgical information and post-surgical psychosexual support
- **Yu 2016 (n = 43).** Significant decreases in IIEF-15, increases in SAS and SDS; flaccid penile length positively associated with intercourse satisfaction; anxiety negatively associated with erectile, orgasmic, desire, and intercourse-satisfaction domains
- **Romero 2005 (n = 18).** 55.6% had erectile function for intercourse after partial penectomy; 50% of sexually abstinent patients cited shame from small penis size and absence of the glans as the main reason for not resuming
- **Kieffer 2014 (n = 90).** (Partial) penectomy patients had significantly worse orgasm (effect size 0.54), appearance concerns (0.61), life interference (0.49), and urinary function compared with penile-sparing surgery
- **EAU-ASCO.** Partial penectomy is associated with poorer sexual outcomes compared with organ-sparing

The IUF addresses these psychosexual concerns by:
- **Natural-appearing neoglans** reduces appearance-related shame and distress
- **Moist mucosal surface** mimicking native glans
- A separate 14-patient Gulino cohort reported thermal and tactile sensibility in all participants; this is not an IUF-specific sensory result
- Chavarriaga reported a cohort mean EQ-5D-3L-VAS of 84.6, without a preoperative or technique comparator

## Advantages

1. **Single-stage.** Performed simultaneously with partial penectomy
2. **No separate donor site.** Eliminates thigh (STSG) or oral (BMG) donor morbidity
3. **Moist mucosal surface.** Closely mimics native glans
4. **Potential sensory preservation.** Objective IUF-specific sensory results were not in the accessible Chavarriaga abstract; Gulino is a separate technique
5. Belinky reported no stenosis or necrosis in 10 patients; Chavarriaga's abstract does not report those complications
6. **Longer selected-cohort follow-up.** 6-year RFS 90.5%, mean ICIQ-MLUTS 1.7 and EQ-5D-3L-VAS 84.6 in Chavarriaga's cohort

---

## Limitations

1. Requires enough preservable urethral length without compromising margins; the inherited 2–3-cm measurement needs full-method confirmation
2. Ventral curvature was reported in 1/10 in Belinky; the larger abstract did not report a rate
3. **Limited published data.** Two dedicated IUF series (Belinky n = 10, Chavarriaga n = 74) plus a distinct Gulino distal-urethral series (n = 14); no randomized comparison with STSG
4. **Not specifically referenced in NCCN or EAU-ASCO** guidelines, consistent with their reconstruction-after-negative-margins principle, but unnamed
5. **Single-center experience** for the 74-patient series; external validation needed
6. **No standardized cosmetic-satisfaction data**, unlike Pang STSG SR (86.3% satisfaction)

---

## Postoperative Surveillance

Per EAU-ASCO 2023:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

- Follow the current EAU treatment- and nodal-status schedule; partial penectomy or amputation and penile-preserving pathways are not identical
- Patient self-examination — neoglans and penile stump
- Biopsy of any suspicious lesion
- Salvage organ-sparing surgery for small recurrences not involving the corpora
- Support self-examination and rapid re-access; tailor imaging and groin surveillance to nodal risk or status<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

## Future Directions

- **Multicenter validation** across diverse institutions and populations
- **Randomized comparison** of IUF versus STSG neoglans for functional and oncologic outcomes
- **Standardized functional-outcome reporting** (per Pang SR call)
- **Hybrid IUF and BMG meatoplasty** combinations
- **Long-term histologic studies** of inverted urethral mucosa to characterize keratinization / metaplastic changes

---

## Key Takeaways

- Largest identified dedicated IUF cohort: Chavarriaga n = 74, with 6-year OS 86.5%, RFS 90.5% and mean IIEF-5 17.3 in selected patients
- The technique preserves a longer urethral stump and uses mucosa for the neoglans; the inherited 2–3-cm rule awaits full-method verification
- Single-stage and no separate donor site; Belinky reported no stenosis or necrosis in 10 patients, while the larger abstract did not report those events
- Negative urethral and corporal margins are needed; current EAU guidance uses frozen section when completeness is in doubt rather than as an automatic rule
- Belinky reported ventral curvature in 1/10 without penetration difficulty; the larger abstract does not provide a curvature rate
- Reserved for partial penectomy with sufficient preservable urethral length; not for proximal amputations or urethra-involving tumors
