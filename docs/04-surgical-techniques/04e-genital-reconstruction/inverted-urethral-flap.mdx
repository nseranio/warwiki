# Inverted Urethral Flap (IUF) Glanuloplasty

Inverted urethral-flap (IUF) glanuloplasty uses retained distal urethral tissue to cover the corporal stump after selected partial penectomy. It has been described in small penile-cancer case series, including a longer-follow-up cohort, but that evidence does not establish it as a standard reconstruction or a preferred alternative to graft reconstruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For the broader setting, see [Glanuloplasty With Flaps](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/glanuloplasty-flaps), [Glansectomy With STSG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/glansectomy-stsg), and [Penile Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile).

---

## Evidence-Bounded Role

The original report and a later cohort describe inversion of urethral tissue over the distal corporal surface after partial penectomy for selected penile carcinoma.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> These non-randomized reports have different follow-up and outcome definitions, and they do not support a general claim of superior sensation, sexual function, complication rates, or cancer control.

The current EAU penile-cancer guideline includes glansectomy with reconstruction among options for appropriately selected glans-confined disease, but it does not name an inverted urethral flap or rank it over split-thickness skin graft reconstruction. Cancer staging, pathological control, and surveillance remain the primary determinants of treatment selection.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Planning and Follow-up

Feasibility depends on disease extent and urethral involvement, the available and viable urethral tissue, prior treatment, ability to obtain oncologic control, and the individual’s priorities for urinary function, sexual function, appearance, and revision surgery. Those assessments and the specific reconstruction belong to the treating reconstructive and oncologic team.

After organ-sparing cancer surgery, follow-up should assess local recurrence, wound healing, meatal/urethral symptoms, urinary and sexual function, appearance, and the need for further treatment. The appropriate schedule depends on pathological stage and the responsible cancer team.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## What Is Not Established

- A preferred urethral length, inversion method, suture plan, catheter duration, dressing, or postoperative pathway.
- Comparability with eversion techniques, skin-graft reconstruction, or more radical surgery.
- A personal prediction of sensory, erectile, urinary, psychosexual, or quality-of-life outcome.
- Long-term oncologic safety across settings beyond the published selected cohorts.
