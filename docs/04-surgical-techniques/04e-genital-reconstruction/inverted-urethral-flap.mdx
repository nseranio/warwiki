# Inverted Urethral Flap (IUF) Glanuloplasty

The **inverted urethral flap (IUF)** is a single-stage neoglans reconstruction reported with partial penectomy. Chavarriaga's retrospective 74-patient cohort had median follow-up of 72 months; its authors describe it as the largest IUF cohort reported.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The retrospective cohort has no skin-graft or other-flap comparator.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For the comparative overview of all four flap-based glanuloplasty options (IUF, Gulino distal-urethral, Mazza scrotal, Shaeer rectus) see [Glanuloplasty With Flaps](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/glanuloplasty-flaps). For the STSG-based standard, see [Glansectomy With STSG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/glansectomy-stsg). For the broader decision framework, see [Penile Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile).

---

## Historical Development and Rationale

First described by Belinky, Cheliz, Graziano, and Rey (2011, n = 10) and later reported by Chavarriaga et al. at Instituto Nacional de Cancerología (Bogotá), Colombia in a 2022 retrospective cohort of 74 patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The rationale is to preserve a longer urethral stump and use its mucosal surface for the neoglans. Sansalone described approximately 2.5 cm of ventral spatulation, not a fixed overhang beyond the corpora; preservable urethral length remains limited by the tumor margin.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

---

## Anatomical Basis

- The corpus spongiosum surrounds the penile urethra, which has proximal and distal blood supply. The inverted flap described by Sansalone and colleagues depends on perfusion from the proximal urethra; spongiofibrosis can compromise this supply.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- Paired cavernosal, dorsal and bulbourethral arteries have extensive anastomotic connections. The deep dorsal artery supplies the glans.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Buck's fascia envelops the corpora cavernosa and spongiosum circumferentially.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Urethral mucosa is sensate, but IUF-specific objective sensory outcomes have not been reported. The thermal and tactile sensation described by Gulino followed a different technique and does not apply to IUF.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Indications

| Indication | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Partial penectomy for penile SCC** | Reported in Belinky and Chavarriaga.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> When corporal invasion or organ-sparing feasibility is uncertain, EAU-ASCO 2026 weakly recommends penile MRI for planning (ultrasound if unavailable).<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> |
| **Glansectomy with selected distal corporectomy** | Sansalone's 34-patient IUF series involved glans-confined clinical T1–T2 SCC; two patients required distal corporectomy after positive corporal frozen sections.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> |
| **Traumatic partial amputation** | Described as a possible application by Belinky; none of the 10 reported patients had traumatic amputation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Partial penectomy with enough uninvolved urethra to form a tension-free flap without compromising margins | No validated minimum overhang; Sansalone reported approximately 2.5 cm of ventral spatulation, a different measurement<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> |

### Contraindications

- Tumor involving the urethral segment required for the flap or leaving an involved urethral margin; distal urethral involvement can be resected in selected penile cancers, but IUF still requires sufficient uninvolved residual urethra.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
- Prior urethral stricture or urethral surgery: Sansalone and colleagues excluded these patients because spongiofibrosis could impair proximal flap perfusion; assess flap suitability and consider another reconstruction. A urethral-flap case report also cautions against using tissue potentially affected by lichen sclerosus because of stenosis risk.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup><sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
- Proximal penectomy with insufficient residual urethra
- Total penectomy (no residual urethra)

## Surgical Technique

The steps below outline the technique. Measurements, suture sizes, catheter duration and dressings vary by surgeon and are not standardized in the published series.

### Step 1 — Partial penectomy

- Penile-base tourniquet
- Circumferential skin incision at the planned amputation level
- Sharp transection of the corpora
- Confirm negative corporal and urethral margins. NCCN recommends intraoperative frozen sections during penectomy (category 2A); EAU recommends them when complete removal is in doubt (weak).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> The technique reports used intraoperative margin assessment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Step 2 — Urethral preservation (defining step)

- Preserve enough uninvolved, well-perfused urethra for tension-free coverage without compromising margins. Sansalone’s approximately 2.5-cm measurement is the ventral spatulation length, not a required urethral overhang.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- In Sansalone's IUF protocol, the shaft was completely degloved and the urethra mobilized off the corpora to the crura before ventral spatulation to gain flap reach.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- Send the urethral stump for frozen section to confirm a negative margin at the true urethral transection, consistent with the NCCN penectomy recommendation (category 2A).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### Step 3 — Corporal closure

- After partial penectomy, close transected corporal ends as needed for hemostasis; in Sansalone’s glansectomy variant, the flap covered retained tumor-free corporal heads after margin assessment.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- Approximate tunica albuginea for hemostasis and a smooth rounded corporal tip surface

### Step 4 — Urethral flap inversion

- After mobilization and ventral spatulation, evert the viable distal urethral flap on its preserved proximal spongiosal blood supply over tumor-free corporal heads.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- Mucosal (luminal) surface faces outward, creating the moist pink glans-like surface
- The outer spongiosal aspect faces the corporal tips; Sansalone and colleagues fixed the everted urethral edges to the underlying corpora.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

### Step 5 — Neoglans shaping and fixation

- Suture the inverted flap circumferentially to the penile shaft skin at the level of the corporal transection with fine absorbable suture
- Junction creates a corona-like ridge simulating the coronal sulcus
- Sculpt / trim to approximate native-glans shape and contour

### Step 6 — Neomeatus creation

- In a published multicenter IUF protocol, the distal urethra was spatulated ventrally for approximately 2.5 cm and everted over the corporal heads to form the pseudoglans; a separate opening cut through the flap is not described.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- In Sansalone’s technique, fix the spatulated urethral flap to the corpora and shaft skin; the report does not describe a separate meatal-edge suture.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- Position to allow directed urinary stream for standing voiding

### Step 7 — Catheterization and dressing

- Sansalone's 34-patient IUF protocol left a 16 Fr Foley in place for 24 hours.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- Light compressive dressing

## Published Series

### Belinky 2011 — Original Description (n = 10)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Mean age | 61 y (18–71) |
| Indication | Penile SCC — all partial penectomy |
| Mean follow-up | 11 mo (5–17) |
| **Flap necrosis** | 0% |
| **Neomeatal stenosis** | 0% |
| Early tumor recurrence | 1/10 at the right cavernous body at six months despite a reported 2-cm surgical margin; no urethral-level recurrence<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |

Authors' conclusion: "simple, reproducible" with "satisfactory functional and cosmetic results, and an acceptable complication rate."

### Sansalone 2013 — Inverted Distal-Urethral Flap (n = 34)<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

At median 27-month follow-up (range 6–48), all 34 flaps survived. Four final proximal margins were positive after initially clear frozen sections; those patients underwent distal corporectomy with STSG reconstruction. One patient had a local recurrence at six months and required partial penectomy. Hematoma occurred in 2/34 (one surgical evacuation); two patients reported ventral curvature (reported as 9%). Sensation and cosmetic satisfaction were assessed by direct questioning without an objective sensory test or validated questionnaire.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

### Gulino 2007 — Related Distal-Urethral Reconstruction (n = 14)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

A distinct distal-urethral mucosal reconstruction, sharing the general principle of using urethral tissue for a neoglans:

| Parameter | Value |

| Mean follow-up | 13 mo |

## Complications

| Complication | Belinky (n = 10) | Chavarriaga (n = 74) | Gulino (n = 14) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flap necrosis | 0%<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | not reported | not reported |
| Neomeatal stenosis | 0%<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | not reported | not reported |
| Tumor recurrence / RFS | 10% (1 / 10) early tumor recurrence<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Six-year RFS 90.5%; local-recurrence rate not reported in the abstract<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | 0/14 local recurrence at mean 13 mo<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| Ventral penile curvature | 10% (1 / 10)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | not reported | not reported |
| Penile retraction | n/r | n/r | None reported |
| Urethral fistula | not reported | n/r | not reported |

Belinky reported ventral penile curvature in 1/10, without penetration difficulty.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Its mechanism is not described, and no larger-series rate is available.

Belinky reported no meatal stenosis in 10 patients; Chavarriaga did not report the event, so neither a zero rate across IUF series nor an advantage over STSG is established. Pang's review of glansectomy series gives meatal stenosis in 8.1% (range 0–14.3%).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Wang's seven-patient series describes a spiral embedded flap for meatal stenosis after penile cancer surgery.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Oncologic Safety

The main oncologic question is whether preserving a longer urethral stump risks residual disease. The evidence and constraints are:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

1. Preserve a longer urethral stump only when negative urethral and corporal margins remain achievable; use intraoperative pathology according to the operative plan and current guideline context. Frozen sections do not replace permanent pathology: in Sansalone’s 34-patient series, four initially clear frozen proximal margins proved positive on final histology and required distal corporectomy with STSG reconstruction.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
2. Chavarriaga reported 6-year RFS of 90.5% in selected patients.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
3. Urethral involvement and the required resection depend on individual tumor extent. AJCC 8 staging does not make urethral preservation automatically safe.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
4. The EAU-ASCO 2026 guideline gives a strong recommendation to offer organ-sparing surgery and reconstructive techniques for lesions confined to the glans and prepuce (PeIN, Ta, T1–T2) in patients willing to comply with strict follow-up. It names split-thickness skin graft neoglans formation but not the inverted urethral flap.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[23]](#ref23)</sup> NCCN lists partial or total penectomy for T2 or greater disease (category 2A) and does not name IUF as a reconstructive option.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> ESMO-EURACAN 2024 recommends glansectomy with or without distal urethrectomy and split-thickness skin-graft reconstruction for T2 tumors confined to the glans (evidence level III, grade A), without naming IUF.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

## Surveillance — Current EAU Framework<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

Follow-up depends on whether treatment is classified as penile-preserving or amputation and on nodal status. The current EAU table lists three-monthly primary-site review for years 1–2 and annual review for years 3–5 after amputation; penile-preserving treatment uses three-monthly then six-monthly review. Groin examination, ultrasound, FNAC or cross-sectional imaging are risk- and nodal-status dependent. Capable patients are taught self-examination and given rapid re-access. The schedule follows the treating cancer team's classification of the operation and nodal status. NCCN starts primary-site clinical review six-monthly after penectomy in years 1–2 and continues annual review through year 10 (category 2A). Examination includes the penis and inguinal regions.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Psychosexual Impact and Quality of Life

Partial penectomy has a substantial psychosexual impact that IUF aims to mitigate:<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- **Whyte 2022 SR.** Three of four eligible studies reported a decrease across all IIEF domains after partial penectomy, and many patients still maintained satisfactory sex lives. In one study, greater residual penile length was associated with higher postoperative function, and older age and anxiety with lower function.
- **Roumieux 2025 qualitative study.** Nine men described effects of penile-cancer diagnosis and treatment on intimacy, including a "voyage of sexual re-discovery". The authors call for pre-surgical information and post-surgical psychosexual support.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **Yu 2016 (n = 43).** IIEF-15 scores fell and SAS and SDS scores changed significantly.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> Flaccid penile length was positively associated with intercourse satisfaction, and anxiety was negatively associated with erectile, orgasmic, desire and intercourse-satisfaction domains.
- **Romero 2005 (n = 18).** 55.6% had erectile function sufficient for intercourse after partial penectomy.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> Among sexually abstinent patients, 50% cited shame about small penis size and absence of the glans as the main reason for not resuming.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Kieffer 2014 (n = 90 questionnaire respondents).** Men who had (partial) penectomy reported significantly more problems than those with penile-sparing surgery with orgasm (effect size 0.54), appearance concerns (0.61), life interference (0.49) and urinary function (83% vs 43%).<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **EAU-ASCO 2026.** Strong recommendation to offer organ-sparing surgery and reconstructive techniques for glans- and prepuce-confined lesions (PeIN, Ta, T1–T2) in patients who will comply with strict follow-up. Frozen section of resection margins is a weak recommendation when completeness is in doubt, and routine use is not recommended.

IUF is intended to address these concerns by giving a glans-like mucosal surface. Sansalone’s selected 25-patient IUF cohort required a preoperative IIEF-15 erectile-function score of at least 17 and a postoperative penile stump of at least 3 cm, and excluded recurrence, metastasis and surgical complications. Mean erectile-function domain score fell from 28.68 before surgery to 21.28 at three months (p &lt; 0.001); without a non-IUF comparator this does not measure IUF-specific benefit.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> Chavarriaga separately reported mean EQ-5D-3L-VAS of 84.6 and IIEF-5 of 17.3; the cited abstract gives no preoperative scores or technique comparator.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Gulino's 14-patient cohort reported thermal and tactile sensibility in all participants, but that was a different distal-urethral technique.

## Advantages

1. **Single-stage.** It is performed at the same operation as partial penectomy.
2. **No separate donor site.** It avoids thigh (STSG) or oral (BMG) donor morbidity.
3. **Moist mucosal surface.** The surface resembles native glans.
4. **Possible sensory preservation.** IUF-specific objective sensory results are not available.
5. **Complications.** Belinky reported no stenosis or necrosis in 10 patients; Chavarriaga does not report those events.
6. **Follow-up.** Chavarriaga's selected cohort reported 6-year RFS of 90.5%, mean ICIQ-MLUTS voiding score 1.7 and EQ-5D-3L-VAS 84.6.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Limitations

1. Enough preservable urethral length is required without compromising margins; the commonly cited 2–3 cm is not a validated threshold.
2. Ventral curvature occurred in 1 of 10 in Belinky; Chavarriaga does not report a rate.
3. **Limited published data.** In addition to Belinky (n = 10) and Chavarriaga (n = 74), Sansalone and colleagues published an inverted distal urethral flap series in 2013.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[21]](#ref21)</sup> Gulino's distal-urethral reconstruction is distinct, and no randomized comparison with STSG exists.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
4. **Not a named technique** in the EAU-ASCO guideline recommendations.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
5. **Retrospective design.** Chavarriaga's series is a chart review that needs external validation.
6. **No standardized cosmetic-satisfaction data**, unlike Pang’s mixed glansectomy review, which reported 86.3% satisfaction with overall appearance or outcomes.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

---

## Postoperative Surveillance

Follow-up after partial penectomy, per EAU-ASCO 2023 and the EAU chapter:<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

- Follow the EAU schedule for the treatment and nodal status; amputation and penile-preserving pathways differ.
- Patient self-examination covers the neoglans and penile stump.
- Biopsy any suspicious lesion.
- Salvage organ-sparing surgery is an option for small recurrences not involving the corpora.
- Imaging and groin surveillance follow nodal risk or status.

---

## Future Directions

- Multicenter validation across institutions and populations is needed.
- A randomized comparison of IUF and STSG neoglans would address functional and oncologic outcomes.
- Standardized functional-outcome reporting was called for in the Pang review.
- Hybrid IUF and BMG meatoplasty combinations have not been studied.
- Long-term histologic studies could characterize keratinization or metaplastic change in inverted urethral mucosa.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The largest reported dedicated IUF cohort is Chavarriaga's n = 74, with 6-year OS 86.5%, RFS 90.5% and mean IIEF-5 17.3 in selected patients.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- The technique preserves a longer urethral stump and uses its mucosa for the neoglans; the commonly cited 2–3 cm is not a validated threshold.
- It is single-stage with no separate donor site. Belinky reported no stenosis or necrosis in 10 patients; Chavarriaga does not report those events.
- Negative urethral and corporal margins are required. EAU guidance suggests frozen section when completeness is in doubt, not automatically.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
- Belinky reported ventral curvature in 1 of 10 without penetration difficulty; Chavarriaga does not report a curvature rate.
- IUF after partial penectomy requires enough uninvolved, well-perfused residual urethra for flap coverage and negative margins; proximal amputation or involvement of the segment needed for the flap can preclude it.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
