# Genitourinary Vascularized Composite Allotransplantation (Penile / Total Penis-Scrotum-Lower-Abdominal-Wall VCA)

**Penile VCA** is the transplantation of a complete penile allograft — corpora cavernosa, corpus spongiosum, urethra, neurovascular bundles, and skin envelope — from a deceased donor to a recipient with severe penile tissue loss. The **Johns Hopkins 2018 total penis-scrotum-lower-abdominal-wall VCA** (Redett, NEJM 2019) is an extensive reported genitourinary VCA, incorporating penile and scrotal tissue plus a segment of the lower abdominal wall as a single composite allograft.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The **2023 review** summarized five penile transplants, including two explantations and three grafts retained at its reporting cutoff. These are historical published counts, not a current global registry total.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

This page consolidates the penile and total penis-scrotum-lower-abdominal-wall variants. For broader scrotal context see [Scrotal Reconstruction Techniques](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/scrotal-reconstruction); for autologous penile alternatives see [Penile Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile) and the [Radial Forearm Free Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/radial-forearm).

## The Johns Hopkins Total Penis-Scrotum-Lower-Abdominal-Wall VCA

**Recipient.** Young military servicemember with IED injuries: total loss of penis beyond a small stump, absence of scrotal tissue and testes, substantial lower-abdominal-wall defects — not amenable to conventional autologous reconstruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Allograft composition.**

- Complete penile shaft (corpora cavernosa, corpus spongiosum, urethra).
- Bilateral scrotal skin and dartos; **testes were not transplanted**.
- Lower abdominal wall tissue.
- Preserved neurovascular structures: dorsal penile arteries, veins, nerves; bilateral femoral and external pudendal arteries; saphenous veins.

**Vascular strategy.** **Deep inferior epigastric arteries** revascularized the dorsal penile circulation; **external pudendal arteries** perfused the proximal scrotal and abdominal-wall components. This reduced the number of microsurgical anastomoses while ensuring reliable perfusion of all components.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Immunosuppression.** Donor **bone-marrow-based immunomodulation** with transition to **tacrolimus monotherapy**; stable leukocyte counts and renal function at 3 years on single-agent maintenance.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

**Functional outcomes (~1 yr).** Near-normal erectile function; orgasmic capability; restored standing urination; dramatic QoL / self-image improvement; continuous nerve regeneration documented at 3 years post-transplant.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Indications and Patient Selection

**Published or proposed candidate settings.** Penile VCA remains investigational; these are not universally accepted clinical indications.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

- Traumatic penile loss — IED / blast / industrial / animal attack.
- Ritual circumcision complications (leading cause of penile loss in young men in South Africa).
- Penile cancer after total penectomy.
- Severe congenital or oncologic defects have been proposed as expanding indications rather than established uses.
- Trans-masculine gender affirmation has **cadaveric anatomical feasibility** data only and remains preclinical/investigational.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

**Baltimore Criteria (Lopez 2023, *Nat Rev Urol*).**<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- Penile defect not amenable to / unacceptable after conventional reconstruction.
- Adequate psychological stability and motivation.
- Absence of active psychiatric disease or substance abuse.
- Demonstrated medication-compliance capacity.
- Adequate social support.
- Informed consent for lifelong immunosuppression, rejection, infection, malignancy, graft-loss risk.
- Multidisciplinary evaluation (surgery, medicine, psychiatry, social work, nurse coordinators).

**Updated 2023 recommendations.** Counsel on long-term nerve-regeneration improvement and tacrolimus-monotherapy feasibility; replace one-time STI surveillance with longitudinal partnership; proactively address donor-recipient color mismatch ethics.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Surgical Technique

Published operations used different allograft extents, recipient anatomy, and vascular strategies. The details below are source-specific technical lessons, not a standardized protocol.

### Donor procurement

- Identify dorsal penile arteries, deep dorsal vein, dorsal nerves, cavernosal arteries, external pudendal vessels.
- En-bloc harvest with maximal proximal corporal and urethral length.
- For the Johns Hopkins extended allograft — scrotal skin, abdominal-wall tissue, bilateral femoral / external pudendal vessels.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Cosmetic donor reconstruction** with an abdominal skin-flap phallus was used in the Cape Town report to address donor appearance and family concerns.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Cold preservation with standard organ-preservation solutions.

### Recipient preparation

- Excise scar tissue from the penile stump.
- Mobilize recipient dorsal arteries, inferior epigastrics, external pudendal arteries.
- Identify dorsal nerves for coaptation.
- Prepare urethral stump.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Vascular anastomoses — perfusion territories

Tuffaha 2014 cadaveric study — each system has **distinct perfusion territory**:<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

| Artery | Territory |
|---|---|
| **Dorsal penile** | Glans and corpus spongiosum |
| **Cavernosal** | Corpora cavernosa (minimal communication to skin) |
| **External pudendal** | Shaft and surrounding skin |
| **Deep inferior epigastric** | Recipient option when native dorsal arteries are obliterated (Cape Town, Johns Hopkins)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |

**Venous options.** Deep dorsal vein (GEM coupler ring); saphenous veins (Johns Hopkins extended allograft).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

**Modified surgical model (Ruiz 2023, 12 cadavers).** **Anterior pubic osteotomy** was proposed to access the root of the cavernous bodies and the Alcock ducts for more-proximal internal-pudendal anastomoses. This is anatomical feasibility work, not a demonstrated clinical outcome.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

### Neural coaptation

Bilateral **dorsal nerve** epineural repair (9-0 nylon, loupes or microscope). Regeneration is progressive — **continuous at 3 years** post-transplant (Johns Hopkins).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

### Urethral anastomosis

Published cases describe spatulated end-to-end repair, interrupted absorbable suture, postoperative catheterization, and imaging before removal; details varied by operation. The retained grafts summarized in the 2023 review achieved spontaneous voiding, but the denominator is extremely small.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Corporal anastomosis

The Cape Town case used watertight interrupted repair of the tunica albuginea with 2-0 polyglycolic acid; this is a case-specific technique, not a universal VCA standard.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Immunosuppression

No standardized penile-VCA protocol exists. The case regimens below are descriptive. Knoedler 2026 provides a broader systematic review across VCA types, but its 136-recipient cohort included only **one penile recipient**.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

**Induction.**

- **ATG** — used in the Cape Town, MGH, and Johns Hopkins reports; Cape Town reported 100 mg/d for 10 days.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Donor bone-marrow infusion** — Johns Hopkins immunomodulatory protocol enabling tacrolimus monotherapy.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

**Maintenance.**

- Standard triple therapy — tacrolimus + MMF + prednisone (Cape Town, MGH).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Tacrolimus monotherapy** (Johns Hopkins) — stable leukocyte counts and renal function at 3 yr after bone-marrow protocol.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- The Cape Town case used an initial tacrolimus target of **10–15 ng/mL**; this is not a general dosing recommendation.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

**Rejection across VCA types—not penile-specific (Knoedler 2026).** In 46 reports covering 136 recipients, acute rejection occurred in 81/136 (60%) within year 1. Reported Banff grades and treatments overlap because recipients could have multiple episodes: grade I 36%, II 54%, III 37%, IV 0.7%; corticosteroids 72%, tacrolimus 36%, ATG 14%, alemtuzumab 8.1%, and rituximab 4.4%. These aggregate data were dominated by upper-extremity, face, and abdominal-wall VCA and should not be used as penile-VCA incidence estimates.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

**Penile-specific.** MGH — Banff III rescued with ATG; Cape Town — no clinical rejection at 24 mo; Johns Hopkins — the cited follow-up reported a monotherapy strategy. Maintenance immunosuppression is still required; this does not establish immune tolerance.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

**Erectile-tissue physiology.** In Sopko's 2017 ex-vivo model, rejection impaired cavernous-tissue physiology and tacrolimus preserved relaxation better than cyclosporine. This laboratory comparison does not establish clinical superiority of one maintenance regimen.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

**Case-specific prophylaxis.** The Cape Town report used TMP-SMX for Pneumocystis and isoniazid for tuberculosis for one year; prophylaxis should follow recipient risk and transplant-program protocols.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Functional Outcomes

| Domain | Result |
|---|---|
| **Urinary** | Spontaneous voiding through transplanted urethra in all retained grafts; Cape Town Qmax 16.3 mL/s at 24 mo; Johns Hopkins restored standing urination.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Erectile** | Cape Town — spontaneous erections at 3 wk, intercourse at 5 wk, IIEF satisfaction 8/10 at 24 mo. MGH — tumescence at 7 mo. Johns Hopkins — near-normal at ~1 yr. Penile rehab with tadalafil 5 mg daily × 3 mo (Cape Town).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **Sensation / nerve regeneration** | Cape Town — normal orgasm and ejaculation at 24 mo. MGH — partial sensation at 7 mo. Johns Hopkins — **continuous regeneration at 3 yr**.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **Reproductive** | Cape Town — the recipient's partner conceived naturally at 6 mo (stillbirth at term in another province). The conception involved the recipient's retained testes and reproductive tract; a penile allograft does not supply donor gonads.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **QoL** | Cape Town SF-36v2 MCS 25 → 57 (6 mo) → 46 (24 mo); PCS 37 → 60 → 59. MGH — improved health satisfaction, self-image, optimism. Johns Hopkins — dramatic QoL / self-image improvement.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |

## Complications

The 2023 review reported that all five historical transplants had vascular congestion and/or hematoma requiring surgical intervention, and all but one had some degree of allograft-tissue necrosis. This is a five-case historical description, not a stable complication rate.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- Cape Town — arterial thrombus revision at 8 h; infected hematoma + proximal skin necrosis POD 6.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- MGH — hematoma evacuation POD 2; eschar debridement POD 13.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- Johns Hopkins — specific vascular complications not detailed in NEJM correspondence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Rejection.** MGH steroid-resistant Banff III rescued with ATG; others as above.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

**Immunosuppression-related.** Cape Town — AKI at 7 mo (reversed by tacrolimus dose reduction); acne; HTN; phaeohyphomycosis (*Alternaria*) at 8 mo.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

**Psychological rejection.** Guangzhou explantation POD 14 — patient and partner could not tolerate the transplant — unique complication of genital VCA.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

**Graft loss.** Two of the five cases summarized in the 2023 review underwent explantation. This tiny historical denominator is not a stable prospective graft-loss probability.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Ethical Considerations<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

- **Risk-benefit** — VCA is **not life-saving**; lifelong immunosuppression with infection / malignancy / renal-toxicity / metabolic risk must be weighed against QoL gain.
- **Donor consent and family ethics** — cosmetic donor reconstruction was used in a reported procurement strategy and can be important to donor-family discussions.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Donor-recipient color mismatch** — visibly genital tissue makes mismatch more psychologically loaded than other VCA types.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **STI risk** under immunosuppression — longitudinal partnership preferred over one-time surveillance.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Compliance** — noncompliance likely contributed to ≥ 1 graft loss; multidisciplinary partnership essential.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Gender affirmation.** Cristofari 2023 — anatomical feasibility of one-stage GUVCA for trans men including penis + scrotum + inferior bladder patch with urinary sphincter + prostate + seminal vesicles + pubic-bone strip — raises additional ethical questions about expanding indications.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

Caplan 2017 called for explicit guidance on tissue donation, consent, subject selection, surgical-team qualifications, and management of failure or dissatisfaction before broader clinical use.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

## Preclinical Models

- **Rat penile transplant model (Fidder 2019).** Syngeneic and allogeneic heterotopic transplant in Lewis / Brown Norway rats; rejection in a 4-stage progression with epidermal sloughing at POD 7 and full rejection POD 14–16; **distal-to-proximal** pattern (skin / urethral lining first → tunica albuginea / corpora). Novel 4-grade rejection classification analogous to 2007 Banff Criteria.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **Ex-vivo human (Sopko 2017).** Mixed lymphocyte reaction with human cavernous tissue; tacrolimus preserved corporal relaxation better than cyclosporine in this laboratory model.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Cadaveric feasibility.** Tuffaha 2014 — distinct dorsal / cavernosal / external-pudendal perfusion territories. Ruiz 2023 — anterior-pubic-osteotomy approach. Cristofari 2023 — GUVCA-for-gender-affirmation feasibility in 20 cadavers + 5 simulated transplants.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## VCA vs Autologous Phalloplasty

No head-to-head comparative trial supports superiority claims. This table contrasts structural tradeoffs and observations from separate case literatures.

| Feature | Autologous (RFFF / ALT) | Penile VCA |
|---|---|---|
| Tissue type | Non-genital (forearm / thigh) | True penile tissue |
| Erection mechanism | A prosthesis is generally required for rigidity | Spontaneous erections have been reported in retained penile allografts |
| Sensation | Sensory recovery varies after nerve coaptation | Progressive sensory recovery and orgasm have been reported in individual cases |
| Urethral function | Stricture / fistula risk depends on technique and population | Spontaneous voiding reported in retained grafts; too few cases for a valid complication comparison |
| Fertility | Depends on retained reproductive organs and sperm availability; phalloplasty does not itself determine fertility | Natural conception reported using the recipient’s own reproductive organs; penile VCA does not supply donor testes |
| Cosmesis | Variable; commonly tube-in-tube construction | Native penile tissue; appearance outcomes are reported case by case |
| Immunosuppression | None | **Lifelong** |
| Donor-site morbidity | Recipient flap-donor-site morbidity | No recipient autologous flap donor site, but deceased-donor recovery and donor-family ethical considerations remain |
| Reoperation | Urethral, flap, or prosthesis revisions may be needed | Vascular and wound revisions have occurred; comparative rates are not established |
| Availability | Established specialist reconstruction | Highly limited investigational transplantation; five cases in the 2023 review |

## Future Directions

- **Proposed expanding indications** — congenital and oncologic defects; gender-affirming GUVCA remains supported only by cadaveric feasibility work.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Immunosuppression minimization** — the Johns Hopkins bone-marrow-based protocol enabled tacrolimus monotherapy with reported stable renal function at 3 years; it did not establish immune tolerance.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Standardization** — OPTN began VCA oversight in the US in 2014; the 2021 analysis reported 62 **all-type US VCAs**, not penile transplants. Surgical, immunosuppression, rejection-monitoring, and outcome protocols remain heterogeneous.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- **Anterior pubic osteotomy** approach for more reliable internal-pudendal anastomoses.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Reimbursement and institutional support** for transition from research to standard of care.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
