# Ventral Slit Scrotal Flap (VSSF — Westerman / Tausch)

The **ventral slit scrotal flap (VSSF)** is a single-stage local-flap technique for selected adults with LS- or phimosis-related penile trapping and a ventral shaft-skin deficit after release. A ventral midline scrotal incision with horizontal relaxing incisions creates local flaps rotated onto the shaft without a graft. In Westerman and colleagues' 15-patient series, all were discharged the same day, mean operative time was 83 minutes, no perioperative complications were reported, and 11/15 remained satisfied without further intervention at the reported follow-up. These observations do not prove superiority or durable freedom from recurrence beyond the series' follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For the integrated AABP workflow, see [Buried Penis Repair](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/buried-penis-repair). For higher-complexity escutcheonectomy and grafting pathways, see [Escutcheonectomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/escutcheonectomy) and [Penile Skin Grafting](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile-skin-grafting). For circumferential-defect scrotal flaps, see [Fakin](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/bipedicled-anterior-scrotal-flap), [Murányi](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/muranyi-scrotal-tunnel-flap), [Yao](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/yao-butterfly-scrotal-flap), and [Pribaz / McLaughlin staged](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/staged-scrotal-flap).

## Rationale and Position in the Algorithm

AABP is heterogeneous: obesity (approximately 45%), LS (approximately 23%), genital lymphedema (approximately 20%), or combinations.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Traditional complex repairs (escutcheonectomy and STSG ± panniculectomy) are inpatient procedures with 27–50% complication rates.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The VSSF addresses an anatomic subset with usable residual shaft skin and a ventral deficit after release. That may overlap a low-complexity buried-penis category, but Pariser/Tausch classification labels should not be treated as proof that every such patient needs this exact flap.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

---

## Indications and Contraindications

### Indications

- **Adult buried penis from LS** with phimotic-ring trapping (87% of the original series)
- **Adult buried penis from phimosis (non-LS)** with ventral skin deficiency after release
- **Viable dorsal penile shaft skin.** Prerequisite
- **Ventral-only deficiency** after the slit
- **Intact uninvolved scrotal skin** with adequate laxity; PAS classification may help document anatomy but does not itself validate VSSF selection
- **Outpatient candidate**

### Contraindications

- **Circumferential nonviable penile skin**: convert to complete excision and STSG or FTSG<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Severe abdominal lipodystrophy** requiring escutcheonectomy / panniculectomy (Pariser IV–V)
- **Genital lymphedema involving the scrotum.** Scrotal donor unsuitable
- **Scrotal-skin LS involvement.** Flap donor must be disease-free

## Operative Technique

### 1. Ventral slit of the phimotic ring

- Identify the constricting phimotic / scarred ring
- **Ventral midline slit** through the ring extending proximally toward the penoscrotal junction; release the trapped penis
- Assess and treat diseased / LS-involved tissue according to its extent; do not imply every LS area requires wholesale excision
- **Assess dorsal skin viability.** If circumferentially nonviable, convert to complete excision and STSG

### 2. Assess the ventral defect

- Measure length and width of the exposed ventral shaft (dorsal skin remains intact and covers the dorsal hemicircumference)

### 3. Ventral midline scrotal incision

- Extend a midline scrotal incision along the raphe from the penoscrotal junction inferiorly; length matches the ventral defect

### 4. Horizontal relaxing (back-cut) incisions

- The original technique makes 2-cm horizontal relaxing incisions at the distal end of the midline scrotal incision and mobilizes deep dartos with the skin to preserve cutaneous blood supply<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- These permit advancement of the scrotal skin as bilateral local rotation flaps

### 5. Scrotal flap rotation

- Each flap rotated superiorly and laterally to cover the corresponding half of the ventral defect
- Right flap to right hemi-ventral defect; left flap to left hemi-ventral defect
- Inset tension-free

### 6. Flap inset and suturing

- Distal edges to subcoronal skin / glans margin
- Proximal edges to the penoscrotal-junction skin
- Lateral edges to the viable dorsal penile-skin edges at 3 and 9 o'clock
- The two flaps meet at the ventral midline for longitudinal closure
- Interrupted absorbable sutures

### 7. Penopubic and penoscrotal angle fixation

- The full original technique anchors the tunica albuginea at the penile base to the deep dermis of dorsolateral skin with 2-0 braided nonabsorbable sutures to restore the penopubic angle, then recreates the penoscrotal angle with permanent sutures. Do not substitute a generic Buck's-fascia anchoring instruction. The cohort does not isolate the effect of fixation on recurrence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### 8. Donor closure

- Primary closure of the scrotum; relaxing incisions closed or allowed to heal by secondary intention depending on tension

### 9. Dressing and discharge

- Light compressive dressing
- All 15 patients in the original series discharged same-day

## Position in the Tausch / Pariser Algorithm

| Pariser tier | Anatomy | Procedure | VSSF role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | Viable penile skin, local-flap-sufficient | Unburying + local flap | **Primary technique** |
| II | Nonviable penile skin, no abdominal / scrotal component | Skin excision + STSG | n/a |
| III | Scrotal surgery needed | Scrotoplasty ± STSG | n/a |
| IV | Escutcheonectomy needed | Escutcheonectomy + STSG | n/a |
| V | Panniculectomy needed | Panniculectomy + escutcheonectomy + STSG | n/a |

Tausch describes a tailored buried-penis approach. Ventral release, local flap or graft choice should follow the actual skin viability, scrotal donor condition and abdominal contribution; this table is an orientation aid, not a universal automatic sequence.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## PAS-Driven Selection

PAS descriptors can document residual penile skin, abdominal contribution and scrotal donor suitability, but VSSF outcomes have not been validated by these exact PAS combinations. Use them as anatomical prompts, not a tested eligibility rule.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| PAS axis | Favors VSSF | Requires escalation |
|---|---|---|
| **P** | P1, P2a | P2b, P2c |
| **A** | A0, A1 | A2 (panniculectomy) |
| **S** | S0 | S1, S2 (scrotoplasty / scrotectomy) |

## Outcomes — Westerman / Tausch (n = 15)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Parameter | Result |
|---|---|
| Mean age | 51 y (26–75) |
| Mean BMI | **42.6 kg/m²** (29.8–53.9) |
| LS etiology | 13 / 15 (87%) |
| Phimosis (non-LS) | 2 / 15 (13%) |
| Setting | **Outpatient — 100% same-day discharge** |
| Mean OR time | **83 min** (35–145) |
| Mean EBL | **57 cc** (25–200) |
| **Perioperative complications** | **0 / 15** |
| Satisfied without further intervention | **11 / 15 (73.3%)** at mean 12 months (range 3–29); qualitative chart-review assessment |
| Recurrence | 3 / 15 (20%) |
| Subsequent STSG for recurrence | 2 / 3 → successful |

### Recurrence analysis

| Cause | Why VSSF was insufficient |
|---|---|
| **LS progression** | Chronic disease — inherent to any LS-related repair, not VSSF-specific<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **Panniculus migration** | Suprapubic adiposity not addressed — these patients need escutcheonectomy / Pariser IV<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Edematous groin tissue** | Lymphedema not addressed |

The three recurrences among 15 should be read in their own follow-up context. Plamadeala's larger AABP cohort used a different case mix and procedure mix; its recurrence associations do not prove that a more extensive operation would have prevented recurrence in these VSSF patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Comparison With Other AABP Pathways

| Feature | **VSSF (Westerman)** | Escutcheonectomy + STSG (Staniorski) | Escutcheonectomy + FTSG (Jeng) | Plamadeala low-complexity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Complexity tier | Pariser I (low) | IV–V (high) | IV–V (high) | I–II (low) |
| Setting | **Outpatient** | Inpatient | Inpatient | Variable |
| Skin graft | **None** | STSG | FTSG (escutcheon) | Variable |
| Mean OR time | **83 min** | n/r | n/r | n/r |
| Perioperative complications | **0%** | 50% | 56% | 13.3% |
| Wound dehiscence | n/r | 31% | n/r | n/r |
| Wound infection | n/r | 30% | 41% | n/r |
| Recurrence | 20% | 3.9% revision | 13% surgical failure | 12.7% overall |
| Satisfaction | 73.3% | n/r | n/r | 86.8% |
| Separate donor site | **None** | Thigh or escutcheon | Escutcheon | Variable |

These are different severity groups and outcome definitions, not a head-to-head effectiveness comparison. In particular, VSSF recurrence, STSG revision and FTSG surgical failure are not interchangeable endpoints.

## Advantages

1. **Outpatient in this series.** All 15 were discharged same-day; other AABP techniques were not compared
2. **No skin grafting.** Eliminates thigh donor or escutcheon-graft harvest
3. **No perioperative complications reported among 15**, not evidence of zero risk or uniqueness among AABP techniques
4. Short OR time (83 min) and minimal blood loss (57 cc)
5. **Preserves usable native skin** when feasible; postoperative sensation was not quantified in the accessible abstract
6. **Scrotal skin can be a pliable local option**; hair distribution and tissue match vary
7. **Technically straightforward.** Basic local-flap principles, no microsurgical expertise
8. **Feasible in obese patients.** Mean BMI 42.6 kg/m² in the series
9. **Low resource use.** Fits an ambulatory reconstructive practice

---

## Limitations and Disadvantages

1. **Limited to ventral-only deficits.** Does not cover circumferential defects
2. **Three recurrences among 15.** Cannot be ranked against a separate cohort's revision rate
3. **Anatomy-specific limit.** Extensive nonviable shaft skin, diseased scrotum or a major abdominal component may need a different or combined operation; no PAS cutpoint alone proves failure
4. **LS progression risk.** Chronic disease drives ongoing re-scarring regardless of technique<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
5. **Small series (n = 15).** Limited evidence versus Plamadeala n = 204 or Tausch n = 56
6. **No validated PROMs** in the original series; modern AABP literature uses IIEF-15, IPSS, POSAS<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
7. **Limited follow-up.** Mean 12 months (range 3–29), without standardized long-term patient-reported measures
8. **Potential penoscrotal webbing or recurrent concealment.** The original operation includes angle fixation, but its independent contribution to durability was not measured
9. **Hair-bearing variability.** Inspect individual anterior scrotal donor skin

---

## Place in the Broader Tausch Series (n = 56)

The VSSF is the entry point of the Tausch algorithm:<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- Ventral release and exposure are described in the tailored approach; do not infer all AABP repairs use identical steps
- Usable skin may permit local-flap closure; VSSF is one option
- Nonviable: complete excision and STSG
- Severe abdominal lipodystrophy: adjacent-tissue transfer
- Genital lymphedema: excision of lymphedematous tissue and primary closure ± STSG
- **Overall success 88%** (49 / 56)

Pariser's 64-patient complexity comparison provides risk context across different operations, not validation that VSSF itself has a zero major-complication or failure rate.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Patient Selection — When VSSF Wins

| Choose VSSF | Consider escalation |
|---|---|
| Trapped penis from LS or phimosis with viable dorsal skin | Circumferential nonviable penile skin → STSG / FTSG<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Ventral-only deficiency after ring release | Significant suprapubic fat pad (PAS A2) → escutcheonectomy + STSG (Pariser IV) |
| Normal scrotal skin (PAS S0), adequate laxity | Abdominal pannus contributing → panniculectomy + escutcheonectomy + STSG (Pariser V) |
| Limited abdominal contribution and viable donor skin | Genital lymphedema → individualized scrotal excision/coverage plan |
| Outpatient preference, low-morbidity priority | Circumferential paraffinoma defect → Fakin / Murányi / Yao / Pribaz staged<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| Preference to avoid graft harvest when flap anatomy is suitable | |

---

## Key Takeaways

- A 15-patient outpatient series reported no perioperative complications and 11/15 satisfied without further intervention; this does not establish a class-leading safety rate
- Ventral release and local scrotal rotation flaps are useful when residual skin and donor tissue are suitable; Pariser/PAS labels are not a validated VSSF-only rule
- **Three of 15 recurred** from different mechanisms; other cohorts' procedure complexity does not prove which escalation would prevent an individual recurrence
- The full original technique fixes the penopubic angle to tunica albuginea or deep dermis and reconstructs the penoscrotal angle; it does not prove that fixation alone prevents recurrence
- LS chronic progression is a disease-specific recurrence risk distinct from the technique itself
