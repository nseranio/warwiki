# Ventral Slit Scrotal Flap (VSSF — Westerman / Tausch)

The **ventral slit scrotal flap (VSSF)** is a single-stage local-flap technique for selected adults with LS- or phimosis-related penile trapping and a ventral shaft-skin deficit after release. A ventral midline scrotal incision with horizontal relaxing incisions creates local flaps rotated onto the shaft without a graft. In Westerman and colleagues' 15-patient series, all were discharged the same day, mean operative time was 83 minutes, no perioperative complications were reported, and 11/15 remained satisfied without further intervention at the reported follow-up. Westerman's series offers no comparative efficacy estimate or long-term recurrence data.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For the integrated AABP workflow, see [Buried Penis Repair](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/buried-penis-repair). For higher-complexity escutcheonectomy and grafting pathways, see [Escutcheonectomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/escutcheonectomy) and [Penile Skin Grafting](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile-skin-grafting). For circumferential-defect scrotal flaps, see [Fakin](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/bipedicled-anterior-scrotal-flap), [Murányi](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/muranyi-scrotal-tunnel-flap), [Yao](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/yao-butterfly-scrotal-flap), and [Pribaz / McLaughlin staged](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/staged-scrotal-flap).

## Rationale and Position in the Algorithm

In Plamadeala’s 204-patient AABP reconstruction cohort, the recorded primary etiologies were obesity (92/204, 45.1%), LS (47/204, 23.0%), and genital lymphedema/granuloma (41/204, 20.1%).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Complex repairs (escutcheonectomy and STSG, with or without panniculectomy) are usually inpatient procedures. Within 90 days, complications occurred in 50% of Staniorski's high-complexity series<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>; within three months, they occurred in 32.6% of the Pariser III-or-higher group (27.0% of the whole cohort) in Plamadeala's multicentre series.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The VSSF addresses an anatomic subset with usable residual shaft skin and a ventral deficit after release. That anatomy may overlap a low-complexity buried-penis category; Pariser/Tausch labels alone do not determine VSSF selection.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Indications and Contraindications

### Indications

- Trapped-type adult buried penis with viable dorsal skin; in Westerman’s 15-patient series, 13 (87%) had biopsy-proven LS and 11 (73%) had phimosis<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Adult buried penis from phimosis (non-LS) with ventral skin deficiency after release
- **Viable dorsal penile shaft skin.** Prerequisite
- Ventral-only deficiency after the slit
- Intact uninvolved scrotal skin with adequate laxity; PAS classification may help document anatomy but does not itself validate VSSF selection
- Outpatient candidate; initiate lifestyle and risk-factor modification, particularly weight-loss support, during preoperative planning. The 2026 EAU AABP guideline rates this a strong recommendation to reduce surgical complications and optimize outcomes.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- Assess voiding symptoms and the meatus for urethral disease when planning VSSF; 5/15 men in the original series had concurrent urethral stricture disease, and selected patients underwent concurrent meatal or urethral procedures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Contraindications

- Circumferential nonviable penile skin: convert to complete excision and STSG or FTSG<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Severe abdominal lipodystrophy requiring escutcheonectomy / panniculectomy (Pariser IV–V)
- Genital lymphedema involving the scrotum (scrotal donor unsuitable)
- Scrotal-skin LS involvement (the flap donor must be disease-free)

## Operative Technique

### 1. Ventral slit of the phimotic ring

- Identify the constricting phimotic or scarred ring and place a Foley catheter to identify the urethra.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Make a ventral midline slit through the ring until the glans is visible; use a 2-0 silk glans traction suture to expose the penis and reduce adherent lateral foreskin, then extend the release proximally toward the penoscrotal junction as needed.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Assess and treat LS-involved tissue according to its extent while preserving usable shaft skin when feasible. For coexisting LS phimosis unresponsive to guideline-conform topical treatment, the 2026 S3 guideline strongly recommends circumcision, preferably removing the complete foreskin; it strongly recommends histopathology of removed foreskin and perioperative topical corticosteroid ointment. Arrange LS follow-up initially every 3–6 months and about yearly once stable.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- Assess dorsal skin viability. If shaft skin is circumferentially nonviable, excise it completely and plan graft coverage; STSG is described for AABP repair, while FTSG from excised escutcheon has been reported in selected adults with concurrent LS.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### 2. Assess the ventral defect

- Mobilize viable dorsal skin and fix basal tunica albuginea to deep dorsolateral dermis with 2-0 braided nonabsorbable sutures before scrotal-flap harvest; then measure the ventral defect’s length and width while native skin covers the dorsal hemicircumference.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### 3. Ventral midline scrotal incision

- Extend a midline scrotal incision along the raphe from the penoscrotal junction inferiorly; length matches the ventral defect

### 4. Horizontal relaxing (back-cut) incisions

- The original technique makes 2-cm horizontal relaxing incisions at the distal end of the midline scrotal incision and mobilizes deep dartos with the skin to preserve cutaneous blood supply<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- These permit advancement of the scrotal skin as bilateral local rotation flaps

### 5. Scrotal flap rotation

- Each flap rotated superiorly and laterally to cover the corresponding half of the ventral defect
- Right flap to right hemi-ventral defect; left flap to left hemi-ventral defect
- Inset tension-free

### 6. Flap inset and suturing

- Distal edges to subcoronal skin / glans margin
- Proximal edges to the penoscrotal-junction skin
- Lateral edges to the viable dorsal penile-skin edges at 3 and 9 o'clock
- The two flaps meet at the ventral midline for longitudinal closure
- Interrupted absorbable sutures

### 7. Penopubic and penoscrotal angle fixation

- After flap rotation and inset, recreate the penoscrotal angle with 2-0 braided permanent sutures. Penopubic fixation occurs before scrotal-flap harvest; do not substitute a generic Buck’s-fascia anchoring instruction. The cohort does not isolate either fixation step’s effect on recurrence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### 8. Donor closure

- In Westerman’s technique, advance lateral scrotal tissue medially and close the donor site primarily in layers with absorbable sutures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### 9. Dressing and discharge

- Light compressive dressing
- All 15 patients in the original series discharged same-day

## Position in the Tausch / Pariser Algorithm

| Pariser tier | Anatomy | Procedure | VSSF role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | Viable penile skin, local flap sufficient | Unburying + local flap | VSSF is one option when release leaves a ventral-only defect and a healthy, mobile scrotal donor.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| II | Skin graft used without a higher-tier component | Penile graft coverage (STSG or selected FTSG according to defect and donor anatomy)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | VSSF alone does not cover a circumferential defect<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| III | Scrotal surgery needed | Scrotoplasty ± STSG | n/a |
| IV | Contributory escutcheon | Escutcheonectomy; penile coverage follows shaft-skin viability | VSSF alone does not address the escutcheon |
| V | Contributory abdominal pannus | Panniculectomy; add escutcheon and penile-skin procedures according to anatomy | VSSF alone does not address the pannus |

Tausch describes a tailored buried-penis approach. The table is an orientation aid; ventral release and local flap or graft choice should follow actual skin viability, scrotal donor condition and abdominal contribution, without an automatic sequence.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## PAS-Driven Selection

PAS descriptors can document residual penile skin, abdominal contribution and scrotal donor suitability, but VSSF outcomes have not been validated by these exact PAS combinations. Use them as anatomical prompts, not a tested eligibility rule.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| PAS axis | Supports VSSF assessment | Requires separate anatomical planning |
|---|---|---|
| **P** | P1a: sufficient shaft skin without contributory escutcheon | P2a/P2b: sufficient skin but assess contributory escutcheon; P1b/P2c: insufficient skin requires coverage planning; P2x: reassess skin after release |
| **A** | A0/A1: no contributory pannus | A2: assess contributory pannus separately; A3: all 7 patients in the validation cohort underwent panniculectomy |
| **S** | S0: normal scrotal donor | S1: assess sulcus and donor mobility; S2/S3: plan around scrotal lymphedema |

## Outcomes — Westerman / Tausch (n = 15)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Parameter | Result |
|---|---|
| Mean age | 51 y (26–75) |
| Mean BMI | 42.6 kg/m² (29.8–53.9) |
| LS etiology | 13 / 15 (87%) |
| Phimosis (may overlap LS) | 11 / 15 (73%) |
| Setting | Outpatient — 100% same-day discharge |
| Mean OR time | 83 min (35–145) |
| Mean EBL | 57 cc (25–200) |
| **Perioperative complications** | 0 / 15 |
| Satisfied without further intervention | 11 / 15 (73.3%) at mean 12 months (range 3–29); qualitative chart-review assessment |
| Recurrence | 3 / 15 (20%) |
| Treatment after recurrence | 2 / 3 underwent abdominoplasty with STSG; both reported satisfaction at 6 and 23 months after revision |

### Recurrence analysis

| Reported recurrence factor | Interpretation and limitation |
|---|---|
| **LS progression** | Westerman considered progressive LS scarring a possible contributor to recurrence, but the 15-patient series cannot separate disease progression from selection or repair effects; outcomes of other genital or urethral LS operations do not establish a VSSF-specific mechanism.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **Panniculus migration** | Reassess contributory suprapubic tissue and shaft-skin coverage; lipectomy or abdominoplasty, with grafting when needed, may be appropriate in selected patients. Escutcheonectomy is a category IV component in Pariser’s classification, not a requirement for every recurrence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Edematous groin tissue** | Recurrent concealment by edematous groin tissue was reported; developing lymphedema and selection were considered possible contributors.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |

The three recurrences among 15 should be read in their own follow-up context. Plamadeala's larger AABP cohort used a different case mix and procedure mix.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Complexity tier | Not assigned in Westerman series (local scrotal flap)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | At least IV by reported escutcheonectomy; Pariser tier not assigned<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | At least IV by reported escutcheonectomy; Pariser tier not assigned<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | I–V (70.6% III or higher)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

| Skin graft | None | STSG | FTSG (escutcheon) | Variable |

| Reported complications (different observation windows) | 0% perioperative<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 50% within 90 days<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | 56% postoperative (median follow-up 8 months)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | Within 3 months: 13.3% (Pariser I–II); 32.6% (III or higher); 27.0% overall<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Wound dehiscence | n/r | 31%<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | n/r | n/r |
| Wound infection | n/r | 30%<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | 41%<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | n/r |
| Recurrence / revision / surgical failure (different endpoints) | 20% recurrence<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 3.9% revision for poor outcome<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | 13% surgical failure requiring reoperation (1 / 32 recurred)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | 12.7% recurrence (whole cohort)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

These are different severity groups and outcome definitions, not a head-to-head effectiveness comparison. In particular, VSSF recurrence, STSG revision and FTSG surgical failure are not interchangeable endpoints.

## Advantages

1. All 15 patients in this series were discharged the same day; other AABP techniques were not compared.
2. No skin graft is used, which eliminates thigh donor or escutcheon-graft harvest.
3. No perioperative complications were reported among the 15 patients.
4. Mean operative time was 83 minutes and mean blood loss 57 cc.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
5. Usable native skin is preserved when feasible. Postoperative sensation was not quantified in the abstract.
6. Scrotal skin can be a pliable local option, although hair distribution and tissue match vary.
7. The operation uses basic local-flap principles and needs no microsurgical expertise.
8. The series had a mean BMI of 42.6 kg/m², and the technique has been described in obese patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
9. The outpatient setting fits an ambulatory reconstructive practice.

---

## Limitations and Disadvantages

1. The flap covers ventral-only deficits and does not cover circumferential defects.
2. Three of 15 patients had recurrence. This cannot be ranked against a separate cohort's revision rate.
3. Extensive nonviable shaft skin, a diseased scrotum or a major abdominal component may need a different or combined operation. No PAS cutpoint alone proves failure.
4. LS may recur after repair; one VSSF recurrence was attributed to LS. Recurrence risk varies with disease extent and operation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
5. The series is small (n = 15), compared with Plamadeala n = 204 and Tausch n = 56.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup><sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
6. The original series used no validated PROMs. Later AABP series use IIEF-15 and IPSS.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
7. Follow-up was limited to a mean of 12 months (range 3–29), without standardized long-term patient-reported measures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
8. Penoscrotal webbing or recurrent concealment is possible. The original operation includes angle fixation, but its independent contribution to durability was not measured.
9. Hair distribution in the anterior scrotal donor skin varies between patients and should be inspected individually.

## Place in the Broader Tausch Series (n = 56)

The VSSF corresponds to the viable-skin branch of the Tausch algorithm:<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- Ventral release and exposure are described in the tailored approach
- Usable skin may permit local-flap closure; VSSF is one option
- Nonviable: complete excision and STSG
- Severe abdominal lipodystrophy: adjacent-tissue transfer
- Genital lymphedema: excision of lymphedematous tissue and primary closure ± STSG
- Overall success, defined as resolution of symptoms without additional procedures, was 88% (49 / 56)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

Pariser's 64-patient complexity comparison provides risk context across different operations, not validation that VSSF itself has a zero major-complication or failure rate.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Patient Selection — When VSSF Wins

| Choose VSSF | Consider escalation |
|---|---|
| Trapped penis from LS or phimosis with viable dorsal skin | Circumferential nonviable penile skin → STSG / FTSG<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Ventral-only deficiency after ring release | Contributory suprapubic tissue → assess the pannus and escutcheon separately; graft only if shaft-skin coverage requires it |
| Healthy scrotal donor with adequate laxity | Contributory pannus → assess abdominal correction; add escutcheonectomy or grafting only for separate escutcheon or penile-skin findings |
| Limited abdominal contribution and viable donor skin | Genital lymphedema → individualized scrotal excision/coverage plan |
| Outpatient preference, low-morbidity priority | Circumferential paraffinoma defect → Fakin / Murányi / Yao / Pribaz staged<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| Preference to avoid graft harvest when flap anatomy is suitable | |

## Key Takeaways

- A 15-patient outpatient series reported no perioperative complications and 11/15 satisfied without further intervention.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Ventral release and local scrotal rotation flaps are useful when residual skin and donor tissue are suitable. Pariser and PAS labels are not a validated VSSF-only rule.
- Three of 15 patients had recurrent concealment attributed to different possible contributors.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- The original technique sutures basal tunica albuginea to the deep dermis of dorsolateral skin to restore the penopubic angle, then reconstructs the penoscrotal angle with permanent sutures. The series did not isolate the effect of fixation on recurrence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- LS progression is a disease-specific recurrence risk distinct from the technique itself.
