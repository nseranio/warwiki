# Skin Grafting of the Scrotum

**Split-thickness skin grafting (STSG)** provides thin, broad coverage for extensive superficial scrotal skin loss when healthy tissue cannot be closed comfortably. The choice rests on the viable recipient bed and the defect, not on a fixed 50% threshold. Total skin loss and exposed testes do not automatically call for a flap.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

See the [scrotal reconstruction overview](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/scrotal-reconstruction), [STSG principles](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/stsg) and [FTSG principles](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/grafts/ftsg) for the broader reconstruction and donor-site decisions.

## When grafting is suitable

| Setting | Notes |
|---|---|
| Fournier's gangrene or necrotizing infection | Most common indication. The wound needs source control, adequate perfusion and a plan to prevent collections and shear.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Genital lymphedema | Coverage follows excision. Morey's nine patients had satisfactory outcomes without observed recurrence.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Adult acquired buried penis | Escutcheonectomy, scrotoplasty and penile resurfacing. Tang used STSG fixed with dilute fibrin glue in 5 men.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Hidradenitis, tumor excision, removal of injected foreign material | Coverage after excision when residual skin cannot close the defect.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Trauma and penoscrotal avulsion | Definitive coverage when closure is not possible.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

In the UCSF series (Alwaal, n = 54), the indications were tissue loss such as Fournier's gangrene (13), lymphedema (13), buried penis (12), foreign-body injection (8), hidradenitis (4) and tumor (4).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

In cancer, margins and reconstructive timing follow the oncological plan. A deep dead space or a recipient surface that cannot support a graft may instead need vascularized flap coverage.

## Recipient bed and testicular position

| Finding | Implication |
|---|---|
| Healthy preserved dartos or other vascularized soft tissue | Preserve useful tissue. Dartos is not intrinsically a poor graft bed. |
| Viable spermatic coverings or appropriately prepared granulation | May support grafting after assessment of the actual vascularity and stability. |
| Exposed testes | Evaluate the covering layers and recipient surface. Exposure alone does not require a flap. |
| Absent tunica vaginalis | Some authors hold that grafting cannot be performed on testes stripped of tunica vaginalis, but this is not universally accepted. The actual bed, depth and testicular protection are assessed.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Necrotic, inadequately debrided or poorly perfused surface | Address the cause or choose another reconstruction before grafting. |

The tunica vaginalis is a testicular covering and is distinct from the tissue around the spermatic cord. Viable layers are not stripped to reach a named surface, and a graft is not placed on a poorly vascularized surface because a protocol names it.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

**Orchidopexy and testicular apposition.** Hayon performed orchidopexy after the initial debridement in 10 patients. The testes and spermatic cords were mobilized, the testes and inferior penis were brought together with interrupted Vicryl, and redundant cord was coiled under the abdominal wall. Tan preserved viable spermatic fascia and sutured the testes together with a broad contact surface so that their fascial layers fused into one scrotal sac before wrap-around grafting. In 27 patients, 66% had thin, pliable scrotums that closely resembled the original and 18% had well-formed sacs with slight adhesions.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> These are selected operative approaches. Unnecessary cord dissection, coiling, compression or forced superior repositioning adds risk.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Graft choice and donor site

STSG gives broad, thin coverage. Thickness and meshing depend on the recipient bed, the contour wanted, drainage and available donor skin. Hayon used a graft 0.018 inch (about 0.46 mm) thick, meshed 2:1, with a bolster sutured for 5 to 7 days. These are one series' parameters.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

FTSG is used selectively. Most of the comparative literature concerns penile resurfacing in adult acquired buried penis and not scrotal coverage. Gül's retrospective cohort included 22 grafted patients (9 FTSG, 13 STSG) and found no significant difference in complications or recurrence, with a small sample.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Monn used FTSG from resected escutcheon in 13 patients with buried penis, and all grafts took.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

| Donor site | Notes |
|---|---|
| Thigh | The usual STSG donor, with a large yield.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Buttock, upper arm, back | Alternatives, chosen by available skin, hair, thickness, prior scars and preference. |
| Scalp | Ye grafted a Fournier wound from the scalp in one patient.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Escutcheon (suprapubic fat pad) | Full-thickness donor in buried-penis repair when the tissue is resected anyway. It requires healthy skin.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |

## Operative sequence

1. **Establish readiness.** Debride nonviable tissue, control the source of infection and assess the patient and recipient bed. Hayon grafted once patients were medically stable with no further debridement planned.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
2. **Define the coverage and position.** Preserve useful coverings, protect the cords and testes, and plan any apposition or fixation without tension.
3. **Harvest and prepare the graft.** Choose thickness, dimensions and meshing for the defect, avoid excess tension and keep orientation.
4. **Apply to a viable bed.** Obtain close contact, secure the edges, control bleeding and prevent fluid pockets and shear.
5. **Protect the reconstruction.** Use a bolster or NPWT and a tailored activity plan. Dressing pressure must not compromise perfusion or compress exposed structures.
6. **Reassess.** Review graft adherence, infection, collections and perfusion, and adjust support, mobilization and wound care to healing.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

Dilute fibrin sealant secured the graft-bed interface in Tang's small buried-penis series. It is an adjunct and does not replace a viable bed.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## NPWT for wound preparation and graft fixation

Preparation before grafting and fixation after grafting are separate questions. Fournier case reports describe both. Ye applied NPWT for 10 days before grafting and 7 days over the graft in one patient, and 98% of the graft was alive at discharge. Zhao reported NPWT with STSG in one man with penoscrotal defects after trauma.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Urgent debridement and antibiotics remain the core acute treatment. The EAU Fournier section gives a weak recommendation to restrict adjuncts to trials, based on a search that ended in July 2017.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

For graft fixation across body sites, randomized evidence is available:

| Evidence | Finding |
|---|---|
| Cao randomized trial, 86 patients | Overall take was 97.2% with NPWT versus 90.2% with a tie-over bolster (p = 0.005). In irregular, high-mobility areas take was 97.6% versus 81.7%. Graft displacement occurred in 1 versus 9 patients.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| Lee 2025 meta-analysis, 16 RCTs | 411 NPWT patients and 401 controls. NPWT raised overall graft take by 8.3% (95% CI 2.97–13.63; I² = 85%) and by 10.0% at −80 mmHg. Graft loss (OR 0.44) and reoperation (OR 0.31) were lower.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Jiang 2021 meta-analysis, 10 RCTs | Take improved at −80 mmHg and did not improve at −125 mmHg. Data on adverse events and optimal pressure were limited.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |

None of these trials was scrotal-specific. Device settings and protective layers follow the wound, perfusion, device instructions and the surgical plan.

## Two-stage contour revision

Konofaos described six patients with total scrotal skin loss and exposed testes. Wrap-around grafting was followed by reapproximation of the grafted testes and shaping of the neoscrotum. Primary closure was achieved in all, and cosmetic and functional outcomes were satisfactory at mean 18 months, with adequate volume and no testicular compression. Its drawback is that it requires two stages.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

## Outcomes and complications

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Graft take above 90% | 52 of 54 (Alwaal, mixed genital indications).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Erection and voiding | Maintained or improved erection and normal voiding in those 52 patients.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Scrotal STSG, orchidopexy and graft | 10 patients, no chronic pain, discomfort or further intervention at median 8 months. Median length of stay after orchidopexy and after grafting was 18 and 9 days.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| STSG in buried-penis repair | 80% to 100% graft take at 2 months (Tang, n = 5).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| FTSG in buried-penis repair | All grafts took (Monn, n = 13).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Patient satisfaction in buried-penis grafting | 81.3% (Gül, ad-hoc questionnaire).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |

Penile complication data are not scrotal-graft risk. Jeng's 32-patient escutcheon-FTSG cohort for buried penis with lichen sclerosus (median BMI 44, 69% diabetic) had an overall complication rate of 56% and a wound infection rate of 41%. Four patients (13%) needed reoperation for graft loss, recurrence or lymphedema. All patients with partial or complete graft loss had diabetes. Median follow-up was 8 months.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

Counseling covers partial or complete loss, infection, collections, scarring or contraction, altered sensation, pain and appearance. Donor sites can also be painful or scarred. The 2023 Cochrane review of hydrogel donor-site dressings found insufficient evidence on their effect compared with other dressings.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

**Fertility.** In a rat study, grafting produced worse testicular histology than flaps or controls. Human evidence is not available. Karian's review notes limited objective data on spermatogenesis after scrotal reconstruction. Reproductive goals are discussed, and persistent pain, testicular abnormalities or fertility concerns are evaluated.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
