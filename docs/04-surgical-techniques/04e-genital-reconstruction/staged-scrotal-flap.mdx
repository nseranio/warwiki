# Staged Bipedicled Scrotal Flap (Pribaz / McLaughlin Technique)

A staged bipedicled scrotal flap is one reported pathway for resurfacing an acquired penile skin deficit. In the 2024 Pribaz/McLaughlin series, eight patients with varied acquired defects underwent staged bipedicle scrotal-flap coverage; the abstract reports satisfactory postoperative outcomes in all eight and minor complications in two.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> This small uncontrolled series cannot establish a preferred method, an expected result, or selection criteria for an individual patient.

For alternative coverage approaches and overall planning, see [Penile Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile), [Bipedicled Anterior Scrotal Flap (Fakin)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/bipedicled-anterior-scrotal-flap), [Modified Bipedicle Scrotal Tunnel Flap (Murányi)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/muranyi-scrotal-tunnel-flap), and [Penile Skin Grafting](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile-skin-grafting).

## Evidence context

The accessible abstract describes staged coverage, but the full 2024 source was not recovered in this audit. A separate retrospective comparison in patients treated for penile foreign-body granuloma included 23 single-stage and 12 two-stage scrotal-flap reconstructions; its abstract reported lower postoperative fever and fewer Clavien–Dindo complications in the two-stage group, alongside a longer hospital stay.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> That observational, condition-specific comparison should not be generalized to other causes of penile skin loss.

## Interpretation limits

- The published experience is small and heterogeneous; diagnoses, wound conditions, operative details, and outcome definitions may differ between reports.
- The accessible records do not establish a standard staging interval, surgical protocol, contamination threshold, donor-site risk, sexual/voiding outcome, or long-term durability.
- Neither source establishes superiority over grafting or other local/regional reconstruction in a broader population.
- Reconstruction requires individualized assessment of the defect, infection or tissue status, urethral/corporal involvement, available tissue, patient goals, and surgeon expertise.
