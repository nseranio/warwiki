# Staged Bipedicled Scrotal Flap (Pribaz / McLaughlin Technique)

The **staged bipedicled scrotal flap** is a two-stage penile resurfacing option: the shaft is temporarily buried in scrotal skin, then unburied after a clinically selected interval. McLaughlin, Abbassi and Pribaz reported eight selected patients, all with satisfactory outcomes and two with minor complications; the abstract does not report a zero-loss rate, an exact interval or superiority for infected wounds.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Zucchi's related trapped-penis technique used a 6–12-week interval in ten different patients.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

For single-stage variants, see [Bipedicled Anterior Scrotal Flap (Fakin)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/bipedicled-anterior-scrotal-flap), [Modified Bipedicle Scrotal Tunnel Flap (Murányi)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/muranyi-scrotal-tunnel-flap), and [Modified Bilateral Butterfly Scrotal Flap (Yao)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/yao-butterfly-scrotal-flap). For graft alternatives, see [Penile Skin Grafting](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile-skin-grafting). For the full decision framework, see [Penile Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile).

---

## Rationale

The staged approach uses a period of tissue adherence and vascular ingrowth before separation. Experimental flap-division thresholds and time courses are not validated timing rules for penile scrotal flaps; the interval must follow the described variant, wound healing and perfusion assessment.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

Reasons to choose staged over single-stage scrotal flap reconstruction:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- **After infection control and adequate debridement.** A staged plan may help when tissue viability or coverage is uncertain. A shaft is not buried in an actively infected or necrotic wound as a blanket rule
- **Uncertain wound-bed viability.** Residual foreign material, prior radiation, prior failed reconstruction
- **Observed complication-free proportions.** Lumbiganon's retrospective foreign-body-granuloma comparison were 83.3% for two-stage versus 43.5% for single-stage; selection bias and small groups preclude a universal superiority rule<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Patient-specific staging choice.** Delayed division allows healing and vascular ingrowth, but does not guarantee viability

---

## Indications

- **Circumferential shaft defects** where single-stage is too risky
- **Penile foreign-body granuloma** (paraffinoma / siliconoma) with contaminated bed or residual material on corpora
- **Fournier's gangrene.** After radical debridement before the wound is clean enough for single-stage coverage
- **Penile cancer.** Only after appropriate oncologic margin assessment and donor-tissue planning; staging must not substitute for margin control
- **Circumcision complications.** After infection or necrosis has been treated and a suitable wound bed established
- **Iatrogenic trapped penis.** Zucchi variant<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Trauma.** Bite / avulsion injuries with contaminated wounds

## Stage 1 — Penile Burial ("Scrotalization")

The Zucchi sutures and 6–12-week interval come from its abstract.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> The McLaughlin/Pribaz abstract does not give the pocket plane, catheter regimen or interval.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The steps below are general scrotal-flap technique to be adapted to the patient.

### 1. Circumferential incisions and excision

- Subcoronal circumferential incision and a second at the penoscrotal junction (or proximal margin of diseased tissue)
- Excise all involved skin / scar / granuloma to Buck's fascia; fully denude the shaft
- If contaminated: thorough irrigation and debridement

### 2. Scrotal pocket / tunnel

- Horizontal or longitudinal anterior-scrotal incision
- Develop a subcutaneous pocket between scrotal skin / dartos and external spermatic fascia / tunica vaginalis
- Pocket sized to accommodate the entire shaft without compression
- **Zucchi variant.** Intrascrotal tunnel through the full thickness of the scrotum with glans emerging from the opposite side<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### 3. Penile transposition

- Transpose the denuded shaft into the pocket / tunnel
- **Bring the glans externally.** Needed for urethral access and meatal patency

### 4. Fixation sutures

- 3–4 nonresorbable 0 / 2-0 quilting sutures crossing through the entire scrotum, ventral to the penis (Zucchi)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Eliminate dead space; maximize scrotal-skin / shaft contact for neovascularization

### 5. Closure and dressing

- Scrotal skin closed over the buried shaft with interrupted absorbable suture
- Foley catheter; light compressive dressing
- Counsel that the interval depends on the variant and on healing. Zucchi used 6–12 weeks; the McLaughlin/Pribaz abstract does not state an interval

Stage 1 mean operating time was 75 ± 15 min (Zucchi)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

---

## Interval Period (3–12 weeks)

- Neovascularization from corpora cavernosa / Buck's fascia into the overlying scrotal skin
- Scrotal skin becomes adherent to the shaft via fibrovascular ingrowth
- Voiding through the exposed glans
- Intercourse is not possible during this period
- Monitor for infection, hematoma, skin necrosis
- Optimal Stage-2 timing by clinical assessment of skin adherence and wound healing
- Experimental perfusion fluorometry is background research, not a validated 25% threshold or 11-day division rule for staged penile scrotal flaps<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Stage 2 — Penile Unburial ("Descrotalization")

### 1. Incision planning

- Palpate the buried shaft contour
- Mark circumferential incisions around the shaft at the penoscrotal junction (proximal) and the coronal sulcus (distal)
- Preserve enough cuff of adherent scrotal skin on the shaft, and enough remaining scrotal skin for donor closure

### 2. Bilateral circumferential scrotal-skin incision

- Divide the scrotal skin around the penis into:
  - **Penile portion.** Adherent skin that becomes the new penile envelope
  - **Scrotal portion.** Remaining skin for scrotal reconstitution

### 3. Penile shaft liberation

- Elevate the shaft (now covered by adherent scrotal skin) out of the pocket
- The neovascularized skin remains attached to the shaft via its new blood supply from corpora / Buck's
- The scrotal attachments are divided only after the adherent tissue is judged adequately perfused; the degree and timing of recipient-bed vascular ingrowth are not quantified in the Pribaz abstract

### 4. Flap inset

- Distal circumferential inset to the subcoronal margin / glans
- Proximal inset to the penile base / pubic skin
- Trim excess scrotal skin to a smooth, non-redundant envelope
- Dorsal and ventral closure with interrupted absorbable suture

### 5. Scrotal donor-site closure

- Advance remaining scrotal skin medially; close primarily over the testes

### 6. Dressing and postoperative care

- Light compressive dressing
- Short-term Foley if needed
- Intercourse typically resumed 4–8 weeks after Stage 2<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

Stage 2 mean operating time was 45 ± 10 min (Zucchi)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Outcomes

### Pribaz / McLaughlin (n = 8)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- Etiologies: infection, scar, urologic procedure complications
- All eight were reported to have satisfactory outcomes; the abstract does not separately enumerate flap-loss events
- Minor complications 2 / 8 (25%); the abstract does not report major complications
- Level of evidence: therapeutic V

### Zucchi (n = 10, iatrogenic trapped penis)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- Stage 1 OR 75 ± 15 min; Stage 2 OR 45 ± 10 min
- Major complications 0; minor 1 / 10 (superficial scrotal hematoma)
- Median follow-up 20 mo (range 6–72)
- Median VAS 97 / 100 (range 85–100) in this ten-person cohort; it cannot be ranked against unlike series
- 100% recovered normal spontaneous erection
- 100% regular intercourse at 4–8 weeks after Stage 2

### Napolitano SR (152 paraffinoma cases, 1956–2022)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

This review describes favorable selected-case results but does not establish comparative superiority or an active-infection indication.

## Head-to-Head: Two-Stage vs Single-Stage (Lumbiganon 2023, n = 35)

| Outcome | Single-stage (n = 23) | Two-stage (n = 12) | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wound infection | 8.7% | 0% | NS |
| Wound dehiscence | 21.7% | 8.3% | NS |
| Reoperation rate | 26.1% | 8.3% | NS |
| Postoperative fever | 56.5% | 8.3% | RR 6.78 (1.01–43.83) |
| Complication-free rate | 43.5% | 83.3% | RR 0.53 (0.31–0.89) |
| Total hospital stay | 7.4 ± 3.2 d | 10.9 ± 1.6 d | MD −3.42 d |

In this retrospective 35-flap-patient comparison within 42 treated patients, the two-stage group had a higher complication-free proportion and less postoperative fever but longer total hospital stay. This does not prove that every complication subtype or every patient population benefits from staging; wound infection, dehiscence and reoperation differences were not statistically significant.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Pribaz / McLaughlin vs Zucchi (related two-stage techniques)

| Feature | Pribaz / McLaughlin | Zucchi |
|---|---|---|
| Primary indication | Acquired defects (infection, scar, urologic) | Iatrogenic trapped penis (post-circumcision, post-cancer) |
| Interval | Not stated in the abstract | 6–12 wk |
| Fixation sutures | Not specifically described | 3–4 nonresorbable quilting sutures through entire scrotum |
| Pocket vs tunnel | Pocket (anterior scrotum) | Tunnel through full scrotal thickness |
| Glans exposure | Protrudes | Protrudes opposite side |
| Series size | 8 | 10 |
| Follow-up | Not specified | Median 20 mo |
| Satisfaction | Eight reported satisfactory, measure unspecified in abstract | Median VAS 97 / 100 |

The Zucchi variant reports a 6–12-week interval and three or four nonresorbable trans-scrotal sutures for adherence; superiority of its vascularization over McLaughlin's design is untested.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Advantages

1. **A two-stage option** for selected defects when delayed division is desirable; Lumbiganon's small retrospective comparison favors staging on some outcomes, not all<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
2. **Time for healing and adherence** after infection is controlled and the wound bed prepared; active infection is not a routine burial indication
3. **Different vascular support after division** is the intended mechanism, but tissue viability still requires assessment
4. **Zucchi operative times** averaged approximately 75 min and approximately 45 min for its two stages, not all staged techniques<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
5. **Patient-reported recovery** was favorable in small selected series; no head-to-head proof of less pain or tethering than Murányi<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
6. Zucchi median satisfaction VAS was 97/100 in ten patients, not a cross-technique ranking<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
7. **Erection and intercourse recovery reported in Zucchi's ten**; the McLaughlin abstract does not quantify erectile preservation
8. **No major complications reported** in these selected small series; absence of a reported event is not proof of zero necrosis risk

---

## Limitations and Disadvantages

1. **Two operations.** Cumulative anesthesia / surgical risk
2. **Longer total hospital stay** (10.9 ± 1.6 d versus 7.4 ± 3.2 d single-stage)<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
3. **Temporary buried penis 3–12 weeks.** Psychological burden, voiding issues, no intercourse
4. **Delayed return to sexual function.** 7–20 weeks from initial surgery to intercourse
5. **Small series evidence** (n = 8 + n = 10) versus single-stage Fakin (n = 43) and Murányi (n = 49)
6. **Possible scrotal-flap morbidity.** Testicular ascension and retraction warrant surveillance; Mendel's rates come from another bilateral-flap cohort, not a class-wide staged-flap estimate<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
7. **Hair-bearing variability.** Donor skin must be inspected individually
8. **Nocturnal erections** during burial may theoretically disrupt neovascularization; not reported as a clinical problem

## Marín-Martínez Paraffinoma Algorithm

Two-center experience (Spain / Israel, n = 8):<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

- Limited skin involvement and viable shaft skin lead to single-stage excision with primary closure or a local flap
- Extensive skin involvement (liquid paraffin) leads to two-stage surgery or skin graft
- All liquid-paraffin patients had extensive involvement requiring two-stage or grafting
- 100% erectile-function preservation
- Authors emphasize a combined urology / plastic-surgery team for optimal outcomes

## Emerging Adjuncts

### ICG-directed scrotal flap design (Klein 2026)

A multicenter observational study of 15 patients with adult-acquired buried penis used ICG lymphography to map lymphatic drainage. Drainage of the posterior scrotum was completely or partially preserved in all patients, and the posterior scrotal flap boundaries excluded tissue with lymphatic congestion. The study did not measure postoperative lymphedema, and its application to staged designs has not been tested.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

### Perfusion fluorometry for Stage-2 timing

Animal and cross-leg flap fluorometry data are hypothesis-generating only here; no penile-scrotal-flap study validates the ≥25% threshold or earlier division as safe.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Patient Selection — When Staged Wins

| Choose Pribaz / McLaughlin staged | Consider single-stage instead |
|---|---|
| Infection controlled and debridement complete, but staged coverage judged preferable after individual assessment | A suitable wound bed and viable donor tissue support either single- or two-stage options |
| Uncertain wound-bed viability (RT, residual material, prior failed reconstruction) | Patient strongly prefers a single operation |
| High-comorbidity patient (diabetes, smoking, immunosuppression) — flap survival concern | Insufficient scrotal skin → STSG / FTSG or regional flap |
| Iatrogenic trapped penis (Zucchi variant) | |
| Patient accepts two operations after discussion of uncertain comparative benefit | |

---

## Key Takeaways

- The two-stage burial-then-unburial sequence is an option in selected patients; one small retrospective foreign-body-granuloma comparison found 83.3% versus 43.5% complication-free, without proving class-wide superiority
- Zucchi used 6–12 weeks; the McLaughlin/Pribaz abstract states no interval. Healing and perfusion guide division.
- Infection is controlled and nonviable tissue debrided before reconstruction; no cited study establishes burying into an actively infected bed as safe
- Zucchi describes three or four nonresorbable trans-scrotal sutures for adherence; stronger neovascularization than other designs is unproven
- Tradeoff: two operations, 7–20-week delay before intercourse
