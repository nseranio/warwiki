# Genito-Crural Island Perforator Flap (GCIPF) for Vulvar Reconstruction

The **genito-crural island perforator flap (GCIPF)** is a fasciocutaneous island flap harvested near the genitocrural (labiocrural / genitofemoral) sulcus. The Toulouse group's GIP design is supplied by branches of the internal pudendal vessels, although the usable perforator must be confirmed for each patient. O'Dey's aOAP flap is a related sulcus design with a different described pedicle.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> In the Toulouse group's single-center, retrospective 27-patient/46-flap series, no total flap necrosis was reported, median hospitalization was 4 days, and 78.3% were reported to achieve complete vulvar healing in less than four weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> These are selected-cohort observations, not a comparison establishing first-line superiority.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For the broader treatment menu see the [Vulvar Reconstruction Atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar). For related parent / sibling flaps see the foundations [Lotus Petal](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/lotus-petal), [Singapore / pudendal-thigh](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/singapore-pudendal-thigh), [IGAP / Gluteal-Fold](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/igap-gluteal-fold), [IPAP](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/ipap), and [SCIP](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/scip) pages.

## Anatomic Basis and Nomenclature

The genitocrural sulcus (sulcus genitofemoralis / labiocrural fold) is the natural crease between the vulva and the medial thigh. It is a donor site with thin, pliable tissue immediately adjacent to the vulva.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

The GCIPF can be considered alongside several historically described flaps from nearby anatomic territories, without treating their pedicles or outcomes as interchangeable:

| Related flap | First described | Relationship to GCIPF |
|---|---|---|
| Pudendal-thigh (Singapore) | Wee & Joseph 1989 | Original axial-pattern flap from the genitocrural region<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| Lotus petal | Yii & Niranjan 1996 | Genitocrural sulcus = "upper petal" of the lotus design<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Anterior obturator artery perforator (aOAP)** | O'Dey 2010 | Related sulcus vascular territory<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| IPAP flap | Hashimoto 2014 | Distinct internal-pudendal perforator source in the broader perineal territory<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Perineal perforator switch flap (PPSF) | Shin 2022 | Tunneled island perforator flap around perforators close to the genitofemoral sulcus<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |

The Toulouse name describes the flap location and island design. O'Dey's aOAP, Hashimoto's IPAP and Shin's perineal switch flap are useful adjacent concepts, but their distinct pedicle anatomy and outcomes must not be merged with Commenge's GCIPF cohort without the original operative maps.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Vascular Anatomy

### Related aOAP anatomy — not a fixed GCIPF pedicle (O'Dey 2010 cadaveric study, 10 bodies / 20 specimens)<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- The sulcus genitofemoralis is supplied by a perforator from the anterior branch of the obturator artery with its accompanying vein.
- The aOAP was identified in 20/20 specimens in this cadaveric sample; this does not guarantee the vessel or usable flap territory in every patient.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Two perforator types:
  - **Musculocutaneous (80%, 16/20).** Pierces the gracilis muscle.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
  - **Septocutaneous (20%, 4/20).** Passes the posterior border of the gracilis.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Perforator location: 1.3 ± 0.3 cm lateral to the inferior pubic ramus.
- aOAP skin territory: approximately 7 × 15 cm centered on the sulcus genitofemoralis.

### Contributing supply (Höckel 2008)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

| Source | Territory |
|---|---|
| External pudendal aa. (superficial / deep) | Anterior / upper perineal |
| Internal pudendal a. perforators | Medial / posterior |
| Obturator a. perforators | Lateral (dominant per O'Dey) |
| Medial circumflex femoral a. | Medial thigh |

These sources can contribute to regional vascularity, but a specific flap survives on its preserved pedicle; an adjacent vascular network does not remove the need to verify that pedicle after prior surgery or radiation.

### Sensory innervation

- **Perineal branches of the pudendal nerve.** Primary supply to the medial portion.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Ilioinguinal and genitofemoral nerves.** Contribute to the anterior / upper territory.
- Some island-flap designs can preserve cutaneous innervation, but sensibility after GCIPF specifically was not quantified.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Indications

| Indication | Detail |
|---|---|
| Hemivulvectomy | Unilateral GCIPF<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Vulvectomy (extent not reported for bilateral GCIPF) | Bilateral GCIPF was used in 19/27 patients in the Commenge series; the abstract does not assign those 19 to radical or total vulvectomy. The Toulouse algorithm lists bilateral gluteal-fold IPAP as its first choice after total vulvectomy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Vulvar squamous cell carcinoma | Primary indication |
| Extramammary Paget disease | Assess defect after oncologic resection |
| Vulvar melanoma | Assess margins and available tissue; no disease-specific GCIPF outcome established here |
| Vulvar dysplasia / VIN | Included in the publisher's indexed study methods, but subgroup outcomes need the full article |

The authors call it a routine option within their tertiary center. For oncologic safety, select genitocrural donor tissue outside the neoplastic tissue compartment.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Their indexed results describe 27/128 vulvectomies (21.1%) using at least one GCIPF, with primary closure in 61.7% and another flap used in 22 patients; this is not first-line use for all vulvar defects.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgical Technique — general island-perforator principles<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

The sequence below gives general island-perforator planning principles. Commenge's abstract does not describe the operative steps, so the sequence is not a fixed GCIPF protocol.

1. **Oncologic plan and position.** Jointly plan excision and reconstruction; consider frozen section if margins are hard to assess, and consider delaying flap reconstruction until pathology is available if clearance remains uncertain. A positive deep margin can require removing a thin flap.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> Position in lithotomy.
2. **Design.** Island skin paddle along the genitocrural sulcus / labiocrural fold, centered over the perforator, sized to the defect.
3. **Perforator identification.** Map a usable patient-specific perforator. O'Dey's approximately 1.3-cm landmark describes the aOAP cadaveric sample, not a guaranteed GCIPF point.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
4. **Incision and elevation.** In the Toulouse GIP design, incise the peripheral skin to the underlying aponeurosis anteriorly for mobility; then raise the flap as an island on its perforator pedicle, preserving soft tissue around that pedicle.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
5. **Transfer.** The Toulouse GIP design passes through a subcutaneous tunnel in the central posterior labia majora to reach a medial defect; check reach, perfusion and freedom from pedicle compression or torsion.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> O'Dey's tunneled aOAP is a separate variant and its aesthetic claim is not a GCIPF comparative result.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
6. **Inset.** Sutured with attention to 3D vulvar-contour restoration.
7. **Donor closure.** Primary, with the scar concealed in the natural labiocrural fold.

Technical points:

- Raise unilaterally or bilaterally depending on defect size.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Provides thin, pliable tissue closely matching vulvar skin, unlike bulkier musculocutaneous flaps.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- A tunneled aOAP-type island may conceal the scar in selected anatomy when pedicle perfusion and inset are maintained; aesthetic outcomes remain uncertain.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Position in the Toulouse Algorithm (2025)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The Toulouse paper proposes a location-based author algorithm that often favors perforator options when their pedicle and tissue match the defect. The Toulouse group favors GIP for medial anterior or posterior defects when the lateral labia majora remain. It lists bilateral gluteal-fold IPAP first after total vulvectomy and combines GIP with DIEP for selected extended vulvovaginal defects only when the outer labia remain.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The Toulouse algorithm lacks direct comparisons with closure or musculocutaneous flaps.

- GCIPF, IPAP and DFAP are distinct options whose donor territory and defect reach differ.
- Musculocutaneous flaps are reserved for selected cases when perforator flaps are not feasible.
- Aim: restore contour and function while preserving oncologic treatment; comparative self-image, sexual-function and cancer outcomes for GCIPF remain unestablished.

## Comparison with Related Perforator Flaps

| Feature | GCIPF | IPAP | DFAP / PAP |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pedicle source | Toulouse GIP: internal pudendal branches; confirm the individual perforator<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | Internal pudendal a. (different paddle and transfer) | Profunda femoris a. |
| Donor site | Genitocrural sulcus (labiocrural fold) | Ischiorectal fossa / gluteal fold | Posteromedial thigh |
| Tissue thickness | Very thin and pliable | Thin (can be thinned) | Moderate (can be thinned) |
| Skin territory | Patient-specific; ~7 × 15 cm belongs to O'Dey's aOAP cadaveric description, not a tested GCIPF limit<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | Variable | Variable |
| Reported use | Selected medial anterior or posterior vulvar defects when lateral labia majora remain; the Toulouse algorithm uses other flaps when lateral tumor resection leaves no labia majora<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | Selected perineal/vulvar defects | Selected lateral/extended defects |
| Scar location | Labiocrural fold (concealed) | Gluteal fold (concealed) | Posteromedial thigh (visible) |
| Flap survival | No total loss among 46 GCIPFs in one center<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Depends on a different study population | Depends on a different study population |
| Wound complications in Han's **non-GCIPF** 47-patient cohort | Not studied | 12.9% for 31 IPAP patients | 37.5% for 16 PAP or TUG patients combined (p = 0.04)<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |

<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

Han et al. (2023) observed fewer wound complications with IPAP (12.9%, n = 31) than the combined PAP/TUG group (37.5%, n = 16; p = 0.04) in a retrospective 47-patient cohort.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Comparison with Other Vulvar Reconstruction Approaches

### Perforator vs non-perforator flaps (Wendelspiess meta-analysis 2024, n = 1,840)<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- Overall short-term surgical complication rate comparable between perforator (n = 276) and non-perforator (n = 1,564) flaps (p &gt; 0.05).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- The numerical trend toward fewer complications with perforator flaps was not statistically significant (p = 0.459), and between-study heterogeneity was high (I² = 91%).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- QOL assessment scarce across studies.

### Pedicled perforator flaps for vulvar reconstruction (Huang 2015, n = 16 / 27 flaps)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

- Multiple perforator flap types (DFAP, MCFAP, EPAP, IPAP, free-style).
- All 27 mixed perforator flaps survived in this 16-patient series; this is not a GCIPF result.
- All donor sites closed primarily; the abstract reported no donor-site morbidity during its observed follow-up.
- All patients satisfied with cosmetic / functional results (except 1 requiring debulking).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- The authors proposed thinner fasciocutaneous coverage with favorable outcomes; their series did not directly compare outcomes with musculocutaneous flaps.

### Perineal perforator switch flap (Shin 2022, n = 16 / 27 flaps)<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- Perforators identified close to the genitofemoral sulcus, an adjacent, not necessarily identical, GCIPF territory.
- Island flap centered on the perforator, tunneled subcutaneously without pedicle skeletonization.
- 100% flap survival; no major surgical complications.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Mean OR time 79.4 ± 19.7 min; hospitalization 5.69 ± 0.79 d.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- In a nonrandom comparison with the authors' earlier propeller-flap experience, esthetic scores favored PPSF for symmetry (p = 0.015) and labial shape (p = 0.031); not a GCIPF comparison.
- Did not delay adjuvant RT.

## Advantages

| Advantage | Detail |
|---|---|
| Ideal tissue match | Thin, pliable, immediately adjacent to the vulva — closely matches vulvar skin in color / texture / thickness<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Concealed donor scar | Hidden in the labiocrural fold; scars limited within the urogenital region<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| No total flap necrosis | 0% total flap loss in Commenge (46 flaps)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Rapid healing | 78.3% complete vulvar healing within 4 wk<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Short hospitalization | Median 4 d (range 1–15)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| No reported adjuvant-RT delay | Observed only in patients who received adjuvant RT; subgroup size not available in the abstract<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Practical nearby donor | The authors characterize their selected-center procedure as simple and reliable; this is their experience, not a comparative guarantee<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Related vascular map | aOAP found in 20/20 cadaveric specimens, ~1.3 cm lateral to inferior pubic ramus; GCIPF-specific pedicle reliability not thereby established<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| No muscle sacrifice | Fasciocutaneous / perforator design<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Potential sensory preservation | Depends on design and preserved nerve branches; no quantified GCIPF sensory outcome in the original abstract<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Versatile | Unilateral or bilateral; hemivulvectomy through total vulvectomy<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Primary donor closure | All donor sites closed primarily<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| May be compatible with selected groin operations | Map the planned dissection and donor pedicle; medial groin extension can compromise available vessels<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |

## Limitations

| Limitation | Detail |
|---|---|
| Limited dedicated evidence | Commenge's single-center 27-patient/46-flap series<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| 33.3% overall complication rate | No total flap necrosis, but wound dehiscence (17.4%) and local infection (21.7%) common<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Limited bulk | A thin island alone may not fill post-exenteration dead space; select additional vascularized bulk to match the defect. The cited ~7 × 15-cm territory is aOAP cadaveric anatomy, not a validated GCIPF size ceiling<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| Perforator variability | O'Dey's aOAP sample had 80% musculocutaneous and 20% septocutaneous course; individual GCIPF pedicle still requires mapping<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| No long-term QOL data | Commenge reports surgical outcomes only; no validated QOL or sexual-function assessment<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Potential conflict with medially extended LND | Vascular pedicle at risk if groin dissection extended medially<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| Lack of comparative studies | No head-to-head comparison with IPAP, DFAP, V-Y, or lotus-petal for the GCIPF specifically |

---

## Key Takeaways

1. GCIPF is a thin island-perforator option from the labiocrural fold; its actual pedicle and sensory preservation require individual confirmation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
2. O'Dey mapped aOAP location and an approximately 7 × 15-cm territory in 20 cadaveric specimens; GCIPF dimensions were not assessed.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
3. Commenge's 27-patient/46-flap selected series reported no total loss, 78.3% complete vulvar healing within four weeks and median four-day hospitalization; adjuvant RT was not delayed in treated patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
4. The Toulouse algorithm is an author-proposed location-based approach, not a guideline or direct superiority trial; in that center 27/128 vulvectomies received a GCIPF.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
5. A thin nearby donor and concealed labiocrural scar can be useful when the perforator, reach and defect fit; sensibility and appearance are not guaranteed.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
6. The flap cannot fill substantial pelvic dead space alone, and durable GCIPF-specific comparative, functional and oncologic outcomes remain unestablished.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
