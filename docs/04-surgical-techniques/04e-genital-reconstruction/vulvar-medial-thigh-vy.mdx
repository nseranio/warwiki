# Medial Thigh V-Y Advancement Flap for Vulvar Reconstruction

The **medial thigh V-Y advancement flap** is a fasciocutaneous island flap raised from the proximal medial thigh and advanced medially into a vulvar defect in V-Y fashion. It is one of the most widely used flaps in vulvar reconstruction — the **"workhorse"** for lateral and hemivulvectomy defects given its reliability, technical simplicity, and low complication rate.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For the broader treatment menu see the [Vulvar Reconstruction Atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar). For underlying V-Y geometry see the foundations [V-Y Advancement Flap](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/v-y-advancement) page; for adjacent alternatives see [Limberg / Rhomboid](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar-limberg-flap), [Lotus Petal](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/lotus-petal), and [Singapore (pudendal-thigh)](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/singapore-pudendal-thigh) flaps.

## Indications

| Indication | Detail |
|---|---|
| Lateral vulvar defects | Unilateral hemivulvectomy |
| Total vulvectomy | Bilateral flaps |
| Large defects extending to the vulvo-crural fold | V-Y remains preferred for these excisions |
| Moderate defects too large for tension-free primary closure | First-line option |
| Recurrent disease requiring secondary reconstruction | Reliable salvage<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |

<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The medial thigh V-Y flap is positioned alongside the pudendal-thigh flap as first-line for lateral / hemivulvectomy defects. For total vulvectomy, bilateral medial thigh V-Y flaps can be used; the **pubolabial V-Y flap** (Moschella and Cordova) is a preferable single-flap alternative when groin dissection has not been extended medially.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Vascular Anatomy and Blood Supply

The medial thigh V-Y flap has a robust, redundant vascular supply — multiple sources accounting for its exceptional reliability:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Primary supply

- **Underlying fascial plexus** — a suprafascial communicating plexus formed by three longitudinal rows (anterior, medial, posterior) in the medial thigh.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **3–4 nonaxial vessels** enter the proximal plexus consistently within 5 cm of the perineum — the critical proximal supply.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

### Contributing arteries

| Source | Territory / detail |
|---|---|
| Superficial / deep external pudendal aa. | Upper medial thigh (anteromedial-thigh squares 1 and 4)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Superficial circumflex femoral a. | Upper medial thigh territory<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Profunda femoris perforators | Dominant first-medial-branch perforator to proximal posteromedial thigh, ~8 cm distal to groin crease, 2 cm posterior to gracilis<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Adductor magnus musculocutaneous perforators | Two proximal perforators linked in an intramuscular Y; among the largest skin perforators in the body (0.8–1.1 mm)<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| Superficial femoral a. perforators | Mid / proximal medial thigh; direct cutaneous branch at ~22.8 cm below pubic tubercle between sartorius and gracilis<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |

### Venous drainage and innervation

- Venae comitantes accompanying the arterial perforators; great saphenous vein can be co-harvested as a vein graft.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- Sensory contribution from the **anterior branch of the femoral nerve**.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- Local dermatome innervation is not substantially affected by flap mobilization, preserving sensation at the reconstructed site.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Surgical Technique

**Position**: lithotomy with thighs abducted.

Persichetti et al. (2003, n = 26):<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

1. **Design** — triangular (V-shaped) skin island on proximal medial thigh, apex pointing laterally (distally), base oriented toward the vulvar defect.
2. **Incision** through full-thickness skin and subcutaneous tissue along the V.
3. **Fascial inclusion** — incorporate the muscular fascia (septo-fascio-cutaneous design), sparing intermuscular septal perforators. Critical: the fascia must be included to preserve the fascial plexus blood supply.
4. **Elevation** distal-to-proximal in a subfascial plane, preserving perforators entering from the deep surface.
5. **Advancement** — slide the triangular island medially on its subcutaneous pedicle.
6. **Inset** with interrupted sutures; reconstruct competent urethral and anal meati with intercalated flaps when needed.
7. **Donor closure** — lateral donor primarily closed V-to-Y, converting the V-shaped donor into a Y-shaped scar.

### Key technical points

- Raise **unilaterally** (hemivulvectomy) or **bilaterally** (total vulvectomy).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Including a cuff of **gracilis muscle** at the flap origin increases advancement distance and enables more extended-defect coverage.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Flap can be **fasciocutaneous** (fascia only) or **musculofasciocutaneous** (with muscle cuff) depending on defect depth.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Wang et al. demonstrated reliable elevation up to **9 × 20 cm** without using gracilis as a vascular carrier.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Modifications

| Modification | Author / year | Concept |
|---|---|---|
| **Stepladder V-Y** | Saito 2009<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | Converts the straight V-Y suture line into a zigzag pattern to prevent scar contracture; n = 3, 100% survival, no revisions |
| **Interrupted V-Y** | Sakamoto 2013<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> | V-Y interrupted in the defect area by a bipedicled flap; reduces bulkiness, prevents tip necrosis; n = 5, all flaps survived |
| **Septo-fascio-cutaneous island** | Persichetti 2003<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | Incorporates muscular fascia while sparing intermuscular septal perforators; allows competent meati reconstruction with intercalated flaps |
| **Bilateral extended V-Y** | Ulusoy 2001<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> | Additional limbs from the advancing edges hinge as transposition flaps to close the central portion; midline zigzag scar avoids tension |

## Outcomes

| Study | n / flaps | Complications | Headline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tateo 1996<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | Series | Not detailed | "Extremely safe" due to rich subcutaneous vascular network; near-normal sensation |
| Persichetti 2003<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | 26 patients | Scarring limited / acceptable | One-stage reconstruction with acceptable sensation; competent meati reconstruction |
| Salgarello 2005<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 31 flaps in 20 patients (mixed V-Y) | 3/31 minor tip necrosis; 3/31 major breakdown | Designates V-Y as the vulvar "workhorse"; lowest complication rate among options |
| Saito 2009 (stepladder)<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | 3 | 0% — all flaps survived | No scar contracture; no revisions |
| Sakamoto 2013 (interrupted)<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> | 5 | 0% — all flaps survived | Reduced bulkiness; prevented tip necrosis |
| Confalonieri 2017<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> | 128 V-Y flaps | 21% overall | No significant difference vs lotus petal (p = 0.588); tunneled LPF superior in functional / cosmetic results |
| Tock 2019<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> | V-Y group | 25% reoperation; LOS 14 d | Shortest LOS and lowest reoperation rate among three compared flap types |

## Comparison with Other Flaps

### V-Y vs Lotus Petal (Confalonieri 2017, n = 284)

| | V-Y | Lotus Petal |
|---|---|---|
| Overall complication rate | 21% (27/128) | 13% (14/106) |
| Significance | NS (p = 0.588) | — |
| Tunneled LPF superiority | — | Superior functional / cosmetic results for primary vulvar malignancies |

<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

### V-Y vs Rhomboid vs Gluteal-Thigh (Tock 2019)

| | V-Y | Rhomboid | Gluteal-Thigh |
|---|---|---|---|
| Median LOS | **14 d** | 17 d | 24 d (p = 0.002) |
| Reoperation rate | **25%** | 41% | 69% (p = 0.04) |
| Most common complication | Partial necrosis | Partial necrosis | Partial necrosis |
| OS / RFS | Comparable | Comparable | Comparable |

<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

### V-Y vs Direct closure (Benedetti Panici 2014; tumors &gt; 4 cm)

| | V-Y | Direct closure |
|---|---|---|
| Dehiscence | 11% | 40% (p = 0.017) |
| Hospital stay | 7 d | 10 d (p = 0.007) |
| Mean OR time | 210 min | 120 min (p &lt; 0.001) |

## Advantages

| Advantage | Detail |
|---|---|
| Versatility | Single workhorse for moderate-to-large lateral / hemivulvectomy / total-vulvectomy defects<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Reliability | Rich, redundant vascular supply — flap loss extremely rare<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Technical simplicity | Straightforward elevation and advancement; no microsurgical expertise required<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Low complication rate | Lowest reoperation rate (25%) and shortest LOS (14 d) among compared vulvar flaps<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| Preserved sensation | Sensory innervation not substantially affected by mobilization<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Minimal donor morbidity | Primary V-to-Y closure; medial-thigh scar concealed<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Tissue match | Medial-thigh skin provides excellent color / texture / thickness match<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Compatible with inguinofemoral LND | Can be raised simultaneously or sequentially |
| Large dimensions | Up to 9 × 20 cm without muscle carrier<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |

## Limitations and Complications

| Limitation | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Scar contracture along the straight V-Y suture line | Stepladder modification<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| Tip necrosis (~10%) | Interrupted V-Y modification<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| Bulkiness in obese patients | Interrupted V-Y modification<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| Limited advancement distance | Include muscle cuff for greater reach (added donor morbidity)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Not suitable for very large or extended defects | Use musculocutaneous flaps (VRAM, TFL, gluteal-thigh)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Inferior functional / cosmetic outcomes vs tunneled LPF for primary malignancies | Consider LPF when primary disease<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> |

## Position in Reconstructive Algorithms

| Algorithm | Position |
|---|---|
| Toulouse (2025) | Perforator flaps (IPAP, DFAP) first-line over traditional V-Y due to lower donor morbidity; V-Y remains reliable second-line<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Salgarello (2005) | V-Y is the workhorse for small-to-large defects; musculocutaneous reserved for very large / extended<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Höckel (2008) | Medial-thigh V-Y for lateral / hemivulvectomy defects; bilateral for total vulvectomy<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

---

## Key Takeaways

1. The medial-thigh V-Y flap is the vulvar reconstructive workhorse for lateral / hemivulvectomy defects.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
2. Robust, redundant vascular supply (fascial plexus + adductor / profunda / pudendal / SCFA perforators) makes flap loss rare.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
3. In comparative series the V-Y has the lowest reoperation rate (25%) and shortest LOS (14 d) of the major vulvar flaps.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
4. Stepladder and interrupted-V-Y modifications mitigate the two main drawbacks (scar contracture and tip necrosis / bulk).<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
5. Modern algorithms (Toulouse 2025) increasingly favor perforator flaps (IPAP, DFAP) first-line, but the V-Y remains the reliable workhorse fallback.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
