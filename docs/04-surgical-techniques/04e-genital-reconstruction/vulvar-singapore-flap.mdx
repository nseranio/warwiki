# Pudendal-Thigh (Singapore) Flap for Vulvar and Vaginal Reconstruction

The **pudendal-thigh (Singapore) flap** is a sensate axial-pattern fasciocutaneous flap based on the **terminal branches of the internal pudendal artery (superficial perineal / posterior labial artery)**, first described by **Wee and Joseph (1989)** from Singapore. Originally designed for vaginal reconstruction, it has become one of the most versatile flaps in vulvoperineal surgery — and is the parent flap from which the lotus petal and IPAP flaps evolved.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

For the broader treatment menu see the [Vulvar Reconstruction Atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar). For the related derivatives see [Lotus Petal Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar-lotus-petal) and the foundations [Singapore (pudendal-thigh)](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/singapore-pudendal-thigh) page. For adjacent vulvar atlas pages see [Medial-Thigh V-Y](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar-medial-thigh-vy), [Gluteal V-Y](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar-gluteal-vy), [Pubolabial V-Y](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar-pubolabial-vy), and [Limberg / Rhomboid](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar-limberg-flap).

## Historical Development and Nomenclature

| Year | Contribution |
|---|---|
| 1989 — Wee & Joseph (Singapore) | First neurovascular pudendal-thigh flap for vaginal reconstruction; bilateral flaps transposed to the midline and sutured into a skin-lined cul-de-sac. Name "Singapore flap" derives from the authors' institution.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| 1992 — Woods et al. (Mayo) | "Modified Singapore flap" — labia majora released, flap rotated rather than transposed. Reported superior results over &gt; 30 prior vaginal reconstructions.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| 1996 — Yii & Niranjan | Lotus petal flap — refinement recognizing the perineal-flap petals resemble a lotus. See [Vulvar Lotus Petal](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar-lotus-petal). |
| 2001 — Monstrey et al. | Expanded indications as an island flap — RVF, urethral defects, in addition to vaginal reconstruction.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| 2014 — Hashimoto et al. | IPAP flap — perforator-based, free-style pedicle perforator evolution.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> |

Singapore / pudendal-thigh / lotus petal / gluteal-fold / IPAP all share the same vascular territory — the terminal branches of the internal pudendal artery — but differ in design philosophy, geometry, and movement pattern.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

## Vascular Anatomy and Blood Supply

### Three-territory flap concept (Tham 2010, 5 lead-oxide-injected specimens)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

The pudendal-thigh flap is a three-vascular-territory flap:

| Territory | Source | Plane |
|---|---|---|
| **1 (medial / base)** | Posterior labial aa. (terminal branches of superficial perineal / internal pudendal a.) | **Deep to Colles' fascia** |
| **2 (middle)** | Obturator a. anterior-branch cutaneous perforators | Superficial to Colles' fascia |
| **3 (lateral / apex)** | External pudendal a. branches | Superficial to Colles' fascia |

The **pedicle is close to the midline** — which explains why the lateral / apical portions of large flaps have a precarious blood supply and are prone to **apical necrosis**. Recommendations: design the flap more medially and consider a delay procedure in large flaps.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### Perineal perforator anatomy (Jin 2009)<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

Microdissection (22 sides / 11 cadavers) identified four relatively constant perineal perforators — inguinal and perineal branches of the superficial external pudendal a., obturator anterior cutaneous branch, and lateral branch of the posterior labial (pudendal) a. — all direct perforators forming upper, middle, and lower deep-fascial anastomoses above the adductor wall.

### Sensory innervation

- **Perineal branches of the pudendal nerve** — primary sensation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Posterior femoral cutaneous nerve** — gluteal-fold territory contribution.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Ilioinguinal and genitofemoral nerves** — upper perineal contribution.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- The flap retains cutaneous innervation — a sensate reconstruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Indications

### Vulvar reconstruction

- Lateral / hemivulvectomy defects — unilateral.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Total vulvectomy defects — bilateral.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Radical vulvectomy for vulvar carcinoma.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### Vaginal reconstruction (the original primary indication)

- **Congenital vaginal agenesis** (MRKH) — bilateral flaps for neovagina.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Acquired vaginal defects** — after oncological resection (vaginectomy, pelvic exenteration).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Postradiation vaginal stenosis** — after pelvic RT.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Distal circumferential vaginal defects** — bilateral Singapore flaps are "currently the most effective option" (Höckel).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### Other perineal indications

- **Rectovaginal fistula** repair — unilateral flap.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Posterior urethral defects** in scarred perineum.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Obstetric fistula** with vaginal stenosis — particularly useful in low-resource settings.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Perineal reconstruction** after APR.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Fournier's gangrene** — one of the most commonly utilized flaps.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

### Cordeiro classification (2002) for acquired vaginal defects<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

| Type | Reconstruction |
|---|---|
| **IA** — partial anterior / lateral vaginal wall | **Pedicled Singapore fasciocutaneous flap (first-line)** |
| IB — partial posterior vaginal wall | Rectus abdominis flap |
| IIA — circumferential upper 2/3 vagina | Rolled rectus flap or sigmoid colon |
| IIB — total circumferential vaginal defect | Bilateral gracilis flaps |

## Surgical Technique

### Original technique (Wee & Joseph 1989)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

1. **Position** — lithotomy.
2. **Design** — bilateral elliptical skin islands marked in the groin crease / labiocrural fold just lateral to the labia majora, base at the perineal body.
3. **Incision and elevation** — through skin, subcutaneous tissue, and deep fascia of the thigh including the adductor epimysium.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
4. **Transposition** — flaps transposed toward the midline on the vascular pedicle.
5. **Neovagina formation** — bilateral flaps sutured together into a skin-lined cul-de-sac opening at the introitus.
6. **Donor closure** — primary, with inconspicuous scar in the groin crease.

Key technical points:

- No stents or dilators are needed postoperatively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Reconstructed vagina has a natural angle for intercourse and is sensate.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Flap sizes 9 × 4 cm to 15 × 6 cm.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### Modified Singapore flap (Woods 1992)<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- Labia majora released, allowing anterior retraction; flap rotated rather than simply transposed.
- Used in 8 patients including those with heavy / intraoperative radiation.
- No flap failure despite adverse circumstances; "exceptionally good" contour.

### Island flap modification (Monstrey 2001)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- True island flap mobilized on its subcutaneous pedicle — maximizes mobility.
- Allows unilateral use for fistula repair and urethral reconstruction.

### Liposuction-thinned (Selçuk 2013)<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

- Two-stage: liposuction-thin the planned flap territory, then elevate 3 months later.
- n = 6 MRKH patients (ages 19–24); thinner flaps and more natural vaginal anatomy; adequate vaginal depth at mean 13 mo.

## Outcomes

| Study | n / flaps | Indication | Flap survival | Headline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wee & Joseph 1989<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 3 (bilateral) | Exenteration (1), congenital (2) | **100%** | Original — sensate, natural angle, no stents |
| Woods 1992 (modified)<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | 8 | Vaginal reconstruction | **100% (no flap failure)** | Best results across 30+ prior reconstructions; works even in irradiated patients |
| Gleeson 1994<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | 8 (6 exenteration / 2 vaginectomy) | Oncological | 7/8 (1 bilateral complete necrosis) | Apical necrosis 50%; fistula 25%; only 1/8 had successful coitus |
| Monstrey 2001<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | 19 pts / 31 flaps | Agenesis (8) / oncological (5) / fistula (4) / urethral (2) | **100%** | Excellent functional outcome; hair growth noted |
| Cordeiro 2002<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> | 37 pts / 51 flaps | Acquired vaginal defects (Singapore for Type IA) | High | Singapore flap = first-line for partial anterior / lateral vaginal defects |
| Selvaggi 2003<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | 2 (bilateral) | MRKH with functional uterus | **100%** | Restored uterovaginal continuity and normal menstruation |
| Casey (Mayo) 2004<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | 45 (Singapore) | Vaginal reconstruction | High | Overall complication 46.7% / flap-specific 37.8% — higher than VRAM |
| Jurado 2009<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | 8 (Singapore) | Pelvic exenteration | Variable | Stenosis 28.6%; necrosis 50%; shortness 100% — inferior to TRAM |
| Selçuk 2013 (thinned)<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> | 6 | Müllerian agenesis | 5/6 complete (1 partial loss) | Thinner flap; more natural anatomy |
| Pope 2018 / 2020<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | 32 | Obstetric fistula with stenosis | High | Reliable in low-resource settings; no stenting needed |

## Comparison with Other Flaps for Vaginal Reconstruction

### Singapore vs VRAM vs Gracilis (Casey 2004, n = 99 consecutive, Mayo)<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

| Parameter | Singapore (n = 45) | VRAM (n = 41) | Gracilis (n = 13) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overall complication rate | 46.7% | **31.7%** | 61.5% |
| Flap-specific complication rate | 37.8% | **22.0%** | 53.8% |
| Small-bowel obstruction | Higher | **Protective effect** | Higher |
| Vaginal dilatation required | More frequent | Less frequent | Less frequent |
| Postoperative sexual activity | Predicted by preoperative activity (85.2%); not affected by flap type | Same | Same |

VRAM had the lowest overall and flap-related complication rate and became the Mayo Clinic's vaginal reconstructive flap of choice.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Singapore vs TRAM for pelvic exenteration (Jurado 2009)<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

| Parameter | Singapore (n = 8) | TRAM (n = 21) |
|---|---|---|
| Vaginal stenosis | 28.6% | **19.0%** |
| Necrosis | 50% | **14.5%** |
| Vaginal shortness | 100% | **0%** |
| CRA dehiscence (with low CRA) | 83.3% | **28.6%** |

TRAM was the preferable option for vaginal reconstruction after pelvic exenteration; the Singapore flap is not recommended with low colorectal anastomosis.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

### Position in the Höckel algorithm (2008)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

| Defect | Reconstruction |
|---|---|
| Lateral / hemivulvectomy | Unilateral pudendal-thigh OR medial-thigh V-Y |
| Total vulvar | Bilateral pudendal-thigh (or pubolabial V-Y if groin dissection not extended medially) |
| Planar partial vaginal | Pudendal-thigh (larger skin islands than labial flaps but bulkier base may bulge-out vulva) |
| Distal circumferential vaginal | Bilateral pudendal-thigh — "currently the most effective option" |
| Extended vaginal (exenteration) | Musculocutaneous, bilateral pudendal-thigh, or sigmoid; tubularized rectus abdominis preferable over bilateral gracilis or pudendal-thigh |

## Cutaneous Sensibility (Coltro 2015)<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

A prospective study of 25 patients showed that tactile, pain, thermal, and vibration sensibility on the IPAP flap (perforator-based evolution of the pudendal-thigh flap) showed **no statistically significant difference** from preoperative gluteal-fold sensibility at 12 months — confirming the sensate-flap claim translates to objective sensory preservation.

## Advantages

| Advantage | Detail |
|---|---|
| Sensate reconstruction | Pudendal-nerve innervation; reconstructed vagina / vulva is sensate<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |
| Natural vaginal angle | Neovagina with natural angle for intercourse<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| No stents / dilators required | Unlike skin grafts or bowel-based neovaginas<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Simple, safe, reliable | No microsurgical expertise required<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| Inconspicuous donor scar | Hidden in the groin crease / labiocrural fold<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Primary donor closure | Closed primarily<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Versatile | Unilateral or bilateral; vulvar / vaginal / urethral / fistula<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Suitable for low-resource settings | Reliable in obstetric-fistula populations<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| No muscle sacrifice | Fasciocutaneous design<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Cutaneous sensibility preserved | Coltro 2015 — no significant difference vs preoperative gluteal-fold sensibility<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |

## Limitations

| Limitation | Detail |
|---|---|
| Apical necrosis in large flaps | Gleeson 50%; Jurado 50% after exenteration — explained by the three-territory anatomy<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[8]](#ref8)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| Bulkier base than labial flaps | May bulge out parts of the vulva after subcutaneous rotation<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| Higher complication rate than VRAM | Mayo n = 99 — 46.7% vs 31.7%<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Inferior to TRAM for exenteration | 100% vaginal shortness, 50% necrosis, high CRA dehiscence<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| Disappointing functional results in irradiated / exenteration patients | Gleeson: only 1/8 successful coitus; no patient with bilateral flaps or prior RT achieved successful intercourse<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Hair growth | Hair-bearing donor — chronic vaginal discharge, patient dissatisfaction; managed by pre / postoperative laser depilation<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| Vulvar protrusion | Gleeson: 2/8 with vulvovaginal symptoms discouraging genital contact<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Vaginal dilatation may still be needed | Despite original claim — Mayo found Singapore patients dilated more frequently than VRAM<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| Prior radiotherapy | Strongly restricts options — irradiated donor tissue should be avoided<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |

## Relationship to Lotus Petal and IPAP Flaps

| Feature | Pudendal-Thigh (Singapore) | Lotus Petal | IPAP |
|---|---|---|---|
| First described | Wee & Joseph 1989<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Yii & Niranjan 1996 | Hashimoto 2014<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> |
| Design philosophy | Axial-pattern fasciocutaneous | Fasciocutaneous transposition | Free-style perforator |
| Vascular basis | Superficial perineal a. (IPA terminal branch) | IPA perforators | IPA perforators (Doppler-identified) |
| Fascial plane | Subfascial (incl. deep fascia + epimysium) | Subfascial or supra-fascial | Supra-fascial |
| Flap movement | Transposition / rotation | Transposition | Propeller / transposition / V-Y |
| Thinning possible | Limited (two-stage liposuction) | Yes (thinned cutaneous variant) | Yes (routine thinning) |
| Primary indication | Vaginal reconstruction | Vulvoperineal | Vulvar / vaginal / buttock / perineal |

<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

The evolution from pudendal-thigh → lotus petal → IPAP represents a progression from a territory-based axial flap to a perforator-based free-style flap — enabling thinner flaps and more versatile movement patterns while maintaining the same reliable IPA territory.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

## Key Takeaways

1. The pudendal-thigh (Singapore) flap (Wee & Joseph 1989) is a sensate fasciocutaneous flap on the terminal branches of the internal pudendal artery, originally designed for vaginal reconstruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
2. It is a **three-vascular-territory** flap — pedicle is close to the midline; lateral / apical portions of large flaps are vulnerable to necrosis.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
3. 100% flap survival in the original and Monstrey series, but larger oncological series reveal significant apical necrosis (up to 50%), particularly after pelvic exenteration.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[8]](#ref8)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
4. For vaginal reconstruction after pelvic exenteration, the VRAM / TRAM flap is **superior** in complication rates and functional outcomes.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
5. For partial vaginal defects, congenital agenesis, fistula repair, and vulvar reconstruction, the pudendal-thigh flap remains an excellent option — simple, reliable, sensate, no stents required.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
6. Evolved into the [lotus petal flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar-lotus-petal) (1996) and IPAP flap (2014), which offer greater design flexibility and are increasingly preferred in modern algorithms.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
7. Hair growth and vulvar bulging are the most common long-term functional complaints; laser depilation mitigates the former.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
