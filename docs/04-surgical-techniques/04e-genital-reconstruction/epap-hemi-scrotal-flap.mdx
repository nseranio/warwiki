# Sensate External Pudendal Artery Perforator (EPAP) Hemi-Scrotal Flap

The **sensate external pudendal artery perforator (EPAP) hemi-scrotal flap** is a reported local perforator-flap approach for penile shaft skin coverage. The directly identified clinical evidence is one 2025 case report; it should be understood as a technical feasibility report rather than an established standard, comparative result, or operative protocol.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For broader context, see [Penile Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile) and [Bipedicled Anterior Scrotal Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/bipedicled-anterior-scrotal-flap).

## Reported anatomy

A 2009 cadaveric microvascular study of 15 male specimens described inferior external pudendal and perineal arterial systems distributed across two lateral and one central scrotal skin territories, with inter-anastomoses.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> This anatomical description supports the rationale for scrotal flap design but does not establish a fixed perforator location, a mandatory Doppler strategy, or reliable flap dimensions for an individual.

## Index case

Tsukuura and colleagues reported one 40-year-old man with a circumferential penile skin deficit after penile replantation. They designed a 9 × 14 cm hemi-scrotal flap with preoperative Doppler, isolated an external-pudendal perforator and anterior scrotal nerve, and closed the donor site primarily. The postoperative course was reported as uneventful; at seven months, the authors reported good colour match and sensation, an inconspicuous donor site, and recovered function without pain or stretching sensation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

These findings belong to a single patient, one injury setting, and short follow-up. They cannot establish flap survival probability, sensory preservation, erectile or sexual outcomes, donor-site risk, long-term durability, or applicability to other diagnoses.

## Interpretation and evidence limits

- The report describes an isolated perforator/nerve-preservation approach, but contains no head-to-head comparison with bipedicled scrotal flaps, grafts, staged methods, or other local/perforator flaps.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Statements in the report about wider rotation, easier inset, or possible superiority are author interpretations from the case, not demonstrated comparative outcomes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- The reported 9 × 14 cm flap, Doppler use, dissection plane, and donor closure are case-specific details, not general requirements.
- Perforator variation, wound factors, and reconstruction goals require individualized assessment by an appropriately experienced reconstructive team.

## Key takeaways

1. EPAP hemi-scrotal flap is a described local perforator-flap option with one published penile-reconstruction case.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
2. That report documented a favourable seven-month outcome in its index case, but cannot predict outcomes for other patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
3. Cadaveric vascular anatomy is supportive background, not a substitute for patient-specific operative planning.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
