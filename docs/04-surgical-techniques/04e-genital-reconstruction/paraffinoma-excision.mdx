# Paraffinoma Excision and Penile Resurfacing

Penile paraffinoma, also termed sclerosing lipogranuloma or oleogranuloma, is a foreign-body reaction after injection of non-medical materials such as oils or petroleum products for augmentation. Published management reports often involve surgical excision of affected tissue followed by individualized coverage or reconstruction; the evidence is mostly case reports and retrospective series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For causative materials and clinical assessment, see [Non-Autologous Penile Injectables](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/non-autologous-injectables). For reconstruction options, see [Penile Skin / Shaft Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile-skin-reconstruction) and [Penile Skin Grafting](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile-skin-grafting).

---

## Clinical Boundary

Symptoms, tissue involvement, skin compromise, prior attempts at treatment, infection/inflammation, and the patient’s goals vary substantially. A thorough in-person assessment is necessary to determine whether surgery is appropriate and, if so, how much tissue can be safely removed and what coverage is feasible.

The goal of surgery is control of symptomatic or compromised affected tissue while preserving function when possible. The source record does not support a universal rule for extent of excision, imaging, timing, or reconstruction. Treatment plans should be made by an experienced urologic and reconstructive team and include counselling about wound healing, contour, sexual function, uncertainty, and possible further procedures.

## Reconstruction

Published approaches include native-skin preservation or closure where feasible, local scrotal-flap coverage, staged reconstruction, and skin-graft pathways. These are alternatives selected for the individual defect and available tissue—not interchangeable choices with proven ranking or predictable results.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Outcome reports should distinguish the underlying injected material, tissue extent, preoperative complications, reconstruction performed, wound outcomes, urinary and sexual outcomes, patient-reported experience, and need for revision. Existing heterogeneous reports do not support fixed complication, satisfaction, or functional-success estimates.

## What Is Not Established

- A standard excision depth, imaging work-up, scrotal-flap design, graft protocol, or postoperative pathway.
- A preferred single-stage versus staged approach.
- Reliable rates of erectile preservation, infection, flap/graft loss, reoperation, or satisfaction.
- Generalization from paraffin cohorts to other injected materials or to non-injection penile defects.
