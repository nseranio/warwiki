# Paraffinoma Excision and Penile Resurfacing

**Paraffinoma** — also called **sclerosing lipogranuloma** or **oleogranuloma** — is a chronic foreign-body granulomatous response after subcutaneous penile injection of materials such as **liquid paraffin, mineral oil, Vaseline / petroleum jelly**, or liquid silicone for augmentation. Symptomatic infiltrative disease, ulceration, necrosis, infection, deformity, or functional impairment commonly leads to excision/debridement and reconstruction; management is individualized to symptoms, anatomic extent, skin viability, injected material, and patient goals. No formal guideline or comparative trial establishes one universal operation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

This is the dedicated atlas page for paraffinoma excision and reconstruction. For the **causal substances, pathophysiology, imaging, and complication phenotype**, see [Non-Autologous Penile Injectables](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/non-autologous-injectables). For the male cosmetic decision framework, see [Male Cosmetic Genital Surgery](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/male-cosmetic). For the broader penile-skin-reconstruction umbrella (Fournier's, lichen sclerosus, oncologic resection, avulsion), see [Penile Skin / Shaft Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile-skin-reconstruction).

## Indications

In Pang's review of heterogeneous male genital injections, **78.8%** of reported patients underwent surgery; in its 35-patient single-center cohort, **32 (91.4%)** did so. These figures describe selected symptomatic reports and mixed injected materials, not the probability that every patient with paraffinoma requires an operation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> When resection is chosen, remove clinically involved foreign-body tissue while preserving critical structures and viable coverage where feasible; reconstruction follows the resulting defect.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> Reported operative triggers include:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- Firm, irregular or indurated mass with deformity or cosmetic dissatisfaction (57.1% in Pang's 35-patient cohort)
- Pain or swelling (45.7% in Pang's cohort)
- Erectile dysfunction
- Voiding difficulty / phimosis or failed urethral catheterization
- Skin necrosis or ulceration (necrosis occurred in 13/18 patients in one advanced oleogranuloma referral series; this is not a general prevalence estimate<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>)
- Migration with extension into the scrotum, perineum, or abdominal wall (the abdominal-wall citation is a separate-site case, not a penile outcome series<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>)

## Preoperative Considerations

- **Extent of infiltration** — diagnosis is primarily clinical. In selected extensive, uncertain, or deep disease, penile/scrotal MRI can help map subcutaneous and scrotal involvement, assess the relationship to Buck's fascia and neurovascular structures, and support planning or differential diagnosis; a single penile MRI case report does not establish diagnostic accuracy or exclude malignancy.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> An inherited breast-paraffinoma imaging series supplies only non-penile signal context and is not direct evidence for penile diagnosis.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Native shaft-skin viability** — the central determinant of reconstructive choice; the more native shaft skin that can be preserved, the simpler the reconstruction.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Multidisciplinary input** from urology and reconstructive surgery may be useful when disease is extensive or coverage is complex; the accessible evidence does not compare team configurations.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Goals and psychological support** — explore expectations, the motivation for injection, body-image distress, sexual concerns, and realistic postoperative appearance; involve appropriate support when concerns are clinically significant.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Disclosure and counseling** — patients are frequently reluctant to disclose injection history; a non-judgmental approach improves accuracy of timeline and substance reporting.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Reconstructive Options

Six reconstructive pathways recur in the case-report/case-series literature. Selection is guided by the amount and location of involved skin, scrotal-tissue availability, prior surgery, contamination/necrosis, patient preference, and reconstructive expertise.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

| Technique | Indication | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Local excision + primary closure** | Localized disease with sufficient viable native shaft skin | Preserves native skin when the defect closes without undue tension; patient selection drives the apparent results.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **Bilateral scrotal flap — single stage** | Extensive/circumferential shaft-skin loss with suitable scrotal tissue | Widely described in specialist series. In Murányi's 49-patient single-surgeon cohort, 30 completed the long-term questionnaire and 27/30 met the authors' patient-reported success definition.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Bilateral scrotal flap — two stage** | Extensive disease when a staged course is acceptable or preferred | Stage 1 buries the denuded shaft in scrotal tissue; stage 2 releases and fashions coverage. In Lumbiganon's retrospective comparison, 10/12 two-stage patients versus 10/23 single-stage patients had no Clavien-Dindo complication, but selection was nonrandomized and total hospital stay was longer.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **Split-thickness skin graft (STSG)** | Scrotal skin unavailable/insufficient or graft coverage otherwise selected | Kang's 2026 retrospective 11-patient protocol combined Matriderm, STSG and NPWT; 10/11 had near-complete graft take. This is one single-arm technique series.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| **Full-thickness skin graft (FTSG)** | Selected defects and donor sites | Reported donor sites and techniques vary; comparative superiority over STSG or flaps is unestablished.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| **Modified bipedicle scrotal flap / Y-V geometry** | Extensive disease with suitable scrotal tissue | Designed to separate high-tension junctions and reduce distal flap compromise; evidence consists of technique series rather than a randomized comparison.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |

### Pang single-center distribution

In Pang's 35-patient single-center cohort, 32 patients underwent surgery. Primary procedures included:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- Local excision + primary closure: 19/32 (59.4%)
- Circumcision: 5/32 (15.6%)
- Excision with split-skin graft or scrotal flap reconstruction: 5/32 (15.6%)
- Eighteen of 35 patients underwent more than one procedure, including planned/staged and unplanned procedures; this is not synonymous with a 51% complication reoperation rate.

## Surgical Technique — Bilateral Scrotal Flap (Single Stage)

The following is the modified single-stage bipedicle scrotal-flap sequence reported by Murányi and related technique papers for extensive shaft involvement. It is specialist operative teaching, not a universal protocol.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

1. **Subcoronal incision.** Circumferential incision just proximal to the corona, sparing the glans.
2. **Penoscrotal incision.** Second circumferential incision at the penoscrotal junction.
3. **Complete shaft degloving.** Excise all involved skin and subcutaneous tissue down to **Buck's fascia**, preserving the dorsal neurovascular bundle, urethra, and corpora cavernosa / spongiosum.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
4. **Design and mobilize bilateral scrotal flaps** with a robust subcutaneous pedicle.
5. **Subcutaneous tunnel.** Create a tunnel between the scrotal incision and the proximal penile incision.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
6. **Pull-through.** Deliver the denuded penis through the scrotal tunnel.
7. **Flap inset.** Advance and suture the flap to the subcoronal margin and close the ventral inverted-V incision longitudinally. The geometry is intended to separate high-tension junctions; it does not eliminate marginal necrosis.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
8. **Layered scrotal closure.**

### Two-stage variant

- **Stage 1.** Excision of involved tissue; the denuded penis is buried within a scrotal tunnel. Reported intervals to release vary by technique and patient.
- **Stage 2.** Release of the penis from the scrotum; scrotal skin is fashioned into definitive coverage. Lumbiganon 2023 reported no Clavien-Dindo complication in 10/12 two-stage versus 10/23 single-stage patients; wound infection, dehiscence and reoperation differences individually were imprecise and not statistically established, while postoperative fever was higher and total stay shorter in the single-stage group. The retrospective comparison is vulnerable to selection and small-sample confounding.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

### NPWT-assisted STSG (single-stage alternative)

- Kang 2026 protocol: complete excision, application of dermal substitute (Matriderm), STSG, then **negative-pressure wound therapy at −125 mmHg**.
- **10/11 near-complete graft take**; one partial graft loss/hematoma required evacuation, one patient had chronic discharge from remnant filler, and median satisfaction was 37/45 at median 18-month follow-up.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- This provides a reported single-stage option when graft coverage is selected; comparative durability against flap reconstruction is unknown.

## Outcomes

| Series | n | Technique | Success | Complications |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Murányi 2022<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | 49 treated; 30 questionnaire respondents | Modified bipedicle scrotal flap | 27/30 met authors' questionnaire success definition | 13/49 had 26.5% overall complication rate; ED reported by 2/30 respondents |
| Lumbiganon 2023<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | 42 total; 23 single-stage and 12 two-stage compared | Single- vs two-stage scrotal flap | No validated global success endpoint | No Clavien-Dindo complication: 10/23 vs 10/12; fever: 13/23 vs 1/12 |
| Suleiman 2024<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | 18 | Primary closure (9) or two-stage scrotal flap (9) | All 18 underwent surgery; the abstract does not supply a success rate | Necrosis was a presentation finding in 13/18, not a postoperative complication |
| Marín-Martínez / Dekalo 2023<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | Two tertiary centers | Single- and two-stage algorithm | Authors reported aesthetic/functional outcomes | Full methods, denominators and tables remain pending in this audit |
| Kang 2026<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> | 11 | NPWT + dermal substitute + STSG | Near-complete graft take in 10/11 | One hematoma/partial loss; one remnant-filler discharge |

Many reports preserve erectile/intercourse ability, but outcome instruments and denominators are inconsistent and selection is strong. Murányi's technique dissects between involved dartos and Buck's fascia; its 30 questionnaire respondents all reported intercourse ability, while two reported erectile dysfunction. Do not translate this into a guaranteed functional outcome.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Complications

| Complication | Rate / note |
|---|---|
| Wound infection | Lumbiganon: 2/23 (8.69%) single-stage vs 0/12 two-stage; the confidence interval was very wide<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| Wound dehiscence | Lumbiganon: 5/23 (21.74%) single-stage vs 1/12 (8.33%) two-stage; no statistically established difference<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| Postoperative fever | Lumbiganon: 13/23 (56.52%) single-stage vs 1/12 (8.33%) two-stage<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| Partial graft / flap loss | Technique- and cohort-specific; Kang reported one partial graft loss/hematoma among 11 STSG patients<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| Penile lymphedema | Murányi: 1/30 questionnaire respondents<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Chronic discharge from remnant filler | Reported after residual foreign material and may prompt further assessment or surgery<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| Erectile/sexual symptoms | Reported inconsistently; preoperative disease and reconstruction both complicate attribution<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Additional procedures | Pang reported more than one procedure in 18/35 patients, a count that includes planned/staged care and should not be labeled solely as complication-driven reoperation<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

## Non-Surgical Options

Evidence for nonoperative treatment is limited and heterogeneous. Pang's systematic review found 21.2% managed conservatively, but it did not establish comparative effectiveness; necrosis, infection, progressive ulceration, obstruction, suspected malignancy, or major functional impairment require prompt specialist assessment.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- **Intralesional triamcinolone** and **hot-water baths** have been described as temporizing measures for patients who decline surgery.
- These reports do not show reliable reversal of established granulomatous infiltration and should not delay necessary debridement or diagnostic evaluation.

---

## Key Principles

- **Resect involved foreign-body tissue while preserving critical anatomy and viable coverage.** Residual material can perpetuate symptoms, but the safe plane and resection extent are individualized.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Choose coverage to fit the defect and donor tissue.** Primary closure, scrotal flaps and skin grafts all have reported roles; no comparative trial establishes one preferred pathway.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Single- versus two-stage tradeoffs are not settled.** One small retrospective comparison found fewer aggregate complications but longer total stay with staging; individual wound-event comparisons were imprecise.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **NPWT + dermal substitute + STSG** has promising single-arm 11-patient data, not comparative validation.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Counsel about uncertain functional and cosmetic outcomes.** Intercourse ability was retained in selected series, but erectile symptoms, lymphedema, wound problems and additional procedures occurred.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Modified Y-V/inverted-V geometry** is intended to reduce high-tension junctions; it does not eliminate marginal skin necrosis.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Address expectations and body-image concerns** alongside physical reconstruction.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
