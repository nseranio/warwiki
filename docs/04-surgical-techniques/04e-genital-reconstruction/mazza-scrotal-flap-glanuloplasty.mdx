# Scrotal Flap Glanuloplasty (Mazza and Cheliz Technique)

The **Mazza / Cheliz scrotal flap glanuloplasty** is a two-stage pedicled scrotal-flap reconstruction reported for a penile stump after trauma or cancer. The original 2001 series included 34 patients with mean follow-up of 73.2 months and addressed inadequate skin, stump retraction and neomeatal stenosis. It was an uncontrolled single-center series with no comparison to other neoglans methods.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For the urethral-flap alternatives, see [Inverted Urethral Flap (IUF) Glanuloplasty (Belinky / Chavarriaga)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/inverted-urethral-flap) and [Gulino Distal Urethral Glanuloplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/gulino-everted-urethral-flap). For the comparative umbrella, see [Glanuloplasty With Flaps](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/glanuloplasty-flaps). For the STSG-based standard, see [Glansectomy With STSG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/glansectomy-stsg). Decision framework: [Penile Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile).

For scrotal-flap applications to the penile shaft instead of the neoglans, see [Bipedicled Anterior Scrotal Flap (Fakin)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/bipedicled-anterior-scrotal-flap), [Yao Butterfly](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/yao-butterfly-scrotal-flap), [Murányi tunnel](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/muranyi-scrotal-tunnel-flap), [Total Anterior Scrotal Flap (Zhao)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/total-anterior-scrotal-flap), [Staged Bipedicled (Pribaz / McLaughlin)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/staged-scrotal-flap), [Reverse Bilateral (Gao)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/reverse-anterior-scrotal-flap), and [Sensate EPAP Hemi-Scrotal (Tsukuura)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/epap-hemi-scrotal-flap).

---

## Historical Context and Rationale

The authors presented scrotal skin as thin, elastic, well vascularized and anatomically adjacent for coverage of a penile stump after partial penectomy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Cheliz later co-authored the Belinky inverted-urethral-flap report. The abstracts do not describe a formal lineage between the two operations.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Benderev (1988) had previously described a suprapubic and scrotal pedicled flap that covers a new proximal penile shaft after partial penectomy. It was not designed for neoglans creation.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Anatomical Basis

### Scrotal vascular anatomy (Carrera 2009, 15 cadavers)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

Three cutaneous territories supplied by two systems and widely inter-anastomosed:

1. **Inferior external pudendal arteries** (femoral branches) supply two lateral cutaneous territories, accessing at the midpoint of the scrotal root and fanning over the corresponding hemiscrotum
2. **Perineal arteries** (internal pudendal branches) supply the central cutaneous territory via two main scrotal arteries running deeply alongside the scrotal septum from the posterior face

### Scrotal-skin tissue properties

- **Thin and elastic.** Among the thinnest skin in the body; dartos fascia provides smooth-muscle elasticity<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Well-vascularized** dartos arterial network
- **Redundant.** Provides adequate donor skin without compromising scrotal or testicular coverage
- **Hair-bearing.** The principal disadvantage; follicles persist after transfer, and depilation is needed in a subset
- **Sensate.** Anterior scrotal nerves (ilioinguinal branches) and posterior scrotal nerves (perineal branches)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

---

## Indications

- **Penile stump after cancer treatment.** The original report also discusses traumatic stumps but gives no indication breakdown.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Traumatic partial amputation** when the stump requires coverage and neoglans
- Any partial penectomy with inadequate skin coverage and retraction risk

Selection considerations include:

- The urethral stump is too short for IUF or Gulino techniques
- The penile stump has retracted into the scrotum and requires exteriorization
- **Sufficient scrotal redundancy** is available for flap harvest

## Surgical Technique — Two-Stage

The original abstract describes the two-stage concept, flap transfer, suture of the urethral end to a hole in the flap, inset of the flap borders to the albuginea, and pedicle division at four to six weeks. The artery-level design and finer steps below combine that abstract with general scrotal-flap anatomy and are adapted to the individual patient.

### Stage 1 — Scrotal Flap Transfer

1. **Penectomy and stump preparation.** Confirm negative margins after partial penectomy; NCCN recommends intraoperative frozen sections during penectomy (category 2A).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> Obtain hemostasis at the corporal tips and identify the urethral stump.
2. **Scrotal flap design.** Design a pedicled scrotal flap with dimensions matched to the stump and its distal end sized to cover the corporal tips.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
3. **Flap elevation and transfer.** Elevate on the pedicle preserving vascular supply at the base; transfer the distal extreme to the penile stump
4. **Neomeatus creation.** Suture the urethral end to a hole created in the scrotal flap, creating the neomeatus
5. **Flap fixation.** Suture flap borders to adjacent tunica albuginea of the corpora; sculpt to natural-glans contour
6. **Pedicle maintenance.** Leave the scrotal-skin bridge intact; supplies the flap during neovascularization from the underlying corporal bed; penis remains tethered to scrotum during this interval

### Interval — 4–6 weeks

The flap is expected to gain enough blood supply from the underlying wound bed to survive without the original pedicle.

### Stage 2 — Pedicle Division

7. **Pedicle assessment** at 4–6 weeks
8. **Pedicle resection.** Divide and free the penis from the scrotum; close remaining scrotal skin primarily; trim and suture the flap edges on the penile stump for a smooth, circumferential neoglans surface

### Demographics

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|

| Mean age | 43.2 y |
| Indication | Partial penectomy (cancer and trauma) |
| **Mean follow-up** | **73.2 months (~ 6.1 y)** |

| Outcome | Result | Details |

## Key Findings

### Reported absence of penile retraction<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The authors reported no penile retraction and satisfactory voiding at mean 73.2 months. The abstract gives no separate retraction-event table and no tethering mechanism.

### Sexual potency reported in 7/34

The abstract reports preserved potency in 7/34 (20.5%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The cross-study percentages below come from different operations, disease extent, instruments and follow-up. They do not show that the flap caused worse potency. Figures for the other techniques are taken from the linked [IUF](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/inverted-urethral-flap), [Gulino](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/gulino-everted-urethral-flap) and [glansectomy with STSG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/glansectomy-stsg) pages.

| Technique | Sexual / erectile function | n |
|---|---|---|
| **Mazza scrotal flap** | **20.5% potency**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 34 |
| Belinky / Chavarriaga IUF | IIEF-5 17.3 (mild-moderate ED) | 74 |
| Gulino distal-urethral reconstruction | 71% rigid erections (10/14 at mean 13 months)<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> | 14 |
| Glansectomy cohorts (Pang review; mixed reconstructions) | 91.1% preserved erectile function across reporting studies<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> | 327 glansectomies overall |
| Zhao scrotal flap (shaft defects) | 15/18 (83%) reported satisfied intercourse at 0.5–5 years<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | 18 |

The abstract does not separate baseline function, cancer or trauma extent and follow-up assessment. No fibrosis, nerve or tissue-type mechanism can be inferred from this number.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### Hair growth — donor-site-specific issue

The original series reports definitive neoglans depilation in 6/34 (17.6%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Hair burden depends on the donor skin selected and may need pre- or postoperative management. It applies to hair-bearing scrotal skin and is not a direct comparison with other tissues.

**Gil-Vernet 1995.** Insulated-needle thermocoagulation; average 3 sessions at 4-week intervals; no infections. The median area around the scrotal raphe is nearly hairless (Osman). Flap design can use it to limit hair burden.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Mazza vs Other Scrotal-Flap Applications

Mazza (neoglans) is distinct from the other scrotal-flap uses for shaft coverage:

| Study | n | Indication | Stages | Potency / erection | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mazza / Cheliz 2001**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 34 | **Neoglans after penectomy** | **2** | **20.5%** | Normal-appearing |
| Zhao 2009<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | 18 | Shaft defects (circumcision, animal bite or tumor dissection) | 1<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> | 15/18 (83%) reported satisfied intercourse at 0.5–5 years | Bilateral (6) / total anterior (12) |
| Fakin 2017<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | 43 | Shaft defects after siliconoma excision | 1<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> | All reported postoperative erection and intercourse ability | Bipedicled anterior; mean satisfaction 4.37 / 5; mean follow-up 10.69 months |
| Mendel 2023<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | 22 | Shaft defects (buried penis, foreign body) | 1–2 | Median EHS 3.5 / 4 among 12 questionnaire respondents | Median global satisfaction 8 / 10 among 12 respondents; testicular ascension 5/22 (22.7%) |
| McLaughlin 2024<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> | 8 | Shaft defects (various) | 2 | Satisfactory | Bipedicled |
| Yao 2022<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> | 7 | Foreskin defects | **1** | n/r | Modified bilateral butterfly |

Shaft-defect cohorts involve different anatomy and outcome measures. Their erectile-function percentages are not comparable with Mazza's 7/34 potency report.

## Later Scrotal-Flap Designs for Different Defects

| Innovation | Author | Year | Advantage |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Modified bilateral "butterfly" flap** | Yao<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> | 2022 | **Single-stage** bilateral design (no pedicle phase) |
| **Bipedicled anterior scrotal flap** | Fakin<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | 2017 | n = 43, 100% erection, 4.37 / 5 satisfaction |
| **Sensate EPAP hemi-scrotal flap** | Tsukuura<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | 2025 | Single case report with 7-month follow-up: sensate (anterior scrotal nerve preserved), inconspicuous lateral scar, wider rotation arc |
| **Bilateral pedicled** | Mendel<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | 2023 | Among 12/22 questionnaire respondents, median EHS was 3.5 / 4 and median global satisfaction was 8 / 10; early dehiscence occurred in 7/22 and late testicular ascension in 5/22 |

## Depilation Techniques for Scrotal-Flap Hair<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

| Approach | Details |
|---|---|
| **Electrolysis (thermocoagulation)** | Gil-Vernet insulated-needle technique; average 3 sessions, 4-wk intervals; no infections |
| **Laser hair removal** | Nd:YAG, alexandrite, or diode for permanent reduction on transferred skin |
| Preoperative depilation | When planned for hair-bearing donor skin, allow time for serial sessions; Gil-Vernet's urethroplasty series used an average of three thermocoagulation sessions at four-week intervals.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **Flap-design optimization** | Incorporate the **median raphe** (nearly hairless) midline strip to minimize neoglans hair |

## Psychosexual Impact

The Mazza/Cheliz series reported preserved potency in 7/34 men (20.5%); the sources below provide context for psychosexual outcomes after penile-cancer treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)[[18]](#ref18)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

- **Harju 2021 (107 sent questionnaires; 68 respondents).** Lower overall sexual functioning, erectile function and change in sexual function were associated with lower HRQoL among respondents operated for penile cancer.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> Mean HRQoL was significantly lower than the age-standardized Finnish population average
- **Kieffer 2014 (90 respondents of 147 invited).** Men who had (partial) penectomy reported more problems than those with penile-sparing surgery with orgasm (effect size 0.54), appearance (0.61), life interference (0.49) and urinary function (83% vs 43%).<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- **Roumieux 2025 (qualitative, 9 men).** Themes included a "voyage of sexual re-discovery" and the need for pre-surgical information and post-surgical psychosexual support
- **EAU-ASCO.** Partial penectomy is associated with poorer sexual outcomes than organ-sparing surgery

## Guideline Context

Neither NCCN nor EAU-ASCO mentions the Mazza technique specifically.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

- The 2023 EAU-ASCO update supported organ-sparing surgery and reconstruction in selected patients.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> The 2026 guideline recommends these options for glans- or prepuce-confined PeIN, Ta or T1–T2 disease when strict follow-up is acceptable (strong), and partial penectomy for corporal invasion (T3) or refusal of organ-sparing surgery or strict follow-up (strong). It does not endorse this named flap.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- NCCN Version 2.2026 does not describe this named flap.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

Mazza is consistent with the principle of reconstruction after confirmed negative margins.

## Advantages

1. **No penile retraction reported** in the authors' 34-patient series
2. **Long follow-up within this series:** 73.2 months mean
3. **Cohort size:** n = 34<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>, sizable for a rare indication
4. **Normal-appearing penis.** Described qualitatively as achieving a normal cosmetic result
5. **Unobstructed urinary flow** was described qualitatively in the original series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
6. **One meatal stenosis (2.9%)**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> managed with a minor procedure in this series
7. **Vascularized regional tissue.** Supported by scrotal vascular anatomy
8. **Thin, elastic donor skin.** A practical regional-tissue property
9. **No distant donor site**
10. **Does not require using urethral tissue as the neoglans donor**, which may matter when a urethral-flap design is unsuitable

---

## Limitations

1. **Two-stage procedure** with 4–6 wk pedicle phase
2. **Potency reported in 7/34 (20.5%).**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Baseline function is not reported in the abstract
3. **Hair growth requiring depilation (17.6%).**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> A donor-skin issue in this series
4. **Partial flap necrosis in 2/34 (5.8%).**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Cross-series graft or flap percentages are not comparative estimates
5. **Keratinized non-mucosal surface.** Does not mimic native glans epithelium
6. **Penile-scrotal tethering during pedicle phase.** Discomfort, functional limitation
7. **No validated QoL instruments.** Qualitative outcomes only
8. **Sensation not specifically reported**
9. **Not specifically endorsed by NCCN / EAU-ASCO**
10. **Single-center experience.** No external validation
11. **Potential testicular ascension** (Mendel 22.7% with bilateral scrotal flaps for shaft reconstruction)<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

---

## Future Directions

- **EPAP hemi-scrotal flap for a shaft defect.** A single case report described perforator-flap coverage after penile replantation; no separate pedicle-division stage was described.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Standardized preoperative depilation** protocols (laser / electrolysis) of the planned donor site
- **Hybrid approaches.** Scrotal flap for shaft coverage and urethral flap for neoglans creation
- **Validated functional outcome assessment.** Prospective use of IIEF-15, IPSS, EQ-5D, EORTC QLQ-C30
- **Direct comparison** of scrotal flap versus IUF versus STSG in matched cohorts
- **Sensate flap development** incorporating the anterior scrotal nerve to improve sensory outcomes (EPAP precedent)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

---

## Key Takeaways

- Original report: n = 34, mean follow-up 73.2 months, with no penile retraction described<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Potency was preserved in 7/34 (20.5%)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>; other cohorts used different instruments and anatomy, and the figures cannot be ranked
- Definitive depilation was required in 6/34 (17.6%)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>, a donor-hair consideration
- **Two-stage.** Requires pedicle division at 4–6 weeks
- Selection requires adequate scrotal donor tissue and an oncologically appropriate stump. Comparative evidence does not define its place relative to urethral-flap or graft methods
- Later perforator-based and single-stage scrotal designs are alternatives for different defects. None has been shown to improve on this neoglans technique
