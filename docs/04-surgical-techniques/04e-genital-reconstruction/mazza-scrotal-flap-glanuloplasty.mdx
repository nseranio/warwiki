# Scrotal Flap Glanuloplasty (Mazza and Cheliz Technique)

The Mazza/Cheliz technique is a reported staged pedicled scrotal-flap reconstruction used to create a neoglans after partial penectomy. It has been described in a single published series and should be treated as a specialized option, not as a standard reconstruction or a proven solution for penile retraction, urinary function, or sexual function.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For related context, see [Glanuloplasty With Flaps](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/glanuloplasty-flaps), [Glansectomy With STSG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/glansectomy-stsg), and [Penile Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile).

---

## Evidence-Bounded Role

The published report describes transfer of pedicled scrotal tissue for reconstruction after partial penectomy, with a later division stage.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The full primary source package, including its technical detail, patient selection, follow-up methods, and complication definitions, was not recovered in this audit. The page therefore does not specify flap design, vessel, timing, tissue dimensions, meatal construction, or postoperative care.

In penile cancer care, the current EAU guideline includes glansectomy with reconstruction among options for appropriately selected glans-confined disease, without prescribing a scrotal-flap technique or ranking it over graft or urethral-flap reconstruction.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Considerations

Scrotal tissue has distinct donor-site and recipient-site characteristics, including the possibility of hair-bearing transfer and the need for staged treatment. Whether those trade-offs are acceptable depends on tissue availability and quality, defect characteristics, urethral involvement, previous treatment, oncologic requirements, and the person’s priorities. No recovered comparative evidence establishes when this flap should be selected over another reconstructive option.

After oncologic reconstruction, assessment should include local recurrence, wound/flap healing, meatal or urethral symptoms, urinary and sexual function, appearance, quality of life, and need for further intervention. The surveillance schedule is determined by pathology and the responsible cancer team.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## What Is Not Established

- That this is the largest, best, or preferred flap-based neoglans procedure.
- A standard flap design, stage interval, depilation strategy, or postoperative regimen.
- A reliable rate of retraction prevention, potency, urinary function, flap loss, stenosis, or cosmetic satisfaction.
- Superiority or inferiority versus urethral flaps, graft reconstruction, or more radical surgery.
