# Testicular Reimplantation

Testicular reimplantation is an uncommon microsurgical reconstruction used in two distinct settings: replantation after traumatic complete avulsion/amputation and autotransplantation of a selected high intra-abdominal testis. These are specialist procedures, not routine alternatives to standard trauma care or conventional orchiopexy.

For condition-level context and limitations, see [Testicular Reimplantation](/docs/clinical-conditions/03g-genital-scrotal/testicular-reimplantation). Related pages: [Testicular Torsion](/docs/special-populations/05a-trauma-emergencies/testicular-torsion), [Genital / Scrotal Trauma](/docs/special-populations/05a-trauma-emergencies/genital-scrotal-trauma), and [Testicular Prosthesis](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/testicular-prosthesis).

## Contexts reported in the literature

### Traumatic replantation

Published traumatic replantations are rare case reports. A 2018 review identified only eight reported traumatic testicular replantations; it distinguishes testicular replantation from other fertility-preservation approaches and describes substantial uncertainty about functional outcomes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The American College of Surgeons guideline emphasizes preservation where feasible, with individualized assessment of tissue/vessel injury, ischemia, and available microsurgical capability.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Autotransplantation for a high intra-abdominal testis

Microvascular autotransplantation has been described for selected high intra-abdominal testes when conventional options cannot achieve a satisfactory result. Historical specialist series report anatomical survival, but their small size, era, referral patterns, and outcome definitions limit generalization.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The operative record should not be read as an endorsement over primary orchiopexy or one-/two-stage Fowler–Stephens approaches.

## Interpretation limits

- Published evidence consists mainly of case reports and small historical single-center series.
- The sources do not establish a universal ischemia threshold, vessel choice, cooling method, microsurgical suture protocol, anticoagulation regimen, postoperative pathway, or expected fertility outcome.
- Anatomic viability does not by itself establish endocrine function, spermatogenesis, future fertility, or durable patient-reported benefit.
- Cryopreserved testicular-tissue transplantation is a separate experimental fertility-preservation field; it is not established clinical reimplantation.
- Decisions require an experienced multidisciplinary microsurgical team and should reflect anatomy, tissue viability, timing, alternatives, patient/family goals, and local capability.
