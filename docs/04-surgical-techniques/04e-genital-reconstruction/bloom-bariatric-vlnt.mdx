# BLOOM — Bariatric Lymphedema One-Stage Operative Management

**BLOOM** is a reported single-anesthetic combination of laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy for obesity and vascularized gastroepiploic lymph node transfer (VLNT) for lymphedema. The 2025 report describes one 44-year-old woman with a BMI of 35 and secondary lower-limb lymphedema, in whom gastroepiploic nodes from the sleeve gastrectomy were repurposed as the node flap. She recovered uneventfully with improvement in BMI and lymphedema, and the authors describe the approach as a double-pronged treatment. This is an early feasibility observation. It establishes neither an indication nor a benefit over staged treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The reported patient had limb lymphedema, and no genital case has been described. For established assessment and treatment principles, see [Genital Lymphedema](/docs/clinical-conditions/03g-genital-scrotal/genital-lymphedema), [Complex Decongestive Therapy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/complex-decongestive-therapy), and [Vascularized Lymph Node Transfer](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vascularized-lymph-node-transfer). Related pedicled options are [3R / SCIP-LFT](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/3r-scip-lft), [CHASCIP](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/chascip) and [Complete Functional Lymphatic-System Pedicled Transfer](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/complete-functional-lymphatic-pedicled-transfer). Excision or liposuction of the solid component is covered in [SAPL / Liposuction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/sapl-liposuction), and early physiologic surgery in [LVA](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/lymphaticovenous-anastomosis).

## Reported concept and donor anatomy

The authors reported improvement after simultaneous bariatric surgery and VLNT. The publisher's supplementary-video description identifies the harvested nodes as left gastroepiploic. Right gastroepiploic artery dimensions and station-4d node maps from other donor studies describe different harvests and are not the anatomy of this operation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Sleeve gastrectomy resects the greater curvature, which carries the gastroepiploic vascular arcade and perigastric station-4d nodes. The BLOOM concept uses that tissue as a vascularized node flap instead of discarding it.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Whether a usable pedicle can be preserved on the resected side is the technical question, because the vessels are divided during the gastrectomy. If the two procedures are considered together, flap design, pedicle preservation, recipient vessels and ischemia management need advance coordination between the bariatric and reconstructive teams. A usable vascularized flap cannot be assumed to remain in an otherwise routinely divided gastric specimen. Shared access does not establish zero additional dissection, donor morbidity or perioperative risk.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Gastroepiploic VLNT anatomy and evidence

The omental gastroepiploic flap is a widely used VLNT donor. Laparoscopic harvest with ICG guidance has been described.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> The anatomic data below come from standalone gastroepiploic harvests.

| Anatomic finding | Value |
|---|---|
| Right gastroepiploic artery diameter at origin (CT angiography, 34 patients) | 2.49 ± 0.66 mm<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Distance from artery origin to the most proximal node | 3.99 ± 2.21 cm<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Nodes per patient in the artery's lymphosome | 2.7 ± 2.12; each node within 7.00 ± 6.2 mm of the artery<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Mean station-4d nodes by omental third (proximal, middle, distal) | Cadaver 2.5, 1.4, 0.5; gastrectomy specimens 4.9, 2.7, 0.7<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Share of nodes in the proximal two-thirds from the vessel origin | 95%<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Flap in eight clinical VLNT cases | 25.5 ± 6.9 × 4.1 ± 0.7 cm with 6.5 ± 1.9 nodes<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |

The proximal third held significantly more nodes than the distal third in cadavers (p = 0.024) and gastrectomy specimens (p = 0.016), and the authors recommend including the proximal two-thirds of the flap.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> These specimens came from early gastric cancer resections with lymphadenectomy, not from sleeve gastrectomy for obesity.

| Study | Design and n | Reported outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Brown 2022<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | Prospective, 89 patients (omentum 73%) | At 2 years: 20% mean limb-volume reduction, 93% reduction in cellulitis, 34% no longer needed compression; no donor-site lymphedema |
| Schaverien 2021<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | Prospective, 134 patients (21 omental or right gastroepiploic) | At 24 months: 45.7% mean limb-volume change, 97.9% reduction in cellulitis; no flap loss; outcomes similar across flap types |
| Elia 2022<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> | Retrospective case-control, 89 patients, single vs double gastroepiploic transfer | Circumference reduction at 12 months was higher with double inset (above knee 72.2% vs 70.6%; below knee 61.6% vs 59.1%; p &lt; 0.05); cellulitis reduction did not differ |
| Grünherz 2024<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | Prospective, 70 patients (49 gastroepiploic) | Mean volume loss 9% after gastroepiploic VLNT, with no significant difference between donor sites; improved patient-reported outcomes |
| Eaimkijkarn 2026<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | Retrospective, 12 patients (14 limbs), laparoscopic omental | Mean 29% circumference reduction at 27 months; 93% flap survival; recurrent lymphangitis resolved |
| Shah 2026<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> | Meta-analysis, 395 patients, lower-limb VLNT | Circumference reduction 24.79% above and 29.50% below the knee, higher with gastroepiploic flaps (p = 0.002 and p &lt; 0.001); adverse events 12.22% |

Brown and colleagues concluded that the omentum "does not have any donor site lymphedema risk," which makes it an attractive first choice, and Shah and colleagues concluded that gastroepiploic flaps should be the most used donor. Both conclusions rest on observational or retrospective data.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> Reductions in observed cellulitis episodes are clinical outcomes, not proof of complete immune restoration. An intra-abdominal donor avoids harvesting nodes from a limb-draining basin, and it adds abdominal, vascular and flap risks of its own.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Obesity management remains important

Obesity can contribute to lymphatic dysfunction and complicate swelling control. Obesity-induced lymphedema may occur once BMI exceeds 40, and lymphatic dysfunction is almost universal once BMI exceeds 60. Excess adipose tissue is proposed to compress lymphatic vessels, reduce collecting-vessel pumping and cause lymph node hypoplasia and fibrosis, and impaired lymph uptake is proposed to drive further adipose deposition. Weight reduction is described as the fundamental treatment for obesity-induced lymphedema.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Established lymphatic damage and fibrosis may persist after weight loss. Animal evidence for improved pumping or inflammation does not prove that bariatric surgery restores normal human lymphatic function.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The WISER Survivor randomized trial assigned 351 overweight women with breast cancer-related arm lymphedema to weight loss, home exercise, both, or control care. Weight loss was about 7 to 8% in the weight-loss groups, and it did not improve lymphedema outcomes at 12 months. All groups received lymphedema care. This finding neither tests BLOOM nor determines whether obesity-related leg or genital disease benefits from a particular operation.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Selection and counseling

Each component needs an independent clinical indication. BMI, ISL stage or a history of pelvic cancer treatment does not by itself establish a BLOOM indication. Assess the cause of swelling, fibrosis, remaining lymphatic pathways, conservative-treatment response, bariatric eligibility, nutritional status, previous abdominal surgery and the patient's priorities with the relevant specialists.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Discuss the alternatives: continued conservative and obesity treatment, separate operations when indicated, lymphatic bypass where suitable collectors remain, and excision or liposuction for selected solid tissue burden. A combined operation may avoid a second anesthetic, but comparative complication rates, total recovery burden and long-term benefit remain unproven.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The index patient had a BMI of 35, at the lower end of bariatric-surgery indications, and most VLNT series enroll patients with lower BMI. Whether VLNT works equally well at BMI 35 or above is unknown, because obesity itself impairs lymphatic function and could blunt the neolymphangiogenic response.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Two design points need separate answers: the viability of nodes taken from a resected specimen after warm ischemia, and whether enough artery and vein length can be kept for microanastomosis.

## Other combined-harvest concepts

Lymphatic tissue has also been taken during a concurrent reconstruction so that no separate donor site is needed. Yoshimatsu and colleagues raised a lymphatic SCIP flap from the zone 4 region of the abdomen during DIEP breast reconstruction in seven women, and all flaps survived.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> Saaristo and colleagues used a modified lower abdominal flap containing nodes and vessels around the superficial circumflex pedicle in nine women with lymphedema who were having breast reconstruction, and the upper-limb perimeter decreased in seven of nine.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> Both are small series in breast cancer-related lymphedema.

## Outcomes to document

Record weight change separately from limb volume or circumference, symptoms and quality of life, cellulitis episodes, compression needs, subsequent procedures, recipient and donor complications, and duration of follow-up. Without a comparative design, improvement after combined treatment cannot be apportioned to weight loss, node transfer or other interventions.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Open questions include specimen-handling protocols during the transition from gastrectomy to node harvest, the optimal BMI range, and a comparison with GLP-1 receptor agonist therapy plus VLNT.
