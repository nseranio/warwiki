# Upper Tract Reconstruction

Upper tract reconstruction is the set of operations used to restore unobstructed drainage from the renal pelvis to the bladder while preserving renal function. In practice, the main variables are location, defect length, ischemic burden, etiology, and the salvageability of the ipsilateral renal unit. Short healthy defects tolerate direct repair; long or hostile segments require grafts, bowel, contralateral drainage, or renal relocation.

## Decision Framework

Successful ureteral reconstruction depends on a small set of universal principles regardless of technique: adequate debridement of devitalized tissue, preservation of periureteral blood supply, a tension-free, watertight, spatulated mucosa-to-mucosa anastomosis, ureteral stenting, and retroperitoneal drainage. Two variables drive technique selection — stricture location (UPJ / proximal · mid · distal) and defect length (short ≤ 2–3 cm · moderate 3–8 cm · long &gt; 8 cm). **Buccal mucosa graft (BMG) ureteroplasty** can avoid bowel replacement in selected strictures with a suitable ureteral plate and vascular bed. A 2023 review pooled separate single-arm oral-graft and ileal-ureter cohorts with very different mean stricture lengths (3.73 versus 11.55 cm); its percentages do not establish comparative superiority. Use the [BMG hub](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/bmg-onlay-ureter) for current cohort outcomes and limits.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Modifying Factors

| Factor | Impact on Technique Selection |
|---|---|
| **Prior pelvic radiation** | Assess bladder capacity, compliance, mobility and perfusion before a bladder flap; choose among reimplantation, graft repair, bowel or [autotransplantation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/renal-autotransplantation) |
| **Small / contracted bladder** | Psoas hitch and Boari flap may not be feasible; consider bowel interposition |
| **Bilateral ureteral disease** | Avoid [TUU](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/trans-ureteroureterostomy); consider bilateral reconstruction or ileal ureter |
| **Impaired renal function** | Assess recoverability and metabolic reserve; bowel interposition adds acid-load risk. A cohort-derived eGFR 40 threshold is not a universal rule |
| **Right-sided stricture with intact appendix** | Consider [appendiceal onlay / interposition](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/appendiceal-ileal-onlay) |
| **Failed prior reconstruction** | Reassess anatomy and cause of failure; select revision, graft, bowel or autotransplantation accordingly |
| **Solitary kidney** | Prioritize renal preservation and durable drainage; there is no functioning contralateral unit for conventional TUU, and bowel safety still requires individual assessment |
| **Unstable patient / contaminated field** | Damage control: percutaneous nephrostomy + delayed reconstruction |
| **Buccal mucosa unavailable / inadequate** | [Lingual mucosal graft](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/bmg-onlay-ureter) (ureter) or appendiceal onlay |

### Reconstructive Options

The following are options and adjuncts and do not form a mandatory sequence or validated morbidity ranking. Length ranges are approximate; perfusion, bladder function, tissue quality and recoverable renal function can change the choice.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

1. **Endoscopic management:** [endoureterotomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/endoureterotomy) or balloon dilation for short, non-ischemic, non-irradiated strictures
2. **Primary anastomosis:** [ureteroureterostomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/ureteroureterostomy), [reimplantation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/ureteral-reimplantation), or [pyeloplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/pyeloplasty)
3. **Bladder-mobilization adjuncts:** psoas hitch, then [Boari flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/boari-flap-psoas-hitch)
4. **Adjunctive maneuvers:** [downward nephropexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/downward-nephropexy) (gains 3–5 cm of length; used in 19.6% of complex reconstructions in one single-center series)<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
5. **Tissue grafting:** [BMG onlay](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/bmg-onlay-ureter), lingual mucosa, or [appendiceal onlay](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/appendiceal-ileal-onlay)
6. **Bowel interposition:** Yang-Monti or classic [ileal ureter](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/ileal-ureter)
7. **Cross-drainage:** [transureteroureterostomy (TUU)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/trans-ureteroureterostomy)
8. **[Renal autotransplantation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/renal-autotransplantation)**: extensive ureteral loss when vascular anatomy and renal reserve make this preferable to other feasible repairs
9. **Nephrectomy:** last resort when ipsilateral renal function does not warrant reconstruction or all reconstructive options have failed
