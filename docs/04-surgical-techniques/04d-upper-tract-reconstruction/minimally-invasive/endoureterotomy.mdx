# Endoureterotomy

Endoureterotomy is an endoscopic incision through the full thickness of a ureteral stricture — retrograde (ureteroscopic) or antegrade (percutaneous) — using a cold knife, scissors, holmium laser, or, historically, a cutting balloon such as the Acucise catheter. The incision is tailored to stricture location so that it opens into a well-vascularized extraureteric plane (lateral for the upper ureter, anteromedial for the mid ureter, anterior or anterolateral for the distal ureter, posterolateral at the UPJ to avoid crossing vessels), then a stent is left across the defect for 6–8 weeks to allow re-epithelialization over an open lumen.

Durable success is tightly tied to patient selection: short (&lt;1 cm), non-ischemic, non-irradiated strictures with preserved ipsilateral renal function — on the order of 60–80% when those criteria are met. Long strictures, ischemic or radiation-damaged segments, strictures with a poorly functioning kidney, and most malignant strictures fall off sharply and are better served by formal reconstruction. Endoureterotomy is therefore best understood as a low-morbidity salvage or bridging step rather than a substitute for open, laparoscopic, or robotic ureteral reconstruction.
