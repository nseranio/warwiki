# Balloon Dilation of the Upper Tract

**Non-drug-coated balloon dilation** is a well-established minimally invasive endourologic technique for managing benign upper urinary tract strictures — including ureteral strictures, ureteropelvic junction (UPJ) obstruction, and ureteroenteric anastomotic strictures. Pooled data show **technical success ~89%** with **moderate long-term patency ~54% at 6–12 months**. The procedure is best suited to **short strictures (≤2 cm) of recent onset (≤3 months) with intact vascular supply**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For drug-coated (paclitaxel) balloons, see [Drug-Coated Balloon Therapy](/docs/surgical-techniques/drug-coated-balloon). For sharp endoscopic incision, see [Endoureterotomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/endoureterotomy).

---

## Indications

| Scenario | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Benign ureteral strictures** | Iatrogenic injury, ureteroscopy, radiation, or inflammatory.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **UPJ obstruction** | Primary or recurrent after failed pyeloplasty.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Ureteroenteric anastomotic strictures** | After radical cystectomy with ileal conduit, neobladder, or continent pouch.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **Post-surgical anastomotic strictures** | After pyeloplasty, ureteral reimplantation, or ureteroneocystostomy.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |

---

## Technique

Performed via either a **retrograde** (cystoscopic / ureteroscopic) or **antegrade** (percutaneous nephrostomy) approach under fluoroscopic guidance.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

| Parameter | Typical setting |
|---|---|
| **Balloon type** | Semicompliant or high-pressure; profiles 5–7 mm in pediatric cases, larger in adults.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **Inflation pressure** | **10–20 atm**, sustained for 5–15 min.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Post-dilation drainage** | Double-J ureteral stent or nephrostomy for **4–8 weeks** on average; some protocols extend to several months.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Standardization** | No consensus on optimal balloon type, pressure, number of inflations, or stent retention time.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |

---

## Efficacy by Scenario

| Clinical scenario | Short-term (3–6 mo) | Long-term (≥12 mo) |
|---|---|---|
| Benign ureteral strictures (pooled meta) | 60% | **54%**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Short strictures (≤2 cm), intact vascularity | **89%** | —<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Long strictures (\>2 cm) | 37.5% | —<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Primary UPJ obstruction | 86–95% (3 mo) | 71–76% (24 mo)<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Recurrent UPJ after pyeloplasty | 73% (3 mo) | 53% (12 mo)<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Ureteroenteric strictures | 61% (6 mo) | **16–27%** (12–60 mo)<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| Acute strictures (≤3 mo onset) | 88% | —<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |

---

## Predictors of Outcome

**Favorable factors:**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- Stricture length **≤2 cm**.
- **Early intervention** (≤3 months from onset).
- **Intact vascular supply** (no prior radiation, no devascularizing dissection).
- **Primary** rather than recurrent stricture.

**Failure risk factors:**<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- **Multiple strictures** (independent predictor on multivariate analysis).
- **Compromised vascular supply** (post-radiation, ischemic).
- **Long strictures**.
- **Prior failed repair**.
- **Smaller balloon circumference**.

---

## Comparison With Surgical Reconstruction

| Parameter | Balloon Dilation | Laparoscopic / Robotic Pyeloplasty |
|---|---|---|
| Operative time | ~67 min | 150–178 min<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| Blood loss | Minimal | Greater |
| Length of stay | ~3 d | 5–7 d |
| Cost | Lower | Higher |
| Long-term patency | Inferior | Significantly better at 12 and 24 mo<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |

For ureteroenteric strictures, **open surgical revision** achieves **69%** patency at 60 months versus only **27%** for endourologic approaches.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

---

## Adjunctive Techniques

- **Balloon dilation + endoureterotomy** — higher success than dilation alone (87% vs 78% at 1 yr for lower-ureteral strictures).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Laser incision + triamcinolone injection followed by balloon dilation** — **83% long-term success** for ureteroenteric strictures.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Acucise balloon catheter** (combined electrocautery incision + dilation) — 75% success for post-reimplantation strictures.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

---

## Clinical Role

Balloon dilation is best positioned as a **first-line minimally invasive option** for selected patients — short, acute, primary strictures with intact vascularity — and as a **temporizing or alternative approach** in patients unfit for open reconstruction. **Re-dilation is feasible** for recurrence; most recurrences develop within the first 12 months, mandating long-term surveillance.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
