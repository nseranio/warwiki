# Downward Nephropexy

**Downward nephropexy** is the deliberate caudal mobilization and fixation of the kidney as an **adjunctive maneuver during upper-ureteral reconstruction** to gain effective ureteral length and bridge a defect that would otherwise be too long for tension-free repair. It is conceptually opposite to the historical (upward) nephropexy performed for symptomatic nephroptosis — here the kidney is intentionally displaced **lower** than its native position.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

This is the dedicated atlas page for the reach-extending maneuver. For the bladder-flap and psoas-hitch operations it is most often paired with, see [Boari Flap & Psoas Hitch](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/boari-flap-psoas-hitch). For the upper-tract reconstruction decision matrix, see [Upper Tract Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction). For long-segment salvage when reach is inadequate, see [Ileal Ureter](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/ileal-ureter) and [Renal Autotransplantation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/renal-autotransplantation).

---

## Concept

The kidney is mobilized completely from Gerota's fascia, perirenal fat, and peritoneal reflections, then **moved caudally** and secured to the psoas muscle or posterior abdominal wall. The caudal shift **shortens the distance from the renal pelvis (or ureteropelvic junction) to the bladder or distal ureteral stump**, producing a tension-free anastomosis that would not otherwise be feasible.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| **Caudal displacement achieved** | Mean **3.3 cm** (range 3–5 cm)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Use frequency in series** | ~**19.6%** of complex upper-tract reconstructions<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Approach** | Open 83% / Laparoscopic 17%; robotic increasingly described<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |

---

## Indications

Downward nephropexy is reach-extending — used when ureteral length is insufficient for a tension-free anastomosis. Specific scenarios in the largest series:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

| Setting | Frequency of nephropexy use |
|---|---|
| **Ureterocalicostomy** | **71%** of cases (Hofer)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Ureteroureterostomy** | **33%** |
| **Boari flap** | **31%** (and 58% of proximal-stricture Boari reconstructions vs 12% of distal in Mauck)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Ileal ureter interposition** | **8%** |
| **Iatrogenic ureteral avulsion / extensive loss** | Reported up to 15-cm avulsions salvaged with combined nephropexy + appendiceal interposition + psoas hitch<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Redo reconstruction** | **~ two-thirds** of patients had a prior failed reconstruction<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |

Most useful at the **proximal ureter** — paired with Boari flap, ureterocalicostomy, or ureteroureterostomy when the bladder side has been maximally mobilized and additional length is still needed from above.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Surgical Technique

1. **Complete renal mobilization.** Free the kidney from Gerota's fascia, perirenal fat, and peritoneal reflections circumferentially. Preserve the renal hilum and vascular pedicle — gentle handling of the renal artery and vein during mobilization is the critical safety step.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
2. **Caudal displacement.** Translocate the kidney inferiorly. Mean achievable shift is ~3.3 cm (3–5 cm).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
3. **Fixation (nephropexy).** Secure the kidney in its new lower position to the psoas muscle or posterior abdominal wall with **nonabsorbable sutures**, taking care not to compromise the vascular pedicle.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
4. **Concomitant ureteral reconstruction.** Complete the chosen reach-dependent operation (Boari flap, ureteroureterostomy, ureterocalicostomy, appendiceal onlay, or ileal ureter) with the additional length the nephropexy has provided.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Outcomes

| Series | n | Setting | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hofer 2016**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 18 | Mixed reach-dependent reconstructions | **88.9% ureteral patency** at mean 50.4 mo (range 3–87 mo); 2 patients (11.1%) → nephrectomy for failed reconstruction with persistent symptomatic hydronephrosis |
| **Mauck 2011**<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | 20 (Boari flap) | Proximal vs distal strictures with nephropexy | Failure 17% proximal vs 12% distal — comparable durability, but proximal cases had complication rate 75% vs 35%, hospital stay 8.0 vs 4.4 days, EBL 447 vs 224 mL |
| **Gn 2018**<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | 1 | Robotic appendiceal interposition + lower-pole ureterocalycostomy + downward nephropexy + psoas hitch for 15-cm iatrogenic avulsion | Stable renal function and no obstruction at 6 mo |

Erectile and continence outcomes are not directly relevant — the principal trade-offs are renal-vascular safety and the operative complexity that nephropexy signals.

---

## Complications and Considerations

- **Higher complication burden in proximal reconstructions** that depend on nephropexy compared with distal reconstructions — longer operations, greater blood loss, longer hospital stay.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Vascular pedicle injury** during full circumferential renal mobilization is the principal intraoperative risk; gentle handling and preservation of the renal artery and vein are mandatory.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Renal ischemia** can result from kinking or stretching of the pedicle when the kidney is repositioned — confirm reperfusion after fixation.
- **Failure of fixation** with cranial migration over time is uncommon but possible; nonabsorbable suture fixation to the psoas / posterior abdominal wall is standard.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- The presence of nephropexy is a **marker of a higher-complexity reconstruction** rather than an independent failure driver — Mauck and Hofer both report comparable patency with and without it once the reach problem is solved.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Distinction from Traditional (Upward) Nephropexy

The historical operation called "nephropexy" was performed for **symptomatic nephroptosis** — a kidney that descends > 5 cm during orthostasis and produces flank pain or intermittent obstruction. In that operation, the kidney is fixed **upward**, into its normal anatomic position, against the posterior abdominal wall or psoas.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

**Downward nephropexy is the conceptual opposite** — the kidney is intentionally displaced **caudally** and fixed below its native position to facilitate ureteral reconstruction. The two operations share the technique of renal mobilization plus fixation but differ in direction, indication, and reconstructive context.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Place in the Reach-Extension Toolkit

For a defect that exceeds primary tension-free anastomosis, the reconstructive options are stacked from least to most morbid:<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

1. **Bladder mobilization**
2. **Psoas hitch**
3. **Boari flap** (± psoas hitch)
4. **Downward nephropexy** (gains 3–5 cm; ~19.6% of series)
5. **Transureteroureterostomy** (selected cases)
6. **Bowel interposition** — appendiceal onlay (right) or ileal ureter
7. **Renal autotransplantation** — when anatomy precludes bowel

Downward nephropexy is most useful as the **fourth-line reach maneuver** before committing to bowel interposition or autotransplantation, and is particularly valuable in **redo reconstructions** where prior surgery has already foreshortened the available ureter.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Key Principles

- Downward nephropexy is a **reach-extending adjunct**, not a primary reconstruction — it gains a mean ~3.3 cm (range 3–5 cm) of effective ureteral length.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Most often paired with **Boari flap, ureterocalicostomy, or ureteroureterostomy**; less commonly with bowel interposition.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- ~**Two-thirds of patients** in the largest series were undergoing **redo reconstruction** — nephropexy is a salvage maneuver in foreshortened anatomy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Hofer 2016: **88.9% ureteral patency** at mean 50 months; 11% nephrectomy for failed reconstruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- The presence of nephropexy **signals operative complexity** (longer time, greater EBL, higher complication rate) but does not itself drive failure once reach is achieved.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Vascular-pedicle preservation** during mobilization is the principal safety issue; nonabsorbable fixation to psoas / posterior abdominal wall is standard.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Conceptually the **opposite** of the upward nephropexy used historically for nephroptosis — same anatomic operation, opposite direction and indication.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
