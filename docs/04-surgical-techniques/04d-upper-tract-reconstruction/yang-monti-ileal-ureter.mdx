# Yang-Monti Ileal Ureter

The **Yang-Monti ileal ureter** detubularizes short ileal segments and retubularizes them transversely into a narrow conduit. The original series used 5–7.5 cm of ileum, while a seven-patient whole-ureter series used five or six 2.5-cm segments. In the largest series, the median defect length was 11 cm (range 8–16).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[11]](#ref11)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> The reconfiguration trades bowel diameter for length, designed to reduce several drawbacks of the classic [ileal ureter](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/ileal-ureter): excessive mucosal surface area, mucus burden, metabolic absorption, and the difficulty of fashioning a nonrefluxing reimplant from a wide bowel tube.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Related: see [Yang-Monti Channel](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/yang-monti) for the catheterizable-channel application of the same retubularization principle.

---

## Lineage

The Yang-Monti retubularization principle is also used for continent catheterizable channels. Ali-el-Dein and Ghoneim reported its use to bridge long ureteral defects in 2003.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The principle:

1. **Isolate** a short ileal segment (5–7.5 cm) on its mesentery.
2. **Subdivide** the segment into 2 or 3 equal parts.
3. **Open** each sub-segment longitudinally along a paramesenteric line, preserving the vascular pedicle.
4. **Unfold** and lay the opened segments side by side as a flat ileal strip 12–18 cm long.
5. **Retubularize** the strip transversely over a stent into a narrow tube of approximately ureteral caliber.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Design Rationale

| Drawback of classic ileal ureter | How Yang-Monti addresses it |
|---|---|
| Long bowel harvest (15–25 cm) → metabolic / nutritional burden | The original series used 5–7.5 cm for a 12–18 cm substitute; a seven-patient whole-ureter series generally used five or six 2.5-cm segments (12.5–15 cm of ileum).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| Wide caliber requires tapering | Retubularized tube has near-ureteral diameter; tapering not required.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Large mucosal surface → mucus obstruction | The smaller urine-exposed mucosal area is intended to reduce mucus production.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis | Smaller absorptive surface. No metabolic complications were reported in the largest long-term series.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| More difficult nonrefluxing reimplant | Narrow caliber permits submucosal-tunnel ileovesicostomy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Bowel length limits combinability | Short segment combines well with a Boari flap or psoas hitch.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

---

## Indications

The Yang-Monti ileal ureter is used when ureteroureterostomy, a Boari flap with psoas hitch, transureteroureterostomy, or a suitable oral-mucosal or appendiceal repair cannot bridge the defect or has failed.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> In a 2026 systematic review, oral-mucosal grafts were used mostly for strictures &lt;5 cm and appendiceal techniques for defects up to 7.5 cm; heterogeneous studies precluded meta-analysis.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> In the 36-patient Yang-Monti series, defects measured 8–16 cm.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

| Etiology | Frequency / notes |
|---|---|
| **Iatrogenic strictures** | Most common; 16/36 (44%) in the largest series.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Bilharzial (schistosomal) strictures** | <sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Tuberculous strictures** | <sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Retroperitoneal fibrosis** | <sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Radiation strictures** | Reported in a seven-patient Yang-Monti total-replacement series and a separate conventional ileal-ureter cohort.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Traumatic ureteral injury** (incl. gunshot) | <sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Malignancy-related ureteral loss** | <sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Failed prior pyeloplasty or reimplant** | Particularly pediatric.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |

## Patient Selection and Contraindications

- **Renal reserve.** For long-segment ureteral injury, the 2026 EAU trauma guideline says to avoid intestinal interposition in patients with impaired renal function or known intestinal disease; in a 36-patient Yang-Monti series, Ali-El-Dein et al. proposed eGFR &lt;40 mL/min/1.73 m² as a relative contraindication.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Preoperative mapping and function.** A seven-patient Yang-Monti total-replacement series included serum creatinine and split renal function in its preoperative assessment and placed nephrostomy tubes before reconstruction in all seven patients; a 46-patient conventional ileal-ureter series used simultaneous antegrade and retrograde urograms to define stricture length and location.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Selected patients with CKD or a solitary kidney** had favorable results, but 4 of 36 patients experienced renal-function deterioration.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Bowel suitability.** Prior extensive bowel resection, inflammatory bowel disease, or severe radiation enteritis may make ileal harvest unsuitable; these were contraindications or relative contraindications in a conventional ileal-ureter cohort and require assessment before Yang-Monti reconstruction.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Bladder storage before ileovesical implantation.** Assess capacity and compliance when impairment is suspected. Severe bladder dysfunction was a relative contraindication in one 46-patient conventional ileal-ureter cohort; its thresholds are not validated for Yang-Monti reconstruction.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Surgical Technique

Open and laparoscopic Yang-Monti ileal ureter reconstruction have been reported.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

1. **Bowel isolation.** Match the ileal harvest to the defect: the original series used 5–7.5 cm, whereas a seven-patient total-ureter series used five or six 2.5-cm segments. Preserve the terminal ileum and ileocecal valve where possible and retain the mesenteric pedicle. Restore bowel continuity; a later four-case series used a stapled side-to-side anastomosis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[11]](#ref11)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
2. **Subdivision.** The original series divided the harvest into two (double Monti) or three (triple Monti) equal parts. Whole-ureter replacement has used five or six 2.5-cm segments; choose segment count for the required conduit length.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
3. **Detubularization.** The original series incised each segment along a paramesenteric line; a later four-case series described an antimesenteric incision. Specify the chosen construction while preserving the segmental vascular pedicle.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
4. **Reconfiguration.** Lay the opened segments side by side and suture them into a continuous flat strip.
5. **Retubularization.** Tubularize the strip over a sizing catheter to form the neoureter. In a four-case report, the double- and triple-Monti operative figures show a 16 Fr Foley catheter for this step.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
6. **Proximal anastomosis.** Anastomose the neoureter to a spatulated proximal ureteral stump when available. A renal pelvic opening can be used for ileopyelostomy in total replacement; ureterocalicostomy has been described for selected UPJ-level defects.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
7. **Distal anastomosis.** Anastomose to a usable distal ureteral stump or implant into the bladder when the distal ureter is unavailable. The original series used a submucosal tunnel for bladder implantation; a later series also described a split-cuff bladder implant. Select the route according to reach, bladder characteristics, and obstruction risk.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
8. **Stenting.** Internal ureteral stenting is used, with removal timed to the repair. In a seven-patient total-replacement series, the authors inserted two JJ stents into the ileal tube and generally retained them for more than 6 weeks, or more than 8 weeks after radiotherapy; antegrade pyelography confirmed patency after stent removal and before nephrostomy removal.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
9. **Leak check and drainage.** A four-case series tested the completed urinary anastomoses with instillation through the bladder and nephrostomy and placed an operative drain; a seven-patient total-replacement series used pelvic drainage to detect urinary or enteric leakage.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
10. **Orientation.** Transverse retubularization changes the native bowel axis; route the neoureter dependently without tension, kinking, or mesenteric torsion while preserving the segmental blood supply.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Variations

- **Onlay technique.** When the native ureteral plate is preserved, the reconfigured strip is used as an onlay rather than full circumferential replacement, which preserves native ureteral tissue.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Segment-by-segment assembly.** A four-case series described retubularizing separate 2–3 cm ileal segments over a Foley catheter and joining the tubes end to end for multi-segment repairs.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Double Monti with sigmoid colon.** Has been described for total ureteral substitution combined with a Boari flap.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Antireflux Considerations

The narrow caliber permits submucosal-tunnel reimplantation. Reflux occurred in 1/10 patients in the original series after a mean 9.6 months of follow-up (range 6–13 months).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> A separate conventional ileal-ureter series discusses antireflux implantation into intestinal reservoirs. Nipple-valve use is not mandatory for every Yang-Monti repair; balance reflux, reservoir pressure and anastomotic obstruction risk.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Long-Term Outcomes

Largest dedicated Yang-Monti series (Ali-El-Dein 2021, n = 36, median follow-up 68 months, range 12–215):<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

| Parameter | Result |
|---|---|
| Renal function | Significant improvement in serum creatinine, split renographic clearance, and eGFR |
| Hydronephrosis | Improved in most patients on MR urography |
| Metabolic complications | None reported |
| UTI | 28% (10/36)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Intestinal obstruction | 2.8% (1/36)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Perioperative complications | Clavien-Dindo grades 1–2 only |
| Reflux (with submucosal reimplant) | ~10% (1/10) historically<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Renal deterioration | 4/36 patients; renal-function change is distinct from a defined patency endpoint<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |

Overall renal function improved, including in selected CKD and solitary-kidney subgroups; 4 patients with stage 3 CKD deteriorated. CKD subgroup labels are inconsistent within the published abstract, which limits any stage-by-stage safety rule.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Follow renal function and imaging for obstruction after reconstruction. No metabolic complications were reported in the 36-patient Yang-Monti series, while hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis occurred in 4/108 patients in a separate conventional ileal-ureter cohort; monitor serum electrolytes and bicarbonate without treating that rate as Yang-Monti-specific.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

---

## Pediatric Application

Laparoscopic Yang-Monti ureteral reconstruction was reported in 6 children (mean age 8.5 yr, mean defect 5.83 cm), most after failed pyeloplasty. All patients showed improvement of differential renal function without obstruction on diuretic renography; mean OR time 314 min, mean blood loss 25 mL. The series also reported 2 grade III complications requiring stent replacement, in addition to grade I–II events.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

---

## Comparison With Conventional Ileal Ureter

Yang-Monti reconstruction uses a shorter bowel harvest and a narrower tube, at the cost of additional bowel suture lines. Published series are small and differ from conventional ileal-ureter cohorts in selection, anatomy and follow-up. These small, unmatched series cannot determine comparative patency or bowel morbidity, or exclude metabolic risk.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Complications

- **Urinary tract infection.** About 28%, the most common complication.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Bowel obstruction and ileus.** Intestinal obstruction occurred in 1/36 in the dedicated long-term series; postoperative or delayed ileus occurred in 2/7 after Yang-Monti total-ureter replacement.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> The 8.3% small-bowel-obstruction rate in a separate conventional ileal-ureter cohort is not a direct comparison.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[11]](#ref11)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Anastomotic stricture.** In a seven-patient Yang-Monti total-replacement series, 1/7 developed proximal ileal-pelvic stenosis requiring reanastomosis at 11 months; in a separate five-patient complete-substitution series, 1/5 required permanent double-J stenting for stricture at mean 41-month follow-up.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> A conventional ileal-ureter cohort reported anastomotic stricture in 4/108 patients (3.7%).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Urinary leak or fistula.** In a five-patient Yang-Monti complete-substitution series, 3/5 had immediate proximal anastomotic leakage requiring drainage, prolonged stenting, or nephrostomy.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> A separate mixed reconfigured-intestine series reported one fistula in an irradiated patient (of 13).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Mucus obstruction.** The smaller urine-exposed mucosal area is intended to reduce a recognized complication of intestinal ureteral substitution; no mucus obstruction was reported among seven Yang-Monti total-replacement patients at median 36.7-month follow-up.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Renal-function deterioration.** 4/36 (11%) in the dedicated series, all with stage 3 CKD.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> The 36-patient Yang-Monti series reported renal deterioration in 4/36.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> In conventional ileal-ureter analyses from one institution, an earlier 108-patient cohort found a 3.7-fold bilateral-repair association, whereas a later 158-patient cohort found no significant association.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
