# Yang-Monti Ileal Ureter

The **Yang-Monti ileal ureter** uses a short segment of ileum (5–7.5 cm) **detubularized and retubularized transversely** to create a narrow-caliber conduit suitable for bridging long ureteral defects (8–18 cm). The reconfiguration trades bowel diameter for length, overcoming the principal drawbacks of the classic [ileal ureter](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/ileal-ureter): excessive mucosal surface area, mucus burden, metabolic absorption, and the difficulty of fashioning a nonrefluxing reimplant from a wide bowel tube.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Related: see [Yang-Monti Channel](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/yang-monti) for the catheterizable-channel application of the same retubularization principle.

---

## Lineage

The Monti principle was originally described for continent catheterizable channels. **Yang** adapted it to ureteral replacement, and **Ali-el-Dein and Ghoneim** refined and popularized it for long ureteral defects.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The core principle:

1. **Isolate** a short ileal segment (5–7.5 cm) on its mesentery.
2. **Subdivide** the segment into 2 or 3 equal parts.
3. **Open** each sub-segment longitudinally along the antimesenteric border.
4. **Unfold** and lay the opened segments side by side as a flat ileal strip 12–18 cm long.
5. **Retubularize** the strip transversely over a stent into a narrow tube of approximately ureteral caliber.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Advantages Over the Classic Ileal Ureter

| Drawback of classic ileal ureter | How Yang-Monti addresses it |
|---|---|
| Long bowel harvest (15–25 cm) → metabolic / nutritional burden | **5–7.5 cm** suffices.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| Wide caliber requires tapering | Retubularized tube has near-ureteral diameter; tapering not required.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Large mucosal surface → mucus obstruction | Smaller mucosal area → less mucus.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| Hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis | Smaller absorptive surface — **no metabolic complications** in the largest long-term series.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Difficult / impossible nonrefluxing reimplant | Narrow caliber permits **submucosal-tunnel ileovesicostomy**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Bowel length limits combinability | Short segment combines well with **Boari flap** or **psoas hitch**.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

---

## Indications

For **long ureteral defects (8–16 cm)** when simpler reconstructive options (ureteroureterostomy, Boari flap with psoas hitch, transureteroureterostomy) are insufficient or have failed.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

| Etiology | Frequency / notes |
|---|---|
| **Iatrogenic strictures** | Most common — ~44% of cases.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Bilharzial (schistosomal) strictures** | <sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Tuberculous strictures** | <sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Retroperitoneal fibrosis** | <sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Radiation strictures** | <sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Traumatic ureteral injury** (incl. gunshot) | <sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Malignancy-related ureteral loss** | <sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Failed prior pyeloplasty or reimplant** | Particularly pediatric.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |

---

## Patient Selection and Contraindications

- **Renal-function threshold.** Relative contraindication when **eGFR \<40 mL/min/1.73 m²** — patients with CKD stage 3b or worse showed deterioration of renal parameters in the Ali-El-Dein 2021 series.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Safe** in CKD stages 1–3a and in the solitary kidney with adequate function.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Prior bowel surgery or radiation enteritis** may limit usable ileum.

---

## Surgical Technique

Open, laparoscopic, or robotic.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

1. **Bowel isolation.** A 5–7.5 cm segment of terminal ileum is isolated on its mesenteric pedicle; restore bowel continuity with an end-to-end anastomosis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
2. **Subdivision.** Divide the isolated segment into **2 equal parts (double Monti)** or **3 parts (triple Monti)**, depending on required length.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
3. **Detubularization.** Open each sub-segment along its antimesenteric (paramesenteric) border.
4. **Reconfiguration.** Lay the opened segments side by side and suture them into a continuous flat strip.
5. **Retubularization.** Tubularize the strip over a stent / catheter (typically 8–10 Fr) into a narrow tube.
6. **Proximal anastomosis.** Spatulated end-to-end anastomosis to the renal pelvis or proximal ureteral stump.
7. **Distal reimplantation.** Reimplant the distal end into the bladder via a **submucosal tunnel** (nonrefluxing ileovesicostomy).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
8. **Stenting.** Internal ureteral stent for 4–6 weeks.
9. **Isoperistaltic orientation** is maintained to promote unidirectional flow.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Variations

- **Onlay technique.** When the native ureteral plate is preserved, the reconfigured strip is used as an **onlay** rather than full circumferential replacement — preserving native ureteral tissue.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Double Monti with sigmoid colon.** Has been described for total ureteral substitution combined with a Boari flap.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Antireflux Considerations

The narrow caliber is the structural reason **submucosal-tunnel reimplantation** is feasible — reflux occurred in only **1/10** patients in the original series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> When implantation is into an intestinal reservoir rather than the native bladder, antireflux constructs (e.g., a distal nipple-valve technique) are recommended; the requirement is less critical when the native bladder is the implantation site.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Long-Term Outcomes

Largest dedicated Yang-Monti series — Ali-El-Dein 2021, n = 36, median follow-up 68 months (range 12–215):<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

| Parameter | Result |
|---|---|
| Renal function | Significant improvement in serum creatinine, split renographic clearance, and eGFR |
| Hydronephrosis | Improved in most patients on MR urography |
| Metabolic complications | None reported |
| UTI | 28% (10/36) |
| Intestinal obstruction | 2.8% (1/36) |
| Perioperative complications | Clavien-Dindo grades 1–2 only |
| Reflux (with submucosal reimplant) | ~10% (1/10) historically<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Functional patency | Stable or improved split renal function in \>80%<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |

Renal-function improvement extended to patients with CKD stages 2–3a and to solitary kidneys; only 4 patients with CKD stage 3 deteriorated.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

---

## Pediatric Application

Laparoscopic Yang-Monti ureteral reconstruction has been performed successfully in children (mean age 8.5 yr, mean defect 5.83 cm), most after failed pyeloplasty. All patients showed improvement of differential renal function without obstruction on diuretic renography; mean OR time 314 min, mean blood loss 25 mL.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Yang-Monti vs Classic Ileal Ureter

| Feature | Yang-Monti | Classic Ileal Ureter |
|---|---|---|
| Bowel segment harvested | 5–7.5 cm | 15–25 cm |
| Conduit length | 12–18 cm | 15–25 cm |
| Conduit caliber | Narrow (ureteral-like) | Wide (bowel-caliber) |
| Tapering required | No | Often |
| Antireflux reimplantation | Feasible (submucosal tunnel) | Difficult / impossible |
| Mucus production | Minimal | Significant |
| Metabolic acidosis risk | Very low / absent | 3.7–4% |
| Bowel-related morbidity | Lower | Higher |
| Functional patency | \>80% | ~83% (no further open surgery) |

## Complications

- **Urinary tract infection** ~28% — most common.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Small-bowel obstruction** ~2.8% (vs 8.3% in classic ileal-ureter series).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Anastomotic stricture** — rare with Yang-Monti; 3.7% reported in classic ileal-ureter series.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Ureteral fistula** — reported in irradiated patients.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Mucus obstruction** — rare due to reduced mucosal surface.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Renal-function deterioration** — 4/36 (11%) in the largest series, primarily CKD stage 3; bilateral reconstruction carries a 3.7× increased risk of worsening renal function.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
