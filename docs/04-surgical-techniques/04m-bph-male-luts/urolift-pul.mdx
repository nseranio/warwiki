# UroLift — Prostatic Urethral Lift (PUL) for BPH

The **UroLift system** (Teleflex, originally NeoTract) is an FDA-approved minimally invasive treatment for LUTS secondary to BPH that uses permanent nitinol–stainless-steel implants to mechanically retract obstructive prostatic lobes and widen the urethral lumen without cutting, heating, or removing tissue.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Among BPH MISTs it is the implant-based option. Rezūm is thermal, Aquablation uses hydroablation, Optilume BPH is a drug-coated balloon, iTIND is a temporary nitinol device, and TURP and HoLEP resect or enucleate tissue. The AUA 2026 guideline states that clinicians should offer PUL for prostates of 30 to 80 cc without an obstructing median lobe (Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B).<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> For positioning against other BPH options, see [BPH & Male LUTS — chooser](/docs/surgical-techniques/bph-male-luts).

---

## Mechanism of action

Each UroLift implant is a double-ended hook with three components<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>:

- **Super-elastic nitinol capsular tab** (anchors on the prostatic capsule)
- **Polyethylene-terephthalate monofilament** (transcapsular tether)
- **Stainless-steel urethral end piece** (anchors on the urethral surface)

The delivery device deploys a fine needle through the prostatic lobe; the capsular tab anchors on the outer prostatic capsule and the urethral piece on the urethral surface. Tensioning compresses the obstructing lobe away from the urethra, opening the lumen.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Unlike the other BPH MISTs, PUL is not ablative, resective or drug-eluting, and the implants are permanent.

---

## Procedure

PUL is performed under local anesthesia with oral or IV sedation (general or spinal anesthesia is also used) and typically takes less than 1 hour<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>:

1. **20 Fr rigid cystoscope** with 0° lens.
2. The implant delivery device is angled anterolaterally to compress the obstructive lobe.
3. Approximately 4 implants are typically placed (2 per lateral lobe at the 2 and 10 o'clock positions); number adapted to anatomy. Multinational experience averaged 4.5 implants per case.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
4. Patients return to normal activity at a mean of 8.6 days.
5. Most patients do not require a postoperative catheter.

---

## Patient selection

The L.I.F.T. pivotal trial enrolled men who met these criteria<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>:

- Men ≥ 50 years
- IPSS ≥ 13
- Qmax ≤ 12 mL/s (some studies allowed ≤ 15 mL/s)
- Prostate volume 30–80 mL (original trial criterion); some real-world settings have treated up to 100 mL
- PVR &lt; 250 mL

### Median lobe — the MedLift extension

The original L.I.F.T. trial excluded obstructive median lobes. MedLift was a non-randomized cohort of 45 men with obstructive middle lobes, compared with historical L.I.F.T. results, and later observational reports describe feasibility in selected median-lobe anatomy. Neither establishes randomized superiority, and their authors are affiliated with the manufacturer.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> The AUA 2026 statement and the EAU 2026 recommendation both apply to prostates without an obstructing middle lobe, and EAU notes that data on obstructed or protruding middle lobes are limited.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

## Clinical efficacy

### L.I.F.T. — pivotal RCT

A total of 206 men were randomized 2:1 (PUL versus sham) at 19 North American / Australian centers<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>:

- 3-month primary endpoint (blinded): IPSS −11.1 (PUL) versus −5.9 (sham), p = 0.003, an 88% greater improvement.
- 12-month: IPSS from 22.1 to 11.1; Qmax +4.0 mL/s.

| Timepoint | IPSS Δ | QoL Δ | BPHII Δ | Qmax Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 mo | −11.1 (50%) | — | — | +4.4 mL/s |
| 12 mo | −11.1 (50%) | — | — | +4.0 mL/s |
| 24 mo | −9.7 (44%) | — | — | +3.7 mL/s |
| 5 yr | −7.9 (36%) | 50% | 52% | +3.9 mL/s (44%) |

In the treated cohort, improvements persisted through 5 years, with IPSS improvement attenuating from 50% at 1 yr to 36% at 5 yr.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> The EAU 2026 guideline reports 5-year improvement of 36% in IPSS, 50% in QoL and 44% in Qmax.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> After the 3-month sham comparison the trial was uncontrolled.

### BPH6 — PUL vs TURP RCT

80 men, 10 European centers, 24-month follow-up<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>:

- Both arms achieved significant IPSS improvement.
- TURP produced greater IPSS reduction and Qmax improvement.
- PUL was superior for quality of recovery and ejaculatory function preservation at 12 months (p &lt; 0.01), and on the composite BPH6 responder endpoint.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> The trial was non-blinded and small.

### Multinational real-world

A retrospective single-arm series of 102 men at 7 centers in 5 countries placed a mean of 4.5 implants. IPSS improved 52%, QoL 53% and Qmax 51% at 12 months (p &lt; 0.001), and 6.5% progressed to TURP.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Sexual function preservation — PUL's strongest signal

Erectile and ejaculatory function are preserved after PUL in the trials and pooled analyses below.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

- 0% de novo erectile dysfunction in pivotal and follow-up studies.
- 0% de novo ejaculatory dysfunction in the L.I.F.T. study and pivotal follow-up.
- The Franco 2026 *Eur Urol* pooled MIST analysis reported anejaculation of 0% with PUL and Optilume, 1% with iTIND, 3% with Rezūm and 5% with TPLA.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- In the Light 2021 *Eur Urol* network meta-analysis (48 papers, 33 RCTs, 5,159 patients, 16 interventions), PUL ranked highest for erectile function at 1, 6, 12 and 24 months versus monopolar TURP, and reached significance at 24 months (MD 3.63, 95% CrI 0.14–7.11). The authors noted that direct evidence is lacking.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- In the Cahill 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis (77 studies, 11,477 patients), IIEF scores improved significantly after PUL and Rezūm. PUL improved both MSHQ-EjD function and bother scores, and Rezūm improved only the bother score.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- In the combined MedLift and L.I.F.T. lateral-lobe dataset, ≥ 40% of sexually active men improved by the MCID for erectile function through 12 months.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Safety profile

Adverse events in L.I.F.T. were typically mild and transient.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> The Cochrane review (Jung 2019) found that PUL likely increases minor adverse events versus sham at 3 months (RR 1.69, 95% CI 1.33–2.16; moderate certainty) and was uncertain about major adverse events.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Common adverse events are dysuria, hematuria, pelvic pain and urgency, which are mostly mild to moderate and resolve within 2 to 4 weeks (EAU 2026 lists hematuria 16–63%, dysuria 25–58%, pelvic pain 5–17.9%, urgency 7.1–10%, transient incontinence 3.6–16% and UTI 2.9–11%).<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> Most patients need no catheter, and the mean return to normal activity is 8.6 days.

### Real-world signal — FDA MAUDE database

MAUDE reports identify uncommon but potentially serious events, including hematoma and hematuria requiring hospitalization. In Porto 2023, 23% of 226 UroLift reports and 7% of 358 Rezūm reports were classified as major; these are proportions of submitted reports, not complication rates among treated patients.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> The separate Weiss report likewise describes a selected reporting dataset.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> FDA explicitly cautions that MAUDE cannot establish incidence or compare adverse-event rates between devices because exposure denominators and complete, verified event capture are unavailable. These reports identify safety signals and do not show that PUL causes more complications than Rezūm.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

## Retreatment and durability — the central limitation

Clinical trial data:

- L.I.F.T.: cumulative surgical retreatment 10.7% at 3 yr and 13.6% at 5 yr.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- BPH6: 6.8% at 1 yr (versus 5.7% TURP).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Meta-analysis (Miller 2020 *J Urol*) of 11 studies and 2,016 patients<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>:

- The annual surgical reintervention rate was 6.0% per year (95% CI 3.0–8.9), and higher in studies with longer follow-up.
- Most common reinterventions: TURP / laser (51%), repeat PUL (32.7%), device explant (19.6%).

Real-world data report higher retreatment:

| Source | Population / database | Retreatment finding |
|---|---|---|
| Helman 2025 *J Endourol*<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | MarketScan, 10,938 men | 5-yr surgical retreatment 22.3% for PUL (vs 7% HoLEP, 24.5% TUMT). 1-yr medical retreatment also higher after MIST vs traditional procedures (42.9% vs 27.3%) |
| Raizenne 2022<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> | NY / CA population study, 2,694 men (PUL, TUNA, TUMT) | 1-yr and 3-yr reoperation 5.5% and 14.9% after PUL, with no significant difference from TUNA or TUMT |
| Gaffney 2021<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> | Premier Healthcare, 175,150 men | PUL OR 1.78 for retreatment within 2 years vs TURP / photovaporization (p &lt; 0.01) |
| Shin 2024 *BJU Int* NMA<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> | 12-month reintervention NMA of new BPH interventions | Ranking at 12 months: TURP, Aquablation, iTIND, Rezūm, then UroLift |
| Feiertag TriNetX analysis, as summarized in the AUA 2026 guideline<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> | Claims database | Cumulative re-procedure 5.1% at 1 yr and 16.1% at 4 yr, vs 7.5% after TURP and 7.8% after PVP at 4 yr |

## Guideline status

**AUA 2026.** Clinicians should offer PUL as an option for prostates of 30 to 80 cc without an obstructing median lobe (Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B). Before PUL, clinicians should counsel that it implants permanent tines that may affect future procedures and future prostate imaging (Expert Opinion).<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> The 2023 amendment used the same volume and median-lobe criteria.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

**EAU 2026.** Offer PUL to men with LUTS/BPO who want to preserve ejaculatory function, with a prostate &lt; 70 mL and no middle lobe (strong recommendation). Advise that retreatment rates are higher after PUL than after TURP (weak recommendation).<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> See the [BPH & Male LUTS chooser](/docs/surgical-techniques/bph-male-luts) for the full 2026 AUA framework.

## Where UroLift fits

### Distinguishing advantages

- Most patients need no postoperative catheter.
- Erectile and ejaculatory function are preserved, with 5-year follow-up from L.I.F.T.
- It can be performed under local anesthesia in the office.
- The AUA 2026 guideline lists HoLEP, ThuLEP, PVP or PAE, not PUL, for patients at high bleeding risk or on anticoagulants.
- Obstructive median lobes have been treated in MedLift and later observational reports, but AUA and EAU recommendations exclude them.
- Quality of recovery was better than after TURP in BPH6, and return to activity averages about 9 days.
- Later TURP or laser procedures and repeat PUL are possible when retreatment is needed, although the implants may affect future procedures and imaging.

### Principal limitations

- Five-year surgical retreatment of 22.3% in MarketScan, second only to TUMT (24.5%) among the procedures compared.
- Modest Qmax improvement (+3.7–4.4 mL/s) compared with Rezūm (+5.6), Optilume BPH (+10.3), Aquablation (+10.3), or TURP (+10.6).
- Permanent implants in the body (versus iTIND's reversible reshape-and-remove or Optilume's non-implant approach).
- Limited to prostates ≤ 80–100 mL (cannot treat very large glands; consider [Aquablation](/docs/foundations/tools/technology/energy-devices), [Simple Prostatectomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/bph-male-luts/simple-prostatectomy), or [PAE](/docs/surgical-techniques/bph-male-luts/prostate-artery-embolization)).
- FDA MAUDE reports describe hematoma and hematuria requiring hospitalization. MAUDE data cannot establish incidence.
- IPSS improvement attenuates over time (50% at 1 yr to 36% at 5 yr).

### Imaging consideration for the reconstructive urologist

UroLift implants are visible on CT as paired metallic densities in the prostatic urethra and capsule. They should not be mistaken for a foreign body, calculus or migrated brachytherapy seed by readers unfamiliar with the device. The stainless-steel urethral end piece creates MRI artifact, and the device carries an MR-conditional label. In a single-center retrospective study of 37 patients, all had artifact, which affected diffusion-weighted images more than T2-weighted images (AUA 2026 guideline summary).<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
