# iTIND — Temporary Implantable Nitinol Device for BPH

The **iTIND** (Olympus / Medi-Tate) is an **FDA-cleared minimally invasive treatment for LUTS secondary to BPH** that **temporarily reshapes the prostatic urethra and bladder neck over 5–7 days** through ischemic incisions, after which the device is **removed — leaving no permanent implant**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> It is mechanistically distinct from every other MIST: neither ablative, resective, drug-eluting, nor a permanent implant. For positioning vs other MISTs and TURP / HoLEP, see [BPH & Male LUTS — chooser](/docs/surgical-techniques/bph-male-luts).

---

## Mechanism of action

The iTIND is a self-expanding **nitinol** (nickel–titanium alloy) device with three elongated struts. Once deployed at the bladder neck and prostatic urethra, sustained **radial force** at the strut–tissue interface causes **ischemic necrosis** and creates **longitudinal incisions at 1, 5, and 11 o'clock**.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Over 5–7 days these incisions remodel the urethral lumen; the device is then retrieved, leaving the widened channels but no foreign material.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

This "reshape-and-retrieve" mechanism is fundamentally different from:

- **Rezūm** (convective thermal ablation of transition zone)
- **HoLEP / TURP / Aquablation** (enucleation / resection / hydroablation)
- **UroLift** (permanent suture-tack implants holding lateral lobes apart)
- **Optilume BPH** (paclitaxel-coated balloon dilation)
- **PAE** (selective embolization-induced infarction)

---

## Device evolution

- **First-generation TIND** (Porpiglia 2015, n = 32): demonstrated feasibility — 45% IPSS reduction and 67% Qmax improvement at 12 months — but with a higher complication profile.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Second-generation iTIND**: redesigned geometry and delivery system; superior safety and efficacy. **All subsequent pivotal and multicenter studies used iTIND**, not first-generation TIND.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

---

## Procedure

Performed under **light sedation or local anesthesia** in ~ 5–10 minutes<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>:

1. The compressed iTIND is delivered through a **22 F rigid cystoscope** into the prostatic urethra and bladder neck.
2. The device self-expands; struts are oriented at **1, 5, and 11 o'clock**.
3. Patient discharged **same day** — **no indwelling Foley required** during the in-situ period.
4. Device **retrieved 5–7 days later** in an outpatient setting under topical anesthesia using a Foley-catheter retrieval technique.
5. Patients return to daily life on average **4.3 days** after retrieval.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

---

## Patient selection

Based on clinical-trial criteria and the AUA Guideline Amendment 2023<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>:

- Men ≥ 50 years
- IPSS ≥ 10
- Qmax &lt; 12 mL/s
- **Prostate volume 25–75 mL** (MT-06 evaluated up to 120 mL<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>)
- **No obstructing median lobe** — the strongest predictor of iTIND failure (p &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- PVR &lt; 250 mL.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

The median-lobe exclusion is non-negotiable. A 2026 systematic review and expert-guidance paper on the median-lobe scenario explicitly states that **iTIND should not be offered** to patients with significant median-lobe obstruction.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Clinical efficacy

### MT-02 — prospective multicenter single-arm

n = 81 across European centers<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>:

| Timepoint | IPSS | Qmax (mL/s) | QoL |
|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline | 22.5 | 7.3 | 4.0 |
| 1 month | 11.7 (−48%) | 11.2 (+53%) | — |
| 12 months | 8.8 (−60%) | 14.7 (+100%) | 2.4 |
| 24 months | 8.5 (−59%) | 16.0 (+110%) | 1.8 |
| 36 months | **8.6 (−58%)** | **15.2 (+115%)** | 1.8 |

Improvements were stable from 12 to 36 months with **no late complications and no additional treatment failures between 24 and 36 months**.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

### Pivotal RCT (NCT02506465) — Chughtai 2021

n = 175 randomized 2:1 (iTIND vs sham) at 16 US / Canadian sites<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>:

- **Primary endpoint (3 mo):** 78.6% iTIND vs 60% sham achieved ≥ 3-point IPSS reduction (p = 0.03).
- **12 mo:** IPSS −9.25 points (p &lt; 0.0001 vs baseline); Qmax improvement +3.5 mL/s.
- Adverse-event rate 38.1% iTIND vs 17.5% sham (mostly mild, transient).

### MT-06 — prospective multicenter

n = 70, broader inclusion (volume up to 120 mL)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>:

- IPSS and Qmax significantly improved at 6 months (p &lt; 0.001).
- No de novo erectile or ejaculatory dysfunction.

---

## Safety profile

Across all studies adverse events are **mild and transient (Clavien-Dindo Grade I–II)**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>:

- **No Clavien-Dindo Grade &gt; II** complications in MT-02.
- Common AEs: pelvic discomfort during the indwelling period (mean VAS **3.2 ± 1.6**), hematuria, dysuria, UTI.
- **No late postoperative complications** between 12 and 36 months.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

---

## Sexual function preservation

A defining attribute<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>:

- **No de novo erectile dysfunction** in any study.
- **No de novo ejaculatory dysfunction** in the RCT or MT-02 through 3 years.
- A dedicated sexual-function analysis (Elterman 2023) showed no SHIM or total-IIEF differences between iTIND and sham at 3 months, or in the iTIND arm at 12 months. Men without baseline ED actually showed a **+6.07-point IIEF improvement** at 12 months (p = 0.034) along with improved ejaculatory function.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- A pooled MIST analysis (Franco 2026 *Eur Urol*) reported **anejaculation rate ~ 1% — the lowest among MISTs evaluated**.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- One narrative review reported a long-term retrograde-ejaculation rate of ~ 4%, not observed in the pivotal studies.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

---

## Retreatment and durability

This is the principal area of concern.

- **MT-02:** of 51 patients followed to 24 months, 5 required surgical retreatment between 12–24 months — **4 of 5 had median lobes** and were protocol deviators.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- At 12 months in MT-02: 2.4% required medical therapy and 2.4% required TURP.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **No additional treatment failures between 24 and 36 months.**<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Italian Delphi consensus** (Passarelli 2025) flagged iTIND alongside UroLift and TPLA as **high-risk for retreatment** — distinct from Rezūm and Aquablation, which were not.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- A 2025 device-profile review concluded iTIND has a **higher retreatment rate than traditional surgery** but remains a safe option in selected patients.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Predictors of failure

The only statistically significant baseline predictor of iTIND failure is the **presence of an obstructive median lobe** (p &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> In the failure analysis of all 81 MT-02 patients, **58.3% of failures (7 / 12) had median lobes**. No other baseline parameter (age, prostate volume, IPSS, Qmax, PVR, PSA) predicted failure.

## Comparison with other MISTs

No head-to-head RCTs vs other MISTs; comparisons are indirect.

| Procedure | IPSS Δ (12 mo) | Qmax Δ (12 mo) | Volume range | Median lobe | Implant left? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **iTIND** | −9.3 (RCT) / −13.7 (MT-02) | **+3.5 (RCT) / +14.7 (MT-02)** | 25–75 mL | **Excluded** | **None** |
| Rezūm | −11.3 | +5.6 | 30–80 mL | Treatable | None |
| UroLift | −11.0 | +4.3 | &lt; 80 mL (no median lobe) | Excluded in original label | **Permanent suture-tacks** |
| Optilume BPH | −11.5 to −12.4 | +10.3 | 20–80 mL | Excluded in trials | None |
| Aquablation | −15.1 | +10.3 | 30–150 mL | Treatable | None |
| PAE | −11.6 to −14.5 | +6.1 | Any size | Treatable | Embolic material |
| TURP | −15.1 | +10.6 | up to ~ 80 mL | Treatable | None |

The Cornu 2023 EAU network meta-analysis judged the relative efficacy of iTIND to be **less clear than PAE, PUL, and WVTT** because of risk-of-bias concerns in the pivotal RCT.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> In the Franco 2026 pooled MIST analysis, iTIND met proposed criteria for procedural success (≥ 50% IPSS Δ and Qmax improvement at 12 months) with the lowest anejaculation rate of any MIST.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

---

## Where iTIND fits

**Distinguishing features:**

- **No permanent implant** (vs UroLift's permanent suture-tacks).
- **No tissue ablation, resection, or drug delivery.**
- **Shortest procedure time** (~ 5 min) and same-day discharge with **no indwelling catheter** during the in-situ period.
- **Strongest ejaculatory-preservation profile** alongside UroLift; pooled anejaculation rate ~ 1%.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

**Principal limitations:**

- **Weakest Qmax improvement among MISTs** in the pivotal RCT (+3.5 mL/s) — though single-arm cohorts report much larger gains. The RCT vs single-arm gap suggests selection effects and modest absolute deobstruction.
- **Strict median-lobe exclusion** narrows the eligible population.
- **Limited to smaller / moderate prostates without median lobe**.
- **Higher retreatment risk** than Rezūm or Aquablation, per Delphi consensus and device-profile review.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

---

## Guideline status

The **AUA 2023 Guideline Amendment** lists iTIND (as TIPD — Temporarily Implanted Prostatic Device) under **Expert Opinion**: may be offered for prostate volume 25–75 mL with no obstructive median lobe.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> The evidence level is lower than for Rezūm or UroLift, both of which carry conditional recommendations supported by stronger comparative data.
