# Rezūm — Water Vapor Thermal Therapy (WVTT) for BPH

**Rezūm** (Boston Scientific) is a minimally invasive water vapor thermal therapy that uses radiofrequency-generated steam to ablate obstructive prostatic transition-zone tissue in men with LUTS secondary to BPH. In the sham-controlled pivotal trial, symptom improvement persisted through 5 years, no sustained de novo erectile dysfunction was reported, and surgical retreatment was 4.4%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The AUA 2026 BPH guideline states that clinicians should offer WVTT as an option for prostates of 30–80 cc (Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level Grade B).<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> For positioning against other minimally invasive surgical therapies (MISTs), TURP and HoLEP, see the [BPH & Male LUTS chooser](/docs/surgical-techniques/bph-male-luts).

---

## Mechanism of action

Per the manufacturer's device description, radiofrequency energy heats sterile water into vapor (approximately 103 °C), delivered through a retractable needle in a transurethral handpiece. Each injection lasts approximately 9 seconds and delivers approximately 208 calories of convective thermal energy into the transition zone.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The vapor disperses through the interstitial spaces and releases stored thermal energy on contact, which causes cell death of hyperplastic tissue. The AUA guideline describes the treated tissue regressing through necrosis over the following weeks.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> Manufacturer material describes thermal effects as confined to the prostate, with the urethra, bladder neck and external sphincter spared, and prostate volume falling by about 40% at 1 year.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Procedure details

- **Setting:** office-based or ambulatory outpatient, under local anesthesia, oral sedation or conscious sedation.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- **Position:** dorsal lithotomy.
- **Technique:** cystoscopic confirmation of prostate contours; treatment needle positioned approximately 1 cm distal to the bladder neck, with injections delivered every 1 cm toward the verumontanum. The number of injections per lobe is determined by prostatic urethral length (mean approximately 11; range 4–28 in the Canadian registry).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Median lobe:** treated at physician discretion in the pivotal trial.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Procedure time:** mean approximately 5–10 min (4.8 min in the Canadian registry).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Post-procedure catheterization:** the mean was 9.8 days in the Canadian registry.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> The AUA guideline notes that duration varies by practice, prostate volume and bladder function, from as short as 1 day for small prostates to a few weeks for very large prostates or poor bladder function.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- **Pain under local anesthesia:** mean VAS 3.5 / 10, with high patient satisfaction.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Patient selection and indications

- The pivotal trial enrolled prostates of 30–80 mL. Real-world series include glands of 20–160 mL.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> The AUA 2026 guideline notes that recent evidence supported expanding the FDA indication to prostates up to 150 cc, and advises this only for appropriate patients and experienced clinicians.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- Moderate-to-severe LUTS (IPSS ≥ 13, Qmax ≤ 15 mL/s) refractory to or intolerant of medical therapy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Applicable to lateral lobes and median lobe obstruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> 
- An option for men who wish to preserve erectile and ejaculatory function.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- A retrospective single-office series concluded that Rezūm can be offered to men with mild LUTS who have bothersome nocturia and wish to stop BPH medications. In that series the mild-LUTS group's IPSS worsened at 1 month and returned to baseline by 3 months.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- In a single-center multivariable analysis, diabetes was associated with a 3.67-point higher IPSS at 3 months.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Clinical efficacy

The pivotal multicenter RCT (n = 197, sham-controlled) and its 5-year follow-up reported:

| Outcome | At 3 months | At 5 years |
|---|---|---|
| IPSS reduction | ~ 50% | 48% |
| Quality of life (QoL) | ~ 50% | 45% |
| Peak flow rate (Qmax) | +6.2 mL/s | 44% improvement |
| BPH Impact Index | ~ 50% | 48% |
| Prostate volume reduction | — | ~ 40% |
| Surgical retreatment rate | — | 4.4% |

Sources for the table: final 5-yr trial<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>; 3-yr<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>; 2-yr durability<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>. The 3-month figures come from the 2016 trial, in which IPSS fell by 11.2 points with Rezūm versus 4.3 with the sham procedure.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Sham-controlled comparison is limited to 3 months. Control-arm crossover follows, so the 5-year data are uncontrolled.

A Canadian prospective registry (n = 229, including 83 with prostates ≥ 80 mL) reported IPSS improvement of 59% and Qmax improvement of 74% at 12 months.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> In a single-center analysis, IPSS improved significantly in all prostate-volume tertiles, and medium and large glands reached a 25% IPSS decrease sooner than small glands.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

In the retrospective single-office series, 66–88% of patients across LUTS-severity groups had stopped BPH medications at 12 months.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Safety profile

Most adverse events reported are transient and mild (Clavien-Dindo Grade I–II). The Cochrane review, which found one sham-controlled trial, reported more minor adverse events with Rezūm than sham (RR 1.89) and a much higher need for indwelling catheters (RR 35.58), and was very uncertain about major adverse events.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> Reported rates vary between series:<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- Gross hematuria is the most common event (59–80% in real-world data) and is typically self-limited.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- Dysuria approximately 21–23%.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- Urinary urgency approximately 20%.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Urinary retention requiring catheterization approximately 19–34%.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- UTI approximately 20%.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- No sustained de novo erectile dysfunction was reported through 5 years in the pivotal RCT.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- The pooled anejaculation rate after Rezūm was 3% in a 2026 systematic review.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- No Clavien-Dindo Grade ≥ III events in the Canadian registry.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

---

## Sexual function preservation

The pivotal RCT reported no device- or procedure-related sexual dysfunction and no sustained de novo erectile dysfunction through 5 years.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The Italian multicenter cohort showed a small but statistically significant improvement in IIEF-5 at 6 months, which the authors attributed to alpha-blocker discontinuation.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> In a randomized comparison with TURP, retrograde ejaculation occurred in 2% after WVTT and 80% after TURP, and another series reported 3.7% and 82.7%.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

---

## Comparison with other treatments

A European Urology network meta-analysis of 63 RCTs found:<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

- WVTT (Rezūm), PUL ([UroLift](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/adjunct-specialty/urolift)), and PAE provide symptom and QoL improvements similar to TURP at short-term follow-up. 
- TURP provides the greatest improvement in objective flow rate.
- Ejaculatory function favors WVTT and PUL over TURP.
- No significant difference in urinary domain scores between UroLift and Rezūm at 24 months.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

A real-world head-to-head comparison of Rezūm versus UroLift (307 versus 110 patients) showed similar medium-term efficacy in AUA-SS, Qmax, and medication reduction. Rezūm had higher rates of postoperative dysuria (22.8% versus 8.3%) and non-clot retention (18.9% versus 7.3%); UroLift had higher clot retention (7.3% versus 2.6%).<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> 

---

## Durability and retreatment

Five-year data from the pivotal RCT show a surgical retreatment rate of 4.4%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The AUA 2026 guideline reports overall WVTT reoperation rates of 3–4% over 1–5 years, and 4.6–9.7% over 1–3 years in other studies, where inadequately treated adenoma and bladder neck obstruction were common causes. Indirect analyses summarized there show a higher retreatment rate with PUL than with WVTT.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> The 2026 pooled analysis noted that MIST follow-up is mainly limited to 24 months, and the Cochrane network meta-analysis noted limited long-term comparative data.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

---

## Advantages and limitations

**Advantages**

- Office-based, under local anesthesia
- Short procedure time (approximately 5–10 min)
- Erectile and ejaculatory function were preserved in the cited series
- Treats lateral lobes and median lobe
- Symptom improvement persisted through 5 years in the pivotal trial, with 4.4% surgical retreatment
- Many patients stop BPH medications (66–88% in one retrospective series)

**Limitations**

- Post-procedure catheterization is usually needed (mean 9.8 days in the Canadian registry)
- Transient irritative symptoms are common in the early postoperative period
- Less improvement in objective flow rate than with TURP<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- The strongest evidence is for prostates of 30–80 mL
- Maximal benefit takes approximately 3 months
