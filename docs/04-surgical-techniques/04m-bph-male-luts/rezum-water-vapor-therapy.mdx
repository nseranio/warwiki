# Rezūm — Water Vapor Thermal Therapy (WVTT) for BPH

**Rezūm** (Boston Scientific) is a minimally invasive water vapor thermal therapy that uses radiofrequency-generated steam to ablate obstructive prostatic transition-zone tissue in men with LUTS secondary to BPH. In the sham-controlled pivotal trial, symptom improvement persisted through 5 years, no sustained de novo erectile dysfunction was reported, and surgical retreatment was 4.4%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The AUA 2026 BPH guideline states that clinicians should offer WVTT as an option for prostates of 30–80 cc (Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level Grade B).<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> For positioning against other minimally invasive surgical therapies (MISTs), TURP and HoLEP, see the [BPH & Male LUTS chooser](/docs/surgical-techniques/bph-male-luts).

---

## Mechanism of action

Per the manufacturer's device description, radiofrequency energy heats sterile water into vapor (approximately 103 °C), delivered through a retractable needle in a transurethral handpiece. Each injection lasts approximately 9 seconds and delivers approximately 208 calories of convective thermal energy into the transition zone.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup> The vapor disperses through the interstitial spaces and releases stored thermal energy on contact, which causes cell death of hyperplastic tissue. The AUA guideline describes the treated tissue regressing through necrosis over the following weeks.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> The treatment is designed to localize thermal effects within prostatic tissue, and a Cochrane review reports intended sparing of the urethra, bladder neck and external sphincter.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The Canadian IFU nevertheless reports urethral stricture and bladder-neck contracture.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> In a 49-man cohort, median prostate volume fell 34% (IQR 16.7–49.2%) at 12 months.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

## Procedure details

- **Setting:** office-based or ambulatory outpatient, under local anesthesia, oral sedation or conscious sedation.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- **Position:** dorsal lithotomy, with the buttocks brought to the edge of the table so the delivery device can be inserted to full depth and rotated during the procedure.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- **Technique:** cystoscopic confirmation of prostate contours; treatment needle positioned approximately 1 cm distal to the bladder neck, with injections delivered every 1 cm toward the verumontanum. The number of injections per lobe is determined by prostatic urethral length; the Canadian registry reported a mean of 11 injections per patient (range 4–28).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> The manufacturer's directions add these points:<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
  - Measure the bladder-neck-to-verumontanum length by TRUS or cystoscopy. One treatment is a single 9-second vapor delivery.
  - Suggested treatments per lateral lobe are 1 to 2 for less than 2.0 cm, 2 to 3 for 2.0 to 3.0 cm and 3 to 4 for more than 3.0 cm. One delivery device supports at most 15 full treatments.
  - Use a 4 mm, 30-degree, 30 cm rigid lens and room-temperature saline. Run a pre-treatment vapor cycle before the device enters the patient.
  - Rotate the device 90 degrees so the needle enters the crest of the lateral lobe. Follow the slope of the urethra and never aim the needle toward the rectum. Place every treatment proximal to the verumontanum.
  - Do not start vapor if the black depth marker is still visible after needle deployment. Hold the device still during the 9 seconds. Confirm the needle is fully retracted before moving the device.
  - For an intravesical protrusion, place the device 1 cm from the proximal edge of the protrusion with the needle about 45 degrees toward the midline.
- **Median lobe:** treated at physician discretion in the pivotal trial.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The directions suggest one treatment for a median lobe under 2 cm and two or more for a larger one.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- **Procedure time:** mean 4.8 min (range 1.5–14) in the Canadian registry; median 10.5 min (IQR 8.7–15) in the Italian multicenter cohort.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Post-procedure catheterization:** in the pivotal trial, 90.4% of treated men were catheterized before discharge versus 19.7% of sham controls, with a mean duration of 3.4 days (median 2.9 days) versus 0.9 days for controls; 68% of treated catheterizations were at physician discretion rather than for retention.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> A Canadian registry reported a longer mean of 9.8 days.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> The AUA guideline notes that duration varies by practice, prostate volume and bladder function, from as short as 1 day for small prostates to a few weeks for very large prostates or poor bladder function.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- **Pain under local anesthesia:** mean intraoperative VAS 3.6/10 among 71 of 82 patients with a recorded score; 36 of 49 satisfaction-questionnaire respondents rated the procedure 9 or 10/10.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Analgesia in the pivotal trial:** of 196 treated men, 69% had oral sedation, 21% a prostate block and 10% IV sedation.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

## Patient selection and indications

- The pivotal trial enrolled prostates of 30–80 mL, the range in the cited 2021 Canadian directions.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> In June 2025, the FDA cleared the US indication for men aged 50 or older with prostates of 30–150 cm³, including central-zone or median-lobe hyperplasia.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> Real-world series include glands of 20–160 mL.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> The AUA 2026 guideline offers WVTT as a Moderate Recommendation for 30–80 cc and advises selection by patient and clinician experience above 80 cc.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- Moderate-to-severe LUTS (IPSS ≥ 13, Qmax ≤ 15 mL/s) refractory to or intolerant of medical therapy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- The directions contraindicate treatment with an artificial urinary sphincter, a penile prosthesis or an active UTI. Exclude urethral stricture as the cause of obstruction. Allow tissue to heal (for example 30 days) after prostate biopsy or prior prostate surgery. There are no data after pelvic radiation, and the system was not studied in men with prostate cancer.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- Applicable to lateral lobes and median lobe obstruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- Catheter-dependent retention is a separate selection scenario: observational Rezūm series report catheter-free rates of 68–100%, but comparative evidence and standardized follow-up are limited.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>
- An option for men who wish to preserve erectile and ejaculatory function.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- A retrospective single-office series concluded that Rezūm can be offered to men with mild LUTS who have bothersome nocturia and wish to stop BPH medications. In that series the mild-LUTS group's IPSS worsened at 1 month and returned to baseline by 3 months.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- In a single-center multivariable analysis, diabetes was associated with a 3.67-point higher IPSS at 3 months.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- No Rezūm-specific VTE-prophylaxis recommendation exists; as an office-based procedure under local anesthesia or light sedation it falls toward the low-risk end of the general urologic-surgery risk framework on the [VTE Prophylaxis](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/perioperative-eras/vte-prophylaxis) page, but individual risk factors (age, obesity, prior VTE, malignancy) still apply.

## Clinical efficacy

The pivotal multicenter sham-controlled RCT randomized 197 men (136 treatment, 61 sham); 135 received treatment, and 77 completed the 5-year visit.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Outcome | At 3 months | At 5 years |
|---|---|---|
| IPSS reduction | ~ 50%<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | 48%<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Quality of life (QoL) | ~ 50%<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 45%<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Peak flow rate (Qmax) | +6.2 mL/s<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | 44% improvement<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| BPH Impact Index | ~ 50%<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | 48%<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |

| Surgical retreatment rate | — | 4.4% |

Sources for the table: final 5-yr trial<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>; 3-yr<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>; 2-yr durability<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>. The 3-month figures come from the 2016 trial, in which IPSS fell by 11.2 points with Rezūm versus 4.3 with the sham procedure.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Sham-controlled comparison ended at 3 months, after which control-arm crossover occurred. Five-year symptom estimates include 77 of the 136 men originally assigned to Rezūm; six of the 135 treated men (4.4%) underwent surgical retreatment, and 11.1% initiated BPH medication through five years.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

A Canadian prospective registry (n = 229, including 83 with prostates ≥ 80 mL) reported IPSS improvement of 59% and Qmax improvement of 74% at 12 months.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> In a single-center analysis, IPSS improved significantly in all prostate-volume tertiles, and medium and large glands reached a 25% IPSS decrease sooner than small glands.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

In the retrospective single-office series, 66–88% of patients across LUTS-severity groups had stopped BPH medications at 12 months.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

In the 2026 VAPEUR randomized trial of 151 sexually active men, WVTT improved IPSS by 10.8 points versus 6.2 with combination medication at one year; treatment-related adverse events occurred in 40% versus 28%, including serious events in 12% versus 1.3%. Superiority for sexual-function preservation was not demonstrated after multiple imputation.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

## Safety profile

In the pivotal trial, adjudicated procedure- or device-related events through 4 years (treatment and crossover groups) were dysuria 17%, gross hematuria 12%, hematospermia 6%, urinary frequency 6%, decreased ejaculatory volume 5%, suspected UTI 5%, urinary retention 5%, urgency 5%, anejaculation 3%, culture-proven UTI 3%, epididymitis 2%, worsening erectile dysfunction 2% and urethral stricture 2%.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> Among treatment and crossover subjects, 57% had no procedure- or device-related event; 80% of events occurred in the first 30 days, and 89% had resolved as of February 9, 2019.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> Six serious events occurred in four men: prolonged retention from an untreated intravesical lobe, an allergic reaction to a sedative, bladder neck contracture with bladder stones, and urosepsis after cystoscopy.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> Real-world series report higher rates for some events. Most adverse events reported are transient and mild (Clavien-Dindo Grade I–II). The Cochrane review, which found one sham-controlled trial, reported more minor adverse events with Rezūm than sham (RR 1.89) and a much higher need for indwelling catheters (RR 35.58), and was very uncertain about major adverse events.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> Reported rates vary between series:<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- Gross hematuria is the most common event (59–80% in real-world data) and is typically self-limited.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- In a 65-man open-label pilot, related non-serious dysuria affected 14 men (21.5%) within 12 months; a retrospective comparison reported postoperative dysuria in 22.8% of 307 WVTT recipients versus 8.3% of 110 PUL recipients within 90 days.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[15]](#ref15)</sup><sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup>
- In a 65-man open-label pilot, 13 men (20.0%) had related non-serious urinary urgency within 3 months.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup><sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup>
- In a 65-man open-label pilot, related non-serious urinary retention (inadequate voiding for more than 24 hours) affected 22 men (33.8%) within 12 months; a retrospective comparison reported non-clot-related retention in 18.9% of 307 WVTT recipients versus 7.3% of 110 PUL recipients within 90 days.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[15]](#ref15)</sup><sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup>
- In the pivotal-study adverse-event table through four years, suspected UTI was reported in 5% and culture-proven UTI in 3%.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- No sustained de novo erectile dysfunction was reported through 5 years in the pivotal RCT.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- The pooled anejaculation rate after Rezūm was 3% in a 2026 systematic review.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- No Clavien-Dindo Grade ≥ III events in the Canadian registry.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Sexual function preservation

The pivotal RCT reported no sustained de novo erectile dysfunction through 5 years.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The manufacturer's adverse-event table through 4 years still lists decreased ejaculatory volume (5%), anejaculation (3%, none resolved) and worsening erectile dysfunction (2%), so counsel about ejaculatory change.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> The Italian multicenter cohort showed a small but statistically significant improvement in IIEF-5 at 6 months, which the authors suggested might reflect relief of urinary symptoms and better quality of life. They separately reported recovery of ejaculatory function after treatment and alpha-blocker interruption.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> In a randomized comparison with TURP, retrograde ejaculation occurred in 2% after WVTT and 80% after TURP; a retrospective matched cohort of catheter-dependent men reported 3.7% after WVTT and 82.7% after TURP.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

## Comparison with other treatments

A European Urology network meta-analysis of 63 RCTs found:<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

- WVTT (Rezūm), PUL ([UroLift](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/adjunct-specialty/urolift)), and PAE provide symptom and QoL improvements similar to TURP at short-term follow-up.
- TURP provides the greatest improvement in objective flow rate.
- Ejaculatory function favors WVTT and PUL over TURP.
- No significant difference in urinary domain scores between UroLift and Rezūm at 24 months.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

A real-world head-to-head comparison of Rezūm versus UroLift (307 versus 110 patients) showed similar medium-term efficacy in AUA-SS, Qmax, and medication reduction. Rezūm had higher rates of postoperative dysuria (22.8% versus 8.3%) and non-clot retention (18.9% versus 7.3%); UroLift had higher clot retention (7.3% versus 2.6%).<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

---

## Durability and retreatment

Five-year data from the pivotal RCT show a surgical retreatment rate of 4.4%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The manufacturer's directions report that 9 of 188 treated and crossover men (5%) sought alternative treatment within 4 years.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> The AUA 2026 guideline reports overall WVTT reoperation rates of 3–4% over 1–5 years, and 4.6–9.7% over 1–3 years in other studies, where inadequately treated adenoma and bladder neck obstruction were common causes.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> Indirect analyses summarized there show a higher retreatment rate with PUL than with WVTT.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> The EAU 2026 guideline lists 4.4% surgical retreatment over 5 years, and 15.8% at 4 years when reinitiated drug therapy is counted with surgical reintervention.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> It places water vapor therapy among the techniques under investigation and makes no recommendation for it. It cites low- to very-low-certainty Cochrane evidence and a single-center RCT in which TURP gave greater IPSS, Qmax and PVR improvement at 24 months.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> The 2026 pooled analysis noted that MIST follow-up is mainly limited to 24 months, and the Cochrane network meta-analysis noted limited long-term comparative data.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

## Advantages and limitations

**Advantages**

- Office-based, under local anesthesia
- Short procedure time (approximately 5–10 min)
- Erectile function was preserved in the cited series, and the manufacturer reported anejaculation in 3% of treatment and crossover subjects through 4 years.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- Treats lateral lobes and median lobe
- Symptom improvement persisted through 5 years in the pivotal trial treatment arm, with 4.4% surgical retreatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- At 12 months in one retrospective series, 66.0% of men with severe, 80.0% with mild, and 87.5% with moderate baseline LUTS had stopped BPH medication.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

**Limitations**

- Post-procedure catheterization is usually needed (mean 9.8 days in the 229-patient Canadian registry).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Transient irritative symptoms are common in the early postoperative period
- Less improvement in objective flow rate than with TURP<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- The strongest evidence is for prostates of 30–80 mL
- Maximal benefit takes approximately 3 months
