# Rezūm — Water Vapor Thermal Therapy (WVTT) for BPH

**Rezūm** (Boston Scientific) is a **minimally invasive water vapor thermal therapy** that uses radiofrequency-generated steam to ablate obstructive prostatic transition-zone tissue in men with LUTS secondary to BPH. It provides **durable symptom relief through 5 years** with **preservation of sexual function** and a **low surgical-retreatment rate (~ 4–5%)**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> For positioning vs other minimally invasive surgical therapies (MISTs) and TURP / HoLEP, see [BPH & Male LUTS — chooser](/docs/surgical-techniques/bph-male-luts).

---

## Mechanism of action

Radiofrequency energy heats sterile water into vapor (~ 103 °C), delivered via a retractable needle through a transurethral handpiece. Each injection lasts ~ 9 seconds and delivers ~ 208 calories of **convective thermal energy** into the transition zone.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The vapor disperses through tissue interstitial spaces and releases stored thermal energy on contact, inducing **apoptosis and necrosis** of hyperplastic tissue. Thermal effects remain confined to the prostate, sparing the urethra, bladder neck, and external sphincter.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Necrotic tissue is reabsorbed over weeks, reducing prostatic volume by **~ 40%** at 1 year.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Procedure details

- **Setting:** office-based or ambulatory outpatient; can be performed under **local anesthesia, oral sedation, or conscious sedation**.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Position:** dorsal lithotomy.
- **Technique:** cystoscopic confirmation of prostate contours; treatment needle positioned ~ 1 cm distal to the bladder neck, with injections delivered every 1 cm toward the verumontanum. Number of injections per lobe is determined by prostatic urethral length (mean ~ 11; range 4–28).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Median lobe:** can be treated per physician discretion.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Procedure time:** mean ~ 5–10 min.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Post-procedure catheterization:** typically required for a mean of **7–10 days**.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Pain under local anesthesia:** mean VAS **3.5 / 10**, with high patient satisfaction.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

---

## Patient selection and indications

- **FDA-cleared for prostate volumes 30–80 mL**; real-world data support efficacy across a broader range (20–160 mL).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- Moderate-to-severe LUTS (IPSS ≥ 13, Qmax ≤ 15 mL/s) refractory to or intolerant of medical therapy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Applicable to **lateral lobes and median lobe** obstruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- Particularly attractive for men who wish to **preserve sexual function** (erectile and ejaculatory).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- May also be offered to patients with mild LUTS who have bothersome nocturia and wish to discontinue BPH medications.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- Patients with **diabetes** may have attenuated IPSS improvement.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Clinical efficacy

The pivotal multicenter RCT (n = 197, sham-controlled) and its 5-year follow-up demonstrated:

| Outcome | At 3 months | At 5 years |
|---|---|---|
| IPSS reduction | ~ 50% | **48%** |
| Quality of life (QoL) | ~ 50% | **45%** |
| Peak flow rate (Qmax) | +6.2 mL/s | 44% improvement |
| BPH Impact Index | ~ 50% | 48% |
| Prostate volume reduction | — | ~ 40% |
| **Surgical retreatment rate** | — | **4.4%** |

References for table: Final 5-yr trial<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>; 3-yr<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>; 2-yr durability<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>.

Real-world registries confirm pivotal data. A large Canadian prospective registry (n = 229, including 83 with prostates ≥ 80 mL) showed IPSS improvement of **59%** and Qmax improvement of **74%** at 12 months.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Symptom improvement is significant across all volume tertiles; medium and large glands may improve more quickly than small glands.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

At 12 months, **66–88%** of patients are able to discontinue BPH medications.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

---

## Safety profile

Most adverse events are **transient and mild** (Clavien-Dindo Grade I–II):<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

- **Gross hematuria** — most common AE (59–80% in real-world data; typically self-limited).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Dysuria** ~ 21–23%.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Urinary urgency** ~ 20%.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Urinary retention** (requiring catheterization) ~ 19–34%.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **UTI** ~ 20%.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **No de novo erectile dysfunction** through 5 years in the pivotal RCT.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Ejaculatory function preserved** in most patients; anejaculation rate ~ 3%.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- No Clavien-Dindo Grade ≥ III events in the Canadian registry.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

---

## Sexual function preservation

A key differentiator. The pivotal RCT reported **no device- or procedure-related sexual dysfunction and no sustained de novo erectile dysfunction through 5 years**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The Italian multicenter cohort actually showed a slight but statistically significant improvement in IIEF-5 at 6 months, likely attributable to alpha-blocker discontinuation.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> This profile sets Rezūm apart from TURP, which carries retrograde-ejaculation rates of **65–75%**.

---

## Comparison with other treatments

A European Urology network meta-analysis of 63 RCTs found:<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

- WVTT (Rezūm), PUL ([UroLift](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/adjunct-specialty/urolift)), and PAE provide symptom and QoL improvements **similar to TURP** at short-term follow-up.
- **TURP** provides the greatest improvement in **objective flow rate**.
- **Ejaculatory function** favors WVTT and PUL over TURP.
- No significant difference in urinary domain scores between UroLift and Rezūm at 24 months.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

A real-world head-to-head comparison of Rezūm vs UroLift (307 vs 110 patients) showed similar medium-term efficacy in AUA-SS, Qmax, and medication reduction. Rezūm had higher rates of postoperative dysuria (22.8% vs 8.3%) and non-clot retention (18.9% vs 7.3%); UroLift had higher clot retention (7.3% vs 2.6%).<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

---

## Durability and retreatment

Five-year data from the pivotal RCT show a surgical retreatment rate of only **4.4%**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> This compares favorably with UroLift, which has reported retreatment rates of ~ 13–14% at 5 years in some series. The Cochrane review noted that long-term comparative data remain limited and most MIST follow-up is primarily within 24 months.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

---

## Advantages and limitations

**Advantages**

- Office-based, can be performed under local anesthesia
- Short procedure time (~ 5–10 min)
- Preserves erectile and ejaculatory function
- Treats lateral lobes and median lobe
- Durable 5-year outcomes with low retreatment rates
- Allows BPH-medication discontinuation in most patients

**Limitations**

- Post-procedure catheterization required (~ 7–10 days)
- Transient irritative symptoms common in the early postoperative period
- Less improvement in objective flow rate compared with TURP<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- Most robust evidence limited to prostates 30–80 mL (though expanding)
- Onset of maximal benefit takes ~ 3 months (not immediate)
