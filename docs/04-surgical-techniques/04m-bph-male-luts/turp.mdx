# TURP — Transurethral Resection of the Prostate

**Transurethral Resection of the Prostate (TURP)** is the surgical treatment for LUTS secondary to BPH that has long been called the "gold standard" (Mayer 2012, Rassweiler 2006).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> It is the reference comparator for newer surgical and minimally invasive therapies.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The AUA 2026 guideline states that clinicians should offer bipolar or monopolar TURP as an option for LUTS/BPH (Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade A).<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> For positioning against other BPH options, see [BPH & Male LUTS — chooser](/docs/surgical-techniques/bph-male-luts).

---

## Mechanism

An electrosurgical loop is passed through a resectoscope and used to resect obstructing prostatic adenoma piecemeal. The loop cuts tissue and coagulates blood vessels at the same time. Resected chips fall into the bladder and are evacuated at the end of the procedure, providing tissue for histological analysis; incidental prostate cancer is detected in approximately 9.8% of TURP specimens.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### Monopolar TURP (M-TURP)

- Single active electrode (the loop); current passes through the patient's body to a return pad.
- Requires non-conductive hypo-osmolar irrigation (glycine, sorbitol, or mannitol), which creates the risk of TUR syndrome (dilutional hyponatremia from fluid absorption).
- Resection time is limited to approximately 60–90 minutes to minimize fluid-absorption risk.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

### Bipolar TURP (B-TURP)

- The bipolar circuit is at the resectoscope tip and energy does not traverse the patient.
- Uses normal saline (0.9% NaCl) as irrigant, which lowers the risk of TUR syndrome (RR 0.17 in the Cochrane review) without eliminating it.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Allows longer resection times and may reduce thermal damage to surrounding tissues.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

---

## Procedure

TURP is performed under general or spinal or regional anesthesia. Operative time is typically 30–60 min depending on size.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[7]](#ref7)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

1. **24–28 Fr resectoscope** introduced through the urethra under direct vision.
2. Bladder inspected for stones, diverticula, or tumors.
3. Identify landmarks: bladder neck (proximal), verumontanum (distal limit of resection), external sphincter.
4. Systematic resection, typically median lobe first (if present), then lateral lobes, working from bladder neck distally toward verumontanum.
5. Create a midline trench to allow the lateral lobe to fall backward, then resect remaining adenoma while preserving the thin surgical capsule.
6. Hemostasis: coagulate arterial bleeders (typically at 1, 5, 7, 11 o'clock).
7. Tissue chips evacuated using Ellik evacuator.
8. **Three-way Foley** placed for continuous bladder irrigation.

### Operative parameters (pooled contemporary RCTs)<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

- Mean resection time: 38.5 min
- Mean resected tissue: 25.8 g
- Mean catheterization: 2.5 d
- Mean length of stay: 3.6 d (varies by country and practice)

## Patient selection

Based on AUA / EAU guidelines<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[8]](#ref8)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>:

- Moderate-to-severe LUTS (IPSS ≥ 12) refractory to medical therapy.
- Prostate volume up to about 80 mL. The AUA 2026 Panel could not recommend a specific volume cutoff and gave as expert opinion that monopolar TURP is best used for glands ≤ 80 cc.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> A prospective comparative study reported TURP in glands of 70–150 mL, and enucleation or simple prostatectomy is generally preferred above 80 mL.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Absolute indications.** Refractory urinary retention, recurrent UTIs, bladder stones, gross hematuria, renal insufficiency secondary to BPH.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Treats obstructive median lobes.
- Provides tissue for histological analysis, which allows incidental prostate cancer detection.

## Clinical efficacy

The JAMA review reports symptom and flow improvements that persist to 60 months.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Baseline Qmax and PVR are from the Bavarian cohort.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

| Parameter | Baseline | 12 months | 60 months |
|---|---|---|---|
| IPSS | ~ 22–24 | −15.1 | −13.2 |
| Qmax (mL/s) | ~ 10.4 | +10.6 | +6.3 |
| PVR (mL) | ~ 180 | ~ 31 (early postoperative) | — |
| QoL | — | 69% | sustained |

A meta-analysis of 20 RCTs confirmed 70% IPSS reduction and 69% QoL improvement with up to 5 yr follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The JAMA review treats TURP as the reference procedure for comparison with newer surgical options.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

A prospective comparative study of TURP in 56 men with prostates of 70–150 mL and 57 with smaller glands found larger improvements in IPSS and Qmax in the larger glands, longer operative time and similar complication rates.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> At 1 yr PVR was lower in the smaller-gland group.

## Monopolar vs Bipolar — the Cochrane comparison

The 2019 Cochrane systematic review (59 RCTs, 8,924 participants) compared the two directly.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

| Parameter | M-TURP | B-TURP | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| IPSS / Qmax / QoL / IIEF | similar | similar | no difference |
| TUR syndrome | 24 / 1,000 | 4 / 1,000 | B-TURP lower (RR 0.17, 95% CI 0.09 to 0.30; moderate certainty) |
| Transfusion | 48 / 1,000 | 20 / 1,000 | B-TURP lower (RR 0.42, 95% CI 0.30 to 0.59; moderate certainty) |
| Erectile dysfunction | similar | similar | no difference |
| Urinary incontinence | similar | similar | no difference |
| Retreatment | similar | similar | no difference |
| Bladder neck stricture | 0% | 8.5% | Lower with M-TURP in one RCT of 137 patients<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |

In moderate-to-large prostates specifically, a meta-analysis of 3 RCTs and 4 observational studies (496 patients) found B-TURP had shorter length of stay (p = 0.01), shorter catheterization (p = 0.05) and lower TUR syndrome (p = 0.03), with no difference in Qmax at 3, 6 or 12 months.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Safety profile

The Bavarian statewide multicenter prospective study (n = 10,654) is the largest contemporary safety dataset.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### Perioperative

- 30-day mortality was 0.10% (historical figures 0.2–0.4%).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[12]](#ref12)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- 90-day mortality after TURP was 1.16% in a Finnish nationwide cohort of 39,320 patients undergoing BPH surgery (1.10% overall), and excess 90-day mortality was &lt; 0.5% in all age groups.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- Cumulative short-term morbidity 11.1%.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Transfusion 2.9% (contemporary); decreased from 7.1% historically to 0.4% in the most recent era.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- TUR syndrome 1.4% in the Bavarian cohort; 24 per 1,000 with monopolar and 4 per 1,000 with bipolar TURP in the Cochrane review.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Clot retention 2–5%.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- UTI 1.7–3.6%.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- Failure to void / AUR 5.8–6.8%.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- Return to OR for bleeding 2.0–5.6%.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

### Late

- Urethral stricture 2.2–9.8%.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- Bladder-neck contracture 0.3–9.2%.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- Late stress urinary incontinence &lt; 0.5%.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

### Three-decade temporal trend

Reported complication rates fell across three periods (early 1979–1994 vs recent 2000–2005)<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>:

| Outcome | Historical | Recent era |
|---|---|---|
| Transfusion | 7.1% | 0.4% |
| TUR syndrome | 1.1% | 0.0% |
| Clot retention | 5% | 2% |
| UTI | 8.2% | 1.7% |
| Mortality | 0–0.25% (range across the review) | not reported separately |

## Sexual function

Sexual dysfunction is the main quality-of-life trade-off of TURP.

- Retrograde ejaculation occurs in 60–75% and is the most common sexual side effect.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> In a prospective series of 1,014 men (Muntener 2007), the ejaculatory function score worsened significantly (p &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- Erectile dysfunction occurs in 10–14% in most series.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The Muntener series found erectile function scores did not change significantly after TURP (p = 0.11), and 3 of 4 patients remained sexually active.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- In a meta-analysis of 10 RCTs (Liu 2020), endoscopic enucleation had slightly higher IIEF-5 scores than TURP at 48 mo and later (mean difference about 1 point), and retrograde ejaculation rates did not differ significantly.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

### Mechanism of dysfunction<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

- Retrograde ejaculation: destruction of the bladder-neck mechanism during resection.
- Erectile dysfunction: potential thermal or mechanical injury to the neurovascular bundles via posterolateral capsular perforation.
- B-TURP may reduce thermal damage, but the Cochrane review found similar erectile function (IIEF-5) with the two techniques.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Retreatment and durability

| Source | n | Follow-up | Retreatment rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| He 2023 SR (cumulative reoperation)<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | 130,106 across procedures | 1 / 2 / 3 / 5 yr | 4.0% / 5.0% / 6.0% / 7.7% |
| Eredics 2018 Austrian nationwide<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | 20,388 | 8 yr | 8.3% re-TURP; 12.7% all endourological |
| Frendl 2021 New York ambulatory claims<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> | 15,982 (36% TURP) | 7 yr | 15.3% (failure = reoperation or AUR &gt; 30 d) |
| Nackeeran 2025 VA nationwide, TURP after 2001<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> | 35,817 | 5 yr | 15.4% (new procedure within 5 yr); OR 1.49 vs TURP before 2001 |
| Wei 2025 *JAMA* review<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | — | not stated in abstract | 5% |
| Reich 2008 Bavarian early<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | 10,654 | early | 5.6% surgical revision |

In the VA cohort, TURP after 2001 was associated with 49% higher odds of a new procedure within 5 yr than TURP before 2001 (OR 1.49, p &lt; 0.001), and more men were on BPH medication afterward. The authors suggested that reduced trainee exposure may contribute; the study cannot establish the cause.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

## Learning curve and training trends

TURP has been considered a technically demanding procedure that takes significant experience to master. A pooled analysis of contemporary RCTs of monopolar TURP showed a mean resection time of 38.5 min, which suggested improved resection efficiency over earlier decades.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

Training-volume considerations:

- In the Nackeeran 2025 VA analysis, TURP after 2001 was associated with 49% higher odds of a new procedure within 5 yr than TURP before 2001. The authors raised reduced trainee exposure as one possible explanation.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- TURP remains the 10th most common operation in the UK NHS, with approximately 25,000 prostate operations annually (approximately 80% TURP).<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- A learning curve of 50–100 supervised cases is commonly quoted for proficiency.

---

## Where TURP fits in 2026

### Distinguishing strengths

- **Efficacy.** A 70% IPSS reduction and Qmax improvement are reported to 5 yr.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Tissue for histology.** Incidental prostate cancer was found in 9.8% of specimens in the Bavarian cohort.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Availability.** No specialized equipment beyond a standard resectoscope is needed.
- **Median lobes.** TURP treats obstructive median lobes.
- **Complication profile.** Reported morbidity and mortality have declined across three decades.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

### Principal limitations

- **Retrograde ejaculation.** It occurs in 60–75%, compared with 7–10% after Aquablation, 0–3% after Rezūm, approximately 1% after iTIND and 0% after UroLift (uncited figures from the table above).
- **Size.** The AUA Panel's expert opinion is that monopolar TURP suits glands ≤ 80 cc. TURP has been reported in glands up to about 150 mL by experienced surgeons, and enucleation is generally preferred for very large glands.
- **Hospital stay.** The pooled length of stay was 3.6 d, longer than with office-based MISTs.
- **Trainee exposure.** Exposure may be declining in the era of medical therapy and MISTs.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

BPH surgery is trending toward enucleation (AEEP) for large prostates and MISTs when preserving sexual function is the priority. TURP remains the most commonly performed procedure in England.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
