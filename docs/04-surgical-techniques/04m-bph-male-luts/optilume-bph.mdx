# Optilume BPH — Paclitaxel-Coated Balloon for LUTS Secondary to BPH

The **Optilume BPH Catheter System** (Laborie / Urotronic) is an FDA-approved drug-device combination in which balloon dilation of the prostatic urethra is followed by a paclitaxel-coated balloon, to treat lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS) secondary to BPH. It pairs mechanical dilation with local antiproliferative drug delivery, intended to prevent the tissue recoil and re-narrowing that limited earlier balloon prostatoplasty.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> For positioning against other minimally invasive surgical therapies (MISTs), TURP and HoLEP, see the [BPH & Male LUTS chooser](/docs/surgical-techniques/bph-male-luts).

**Optilume BPH and the Optilume urethral DCB are different devices.** They share the paclitaxel-coated-balloon platform but have distinct indications. Optilume BPH treats LUTS secondary to BPH at the prostatic urethra (PINNACLE, EVEREST-I, SUMMIT studies). The urethral DCB treats recurrent anterior urethral stricture (ROBUST trials); see [Drug-Coated Balloon Therapy](/docs/surgical-techniques/drug-coated-balloon). 

---

## Mechanism of action

The device combines two therapeutic principles:

1. **Mechanical balloon dilation** of the prostatic urethra opens the obstructed channel and creates an anterior commissurotomy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
2. **Local paclitaxel delivery** during balloon inflation. Paclitaxel stabilizes microtubules and arrests dividing cells, inhibiting smooth-muscle and fibroblast proliferation that would otherwise drive re-stenosis and tissue recoil.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The concept is analogous to drug-coated balloons used in vascular interventions and to the Optilume urethral DCB. Optilume BPH is not ablative, resective or implant-based, which distinguishes it mechanistically from Rezūm (thermal ablation), HoLEP, TURP and Aquablation (resection, enucleation and hydroablation), UroLift (implant) and PAE (embolization).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Patient selection

The AUA 2026 BPH guideline states that clinicians may offer the intraprostatic drug-coated balloon for prostates of 20–80 cc, a prostatic urethral length of 32–55 mm and no obstructing median lobe (Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level Grade C).<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> The device is contraindicated with paclitaxel hypersensitivity. Because paclitaxel can persist in semen for up to 12 months, the guideline advises a condom or abstinence for 30 days and avoiding conception for at least 1 year.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

Trial entry criteria were as follows<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>:

- Men aged 50 years or older (PINNACLE: 50–80 years)
- Moderate-to-severe LUTS secondary to BPH (PINNACLE: IPSS ≥ 13)
- Prostate volume 20–80 g
- Peak urinary flow rate (Qmax) 5–15 mL/s in EVEREST-I and 5–12 mL/s in PINNACLE
- Prostatic urethral length 30–55 mm in EVEREST-I and 32–55 mm in PINNACLE
- No obstructing median lobe (the studies excluded median-lobe-dominant BPH)

Optilume BPH is an option when preservation of erectile and ejaculatory function is a priority, when an outpatient setting is desired, or when the patient wants a non-implant, non-ablative treatment.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

Median-lobe obstruction is a relative contraindication, and prostate volumes above 80 g are outside the studied range. Randomized data extend to 1 year against sham and uncontrolled follow-up to 4 years.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[8]](#ref8)[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

## Procedure

- **Setting:** outpatient, in an ambulatory surgery center or the office. In PINNACLE, 84.7% of the active arm and 87.5% of the sham arm were treated under deep sedation or general anesthesia; the AUA guideline notes that local anesthesia is possible.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- **Access:** transurethral delivery under direct cystoscopic visualization.
- **Predilation:** an uncoated dilation balloon is positioned under vision and inflated for about 1 minute to fracture the anterior commissure.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- **Drug delivery:** the paclitaxel-coated balloon is exchanged in, positioned the same way and inflated for at least 5 minutes, which extends the anterior commissurotomy and deposits drug on the treated tissue. Four sizes (30–45 mm treatment length) are matched to the prostatic urethral length.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Catheterization:** a Foley catheter is placed after the procedure. The PINNACLE protocol specified 2 days, and mean catheterization was 3.0 days in the active arm and 2.5 days in the sham arm.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Clinical efficacy

Three study programs make up the evidence base.

### EVEREST-I (early feasibility, single-arm)

Open-label, single-arm study of 80 men at 6 Latin American sites.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> 

| Outcome | Baseline | 1 year | 2 years |
|---|---|---|---|
| IPSS | 22.3 | 7.9 | 8.2 |
| Qmax (mL/s) | 10.9 | 18.4 | 17.2 |
| ≥ 40% IPSS responder rate | — | 81% | not reported in the abstract |

### PINNACLE (pivotal RCT)

Prospective, double-blind, sham-controlled RCT of 148 men (100 active, 48 sham) randomized 2:1 at 18 U.S. and Canadian centers. It is the only randomized evidence. Blinding was maintained to 1 year, and follow-up beyond 1 year was limited to the active arm.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

| Outcome | Active (1 yr) | Sham (3 mo) | Active (2 yr) |
|---|---|---|---|
| IPSS reduction | −11.5 ± 7.8 | −8.0 ± 8.3 | −12.4 (IPSS 23.4 → 11.0) |
| Qmax improvement | +10.3 mL/s (+125%) | — | +116.8% |
| Symptomatic responder (≥ 30% IPSS Δ, no retreatment) | — | — | 67.5% (56/83) |
| Sexual function (IIEF / MSHQ-EjD) | preserved | — | preserved |

No device- or treatment-related serious adverse events were reported beyond 12 months.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> Within the first year, five possibly device-related serious events occurred: four postprocedural hematuria events needing cystoscopic management or extended observation, and one urethral false passage.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### EVEREST-I long-term follow-up

In the same 80-man cohort, IPSS was reduced by 12.1 points and IPSS quality of life by 2.8 points at 4 years, and the mean Qmax improvement from baseline was +5.6 mL/s. No treatment-related adverse events were reported during long-term follow-up. The study was funded by the manufacturer and was single-arm.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

### SUMMIT (real-world, multi-center)

Prospective open-label study of 30 men at 5 Canadian centers.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> 

| Outcome | Baseline | 12 months |
|---|---|---|
| IPSS | 23.5 | 10.8 |
| Qmax (mL/s) | 8.7 | 14.7 |
| Sexual function | — | no significant change |

## Safety profile

The most common adverse events across studies were<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>:

- Hematuria, the most frequent (40% in the PINNACLE active arm), usually self-limited
- Urinary tract infection (14%)
- Dysuria (9.2%)
- Urge or mixed incontinence (8.2%), mild stress incontinence (7.1%) and bladder spasms (6.1%)
- Transient urinary retention

In PINNACLE these events were typically mild to moderate and resolved within 1 month.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Complication rates in EVEREST-I were significantly affected by device diameter.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Systemic paclitaxel exposure was minimal in the PINNACLE pharmacokinetic subset (mean maximum plasma concentration 0.4 ng/mL).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

---

## Sexual function preservation

EVEREST-I, PINNACLE (1 and 2 years) and SUMMIT reported no significant deterioration in erectile (IIEF-EF) or ejaculatory (MSHQ-EjD) function.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> Narrative reviews place it with UroLift and iTIND among the MISTs with the best sexual-function profiles, in contrast to TURP (retrograde ejaculation 60–75%, ED approximately 14%) and Aquablation (retrograde ejaculation 7–10%).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

---

## Comparison with other MISTs

No head-to-head RCTs directly compare Optilume BPH with other MISTs; comparisons are indirect via narrative reviews and network meta-analyses.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

| Procedure | IPSS Δ (12 mo) | Qmax Δ (12 mo) | Volume range | Anesthesia | Median lobe |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Optilume BPH | −11.5 to −12.4 | +10.3 mL/s | 20–80 g | Local / sedation | Excluded in trials |
| Rezūm | −11.3 | +5.6 | 30–80 g | Local / sedation | Treatable |
| UroLift (PUL) | −11.0 | +4.3 | &lt; 80 g (no median lobe) | Local / sedation | Excluded in original label |
| iTIND | −10.5 | +3.9 | up to ~ 75 g | Local / sedation | — |
| Aquablation | −15.1 | +10.3 | 30–150 g | General / regional | Treatable |
| PAE | −11.6 to −14.5 | +6.1 | Any size | Local / sedation | Treatable |
| TURP | −15.1 | +10.6 | up to ~ 80 g | General / regional | Treatable |

In PINNACLE the Qmax improvement (+10.3 mL/s at 1 year) is higher than the +3–6 mL/s usually reported for UroLift, Rezūm and PAE, and close to the values reported for TURP and Aquablation; the EVEREST-I 4-year improvement was +5.6 mL/s.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)[[22]](#ref22)</sup> The Cornu network meta-analysis, which did not include Optilume BPH or Aquablation, found that TURP gave the largest flow-rate improvement, while symptom scores and quality of life with PAE, PUL and WVTT were similar to TURP.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> These cross-trial comparisons are indirect and rest on different populations, so the table is a rough guide only.

## Durability and retreatment

| Procedure | Surgical retreatment | Follow-up horizon |
|---|---|---|
| Optilume BPH | 67.5% remained symptomatic responders at 2 yr (PINNACLE); EVEREST-I (uncontrolled) reported 4-yr outcomes without a retreatment rate in the abstract<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[22]](#ref22)</sup> | 2 yr (randomized cohort), 4 yr (single-arm) |
| Rezūm | 4.4% surgical retreatment at 5 yr<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | 5 yr |
| UroLift | ~ 6% / yr; 5-yr real-world ~ 22%<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> | 5 yr |
| Aquablation | ~ 4.1% at 5 yr<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | 5 yr |
| PAE | Retreatment rate is higher than with TURP (RR 3.20 short term, 3.80 at 13–24 months)<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> | 1–2 yr |
| iTIND | ~ 10% at 24 mo<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | 2 yr |
| TURP | Lowest retreatment of all options<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> | 5+ yr |

A network meta-analysis of reintervention rates ranked TURP first at 12 months, followed by Aquablation, iTIND, Rezūm and UroLift. Optilume BPH was not included and has no 5-year data.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> An Italian Delphi consensus flagged a higher retreatment risk for UroLift and iTIND but not for Rezūm or Aquablation.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

## Where Optilume BPH fits

Optilume BPH differs from other MISTs in these ways<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>:

- The 1-year Qmax improvement in PINNACLE is comparable to that reported for resective procedures (TURP, Aquablation) and larger than for most non-resective MISTs.
- Erectile and ejaculatory function did not change significantly across studies, as with UroLift, iTIND and Rezūm.
- It is delivered as an outpatient procedure with no implant and no tissue resection.
- Its mechanism (paclitaxel-coated balloon dilation) is neither ablative, resective nor implant-based.

Limitations:

- Randomized data end at 1 year against sham. Uncontrolled follow-up reaches 4 years.
- Median-lobe obstruction was excluded from the trials.
- Prostate volume is limited to 20–80 g.
- The AUA 2023 Guideline Amendment predates the pivotal trial's publication and did not address the device. The 2026 guideline added a Conditional Recommendation (Grade C).<sup>[[19]](#ref19)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

---

## Regulatory status

Optilume BPH is FDA-approved as a minimally invasive surgical therapy for LUTS due to BPH.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The same paclitaxel-coated balloon platform is FDA-approved for recurrent anterior urethral strictures under the ROBUST trial program; see [Drug-Coated Balloon Therapy](/docs/surgical-techniques/drug-coated-balloon).<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
