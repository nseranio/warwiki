# BPH & Male LUTS

This atlas page is the treatment-selection hub for benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH), benign prostatic obstruction (BPO), and bothersome male lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS). It deliberately separates the procedural choice from the disease background: for pathophysiology, complications, natural history, and medication pharmacology, see [Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia](/docs/clinical-conditions/03b-voiding-outlet/bladder-outlet-obstruction), [Alpha Blockers](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/voiding-outlet/alpha-blockers), [5-alpha Reductase Inhibitors](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/voiding-outlet/alpha-reductase-inhibitors), [PDE5 Inhibitors](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/sexual-medicine-andrology/pde5-inhibitors), [Anticholinergics](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/storage-oab/anticholinergics), and [Beta3 Agonists](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/storage-oab/beta3-agonists).

The operative question is what the least invasive treatment is that will solve this patient's dominant problem without spending the outcome they care about most.

---

:::info[Current guideline edition — 2026]

The 2026 AUA BPH guideline, published online May 7, replaces the 2023 amendment as the current reference. Its three parts cover evaluation (Part I),<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> medical therapy (Part II),<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> and procedural / surgical treatment (Part III);<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> the evidence search extends through December 15, 2025. Shared decision-making should explicitly address expected symptom relief, durability, sexual effects, retreatment, and follow-up. Older edition-specific statements on individual pages should be checked against the [current AUA guideline](https://www.auanet.org/guidelines-and-quality/guidelines/bph-guideline) and the relevant device instructions before applying eligibility criteria.

## Decision Framework

### 1. Name the symptom phenotype

Male LUTS should be sorted into three overlapping buckets before treatment selection:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

| Phenotype | Symptoms | Practical implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Voiding / obstructive** | Hesitancy, weak stream, intermittency, straining, incomplete emptying | Think BPO, urethral stricture, bladder neck stenosis, dysfunctional voiding, or detrusor underactivity |
| **Storage / irritative** | Frequency, urgency, nocturia, urgency incontinence | Think OAB, obstruction-driven detrusor overactivity, nocturnal polyuria, diuretics, sleep disease, diabetes, or neurologic disease |
| **Post-micturition** | Post-void dribbling, terminal dribble, incomplete-emptying sensation | Often mixed outlet + pelvic-floor + urethral trapping physiology |

Mixed symptoms are the rule. The dominant bother determines the first treatment target, while the outlet evaluation determines whether storage-directed treatment is safe.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### 2. Perform the core evaluation

For all men with bothersome LUTS, the core evaluation should document baseline severity, screen for alternative diagnoses, and identify complications that force early intervention.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

| Element | What it answers |
|---|---|
| **History + medication review** | Fluid timing, caffeine/alcohol, diuretics, anticholinergics, antihistamines, sympathomimetics, opioids, neurologic disease, constipation, sleep symptoms, sexual function |
| **IPSS / QoL score** | Severity: mild 0-7, moderate 8-19, severe 20-35; a 3-point decrease is usually clinically meaningful |
| **Focused exam + DRE** | Prostate texture, nodularity, neurologic clues, palpable bladder; DRE estimates volume poorly |
| **Urinalysis** | Infection, hematuria, glycosuria, proteinuria |
| **PSA when it will change management** | Cancer-screening decision and surrogate for gland size / progression risk; PSA &gt;1.5 ng/mL often suggests prostate volume &gt;30 mL<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **PVR** | Emptying safety, retention risk, antimuscarinic / beta3 safety |
| **Uroflowmetry** | Qmax and curve shape; low flow can mean obstruction or weak detrusor |
| **Diary / frequency-volume chart** | Nocturia, polyuria, urgency, and fluid-timing mismatch |

Pre-procedural planning should add prostate volume and shape assessment, median-lobe / intravesical protrusion assessment, and cystoscopy or urodynamics when the diagnosis is uncertain or the result would change the procedure.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### 3. Identify early-procedure triggers

Do not slowly escalate medication when BPH is already complicated. Move to urologic procedural planning when any of the following are present:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- Refractory, recurrent, or catheter-dependent urinary retention
- Recurrent UTI attributable to incomplete emptying
- Bladder stones
- Recurrent gross hematuria attributed to BPH after malignancy / stone evaluation
- Hydronephrosis or renal deterioration from outlet obstruction
- Large or rising PVR with decompensation concern
- Patient preference to avoid chronic medication after informed counseling

### 4. Choose a path by bother, risk, anatomy, and preference

| Clinical scenario | First treatment path | When to move on |
|---|---|---|
| **Mild or non-bothersome LUTS** | Watchful waiting with lifestyle / behavioral changes; EAU 2026 advises review at six months, then annually if stable<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | Worsening IPSS, new bother, retention, hematuria, infection, stones, renal deterioration |
| **Bothersome moderate-to-severe LUTS, no complications** | Discuss behavioral, medical, and procedural options in light of patient goals | Reassess benefit, burden, adverse effects, and preference at follow-up |
| **Voiding-predominant, any prostate size** | Alpha blocker; tadalafil if ED / sexual-function priority and no nitrate contraindication | Persistent obstruction or recurrent retention |
| **Large prostate / PSA surrogate / progression risk** | 5-ARI, often with alpha blocker during onset period | Persistent bother after 6 months or complications |
| **Storage-predominant with acceptable PVR** | Beta3 agonist or antimuscarinic, usually after outlet risk is assessed; the EAU guideline advises against antimuscarinics when PVR is &gt; 150 mL (weak recommendation)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | Persistent urgency after outlet therapy or unsafe emptying |
| **Complicated BPH or refractory LUTS** | Procedure selected by prostate size, median lobe, retention status, anticoagulation, sexual goals, anesthetic risk | Retreatment or salvage pathway if symptoms recur |

### 5. Select the procedure by the trade-off the patient accepts

TURP, HoLEP / endoscopic enucleation, and simple prostatectomy remain the durability anchors. In a 2023 network meta-analysis, short-term symptom and quality-of-life outcomes after PAE, PUL, and water vapor therapy appeared similar to TURP; TURP produced greater flow gains, while ejaculatory outcomes favored water vapor therapy and PUL.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> A 2021 Cochrane review found major-adverse-event and retreatment comparisons uncertain for several MISTs.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

| Priority | Better-fitting options | Main caution |
|---|---|---|
| **Maximum durability / strongest deobstruction** | HoLEP / laser enucleation, bipolar TURP, simple prostatectomy for very large glands | Ejaculatory dysfunction is common; transient incontinence can follow enucleation |
| **Very large gland** | HoLEP / enucleation, robotic or open simple prostatectomy, selected Aquablation for 80–150 mL glands (WATER II single-arm cohort)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>, PAE in selected high-risk patients | Office MISTs may underdeliver when bulk reduction is needed. WATER III found similar three-month IPSS changes after Aquablation and laser enucleation in 80–180 mL glands, but only 66 of 170 men with three-month data were randomized; comparative durability remains unknown.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| **Bleeding risk / anticoagulation** | GreenLight PVP, HoLEP in expert hands, PAE in selected patients | Anticoagulation strategy still requires proceduralist-specific planning |
| **Ejaculation preservation** | PUL, water vapor thermal therapy, iTIND, aquablation, Optilume BPH | Usually accepts less deobstruction, slower onset, or less mature retreatment data; in network meta-analysis, ejaculatory function favored water vapor therapy and PUL over TURP<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>. In the FDA iTIND pivotal trial, the prespecified three-month IPSS comparison with sham was not statistically significant (p = 0.104), although the 12-month within-group co-primary endpoint was met.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> The Optilume BPH label contraindicates use in patients with known hypersensitivity to paclitaxel or structurally related compounds, active urinary tract infection, an artificial urinary sphincter, or a penile prosthesis. It advises abstinence or condom use for 30 days after treatment and highly effective contraception for men with partners of childbearing potential to avoid fathering a child for at least 12 months after treatment.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| **Median lobe / intravesical protrusion** | TURP, HoLEP / enucleation, aquablation, water vapor therapy in selected median lobes | Classic PUL selection is less favorable for obstructive median-lobe anatomy.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Office / ambulatory treatment** | PUL, water vapor therapy, iTIND, Optilume BPH, PAE | PUL has a higher retreatment rate than TURP; a systematic review also found higher reintervention after PAE than TURP or enucleation. Direct long-term comparisons with TURP or HoLEP remain limited for water vapor therapy, iTIND, and Optilume BPH.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Catheter-dependent retention** | HoLEP / enucleation, TURP, simple prostatectomy (AUA 2026 recommends a resective technology, Expert Opinion); selected PAE if surgical risk is prohibitive | PAE may yield similar IPSS improvement to TURP at 12 weeks–12 months in mixed LUTS/BPO trials, but likely has more retreatment; this does not establish catheter-free benefit in retention.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> In one five-year RCT, 20/48 PAE-treated men underwent TURP and only 18/48 attended five-year follow-up; catheter-free outcomes for the 9/48 with baseline indwelling catheters were not reported separately.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> Confirm detrusor contractility when chronic retention suggests underactive bladder |

## Follow-Up Anchors

| Treatment | First reassessment | What counts as response |
|---|---|---|
| **Behavioral therapy** | 6-12 weeks | Lower bother, fewer urgency/nocturia episodes, stable PVR |
| **Alpha blocker** | Days to 4 weeks | IPSS decrease >=3, better stream, fewer voiding symptoms |
| **Tadalafil** | 4-6 weeks | IPSS decrease >=3 plus sexual-function benefit if ED present |
| **Anticholinergic / beta3 agonist** | 4-8 weeks | Fewer urgency/frequency episodes without unsafe PVR rise |
| **5-ARI** | 3-6 months | Symptom stability or improvement, prostate shrinkage, reduced progression risk; PSA interpretation adjusted |
| **MIST** | 6 weeks, 3 months, 12 months | IPSS / QoL response, catheter-free voiding, no recurrent retention |
| **TURP / enucleation / simple prostatectomy** | 4–6 weeks after catheter removal; further review if symptoms persist or adverse events occur<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | Assess symptom relief, flow, PVR, erectile and ejaculatory function; monitor incontinence, stricture, and BNC |

AUA 2026 advises evaluating every man within six months of starting treatment, with a standardized symptom score and an adverse-event review; most medications show effect by about 4 weeks, while 5-ARIs take 3-6 months.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
