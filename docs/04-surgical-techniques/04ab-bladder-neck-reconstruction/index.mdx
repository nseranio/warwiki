# Bladder Neck Reconstruction

This page covers reconstruction of the **bladder neck and vesicourethral anastomosis** — the salvage of bladder neck contracture (BNC) and vesicourethral anastomotic stenosis (VUAS) after radical prostatectomy, endoscopic prostate surgery, or radiation, plus the continence-creating outlet procedures (Young-Dees-Leadbetter, Kropp, Pippi-Salle, bladder-neck closure) used when the outlet is irreparably incompetent. For reservoir-side reconstruction (augmentation cystoplasty, catheterizable channels, ileovesicostomy), see [Bladder Augmentation & Catheterizable Channels](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction).

> Success rates should be interpreted cautiously — definitions of success vary (patent lumen, continence, freedom from reintervention), follow-up intervals differ, and most data come from small case series.

## Master Decision Framework

The matrices below summarize the primary decision points for bladder-neck contracture (post-endoscopic-prostate-surgery) and vesicourethral anastomotic stenosis (post-radical-prostatectomy ± radiation). Both conditions share the AUA 2023 escalation pattern — endoscopic incision → repeat endoscopic ± adjunct → open / robotic reconstruction — but differ critically in their **proximity to the external sphincter**, the **role of radiation**, and the **continence stakes** at every tier. The AUA grants a **Conditional Recommendation (Grade C)** for robotic or open reconstruction in recalcitrant disease, with reconstruction tailored to prior radiation, continence status, and patient preference.

**Setting.** BNC after endoscopic prostate surgery (TURP, HoLEP, ThuLEP, monopolar / bipolar resection). Incidence ~2–4.7%. Per AUA 2023, transurethral incision and bladder-neck resection have comparable outcomes, and **repeat endoscopic treatment may be necessary**. ≥ 2 prior failed endoscopic treatments is the strongest predictor of further endoscopic failure. Open Y-V plasty has reported 100% patency (Shamout 2022) but a high rate of persistent storage symptoms; staged AUS or sling after stable patency yields 91% patient satisfaction with continence (Brede 2014).

| Clinical Scenario | First-Line | Second-Line (if first fails) | Recalcitrant / Refractory |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First-time post-TURP / endoscopic-BPH BNC** | [TUIBNC](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/tuit-triamcinolone) (Collins / cold knife at 3 & 9 o'clock) or BN resection ± steroid injection | Repeat endoscopic ± mitomycin C, triamcinolone, or betamethasone (Wu 2024); endoscopic Y-V (Abramowitz 2021) | Open / robotic [Y-V plasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/yv-plasty) or [T-plasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/t-plasty-bnc) |
| **Highly recurrent BNC (≥ 2 endoscopic failures, non-radiated)** | Open or laparoscopic [Y-V plasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/yv-plasty) (Shamout 2022; Zang 2022) | [Open retropubic reanastomosis](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/transperineal-reanastomosis) (Pfalzgraf 2011); [robotic subtrigonal BMG inlay](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/subtrigonal-inlay) (Avallone 2019) | [Urinary diversion](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion) |
| **BNC + concurrent severe SUI** | Stabilize patency endoscopically or surgically → delayed [AUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) or [male sling](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/male-urethral-slings) (Brede 2014; ~91% satisfaction) | Reconstruction + delayed [AUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) | [Urinary diversion](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion) |
| **Orthotopic neobladder BNC** | Endoscopic incision + 1-month CIC (58% vs 32% success with vs without; Pariser 2015) | Repeat endoscopic ± steroid injection | [Continent catheterizable channel (Mitrofanoff)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/catheterizable-channels); [urinary diversion](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion) |
| **Devastated bladder outlet (failed reconstruction; densely fibrotic)** | Permanent suprapubic tube or chronic catheter drainage | [Urinary diversion](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion) ± cystectomy (~5–8% of refractory BNC; Martins 2021) | [Continent catheterizable channel (Mitrofanoff)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/catheterizable-channels) |

  <TabItem value="vuas">

**Setting.** VUAS after radical prostatectomy ± radiation. Incidence ~4.8% post-RP at median 3.4 months. Risk modifiers: adjuvant radiation, BMI, larger prostate, perioperative urine leak, transfusion, non-nerve-sparing technique; robotic prostatectomy and complete nerve-sparing are protective (Britton 2023). Single-procedure endoscopic success ~44%, but **91% of patients are ultimately managed successfully with repeat endoscopic procedures** (mean 2.1 treatments; LaBossiere 2016) — unlike anterior urethral strictures, repeat endoscopic treatment for VUAS is justified. Holmium laser may be superior to other endoscopic modalities (LaBossiere 2016). The robotic platform offers comparable patency to open techniques with significantly lower de novo incontinence (16.6% vs 100% for perineal; Savun 2025).

| Clinical Scenario | First-Line | Second-Line (if first fails) | Recalcitrant / Refractory |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First-time VUAS after RP (non-radiated)** | [Endoscopic incision](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/tuit-triamcinolone) (holmium laser preferred) or dilation | Repeat endoscopic ± mitomycin C (conflicting data per AUA); consider [DCB (Optilume)](/docs/surgical-techniques/drug-coated-balloon) (Berg 2025; HR 0.40 for recurrence) | Robotic reconstruction (TURNS / RBNR; Shakir 2022, Kirshenbaum 2018; ~75% patency, ~82% continence) |
| **First-time VUAS after RP + radiation** | [Endoscopic incision](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/tuit-triamcinolone) ± mitomycin C (expect shorter recurrence interval; Hacker 2022, Rozanski 2021) | Repeat endoscopic ± [DCB](/docs/surgical-techniques/drug-coated-balloon) | [Perineal dorsal onlay BMG urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/dorsal-bmg-bnc) (Sterling 2024; no de novo SUI, 86.6% satisfaction); robotic reconstruction |
| **Recurrent VUAS (≥ 2 endoscopic failures)** | Robotic reconstruction (RBNR / RTV-BNR; Lee / Eun 2025 — 91% patency, no de novo SUI) | [Perineal D-BMG urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/dorsal-bmg-bnc); endourethroplasty (Doležel 2024) | [Continent catheterizable channel](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/catheterizable-channels); [urinary diversion](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion) |
| **Obliterative VUAS (~47% of reconstruction candidates)** | Robotic scar excision + mucosa-to-mucosa re-anastomosis ± [transperineal urethral advancement](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/transperineal-reanastomosis) (Rodriguez 2024); [bladder flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/tanagho-flap) | Open retropubic reanastomosis (~70–100% patency, ~10% de novo SUI; Pfalzgraf 2011) | [Urinary diversion](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion) or [continent catheterizable channel (Mitrofanoff)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/catheterizable-channels) |
| **VUAS + concurrent severe SUI** | Achieve stable patent outlet first → tissue maturation 3–6 mo → delayed [AUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) or [male sling](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/male-urethral-slings) | Robotic / retropubic reconstruction (avoid perineal — 83–100% de novo SUI) + delayed [AUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) | [Urinary diversion](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion) |
| **Devastated posterior urethra (multiple failed reconstructions)** | Permanent suprapubic tube | [Urinary diversion](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion) ± cystectomy | [Continent catheterizable channel (Mitrofanoff)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/catheterizable-channels) |

**Key BNC-vs-VUAS distinguishing principles.** VUAS sits adjacent to the external sphincter, making continence preservation the dominant concern at every tier. Repeat endoscopic treatment is justified (91% cumulative success) — unlike anterior urethral strictures. Radiation history is the strongest modifier of treatment success, and the robotic platform has emerged as the preferred reconstructive approach when continence preservation matters. Dorsal onlay buccal mucosal graft (D-BMGU) is uniquely valuable in the post-prostatectomy + radiation subgroup because it avoids urethral transection.
