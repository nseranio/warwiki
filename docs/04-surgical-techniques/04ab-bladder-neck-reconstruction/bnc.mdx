# Bladder Neck Contracture (BNC)

Reconstruction of the bladder neck after **endoscopic prostate surgery** (TURP, HoLEP, ThuLEP, monopolar / bipolar resection). Incidence ~2–4.7%. Per AUA 2023, transurethral incision and bladder-neck resection have comparable outcomes, and **repeat endoscopic treatment may be necessary**. ≥ 2 prior failed endoscopic treatments is the strongest predictor of further endoscopic failure. Open Y-V plasty has reported 100% patency (Shamout 2022) but a high rate of persistent storage symptoms; staged AUS or sling after stable patency yields 91% patient satisfaction with continence (Brede 2014).

> Success rates should be interpreted cautiously — definitions of success vary (patent lumen, continence, freedom from reintervention), follow-up intervals differ, and most data come from small case series.

---

## Decision Framework

| Clinical Scenario | First-Line | Second-Line (if first fails) | Recalcitrant / Refractory |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First-time post-TURP / endoscopic-BPH BNC** | [TUIBNC](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/transurethral-incision-bnc) (Collins / cold knife at 3 & 9 o'clock) or BN resection | Repeat endoscopic ± mitomycin C; [TUITMR](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/transurethral-incision-transverse-mucosal-realignment) (Abramowitz 2021) | Open / robotic [Y-V plasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/yv-plasty) or [T-plasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/t-plasty-bnc) |
| **Highly recurrent BNC (≥ 2 endoscopic failures, non-radiated)** | Open or laparoscopic [Y-V plasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/yv-plasty) (Shamout 2022; Zang 2022) | [Open retropubic reanastomosis](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/transperineal-reanastomosis) (Pfalzgraf 2011); [robotic subtrigonal BMG inlay](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/subtrigonal-inlay) (Avallone 2019) | [Urinary diversion](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion) |
| **BNC + concurrent severe SUI** | Stabilize patency endoscopically or surgically → delayed [AUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) or [male sling](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/male-urethral-slings) (Brede 2014; ~91% satisfaction) | Reconstruction + delayed [AUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) | [Urinary diversion](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion) |
| **Orthotopic neobladder BNC** | Endoscopic incision + 1-month CIC (58% vs 32% success with vs without; Pariser 2015) | Repeat endoscopic ± steroid injection | [Continent catheterizable channel (Mitrofanoff)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/catheterizable-channels); [urinary diversion](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion) |
| **Devastated bladder outlet (failed reconstruction; densely fibrotic)** | Permanent suprapubic tube or chronic catheter drainage | [Bladder-neck closure](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/outlet-continence/bladder-neck-closure) + [continent catheterizable channel (Mitrofanoff)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/catheterizable-channels) | [Urinary diversion](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion) ± cystectomy (~5–8% of refractory BNC; Martins 2021) |
