# Transvesical Approaches to BNC and VUAS

Transvesical access reaches the bladder neck and vesicourethral anastomosis from inside the bladder, without dissecting the space of Retzius. Three operations have been reported through this route: robotic bladder-neck reconstruction with mucosa-to-mucosa anastomosis for VUAS, subtrigonal buccal graft inlay for refractory bladder-neck contracture (BNC), and transvesical prostatectomy for recalcitrant BNC with residual prostate after HoLEP. They treat different anatomy and do not share a success rate.

## Robotic transvesical bladder-neck reconstruction

Lee and Eun (2025) describe a posterior transvesical approach for VUAS. The bladder is opened and the fibrotic scar is excised circumferentially. A tension-free, watertight mucosa-to-mucosa anastomosis is then made between bladder-neck mucosa and the posterior urethra. The retrospective single-institution cohort included 11 patients treated in 2021 to 2024. All strictures were shorter than 2 cm, and two patients (18%) had prior salvage radiotherapy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Finding | Index cohort |
|---|---|
| Success definition | No further surgical intervention for recurrent stenosis |
| Success | 10/11 (91%) at median 22 months |
| New SUI | None reported |
| Median operative time / EBL | 189 minutes / 100 mL |
| Major-complication definition | Clavien grade **greater than 2**, not grade 2 or higher |

Working from within the bladder gives direct view of the mucosal edges and may reduce the need to dissect a hostile retropubic plane after prostatectomy, radiation or repeated endoscopy. It does not mean that every transvesical operation avoids the retropubic space entirely or never needs a flap. The series is small and has no comparator, so it does not show less SUI or better patency than other approaches.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The retropubic robotic approach used by the TURNS consortium dissects the space of Retzius, excises the fibrotic tissue and reconstructs with a primary anastomosis or an anterior bladder flap. The 32-patient TURNS cohort had 50% prior radiation and 47% obliterative VUAS. At median 12 months, 24 (75%) were patent and 26 (81%) were voiding per urethra. Of 13 men without preexisting SUI, 11 (85%) remained continent.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The earlier 12-patient TURNS report had 75% patency at median 13.5 months, and 82% of men without preoperative incontinence were continent. Its 216-minute operative time and 85 mL EBL are means from that cohort, not medians from the 32-patient study.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> A systematic review of ten robotic bladder-neck reconstruction series (119 men; techniques and etiologies varied) reported pooled patency of 80% and pooled de novo incontinence of 17% (8/47).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

| Feature | Robotic transvesical (Lee 2025) | Robotic retropubic (TURNS, Shakir 2022) |
|---|---|---|
| Access | Posterior, through the bladder | Anterior, space of Retzius |
| Reconstruction | Mucosa-to-mucosa anastomosis | Primary anastomosis or anterior bladder flap |
| Patients | 11, VUAS only | 32, VUAS |
| Radiation | 18% | 50% |
| Stenosis | All shorter than 2 cm | 47% obliterative |
| Patency | 10/11 at median 22 months | 24/32 at median 12 months |

The cohorts differ in radiation exposure, stenosis length and obliteration, so the table is not a head-to-head comparison.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Subtrigonal buccal graft inlay

Avallone and Flynn (2019) describe one robotic transvesical subtrigonal buccal mucosal graft (BMG) inlay for refractory BNC after photovaporization of the prostate. The bladder is opened, the contracture is approached from the subtrigonal aspect, and a graft (5 × 5 × 4 cm in this case) is inlaid to augment the bladder-neck lumen. Qmax improved from 2 to 27 mL/s and PVR from 200 to 3 mL. The urethral catheter was removed at 2 weeks and the suprapubic catheter at 4 weeks, when VCUG showed no obstruction or extravasation. There was no recurrence at last follow-up. This is a single case report and does not establish an expected success rate.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

See [subtrigonal inlay](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/subtrigonal-inlay) for the named technique. Its graft bed and orientation differ from a perineal dorsal-onlay operation.

## Transvesical prostatectomy after HoLEP

Yu and colleagues (2025) report eight selected men with recurrent BNC after HoLEP and residual prostate tissue, treated by transvesical robot-assisted radical prostatectomy. The residual prostate was excised transvesically and a new vesicourethral anastomosis was created. All eight met the authors' success endpoint (17 Fr cystoscope passage or flow above 15 mL/s) and were continent at 3 months. Minimum follow-up was 6 months. At 6 months Qmax rose from 4.15 to 17.7 mL/s, PVR fell from 190 to 17.5 mL and IPSS fell from 31 to 10.5. Median operative time was 123 minutes, median EBL 50 mL and median hospital stay 7.5 days.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

This is a small feasibility series of an extensive salvage operation. It removes the prostate as the presumed source of recurrent contracture, and it does not establish that residual prostate causes all recurrent BNC. The operation applies only when prostate tissue remains, and it is not an option for VUAS after a completed radical prostatectomy. See [salvage prostatectomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/outlet-continence/salvage-prostatectomy) for the reconstructive context.

## Clinical role and limitations

Access and reconstruction are chosen by stenosis site, length, residual lumen, tissue quality, prior surgery or radiation, bladder function and continence goals. EAU states that reconstruction may be considered when endoluminal treatment repeatedly fails or the stenosis is obliterated, with the choice depending on length, location, calibre, etiology, continence, bladder function, radiation history, patient preference and surgeon expertise.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> The AUA 2023 amendment gives robotic or open reconstruction for recalcitrant BNC or VUAS a Conditional Recommendation (Evidence Level Grade C). The 2025 transvesical reports postdate it.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

All three reports above are small retrospective series or a case report from single institutions. Follow-up is short to intermediate, and the techniques require advanced robotic skill and experience with intravesical robotic surgery. No direct comparison establishes an optimal transvesical technique or an advantage over [Y-V plasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/yv-plasty), perineal repair or standard retropubic reconstruction. Counseling covers recurrence, incontinence, tissue injury and possible additional reconstruction.
