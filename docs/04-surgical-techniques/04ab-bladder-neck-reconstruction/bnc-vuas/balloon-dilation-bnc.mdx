# Balloon Dilation for Bladder Neck Contracture

**Balloon dilation** for bladder neck contracture (BNC) and post-prostatectomy vesicourethral anastomotic stenosis (VUAS) serves two distinct roles: a **standalone treatment** with modest success (~59% in the Ramchandani 1994 series), and — more commonly today — as the **first step before incision or resection**, where the combined approach achieves 82–94% patency. **Drug-coated balloon (DCB) dilation** is an emerging refinement with significantly improved recurrence-free survival vs standard endoscopic treatment in posterior urethral stenosis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For the incision counterpart, see [Transurethral Incision of BNC (TUIBNC)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/transurethral-incision-bnc). For the resection counterpart, see [Transurethral Bladder Neck Resection (TURBN)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/transurethral-bladder-neck-resection). For the dedicated DCB / Optilume page (anterior urethra context, with cross-relevant data), see [Drug-Coated Balloon](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/drug-coated-balloon-pointer). For the broader BNC framework, see [Bladder Neck Contracture](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc).

## Standalone Balloon Dilation

The Ramchandani 1994 study evaluated **fluoroscopically guided transurethral balloon dilation** as a standalone treatment for VUAS after radical prostatectomy in 27 patients:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- **Success rate**: **59%** (16/27).
- **No de novo urinary incontinence** in any patient — a notable advantage over bladder neck incision, where one patient developed de novo incontinence.
- **Refractoriness to dilation** was attributed to **dense scar tissue** that could not be adequately disrupted by radial pressure alone.
- Of the 11 patients who failed balloon dilation, 10 underwent subsequent transurethral incision; 70% required repeat attempts or additional dilation.

The lower success of standalone dilation vs incision or resection (59% vs 72–82%) reflects its fundamental limitation: dilation **stretches and fractures** scar tissue but does **not excise or deeply divide** it, leaving the fibrotic substrate largely intact.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Balloon Dilation as a Preparatory Step

In contemporary practice, balloon dilation is most commonly used as the **first step in a combined procedure** — dilating the contracture to admit the resectoscope, then performing deep incisions or resection.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

| Combined approach | Series | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **24 Fr balloon → bilateral Collins-knife incisions at 3 & 9 o'clock** (Nealon 2022) | n = 123 | **82%** single-procedure / **94%** two-procedure success<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Transurethral columnar balloon dilation + holmium laser incision** (Sun 2023) — day-surgery model | n = 41 | All completed within 24 h; significant IPSS / Qmax / QoL / PVR improvement at 1 and 6 mo (p < 0.01)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |

For the canonical TUIBNC technique (Nealon protocol) including the dilation step, see [TUIBNC](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/transurethral-incision-bnc).

## Drug-Coated Balloon (DCB) Dilation

The **Berg 2025 real-world comparative study** evaluated **DCB dilation vs standard endoscopic treatment** in 141 patients with posterior urethral stenosis (VUAS and BNC):<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

| Domain | DCB (n = 65) | Standard (n = 76) | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recurrence-free survival | Significantly improved | — | p = 0.013 |
| Multivariate predictor of recurrence-free survival | Treatment modality | — | **HR 0.40** (95% CI 0.19–0.87, p = 0.021) — 60% reduction |
| Functional outcomes | PVR 55 → 0 mL (p < 0.05); IPSS / Qmax / QoL improved | — | DCB favorable |

An international survey of 102 urologists using DCB found **65% report off-label use for bladder-neck stenoses**; higher-volume users (≥ 10 cases / year) were significantly more likely to use DCB for BNC (OR 4.66, 95% CI 1.55–14.03, p = 0.006).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

For the device biology, ROBUST trial program, and full DCB evidence base, see [Drug-Coated Balloon](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/drug-coated-balloon-pointer).

## Comparison Across Balloon Approaches

| Approach | Success | Incontinence risk | Key advantage | Key limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Standalone balloon dilation** | 59% | **Very low (0% Ramchandani)** | Lowest incontinence risk; simple technique | Lowest efficacy; dense scar refractory<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Balloon dilation + incision / resection** | 82–94% (1–2 procedures) | Moderate | Standardized, high patency | Requires resectoscope; sphincter risk<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Columnar balloon + holmium laser** | ~97.6% at 12 mo | 14.6% urge incontinence | Day-surgery feasible | Small series; limited follow-up<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Drug-coated balloon (Optilume)** | 85–90% short-term | Not well characterized | **60% reduction in recurrence (HR 0.40)** | Off-label for BNC; limited long-term data<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |

## Complications

Balloon dilation carries the **lowest complication profile** among endoscopic BNC treatments:

| Complication | Rate / note |
|---|---|
| **De novo urinary incontinence** | **0%** with standalone dilation (Ramchandani) — the principal advantage; sphincter mechanism is not deeply disrupted<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Urge incontinence (combined balloon + holmium laser) | ~14.6%<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Recurrence** | ~**41% standalone** — usually due to dense scar; primary limitation<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Bleeding, UTI | Uncommon |
| Perforation / extravasation | Rare; possible with aggressive dilation |

## Clinical Positioning

The AUA 2023 guideline notes that all endoscopic techniques have similar success rates (50–80%) for first-time VUAS, and no studies directly compare the different treatment strategies.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

In practice:

- **Standalone balloon dilation** is best suited to **mild, early, or first-time BNC** without anticipated dense scar — when **minimizing incontinence risk is the priority** and lower efficacy is acceptable.
- **Balloon dilation as a preparatory step** before incision / resection is the most widely used combined approach (canonical Nealon protocol).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Drug-coated balloon dilation** is an emerging option with promising early data — **60% reduction in recurrence** (Berg 2025) — though it remains off-label for BNC and long-term durability data are needed.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

---

## Key Principles

- Balloon dilation has **two distinct roles**: standalone treatment (~59% success) and preparatory step before incision / resection (combined success 82–94%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Standalone dilation has the lowest incontinence risk** of all endoscopic BNC treatments — the principal advantage.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Standalone is fundamentally limited by dense scar** that radial pressure alone cannot disrupt — explains the ~41% recurrence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **The Nealon 2022 standardized protocol** (24 Fr balloon → bilateral 3 & 9 o'clock Collins-knife incisions) is the canonical combined approach with 82% single-procedure and 94% two-procedure success.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Drug-coated balloon dilation** reduces recurrence by ~60% vs standard endoscopic treatment in posterior urethral stenosis (Berg 2025) — promising but off-label for BNC.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Day-surgery columnar balloon + holmium laser** is feasible with high short-term success but limited follow-up.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- DCB use for BNC is concentrated among **higher-volume urologists** (≥ 10 cases / year, OR 4.66) — suggesting a learning curve and selection effect.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
