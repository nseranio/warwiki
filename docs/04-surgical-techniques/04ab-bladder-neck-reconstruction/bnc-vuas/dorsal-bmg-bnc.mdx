# Dorsal Onlay Buccal Mucosal Graft Urethroplasty for Posterior Stenosis

Dorsal BMG augmentation is an option for selected refractory posterior stenoses with a usable urethral plate and viable recipient bed. It aims to widen the lumen without circumferential transection. Nontransection has not been shown to preserve the sphincter or to cause less incontinence than another repair. Bladder-neck stenosis, post-prostatectomy VUAS and bulbomembranous stenosis are different anatomical populations.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Related pages: [BNC](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc), [VUAS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/vuas), and [subtrigonal inlay](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/subtrigonal-inlay) (the transvesical graft technique).

## Selection

Assess stenosis location, length and caliber, prior radiation or operations, baseline continence, fistula or necrosis, bladder function and future drainage needs. The Sterling cohort excluded obliterative disruptions requiring excisional reconstruction and did not include severely contracted, fistulizing or necrotic bladders, so its results do not extend to those cases.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The EAU guideline advises individualized posterior reconstruction in experienced centers. Complete obliteration is not suitable for endoluminal treatment. Open or robotic reconstruction, chronic drainage and diversion are the alternatives, chosen by tissue and bladder status. No guideline names dorsal BMG as the preferred technique for radiated or nonradiated VUAS.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Shahrour perineal technique

The original four-patient VUAS report describes these steps:<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

1. Perineal exposure and bulbar dissection, with dorsal access beneath the pubic bone toward the anastomosis.
2. Dorsal opening through the stenosis to the bladder neck, preserving viable tissue and protecting the sphincter and rectum.
3. Buccal graft fixation proximally at 11, 12 and 1 o'clock; a [ski/J-hook needle](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/ski-needle-j-hook) can assist deep suturing.
4. Interrupted graft quilting to pubic periosteum and continuous fixation to the urethral edges with 4-0 polyglactin.
5. Catheter drainage followed by urethrogram and a voiding trial at 3 weeks in that report.

**The recipient bed depends on the technique.** Pubic periosteal quilting is described in Shahrour's operation. Other dorsal BMG approaches fix the graft to periurethral tissue, corpora or intercrural tissues. A perineal approach can avoid abdominal access in selected anatomy but does not remove the risk of rectal injury or the possible need for a combined approach.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Outcomes with correct populations

| Study | Population | Outcome and limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Shahrour 2019 | 4 post-prostatectomy VUAS patients, 3 radiated | All patent at 3 months. **All were incontinent before and after surgery**, so the series cannot measure new incontinence risk in initially continent patients.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| Sterling 2024 | 45 post-prostatectomy plus radiation patients: **21 anastomotic and 24 bulbomembranous stenoses** | 7 recurrences; median follow-up 21 months among patients without recurrence. No de novo SUI was reported. Baseline and postoperative continence counts differ between sections of the publication, so a 0/45 at-risk rate cannot be derived.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Angulo 2021 | 107 membranous/bulbomembranous stenoses after benign prostate surgery; **BNC excluded** | 10 recurrences at mean 59.3 months. One new SUI case among **87 patients known continent before surgery**; the paper's 0.9% uses all 107 as denominator. It is not a BNC cohort.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Doležel 2024 | 38 mixed posterior cases, 27 perineal and 11 endourethroplasty | Three-year recurrence-free survival 65% overall, 81% when auxiliary DVIU is included; new incontinence 2/18 initially continent patients across both approaches. Neither endpoint is specific to dorsal perineal BMG.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |

These uncontrolled cohorts do not show superiority over excision and reanastomosis or Y-V plasty. Satisfaction scores apply to the responding patients, not every enrolled patient.

## Recurrence, complications and follow-up

In Angulo's analysis, which had few events (10), radiation, two or more prior dilations and postoperative complications were associated with recurrence. The article gives differing adjusted estimates for prior dilation in the abstract and main results.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Discuss recurrence, oral donor-site morbidity, urinary leakage, infection, fistula, sexual effects and new or worsened SUI. Reassess symptoms, flow, residual and patency after healing. A short recurrence may be amenable to a further procedure, and Doležel counted this as auxiliary treatment separate from primary success.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For persistent SUI, assess bladder function and establish stable patency before considering an AUS. Prior radiation and urethral reconstruction can complicate later device surgery, and nontransection has not been shown to make AUS placement safer.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
