# Dorsal Onlay Buccal Mucosal Graft Urethroplasty for BNC and VUAS

**Dorsal onlay buccal mucosal graft urethroplasty (D-BMGU)** is the **preferred graft-placement technique** among reconstructive urologists for bulbar urethral strictures (favored by 66% of GURS members over ventral placement)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> and has emerged as a key option for managing refractory **[VUAS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/vuas)** and **[BNC](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc)** after radical prostatectomy (with or without radiation), as well as posterior urethral stenosis after endoscopic prostate procedures (TURP, HoLEP, GreenLight).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The technique is particularly valuable in the post-prostatectomy / radiation setting because it is **non-transecting**, minimizing manipulation of the external urinary sphincter and reducing de novo stress urinary incontinence.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Surgical Technique

### Barbagli classic dorsal onlay (bulbar strictures)

The original Barbagli technique:<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

1. Perineal midline incision; lithotomy position.
2. **Complete circumferential mobilization** of the bulbar urethra from the corpora cavernosa.
3. The urethra is rotated 180° to expose the dorsal surface.
4. **Dorsal urethrotomy** through the strictured segment (Sachse urethrotome or cold knife).
5. BMG harvested from the inner cheek, defatted, and trimmed.
6. The graft is sutured to the **tunica albuginea of the corpora cavernosa** on one side, then the urethral margins are sutured to the graft edges.
7. The urethra is rotated back to its anatomic position; the remaining graft edge is secured to the corpora.
8. 16–18 Fr catheter, often with suprapubic tube.

A **modified Barbagli technique** improves visualization by suturing both graft margins to the urethral edges under direct vision before rotating the urethra back and fixing the graft to the tunica albuginea — comparable success rates (~83%).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Kulkarni one-sided dorsolateral onlay (long / panurethral strictures)

The Kulkarni technique is a vessel-sparing modification:<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

1. The urethra is dissected from **one side only** (unilateral mobilization), preserving the contralateral blood supply and bulbar artery.
2. The urethra is opened along the **dorsolateral** aspect.
3. BMG placed as a dorsolateral onlay, sutured to the corporal body on one side.
4. **Penile invagination** through the perineal incision allows access to the entire anterior urethra without a separate penile incision.

vs the conventional Barbagli for long strictures (>8 cm), the Kulkarni technique demonstrated **shorter operative time** (180 vs 196 min), **less blood loss** (164 vs 202 mL), **fewer perioperative complications** (16% vs 38%), and **higher success rates** (87% vs 70%).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Erectile function is well preserved.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### Dorsal BMG for VUAS / BNC (post-prostatectomy)

Shahrour technique:<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

1. Perineal incision with bulbar urethral dissection.
2. **Dorsal dissection carried underneath the pubic bone** to reach the vesicourethral anastomosis.
3. The urethra is opened dorsally through the strictured segment to the bladder neck.
4. BMG harvested and sutured to the bladder neck at the **11, 12, and 1 o'clock** positions.
5. **Dorsal quilting sutures** placed through the **periosteum of the pubic bone** secure the graft.
6. The graft is sutured to the urethral edges in a continuous fashion.

This approach avoids extensive urethral mobilization, reduces the risk of rectal injury, and eliminates the need for a combined abdominoperineal approach.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Outcomes

### Bulbar urethral strictures

| Comparison | Result |
|---|---|
| Dorsal onlay (Barbagli)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | **83–97%** success at 31–79 mo |
| Dorsal vs ventral onlay (meta-analysis, n=655)<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | No difference in success (RR 1.00, p=0.97); transient ED lower with ventral (RR 0.24, p=0.006); permanent ED similar |
| Dorsal onlay vs AAU (n=507)<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> | Dorsal onlay superior (HR 4.8 for recurrence with AAU, p=0.002) |
| Kulkarni vs Barbagli (long strictures > 8 cm)<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | 87% vs 70%; less blood loss, fewer complications with Kulkarni |
| Non-transecting AAU with dorsal BMG<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | 90.5% anatomical success at 18 mo (vessel-sparing approach) |

### VUAS / BNC (post-prostatectomy ± radiation)

Multi-institutional study of 45 men with VUAS after prostatectomy + radiation (Sterling 2024):<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- **7 recurrences** at median 21 mo follow-up.
- **No de novo incontinence** — a critical advantage over transecting techniques.
- Significant improvement in PVR, uroflow, IPSS, and IPSS-QoL.
- **86.6% satisfaction** (GRA +2 or better).

Posterior urethral stenosis after endoscopic prostate procedures (Angulo 2021, n=107):<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- **90.7% success** at mean 59 mo.
- Only **0.9% de novo SUI**.
- Independent predictors of recurrence: postoperative complications (OR 12.5), history of radiation (OR 8.3), and ≥ 2 prior dilatations (OR 8.3).

Substitution urethroplasty with BMG for VUAS / posterior urethral stenosis via perineal approach (Doležel 2024):<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> 3-year stricture recurrence-free survival of **65%** (81% with auxiliary DVIU); de novo incontinence in only 2 of 18 continent patients. See also the [BMG endourethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/endourethroplasty) page for the matched fully endoscopic approach.

## Rationale for Dorsal Placement

- **Mechanical support:** The graft is spread and quilted against the rigid tunica albuginea of the corpora cavernosa, providing a stable vascular bed for graft take (imbibition and inosculation).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Reduced sacculation:** Unlike ventral placement, the graft does not hang unsupported, reducing graft diverticulum formation.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Less technique-dependent:** The dorsal approach may be more forgiving for less experienced surgeons because the graft is inherently supported.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Preserved spongiosal blood supply:** In the Kulkarni modification, one-sided dissection preserves the contralateral bulbar artery.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

The dorsal approach requires **more extensive urethral mobilization** (circumferential in Barbagli), which may contribute to higher rates of **transient erectile dysfunction** vs ventral onlay (25% vs 5% in one comparative series of proximal bulbar strictures).<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> A 2025 meta-analysis confirmed permanent ED rates are similar between dorsal and ventral approaches.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

---

## Complications

- **Transient ED:** 5–25% (higher with dorsal than ventral in some series).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Permanent ED:** 1.7–5.9% (similar between dorsal and ventral).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Postvoid dribbling:** Up to 42% (common but often mild).<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Urinary tract infection:** 3–4%.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Orchalgia:** ~10%.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Donor site morbidity:** 4–9% (oral numbness, tightness).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **De novo SUI:** Very low (\<1% in posterior urethral stenosis series).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Stricture recurrence:** Increases with stricture length ≥ 5 cm, iatrogenic etiology, prior radiation, and use of transecting (AAU) technique.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[11]](#ref11)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

---

## Special Considerations

- **Lichen sclerosus:** Significantly higher recurrence rates (50% with single-stage repair); staged urethroplasty preferred.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- **Radiation history:** An independent predictor of recurrence (OR 8.3) in posterior urethral stenosis. Anastomotic urethroplasty is still preferred by 63% of GURS surgeons for short bulbomembranous stenosis post-radiotherapy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Panurethral strictures:** 90% of GURS surgeons prefer multiple BMGs over combined graft/flap.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Graft harvest:** 99% of reconstructive urologists prefer buccal mucosa; for long grafts, 56% harvest from both cheeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
