# Dorsal Onlay Buccal Mucosal Graft Urethroplasty for Posterior Stenosis

Dorsal BMG augmentation is an option for selected refractory posterior stenoses with a usable urethral plate and viable recipient bed. It aims to widen the lumen without circumferential transection. Nontransection has not been shown to preserve the sphincter or to cause less incontinence than another repair. Bladder-neck stenosis, post-prostatectomy VUAS and bulbomembranous stenosis are different anatomical populations.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Related pages: [BNC](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc), [VUAS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/vuas), and [subtrigonal inlay](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/subtrigonal-inlay) (the transvesical graft technique).

## Selection

Assess stenosis location, length and caliber, prior radiation or operations, baseline continence, fistula or necrosis, bladder function and future drainage needs. Sterling’s post-prostatectomy/post-radiation series treated only nonobliterative stenoses traversable by guidewire; patients with obliterative stenosis or anastomotic disruption underwent excision and primary anastomosis, and patients with bladder capacity &lt;100 mL, fistula or radionecrosis would not be offered the graft repair.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> EAU 2026 recommends urinary diversion in recurrent or complex cases with loss of bladder capacity and/or incapacitating local symptoms (Weak).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

EAU 2026 advises that VUAS reconstruction be performed in experienced high-volume centers, particularly after radiotherapy. It gives a strong recommendation against endoluminal treatment of VUAS, bulbomembranous or bladder neck stenosis with complete obliteration, and a weak recommendation for redo vesicourethral anastomosis (VUA) in obliterative VUAS or VUAS refractory to endoluminal treatment, in non-irradiated patients and irradiated patients with adequate bladder function (§6.3.5.a). The choice of reconstruction depends on stenosis length, location and caliber, continence, bladder function, prior radiotherapy, patient preference and surgeon expertise. For radiation-induced bulbomembranous stenosis refractory to endoscopy, EAU 2026 recommends either excision and primary anastomosis or augmentation for short (&lt;2.5 cm) stenoses (Weak), and augmentation for long (&gt;2.5 cm) stenoses (Weak).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> For treatment-refractory bladder neck stenosis after surgery for benign prostatic obstruction, EAU 2026 recommends Y-V or T-plasty (Weak). It describes robot-assisted subtrigonal buccal mucosa inlay only as a case report requiring further investigation and does not include dorsal BMG in that recommendation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Shahrour perineal technique

The original four-patient VUAS report describes these steps:<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

1. Perineal exposure and bulbar dissection, with dorsal access beneath the pubic bone toward the anastomosis.
2. Dorsal opening through the stenosis to the bladder neck, preserving viable tissue and protecting the sphincter and rectum.
3. Buccal graft fixation proximally at 11, 12 and 1 o'clock; a later posterior substitution-urethroplasty report describes a [ski/J-hook needle](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/ski-needle-j-hook) for deep apical sutures.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
4. Interrupted graft quilting to pubic periosteum and continuous fixation to the urethral edges with 4-0 polyglactin.
5. Catheter drainage followed by urethrogram and a voiding trial at 3 weeks in that report.

**The recipient bed depends on the technique.** Pubic periosteal quilting is described in Shahrour's operation. In Sterling’s one-sided dorsolateral approach, the urethrotomy extends about 1.5 cm into healthy tissue at each end; a shallow scar wedge is excised at 11–1 o’clock without cautery near the membranous urethra or cavernous nerves, and the graft is quilted to proximal periurethral tissue and distal corpora cavernosa.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> A perineal approach can avoid abdominal access in selected anatomy but does not remove the risk of rectal injury or the possible need for a combined approach.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Outcomes with correct populations

| Study | Population | Outcome and limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Shahrour 2019 | 4 post-prostatectomy VUAS patients, 3 radiated | The report described 100% success at 3 months. **All were incontinent before and after surgery**, so the series cannot measure new incontinence risk in initially continent patients.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| Sterling 2024 | 45 post-prostatectomy plus radiation patients: **21 anastomotic and 24 bulbomembranous stenoses** | 7 recurrences; median follow-up 21 months among patients without recurrence. No de novo SUI was reported, but 17/45 men (38%) subsequently received an AUS for postoperative incontinence. Baseline and postoperative continence counts differ between sections of the publication, so a 0/45 at-risk rate cannot be derived.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Angulo 2021 | 107 membranous/bulbomembranous stenoses after benign prostate surgery; **BNC excluded** | 10 recurrences at mean 59.3 months. One new SUI case among **87 patients known continent before surgery**; the paper's 0.9% uses all 107 as denominator. It is not a BNC cohort.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Doležel 2024 | 38 mixed posterior cases, 27 perineal and 11 endourethroplasty | Three-year recurrence-free survival 65% overall, 81% when auxiliary DVIU is included; new incontinence 2/18 initially continent patients across both approaches. Neither endpoint is specific to dorsal perineal BMG.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Policastro 2021 | 79 men with post-radiation posterior urethral stenosis (median stenosis 3.0 cm; EBRT, brachytherapy or both, with or without prior prostatectomy) | 82.3% stenosis-free at median 21-month follow-up (14/79 recurrence); de novo SUI in 3/37 (8.1%) preoperatively continent patients. This is the radiation-specific cohort; it does not include a BNC population.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |

These uncontrolled cohorts have no comparison with excision and reanastomosis or Y-V plasty. In a separate two-center retrospective study of 212 men with radiation-induced bulbomembranous stenosis, BMG onlay (n = 49) was associated with more cystoscopically defined recurrence (lumen &lt;16 Fr) than anastomotic repair (n = 163; adjusted HR 3.43, 95% CI 1.03–11.47); median follow-up among men without recurrence was 97 months. Reported incontinence was 18% in each group. This comparison does not establish the preferred repair for VUAS or BNC.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> Satisfaction scores apply to the responding patients, not every enrolled patient.

## Recurrence, complications and follow-up

In Angulo's analysis, which had few events (10), radiation, two or more prior dilations and postoperative complications were associated with recurrence. The article gives differing adjusted estimates for prior dilation in the abstract and main results.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Discuss recurrence, oral donor-site morbidity, urinary leakage, infection, fistula, sexual effects and new or worsened SUI. EAU 2026 recommends warning patients about de novo incontinence after reconstruction of BNS or bulbomembranous stenosis following surgery for benign prostatic obstruction, and about de novo incontinence and new erectile dysfunction after radiation-induced bulbomembranous repair (Strong).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> For the separate transperineal redo VUA operation, it recommends warning that incontinence is inevitable and staged anti-incontinence surgery may be needed after at least 3–6 months (Strong).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Sterling's 45-patient cohort also reported one venous thrombotic event requiring anticoagulation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Reassess symptoms, flow, residual and patency after healing. In Sterling's cohort, voiding cystourethrogram/retrograde urethrogram preceded catheter removal at 3 weeks; suspected extravasation prompted another week of drainage. Recurrence in that study meant any subsequent intervention.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> A short recurrence may be amenable to a further procedure, and Doležel counted this as auxiliary treatment separate from primary success.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For persistent SUI, assess bladder function and establish stable patency before considering an AUS. In Sterling's cohort, cystoscopy before implantation ruled out recurrent stenosis.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Prior radiation and urethral reconstruction can complicate later device surgery, and nontransection has not been shown to make AUS placement safer.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
