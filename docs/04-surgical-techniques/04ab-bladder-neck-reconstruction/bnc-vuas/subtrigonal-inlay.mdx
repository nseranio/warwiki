# Subtrigonal Inlay and Related BMG Approaches for Posterior Stenosis

**Subtrigonal inlay** refers here to the specific robotic transvesical buccal graft operation described by Avallone, Flynn and colleagues. Perineal dorsal onlay and transurethral grafting are related tissue-augmentation approaches but are different named techniques, with different anatomy, selection and evidence. All of them augment the stenotic segment with buccal mucosa instead of excising it and re-anastomosing.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Robotic subtrigonal inlay

The original report involved one 70-year-old man with refractory bladder-neck contracture after photovaporization of the prostate. Multiple endoscopic interventions had failed.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The robotic transvesical approach provides intravesical exposure of the bladder neck. The stenosis is opened and buccal mucosa is inlaid at the subtrigonal (posterior bladder-neck) defect, beneath the trigone. Viable tissue is preserved, the ureteral orifices are protected, the graft is secured against its recipient bed and the cystotomy is closed. The original video shows the incision and graft orientation. The pubic-periosteal quilting used in the perineal operation does not apply here.

| Index-case finding | Result |
|---|---|
| Graft dimensions as reported | 5 × 5 × 4 cm |
| Hospital discharge | Postoperative day 2 |
| Catheters | Urethral catheter 2 weeks; suprapubic catheter 4 weeks |
| Healing assessment | VCUG at 4 weeks without obstruction or extravasation |
| Flow and residual | Qmax 2 to 27 mL/s; PVR 200 to 3 mL |
| Recurrence | None at reported last follow-up; the abstract does not provide a durable comparative endpoint |

This is technical feasibility evidence, not a 100% expected success rate. Its catheter schedule is a case-specific protocol, not a universal rule.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Related approaches

| Approach | Anatomical distinction | Evidence scope |
|---|---|---|
| [Perineal dorsal BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/dorsal-bmg-bnc) | Dorsal urethral opening reached from below; graft bed depends on technique | Observational cohorts with different stenosis sites and radiation exposure |
| [Endoscopic BMG](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/endourethroplasty) | Transurethral recipient-bed preparation and graft delivery | Small cohorts and technical cases; no proven superiority |
| Single-port robotic BMG | Supraumbilical single-port access, intraabdominal or extraperitoneal transvesical, with perineal dissection if needed; graft anastomosis with rectus abdominis, omental or gracilis flaps as needed | Liu's nine-patient series (7 VUAS, 4 urethral strictures, some patients with more than one diagnosis) had mean defect length 3.9 cm; 5 required ancillary procedures (rectus flap in 3, omental in 1, gracilis in 1). Median operative time was 377 minutes, blood loss 200 mL, stay 2 days and follow-up 11.7 months. Thirty-day complications were UTI, epididymitis, anemia, recurrent stricture and SBO requiring surgery (one each). It had **no open comparator**.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| [Y-V plasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/yv-plasty) | Native bladder-flap advancement into an opened stenosis | It is not synonymous with circumferential excision and primary reanastomosis |

## Interpreting posterior BMG evidence

Angulo's 107-patient study involved membranous or bulbomembranous stenoses after benign prostate surgery and excluded BNC. It reported 10 recurrences at mean 59.3 months and one new SUI event among 87 patients known continent before surgery. It does not show that subtrigonal inlay or dorsal BMG is the best treatment for post-TURP BNC. Predictors of recurrence were postoperative complications (OR 12.5), prior radiation (OR 8.3) and two or more prior dilations (OR 8.3).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Sterling's 45-patient post-prostatectomy or post-radiation cohort included 21 anastomotic and 24 bulbomembranous stenoses, with seven recurrences. No de novo SUI was reported, but the publication's continence counts differ between sections, so a 0/45 at-risk rate cannot be derived. Policastro's 79-patient post-radiation series (median stenosis length 3.0 cm) reported 14 recurrences (17.7%) and 3/37 new SUI cases (8.1%) among initially continent patients, at median 21 months. Of 29 men with SUI before surgery, all but one remained incontinent.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

In Viegas's robotic Y-V series, 5 of 6 VUAS patients had new SUI, but one already had an AUS and three required concomitant AUS implantation. That figure cannot be compared directly with the BMG cohorts, which differ in anatomy, baseline function and selection. These studies do not show that BMG tolerates radiation better than excisional repair.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Clinical role

BMG augmentation needs a viable graft bed, secure fixation and drainage. Patients are counseled about oral donor-site morbidity and the possibility of recurrence or incontinence. Augmentation can preserve urethral continuity in appropriate anatomy, but it has not been shown to preserve the external sphincter.

Choose reconstruction according to stenosis site, length, residual lumen, radiation or tissue damage, bladder function, continence and the patient's goals. No guideline names subtrigonal inlay or dorsal BMG as the preferred treatment for radiated or nonradiated VUAS. Complete posterior obliteration is not treated endoluminally. AUS surgery follows stable patency, with individualized risk assessment.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Graft choice

The AUA/SUFU 2023 amendment states that surgeons should use oral mucosa as the first choice when grafts are used for urethroplasty (Expert Opinion), and that buccal or lingual mucosa may be used as equivalent alternatives (Strong Recommendation, Evidence Level A).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> A 2025 meta-analysis of eight comparative studies (655 patients) of bulbar strictures found similar success for dorsal and ventral onlay (RR 1.00, 95% CI 0.94–1.06) and lower transient erectile dysfunction with ventral onlay (RR 0.24); permanent ED was similar.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> In a 2022 survey of GURS members (134 of 350 responded), 99% preferred buccal mucosa as the primary site, and 66% favored dorsal graft placement for bulbar urethroplasty. The majority preferred anastomotic urethroplasty to BMG for short post-radiation bulbomembranous stenosis (63% vs 37%).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> These data concern anterior and bulbar strictures and do not settle which posterior operation is best. Graft biology is covered in the foundations pages.
