# T-Plasty for Recurrent Bladder Neck Stenosis

**T-plasty** is a modification of [Y-V plasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/yv-plasty) that advances two bladder-wall flaps, instead of one, into an opened stenotic bladder neck. The Hamburg group (Reiss, Rosenbaum, Dahlem and colleagues) developed it for highly recurrent stenosis after transurethral surgery for benign prostatic obstruction and reported it in two overlapping single-center series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> A robot-assisted version has since been described in seven patients.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Evidence for post-prostatectomy VUAS or radiated patients is lacking.

---

## Rationale

The standard Y-V plasty advances a single V-shaped bladder flap into the incised contracture. The Hamburg authors designed the T-plasty to reconstruct a wide bladder neck with two well-vascularized flaps in a single stage, without a graft, tissue interposition or staged procedure.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> These advantages are the authors' stated rationale. No comparison with a single-flap repair exists, so superior patency, blood supply or a "backup" flap has not been shown.

---

## Operative concept

The following outline is general. The original publications give the specific incision geometry, which is not reproduced in the abstracts.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

1. **Access.** The bladder is exposed through retropubic access.
2. **Localize the stenosis.** A cystoscope or sound passed transurethrally identifies the level and extent of the stenosis from the abdominal side.
3. **Open the stenosis.** The scar is incised through its full thickness into a viable urethral lumen.
4. **Create two flaps.** A modified bladder-wall incision creates two flaps, one on each side of the opened outlet.
5. **Advance and suture.** The flaps are advanced into the defect and sutured without tension to each other and to the urethral edges.
6. **Close and drain.** The bladder defect is closed and the bladder is drained by catheter. The publications do not specify catheter duration.

Preserve bladder-wall vascularity and protect the sphincter and ureteral orifices. Additional reconstruction or interposition depends on the defect and the assessment of healing.

## Published cohorts

The two Hamburg reports overlap, so their patients cannot be added together. The expanded study identified all patients who underwent T-plasty at the center between December 2008 and July 2016.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

| Parameter | Reiss 2016, index report<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | Rosenbaum 2017, expanded cohort<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
|---|---|---|
| Patients | 10, with multiple prior endoscopic treatments | 30, including 25 after TURP (83.3%) |
| Age | Mean 69.2 years (range 61 to 79) | Median 69 years (IQR 62 to 73) |
| Follow-up | Mean 26 months (range 3 to 46) | Median 45 months (IQR 18 to 64); 3 lost to follow-up |
| Success | 100% | 100% (27/27 evaluable) |
| Serious complications | None of Clavien grade III or higher | No severe blood loss or severe perioperative complications |
| De novo incontinence | None | 1 patient; mean ICIQ-SF 1.2 (SD 2.27) |
| Function | Stream very strong to moderate in 80%; mean IPSS 11.3 (range 4 to 29); mean IPSS-QoL 2.4 (range 1 to 5) | Qmax 6.79 to 24.42 mL/s (p = 0.009); PVR 140.77 to 14.5 mL (p = 0.004) |
| Satisfaction and quality of life | Satisfaction very high or high in 90%; QoL improved in 90%; SF-8 comparable to the reference population | Pleased or delighted 88.5%; QoL strongly improved 75% |

EAU guidelines summarize bladder neck reconstruction with Y-V or T-plasty for refractory stenosis after BPH surgery as having patency of 83% to 100% and incontinence in up to 14% (level of evidence 3).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

Paired flow measurements were available for only six patients and paired residual measurements for ten in the expanded cohort. These improvements do not describe all 30 patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The robot-assisted T-plasty series (Lumen 2025) included 7 patients, 6 after TURP and 1 after simple prostatectomy, with a median of 3 prior endoscopic treatments. Median operative time was 123 minutes and three patients had a grade 2 complication. At a median follow-up of 27 months (range 4 to 74), all were free of recurrence, with no de novo incontinence or erectile dysfunction.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Comparison with other reconstructions

These are case-series figures from different populations, definitions of success and follow-up. They do not rank the techniques.

| Feature | T-plasty (open) | Robotic Y-V plasty | TUITMR | Dorsal onlay BMG |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flap or graft | Two bilateral bladder flaps | One V-shaped bladder flap | Mucosal realignment, no flap | Free buccal graft |
| Series | 30 (Hamburg) | 30 (RAYV) | 19 | 107 |
| Success | 100% (27/27 evaluable) | 25/30 (83.3%) at 24 months | 89% after one procedure, 100% after a second | 90.7% (stenosis recurred in 10) |
| Follow-up | Median 45 months | Median 27 months | Median 6 months | Mean 59.3 months |
| Population | Post-TURP and other BPH surgery | BNS and VUAS | BNC and VUAS; 32% radiated | Membranous or bulbomembranous stenosis after endoscopic prostate surgery |
| Serious complications | None severe | 2 major (Clavien IIIa and IV) | No major complications | Not tabulated here |
| Graft harvest | No | No | No | Yes (buccal) |

Sources: <sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>. De novo stress incontinence after robotic Y-V plasty depends on the population; in a 21-patient series, 23.8% developed it, all in the VUAS group and most needing or already having an AUS.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

The Angulo cohort concerned membranous or bulbomembranous stenoses and is not a bladder-neck comparator. It found that postoperative complications, prior radiation and two or more dilatations before urethroplasty predicted recurrence.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

- **Single center.** All open T-plasty patients were treated at one center. Reproducibility and the learning curve have not been assessed.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Population.** The published patients had stenosis after transurethral BPH surgery. The technique has not been reported for post-prostatectomy VUAS or after radiation, where reconstruction is hardest.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **No comparison.** No head-to-head data exist against Y-V plasty, BMG or endoscopic techniques.
- **Incomplete follow-up.** Three of 30 patients were lost to follow-up, and functional measurements were incomplete.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Patient selection

Reconstruction is considered for recurrent stenosis when repeated endoscopic treatment is no longer acceptable or appropriate. The residual lumen, stenosis length, prior operations, radiation, bladder capacity and compliance, continence and available viable tissue are assessed. No fixed number of failed endoscopic treatments is required. The published T-plasty patients had multiple prior endoscopic treatments.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

[TUIBNC](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/transurethral-incision-bnc), [Y-V plasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/yv-plasty), [BMG augmentation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/dorsal-bmg-bnc) and [reanastomosis](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/primary-reanastomosis) are chosen by the anatomy of the individual patient. Case-series success rates do not rank them.
