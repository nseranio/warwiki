# Transurethral Incision of Bladder Neck Contracture (TUIBNC)

**Transurethral incision of bladder neck contracture (TUIBNC)** is the **first-line endoscopic operation** for bladder neck contracture (BNC) and post-prostatectomy vesicourethral anastomotic stenosis (VUAS). The standardized technique — **balloon dilation followed by deep bilateral incisions at 3 and 9 o'clock** — achieves single-procedure patency rates of **72–82%** and cumulative success of **86–94% after two procedures**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For the broader BNC framework, decision matrix, and treatment database, see [Bladder Neck Contracture](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc). For VUAS-specific reconstruction context, see [VUAS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/vuas). For open / robotic reconstruction options, see [Y-V Plasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/yv-plasty), [T-Plasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/t-plasty-bnc), and [Combined Abdominoperineal Approach](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/combined-abdominoperineal).

---

## Definition and Indications

BNC is fibrotic narrowing of the bladder neck, typically presenting at **median 5.8 months** after prostate surgery with obstructive LUTS, decreased flow, and elevated PVR.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Most common etiologies:

- **Radical prostatectomy** ± radiation — the dominant cause in referral series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **TURP** (especially bipolar, ~4.7% incidence) and **HoLEP** (~1.3%).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Other endoscopic prostate procedures.

The **AUA Urethral Stricture Disease Guideline (2023)** establishes that **bladder-neck incision and bladder-neck resection have comparable outcomes** — choice may be guided by surgeon preference and visualization.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Standardized Surgical Technique

The Nealon / Ramirez technique — used in the largest contemporary series — is the canonical standardized approach.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

1. **Initial dilation.** A **24 Fr balloon dilator** is used to dilate the contracture and allow passage of the resectoscope.
2. **Deep bilateral lateral incisions.** A **Collins knife** (cold knife or electrocautery) is used to make **deep incisions at the 3 and 9 o'clock positions**, extending **through the scar tissue into the perivesical fat**. Lateral positioning avoids the neurovascular bundles and the external urinary sphincter.

### Variations

| Variant | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Holmium laser incision** | Comparable outcomes to Collins-knife incision; useful for day-surgery models<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **Adjunctive intralesional injection** (corticosteroids, mitomycin-C) | Variable utility — see below |
| **TUIBN + transverse mucosal realignment** (Abramowitz endoscopic Y-V plasty) | 89% single-procedure success; 100% with second procedure; no de novo incontinence<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| **Tri-radial or quadrant-radial incisions** | Alternative to bilateral 3 and 9 o'clock — used in some MMC protocols<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |

The **Nealon 2022 series of 123 patients** demonstrated that **deep bilateral incisions alone (no intralesional agents) achieve comparable patency rates** to series using MMC or corticosteroids — raising the question of whether the incision technique itself drives most of the success.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Outcomes

| Series | n | Technique | 1-procedure success | Cumulative success | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Nealon 2022**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 123 | Balloon dilation + Collins knife at 3 & 9 o'clock | **82.1%** | **94.3%** (2 procedures) | 12 mo |
| Ramirez 2013<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | 50 (78% refractory) | Deep lateral incisions | 72% | 86% (2 procedures) | 12.9 mo |
| Rosenbaum 2021<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | 60 (post-BPH) | Incision or resection | 53% overall | — | Variable |
| Abramowitz 2021<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | 19 | TUIBN + mucosal realignment | **89%** | **100%** (2 procedures) | 6 mo |
| Quarta 2025<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> | 110 | Ho-laser BNI (91.8%) | 64% mild IPSS at 3 mo | — | 3 mo |

The AUA guidelines note **first-time VUAS treatment is successful in ~50–80% of cases**, with all endoscopic techniques showing similar success.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Risk Factors for Failure

| Risk factor | Significance |
|---|---|
| **≥ 2 prior endoscopic BNC procedures** | Most consistently identified across studies (p = 0.03)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **>10 pack-year smoking** | p = 0.039<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Prior pelvic radiation** | Lower success; prospective data lacking<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Shorter interval between prostate surgery and BNC onset** | < 6 months in some analyses<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Smaller prostate volume** at initial BPE surgery | Predictor of failure<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |

---

## Complications

- **Stress urinary incontinence (SUI)** — the principal concern. Up to **78%** of post-prostatectomy BNC patients have concomitant SUI; counsel about de novo or worsened incontinence after TUIBNC.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Urge incontinence** — ~15% in some series.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Recurrence** — 18–47% after a single procedure, typically within the first months (median ~1.8 mo).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Bleeding, UTI** — uncommon; standard endoscopic risks.

---

## Concomitant SUI and Subsequent AUS Placement

For patients with SUI after TUIBNC, **AUS placement** can be safely performed at an average of **2.9 months** after TUIBNC. Only **8% (2/26)** required repeat transurethral procedures for recurrent BNC after AUS placement — indicating > 90% long-term urethral patency.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adjunctive Pharmacotherapy — Mitomycin-C vs Corticosteroids

Both adjuncts are widely used; no head-to-head RCT exists. The Pang 2021 meta-analysis of 13 RCTs found **MMC has the lowest stricture-recurrence odds among all adjuncts** (intralesional OR 0.23, 95% CI 0.11–0.48, p < 0.001), but the safety profile differs materially.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

### Mitomycin-C (MMC)

Antiproliferative / antifibrotic — inhibits fibroblast proliferation and collagen deposition.

**Technique**: Cold-knife tri- or quadrant-radial incisions followed by intralesional MMC injection at each incision site. Common dosing: **0.3–0.4 mg/mL in 0.2–0.4 mL aliquots** at each site, or **2 mg total in 5 mL sterile water**. Many protocols include **1 month of daily CIC** postoperatively.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

| Series | n | Population | 1-proc success | Cumulative | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vanni 2011<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | 18 | All failed prior incision | 72% | 89% (2 proc) | 12 mo |
| Rozanski 2021<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> | 86 | 91% failed prior DVIU | 65% | 90% (≥ 2 proc) | 21.1 mo |
| Sourial 2017<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> | 29 | Recurrent VUAS post-RP | 79% | 86% (with salvage) | 12 mo |
| Hacker 2022<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | 51 | Post-prostate-cancer BNC/VUAS | 45% | 84% (up to 4 proc) | 32 mo |
| Farrell 2015<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> | 37 | Recurrent strictures / BNC | 75.7% | — | 23 mo |
| **TURNS Redshaw 2015**<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> | 55 | 80% failed prior | 58% | 75% (2 proc) | 9.2 mo |

**Radiation effect**: nonradiated patients consistently outperform radiated. Rozanski 2021: **94% vs 76%** overall success (p = 0.04).<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> Hacker 2022: RP + EBRT had shortest interval to recurrence (p = 0.018).<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

**Safety flag — TURNS multicenter study**: **7% (4/55) experienced serious adverse events related to MMC**, with 3 patients needing or planning **cystectomy**.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> Other series report no long-term MMC-attributable complications.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[18]](#ref18)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> The **AUA 2023 guideline acknowledges conflicting data** about MMC for VUAS and recommends further study.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### Corticosteroids (Triamcinolone Acetonide, Betamethasone)

Suppress inflammatory cascade; reduce fibroblast activity and collagen synthesis. Used for BNC since the 1970s.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

**Technique**: intralesional injection of **triamcinolone acetonide 40 mg/mL, 2 mL total** at multiple sites (commonly 8 injection points) at incision / resection sites. A distinguishing feature of some protocols is **repeated postoperative cystoscopy-guided injections every 4 weeks × 3 sessions**.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

| Series | n | Population | Success | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Zhang 2021**<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> | 28 | Highly recurrent BNC (multiple prior failures) | **92.9%** | 2.8 yr |
| Sun 2022<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> | 46 | BNC post-TURP/PVP (GreenLight + TA) | **100%** (no recurrence) | NS |
| Wu 2024<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | 20 | BNC post-TURP/TUEP (TUIBN + betamethasone) | 65% | 35.8 mo |
| Farah 1979<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> | 7 | Recurrent vesical neck contracture | 100% | NS |
| Kravchick 2013<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> | 14 | Recurrent anastomotic stenosis post-RP (TRUS-guided) | Lowest re-treatment rate | NS |

**Mechanism advantage**: Sun 2022 immunohistochemistry showed repeated triamcinolone injections **significantly reduce collagen I, MMP-3, and TGF-β expression** — providing mechanistic rationale.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

**Safety**: generally well tolerated. UTI 2.9–14%, bleeding 8.8%, extravasation 5.8%; **no serious systemic adverse events reported**. **No risk of severe tissue necrosis** that has been associated with MMC in rare cases.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

### Summary table

| Domain | MMC | Corticosteroids |
|---|---|---|
| Meta-analysis effect | **Lowest recurrence odds** (OR 0.23; Pang 2021)<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | Prolong time to recurrence; modest reduction<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> |
| Best-series success (recurrent BNC) | 89% cumulative (Vanni 2011)<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | 92.9% (Zhang 2021 with repeat injections)<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> |
| Effect of radiation | Lower (76%) but meaningful<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> | Sparse data |
| Serious AEs | **7% in TURNS** — including cystectomy<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> | None reported<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Repeat dosing | Single injection at incision | **Postoperative serial injections** are a unique feature of the Zhang / Sun protocols<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[22]](#ref22)</sup> |
| AUA 2023 stance | **Conflicting data** — further study needed<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | Not specifically addressed |

### Critical comparator — **Nealon 2022**

The Nealon series demonstrated that **standardized deep bilateral incisions alone — no intralesional agent** — achieve **82% single-procedure** and **94% two-procedure** success in 123 patients. These rates are comparable to MMC and corticosteroid series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

This suggests the **incision technique itself** (deep bilateral 3 & 9 o'clock through scar into perivesical fat) is the primary determinant of success. Adjuncts may have added value in highly recurrent / radiated cases but should be selected with attention to MMC's serious-AE profile.

## Recalcitrant BNC Pathway

For patients who fail repeated endoscopic treatment, the **AUA 2023 guideline** recommends considering **robotic or open reconstruction** (Conditional, Grade C).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Robotic reconstruction patency rates: **72.7–75%**, with significant subsequent SUI requiring AUS implantation. Treatment must be tailored to patient preference, prior radiotherapy history, and existing incontinence. See [Y-V Plasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/yv-plasty), [T-Plasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/t-plasty-bnc), and [Combined Abdominoperineal Approach](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/combined-abdominoperineal).

## Key Principles

- **Standardized deep bilateral incisions at 3 and 9 o'clock** through scar into perivesical fat is the workhorse — Nealon 2022 reports 82% / 94% success at 1 / 2 procedures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Bladder-neck incision and resection are comparable** per AUA 2023 — surgeon preference governs choice.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Most consistent failure predictor**: ≥ 2 prior endoscopic BNC procedures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Smoking** (> 10 pack-years) and **pelvic radiation** are additional failure risk factors.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Up to 78% of post-prostatectomy BNC patients have concomitant SUI** — counsel about de novo / worsened incontinence; **AUS can be safely placed ~3 months later** with > 90% urethral patency preserved.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Adjuncts work but are not mandatory** — Nealon 2022 demonstrated that the incision technique itself accounts for most of the success.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **MMC has the lowest meta-analytic recurrence odds** but a **7% serious-AE rate (TURNS)** including cases requiring cystectomy; AUA notes conflicting data.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[19]](#ref19)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Corticosteroids — particularly with repeat postoperative injections (Zhang protocol)** — achieve high success in recurrent BNC with no serious systemic AEs.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- **Endoscopic Y-V (Abramowitz 2021)** — transverse mucosal realignment — is a promising bridging technique with 89% / 100% success and no de novo incontinence.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **After 2 endoscopic failures**, escalate to robotic / open reconstruction; tailor to radiation history and pre-existing incontinence.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
