# Robotic Bladder Flap Posterior Urethroplasty

**Robotic bladder flap posterior urethroplasty** uses an anterior bladder-wall flap to form a new vesicourethral anastomosis after division of a diseased outlet and removal of unsuitable scar. Zhao, Shakir and Zhao described it for selected recalcitrant BNC/VUAS in a nine-patient retrospective technical series. Follow-up was short, and the operation has not been shown to avoid incontinence or diversion.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Anatomical role

The flap supplies bladder tissue when direct reanastomosis may otherwise be difficult. It differs from [Y-V plasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/yv-plasty), which widens an incised stenosis, and from a [Tanagho tube](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/tanagho-flap), which creates a longer tubularized conduit. Viable bladder tissue, adequate mobility, bladder function and the urethral target determine whether this approach is feasible.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Recurrent stenosis is not necessarily complete obliteration. Evaluate its location, length, residual lumen, radiation damage, fistula and baseline continence. The EAU guideline supports individualized reconstruction for appropriate refractory posterior disease. The 2023 AUA guideline gives a Conditional Recommendation (Evidence Level: Grade C) for robotic or open reconstruction of recalcitrant BNS or post-prostatectomy VUAS; neither guideline favors this specific bladder flap over other reconstructions.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Operative sequence

The published operation uses transperitoneal robotic access.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

1. Mobilize the anterior bladder and expose the diseased outlet, protecting adjacent pelvic structures.
2. Localize the stenosis with combined endoscopic and robotic assessment. Granieri's separate robotic Y-V plasty report describes cystoscope placement at the BNC and Firefly localization; its method should not be attributed to Zhao's bladder-flap cohort. Cystoscopic light transillumination does not require ICG injection and is distinct from fluorescence after ICG administration.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
3. Divide the diseased bladder neck and remove unsuitable scar until viable tissue is reached. In Zhao's illustrative post-prostatectomy case, hydrodissection was used to transect the prior VUA before dystrophic urethral and periurethral tissue was excised.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
4. Fashion an anterior bladder-wall flap that reaches the urethral target without tension and preserves its blood supply.
5. Close the native bladder neck, create a new anterior bladder-wall cystotomy and anastomose its mucosa to the urethral stump to form the new VUA.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Confirm hemostasis and watertight closure, and maintain catheter drainage. In Zhao's illustrative case, a suprapubic catheter was placed before the space of Retzius was closed.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
6. Add tissue interposition or associated fistula repair when indicated. Individualize drainage and healing assessment.

The original report and operative video give the flap design. Tissue viability and available length determine the final construction, and the series does not define a fixed flap size or catheter duration.

## Index-series outcomes

| Outcome | Zhao 2022, n=9 |
|---|---|
| Mean operative time | 295.5 minutes overall; 264.5 minutes excluding three patients with ancillary procedures |
| Complications | No intraoperative complications reported; two postoperative complications |
| Follow-up | Mean 21.1 weeks |
| Freedom from recurrent stenosis | 7/9 (78%) without recurrence at mean 21.1 weeks; both recurrences underwent balloon dilation, and one remained patent at mean post-dilation follow-up of 14.5 weeks<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| New stress incontinence | 0/3 patients without preoperative SUI developed de novo SUI<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |

The de novo SUI result applies only to the three of nine patients without preoperative SUI; that result does not establish their overall continence or pad use.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Short follow-up in a small selected cohort does not show durable patency or comparative morbidity.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Alternatives and evidence limits

[Primary reanastomosis](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/primary-reanastomosis), [Y-V plasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/yv-plasty), [transvesical reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/transvesical-approaches), graft augmentation and urinary diversion address different anatomical situations, and percentages from their separate case series are not comparable. Patients are counseled about further stenosis treatment and staged continence surgery, with patency and bladder function reassessed first.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> A 2026 synthesis of 33 reconstruction studies found pooled robotic patency of 92% in BNS (n = 95) and 75% in VUAS (n = 96); mixed techniques, endpoints and follow-up prevent a bladder-flap-specific estimate.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
