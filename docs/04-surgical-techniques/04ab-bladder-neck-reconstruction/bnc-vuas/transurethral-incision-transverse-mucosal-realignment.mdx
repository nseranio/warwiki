# Transurethral Incision with Transverse Mucosal Realignment (TUITMR)

**Transurethral incision with transverse mucosal realignment (TUITMR)** is an endoscopic procedure for nonobliterative bladder-neck contracture (BNC) and vesicourethral anastomotic stenosis (VUAS). The scar is incised cystoscopically, and adjacent healthy bladder mucosa is advanced across the defect with a transurethral suturing device, in the manner of a Y-V plasty. Abramowitz and colleagues described it in 2021. Their 19-patient single-surgeon series reported 89% patency after one procedure and 100% after a second, with no de novo incontinence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> This is a TUITMR-specific finding from two small single-institution cohorts, not a guideline-level statement about endoluminal treatment of BNC/VUAS in general; see [Comparison with established treatment](#comparison-with-established-treatment) below.

See [BNC](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc), [VUAS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/vuas), and [standard incision](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/transurethral-incision-bnc).

## Rationale

After a standard incision the scar bed heals by secondary intention, and the contracture recurs if fibrosis outpaces re-epithelialization. TUITMR is intended to cover the incised scar with viable bladder mucosa, as an open Y-V plasty advances a flap into the stenotic segment. Whether mucosal coverage prevents recurrence has not been tested against standard incision.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Operative concept

The steps below follow the published description.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

1. Assess the lumen cystoscopically and establish wire access into the bladder for safety. Dilate as needed for controlled instrumentation.
2. Incise the stenotic scar to open the lumen while protecting adjacent structures. Do not treat a completely obliterated posterior stenosis endoluminally, and avoid deep incisions at 6 and 12 o'clock.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
3. In the 2021 series, a 5 Fr rigid ureteroscope and [RD180 suturing device](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/rd180-suturing-device) passed together through the retained 26 Fr cystourethroscope sheath. A 2-0 Monoglide suture joined proximal bladder mucosa to distal urethral mucosa and was secured with the [Ti-Knot fastener](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/ti-knot-fastener). The fastener avoids intracorporeal knot tying as healthy bladder mucosa is advanced transversely across the incision.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The later technical report uses the [JNW UrTrac sheath](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/jnw-urtrac-sheath) with a flexible digital ureteroscope; it describes limited lateral resection for VUAS and advises against TUITMR with bladder-neck necrosis with soft-tissue calcification, significant dehiscence, membranous involvement, or rectal or pubic fistula.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
4. Confirm patency and hemostasis and establish catheter drainage. The index series left an 18 Fr catheter for one week.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The index study assessed patency by passage of a 17 Fr flexible cystoscope at 4 months.

The suturing platform is a laparoscopic device adapted for transurethral use, and the learning curve has not been characterized.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Positioning and VTE Prophylaxis

TUITMR is performed transurethrally with the patient in lithotomy. The AUA guideline recommends assessing lower-extremity mobility before positioning and careful extremity padding in lithotomy to avoid pressure on the calf muscles, peroneal nerve and ulnar nerve, with sequential compression devices to reduce both venous thromboembolism and nerve-compression injury; it frames added parenteral VTE prophylaxis mainly as a consideration for open urethral reconstruction, not a brief transurethral procedure such as this one. Neither the AUA nor EAU stricture guideline states a BNC- or VUAS-specific pharmacologic VTE recommendation; apply the general risk framework (age, obesity, prior VTE, malignancy, prolonged operation or immobility) on the [VTE Prophylaxis](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/perioperative-eras/vte-prophylaxis) page.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

Abramowitz used an 18 Fr catheter for one week; the Curtis abstract does not specify catheter duration. The AUA guideline describes variable catheter duration, generally shorter after endoscopic intervention than open reconstruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Published evidence

| Index study characteristic | Abramowitz 2021 |
|---|---|
| Design | Retrospective, 19 patients, one surgeon |
| Period | July 2019 to December 2020 |
| Case mix | 53% VUAS, 47% BNC; 32% previously irradiated |
| Follow-up | Median 6 months; patients with at least 4 months were included |
| Success definition | Passage of a 17 Fr flexible cystoscope at 4 months |
| Initial success | 17/19 (89%) |
| After repeat TUITMR | 19/19 (100%) at four-month cystoscopy after retreatment; one of the two initial failures had an unsuccessful robotic Y-V plasty between TUITMR procedures |
| Incontinence and clip removal | No de novo incontinence reported; retained Ti-Knot clips were found at four-month cystoscopy in 4/19, with three removed in office and one in the operating room |

The index series did not report outcomes for the irradiated subgroup, and its authors called for longer follow-up and reports from other centers.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

A later registry from the same group followed 92 men with BNC after benign prostate surgery (November 2021 to July 2025) for a median of 15.9 months. Anatomic success was 93.5%, and 83.7% needed no reintervention after a single procedure. Final anatomic success after up to two procedures was 96.7%. No patient had de novo incontinence, and complications occurred in 12%, mostly minor urinary tract infection. The senior author is a consultant for Olympus.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The 2021 full text reports preoperative incontinence in 8/19 patients; all eight remained incontinent, and no de novo incontinence was reported among the other 11.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The 2026 registry abstract reports no de novo incontinence but does not give a baseline continence denominator or preoperative AUS and sling status.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> This TUITMR-specific absence of de novo incontinence in two single-institution cohorts (111 patients total, mostly BNC after benign prostate surgery) should not be read as evidence that endoluminal treatment of BNC/VUAS carries no continence risk in general. EAU gives a weak recommendation to warn patients that endoluminal treatment of posterior urethral stenosis can cause de novo incontinence or worsen existing incontinence, and separately reports de novo incontinence of up to 14% after open/robotic Y-V or T-plasty bladder-neck reconstruction and up to 25% after reconstruction of bulbomembranous strictures; those figures describe reconstruction, not endoluminal treatment such as TUITMR.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> See [Bladder Neck Contracture](/docs/clinical-conditions/03b-voiding-outlet/bladder-neck-stenosis) for the fuller continence discussion.

## Comparison with established treatment

Endoscopic treatment of VUAS has variable outcomes. A 2024 systematic review of 40 studies and 1,452 patients estimated pooled success of 72.8% (95% CI 64.4–79.9%) after endoscopic treatment, falling to 62.9% (53.6–71.4%) after correction for publication bias. Radiotherapy was associated with lower success.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> A 2025 review cites patency of 40–72% for traditional dilation or direct vision internal urethrotomy, with about 90% of patients needing repeat intervention within 2 years, and short-term success of 85–90% for drug-coated balloon dilation and TUITMR.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

| Technique | Cohort | Success | Definition |
|---|---|---|---|
| TUITMR | 19 patients, mixed BNC/VUAS, median 6 months<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 89% after one, 100% after two | 17 Fr flexible cystoscope at 4 months |
| TUITMR | 92 men, BNC after benign prostate surgery, median 15.9 months<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | 93.5% anatomic; 83.7% without reintervention after one | Anatomic patency; freedom from reintervention |
| Deep lateral TUIBNC | 123 patients, BNC<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | 82.1% after one, 94.3% after two, at 12 months | Patent bladder neck with 16 Fr cystoscope passage at 2 months |
| Deep lateral TUIBNC | 50 patients, mostly refractory<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | 72% after one, 86% after two | No further surgery for obstruction at mean 12.9 months |
| DVIU with mitomycin C | 86 patients, recurrent BNC/VUAS<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | 65% after one, 90% overall; 94% nonradiated vs 76% radiated | 17 Fr cystoscope passage without further procedures |

These cohorts differ in case mix, follow-up and success definition. Percentages across rows are not head-to-head comparisons, and no study compares TUITMR with incision, mitomycin C, drug-coated balloon or robotic Y-V plasty.

## Clinical positioning

EAU recommends transurethral resection or hot-knife incision as first-line treatment of nonobliterative bladder-neck stenosis after surgery for benign prostatic obstruction (strong) and repeat endoluminal treatment for nonobliterative stenoses (weak). Reconstruction may be considered when endoluminal treatment repeatedly fails or the stenosis is obliterated. EAU advises against endoluminal treatment of complete obliteration (strong), against deep incisions at 6 and 12 o'clock (strong) and against posterior urethral stents (weak). Mitomycin C for posterior stenosis should be used only within a clinical trial.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The AUA 2023 amendment notes that repeat endoscopic treatment may be necessary and that no studies compare treatment strategies for BNC after endoscopic prostate procedures. It gives robotic or open reconstruction for recalcitrant stenosis a Conditional Recommendation (Evidence Level Grade C).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> Neither guideline addresses TUITMR.

Posterior drug-coated balloon treatment is also off-label. In a 141-patient retrospective comparison, drug-coated balloon dilation was associated with longer recurrence-free survival than standard endoscopic treatment (HR 0.40, 95% CI 0.19–0.87).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> In a 319-patient TURNS series, 59 had posterior stenoses, with median follow-up of 5.7 months. The estimated 1-year functional recurrence-free survival in that subgroup was 75.8% and anatomic recurrence-free survival was 59.4%, with wide confidence intervals.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> Neither study compares drug-coated balloon with TUITMR.
