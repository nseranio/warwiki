# BMG Endourethroplasty

**BMG endourethroplasty** is transurethral placement of buccal mucosa into a urethral stricture without an external urethral incision. Posterior grafting is supported mainly by small observational series and technical reports. Distal or fossa-navicularis inlay urethroplasty and injectable micrografts involve different sites and techniques, and their results cannot be pooled as posterior-stenosis evidence.

[Transverse mucosal realignment (TUITMR)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/transurethral-incision-transverse-mucosal-realignment) is a separate, nongraft technique.

## Posterior grafting

Chiou coined the term "endourethroplasty" in 1988. His three-patient report described a two-step procedure: transurethral resection of scar to create a smooth grafting bed, then a full-thickness preputial graft held against the bed by an intraluminal balloon catheter. Two patients were stricture-free at more than 22 months and one at more than 12 months. The report established feasibility, not a standard treatment or a population-level success estimate.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Doležel's retrospective series included 38 patients treated during 1999–2022: 27 perineal repairs and 11 endourethroplasties. Strictures were dilated and/or incised, and an onlay or tubular buccal graft was transferred through an open or endourological approach. Three-year recurrence-free survival was 65% overall, with estimates of 63% for the perineal approach and 73% for endourethroplasty. The 23-year recruitment period is not 23 years of follow-up for every patient. Selection and small groups preclude a conclusion of equivalence or superiority.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The protocol allowed a cold-knife auxiliary DVIU for a short recurrence within 12 months. With auxiliary DVIU counted as part of treatment, the overall three-year result was 81%; that figure includes additional procedures and is not single-operation success. New incontinence occurred in 2/18 patients continent before surgery across both approaches, and is not specific to endourethroplasty.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

:::warning[Selection limits]
EAU advises against endoluminal treatment of a completely obliterated posterior stenosis. Historical or experimental tubular graft descriptions do not justify attempted endoscopic recanalization as routine care. Assess radiation injury, fistula, tissue vascularity, bladder function and continence before selecting reconstruction.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
:::

## Endoscopic BMG with transurethral suturing

Ungerer and Warner (2023) reported one radiated patient with an 8 mm flow-limiting membranous stenosis. Their technical sequence was:<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

1. Dilate the stricture enough for safe instrumentation. With a resectoscope, resect a superficial strip of mucosa about 1 cm wide from the bladder neck past the stricture to create the graft bed.
2. Harvest buccal mucosa in standard fashion and leave the graft outside the urethra.
3. Pass the [RD180 suturing device](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/urethral-specialty/rd180-suturing-device) transurethrally. Place a suture through the proximal end of the graft, then through the bladder neck, and back through the graft. Pulling the suture advances the graft into position on the bladder neck (the "pulley phenomenon").
4. Anchor the graft to the posterior urethra with secure straps and leave a catheter to hold the graft flat. Catheter duration in this report was 4 weeks.

The procedure took 2.5 hours, with 50 mL estimated blood loss and same-day discharge. Cystoscopy at 10 weeks showed good graft viability. Qmax improved from 4 to 20 mL/s, with no recurrence at 6 months. This is a single case and does not establish safety, SUI risk or superiority to other repairs. The superficial endoscopic bed is not the same dissection as the pubic periosteal bed used in some open repairs, and the resection must protect the sphincter and adjacent structures.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Distal urethral inlay grafts

Transurethral ventral and dorsal inlay BMG techniques for distal or fossa-navicularis strictures preserve the glans while replacing diseased lining. They belong to the distal reconstruction pathway and are not part of the BNC or VUAS evidence base.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

**Ventral inlay (Sterling, Nikolavsky).** The described steps are:<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

1. Excise the cicatrix by transurethral ventral urethrotomy until the lumen is at least 24 Fr.
2. Deliver a triangular buccal graft to the proximal extent of the urethrotomy with a double-arm 6-0 polydioxanone suture and secure it externally.
3. Secure the graft to the meatus with 5-0 polyglactin. Quilt it for spread fixation with additional 6-0 polydioxanone.
4. Place a 16 Fr silicone catheter. Patients were discharged the same day and returned for a void trial at 1 week.

**Dorsal inlay (Farrell, Vanni).** The graft is placed dorsally through a transurethral approach. It is glans-sparing and avoids splitting the glans.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

| Series | Design | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Sterling 2023 | Single institution; 44 patients with at least 1 year of follow-up | 95% patent without further intervention at mean 36 months; both recurrences had stenosis extending more proximally and were treated with dorsal onlay BMG<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Daneshvar 2020 | 12 institutions; 68 enrolled, 57 with at least 12 months of follow-up | 54/57 (95%) stricture-free at median 17 months. The percentage is not of all 68 enrolled<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Farrell 2022 | Single center; 16 patients, dorsal inlay | Anatomic success 15/16 (93.8%), functional success 16/16 (100%) at median 28.8 months; 83.3% very satisfied<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |

These are retrospective series. The Sterling and Daneshvar cohorts may overlap, so their patients should not be summed as independent. Graft location, fixation and catheter duration are specific to distal repair and do not transfer to a radiated posterior stenosis.

## Minced or liquid buccal grafts

Liquid grafting suspends mechanically minced buccal micrografts in fibrin glue and injects them into the urethrotomy after DVIU. The concept has been tested in rabbits.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

- **Phase 1 and 2 (Nikolavsky 2016).** Engraftment occurred in 2/3 rabbits in phase 1. In phase 2, all 6 treated animals showed engraftment with resolution or improvement of the stricture on urethrography and urethroscopy. Controls treated with fibrin glue alone showed fibrosis and chronic inflammation without engraftment.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Randomized validation (Scott 2020).** In 26 rabbits randomized to DVIU with liquid graft or DVIU with fibrin glue alone, engraftment occurred in 8/12 (67%) vs 0 (p = 0.0005). Radiographic improvement was 58% vs 38% (p = 0.145), and median change in urethral diameter was 59% vs 41.6% (p = 0.29). Engraftment was more frequent, but a functional difference was not shown.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

Human data are limited to a 30-patient uncontrolled pilot (Gaur 2025) of a minced-graft technique in men with primary bulbar strictures shorter than 2 cm. Success was 93.3% at 12 months and 76.6% at 30 months, with seven recurrences. It does not establish efficacy for posterior BNC or VUAS, or for combining micrografts with a drug-coated balloon. An erratum to the paper has been published.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> The [liquid minced buccal graft page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/liquid-minced-buccal) covers the preclinical and clinical evidence.

## Position in care

Mucosal grafting requires viable tissue contact and secure fixation. Avoiding external dissection has not been shown to preserve continence or to prevent operative injury. Reports should state the stenosis site, prior continence, radiation, outcome definition and any additional procedures.

A 2024 meta-analysis of 40 endoscopic VUAS studies (1,452 patients) estimated pooled success at 72.8%, or 62.9% after trim-and-fill correction for publication bias. Radiotherapy was associated with lower success.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> A 2025 narrative review describes traditional endoscopic treatment (DVIU or dilation, with or without injectables) as successful in 40–72% of patients, with about 90% needing repeat intervention within 2 years. It reports short-term success approaching 85–90% for newer endoscopic techniques such as drug-coated balloon dilation and TUITMR. This is a review's summary of short-term series, not comparative evidence.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

The AUA/SUFU 2023 amendment states that surgeons may perform dilation, vesicourethral incision or transurethral resection for post-prostatectomy VUAS (Conditional Recommendation, Evidence Level C). It states that surgeons may perform robotic or open reconstruction for recalcitrant bladder neck or VUAS stenosis (Conditional Recommendation, Evidence Level C). It does not address endourethroplasty.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> Reconstruction does not always require a combined abdominoperineal approach. Posterior endourethroplasty remains a specialist option that needs comparative and long-term evidence.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
