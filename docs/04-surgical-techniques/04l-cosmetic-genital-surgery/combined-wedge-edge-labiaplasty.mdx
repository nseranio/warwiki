# Combined Wedge-Edge Resection Labiaplasty (Modified Trim + Wedge)

The **combined wedge-edge resection**, also called the **modified trim with wedge component**, is a hybrid labiaplasty that integrates a central full-thickness wedge with edge-margin trimming in the same operation. Cao 2015 described the technique in *Aesthet Plast Surg* (49 patients selected from 524 labia esthetic surgeries; retrospective; mean follow-up 4.5 months). It was developed for patients in whom neither pure trim nor pure wedge alone is sufficient. These are typically patients with bi-dimensional hypertrophy (excess in both the horizontal / protrusion plane and the vertical / length plane), asymmetric / unilateral hypertrophy, or hyperpigmented or irregular edges who also need substantial central volume reduction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> For positioning against other female cosmetic options, see [Cosmetic Genital Surgery — Female](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/female-cosmetic).

**Society positioning.** ACOG Committee Opinion No. 795 (2020) states that procedures to alter sexual appearance or function, other than those for clinical indications, are not medically indicated. It advises counseling patients about the lack of high-quality data on effectiveness and about the risks of pain, bleeding, infection, scarring, adhesions, altered sensation, dyspareunia, and reoperation.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The FIGO 2025 statement takes a similar position: it cites the lack of strong evidence that cosmetic genital surgery is safe and effective.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> Both documents advise screening for body dysmorphic disorder.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> ACOG Committee Opinion No. 686 (2017) advises that labiaplasty in girls younger than 18 years be considered only for significant congenital malformation or persistent symptoms caused directly by labial anatomy, or both.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Rationale — why combine?

Each standalone technique has gaps the other can fill (per the Zahedi 2023 algorithm and the Ucar 2025 comparison):

| Technique alone | Strength | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| **Trim** | Removes hyperpigmented / irregular edge; addresses vertical / length excess; safe in smokers | Cannot reduce central horizontal protrusion; sacrifices native edge<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Wedge** | Reduces central protrusion; preserves natural edge | Cannot remove edge irregularity / pigmentation; cannot address vertical excess; pooled dehiscence 8% (95% CI 5–13%); awkward for asymmetry<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |

Combining both addresses both dimensions of hypertrophy and removes the undesirable edge. Cao and colleagues propose it for asymmetry, especially unilateral hypertrophy; no comparison with either technique alone has been published.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Indications

- **Bi-dimensional hypertrophy.** Excess in both width / protrusion and length.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Dark / irregular edge plus central bulk.** The patient wants the edge removed and substantial central volume reduced in the same operation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Asymmetric or unilateral hypertrophy.** 42.9% of Cao's combined-technique cohort had unilateral reduction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Concurrent labial and lateral clitoral-hood redundancy.** Composite variants (Zhou 2022 trilobal, Xia 2021 three-step) extend the same combined logic to the hood.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

Smokers and cocaine users carry the dehiscence risk of the wedge component and are counseled and screened as for any wedge-based procedure. In the Ucar 2025 series, the three dehiscences after wedge resection all occurred in active smokers.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Surgical technique — Cao 2015 combined wedge-edge resection

The steps below are a synthesis of standard wedge and trim technique. The Cao 2015 abstract reports the indication and results but not step-level detail.

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| **1. Marking** | Lithotomy. Mark the full-thickness wedge centered on the most protuberant portion (apex toward the interlabial sulcus, arms reaching the free edge). Mark the edge-trim line above and / or below the wedge along the segment of redundant or hyperpigmented margin. The trim line typically blends smoothly with the wedge arms so that the final closure does not produce a step deformity.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **2. Anesthesia** | Local with epinephrine, with or without sedation, or general. |
| **3. Wedge excision** | Full-thickness wedge excised through both medial and lateral mucosal surfaces. |
| **4. Edge trim excision** | The protruding / pigmented margin is trimmed along the planned line, either above or below the wedge, or along the entire residual length as needed. |
| **5. Hemostasis** | Bipolar electrocautery; selective suture ligation. |
| **6. Closure (wedge first)** | The wedge is closed first with a multilayer absorbable closure (deep absorbable plus superficial), as for isolated wedge.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **7. Closure (trim second)** | The trim edges are then approximated to the wedge closure with fine absorbable suture, blending the two suture lines into a continuous edge. |
| **8. Contralateral side** | Repeat with deliberate intraoperative comparison for symmetry — often asymmetric resection given the indication for the technique. |

**Cao 2015 perioperative metrics**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>:

- n = 49 patients (retrospective, mean follow-up 4.5 months); 21 (42.9%) had unilateral reduction.
- Mean operative time was 56 min.
- Minor dehiscence occurred in 2 / 49 (4.1%).
- One patient had a revision for postoperative asymmetry.
- All patients were satisfied with aesthetic appearance.

## Composite variants extending the combined logic

| Variant | Description | Outcomes |
|---|---|---|
| **Zhou 2022 trilobal composite** | Wedge for horizontal labial excess + edge trim for vertical excess + third lobe for **lateral clitoral hood**. n = 136 (224 sides) | 91.5% cosmetic / 95.9% functional satisfaction; revision 2.2%; no serious complications<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Xia 2021 three-step excision** | Sequential: prominent clitoral hood skin parallel to labia majora → wedge of protuberant labia → triangle at labia–hood junction. n = 136 | 95.5% satisfaction at 3 mo; complications 4.4%; revision 3.7%; sexual life improved 70.9%<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| **Hamori extended wedge** | Wedge-based, extended superiorly toward the clitoral hood | Video vignette of the author's preferred technique (aesthetic analysis, marking, execution); no outcomes reported<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **Jiang 2021 medial de-epi + lateral edge resection** | Medial-surface de-epithelialization preserves the edge; lateral edge resection trims protruding margin | n = 51 (control 26 with wedge); 94.1% satisfaction vs 96.2% in controls (NS); FSFI improved significantly from baseline only in the modified group, without a significant between-group difference<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |

The Zhou trilobal and Xia three-step both extend combined wedge-edge logic to include a lateral hood lobe, useful when concurrent hood redundancy is the indication.

## Patient-selection algorithm

Adapted from Zahedi 2023 with the combined-technique extension from Cao 2015:<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Patient factor | Favors trim alone | Favors wedge alone | Favors combined |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Edge quality** | Poor (irregular, hyperpigmented) | Good (smooth, normal color) | Poor edge **plus** central bulk |
| **Protrusion morphology** | Primarily vertical / length | Primarily horizontal / central | **Bi-dimensional** |
| **Symmetry** | Symmetric bilateral | Symmetric bilateral | **Asymmetric / unilateral** |
| **Pigmentation goal** | Wants dark edge removed | Wants edge preserved | Wants dark edge removed **plus** volume reduction |
| **Nicotine / cocaine use** | Acceptable | Avoid (dehiscence risk) | Caution — wedge component carries the same risk |
| **Concurrent hood redundancy** | Limited ability to address | Can extend superiorly | **Trilobal / three-step** variants ideal |

## Outcomes summary

| Study | Variant | n | Satisfaction | Dehiscence | Revision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cao 2015** | Combined wedge-edge<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 49 | 100% | 4.1% (minor) | 2% (1/49) |
| **Zhou 2022** | Trilobal composite<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | 136 (224 sides) | 91.5% cosmetic / 95.9% functional | Not reported separately (no serious complications) | 2.2% |
| **Xia 2021** | Three-step excision<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | 136 | 95.5% | 4.4% (all complications) | 3.7% |
| **Jiang 2021** | Medial de-epi + lateral edge<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | 51 | 94.1% | Not reported | Not reported |

Across labiaplasty as a whole, the Géczi 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis (86 studies, 53 with quantitative data) found pooled satisfaction of 94% (95% CI 93–95%), highest for de-epithelialization (97%). Composite reduction had a pooled swelling incidence of 13% (95% CI 2–54%).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> No comparison shows the combined technique to be superior on satisfaction.

## Advantages

- Addresses bi-dimensional hypertrophy in a single operation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Removes hyperpigmented / irregular edge and reduces central volume.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Suited to asymmetry and unilateral hypertrophy, which accounted for 21 of the 49 patients in the Cao series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Composite variants address concurrent lateral clitoral-hood redundancy in the same procedure.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- Reported satisfaction is 91–100% across the published short-term retrospective series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

---

## Disadvantages

- **More technically demanding.** Proficiency in both trim and wedge approaches is required, with careful blending at the suture-line transitions.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Operative time.** Mean operative time was 56 min in Cao 2015; the series has no comparison group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Dehiscence risk at the wedge closure** persists; the same precautions as for isolated wedge apply (multilayer closure; active smoking is a relative contraindication).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Sacrifices the native labial edge** (unlike isolated wedge or de-epithelialization).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Limited published evidence.** The reports are small retrospective single-center series (level IV).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

---

## Postoperative management

The regimen below is a commonly used one; the cited series do not specify it.

- **Activity restriction.** Intercourse, tampon use and strenuous exercise are avoided for 4–6 weeks, with particular care early because of the wedge-component dehiscence risk.
- **Wound care.** Ice 48–72 h; sitz baths 24–48 h onward; topical antibiotic ointment; loose-fitting clothing.
- **Smoking cessation** is advised preoperatively when feasible.
- **Follow-up.** 1 week (wound check; watch the wedge closure), 2 weeks, 6 weeks (clearance), 3–6 months (final outcome).
- **PRO assessment.** FSFI and FGSIS at baseline and ≥ 6 months postoperatively.
