# Longitudinal Incision With Penoscrotal-Angle Reconstruction (Xu)

The **Xu longitudinal incision technique** is a component of the modified penoplasty that Xu et al. described in 2015 for concealed penis. It pairs complete penile degloving with a longitudinal incision at the penoscrotal junction for access, drainage and fixation. The tunica albuginea is then anchored to the proximal dartos to reconstruct the penoscrotal angle, with the aim of limiting retraction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The authors describe the longitudinal incision and a Z-plasty as alternatives within the same operative framework.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For the umbrella technique comparison see [Penoscrotal-Web Correction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/penoscrotal-web-correction); for adult buried penis repair see [Buried Penis Repair (04e)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/buried-penis-repair).

## Indications

| Setting | Indications |
|---|---|
| **Concealed penis in the Xu series** | Xu selected flat or beak-shaped, apparently short penises whose normal length was revealed by pubic-root compression; webbed penis, trapped penis, and micropenis were excluded. Repair used degloving and tunica-to-proximal-dartos fixation through a longitudinal or Z-plasty incision; the 41 outcomes were not stratified by incision.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Concurrent phimosis** | Circumcision integrated into the same procedure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Adult aesthetic webbing alone** | Not studied. Thomas and Navia propose a penoscrotal junction Z-plasty for adult webbing.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |

## Operative Protocol<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Step 1: Circumcision incision and degloving

1. Foreskin circumcision incision along the coronal sulcus.
2. Sharp dissection in the plane immediately superficial to the tunica albuginea.
3. Complete penile degloving, dividing the fibrous dartos bands that tether the penile skin to deeper structures. This frees the shaft from its concealed position.

### Step 2: Longitudinal penoscrotal-junction incision

4. Vertical incision along the ventral midline at the penoscrotal junction through skin and dartos.
5. Dual purpose:
   - **Access** for the fixation step below.
   - **Drainage** of the degloving dead space. The authors describe opening this cavity as favorable for drainage.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Step 3: Penoscrotal-angle fixation

6. Through the longitudinal incision, place fixation sutures from the tunica albuginea to the proximal tunica dartos.
7. This anchors the base of the shaft in its exteriorized position and is intended to limit proximal telescoping. It addresses the mechanism Alter and Ehrlich identified: hypermobility of ventral skin and dartos relative to Buck's fascia and the corpora.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### Step 4: Closure

8. Close the longitudinal and circumcision incisions in layers as a simple linear closure, with no flap transposition.

## Longitudinal Incision vs Z-plasty

The authors describe these as alternatives for the penoscrotal-angle reconstruction step.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Consideration | Longitudinal | Z-plasty |
|---|---|---|
| Geometry | Straight midline | Triangular flap transposition |
| Operative complexity | Low | Moderate |
| Geometric lengthening | None; relies on degloving, fixation and tissue elasticity | Theoretical 75% increase in central-limb length with a classic 60° Z-plasty; actual scar lengthening is usually smaller.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| Drainage of degloving dead space | Opens the cavity in Xu's technique<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Also opens the cavity in Xu's technique<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Flap-tip necrosis, trapdoor deformity, mismatch | No flap, so not applicable | Z-plasty angles below 30° increase tip-necrosis risk; transposed flaps can form trapdoor or pincushion change, and flap alignment requires planning.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> In 100 mixed pediatric genital Z-plasties, one suture line separated and one hypertrophic scar was treated with steroids.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Suggested fit | Xu’s selected non-webbed concealed-penis cohort; adequate tissue elasticity is a technical consideration for linear closure, not a reported incision-selection rule<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Webbing in which skin redistribution is needed; Xu excluded webbed penis, and the adult Z-plasty proposal was not compared with Xu’s incision<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |

A longitudinal incision provides no geometric lengthening, so it is unlikely to suffice when ventral lengthening is needed, as in severe penoscrotal webbing with inelastic tissue. Z-plasty has been described for mixed pediatric genital indications and proposed for adult webbing; the pediatric series’ available abstract does not provide webbing-specific results.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> In a randomized study of 40 uncircumcised children with congenital webbing, recurrence by 12 months was 1/20 after Z-scrotoplasty and 3/20 after Heineke–Mikulicz scrotoplasty (p = 0.605); the trial did not compare Xu’s incision or study adults.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## The Alter Fixation Principle (Shared Across Concealed-Penis Repairs)

Alter and Ehrlich reported that hypermobility of ventral skin and dartos relative to Buck's fascia is a major cause of surgical failure in hidden-penis repair. Because the skin and dartos are inadequately attached, the corporeal bodies telescope proximally into the scrotum and pubis. Their solution was to tack the subdermis of the ventral penoscrotal junction to the tunica albuginea (6 adults and 7 children).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Xu applies the same principle through the longitudinal incision, fixing the tunica albuginea to the proximal dartos. Related modified penoplasty techniques do the same:

- **Casale 1999.** Penile skin was fixed at the penoscrotal and penopubic angles in 12/18 type-1 patients; fixation with sutures to maintain length was reported in 10/18 type-2 patients, without specified fixation sites.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Yang 2013.** Penoscrotal-angle reconstruction as Step 3 of a modified penoplasty (n = 201).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Borsellino 2007.** Penopubic and penoscrotal angle reconstruction via the scrotal raphe (n = 87).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Advantages and Limitations

**Advantages.** The authors describe the technique as straightforward. The longitudinal incision provides access and drainage, and no flap geometry is required. Phimosis is corrected in the same operation. Xu reported no visible scars or erectile discomfort, four cases of distal penile skin edema, and one recurrence requiring repeat surgery during 6 months to 2 years of follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Limitations.**

- Xu reported a single-center series of 41 concealed-penis repairs using a longitudinal incision or Z-plasty, without an incision-specific comparison.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- The abstract does not report which patients received a longitudinal incision and which a Z-plasty, so outcomes are not stratified by approach.
- No validated patient-reported outcomes or standardized cosmetic scoring.
- Follow-up was 6 months to 2 years, so durability through puberty and erections is unknown.
- The technique lacks the geometric lengthening of Z-plasty and may be insufficient for severe penoscrotal webbing in inelastic tissue.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- It was reported in a concealed-penis cohort, not in adult aesthetic webbing, for which Thomas and Navia propose Z-plasty.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
