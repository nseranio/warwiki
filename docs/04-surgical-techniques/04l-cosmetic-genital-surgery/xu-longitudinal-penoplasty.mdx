# Longitudinal Incision With Penoscrotal-Angle Reconstruction (Xu)

The **Xu longitudinal incision technique** is a component of the modified penoplasty that Xu et al. described in 2015 for concealed penis. It pairs complete penile degloving with a longitudinal incision at the penoscrotal junction for access, drainage and fixation. The tunica albuginea is then anchored to the proximal dartos to reconstruct the penoscrotal angle and prevent retraction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The authors describe the longitudinal incision and a Z-plasty as alternatives within the same operative framework.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For the umbrella technique comparison see [Penoscrotal-Web Correction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/penoscrotal-web-correction); for adult buried penis repair see [Buried Penis Repair (04e)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/buried-penis-repair).

---

## Indications

| Setting | Indications |
|---|---|
| **Concealed penis (any subtype)** | Congenital buried penis, webbed penis, or post-circumcision trapped penis, treated with degloving, longitudinal incision and tunica-to-dartos fixation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Concurrent phimosis** | Circumcision integrated into the same procedure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Adult aesthetic webbing alone** | Not studied. Thomas and Navia propose a penoscrotal junction Z-plasty for adult webbing.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |

---

## Operative Protocol<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Step 1: Circumcision incision and degloving

1. Foreskin circumcision incision along the coronal sulcus.
2. Sharp dissection in the plane immediately superficial to the tunica albuginea.
3. Complete penile degloving, dividing the fibrous dartos bands that tether the penile skin to deeper structures. This frees the shaft from its concealed position.

### Step 2: Longitudinal penoscrotal-junction incision

4. Vertical incision along the ventral midline at the penoscrotal junction through skin and dartos.
5. Dual purpose:
   - **Access** for the fixation step below.
   - **Drainage** of the degloving dead space. The authors describe opening this cavity as favorable for drainage.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Step 3: Penoscrotal-angle fixation

6. Through the longitudinal incision, place fixation sutures from the tunica albuginea to the proximal tunica dartos.
7. This anchors the base of the shaft in its exteriorized position and prevents proximal telescoping. It addresses the mechanism Alter and Ehrlich identified: hypermobility of ventral skin and dartos relative to Buck's fascia and the corpora.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### Step 4: Closure

8. Close the longitudinal and circumcision incisions in layers as a simple linear closure, with no flap transposition.

## Longitudinal Incision vs Z-plasty

The authors describe these as alternatives for the penoscrotal-angle reconstruction step.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Consideration | Longitudinal | Z-plasty |
|---|---|---|
| Geometry | Straight midline | Triangular flap transposition |
| Operative complexity | Low | Moderate |
| Geometric lengthening | None; relies on degloving, fixation and tissue elasticity | About 75% of central limb length at 60° (classic Z-plasty geometry) |
| Drainage of degloving dead space | Yes; opens the cavity<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | No specific drainage |
| Flap-tip necrosis, trapdoor deformity, mismatch | No flap, so not applicable | Recognized Z-plasty risks; 2 of 100 pediatric Z-plasties had a healing problem<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Suggested fit | Concealed penis with adequate tissue elasticity | Webbing in which geometric lengthening is needed |

A longitudinal incision provides no geometric lengthening, so it is unlikely to suffice when ventral lengthening is needed, as in severe penoscrotal webbing with inelastic tissue. Z-plasty is the technique used for that problem in a pediatric series and proposed in the adult algorithm.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## The Alter Fixation Principle (Shared Across Concealed-Penis Repairs)

Alter and Ehrlich reported that hypermobility of ventral skin and dartos relative to Buck's fascia is a major cause of surgical failure in hidden-penis repair. Because the skin and dartos are inadequately attached, the corporeal bodies telescope proximally into the scrotum and pubis. Their solution was to tack the subdermis of the ventral penoscrotal junction to the tunica albuginea (6 adults and 7 children).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Xu applies the same principle through the longitudinal incision, fixing the tunica albuginea to the proximal dartos. Related modified penoplasty techniques do the same:

- **Casale 1999.** Penile-skin fixation at penoscrotal and penopubic angles (12/18 type-1 and 10/18 type-2 patients).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> 
- **Yang 2013.** Penoscrotal-angle reconstruction as Step 3 of a modified penoplasty (n = 201).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> 
- **Borsellino 2007.** Penopubic and penoscrotal angle reconstruction via the scrotal raphe (n = 87).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

| Feature | Xu longitudinal | Z-plasty | Borsellino raphe | V-I (Bagnara) | Alter ventral tacking |

| Primary indication | Concealed penis (all types) | Webbed penis / penoscrotal webbing | Concealed penis (all types) | Congenital webbed penis | Hidden penis |

| Flap transposition | None | Required | None | None | None |

## Advantages and Limitations

**Advantages.** The authors describe the technique as straightforward. The longitudinal incision provides access and drainage, and no flap geometry is required. Phimosis is corrected in the same operation. No complications were reported in the index series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Limitations.**

- Single-center, single-series evidence (n = 41), with no replication or comparative trial.
- The abstract does not report which patients received a longitudinal incision and which a Z-plasty, so outcomes are not stratified by approach.
- No validated patient-reported outcomes or standardized cosmetic scoring.
- Follow-up was 6 months to 2 years, so durability through puberty and erections is unknown.
- The technique lacks the geometric lengthening of Z-plasty and may be insufficient for severe penoscrotal webbing in inelastic tissue.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- It was reported in a concealed-penis cohort, not in adult aesthetic webbing, for which Thomas and Navia propose Z-plasty.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
