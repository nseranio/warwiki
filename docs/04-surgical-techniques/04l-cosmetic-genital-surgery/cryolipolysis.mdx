# Cryolipolysis — Suprapubic Fat Reduction

**Cryolipolysis** is a noninvasive, FDA-cleared technology that uses controlled cooling to selectively destroy subcutaneous adipocytes without damaging overlying skin or surrounding structures. When applied to the suprapubic region, it serves as a nonsurgical alternative to liposuction for reducing the fat pad that buries the penile shaft. One prospective study (Azab 2021, n = 46) reported increased apparent penile length after three sessions of suprapubic cryolipolysis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For the broader male cosmetic-genital-surgery decision framework, see the [Male Cosmetic Genital Surgery atlas page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/male-cosmetic). For the surgical alternative, direct fat-pad excision, see [Suprapubic Lipectomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/suprapubic-lipectomy). For the broader buried-penis condition, see [Adult-Acquired Buried Penis](/docs/clinical-conditions/03g-genital-scrotal/buried-penis).

---

## I. Definition and Concept

Cryolipolysis exploits the observation that lipid-rich adipocytes are more susceptible to cold injury than surrounding water-rich cells (skin, nerves, vasculature). A cup-shaped applicator draws a fold of skin and subcutaneous fat between two cooling plates, lowering tissue temperature to approximately −10°C to −11°C for 35–60 minutes. This induces crystallization of cytoplasmic lipids within adipocytes, triggering a cascade of apoptosis, panniculitis, and gradual adipocyte clearance over 2–4 months.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

The concept derives from the clinical observation of "popsicle panniculitis," cold-induced fat necrosis in children's cheeks after prolonged cold exposure, described decades before the technology was developed.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## II. Mechanism of Action

The proposed mechanism proceeds through this sequence, based on human histology, pig and mouse models.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

1. **Cooling phase.** Tissue temperature drops to approximately 0°C to −10°C; subdermal temperatures decline precipitously within 30 minutes.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
2. **Lipid crystallization.** Cytoplasmic lipids within adipocytes crystallize at temperatures that do not damage water-rich cells (skin, nerves, vessels).
3. **Adipocyte membrane disruption.** Crystallization causes loss of cellular integrity and membrane disruption, evident histologically in all treated areas.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
4. **Apoptosis initiation.** Apoptosis is the primary cell-death pathway, with some necrosis also occurring.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
5. **Inflammatory panniculitis.** An inflammatory infiltrate of immune cells (macrophages, lymphocytes) appears at the treatment site within 2–3 days, peaking at 14–30 days.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
6. **Phagocytic clearance.** Macrophages engulf lipid debris and dead adipocytes; this process occurs gradually over 2–4 months.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
7. **Connective-tissue remodeling.** Focal dissolution and homogenization of collagen fibers with dissolution of interlobular fibrous septa; neocapillarization observed at 45–60 days.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
8. **Fat-layer reduction.** Fat thickness decreases measurably. A systematic review of 19 studies reported average reductions of 14.67–28.5% by caliper and 10.3–25.5% by ultrasound.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

**Additional mechanisms.** A pilot study suggests cryolipolysis may also promote conversion of white adipocytes to brown / beige adipocytes ("browning"), with increased expression of UCP-1, PPAR-γ, and PPAR-α markers in treated tissue.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

**Selectivity.** Adipocytes are selectively damaged while skin, nerves, blood vessels, and muscle are spared, based on the higher freezing point of lipids versus water-rich tissues.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## III. FDA Clearance History

Cryolipolysis (CoolSculpting, originally ZELTIQ Aesthetics, now Allergan / AbbVie) has received sequential FDA clearances for multiple body areas.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

- **2010.** Flanks (love handles); first FDA authorization (De Novo).<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup>
- **2012.** Abdomen.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup>
- **2014.** Thighs.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[31]](#ref31)</sup>
- **2015.** Submental area (double chin).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[31]](#ref31)</sup>
- Subsequent clearances for upper arms, back / bra fat, banana roll (infragluteal), and lateral thighs.

**Off-label suprapubic application.** The suprapubic / mons-pubis region is not specifically FDA-cleared for cryolipolysis. Treatment of this area is off-label use of the device; applicators designed for the abdomen or small areas (CoolMini) can be adapted.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

## IV. Suprapubic Application — The Key Study

Azab 2021 is the only published study identified that evaluates cryolipolysis for suprapubic fat reduction to increase apparent penile length.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- **Design.** Prospective, n = 46 males, single-arm (no control group).
- **Population.** Men complaining of "buried short apparent small-size penis" due to suprapubic fat.
- **Protocol.** Three consecutive suprapubic cryolipolysis sessions.

**Results.** Mean apparent stretched penile length (skin to glans tip) increased progressively. The abstract reports the values at sessions 1, 2 and 3 and states that measurements were recorded before the three sessions. If each value was taken before that session's treatment, the final value reflects two completed sessions.

| Session | Apparent SPL |
|---|---|
| Session 1 (baseline) | 12.1 ± 0.5 cm |
| Session 2 | 12.66 ± 0.48 cm |
| Session 3 | 12.88 ± 0.72 cm |
| Total gain | about 0.78 cm (p &lt; 0.001) |

Suprapubic skinfold thickness (adipometer) fell from 2.99 ± 0.49 cm to 2.37 ± 0.48 cm and 2.00 ± 0.37 cm at sessions 1, 2 and 3 (p &lt; 0.001), a reduction of about 33%. The abstract calls the procedure safe but does not itemize adverse events.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The Mineroff 2023 narrative review of minimally invasive treatments for suprapubic adiposity found no clinical studies of cryolipolysis for this indication in its PubMed search and called for such studies; its search did not capture Azab 2021.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> No comparison of cryolipolysis with liposuction for the suprapubic area has been published.

## V. General Efficacy Data (All Body Sites)

### Fat-reduction measurements

In the Ravindran 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis (30 studies, 3,158 participants), the following changes were seen at 12 weeks compared with baseline.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

- **BMI.** MD −1.80 (95% CI −2.98 to −0.62; p = 0.0003).
- **Waist-to-hip ratio.** MD −0.09 (95% CI −0.16 to −0.02; p = 0.001).
- **Abdominal circumference.** MD −3.56 cm (95% CI −4.98 to −2.15; p &lt; 0.001).
- Average reduction in caliper measurement was 14.67–28.5% in the Ingargiola 2015 review of 19 studies.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Average reduction by ultrasound was 10.3–25.5% in the same review.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- No significant impact on lipid levels or liver function tests was noted in any of those studies.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### Patient satisfaction

- The pooled satisfaction rate was 80.4% (meta-analysis).<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- In a prospective single-cohort study of abdomen and flank treatment (112 treated, 106 evaluable), 89.6% were "satisfied" or "very satisfied" at 12 weeks.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- In the same study, 93.4% would recommend it to a friend and 90.6% reported "noticeable" or "very noticeable" fat reduction.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

### Multiple treatments

In McKeown and Payne 2021 (28 patients, 58 body areas, mean 2.8 cycles per area), skinfold thickness fell 40% at 12 weeks in the 27 responders. Skinfold reduction was greater with ≥ 3 cycles than with 1–2 cycles (p = 0.01), and 51 of 58 areas (88%) were rated satisfactory.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

## VI. Treatment Protocol

**Standard treatment parameters.**<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[9]](#ref9)[[17]](#ref17)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

- **Temperature.** Approximately −10°C to −11°C.
- **Duration.** 35–60 minutes per cycle (newer applicators achieve equivalent results in 35 minutes).<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **Applicator selection.** Vacuum cup-shaped applicators of various sizes; flat-cup applicators for inner thighs and arms; small applicators (CoolMini) for submental and small areas.
- **Post-treatment massage.** 2 minutes of manual massage immediately after applicator removal (1 minute of vigorous kneading, then 1 minute of circular massage). In Boey 2014 (efficacy group n = 10, split-abdomen design), fat-layer reduction by ultrasound was 68% greater on the massaged side at 2 months and 44% greater at 4 months.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- **Number of sessions.** 1–3 sessions per area, spaced 4–8 weeks apart.
- **Results timeline.** Gradual fat reduction over 2–4 months, with maximum effect at 3–6 months.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[9]](#ref9)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

**For the suprapubic application** (extrapolated from Azab and general protocols).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- Applicator placed over the suprapubic fat pad (mons-pubis region).
- Standard cooling parameters (−10°C, 35–60 minutes).
- Three sessions, as used by Azab.
- Post-treatment massage of the treated area.
- Care to avoid proximity to the penile shaft and scrotal structures.

Newer dual-applicator systems (CoolSculpting Elite) allow simultaneous bilateral treatment, reducing total procedure time. In the Geronemus 2026 multicountry prospective study (110 treated, 96 evaluable), 83.3% (80/96) were satisfied with midsection results and mean fat-volume loss was 194.8 mL at 12 weeks.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

## VII. Adverse Events

### Common (minor, self-limiting)

Pooled proportions in the 2025 meta-analysis (Ravindran).<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

| Event | Incidence |
|---|---|
| Numbness / paresthesia | 49.5% |
| Erythema | 44.5% |
| Edema | 30.5% |
| Pain | 28.8% |
| Sensitivity | 25.4% |
| Tingling | 15.2% |
| Hyperpigmentation | 2% |

Most adverse events resolve within 1–4 weeks; numbness may persist for approximately 3 months.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> A 2026 prospective observational study of clinic patients recruited as device monitors in Japan (n = 1,553 treatment areas) reported that the overall AE rate decreased from 71.6% immediately after treatment to 1.3% at 3 months.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

### Serious / rare complications

The Hedayati 2020 systematic review (53 articles) lists these.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

- **Severe / persistent pain.** Rare; may last weeks to months.
- **Dysesthesia.** Altered sensation at the treatment site.
- **Motor neuropathy.** Extremely rare case reports.
- **Skin hyperpigmentation.** More common in darker skin types.
- **Contour irregularities.** Indentations, asymmetries, soft-tissue atrophy.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
- **Paradoxical adipose hyperplasia (PAH).** See below.

### Paradoxical adipose hyperplasia (PAH)

PAH is characterized by abnormal enlargement of the treatment area occurring 2–5 months after cryolipolysis, with the treated fat becoming a well-demarcated, firm subcutaneous mass that exceeds the original volume.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[25]](#ref25)[[26]](#ref26)[[27]](#ref27)[[28]](#ref28)</sup>

**Incidence.** Initially reported at 0.0051% (Jalian 2014); subsequent studies suggest the true incidence is substantially higher.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

- **0.05–0.39% per treatment cycle** in the Nikolis 2021 multicenter review (8,658 cycles, 2,114 patients), against a manufacturer-quoted rate of 0.025%.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>
- **0.67%** at one academic center (Cox 2022; 4 cases).<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>
- **4 of 398 patients** at one center (Stroumza 2018).<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>
- **0.22% pooled incidence** (95% CI 0.10–0.47) in a 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis (28 studies, 13,078 patients; low-certainty evidence). Only 4 studies had follow-up of 16 weeks or more.<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup>
- PAH is likely underreported.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[26]](#ref26)[[32]](#ref32)</sup>

**Risk factors.**<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[25]](#ref25)[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

- **Male sex.** Jalian 2014 noted that PAH seemed more common in men, and 55% of cases in the Nikolis series were male. Sex-based risk differences were not statistically significant in the 2025 pooled analysis, so male sex is not an established risk factor.<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup>
- **Older device models.** 76.9% of PAH cases were associated with older CoolSculpting units, and incidence fell by more than 75% with newer models.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>
- **European ethnic origin.** 77.8% of PAH cases were of European ethnic origin; the series gives no population denominator.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>
- No single unifying risk factor identified.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

**Pathophysiology.** Unknown. Histology shows nonspecific panniculitis.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup> One hypothesis suggests that some adipocytes may be "naturally selected" for survival due to inherent cold tolerance, and the inflammatory response may stimulate adipogenesis or hypertrophy in surviving cells.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

**Management.**<sup>[[25]](#ref25)[[26]](#ref26)[[28]](#ref28)</sup>

- Spontaneous resolution has not been reported (Jalian), although 3 of 4 patients in the Stroumza series stabilized without treatment.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[28]](#ref28)</sup>
- Surgical correction with liposuction, abdominoplasty, or both has been used.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)[[26]](#ref26)</sup>
- In the Cox series, none of the 4 surgically treated patients had recurrence at mean follow-up of 13.8 months, and 3 had no residual deformity.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

## VIII. Contraindications

**Absolute.**<sup>[[23]](#ref23)[[29]](#ref29)</sup>

- Cryoglobulinemia.
- Cold agglutinin disease.
- Paroxysmal cold hemoglobinuria.
- Cold urticaria.

**Relative.**<sup>[[23]](#ref23)[[29]](#ref29)</sup>

- Raynaud disease, though Yanes 2021 found no exacerbations in patients with mild–moderate Raynaud disease treated with cryolipolysis.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup>
- Scars or hernias in the treatment area.
- Impaired skin sensation in the treatment area.
- Open or infected wounds.
- Dermatitis or eczema in the treatment area.
- Pregnancy.

**Suprapubic-specific considerations.**

- Proximity to the penile shaft and scrotum: applicator placement must avoid direct cooling of genital structures.
- Thin suprapubic skin in lean patients may increase the risk of skin injury (theoretical).
- No published safety data specific to the suprapubic region exist beyond Azab 2021.

## IX. Comparison: Cryolipolysis vs Suprapubic Lipectomy

| Feature | Cryolipolysis | [Suprapubic Lipectomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/suprapubic-lipectomy) |
|---|---|---|
| Invasiveness | Noninvasive | Minimally invasive (surgical) |
| Anesthesia | None required | Local ± sedation, or general |
| Fat reduction | 10–28% (ranges across studies, all body sites) | 50–80% (immediate) |
| Number of sessions | 1–3+ for optimal results | Usually 1 |
| Downtime | None (return to activities immediately) | 1–2 weeks |
| Results timeline | Gradual over 2–4 months | Immediate (swelling resolves 4–6 weeks) |
| Apparent penile length gain | ~ 0.78 cm (Azab 2021, 3 sessions) | 1–2 cm (estimated) |
| Suprapubic fat reduction | ~ 33% (Azab 2021, 3 sessions) | 50–80% |
| Complications | Minor (numbness, erythema); PAH rare | Hematoma, seroma, contour irregularity, infection |
| Skin tightening | None (may worsen laxity) | None (may worsen laxity) |
| FDA status for suprapubic | **Off-label** | Not FDA-regulated (surgical) |
| Published suprapubic evidence | 1 study, n = 46 (Azab 2021) | Multiple case series |

## X. Systemic Safety

Systemic metabolic effects have not been reported with cryolipolysis.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

- No significant change in serum lipids was noted in any of the 19 studies in the Ingargiola 2015 review.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- No significant change in liver function tests was noted in the same review.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Gradual phagocytic clearance of adipocyte debris avoids the bolus lipid release that could theoretically affect hepatic or cardiovascular function.
- No scarring, ulceration, or permanent skin damage reported in standard use.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## XI. Limitations and Evidence Quality

Several caveats apply to the cryolipolysis literature.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[30]](#ref30)</sup>

- **Commercial bias.** Many studies are industry-sponsored or conducted by investigators with financial relationships with device manufacturers. Atiyeh 2020 raised "serious concerns about integrity of many of these reports, particularly with respect to validity of photographic outcome documentation in addition to objectivity, conflict of interest issues, and commercial bias."<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup>
- **Lack of rigorous methodology.** Most studies are single-arm, uncontrolled, with subjective outcome measures (photographs, patient satisfaction). Few RCTs exist.<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup>
- **Suprapubic data are limited.** Only one study (Azab 2021, n = 46) has evaluated suprapubic cryolipolysis, with no control group, no long-term follow-up, and modest length gains (approximately 0.78 cm).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **No head-to-head comparisons** with liposuction for the suprapubic area exist.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Not a weight-loss procedure.** Cryolipolysis is designed for localized fat reduction in patients near ideal body weight with discrete, pinchable fat bulges and is not effective for generalized obesity or large-volume fat reduction.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## XII. Clinical Summary

Cryolipolysis is an FDA-cleared noninvasive technology for localized fat reduction. Reported fat-layer reductions are 10–28% depending on the measure, satisfaction is 80–90% in the cited studies, and adverse events are mostly minor and self-limiting.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> Paradoxical adipose hyperplasia (PAH) is the main rare complication. Reported incidence ranges from 0.05% to 0.67% in single series, with a pooled estimate of 0.22% (low-certainty evidence); the cases in the Cox series were treated surgically. Sex-based risk differences were not statistically significant in the pooled analysis.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)[[27]](#ref27)[[32]](#ref32)</sup> Patients are counseled about PAH before treatment.

For suprapubic fat reduction and apparent penile-length augmentation, cryolipolysis is a minimally studied noninvasive alternative to liposuction. In Azab 2021 (n = 46, single-arm), suprapubic skinfold thickness fell by approximately 1 cm and apparent stretched penile length rose by approximately 0.78 cm (both p &lt; 0.001) over three sessions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The suprapubic area is not specifically FDA-cleared for cryolipolysis.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> Men who prefer a noninvasive approach must accept multiple sessions and results expected to be more modest than those of surgical alternatives; the cited evidence does not compare the two.
