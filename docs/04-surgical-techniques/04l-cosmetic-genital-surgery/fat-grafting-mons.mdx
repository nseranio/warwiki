# Fat Grafting to the Mons Pubis (Volume Restoration)

**Autologous fat grafting to the mons pubis** is the volume-restoration counterpart to [monsplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/monsplasty). It is used to restore contour to the pubic mound when volume is depleted by aging, weight loss, constitutional thinness or prior surgery. Vulvovaginal fat-grafting series report volume restoration and histologic evidence of neocollagenesis, neoangiogenesis and estrogen-receptor upregulation, which the authors attribute to adipose-derived stem cells (ADSCs) in the graft.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> For positioning versus other female cosmetic options see [Cosmetic Genital Surgery — Female](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/female-cosmetic).

**Society positioning.** The ACOG 2020 Committee Opinion No. 795 states that the safety and effectiveness of procedures to alter sexual appearance or function, other than those done for clinical indications, have not been established.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The Opinion does not address mons fat grafting by name. Screening for body dysmorphic disorder (BDD) comes first in every consultation. Evidence for fat grafting to the mons is predominantly Level IV, and no universal protocol exists.

---

## Indications

Fat grafting to the mons addresses volume deficiency rather than excess. The indications below are drawn from vulvovaginal and genital-beautification series, and none of the cited series reports mons-only outcomes<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[2]](#ref2)[[7]](#ref7)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>:

- **Age-related volume loss.** Atrophy of subcutaneous fat in the mons with aging produces a deflated, skeletonized appearance.
- **Post–massive weight loss.** A concave or deflated mons can follow bariatric surgery or significant weight loss. The pubic-contouring literature stresses eliminating pubic fat without creating a significant concavity, which is the setting in which over-resection produces a volume-deficient mons.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Genitourinary syndrome of menopause (GSM).** Vulvovaginal atrophy causes dryness, dyspareunia and sexual dysfunction. In Menkes 2021, microfat was placed in the labia majora and nanofat in the vagina (n = 50).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Post-surgical contour deformity.** Concavities or irregularities after prior abdominoplasty, panniculectomy or aggressive liposuction are a practical indication. No cited series reports outcomes for this indication.
- **Gender-affirming surgery.** In Patel 2021, 6 of 182 patients (3.3%) had labial fat grafting after penile inversion vaginoplasty, most often for flattened labia majora (83% of the grafted patients).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Aesthetic enhancement.** Fat injection into the mons pubis and labia majora is used for pubic contour and fullness as part of genital beautification.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Scar tissue and pain.** Fat grafting has been used for scar release and scar pain in a mixed face and body series, and in vulvovaginal tissue after grafting the pain-associated marker PGP 9.5 was lower on histology.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

---

## Anatomy and volume considerations

Seleem 2023 (*Aesthet Surg J*, 214 women in two BMI groups) reported calculated volumes and weights of the mons pubis and labia majora<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>:

| Parameter | Normal BMI | Overweight / obese |
|---|---|---|
| **Mons pubis volume** | ~ 200 mL | ~ 300 mL |
| **Mons pubis weight** | ~ 180 g | ~ 270 g |
| **Labium majus volume** | ~ 30 mL | ~ 40 mL |
| **Labium majus weight** | ~ 27 g | ~ 36 g |

Mons height is approximately three-quarters of its length and half its width. The authors propose these dimensions and ratios as a basis for aesthetic planning.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Surgical technique

### Harvest

- **Donor sites.** The abdomen, thighs and flanks are used.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Reviews found no significant difference in graft outcome or cell viability between donor sites.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- Tumescent infiltration before aspiration.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- Syringe aspiration (Toomey) or low-pressure power-assisted liposuction with 3.0–3.7 mm cannulas.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

### Processing

- **Centrifugation** is a common method. In clinical studies it gave more favorable outcomes than sedimentation, although animal studies found no difference and one review concluded that the wash-or-centrifuge decision plays little role in graft survival.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- Filtration and sedimentation are alternatives.
- For genital rejuvenation, both microfat (standard structural grafting) and nanofat (mechanically emulsified, ADSC-rich) may be used: microfat for the mons or majora volume; nanofat for vaginal-mucosa trophic or regenerative effect.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Injection

- **Small-caliber blunt cannulas** (0.9 mm was used for the face in one series) through stab incisions.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Micro-autologous fat transplantation (MAFT)** uses small aliquots (0.05–0.1 mL per pass) in multiple layers and tunnels to maximize graft-to-recipient contact and revascularization.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Subcutaneous plane in multiple passes; small filaments deposited on each cannula withdrawal.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Slower injection speed** was associated with higher retention in the studies reviewed.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- In Lai 2023, an average of 20.8 mL was injected into the vulva and mons pubis (and 21.9 mL into the vagina); volume is titrated to the deficit.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Adjunctive strategies

- **ADSC-assisted fat grafting** had the highest graft-survival ranking (SUCRA 82.17%) among simple, PRP-assisted, SVF-assisted and ADSC-assisted grafting in the Dong 2024 network meta-analysis of 31 trials (1,656 patients, all body sites).<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> ADSC enrichment is not part of routine practice.
- **Combined with fractional CO₂ laser** for vulvovaginal rejuvenation has been described as part of a combined regimen (Toplu 2021).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Mechanism of action

Two mechanisms are proposed<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[11]](#ref11)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>:

1. **Volumetric restoration.** Adipocytes that survive act as a permanent soft-tissue filler and restore contour and fullness.
2. **Regenerative or trophic effects.** ADSCs within the graft are proposed to promote neocollagenesis, neoangiogenesis and estrogen-receptor upregulation in the recipient tissue. In a retrospective series of 20 patients (Lai 2023), histology of vaginal tissue after vulvovaginal fat grafting showed increased neocollagenesis, neoangiogenesis and estrogen-receptor levels, and lower protein gene product 9.5, which is associated with neuropathic pain.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Outcomes

| Endpoint | Result | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| **FSFI (sexual function)** | 43.8 to 68.6 at 6 months (p &lt; 0.001; n = 20, vulvovaginal) | Lai 2023<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **VHI (vaginal health)** | Significantly increased at 1 and 3 months (p &lt; 0.0001; n = 50, GSM) | Menkes 2021<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **FSD (sexual distress)** | Significant improvement; data for 80% of patients normalized at 6 months on both scales | Menkes 2021<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **FGSIS (genital self-image)** | 17.7 ± 1.6 to 22.2 ± 1.8 at 12 months (p = 0.013; n = 97, labia majora augmentation and vaginal tightening) | Cihantimur 2021 (360)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| **Patient satisfaction** | 94.5% satisfied or very satisfied with appearance; 80% with functional results (n = 37; fat to mons and labia majora, with or without laser) | Toplu 2021<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Stable result at 18 months** | Results remained stable (microfat and nanofat for GSM) | Menkes 2021<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

### Fat-graft retention

- One technique review reported retention of 20–120% of injected volume, stabilizing at 60–90 days.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- Higher retention was observed in less mobile areas.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- The mons pubis is relatively stable and well-vascularized, favorable for graft survival in principle, although site-specific retention data for the mons are limited.
- In a face and body series (127 procedures, mainly face and breast), 16% of patients needed at least one additional grafting procedure. No mons-specific touch-up rate is available.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Complications

Complication rates in fat grafting to non-breast, non-buttock sites are low<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[8]](#ref8)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>:

- In the GRAFT registry (7,052 procedures, 94% in women), the complication rate was 2.86% for "other" areas and 1.94% for the face, against 7.29% for the breast and 4.19% for the buttocks. The mons was not reported separately.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Common minor complications.** Ecchymosis and edema at donor and recipient sites are the most common and are self-limited. Transient tenderness in the pubic area and erythema at the graft site also occur.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Less common complications.** In GRAFT, facial grafting had palpable mass (0.54%) and infection (0.54%) as the most common complications. Oil cyst, asymmetry and under- or over-correction also occur. Fat necrosis and fat sclerosis were the most prevalent complications in the network meta-analysis.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- In the two small genital series cited, no infection, lipoma or granuloma was noted (Cihantimur 2021, n = 97) and no major side effects were observed (Menkes 2021, n = 50).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Fat-graft survival optimization

| Factor | Recommendation | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| **Donor site** | No significant difference between sites | Strong 2015; Sinno 2016<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Harvest technique** | No significant difference between harvest techniques when tumescent solution is used; low-shear devices maintain fat structural integrity | Strong 2015; Sinno 2016<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Processing** | Centrifugation gave more favorable clinical outcomes than sedimentation; wash or centrifuge decision plays little role in another review | Strong 2015; Sinno 2016<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Injection speed** | Slower injection is associated with higher retention | Strong 2015<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Injection technique** | Multi-layer, multi-tunnel, small aliquots | MAFT (Lai 2023); Locke 2008<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| **Recipient mobility** | Less mobile areas retain more fat | Strong 2015<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Timing** | Inject soon after harvest | Sinno 2016<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Adjuncts** | ADSC-assisted grafting ranked highest for survival (SUCRA 82%) | Dong 2024 NMA<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |

## Fat grafting vs excisional monsplasty

| Feature | Fat grafting to mons | [Excisional monsplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/monsplasty) |
|---|---|---|
| **Goal** | Volume restoration / augmentation | Volume reduction / lift |
| **Typical patient** | Volume-depleted, atrophic mons (postmenopausal, post-bariatric over-correction) | Excess fat / skin, ptotic mons (post-bariatric, obesity) |
| **Technique** | Autologous fat injection | Skin / fat excision ± liposuction ± fascial suspension |
| **Invasiveness** | Minimally invasive | Moderately invasive |
| **Anesthesia** | Local ± sedation, or general | Usually general |
| **Recovery** | 1–2 weeks | 2–4 weeks |
| **Repeat procedures** | Touch-up sometimes needed (16% in a face and body series) | Rarely needed |

The two procedures may be combined in the same patient at different times, for example excisional monsplasty followed by fat grafting to correct contour irregularities. This sequence is a practical approach and is not supported by a cited series.

## Limitations

- ACOG 2020: the safety and effectiveness of procedures to alter sexual appearance or function have not been established.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- No universal protocol for mons-specific fat grafting; volumetric retention measurements are rarely reported for the mons.
- No randomized trials compare fat-grafting strategies in the mons.
- Most evidence is Level IV retrospective single-center.
- Patients should be counseled about the unpredictability of fat-graft survival and the possible need for repeat sessions.

---

## Postoperative management

- **Activity restriction.** Avoid intercourse, tampon use, and strenuous exercise for 2–4 weeks; protect the donor site.
- **Pressure considerations.** Compression at the donor site; avoid pressure on the recipient site so as not to compress the graft.
- **Wound care.** Ice 48–72 h; loose-fitting clothing.
- **Follow-up.** 1 week (donor and recipient), 6 weeks, 6 months, 12 months, with FSFI / FGSIS / VHI / FSD as relevant to indication.
- **Counseling on durability and touch-ups.** Graft survival is unpredictable, and repeat sessions may be needed.

---

## Key takeaways

- Fat grafting to the mons is minimally invasive. Small retrospective genital series report volume restoration and improvements in sexual function and genital self-image, and none reports mons-only outcomes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Satisfaction with appearance was 94.5% in one series of 37 patients, with 80% satisfied with functional results.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Site-specific retention data for the mons are limited, and counseling includes the possibility of a repeat session.
- The evidence is predominantly Level IV, and no universal protocol for fat grafting has been established.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- Slow injection of small aliquots in multiple layers is the technique described; ADSC enrichment ranked highest for survival in the network meta-analysis but is not routine.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
