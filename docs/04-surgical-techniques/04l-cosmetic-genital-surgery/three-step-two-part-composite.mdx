# Three-Step & Two-Part Composite Excision (Lateral Hood + Labia Minora)

The three-step composite excision (Xia 2021, *Plast Reconstr Surg*) and the two-part composite excision (Duan 2026, *Aesthet Plast Surg*) are two related techniques that address the combined lateral clitoral-hood and labia minora hypertrophy pattern in a single sequenced operation. Both are operative responses to the same problem: isolated labiaplasty often leaves residual lateral hood redundancy that produces aesthetic disharmony, and isolated hoodoplasty often leaves residual labial hypertrophy. The authors of both series report satisfactory results from combining the two procedures in a defined sequence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> For positioning versus other female cosmetic options see [Cosmetic Genital Surgery: Female](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/female-cosmetic).

**Society positioning.** ACOG Committee Opinion No. 795 (2020) lists clitoral hood reduction among female genital cosmetic procedures. It states that procedures done to alter sexual appearance or function, excluding those for clinical indications, are not medically indicated, pose substantial risk, and have no established safety or effectiveness, and it advises assessment for body dysmorphic disorder when indicated.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The FIGO 2025 statement goes further and calls it ethically inappropriate for obstetrician-gynecologists to recommend, perform or refer patients for these procedures.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> The composite series are retrospective single-center reports.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Screening for body dysmorphic disorder belongs at the front of the consultation.

---

## When to use a composite approach

Composite excision is indicated when a patient has both:

- **Lateral clitoral-hood redundancy** (Liu Zone L; Xia conventional combined pattern), and
- **Labia minora hypertrophy** (any morphology: diffuse, focal central, or pigmented edge).

Labiaplasty alone in this anatomy leaves the hood prominent, and hoodoplasty alone leaves the labial protrusion uncorrected. The composite techniques address both in one sequenced operation under a single anesthetic.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Central-zone hood hypertrophy (Liu Zone C) is treated differently; see the [Liu-classification bilateral triangular / inverted-V hoodoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/liu-classification-hoodoplasty).

For the fused lateral clitoral hood and labia minora variant (Xia 2022 CLC Type 3; 105 of 602 sides, 17.5%), Xia's group describes a dedicated 4-step excision. Among Type 3 patients who answered the 3-month questionnaire, satisfaction was 91.7% with the 4-step excision and 56.3% with wedge excision (P = 0.01).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Xia 2021 three-step composite excision

The three-step technique removes three anatomical components in sequence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Step | Anatomic target | Excision |
|---|---|---|
| **Step 1** | **Prominent clitoral hood skin** | Removed parallel to the labia majora, together with the clitoral hood sulcus |
| **Step 2** | **Protuberant labia minora** | Wedge resection of the central protruding portion |
| **Step 3** | **Junction between labia minora and clitoral hood** | Triangle of tissue trimmed to blend the two excisions into a continuous edge |

The distinguishing feature is Step 3, the triangle excised at the labia–hood junction. The authors describe it as blending the two prior excisions into one contour. That a step deformity would otherwise form is the technique's rationale and has not been tested against omitting Step 3.

### Outcomes (Xia 2021, n = 136)

| Endpoint | Result |
|---|---|
| Satisfaction at 3 months (91.0% within 14 days) | **95.5%** |
| Complications | **4.4%** |
| Revision | **3.7%** |
| Preoperative discomfort resolved | 43% |
| Preoperative discomfort significantly relieved | 54.2% |
| Sexual life improved | **70.9%** |

## Duan 2026 two-part composite excision

The two-part technique addresses the same anatomy with two excisions instead of three. The excess clitoral hood is removed first, and the prominent labia minora is then reduced by edge resection, which leaves a "pink" edge.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

| Step | Anatomic target |
|---|---|
| **Step 1** | Lateral clitoral-hood reduction |
| **Step 2** | Labia minora edge resection |

The authors describe it as simple and suggest it is more suitable for inexperienced surgeons.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Outcomes (Duan 2026, n = 68; 2019–2024)

| Endpoint | Result |
|---|---|
| Genital Appearance Satisfaction (GAS) scale, postoperative vs preoperative | **Improved (p &lt; 0.05)** |
| Aesthetic satisfaction | **92.6%** |
| Complications | **7.4%** |
| Revision | **1.5%** |

---

## Three-step vs two-part: practical comparison

| Feature | Three-step (Xia 2021) | Two-part (Duan 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| **Number of excisions** | 3 (hood + labial wedge + junction triangle) | 2 (hood + labia) |
| **Junction handling** | Explicit triangular blending excision | No separate junction excision |
| **Cohort size** | 136 | 68 |
| **Satisfaction** | 95.5% | 92.6% |
| **Complications** | 4.4% | 7.4% |
| **Revision** | 3.7% | 1.5% |
| **Sexual-function reporting** | 70.9% improved sex life | GAS improvement p &lt; 0.05 |
| **Labial excision** | Central wedge | Edge resection |
| **Reported advantage** | Explicit junction blending (authors' rationale) | Simpler design (authors' description) |

The cohorts differ in size, follow-up and satisfaction instrument, and the techniques have not been compared directly.

## Step-by-step technique (three-step variant)

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| **1. Marking** | Lithotomy. Mark the lateral clitoral hood excision parallel to the labia majora, the central labia minora wedge at the most protuberant portion, and the junction triangle so that its edges blend with the two prior excisions when closed. |
| **2. Anesthesia** | Local with epinephrine, ± sedation, or general. |
| **3. Hood excision (Step 1)** | Remove prominent lateral clitoral-hood skin parallel to the labia majora. Identify and preserve the **dorsal neurovascular bundle of the clitoris**. |
| **4. Labial wedge (Step 2)** | Full-thickness wedge resection of the protuberant labia minora; multilayer closure (deep and superficial), as in wedge labiaplasty, where single-layer closure was associated with dehiscence.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **5. Junction triangle (Step 3)** | Excise the marked triangle at the labia–hood junction; close to blend the two prior closures into a continuous smooth edge. |
| **6. Hemostasis and closure** | Bipolar electrocautery; selective ligation; layered absorbable closure throughout. |
| **7. Contralateral side** | Repeat with deliberate intraoperative comparison for symmetry. |

The two-part variant omits the junction triangle (step 5) and uses edge resection of the labia minora in place of the wedge (step 4), per Duan.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Comparison with other composite hoodoplasty techniques

| Technique | n | Satisfaction | Complications | Revision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Xia 2021 three-step**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 136 | **95.5%** | 4.4% | 3.7% |
| **Duan 2026 two-part**<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | 68 | **92.6%** | 7.4% | 1.5% |
| Liu 2022 classification-based (Zone C and L; 789 of 1,135 classified)<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | 789 | 95.7% (6 mo) | 4.3% | 1.9% |
| Shi 2026 modified wedge composite (mean follow-up 1.9 mo)<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | 738 | 96.2% overall | 4.7% | Not reported |
| Eserdağ 2021 inverted-Y (concurrent labiaplasty)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | 63 | 96.9% (2 mo) | No major | Not reported |
| Cao 2015 combined wedge-edge (mean follow-up 4.5 mo)<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | 49 | 100% | 4.1% | 2% |

The composite hoodoplasty literature consists of single-center series with similar reported outcomes (about 92–97% satisfaction, 4–7% complications, 1.5–3.7% revision where reported). None has been compared head-to-head prospectively. ACOG notes the lack of high-quality data supporting effectiveness of these procedures.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Critical safety considerations

Considerations across the composite excision techniques:

- **Preserve the dorsal neurovascular bundle of the clitoris** during the hood excision step.
- **Avoid over-resection of the hood.** Over-resection risks clitoral exposure with hypersensitivity or pain (operative teaching; not quantified in the composite series).
- **Plan the junction explicitly.** Xia's three-step design treats the labia–hood junction as the site of potential step deformity and addresses it with the junction triangle.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The two-part technique has no separate junction excision, so marking must be precise.
- **Multilayer wedge closure** at the labial-wedge step. In a retrospective review of central wedge labiaplasty (n = 77), single-layer closure was associated with dehiscence (P = 0.050).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Symmetry checks between sides.** Composite procedures compound the asymmetry risk of the individual components.
- **Smoking screening.** In a 40-patient retrospective comparison, the three early dehiscences all occurred in active smokers after wedge resection.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Limitations

- All published series are single-center retrospective Level IV evidence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Non-validated outcome instruments are used in most of this literature.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- No head-to-head prospective comparison of the three-step and two-part variants exists.
- Follow-up beyond 3–6 months is sparse.
- The three-step and two-part techniques were described for the conventional combined pattern. The fused variant (Xia 2022 Type 3) has its own 4-step excision.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Postoperative management

The regimen below is typical labiaplasty practice and is not taken from the cited series.

- **Activity restriction.** Avoid intercourse, tampon use, and strenuous exercise for 4–6 weeks, with extra emphasis on the early period given the wedge-component dehiscence risk.
- **Wound care.** Ice 48–72 h; sitz baths 24–48 h onward; topical antibiotic ointment; loose-fitting clothing.
- **Smoking cessation** preoperatively when feasible.
- **Follow-up.** 1 week (wound check, especially at the junction), 2 weeks, 6 weeks (clearance), 3–6 months (final outcome). Follow-up can be extended to 12 months locally.
- **PRO assessment.** FSFI and FGSIS (or GAS) at baseline and ≥ 6 months postoperatively.
