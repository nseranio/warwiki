# Three-Step & Two-Part Composite Excision (Lateral Hood + Labia Minora)

The three-step composite excision (Xia 2021, *Plast Reconstr Surg*) and the two-part composite excision (Duan 2026, *Aesthet Plast Surg*) are two related techniques that address the combined lateral clitoral-hood and labia minora hypertrophy pattern in a single sequenced operation. Both are operative responses to the same problem: isolated labiaplasty often leaves residual lateral hood redundancy that produces aesthetic disharmony, and isolated hoodoplasty often leaves residual labial hypertrophy. The authors of both series report satisfactory results from combining the two procedures in a defined sequence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> For positioning versus other female cosmetic options see [Cosmetic Genital Surgery: Female](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/female-cosmetic).

**Society positioning.** ACOG Committee Opinion No. 795 (2020) lists clitoral hood reduction among female genital cosmetic procedures. It states that procedures done to alter sexual appearance or function, excluding those for clinical indications, are not medically indicated, pose substantial risk, and have no established safety or effectiveness, and it advises assessment for body dysmorphic disorder when indicated.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> For genital surgery sought solely for appearance in women without structural or functional abnormalities, FIGO's 2025 statement calls it ethically inappropriate for obstetrician-gynecologists to recommend, perform or refer patients.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> The composite series are retrospective single-center reports.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Assess for body dysmorphic disorder when indicated early in the consultation. If psychological concerns are suspected, ACOG advises referral for evaluation before surgery.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Counsel about potential pain, bleeding, infection, scarring, adhesions, altered sensation, dyspareunia, and reoperation.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## When to use a composite approach

The Xia and Duan authors describe composite excision when both of the following findings are present; the findings alone do not establish a medical indication for surgery.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- **Lateral clitoral-hood redundancy** (Liu Zone L; Xia conventional combined pattern), and
- **Labia minora hypertrophy**, with attention to the desired free edge: Xia's wedge retains the native edge, whereas Duan's edge resection may leave a "pink" edge; Cao describes a separate wedge-edge method for patients seeking removal of a dark margin.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

Labiaplasty alone in this anatomy leaves the hood prominent, and hoodoplasty alone leaves the labial protrusion uncorrected. The composite techniques address both in one sequenced operation under a single anesthetic.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Central-zone hood hypertrophy (Liu Zone C) is treated differently; see the [Liu-classification bilateral triangular / inverted-V hoodoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/liu-classification-hoodoplasty).

For the fused lateral clitoral hood and labia minora variant (Xia 2022 CLC Type 3; 105 of 602 sides, 17.5%), Xia's group describes a dedicated 4-step excision. Among 52 of 67 Type 3 patients who returned the three-month questionnaire, 33/36 (91.7%) reported overall satisfaction after 4-step excision versus 9/16 (56.3%) after wedge excision alone (P = 0.01).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Xia 2021 three-step composite excision

The three-step technique removes three anatomical components in sequence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Step | Anatomic target | Excision |
|---|---|---|
| **Step 1** | **Prominent lateral clitoral hood skin** | Excised through a spindle-shaped incision along the fold parallel to the labia majora–clitoral hood sulcus<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Step 2** | **Protuberant labia minora** | Wedge resection of the central protruding portion |
| **Step 3** | **Junction between labia minora and clitoral hood** | Triangle of tissue trimmed to blend the two excisions into a continuous edge |

The distinguishing feature is Step 3, the triangle excised at the labia–hood junction. The authors describe it as blending the two prior excisions into one contour. The authors use this step to balance tissue removal from the inner and outer labial layers and avoid labial varus or valgus; its independent effect has not been tested.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Outcomes (Xia 2021, n = 136)

The cohort was aged 9–55 years. For patients younger than 18, ACOG advises education about normal anatomic variation and consideration of labiaplasty only for significant congenital malformation or persistent symptoms directly attributable to labial anatomy.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> ACOG also cautions that non-medically indicated labial surgery before age 18 violates U.S. federal criminal law; state restrictions may apply.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> At or after three months, 111/136 returned the satisfaction questionnaire; the discomfort and sex-life questions had 71 and 86 respondents, respectively. Complication and revision percentages use all 136 patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Endpoint | Result |
|---|---|
| Satisfaction at or after 3 months (91.0% within 14 days) | **95.5%** |
| Complications | **4.4%** |
| Return to surgery | **5/136 (3.7%): four hematoma evacuations and one operation for inadequate resection**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Preoperative discomfort resolved | 30 of 71 respondents at or after 3 months |
| Preoperative discomfort significantly relieved | 39 of 71 respondents at or after 3 months |
| Sexual life improved | **70.9%** |

## Duan 2026 two-part composite excision

The two-part technique addresses the same anatomy with two excisions instead of three. The excess clitoral hood is removed first, and the prominent labia minora is then reduced by edge resection, which leaves a "pink" edge.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

| Step | Anatomic target |
|---|---|
| **Step 1** | Lateral clitoral-hood reduction |
| **Step 2** | Labia minora edge resection |

The authors describe it as simple and suggest it is more suitable for inexperienced surgeons.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Outcomes (Duan 2026, n = 68; 2019–2024)

| Endpoint | Result |
|---|---|
| Genital Appearance Satisfaction (GAS) scale, postoperative vs preoperative | **Improved (p &lt; 0.05)** |
| Aesthetic satisfaction | **92.6%** |
| Complications | **7.4%**<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Revision | **1.5%**<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

## Three-step vs two-part: practical comparison

| Feature | Three-step (Xia 2021) | Two-part (Duan 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| **Number of excisions** | 3 (hood + labial wedge + junction triangle) | 2 (hood + labia) |
| **Junction handling** | Explicit triangular blending excision | No separate junction excision |
| **Cohort size** | 136 | 68 |
| **Satisfaction** | 95.5% | 92.6% |
| **Complications** | 4.4%<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 7.4%<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Return to surgery / revision** | 5/136 (3.7%): four hematoma evacuations, one inadequate-resection revision<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 1/68 (1.5%) underwent revision<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Patient-reported outcome** | 61/86 three-month respondents described improved sex life; no validated sexual-function measure<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Genital Appearance Satisfaction score improved (p &lt; 0.05); sexual function was not assessed in the abstract<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Labial excision** | Central wedge | Edge resection |
| **Reported advantage** | Explicit junction blending (authors' rationale) | Simpler design (authors' description) |

The cohorts differ in size, follow-up and satisfaction instrument, and the techniques have not been compared directly.

## Step-by-step technique (three-step variant)

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| **1. Marking** | Lithotomy. Mark the lateral clitoral hood excision parallel to the labia majora, the central labia minora wedge at the most protuberant portion, and the junction triangle so that its edges blend with the two prior excisions when closed. |
| **2. Anesthesia** | Local with epinephrine, ± sedation, or general. |
| **3. Hood excision (Step 1)** | Excise lateral hood redundancy parallel to the labia majora–hood sulcus in the superficial skin plane; the deeper labial wedge dissection is not carried into the hood. Preserve the dorsal clitoral neurovascular bundle.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **4. Labial wedge (Step 2)** | Xia's group usually performs a full-thickness inferior wedge, using a pinch test for tension-free closure. In the authors' experience, the superior flap length-to-base ratio should not exceed 3:1; they preserve subcutaneous and submucosal tissue in the pedicle to limit tip vascular compromise and dehiscence, then close the labial edge, submucosa, mucosa, and skin in layers.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Single-layer closure was associated with dehiscence in a separate retrospective wedge series.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **5. Junction triangle (Step 3)** | Excise the marked triangle at the labia–hood junction; close to blend the two prior closures into a continuous smooth edge. |
| **6. Hemostasis and closure** | Check hemostasis; bipolar cautery or selective ligation may be used as general operative options. Xia describes scalpel incisions, needle-point monopolar excision, and interrupted 5-0/6-0 Vicryl closure of the hood, labia, and junction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **7. Contralateral side** | Repeat with deliberate intraoperative comparison for symmetry. |

The two-part variant omits the junction triangle (step 5) and uses edge resection of the labia minora in place of the wedge (step 4), per Duan.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Comparison with other composite hoodoplasty techniques

| Technique | n | Satisfaction | Complications | Revision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Xia 2021 three-step**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 136 | **95.5% (106/111 respondents after 3 months)** | 4.4% | 3.7% return to surgery (four hematoma evacuations; one inadequate-resection revision) |
| **Duan 2026 two-part**<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | 68 | **92.6%** | 7.4% | 1.5% |
| Liu 2022 classification-based (Zone C and L; 789 of 1,135 classified)<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | 789 | 95.7% (6 mo) | 4.3% | 1.9% |
| Shi 2026 modified wedge composite (mean follow-up 1.9 mo)<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | 738 | 96.2% overall | 3.3% delayed wound healing; 1.4% perceived asymmetry | Not reported |
| Eserdağ 2021 inverted-Y (concurrent labiaplasty)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | 63 | 96.9% (2 mo) | No major | Not reported |
| Cao 2015 labia minora wedge-edge only (no hoodoplasty; contextual comparator; mean follow-up 4.5 mo)<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | 49 | 100% | 4.1% | 2% |

In the two named series, complications were 4.4% (Xia) and 7.4% (Duan), and Xia reported 95.5% satisfaction among respondents after 3 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Reported revision or return-to-surgery rates are 1.5–3.7%, but Xia's 3.7% includes four hematoma evacuations.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Cao's labia-only series is a contextual comparator. None of the three-step and two-part operations has been compared head-to-head prospectively. Across broader labiaplasty literature, Géczi et al. pooled wedge-resection dehiscence at 8% (95% CI 5–13%) and composite-reduction swelling at 13% (95% CI 2–54%); these groups do not directly compare the named operations.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> ACOG notes the lack of high-quality evidence for the effectiveness of elective genital cosmetic surgery.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Critical safety considerations

Considerations across the composite excision techniques:

- **Preserve the dorsal neurovascular bundle of the clitoris** during the hood excision step.
- **Avoid over-resection of the hood.** Xia's group keeps the upper incision border at or below the clitoris–mons transition, excises superficially, and balances bilateral closure tension to prevent glans deviation. The authors warn that overexcision can impair blood supply, innervation, and lymphatic return.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Over-resection also risks clitoral exposure with hypersensitivity or pain (operative teaching; not quantified in the composite series).
- **Plan the junction explicitly.** Xia's junction triangle balances excision from the inner and outer labial layers to avoid labial varus or valgus; this is the authors' technical rationale.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The two-part technique has no separate junction excision, so mark the hood–labial junction precisely. For edge reduction, Pan et al. advise avoiding traction or a straight cut of the free edge, which can cause scalloping and over-resection, and protecting the clitoral frenulum from a right-angle cut.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Multilayer wedge closure** at the labial-wedge step. In a retrospective review of central wedge (45 patients) and extended central wedge labiaplasty (32 patients), single-layer closure was associated with dehiscence (P = 0.050).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Symmetry checks between sides.** Composite procedures compound the asymmetry risk of the individual components.
- **Smoking screening.** In a 40-patient retrospective comparison, the three early dehiscences all occurred in active smokers after wedge resection.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Limitations

- All published series are single-center retrospective Level IV evidence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Non-validated outcome instruments are used in most of this literature.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- No head-to-head prospective comparison of the three-step and two-part variants exists.
- Xia 2021 reported median follow-up of 10 months (range 1–38) in 136 patients, while its 95.5% satisfaction estimate came from 106/111 respondents at or after three months. Xia 2022 reported median follow-up of 11 months (range 1–41) in 57/67 Type 3 patients, while its 4-step versus wedge satisfaction comparison used 52/67 three-month questionnaire respondents.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- The three-step and two-part techniques were described for the conventional combined pattern. The fused variant (Xia 2022 Type 3) has its own 4-step excision.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Postoperative management

The regimen below is typical labiaplasty practice and is not taken from the cited series.

- **Activity restriction.** Avoid intercourse, tampon use, and strenuous exercise for 4–6 weeks, with extra emphasis on the early period given the wedge-component dehiscence risk.
- **Wound care.** Ice 48–72 h; sitz baths 24–48 h onward; topical antibiotic ointment; loose-fitting clothing.
- **Smoking cessation** preoperatively when feasible.
- **Follow-up.** Xia's group examined patients on postoperative day one because hematomas commonly occur within 24 hours; four of 136 patients required hematoma evacuation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Sudden unilateral pain with rapidly expanding swelling warrants urgent assessment for hematoma.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> Recheck at one week (wound check, especially at the junction), two weeks, six weeks (activity clearance), and three to six months (final outcome); follow-up can be extended to 12 months locally.
- **PRO assessment.** FSFI and FGSIS (or GAS) at baseline and ≥ 6 months postoperatively.
