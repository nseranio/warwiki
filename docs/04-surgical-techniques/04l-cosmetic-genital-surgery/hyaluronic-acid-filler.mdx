# Hyaluronic Acid Filler — Penile Girth Augmentation

**Hyaluronic acid (HA) filler injection** is a minimally invasive office-based procedure for penile girth enhancement. It is the most commonly described injectable filler for this indication, and a narrative review reports that it is better tolerated than other augmentation methods.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Its main advantage is reversibility with hyaluronidase. No standardized clinical guidelines exist, and penile use is off-label. For positioning versus other male cosmetic options see [Cosmetic Genital Surgery — Male](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/male-cosmetic).

**Society positioning.** The SMSNA 2024 position statement (expert opinion) states that limited data suggest a cosmetic benefit of temporary HA and PLA fillers for girth, with an acceptable safety profile, and that injection-site adverse events occurred in 5% to 14% of men. It recommends strongly against permanent fillers such as paraffin and silicone, and it requires that penile dysmorphic disorder be ruled out before any invasive enhancement.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> Reviews describe the evidence as low quality and the procedures as investigational.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> HA fillers are FDA-approved for facial soft-tissue augmentation; penile use is off-label.

---

## Patient population and indications

The primary indication is **small penis syndrome (SPS) / penile dysmorphophobia (PDD)**, a form of body-image or anxiety disorder in which men with objectively normal penile dimensions are dissatisfied with their size. It is distinct from true micropenis (stretched penile length &lt; 2.5 SD below mean).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Most men seeking penile augmentation have penile dimensions within the normal range.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> See [Small Penis Syndrome / PDD](/docs/clinical-conditions/03g-genital-scrotal/small-penis-syndrome) for the full clinical context.

Pre-treatment considerations:

- **Psychosexual counseling first.** Many patients can be reassured without treatment.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Psychosocial improvement does not correlate with penile-size change; the psychological benefit appears partly independent of the physical outcome.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Patients should understand the temporary nature of HA fillers and the need for repeat treatments.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

---

## Mechanism and material properties

HA is a naturally occurring glycosaminoglycan in the extracellular matrix. When injected as a cross-linked gel, it produces volume augmentation through hydrophilic expansion; each molecule binds up to 1,000× its weight in water. Advantages over other injectable materials<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>:

- **Biocompatibility.** Naturally occurring substance with minimal immunogenicity.
- **Reversibility.** Dissolved with hyaluronidase if complications or undesired results.
- **Predictable degradation.** Gradually resorbed over months, allowing controlled retreatment.

---

## Injection technique

No universally standardized protocol exists. Common elements<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[1]](#ref1)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>:

- **Products:** Restylane Sub-Q (Q-med), Macrolane VRF 30, and various high-viscosity, large-particle HA gels for deep-tissue augmentation.
- **Volume:** mean 20–40 mL across studies.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Plane:** dartos / subcutaneous fascia of the penile shaft, between the skin and Buck's fascia, avoiding the neurovascular bundle and corpora cavernosa.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Anesthesia:** local penile block or topical anesthetic; ambulatory / office setting.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

### Described techniques

| Technique | Approach | Key feature |
|---|---|---|
| **"Back & Forth"** (Kwak 2011) | 21 G cannula, fascial layer; manual roller homogenization | Uniform shaft distribution<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Emicircumferential** (Sito 2013) | Semicircular pattern (ventral or dorsal) rather than circumferential | Intended to reduce vascular-compromise risk; in a retrospective chart review, no complications in the HA arm vs 8 granulomas and 1 fat necrosis with lipofilling<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **Multi-point injection** (Boiko 2023) | Multiple shaft points + manual molding | Uniform distribution; large-cohort outcome data<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |

### Key efficacy patterns

- Girth gain is greatest at 1 month and attenuates over 6–18 months, which is attributed to HA resorption. It remains statistically significant at all measured time points.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[9]](#ref9)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- Flaccid girth gains exceed erect girth gains; at 12 months, erect girth gain was only +0.80 cm versus +2.44 cm flaccid.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Flaccid length increases modestly (+1.65–2.55 cm in Zhang 2022), possibly from added volume pushing penile skin forward. Length is not a primary effect.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Erectile function.** IIEF rose by 7.6 (p &lt; 0.01) at 1 month in the uncontrolled Zhang 2022 series.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Psychological benefits (improved genital self-image, reduced inadequacy beliefs, improved confidence) remained steady in follow-up even as physical gains attenuated.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## HA vs polylactic acid (PLA) fillers

The highest-quality comparative data come from a series of multicenter RCTs by Yang et al.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[9]](#ref9)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>:

- At 4 weeks, HA produced significantly greater girth than PLA (p &lt; 0.001 in the 2019 trial). At 24 weeks in the 2020 trial the difference was 0.5 cm (p = 0.031).
- At 48 weeks the HA-PLA difference was no longer significant (p = 0.075). At 18 months, both groups maintained significant gains and the change in girth did not differ between groups.
- HA has a faster onset and is reversible with hyaluronidase.
- PLA is a slower-onset, collagen-stimulating filler that hyaluronidase does not dissolve. The trials did not show a durability difference through 18 months.

## HA vs other penile augmentation methods

| Method | Girth gain | Duration | Reversible | Complications | Key limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **HA filler** | +1.5–3.9 cm | 6–18 mo (temporary) | Yes (hyaluronidase) | 4.3–9.1% (mild, in the series above) | Repeat treatments; attenuation |
| **Lipofilling** (autologous fat) | +3.2–4.5 cm | unpredictable resorption | No | Granuloma, fat necrosis (8 granulomas and 1 fat necrosis in Sito 2013) | Unpredictable resorption; donor-site morbidity |
| **Surgical grafts** (dermal / AlloDerm) | significant | Permanent | No | Skin necrosis, infection, reoperation (53.3% of studies) | Negative impact on penile length; higher morbidity |
| **Silicone implants** (Penuma) | significant | Permanent | Surgical removal | Infection, erosion, seroma, necrosis; removal up to 10% | Risk of severe deformity |

A narrative review concluded that HA filler injections are better tolerated than other penile-augmentation methods, although follow-up periods are short. In retrospective series, surgical grafts improved girth and satisfaction but reduced penile length. Silicone implants carried risks of infection, erosion, seroma and necrosis, with removal in up to 10%.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Safety and complications

Series and randomized trials of HA penile augmentation report low complication rates.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[3]](#ref3)[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- The overall complication rate was 4.3% in the largest series cited here (Quan 2021, n = 230) over 6 months. Injection-related adverse events were 5.1% (2/39) in the HA arm of Yang 2020 and 9.1% (3/33) in the HA arm of the 18-month trial.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

**Reported complications**<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>:

- **Subcutaneous bleeding or bruising.** Most common; self-limiting.
- **Subcutaneous nodules.** Palpable lumps from uneven distribution or focal HA accumulation.
- **Penile edema.** Transient; more common with redundant prepuce.
- **Gel migration.** Displacement from injection site; associated with redundant prepuce.
- **Infection.** Rare; managed with antibiotics.
- **Contour irregularities or asymmetry.** Corrected with hyaluronidase or additional filler.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- No systemic or local allergic reactions occurred in the Quan 2021 series.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- No serious adverse events were reported in the randomized HA-versus-PLA trials.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[9]](#ref9)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

**Distinguish HA from unapproved oil-based injectables.** HA fillers must be differentiated from unapproved oil-based substances (silicone oil, paraffin, mineral oil, Vaseline) injected in non-medical settings; see [Non-Autologous Injectables](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/non-autologous-injectables) for the full per-substance breakdown. These cause sclerosing lipogranuloma ("paraffinoma" or "siliconoma") requiring complex tissue excision and skin transplantation. Patients with prior history of these injections are not candidates for HA filler over the same area.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Reversibility with hyaluronidase

Reversibility is the main advantage of HA over other injectable materials.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

- **Moon 2025 *Int J Impot Res* case series.** In four patients with residual HA after penile girth enhancement, hyaluronidase was used to correct asymmetries, manage adverse effects and prepare the site for reinjection.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- Hyaluronidase is FDA-approved as an adjuvant in subcutaneous fluid administration, to increase dispersion and absorption of other injected drugs, and in subcutaneous urography. Filler dissolution is off-label.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Alam 2018 *JAMA Dermatol* RCT.** In 9 women with filler nodules in the inner arm, hyaluronidase resolved nodules better than saline, higher doses (9 U vs 1.5 U per 0.1 mL) resolved them faster, and the dose effect was more notable for Restylane-L.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- Dermatologic reviews describe hyaluronidase as generally safe; hypersensitivity to the enzyme itself is possible.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

## Durability and retreatment

HA filler augmentation is inherently temporary, both a limitation and a safety feature.

- Peak girth gains at 1 month.
- Gradual attenuation occurs over 6–18 months, attributed to enzymatic degradation and mechanical forces.
- At 12 months, flaccid girth gains retain approximately 70–75% of 1-month peak (e.g., +3.41 to +2.44 cm).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- At 18 months, significant augmentation persists in RCT data (p &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- One Restylane Sub-Q series reported maintenance at 18 months with minimal attenuation (from 11.41 to 11.26 cm).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Repeat treatments** are necessary to maintain results, with cumulative cost.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- Variability in durability reflects HA-product cross-linking density, injection volume, plane, and individual patient metabolism.

---

## Correction of secondary deformities

HA fillers have also been used to correct secondary contour deformities of the penis after prior augmentation procedures (e.g., after excision of non-absorbable substances). In the Yordanov 2021 retrospective series of 5 patients (mean 3.32 mL HA), the filler corrected residual irregularities, including in fibrosis from prior surgeries. Satisfaction was high with no complications at 9 months.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

---

## Limitations of current evidence

- **No standardized injection protocol.** Volumes, products, techniques, and planes vary across studies.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Overall low quality of evidence.** The vast majority of studies (89.7%) are not RCTs.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Short follow-up periods.** Most studies report 6–18 mo; very long-term data lacking.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **High dropout rates.** Up to 43.1% in some RCTs.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Publication bias.** Adverse events likely under-reported.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Lack of standardized outcome measures.** Different studies use different satisfaction and girth-measurement instruments.
- No head-to-head RCTs versus surgical girth enhancement.

---

## Where HA filler fits

- Temporary HA filler is the injectable with the most trial data for cosmetic girth enhancement in men with normal penile dimensions and persistent dissatisfaction (PDD / SPS). It is offered after psychosexual counseling and reassurance.
- HA is reversible with hyaluronidase, and reported complication rates are low.
- Repeat treatments are required to maintain results; counsel patients on cumulative cost and the temporal pattern of attenuation.
- HA is not a length-enhancement intervention; for length see [Penile Traction Therapy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/penile-traction-therapy).
- **Surgical alternatives** (Penuma silicone sleeve, dermal grafts) are permanent. Complication rates in the reviews are higher, and retrospective graft series report reduced penile length.
- A multidisciplinary approach is advised: psychological assessment, realistic expectations, and informed consent about the temporary effect and complications.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
