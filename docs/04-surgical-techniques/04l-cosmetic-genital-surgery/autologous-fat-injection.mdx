# Autologous Fat Injection (AFI) — Penile Girth Enhancement

**Autologous fat injection (AFI)** for penile girth enhancement is one of the older penile-augmentation techniques, popularized in the early 1990s. Fat is harvested by liposuction from a donor site and injected subcutaneously around the penile shaft. The fat is autologous, but resorption is unpredictable (20–80% of injected volume in fat-grafting series), and referral-center reports describe irregular nodules and penile deformity. These problems have made it one of the more controversial techniques in genital cosmetic surgery.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

For the broader male cosmetic-genital-surgery decision framework see the [Male Cosmetic Genital Surgery atlas page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/male-cosmetic). For the related dermal-fat-graft variant and ADM wraps see [Dermal Fat Grafts & AlloDerm Wraps](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/dermal-fat-grafts-alloderm-wraps). For the reversible alternative see [Hyaluronic Acid Filler](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/hyaluronic-acid-filler).

## I. Definition and Concept

Autologous fat injection (autologous fat transfer, lipofilling, fat grafting) for penile girth enhancement involves three sequential steps.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

1. **Harvesting.** Fat aspirated from a donor site (abdomen, flanks, thighs) via liposuction.
2. **Processing.** Lipoaspirate refined (centrifugation, decanting, or filtration) to separate viable adipocytes from blood, oil, and debris.
3. **Injection.** Purified fat injected into the subcutaneous tissue of the penile shaft (between skin / dartos fascia and Buck's fascia) to increase circumference.

Autologous fat is biocompatible, readily available and natural-feeling. The penis is a challenging recipient site because of its mobile, thin-skinned, cylindrical anatomy with a limited vascular bed for graft revascularization, and the mechanical stress of erection / detumescence cycles.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## II. Historical Context

- **Early 1990s.** Penile fat injection popularized alongside SLD as a combined "penile enlargement" procedure, heavily marketed.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **1994.** Trockman and colleagues reported a case of complications (two painful inflammatory nodules requiring excision).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **1996.** Wessells, Lue and McAninch reported 12 men who presented to one referral center over 16 months with complications of penile augmentation performed elsewhere. All 12 had suspensory ligament release and 10 had also received autologous fat injection. Irregular residual fat nodules were the chief complaint in 7, skin deformity and scarring in 4, and scrotalization in 4. Reoperation was needed in 6, wound complications occurred in 6 and sexual dysfunction was reported by 4. The authors concluded that the techniques "should be regarded as experimental."<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **1997.** Alter reported reconstruction of deformities after penile enlargement surgery performed elsewhere. He listed "disappearance of fat, penile lumps and nodules, and shaft deformities" as the complications of fat injection.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **2000s–present.** Refined techniques (multi-layer injection, smaller volumes, Coleman principles) have been reported with fewer complications in small series, but unpredictable fat resorption persists.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## III. Surgical Technique

### A. Fat harvesting

**Donor sites.**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- **Abdomen.** Commonly used; convenient; large volume.
- **Flanks / love handles.** Good fat quality.
- **Inner thighs.** When abdominal fat is insufficient.
- **Buttocks.** Less commonly used.

**Harvesting method.**<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

- **Suction-assisted liposuction (SAL).** Standard; tumescent solution (lidocaine and epinephrine in saline) infiltrated, then aspirated through a blunt-tipped cannula.
- **Syringe aspiration (Coleman technique).** Manual aspiration via 10 mL syringes attached to a blunt cannula; gentler on adipocytes.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Cannula size.** Larger harvesting cannulas (≥ 3 mm) limit shearing and cell damage.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Low negative pressure.** Excessive suction damages adipocytes, and gentle controlled aspiration is preferred.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### B. Fat processing<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

1. **Centrifugation (Coleman).** 1,200g × 3 min; three layers (oil top / concentrated fat middle / blood-fluid bottom). The middle layer is used for injection. This is the most widely standardized method.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
2. **Decanting / gravity sedimentation.** Simpler but less consistent.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
3. **Cotton-pad filtration.** May preserve adipocyte structure better than centrifugation.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
4. **Soft centrifugation.** 400g × 1 min; compromise between concentration and viability.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

**Principle.** In He 2022 (human lipoaspirate; in vitro and in vivo comparison of three processing methods), Coleman centrifugation caused more cell-structure destruction and viability loss than cotton-pad filtration or soft centrifugation, and volume retention rates were similar in the three groups.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> The authors argue that protecting adipose-tissue structure and viability matters for graft quality.

### C. Injection technique (penile-specific)

**Kang 2012 (n = 52).** This series gives the most detailed published technique for penile fat injection. Fat was injected into the superficial, middle and deep layers of Colles' fascia; the remaining details below are from the full paper.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- **Injection plane.** Colles' fascia (dartos fascia), the subcutaneous layer between penile skin and Buck's fascia.
- **Multi-layer injection.** Superficial, middle, and deep layers of Colles' fascia for even distribution.
- **Entry points.** 2–4 small stab incisions at the penile base or along the shaft.
- **Injection volume.** Mean 38.54 mL (range 25–49 mL).
- **Technique.** Small aliquots in a retrograde, fanning pattern with a blunt-tipped cannula; penis held in traction.
- **Circumferential distribution.** Even around the entire circumference to avoid asymmetry.
- **Operative time.** Mean 44 minutes (range 37–49 min).

**Technical principles** (general fat-grafting literature).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- **Small aliquots** (0.1–0.5 mL per pass) maximize surface-area contact with recipient tissue for revascularization.
- **Avoid pooling.** Large bolus deposits undergo central necrosis from inadequate diffusion.
- **Slow injection speed** limits shearing.
- **Blunt-tipped cannulas** lower the risk of intravascular injection.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Avoid injection into Buck's fascia or tunica albuginea,** to avoid the risk of ED, Peyronie's-like fibrosis or corporal injury.

## V. The Central Problem — Unpredictable Fat Resorption

The fundamental limitation of AFI, in the penis and at all body sites, is unpredictable and often substantial graft resorption.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[7]](#ref7)[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

### General fat-grafting resorption data

- Reported resorption is 20–80% of injected volume, depending on technique, recipient site, and patient factors.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- Pooled facial-fat-graft retention 47% (95% CI 41–53%) at 3–24 months (meta-analysis, 27 studies, 1,011 patients).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Conventional fat-grafting survival approximately 44% (versus approximately 64% with cell-assisted lipotransfer).<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

### Penile-specific resorption concerns

- The penis is a uniquely hostile recipient site because of:
  - Thin subcutaneous tissue with limited vascular bed.
  - Constant mechanical stress from erection / detumescence cycles.
  - Mobile anatomy: significant dimensional changes with erection.
  - Gravity-dependent position: fat may migrate or redistribute unevenly.
- Salem 2019 noted fat loss after 6 months in their penile fat-injection series.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- Alter 1997 listed "disappearance of fat" among the three complications of penile fat injection.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Non-uniform resorption, with some areas resorbing more than others, is the presumed basis of the irregular nodularity and asymmetry described in referral series.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## VI. Complications

### A. Irregular fat nodules and lumps — the hallmark complication<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

- **Wessells 1996.** Irregular residual fat nodules were the chief complaint in 7 of 12 men (10 of the 12 had received fat injection).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Alter 1997.** Described "penile lumps and nodules" as a primary indication for reconstructive surgery.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Trockman 1994.** Case report of a 36-year-old man with 2 painful inflammatory nodules requiring surgical excision 3 months after injection.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Kang 2012.** Nodular fat occurred in 1/52 (1.92%) at 6 months. The lower rate may reflect multi-layer injection technique or patient selection.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Mechanism.** Non-uniform fat resorption leaves islands of surviving fat interspersed with areas of complete resorption, creating palpable and visible irregularities. Large bolus deposits undergo central necrosis, forming oil cysts and calcified nodules.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### B. Penile deformity and poor cosmetic appearance

- Wessells: poor cosmetic appearance was the chief complaint in the 12 referral patients.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Skin deformity and scarring occurred in 4/12.
- **Scrotalization** occurred in 4/12. All 12 had suspensory ligament release, so it cannot be attributed to fat injection alone.
- Shaft deformities: asymmetric thickening, irregular contour, "sausage-like" or "lumpy" appearance.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### C. Fat disappearance / resorption

- Complete or near-complete resorption can leave the patient with no girth gain and possibly worse cosmesis than baseline (from scarring and fibrosis).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### D. Sexual dysfunction

- Wessells: 4/12 (33%) patients reported sexual dysfunction.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Proposed mechanisms include pain from nodules during intercourse, altered penile sensation, psychological distress from deformity, and rarely ED from deep injection injury.

### E. Wound complications

- Wessells: 6/12 (50%) wound complications.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Infection, hematoma, seroma at injection sites or donor sites.

### F. Need for reoperation

- Wessells: 6/12 (50%) required reoperation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Deskoulidi: 3/75 (4%) returned for revision of fat injections 6–12 months after surgery.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- Alter: 19 patients underwent 24 reconstructive operations for deformities from penile-enlargement surgery.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### G. Fat necrosis and oil cysts

- Large deposits of injected fat that fail to revascularize undergo liquefactive necrosis, forming **oil cysts** (sterile collections of liquefied fat).
- May calcify over time into palpable hard nodules.
- Can be confused with malignancy on imaging.

### H. Infection

- Rare but reported; superficial cellulitis to deep abscess. Risk increased with non-sterile technique or excessive tissue trauma.

## VII. Reconstruction of Fat-Injection Complications

**Alter 1997** described the repairs in 19 men (24 operations).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- **Fat-nodule excision** through small incisions.
- **Asymmetrical fat-deposit removal** (debulking).
- **Scar revision** for injection-site scars.
- **Limitations.** Complete correction not always possible; some residual deformity may persist.
- Penile appearance and function were reported improved; complications were 1 hematoma requiring drainage, minor wound complications and 1 inadequately reversed V-Y flap.

**Furr 2018** referral-center series: 11 patients with severe complications from genital-enlargement surgery (including AFI), with adverse changes including "sexually disabling penile deformity and severe shortening, curvature, edema, subcutaneous masses, infection, non-healing wounds, and sexual dysfunction." 10/11 underwent corrective surgery, 3 with split-thickness skin grafting.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

For the operative atlas of these reconstructions see [Penile Skin / Shaft Reconstruction (04e)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/penile-skin-reconstruction).

## VIII. Strategies to Improve Fat-Graft Survival

Several enrichment strategies have been investigated, but none has been studied specifically for penile fat grafting.

### A. Platelet-rich plasma (PRP)

- Wu 2021 meta-analysis (11 studies, 1,125 patients): PRP and fat grafting showed significantly higher fat-survival rate and lower recovery time versus conventional fat grafting; no difference in patient satisfaction.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- Fat survival 20.5–54.8% (FG alone) versus 24.1–89.2% (PRP and FG).<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- A 2025 systematic review (27 studies, 15 of them animal studies) judged SVF / ADSC-based methods and PRP the "most promising approaches" for fat-graft enrichment, with substantial heterogeneity in protocols and outcome measures.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

### B. Stromal vascular fraction (SVF)

- Jefri 2026 meta-analysis (18 studies, 893 patients): SVF-enriched fat grafting demonstrated significantly higher fat retention (mean difference +17.20%, 95% CI 11.26–23.15, p &lt; 0.001; I² = 98%).<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- Automated enzymatic SVF preparation achieved the highest retention gains (+22.8%).<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- No major complications were reported, and cyst / fat-necrosis rates were comparable (RR 1.15, 95% CI 0.57–2.33).<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

### C. Cell-assisted lipotransfer (CAL) / adipose-derived stem cells (ADSCs)

- Laloze 2018 meta-analysis (25 studies, 696 patients): CAL showed significantly higher fat survival than conventional grafting (64% versus 44%, p &lt; 0.0001).<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- The benefit was significant only for injection volumes &lt; 100 mL.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- CAL was associated with more complications (8.4% versus 1.5%, p = 0.0019) and did not reduce the number of procedures needed.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

### D. Technical optimization (Nemir 2021)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- Larger harvesting and grafting cannulas: reduce shearing.
- Slow injection speeds: minimize cell damage.
- Dispersion technique: small aliquots distributed throughout tissue, avoiding pooling.
- Minimize environmental exposure during processing.
- Blunt-tipped cannulas: avoid intravascular injection.
- Gentle processing (soft centrifugation or filtration) may preserve adipocyte structure better than standard Coleman centrifugation.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

None of these enrichment strategies has been evaluated in penile fat grafting. The biomechanical environment of the penis (erection / detumescence cycles, thin subcutaneous tissue) may limit the applicability of data from facial and breast fat grafting.

## X. Special Application — Fat Injection With Penile Prosthesis ("Girth Supersizing")

Salem 2019 described autologous fat injection in patients with semi-rigid penile implants for "girth supersizing" (prospective case-control study, Egypt).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- n = 30 married participants: 15 had semi-rigid implant insertion and 15 controls; all received autologous fat injections.
- Median penile girth increased significantly in both groups.
- Fat loss was noted after 6 months; the abstract does not report it by group.
- In the controls, the volume injected correlated positively with the change in girth at 6 months.
- The authors concluded that fat transfer is "simple and safe" for augmenting girth in patients whose girth is less than 11.5 cm after prosthesis insertion.

## XI. Patient Selection and Contraindications

**Appropriate candidates** (based on published series).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

- Men with objectively thin penises (Kang selected patients with circumference ≤ 7.4 cm).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Men with penile dysmorphophobia who have undergone psychological screening and have realistic expectations.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- Adequate donor-site fat for harvesting.
- **Psychosomatically normal individuals** (Spyropoulos excluded patients with psychiatric disorders).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

**Relative contraindications.**

- Unrealistic expectations: patients expecting large, permanent girth increase.
- **Body dysmorphic disorder (BDD).** A multidisciplinary approach including psychological evaluation is advised for men requesting augmentation.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- Insufficient donor-site fat: very lean patients.
- Active penile infection or skin disease.
- Coagulopathy or anticoagulant use (relative, for liposuction component).
- Prior penile surgery with significant scarring (may limit injection plane).

**Psychological screening.** Multiple authors emphasize that "most men seeking these interventions have penile dimensions within the normal range" and recommend a multidisciplinary approach including psychological evaluation.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[19]](#ref19)</sup> Spyropoulos used a structured questionnaire to select only 11 of 28 presenting patients (39%) for surgery.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## XII. Evidence Quality and Limitations

The evidence base for penile AFI is limited and of low quality.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[22]](#ref22)[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

- **No randomized controlled trials** for penile fat injection.
- The largest prospective series (Kang n = 52) had only 6 months of follow-up, insufficient to capture long-term resorption.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Most informative data come from referral-center complication series (Wessells, Alter, Furr), which inherently overrepresent complications but provide safety data.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- In a narrative review of 29 penile-girth-enhancement studies, 26 (89.7%) were not randomized controlled trials, with "overall low quality and limited level of evidence."<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- Romero-Otero 2021 *BJU Int* SR: "the quality of studies on penile-enhancement procedures is still low" and "prevents us from establishing recommendations based on scientific evidence."<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- Vyas 2020 SR: "poor" study quality regarding methodology for patient selection and outcomes reporting; pooled complication rate 14.6% across all augmentation techniques, highest with combined augmentation.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
- Adverse events are probably largely under-reported across all penile-augmentation studies.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

## XIII. Clinical Summary

Autologous fat injection for penile girth enhancement is an older technique with unpredictable results. The fat is autologous, and the donor site is contoured at the same time. Fat resorption is reported at 20–80%, and referral-center series document irregular nodularity, penile deformity and reoperation. In the Wessells series, 7 of 12 men had irregular nodules and 6 needed reoperation. Referral series over-represent complications, and denominators for the procedure are unknown.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

A more recent series using multi-layer injection (Kang, n = 52, single center, 6-month follow-up, no comparator) reported a 1.92% nodule rate and a +2.28 cm girth gain at 6 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Deskoulidi and Caminer (n = 75, 15 years of experience) reported a 4% revision rate for fat injection as part of combined augmentation.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> These results may reflect technique or patient selection; no comparative data exist.

The main unresolved question is long-term durability: most series report only 6-month outcomes, and fat resorption may continue beyond this timeframe.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> Meta-analyses of enrichment strategies (PRP, SVF, cell-assisted lipotransfer) report higher fat-graft survival at other body sites, but these strategies have not been studied in the penis.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

Compared to hyaluronic acid fillers, fat injection offers potential permanence at the cost of greater invasiveness, unpredictability, and higher complication rates. HA fillers provide more predictable, uniform results with the safety net of reversibility (hyaluronidase), though they require repeat injections every 12–24 months.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)[[21]](#ref21)</sup> The choice between techniques depends on patient anatomy, expectations, and tolerance for the respective risk profiles.
