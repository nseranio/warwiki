# Scrotal Botulinum Toxin A Injection ("Scrotox")

**Scrotal BoNT/A injection** ("Scrotox") is an off-label aesthetic use of onabotulinumtoxinA intended to smooth and relax the scrotal envelope. In the published one-patient aesthetic case, toxin was injected into both cremaster and dartos; this does not validate a dartos-only technique.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> OnabotulinumtoxinA has no FDA-approved scrotal indication.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> Surgical reduction scrotoplasty removes skin to tighten the envelope. In the single aesthetic case, cremaster injection lowered the scrotum while dartos injection targeted wrinkles.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> The two procedures serve opposite aesthetic preferences and are not interchangeable.

For the broader male cosmetic decision framework see the [Male Cosmetic Genital Surgery atlas page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/male-cosmetic); for the surgical alternative see [Reduction Scrotoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/reduction-scrotoplasty).

## Mechanism of Action

**General BoNT/A.** Heavy-chain binding leads to endocytosis, light-chain cleavage of SNAP-25, impaired SNARE-mediated synaptic vesicle fusion, blocked acetylcholine release and chemical denervation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Beyond skeletal muscle, BoNT/A blocks efferent autonomic fibers to smooth muscle and exocrine glands.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

**Scrotal dartos pharmacology (Gibson 2002, isolated rat dartos).**<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- Receives a functional sympathetic (α-1 adrenergic) innervation; contracts to noradrenaline.
- **No functional inhibitory innervation.** Relaxation is passive.
- Profoundly thermosensitive: contractile response at 30 °C is reduced by about 80% at 40 °C, mediated by myosin phosphatase.
- Does not respond to carbachol, nicotine, histamine, serotonin, or substance P.

**Implication.** The rat dartos is noradrenergic. BoNT/A may act indirectly in such tissue, through preganglionic sympathetic acetylcholine blockade or effects on sensory or autonomic terminals, as proposed for detrusor and sphincter applications.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The dartos mechanism of BoNT/A has not been specifically elucidated, and human dartos pharmacology has not been characterized in the sources cited here.

## Clinical Applications

### 1. Aesthetic scrotal relaxation ("Scrotox") — the primary use

The Mehdizadeh 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis (38 studies, 1,903 patients) lists scrotal relaxation among nonfacial off-label aesthetic BoNT/A goals. Overall satisfaction across all off-label aesthetic applications was 94% (95% CI 88–98%); across 23 studies with quantitative first-follow-up measurements, the review reported 75.0% sustained results. Most patients were women (94.3%) and most treatments targeted the lower face; the figure is not a scrotal-specific outcome.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Stated aesthetic goals:

- Smoother scrotum (reduction of rugae).
- Lower-hanging scrotum after cremaster relaxation in the single combined cremaster-and-dartos case.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- Reduced cold / stress retraction.
- Perceived increase in scrotal size.

### 2. Penile retraction (penile dartos) — proof of concept (Shaeer 2009)<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- 10 men with short flaccid penis but normal erect and outstretched length.
- 100 U onabotulinumtoxinA into the penile dartos in this 10-man study.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- 70% subjectively reported decreased frequency and amplitude of retraction with improved flaccid length; clinical measurement showed less retraction rather than added length.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Effect completely faded by month 6; no side effects.

### 3. Chronic scrotal pain — spermatic cord block (not dartos)

- **Khambati 2014** (open-label pilot, n = 18, 100 U): 72% of patients reported pain reduction at 1 mo (mean VAS 7.36 to 5.61, p &lt; 0.003). At 3 months, VAS pain and examination tenderness each improved in 10/18 (56%); the report does not identify whether these were the same men.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> One man underwent orchiectomy before month 3; before month 6, four more received repeat toxin and five underwent other surgery, leaving eight without an additional intervention for the six-month assessment.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup><sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Dockray 2020** double-blind RCT (n = 64, 200 IU plus local anesthesia versus local anesthesia alone): no statistically significant difference in any measured outcome. In the optional open-label extension, 9 of 13 control men who received toxin improved.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

In the randomized trial, BoNT/A cord block was not superior to local anesthesia alone for chronic scrotal pain; AUA 2025's ungraded discussion reaches the same conclusion.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

### 4. Cremasteric synkinesia / spasm (Mori 2011)<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

In a single reported case, a 26-year-old man with refractory bilateral cremasteric spasm received three staged injections (two left, one right), each 100 U in 10 mL saline, 6 weeks apart. Pain fell from 8/10 to 3/10 after the first injection, with peak effect at 2 wk and dissipation over 4–6 wk.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Baseline pain was 2–4/10 after the three injections.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Injection Technique (Aesthetic)

**Dose.** No validated aesthetic scrotal dose or dilution is established. The cited 100 U study injected penile dartos, not scrotal dartos, and cannot establish a scrotal regimen.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

**Injection points.**

- The dartos lies immediately beneath thin scrotal skin; the single aesthetic case injected proximal cremaster and punctate dartos muscle, without testing a dartos-only subdermal plane.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- The single published aesthetic case used proximal cremaster injections and dartos injections into prominent furrows; it did not define a 4–8-site-per-side anterior/lateral map.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- The single aesthetic case placed dartos injections into prominent furrows.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- Topical anesthetic or ice is a comfort option for superficial injections, but the single published combined cremaster-and-dartos case used bilateral lidocaine spermatic-cord blocks; topical or ice alone was not evaluated.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

**Onset and duration.**

| Phase | Timing |
|---|---|
| Onset | Exact onset unmeasured; improvement was documented at day 10 in the single aesthetic case<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| Peak effect | Not established for aesthetic scrotal injection; the case documented results at day 10 and month 3<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| Effective relaxation | Scrotal-specific duration not established; penile-dartos effect faded by 6 months<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Complete fade | Not measured for scrotal injection; one combined-injection case was repeated after the effect declined at month 6<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |

Repeat injections required to maintain effect.

### Dose comparison across genital BoNT/A applications

| Application | Dose | Dilution | Target | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## Safety — The Fertility Signal

**Breikaa 2014 (mature rats).**<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> Intracremasteric BoNT/A 10 / 20 / 40 U/kg × 3 over two weeks:

- **Inhibited spermatogenesis** on flow cytometry.
- Histopathology: seminiferous tubules separated by dense fibers (dose-dependent).
- Altered LDH-X and acid phosphatase activity.
- Authors: "Decreased fertility may be a serious problem Botox injections could cause."

**Breikaa 2016 (immature or growing rats).**<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> Confirmed decreased sperm count and quality, delayed maturation on flow cytometry, and significantly increased apoptosis markers, an apoptosis-mediated mechanism.

**Caveats.**

- Rat intracremasteric doses (10–40 U/kg) exceed the 100 U penile-dartos study regimen on a U/kg basis (about 1.4 U/kg for a 70 kg adult); this comparison does not establish a human scrotal dose.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- The only published aesthetic case injected both cremaster and dartos; the intracremasteric rat fertility signal is relevant to its cremaster component, although animal doses cannot quantify human risk.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **No human studies** have specifically evaluated fertility after scrotal dartos BoNT/A injection.
- The BOTOX label reports reduced fertility in male rats after repeated intramuscular doses of 8 or 16 U/kg; the no-effect dose was 4 U/kg. These rat data do not establish a safe human scrotal dose.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- The DAXXIFY (daxibotulinumtoxinA-lanm) label separately reports reduced fertility in male rats at doses associated with paternal toxicity (reduced weight gain and food intake; tested 3, 10 and 20 U/kg), and no effect at 3 U/kg, about 4 times the maximum recommended human dose.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> Its units are product-specific.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- Before BOTOX injection, screen for hypersensitivity, infection at the planned site, neuromuscular disease, compromised swallowing or respiration, interacting drugs such as aminoglycosides and muscle relaxants, and bleeding risk. Counsel about the boxed warning for distant toxin spread, including swallowing or breathing difficulty.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

**Thermoregulatory concern.** Gibson's rat data show that dartos contraction is strongly thermosensitive, and the dartos is generally considered central to scrotal thermoregulation. Chemical relaxation could theoretically blunt cold-induced contraction and disrupt countercurrent heat exchange, with potential effects on the 2–4 °C testicular-below-core gradient needed for spermatogenesis. This is a hypothesis about the Breikaa observations and has not been tested.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> No human studies have measured testicular temperature after Scrotox.

**Potential complications.** The single aesthetic case discussed hematoma, edema, erythema, and inflammation but observed no immediate complication.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> Injection pain, asymmetric relaxation, excessive descent, and spread to adjacent muscles are plausible but unquantified. In a separate cord-block pilot, 3/18 men reported transient scrotal or inguinal paresthesia, which resolved by three months.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Scrotox vs Surgical Reduction Scrotoplasty

| Feature | Scrotox (BoNT/A) | Reduction scrotoplasty |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | Combined cremaster relaxation and dartos wrinkle reduction in the single published aesthetic case<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> | Skin excision ± rearrangement |
| Aesthetic direction | **Elongation / smoothing** | **Tightening / reduction** |
| Duration | Temporary; benefit persisted at month 3 and declined by month 6 in one combined-injection case<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> | Permanent |
| Invasiveness | Injection only | Surgical excision |
| Anesthesia | Bilateral cord block in the single published combined-injection case<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>; no dartos-only standard | General or spinal in a published combined reduction/Z-plasty case<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>; no established regimen for isolated reduction |
| Recovery | A scrotal-specific recovery interval was not measured in the single injection case<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> | In one combined reduction/Z-plasty case, sexual activity resumed at 4–6 weeks<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> |
| Repeat | Retreatment may be considered when the effect wanes; no evidence-based interval is established<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> | Not typically needed |
| Cumulative cost | High (repeat) | One-time |
| Fertility concern | Animal reproductive signal; human risk unknown<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> | Fertility outcomes were not reported in the cited cosmetic-reduction case<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> |
| Status / coding | Off-label BoNT/A use | Scrotoplasty: CPT 55175 (simple) or 55180 (complicated)<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |

The two procedures address opposite aesthetic preferences. They are complementary in the menu and are not interchangeable.

## Regulatory and Counseling

- **Off-label** for any scrotal indication; the BOTOX prescribing information lists no scrotal use.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Evidence level.** One published aesthetic case using combined cremaster and dartos injections, plus indirect evidence from other indications; no controlled aesthetic scrotal trial.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Informed consent** should cover off-label status, uncertain duration and retreatment needs, the animal fertility signal, a theoretical effect on scrotal thermoregulation, and the absence of direct human reproductive-safety data.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **Men desiring future fertility:** Explain that reproductive safety is unestablished. The one-patient case authors advised against elective aesthetic injection in reproductive-age men, and a 2026 systematic review advises avoiding it when fertility preservation is a goal.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

---

## Current Standing

Scrotal BoNT/A is a growing but poorly studied aesthetic procedure. The Mehdizadeh 2026 systematic review lists scrotal relaxation among off-label aesthetic uses and reports high overall satisfaction across the broader, mostly facial, off-label literature; it calls for standardized protocols and refined evaluation methods. There are no dedicated clinical trials, no standardized scrotal-specific dosing protocol, no long-term safety data, and no human fertility studies. The Breikaa animal data are a safety signal that human studies have not addressed.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
