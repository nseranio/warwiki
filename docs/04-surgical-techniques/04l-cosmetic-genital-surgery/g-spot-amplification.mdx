# G-Spot Amplification ("G-Shot")

**G-spot amplification** (the "G-shot") is a cosmetic procedure that injects a dermal filler (typically hyaluronic acid), collagen or autologous fat into the anterior vaginal wall at the putative Gräfenberg spot, with the goal of enhancing sexual pleasure and orgasm. It is among the most controversial female genital cosmetic procedures. The anatomical existence of the G-spot as a discrete structure is disputed, and no randomized trial of G-spot amplification itself is known. ACOG 2020 holds that procedures to alter sexual appearance or function, other than those done for clinical indications, are not medically indicated and that their safety and effectiveness have not been established. FIGO 2025 holds that it is ethically inappropriate to recommend, perform or refer patients for cosmetic genital procedures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> For positioning versus other female cosmetic options see [Cosmetic Genital Surgery — Female](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/female-cosmetic).

**Society positioning and anatomic-existence caveat.**

- **Anatomy.** A literature review concluded that "all published scientific data point to the fact that the G-spot does not exist" as a distinct anatomical entity. The review also concluded that G-spot amplification is not medically indicated.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **ACOG 2020 Committee Opinion No. 795.** Cosmetic genital procedures, including G-spot amplification, are not medically indicated, pose substantial risk, and have not had their safety and effectiveness established. For G-spot amplification, published data are restricted to expert opinion, case reports and small case series.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **FIGO 2025 Statement.** It is ethically inappropriate for obstetrician-gynecologists to recommend, perform, or refer patients for cosmetic genital procedures in women without structural or functional abnormalities.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Screening for body dysmorphic disorder (BDD) comes first in every consultation.

---

## Background — the G-spot controversy

In 1950, Ernst Gräfenberg described a distinct erotogenic zone on the anterior vaginal wall. The term "G-spot" was coined by Addiego, Whipple et al. in 1981.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> A discrete G-spot has not been confirmed anatomically, and an alternative view attributes the responsive area to the broader clitoral-urethral-vaginal complex.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The concept has nonetheless driven commercial interest and the development of G-spot amplification procedures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Procedure

G-spot amplification is a minimally invasive, office-based procedure depositing an injectable substance into the bladder–vaginal septum (anterior vaginal wall), approximately 2–3 cm inside the introitus, at the area believed to correspond to the G-spot.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Injectable agents

| Agent | Description | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| **Hyaluronic acid (HA) dermal filler** | Most commonly used. Cross-linked HA provides temporary volume augmentation. | About 9–12 months (expected reabsorption period of the product in one series)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Collagen-based filler** | Earlier formulations (human-derived collagen) | 3–4 months<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Autologous fat (microfat / MAFT)** | Harvested via liposuction; provides volume and possible regenerative effects via adipose-derived stem cells (ADSCs) | Variable (subject to fat-graft survival); stable at 18 months in one series<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |

### Technique — HA injection

- **Anesthesia.** Local (topical or infiltrative).
- **Injection.** Submucosal deposition into the anterior-vaginal-wall target area. The one HA series cited, which injected the posterior vaginal wall for laxity, used 3–5 mL of cross-linked HA.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Procedure time.** Approximately 15–30 min as an outpatient procedure.

### Technique — autologous fat injection

- Fat harvested from abdomen / thighs / flanks via liposuction; processed (washed, filtered, or centrifuged) into microfat or nanofat; injected into the vaginal wall.
- The Lai 2023 MAFT series (retrospective, n = 20) injected an average of 21.9 mL into the vagina and 20.8 mL into the vulva and mons.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- More involved than HA injection; requires a donor-site procedure.

---

## Proposed mechanism of action

The theoretical rationale is that increasing the volume and turgor of anterior vaginal wall tissue at the G-spot area would:

- Increase mechanical pressure on underlying nerve-rich tissue during intercourse.
- Enhance sensitivity and arousal.
- Facilitate vaginal orgasm.

For autologous fat, tissue regeneration through ADSCs has also been proposed. In Lai 2023, vaginal histology after fat grafting showed neocollagenesis, neoangiogenesis and increased estrogen-receptor expression.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

---

## Evidence — extremely limited for G-spot amplification specifically

The evidence base for G-spot amplification specifically is very limited. ACOG: "scant information on the outcomes (risks and benefits) of G-spot amplification exists in the peer-reviewed literature, and the published data are mostly restricted to expert opinion, case reports, or small case series."<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Adjacent vaginal-augmentation evidence (NOT G-spot-specific)

| Study | Agent | n | Follow-up | Key outcomes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Yi 2026<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | Cross-linked HA (posterior vaginal wall) | 42 | 12 mo | All FSFI domains improved; 81% satisfaction at 12 months; improved Peritron parameters; Level of evidence V |
| Lai 2023<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | Autologous fat (MAFT, vulvovaginal), retrospective | 20 | 6 mo | FSFI 43.8 to 68.6 (p &lt; 0.001); histologic neocollagenesis and neoangiogenesis |
| Menkes 2021<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | Microfat (labia majora) and nanofat (vagina) | 50 | 18 mo | VHI and FSD significantly improved; data for 80% of patients normalized at 6 months; stable at 18 months |
| Clarke 2026 pilot RCT (single-blind)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | PRP (distal anterior vaginal wall) vs saline, premenopausal women without severe sexual dysfunction | 52 | 6 mo | Median FSFI change at 6 weeks (primary outcome) 2.2 vs 0.3; at 6 months 1.6 vs 0.8 (p = 0.05); PGI-I improvement 69.2% vs 34.6% at 6 months (p = 0.01) |
| Bensmail 2025<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | Cross-linked HA (vulvovaginal, postmenopausal); 12-week placebo-controlled phase then open-label | 115 (HA-treated) | 52 wk | Significant reduction in vulvovaginal atrophy symptoms and FSFI improvement, maintained to 52 weeks (open-label after week 12) |

Most of these studies address vaginal augmentation broadly (for laxity, atrophy, or genitourinary syndrome of menopause) rather than G-spot amplification specifically. The Clarke 2026 anterior-vaginal-wall PRP pilot RCT is the closest available proxy. It showed a greater FSFI increase with PRP than with saline at 6 weeks, but the FSFI subscale differences did not reach statistical significance and the 6-month total FSFI difference had overlapping confidence intervals (p = 0.05).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Duration of effect

| Agent | Typical duration |
|---|---|
| HA fillers | About 9–12 months before reabsorption (product-specific expectation); repeat injections to maintain<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Autologous fat | Variable graft survival; results stable at 18 mo in one series<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Collagen | 3–4 mo<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |

## Complications

ACOG lists pain, bleeding, infection, scarring, adhesions, altered sensation, dyspareunia and reoperation as potential complications of cosmetic genital procedures.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The notes in the table are general and are not specific to G-spot amplification:

| Complication | Notes |
|---|---|
| Pain | Generally minimal with HA; donor-site discomfort with fat |
| Bleeding | Submucosal hematoma rare |
| Infection | Rare |
| Scarring / adhesions | Rare |
| Altered sensation | Hyper- or hyposensitivity reported |
| Dyspareunia | Possible, especially with over-correction |
| Filler migration / granuloma | Theoretical with HA; not specifically reported in vaginal G-spot use |
| Donor-site morbidity | Autologous fat technique only |
| Need for reoperation | Re-injection commonly required to maintain effect |

The small vaginal HA and fat-injection series reported no serious adverse events, but small samples and short follow-up limit detection of rare complications.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Distinction — cosmetic G-spot amplification vs therapeutic vaginal augmentation

The two indications differ:

| Use case | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **G-spot amplification for sexual enhancement in healthy women** | Cosmetic; **not medically indicated**; lacks evidence | Expert opinion / case reports / small series only<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Vaginal HA / fat injection for genitourinary syndrome of menopause (GSM)** | Emerging therapeutic application | Bensmail 2025 (12-week placebo-controlled phase, then open-label to 52 weeks); Menkes 2021 microfat and nanofat to 18 months; Mantovani 2022 microfragmented fat (n = 12, 5 months)<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[7]](#ref7)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |

The GSM application has randomized data for HA and small observational series for fat. Patients seeking G-spot amplification for sexual enhancement are not the same population as patients with GSM, and evidence from the latter does not transfer to the former.

## Emerging techniques

| Technique | Description | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| **Cannulated Intravaginal Injection Technique (CIVIT)** | Cannula-based HA delivery intended to give more homogeneous, less traumatic distribution. In one retrospective series of 44 women with genitourinary symptoms, post-injection FSFI and symptom scores were higher than with random needling (FSFI arousal did not differ) | Leylek 2025<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Anterior-vaginal-wall PRP** | Pilot single-blind RCT of a one-time injection; greater FSFI increase than saline at 6 weeks and more women reporting improvement at 6 months (PGI-I 69.2% vs 34.6%, p = 0.01) | Clarke 2026<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| **Microfragmented adipose tissue** | Minimally processed adipose tissue injection for GSM; symptom scores improved at 5 months in a cohort of 12 women | Mantovani 2022<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |

## Key takeaways

- No randomized trial of G-spot amplification specifically is known; ACOG describes the published data as expert opinion, case reports and small series.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- The anatomical existence of the G-spot as a discrete structure is disputed.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **ACOG 2020 and FIGO 2025.** ACOG states that cosmetic genital procedures are not medically indicated. FIGO states that it is ethically inappropriate to recommend, perform or refer patients for them in women without structural or functional abnormalities.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- The broader vaginal-augmentation literature (HA, fat and PRP for GSM and vulvovaginal atrophy) includes randomized data, but those data do not transfer to the cosmetic G-shot context.
- The effect of HA is temporary and repeat injections are needed, so cost and durability limits are part of counseling.

---

## Postoperative management

- **Activity restriction.** Avoid intercourse, tampon use, and strenuous exercise for 1–2 weeks after HA injection; longer (4 weeks+) after autologous fat to protect graft survival.
- **Wound care.** Generally not required after HA injection; sitz baths / loose clothing if discomfort.
- **Donor-site care** for autologous fat: compression, ice, monitor for hematoma.
- **Follow-up.** Initial check at 2 weeks; functional / satisfaction assessment at 3 and 6 months. Counsel that effect will wane; re-injection schedule depends on agent.
