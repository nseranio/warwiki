# Inverted-Y Plasty Clitoral Hoodoplasty

The **inverted-Y plasty clitoral hoodoplasty** is a midline-based reduction technique described by Eserdağ and Anğın in 2021 (*J Minim Invasive Gynecol*). It is a central longitudinal excision of excess prepuce in an inverted-Y design. The stem of the Y runs longitudinally along the prepuce midline and the two arms diverge superiorly toward the mons. The authors state that the geometry allows removal of both vertical and horizontal redundancy and lifts the clitoral hood.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In their single-surgeon case series (n = 63), 96.9% of patients were satisfied with the aesthetic result at 2 months and no major complications occurred.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> For positioning versus other female cosmetic options see [Cosmetic Genital Surgery — Female](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/female-cosmetic).

**Society positioning.** ACOG Committee Opinion No. 795 (2020) lists clitoral hood reduction among female genital cosmetic procedures. It states that these procedures are not medically indicated without a clinical indication, that their safety and effectiveness have not been established, and that published data on risks and benefits are limited. It advises assessment for body dysmorphic disorder (BDD) if indicated.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The FIGO 2025 statement asserts that it is ethically inappropriate for obstetrician-gynecologists to recommend, perform or refer patients for cosmetic genital surgery.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> The hoodoplasty literature is largely retrospective single-surgeon case series without validated outcome instruments.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Rationale and indications

Clitoral hood reduction is most commonly performed concurrently with labiaplasty. Labiaplasty alone in patients with concurrent clitoral-hood excess is described as producing aesthetic disharmony, with the hood appearing disproportionately prominent or "sagging" once the labia are reduced.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> In the Eserdağ founding cohort, 90.5% of patients requested the operation primarily for dissatisfaction with aesthetic appearance and 95.2% requested minimum residual labia, mandating concurrent hood reduction to maintain proportion.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The inverted-Y plasty has been proposed for:

- **Combined vertical and horizontal hood redundancy.** The inverted-Y geometry addresses both axes, where a simple midline strip addresses only vertical excess.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Concurrent labiaplasty.** All 63 patients in the founding series had curvilinear (edge / trim) labiaplasty with the hoodoplasty.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Patients in whom a lifting effect is desired.** The authors describe lifting of the hood in addition to reduction.

## Surgical technique

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| **1. Patient positioning and anesthesia** | Lithotomy. Local or regional anesthesia (lidocaine with epinephrine), with or without sedation; general anesthesia per patient preference and concurrent procedures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **2. Marking** | Excess prepuce marked along the **midline**. The excision is designed as an **inverted Y** — the stem of the Y running longitudinally along the hood midline, the two arms diverging superiorly toward the mons. Width and length of the stem and arms are tailored to the morphology of redundancy. |
| **3. Excision** | Skin and subcutaneous tissue within the marked Y are excised, with care to identify and protect the **dorsal nerve of the clitoris** and the underlying clitoral neurovascular bundle. **Avoid over-resection** — excessive removal can produce clitoral exposure or sensitivity changes. |
| **4. Hemostasis** | Bipolar electrocautery; selective suture ligation. |
| **5. Closure** | Approximation in layers with fine absorbable suture. The final scar is largely concealed within the natural folds of the prepuce. |
| **6. Combination with labiaplasty** | Most series combine the hoodoplasty with curvilinear / edge labiaplasty under the same anesthetic. |

**Operative metrics (Eserdağ 2021)**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>:

- Median operative time for the hoodoplasty component: 34 min (range 25–42).
- Median operative time for the combined labiaplasty: 45 min (range 35–62).
- Single-surgeon private-practice setting; local or regional anesthesia.

**Safety points.** Meticulous identification and preservation of the dorsal nerve of the clitoris; avoid over-resection, which can produce clitoral exposure or altered sensation. Both points apply to every hoodoplasty technique, not only the inverted-Y.

## Outcomes

In the founding n = 63 series (median age 33.2, range 19–47)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>:

- **96.9% aesthetic satisfaction** at 2-month follow-up.
- **No major complications**.

In other retrospective series of hood reduction combined with labia minora reduction, reported satisfaction ranges from 91.0% (within 14 days) to 99.2%, and complication rates from 4.3% to 7.4%. The most common issues were delayed wound healing, perceived asymmetry and revision (1.5–3.7%).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> Follow-up was short (mean 1.9 months in the largest series), and most studies lacked validated outcome instruments and comparison groups.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Comparison with other hoodoplasty techniques

| Technique | Description | Best fit | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Inverted-Y plasty** | Central longitudinal excision with inverted-Y design; reduction + lift | **Combined vertical + horizontal hood excess** | Eserdağ 2021<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Simple longitudinal excision** | Midline strip excision of prepuce | Primarily vertical excess | Triana 2024<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Horseshoe (U-shaped) excision** | U-shaped excision around the hood | Horizontal excess | Triana 2024<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Bilateral triangular / inverted-V resection (Liu classification)** | Tailored by Liu Zone-C / Zone-L classification | Per anatomic zone (n = 789; **95.7% satisfaction; revision 1.9%**) | Liu 2022<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Extended (hockey-stick) central wedge** | Wedge resection extending from labia into lateral hood | Combined labia + lateral-hood excess | Alter 2008<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **Three-step excision** | Sequential lateral hood + labial wedge + junction triangle | Lateral hood + labial hypertrophy (n = 136; **95.5% satisfaction**) | Xia 2021<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **Modified wedge for composite reduction** | Wedge with concurrent hood reduction | Composite labia + hood (n = 738; **99.2% aesthetic / 96.2% overall**; mean follow-up 1.9 months) | Shi 2026<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |

## Advantages

- **Maximum trimming.** The authors report that the design allows maximum trimming of excess hood tissue.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Lifting effect.** The authors describe lifting of the clitoral hood area.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Easily combined** with curvilinear / edge labiaplasty under the same anesthetic.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Concealed scar** within the natural prepuce folds.

---

## Limitations

- **Single-surgeon case series** (n = 63) without a comparison group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **No validated outcome instruments.** In the founding series, satisfaction was assessed with non-validated questionnaires.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **No long-term follow-up beyond 2 months** in the original series.
- **Anatomy-driven technique selection.** Newer classification systems (Liu 2022 Zone-C / Zone-L) suggest that the optimal hood technique depends on the anatomical pattern of excess (central versus lateral, vertical versus horizontal, composite). The inverted-Y is one option within that framework rather than a universal default.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Safety overhead.** Clitoral neurovascular bundle injury risk applies across all hoodoplasty techniques and is not unique to the inverted-Y.

---

## Postoperative management

- **Activity restriction.** Avoid intercourse, tampon use, and strenuous exercise for 4–6 weeks.
- **Wound care.** Ice 48–72 h; sitz baths 24–48 h onward; topical antibiotic ointment; loose-fitting clothing.
- **Sensory counseling.** Altered sensation is among the potential complications ACOG lists for genital cosmetic surgery.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Liu 2022 reported no paresthesia at 6 months.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Follow-up.** 1 week (wound check), 6 weeks (clearance), 3–6 months (final outcome). The founding series reports outcomes only to 2 months.
- **PRO assessment.** FSFI and FGSIS at baseline and ≥ 6 months postoperatively.
