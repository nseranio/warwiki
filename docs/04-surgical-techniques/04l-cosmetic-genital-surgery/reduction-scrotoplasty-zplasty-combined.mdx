# Combined Reduction Scrotoplasty + Penoscrotal Z-Plasty

**Combined reduction scrotoplasty and penoscrotal Z-plasty** is the third pathway in the Thomas & Navia algorithm for the bothersome scrotum. It is used when excessive scrotal laxity and penoscrotal webbing are present together.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The two components, vertical midline scrotal-skin resection and penoscrotal-junction Z-plasty, are performed in a single stage. Schifano and colleagues, reviewing scarce evidence, describe them as "easy, safe, and effective solutions to improve genital cosmesis, being carried out in isolation or in combination."<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For the two component deep-dives see [Reduction Scrotoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/reduction-scrotoplasty) and [Penoscrotal-Web Correction (Z-plasty)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/penoscrotal-web-correction). For the broader male cosmetic framework see the [Male Cosmetic Genital Surgery atlas page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/male-cosmetic).

---

## Indications

Patient meets both criteria on examination:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- **Scrotal laxity.** Scrotal bag hanging &gt; 1–2 cm below the penile tip (discomfort with ambulation, sports, intercourse, or loose clothing).
- **Penoscrotal webbing.** Scrotal skin extending abnormally onto the ventral penile shaft, obscuring the penoscrotal angle and causing perceived penile shortening.

Common contexts:

- **Adjunct to penile-prosthesis implantation.** In Miranda-Sousa's series of 43 men (39 inflatable, 4 semirigid), web release at implant surgery was followed by increased perceived length in 84% and good overall satisfaction in 98% at 3 months, with about 12 min of added operative time. In a comparison group of 37 implant patients without web release, 84% complained of shortening.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Midline reduction at the same sitting extends this approach and has not been studied separately.
- **Post-circumcision webbing layered on age-related laxity.**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Concealed / hidden penis with scrotal redundancy.** Combine with Alter ventral tacking and other adjuncts.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Thomas & Navia Algorithm — where the combined approach fits<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

1. Patient presents with bothersome scrotum.
2. Assess: scrotal laxity? penoscrotal webbing?
3. Three pathways:
   - **Laxity only:** vertical midline scrotal-skin resection.
   - **Webbing only:** penoscrotal-junction Z-plasty.
   - **Both:** combined reduction scrotoplasty and Z-plasty (this page).

## Operative Protocol

### Preoperative planning

1. **Anesthesia.** Supine; local cord block (1% lidocaine with epinephrine and 0.25% bupivacaine) with or without sedation, or general.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
2. **Standing / dependent assessment.** Quantify how far the scrotum hangs below the penile tip and how high the web extends with the penis on stretch.
3. **Mark the Z-plasty.** Central limb along the line of maximal web at the ventral midline; two peripheral limbs of equal length at 60° from each end of the central limb (one onto ventral penile shaft, one onto scrotal skin).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
4. **Mark the midline ellipse** along the median raphe inferior to the Z-plasty design, centered on maximal redundancy.

**Integrated design.** The inferior limb of the Z-plasty must transition into the superior extent of the midline ellipse so the two incision lines are continuous and tissue rearrangement at the penoscrotal junction is not disrupted by the midline excision below it.

### Component 1 — Penoscrotal Z-plasty

5. Full-thickness incisions along all three Z limbs through skin and superficial dartos, preserving urethra / corpus spongiosum.
6. Elevate the two triangular flaps in the subdartos plane with adequate thickness to preserve perfusion.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
7. Divide fibrous dartos bands tethering the ventral shaft.
8. **Transpose.** The flaps are transposed. This converts the vertical web into a transverse or oblique axis and lengthens ventral skin by up to about 75% of the central-limb length at 60°.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
9. Inset and layered closure with absorbable suture.

### Component 2 — Vertical midline scrotal-skin resection

10. Full-thickness elliptical excision along the median raphe, superficial to the scrotal septum and tunica vaginalis.
11. **Adjuncts as needed for larger resections:**<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
    - Septum division communicates the hemiscrotums.
    - Dartos mobilization off the tunica vaginalis.
    - Gubernacular division allows superior testicular repositioning.
    - Spermatic-cord mobilization and scrotopexy at the penoscrotal junction.
12. Layered closure: dartos with 3-0 or 4-0 absorbable braided suture; skin with absorbable suture along the raphe.

### Final steps

13. Meticulous hemostasis throughout (dartos is highly vascular).
14. Compressive supportive garment; remove at 5–7 days. Same-day discharge.

## Geometric and Vascular Considerations

Three scrotal cutaneous territories (two inferior external pudendal and one perineal or central) are richly inter-anastomosed, giving the combined operation a vascular safety margin.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Specific risks to manage:

- **Limit inferior undermining of the Z-plasty flaps.** The midline excision has already narrowed the central vascular territory beneath them.
- **Maintain Z-plasty flap thickness** to preserve the random-pattern blood supply.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Avoid tension at the Z-plasty / midline closure junction.** This is a competing-vector T-junction.
- **Calibrate excision volume.** Midline over-resection puts the Z-plasty flaps under excessive tension; the Z-plasty itself further tightens the envelope. A review of genital hidradenitis reconstruction describes primary closure after resection of up to about 50% of the scrotum;<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> that limit has not been tested for the combined operation.

## Isolated vs Combined Approach

| Feature | Midline resection alone | Z-plasty alone | Combined |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary pathology | Scrotal laxity | Penoscrotal webbing | Both simultaneously |
| Skin excision | Fusiform along raphe | None (rearrangement only) | Fusiform + flap transposition |
| Geometric lengthening | No | Up to ~75% at 60° | Yes, at the penoscrotal junction |
| Scar location | Midline raphe | Penoscrotal junction (oblique) | Both, joined at a T |
| Operative complexity | Low | Moderate | Moderate–high |
| CPT code | **55175** (simple) | **55180** (complicated) | **55180** (complicated) |
| Adjunctive scrotopexy | Optional | Atypical | Often beneficial |
| Algorithm indication | Laxity only | Webbing only | **Laxity + webbing** |

## Outcomes

No published series reports outcomes for the combined procedure as a defined entity. The table lists data from each component; the combined column is not reported.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Outcome | Midline resection | Z-plasty | Combined (extrapolated) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary healing | Not reported | 98% (Álvarez Vega, n = 100 pediatric Z-plasties of mixed indication) | Not reported |
| Hypertrophic scarring | Not reported | 1% (same series) | Not reported |
| Wound dehiscence | Not reported | 1% (suture separation, same series) | Not reported; two suture lines and a T-junction may add risk |
| Recurrence of webbing | N/A | Stable correction at 1–2 years in the 97% with follow-up (same series) | Not reported |
| Recurrence of laxity | Not reported | N/A | Not reported |
| Perceived penile lengthening | Not the primary goal | 84% (web release with IPP, Miranda-Sousa) | Not reported |
| Overall satisfaction | "Satisfactory" (Schifano) | 98% (web release with IPP, Miranda-Sousa) | Not reported |

## Additive Complications Specific to the Combined Approach

- **Increased operative time** versus either component alone.
- **Hematoma / seroma.** Two dissection planes create more potential dead space.
- **Vascular compromise at the Z-plasty / midline junction.** The area of greatest vulnerability; avoid aggressive undermining and excessive tension here.
- **T-junction dehiscence.** Competing tension vectors at the junction of the Z-plasty closure and the midline closure.
- **Over-correction.** Combined skin excision and tissue rearrangement can create an uncomfortably tight scrotum if not carefully calibrated.

General benign-scrotal-surgery risk factors (Rezaee 2022 NSQIP, n = 12,917; 30-day event rate 4.1%) were malnourishment OR 4.1, decreased functional status OR 3.8, bleeding disorders OR 3.4, COPD OR 1.8, age ≥ 40 OR 1.6, smoking OR 1.4, diabetes OR 1.3.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Application Variants

- **Scrotal massive localized lymphedema (MLL) in obesity.** Machol 2014 used laterally based scrotal flaps with or without mid-raphe Z-plasty for anatomic reconstruction after MLL excision (n = 4, average BMI 53.9, average resected 3,492 g): 50% recurrence; mean 2 operations during follow-up; improved urinary function and overall symptoms.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> This is a pathologic rather than aesthetic use of the combined design.
- **Concurrent with penile prosthesis.** Single anesthetic event delivers web release (recovers perceived length), midline reduction (optimizes penile-to-scrotal ratio), and prosthesis placement; addresses the shortening complaint reported by 84% of implant patients without web release in Miranda-Sousa's comparison group.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## CPT Coding

- **CPT 55180.** Scrotoplasty, complicated, covers the flap-transposition and reconstruction components of the combined operation.
- Coded separately from IPP placement codes (54400–54405) when performed concurrently.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Postoperative Care

- Same-day discharge.
- Scrotal support / jockstrap × 2–4 weeks.
- Bolster dressing removed at 5–7 days.
- Activity restriction (no strenuous activity, lifting, or intercourse) × 2–4 weeks.
- Wound check at 1–2 weeks; final assessment at 4–6 weeks.

---

## Evidence Limitations

- The combined procedure is proposed in an algorithm but has no dedicated case series.
- All outcome data come from the component techniques.
- Overall evidence base for aesthetic scrotoplasty is Level IV.
- No validated patient-reported outcomes for the combined procedure.
- No comparative studies versus either component alone.
