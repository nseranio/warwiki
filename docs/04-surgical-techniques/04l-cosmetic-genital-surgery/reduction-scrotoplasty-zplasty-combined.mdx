# Combined Reduction Scrotoplasty + Penoscrotal Z-Plasty

**Combined reduction scrotoplasty and penoscrotal Z-plasty** is the third pathway in the Thomas & Navia algorithm for the bothersome scrotum. It is used when excessive scrotal laxity and penoscrotal webbing are present together.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The two components, vertical midline scrotal-skin resection and penoscrotal-junction Z-plasty, are performed in a single stage. Schifano and colleagues, while noting scarce evidence, describe reduction scrotoplasty and penoscrotal-web correction as "easy, safe and effective solutions to improve genital cosmesis," whether performed alone or with penile prosthesis implantation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For the two component deep-dives see [Reduction Scrotoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/reduction-scrotoplasty) and [Penoscrotal-Web Correction (Z-plasty)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/penoscrotal-web-correction). For the broader male cosmetic framework see the [Male Cosmetic Genital Surgery atlas page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/male-cosmetic).

---

## Indications

Patient meets both criteria on examination:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- **Scrotal laxity.** A low-hanging scrotum causing discomfort with ambulation, sports, intercourse, or clothing. Thomas and Navia describe descent &gt;1–2 cm below the tip of the flaccid penis while standing as an experience-based marker, not a validated cutoff.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Penoscrotal webbing.** Scrotal skin extending abnormally onto the ventral penile shaft, obscuring the penoscrotal angle and causing perceived penile shortening.

Common contexts:

- **Adjunct to penile-prosthesis implantation.** In Miranda-Sousa's series of 43 men (39 inflatable, 4 semirigid), web release at implant surgery was followed at 3 months by increased perceived length in 84% and good overall satisfaction in 98%, with about 12 min of added operative time; 2/43 developed wound hematoma and 3/43 had focal superficial dehiscence.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> In a comparison group of 37 implant patients without web release, 84% complained of shortening.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Midline reduction at the same sitting extends this approach and has not been studied separately.
- **Post-circumcision webbing layered on age-related laxity.**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Concealed / buried penis with scrotal redundancy.** First distinguish acquired buried penis from isolated webbing or laxity and plan reconstruction according to the underlying skin, adipose, and fixation defects; Alter ventral tacking is a selected maneuver, not a routine extension of this cosmetic technique.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

---

## Thomas & Navia Algorithm — where the combined approach fits<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

1. Patient presents with bothersome scrotum.
2. Assess: scrotal laxity? penoscrotal webbing?
3. Three pathways:
   - **Laxity only:** vertical midline scrotal-skin resection.
   - **Webbing only:** penoscrotal-junction Z-plasty.
   - **Both:** combined reduction scrotoplasty and Z-plasty (this page).

---

## Operative Protocol

### Preoperative planning

1. **Anesthesia.** Thomas and Navia describe general or spinal anesthesia for their combined technique.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
2. **Standing and genital assessment.** Examine the scrotum and testes for other pathology, quantify laxity, and assess the web with the penis on stretch. Thomas and Navia mark skin and dartos excision with a standing, flaccid-penis pinch test; before ventral penile skin excision, check erect shaft-skin adequacy to avoid constriction during erection.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
3. **Mark the Z-plasty.** Central limb along the line of maximal web at the ventral midline; two peripheral limbs of equal length at 60° from each end of the central limb (one onto ventral penile shaft, one onto scrotal skin).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
4. **Mark the midline ellipse** along the median raphe inferior to the Z-plasty design, centered on maximal redundancy and ending posteriorly at the perineoscrotal junction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Integrated design.** The inferior limb of the Z-plasty must transition into the superior extent of the midline ellipse so the two incision lines are continuous and tissue rearrangement at the penoscrotal junction is not disrupted by the midline excision below it.

### Component 1 — Penoscrotal Z-plasty

5. Full-thickness incisions along all three Z limbs through skin and superficial dartos, preserving urethra / corpus spongiosum.
6. Elevate the two triangular flaps in the subdartos plane with adequate thickness to preserve perfusion.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
7. Divide fibrous dartos bands tethering the ventral shaft.
8. **Transpose.** The flaps are transposed. This converts the vertical web into a transverse or oblique axis; a basic 60° Z-plasty has a theoretical length gain of about 75% along its central-limb axis, with less gain in practice because of wound tension.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
9. Inset and layered closure with absorbable suture.

### Component 2 — Vertical midline scrotal-skin resection

10. Full-thickness elliptical excision along the median raphe, superficial to the scrotal septum and tunica vaginalis.
11. **Additional maneuvers outside the reported combined case:** Thomas and Navia describe skin/dartos excision, Z-plasty transposition, and layered closure without the deeper dissections listed below; genital hidradenitis reconstruction is a separate clinical setting.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
    - Septum division communicates the hemiscrotums.
    - Dartos mobilization off the tunica vaginalis.
    - Gubernacular division allows superior testicular repositioning.
    - Spermatic-cord mobilization and scrotopexy at the penoscrotal junction.
12. Layered closure: Thomas and Navia used 4-0 poliglecaprone absorbable monofilament in separate dartos and skin planes, approximating dartos at the scrotal septum to restore the median raphe.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Final steps

13. Meticulous hemostasis throughout (dartos is highly vascular).
14. Apply a supportive garment. Thomas and Navia discharged their single reported patient the same day; they did not specify a garment-removal interval.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Geometric and Vascular Considerations

Cadaveric microdissection identifies inferior external pudendal and perineal arterial systems with cutaneous anastomoses; perfusion after this combined incision pattern has not been measured.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Specific risks to manage:

- **Limit unnecessary undermining of the Z-plasty flaps.** This is a precaution based on scrotal vascular anatomy; perfusion after the combined incision pattern has not been measured.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Maintain full-thickness Z-plasty flaps** and avoid high-tension transposition to reduce flap necrosis or sloughing risk.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Avoid tension at the Z-plasty / midline closure junction.** This is a competing-vector T-junction.
- **Calibrate excision volume.** Midline over-resection puts the Z-plasty flaps under excessive tension; the Z-plasty itself further tightens the envelope. A review of genital hidradenitis reconstruction describes primary closure after resection of up to about 50% of the scrotum;<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> that limit has not been tested for the combined operation.

## Isolated vs Combined Approach

| Feature | Midline resection alone | Z-plasty alone | Combined |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary pathology | Scrotal laxity | Penoscrotal webbing | Both simultaneously |
| Skin excision | Fusiform along raphe | None (rearrangement only) | Fusiform + flap transposition |
| Geometric lengthening along Z-plasty central limb | No | Theoretical ~75% at 60°; less in practice<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | Not measured for the combined procedure |
| Scar location | Midline raphe | Penoscrotal junction (oblique) | Both, joined at a T |
| Operative complexity | Low | Moderate | Moderate–high |
| CPT code | 55175 (simple) or 55180 (complicated), per documented work | Confirm code from documented work | Confirm code from documented work |
| Adjunctive scrotopexy | Optional | Atypical | Not reported in Thomas and Navia's combined case; benefit of adding it was not assessed<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Algorithm indication | Laxity only | Webbing only | **Laxity + webbing** |

## Outcomes

Thomas and Navia report one case of vertical midline resection with penoscrotal Z-plasty; Nazario-Perez and colleagues report a different transverse-incision case.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> A 2026 retrospective report enrolled 54 adults with both webbing and laxity, analyzed 45 after nine losses to follow-up, and used bilateral extended V-shaped excision with dartos preserved, a distinct operation.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> No multi-patient series establishes outcomes for the vertical-resection and Z-plasty combination; the table therefore lists component data only.

| Outcome | Midline resection | Z-plasty | Combined (extrapolated) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary skin-flap healing | Not reported | 98% (Álvarez Vega, n = 100 pediatric Z-plasties of mixed indication)<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | Not reported |
| Hypertrophic scarring | Not reported | 1% (same mixed-indication pediatric series)<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | Not reported |
| Wound dehiscence | Not reported | 1% (suture-line separation requiring secondary-intention healing in the same mixed-indication pediatric series)<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | Not reported; two suture lines and a T-junction may add risk |
| Recurrence of webbing | N/A | Not reported separately; 97/100 pediatric patients with mixed indications completed 1–2 years of follow-up, and all completers had stable corrections<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | Not reported |
| Recurrence of laxity | Not reported | N/A | Not reported |
| Perceived penile lengthening | Not the primary goal | Not reported for Z-plasty | Not reported |
| Overall satisfaction | Not quantified in the cited review | Not reported for isolated Z-plasty | Not reported |

## Plausible additional risks of the combined approach (not quantified)

- **Increased operative time** versus either component alone.
- **Hematoma / seroma.** Two dissection planes create more potential dead space.
- **Vascular compromise at the Z-plasty / midline junction.** The area of greatest vulnerability; avoid aggressive undermining and excessive tension here.
- **T-junction dehiscence.** Competing tension vectors at the junction of the Z-plasty closure and the midline closure.
- **Over-correction.** Combined skin excision and tissue rearrangement can create an uncomfortably tight scrotum if not carefully calibrated.

In Rezaee’s 2015–2020 NSQIP analysis of 12,917 adults undergoing elective benign scrotal surgery, 4.1% had a 30-day composite postoperative event (complication, unplanned procedure, or death); adjusted associations with that composite were malnourishment OR 4.1, decreased functional status OR 3.8, bleeding disorders OR 3.4, COPD OR 1.8, age ≥ 40 OR 1.6, smoking OR 1.4, and diabetes OR 1.3.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Application Variants

- **Scrotal massive localized lymphedema (MLL) in obesity.** Machol 2014 used laterally based scrotal flaps with or without mid-raphe Z-plasty for anatomic reconstruction after MLL excision (n = 4, average BMI 53.9, average resected 3,492 g): 50% recurrence; mean 2 operations during follow-up; improved urinary function and overall symptoms.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> This is a pathologic rather than aesthetic use of the combined design.
- **Concurrent with penile prosthesis.** This variant combines web release, midline reduction and prosthesis placement in one operation, but its combined outcomes have not been studied. In Miranda-Sousa’s web-release cohort, 36/43 reported greater perceived length at three months; 31/37 implant patients without web release complained of shortening in clinical records.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> In a separate single-surgeon series of 103 scrotoplasties with initial IPP insertion, 15 developed dehiscence, including one operative washout and implant exchange; diabetes (OR 6.1, 95% CI 1.5–25.0) and V-Y scrotoplasty technique (OR 4.9, 95% CI 1.2–8.6) were associated with dehiscence.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## CPT Coding

- **Scrotoplasty coding.** CPT 55175 describes simple scrotoplasty and 55180 describes complicated scrotoplasty; determine the appropriate code from the documented operation rather than assigning 55180 to every Z-plasty or combined case.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Concurrent penile prosthesis placement.** Confirm whether scrotoplasty is separately reportable from the documented distinct work and current payer or NCCI edits before coding it with the implant.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

---

## Postoperative Care

- Same-day discharge.
- Scrotal support / jockstrap × 2–4 weeks.
- Bolster dressing removed at 5–7 days.
- Restrict strenuous activity and heavy lifting for 2–4 weeks; defer intercourse until wound reassessment. Thomas and Navia's single combined-case patient resumed sexual activity at 4–6 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Wound check at 1–2 weeks; final assessment at 4–6 weeks.

---

## Evidence Limitations

- The combined procedure is proposed in an algorithm but has no dedicated case series.
- All outcome data come from the component techniques.
- Overall evidence base for aesthetic scrotoplasty is Level IV.
- No validated patient-reported outcomes for the combined procedure.
- No comparative studies versus either component alone.
