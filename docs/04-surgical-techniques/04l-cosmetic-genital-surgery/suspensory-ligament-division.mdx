# Suspensory Ligament Division (SLD)

**Suspensory Ligament Division (SLD)** is the **most established surgical technique for penile lengthening**. It releases the ligamentous attachments anchoring the penile root to the pubic symphysis, allowing the **intracorporeal (hidden) portion of the penis** to advance anteriorly. Mean **flaccid length gain 1.3–2.6 cm** in clinical series — but satisfaction is **highly variable (27–83%)** and the procedure remains **investigational per the SMSNA 2024 Position Statement**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> For positioning vs other male cosmetic options see [Cosmetic Genital Surgery — Male](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/male-cosmetic).

:::warning[SMSNA 2024 — investigational]
The **SMSNA 2024 Position Statement** characterizes SLD and other cosmetic penile-enhancement procedures as **investigational** with limited data. **Multidisciplinary approach with psychiatric / psychological evaluation** is recommended to identify patients with penile dysmorphic disorder (PDD) who should not be offered surgical intervention.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
:::

## Anatomy of the penile suspensory ligamentous system

A cadaveric study of 49 specimens identified **three distinct ligaments**<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>:

1. **Fundiform ligament** — the most superficial; arises from Scarpa's fascia (superficial bundles) and the linea alba (deep median bundles); inserts into superficial penile fascia and continues to the scrotal septum. Loose, fibro-fatty tissue **not adherent to the tunica albuginea**.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
2. **Suspensory ligament proper** — deeper, dense fibrous connective tissue arising from the **pubic symphysis** and inserting into **Buck's fascia** of the corpora cavernosa. Two lateral circumferential bundles + one median bundle circumscribing the deep dorsal vein. **Primary target of SLD.**<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
3. **Arcuate (pubic) ligament** — the deepest component; arises from the body of the pubis and pubic symphysis and inserts into Buck's fascia, with lateral attachments to the inferior pubic rami.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

The suspensory ligament maintains the base of the penis in front of the pubis and is a **major support point during erection** — stabilizing the upward erection angle during coitus.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

---

## Mechanism of penile lengthening

SLD does not actually make the penis longer — it **exteriorizes more of the existing intracorporeal shaft** that normally resides behind the pubic symphysis, by releasing the tethering effect of the suspensory ligament.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

**Ramos 2024 cadaveric morphometric study** (16 specimens) — complete ligamentolysis produced uniform length increase averaging **+26.4 mm (SD 14.8; range 4–60 mm)**.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

Key finding: a significant **negative correlation** between pre-ligamentolysis length and gain (r = −0.601, p = 0.014) — **men with shorter baseline lengths achieved proportionally greater gains**. Age, ligament width, pubic-arch depth, and ethnicity did not correlate with gain.

## Surgical techniques

### 1. Classic infrapubic approach with V-Y plasty

The most commonly described technique<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>:

- Penopubic incision with an inverted V-shaped skin flap.
- Division of the suspensory ligament down to the pubic symphysis.
- **V-Y skin advancement** to cover the newly exposed shaft and prevent retraction.
- Optional silicone spacer or fat flap in the dead space between the corpora and pubis.

**Deskoulidi & Caminer 2023** — refined V-Y with **distally based fat flaps** advanced into the dead space + fat injections for simultaneous girth enhancement. Over 15 yr / 75 patients: **+2–4 cm flaccid length gain**, minimal complications, 4% revision for fat injections.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

**Drawbacks of V-Y plasty:** **hypertrophic scarring 51%** in one comparative study, plus visible "hump" at the penile base and potential for retraction.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

### 2. V-Y half-skin half-fat advancement flap with T-closure (Shaeer technique)

Designed to address the drawbacks of standard V-Y plasty<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>:

- The caudal half of the V-flap is deskinned, creating a rectangular fat flap.
- The fat flap is pulled into the gap between the penis base and pubis to prevent reattachment.
- T-shaped closure to avoid pulling the penis back.
- **Stay suture** from glans to thigh maintains the penis in a stretched position postoperatively.
- 6-mo outcome: no length loss, no hump, faint scar.

### 3. Circumcision (subcoronal) ligamentolysis

Avoids the penopubic incision entirely.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

- Ligamentolysis through a subcoronal circumcision incision; penile skin degloved to access the suspensory ligament.
- No V-Y plasty required.

**Mertziotis 2013 comparative study** (n = 82, V-Y vs circumcision):

| Outcome | V-Y plasty | Circumcision approach | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Length / girth gain | similar | similar | — |
| OR time | 151 min | **125 min** | 0.005 |
| Retraction | 11% | 6% | 0.453 |
| **Hypertrophic scar** | **51%** | **0%** | — |
| Satisfaction | lower | **higher** | **0.007** |
| SEAR (Self-Esteem and Relationship) | lower | **higher** | &lt; 0.001 |

### 4. Trans-scrotal approach (Karimian 2026)

n = 21 (14 BDD, 5 micropenis, 2 hypospadias)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>:

- Flaccid penile length **+2.6 ± 1.66 cm** (p &lt; 0.001).
- Stretched penile length **+2.4 ± 0.57 cm** (p &lt; 0.001).
- MGSIS significantly improved.

### 5. Combined with adjuncts to prevent reattachment

- **Acellular dermal matrix (ADM)** — **Zhang 2019** in 15 patients with buried penis (mean BMI 28.9): SLD + ADM filler + suprapubic liposuction → **+4.3 cm on-table** and **+2.4 cm at 3 mo** (p &lt; 0.05).<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Distally based fat flaps** — Deskoulidi 2023.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Silicone spacer** — Li 2006 series.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Efficacy

| Study | n | Technique | Flaccid Δ | Erect Δ | Satisfaction | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Li 2006 *Eur Urol***<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | 42 | SLD ± silicone spacer | +1.3 ± 0.9 cm | NR | **35% overall; 27% in PDD** | 6–78 mo |
| **Deskoulidi-Caminer 2023 *PRS***<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> | 75 | SLD + V-Y + fat flaps | **+2–4 cm** | NR | improved sexual self-esteem | up to 15 yr |
| **Karimian 2026 *Aesthetic Plast Surg***<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 21 | Trans-scrotal SLD | **+2.6 ± 1.66 cm flaccid; +2.4 ± 0.57 cm SPL** | NR | MGSIS significantly improved | not specified |
| **Mertziotis 2013 *Asian J Androl***<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> | 82 | V-Y (35) vs circumcision (47) | similar | NR | **higher in circumcision (p = 0.007)** | not specified |
| **Zhang 2019 *Andrologia***<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> | 15 | SLD + ADM + liposuction | **+2.4 ± 0.8 cm at 3 mo** | NR | 100% satisfied | 3 mo |
| Panfilov 2006 *Aesthet Plast Surg*<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | 88 | SLD + fat transfer | +1.5–4.8 cm (avg 2.42) | NR | NR | not specified |
| **Ramos 2024 cadaveric**<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | 16 | Complete ligamentolysis | **+26.4 mm avg (range 4–60)** | N/A | N/A | N/A |

### Key efficacy patterns

- **Flaccid > erect** length gain — the ligament plays a less significant role in erect length (corpora are already fully distended).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Vyas 2020 SR of 1,192 patients across aesthetic augmentation phalloplasty** — flaccid 8–83% gain; erect 12–53%.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **On-table gain partially lost during healing** due to scar contracture and retraction — 3-mo gain typically 50–60% of intraoperative gain.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Spacer / filler in the infrapubic dead space significantly improves outcomes** by preventing ligament reattachment.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

## SLD combined with penile prosthesis implantation

**Aboul Fotouh 2022 *J Sex Med* prospective RCT** (n = 61) — SLD + pubic lipectomy via Z-plasty during malleable penile prosthesis implantation vs conventional penoscrotal placement<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>:

- SLD group: **+1.5 cm functional penile length** (IQR 0–2) and **+2.5 cm visible length** (IQR 1–3.5) vs 0 cm in controls (p &lt; 0.001).
- Sexual satisfaction, frequency, and self-confidence improved more in SLD group.
- 2-yr length gains preserved.
- Complications were higher in SLD (28.6% vs 9.7%) — primarily wound complications and edema.

### Hardrock Sandwich Technique

**Sertkaya 2025 *Int J Impot Res*** retrospective n = 456 SLD + V-Y + fat injection composite<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>:

- Mean girth +43.1% (+4.51 cm) at POD 1; length +20.8% (+2.17 cm).
- Satisfaction 83.4%, self-confidence 71.1%, self-esteem 75.3%.
- Complications: seroma 6.1%, wound dehiscence 2.3%, infection 2.7%, hematoma 1.4%, skin necrosis 0.2%.

## Complications

SLD carries a **notable complication profile** distinguishing it from non-surgical approaches.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[3]](#ref3)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

### Reported complications

- **Penile retraction / loss of gained length** — most common functional complication; due to scar contracture and ligament reattachment. **6–11%**.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Erection instability** — loss of upward erection angle resulting in a "floppy" or downward-pointing erection. **9.7%** in the prosthesis RCT. Inherent consequence of removing the stabilizing ligament.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Altered erection angle** — downward / horizontal pointing rather than upward, can impair function.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Poor cosmetic appearance** — irregular fat nodules (58%), skin deformity / scarring (33%), scrotalization (33%) in the Wessells 1996 referral series.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **Scrotalization** — scrotal skin advances onto the penile shaft, creating an unsightly appearance and shortening visible penis.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **Wound complications** — 50% in the Wessells 1996 series.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **Sexual dysfunction** — 33% in the same series.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **Hypertrophic scarring** — 51% with V-Y plasty (Mertziotis 2013).<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Glans numbness** — 9.7% in the prosthesis RCT.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **De novo erectile dysfunction** — 3–5% per the PSL-repair literature (Liu 2025).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Penile edema** — common postoperatively (77.4% in one series); typically transient.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Reoperation** — necessary in 50% of patients in the Wessells 1996 referral complication series.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

### Pooled complication rate

Vyas 2020 SR — pooled **complication rate 14.6%**, with combined length + girth procedures having the highest complication rate.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Satisfaction — the central challenge

Patient satisfaction after SLD is **highly variable and often disappointing**, particularly in PDD patients.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[18]](#ref18)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

| Source | Satisfaction |
|---|---|
| **Li 2006 overall** (n=42) | **35%** |
| **Li 2006 PDD subgroup** | **27%** |
| **Wessells 1996 referral complication series** (n=12) | **8% reported subjective length increase** |
| Refined-technique series (fat flaps, spacers, combined approaches) | 75–83% (selection / reporting bias likely) |
| **Vyas 2020 SR** | 50–100% (inconsistent methodology) |

The **disconnect between objective length gains and subjective satisfaction** is recurring. Li et al. concluded SLD "may increase penile length but usually not to a degree that satisfies the patient" and that **men with PDD "should be encouraged to seek psychological help primarily, with surgery reserved as the last resort."**<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Postoperative protocols to prevent retraction

Preventing scar contracture and retraction is critical to maintaining length gains.

- **Penile traction therapy (PTT)** — postoperative use of penile extenders to maintain elongation during healing. Recommended after SLD though not specifically RCT-validated in this setting. See [Penile Traction Therapy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/penile-traction-therapy).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Stay sutures** — temporary glans-to-thigh sutures during early postoperative period.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Spacer placement** — silicone spacer, ADM, or fat flap in the infrapubic dead space to physically prevent reattachment.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[15]](#ref15)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Limitations of evidence

- **No RCTs** comparing SLD to sham surgery or non-surgical alternatives for cosmetic penile lengthening.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Overall low quality of evidence** — 89.7% of penile-augmentation studies are not RCTs.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- **Inconsistent outcome reporting** — different measurement techniques, time points, satisfaction instruments.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Publication bias** — complications likely under-reported; the Wessells 1996 referral series remains one of the few studies specifically documenting them.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **Limited long-term data** — most studies report 3–12 mo; very long-term functional outcomes (erection stability, sexual function) poorly characterized.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **No standardized surgical technique** — approaches vary widely.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Patient-selection criteria are not standardized** — proportion of PDD vs true micropenis vs other indications varies across studies.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

---

## Where SLD fits

SLD remains the **most commonly performed surgical procedure for penile lengthening** and can produce meaningful flaccid length gains, particularly when combined with adjuncts (spacers, fat flaps, skin plasty). However, the procedure carries **significant risks** — erection instability, retraction, poor cosmetic outcomes, and notably low satisfaction in PDD. The **SMSNA 2024 considers these procedures investigational**; a multidisciplinary approach with psychological screening is strongly recommended before any surgical intervention.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[18]](#ref18)[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)[[4]](#ref4)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
