# Monsplasty (Mons Pubis Reduction / Lift)

**Monsplasty** addresses excess skin, fat, and ptosis of the mons pubis through liposuction, direct excision, dermal-fascial suspension, or a combined approach. It is most commonly performed in massive weight loss (post-bariatric) patients as a component of body contouring, alongside abdominoplasty or panniculectomy. The Hamdi 2023 *JPRAS* technique series and the Saheb-Al-Zamani 2022 *Clin Plast Surg* synthesis are the principal references; in men with hidden penis, mons contouring is part of the buried-penis correction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> For positioning versus other female cosmetic options see [Cosmetic Genital Surgery — Female](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/female-cosmetic).

**Society positioning.** The ACOG 2020 Committee Opinion No. 795 states that, in general, the safety and effectiveness of elective female genital cosmetic procedures have not been well documented; high satisfaction in non-validated reports does not establish clinical effectiveness.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> FIGO's 2025 statement considers it ethically inappropriate for obstetrician-gynecologists to recommend, perform, or refer for cosmetic genital surgery sought solely for aesthetic reasons by women without structural or functional abnormalities.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> ACOG advises assessment for body dysmorphic disorder when indicated, and referral before surgery when psychological concerns are suspected.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> When monsplasty is performed for functional indications (hygiene, intertrigo, urinary stream, sexual function, mobility), it is no longer purely cosmetic, and those indications are documented.

---

## Indications

Monsplasty is performed for mons pubis enlargement or ptosis that causes the following problems.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- **Aesthetic concerns.** Visible bulging even under clothing.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Functional impairment.** Difficulty with hygiene maintenance, impaired visualization of the genitalia, sexual dysfunction, skin irritation or intertrigo, urinary-stream alteration.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Psychosocial distress.** Embarrassment, reduced self-esteem, impaired QoL.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

Several monsplasty series enrolled women after massive weight loss, often after bariatric surgery; the operation is also described for age-related, obesity-related, and aesthetic concerns.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> In men with acquired buried penis, pubic contouring is considered when the escutcheon or abdominal pannus contributes to concealment; the repair is planned according to the affected tissue layers (see [buried-penis repair](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/buried-penis-repair)).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## El-Khatib classification of mons ptosis

El-Khatib classifies mons deformity by protrusion and degree of external-genital coverage, with four grades and distinct described approaches.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

| Grade | Findings | Described approach |
|---|---|---|
| **Grade 1** | Mild bulging, minimal or no genital coverage | Liposuction |
| **Grade 2** | Moderate bulging, partial genital coverage | Liposuction-assisted panniculectomy |
| **Grade 3** | Marked bulging and ptosis, complete genital coverage | Liposuction-assisted panniculectomy and dermal-fascial suspension; possible vertical wedge |
| **Grade 4** | Ptosis without bulging, partial or complete genital coverage | Panniculectomy-assisted dermal-fascial suspension and vertical wedge |

**Hamdi 2023** proposes adding monsplasty during abdominoplasty for patients with grade 2 or higher mons ptosis, based on 25 prospective patients assessed at 3 months and 80 retrospective patients with a mean 18-month follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgical techniques

Multiple approaches exist, often used in combination, tailored to the degree of deformity<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>.

| Technique | Indication | Key elements |
|---|---|---|
| **Liposuction alone** | Excess fat with good skin quality and minimal ptosis | Standalone or adjunct; preserves skin envelope; risk of contour irregularity if elasticity poor<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Excisional monsplasty (skin / fat excision)** | Significant skin laxity | Direct excision of redundant skin and subcutaneous fat via horizontal or vertical wedge incisions<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Mons-pubis lift with dermal-fascial suspension** | Moderate–severe ptosis where durable suspension is required | Mons tissues elevated; superficial fibrofatty tissue tacked to the rectus fascia (or musculoaponeurotic system) with suspension sutures, whose material varies by described technique<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[11]](#ref11)[[9]](#ref9)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Superficial fascial system (SFS) suspension (Kitzinger 2014)** | Massive weight loss; integrated with lower body lift | Mons suspended via the SFS; Pittsburgh Rating Scale scores improved significantly (p = 0.03); 82% rated outcomes good or very good<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Combined approach** | Most common, with concurrent abdominoplasty or panniculectomy | Pubic lift + fat excision / liposuction + fascial tacking in a single session<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |

**Operative principles**<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>:

- Excise or suction pubic fat without creating a concavity; under-correction is preferred to over-correction (irreversible).
- Lift excess skin and stabilize the mons tissues to the underlying fascia without excessive elevation that could displace the urethral opening.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> In the Bykowski combined-contouring technique, the lower resection margin is marked with tissues maximally stretched upward, about 6 cm above the anterior vulvar commissure; dissection stays cephalad to the pubic symphysis to avoid genital injury.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- Match dissection depth to the technique. El-Khatib describes removing areolar fat while preserving the lamellar vascular and lymphatic layer; Alter's excisional lift elevates fat off rectus fascia but leaves 1–2 cm of fibrous tissue on the flap for fixation.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Outcomes

| Endpoint | Result | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| **Body-image satisfaction** | Significant improvement (p &lt; 0.001) | Hamdi 2023<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Sexual function** | Sex life improved in 48% of Hamdi's 25 prospective patients at 3 months; sexual functioning also improved (p = 0.009). In Bloom, 51.6% of 31 survey responders reported improved sex life. Bykowski found no significant between-group FSFI change at 3 months (p = 0.11). | Hamdi 2023; Bloom 2012; Bykowski 2017<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Hygiene** | Improved in 32% of Hamdi's 25 prospective patients at 3 months and 61.3% of Bloom's 31 survey responders among 54 eligible women | Hamdi 2023; Bloom 2012<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Visualization of genitalia** | Among Bloom's 31 survey responders of 54 eligible women, improved from 25.8% to 100% (p &lt; 0.01) | Bloom 2012<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Aesthetic appearance** | Self-rated mons appearance 3.18 of 10 preoperatively and 8.58 of 10 postoperatively (p &lt; 0.001) | Bloom 2012<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Urinary symptoms** | At 3 months, median UDI-6 change after abdominal contouring plus monsplasty was −0.01 (n = 20) versus 0 in nonsurgical controls (n = 20; p = 0.03); the effect of monsplasty alone was not isolated. In Bloom's 31 survey responders, self-reported incontinence prevalence fell from 22.6% to 12.9%. | Bykowski 2017; Bloom 2012<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Movement dynamics and clothing fit** | Good to very good across cohort | Marques 2012<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Added mons correction at abdominoplasty** | In a retrospective 30 versus 30 comparison from different treatment eras, the combined group scored better in 6 of 11 BODY-Q domains and had greater photographic mons elevation with less loss of elevation at 12 months; 3/30 developed seroma. | Simone 2022<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |

## Complications

Small monsplasty series report mostly minor complications<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>, but Bykowski observed one postoperative DVT followed by an upper-abdominal hematoma during therapeutic anticoagulation among 20 women undergoing combined abdominal contouring and monsplasty; the mons-specific contribution was not isolated.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

| Complication | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Temporary edema** | Most common minor complication.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Wound dehiscence** | Rare. |
| **Seroma / hematoma** | Drains may mitigate. |
| **Infection** | Rare; one case of infected fascia-suture granuloma requiring removal reported in Kitzinger 2014.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Altered genital sensitivity** | Sensitivity improved in 24% of Hamdi's 25 prospective patients at 3 months and 32.3% of Bloom's 31 survey responders among 54 eligible women.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Scar concerns** | Low-transverse incision typically concealed by underwear; document scar location preoperatively. |
| **Vascular / lymphatic disruption** | Avoid overly aggressive inguinal fat excision; Alter describes genital lymphedema as a theoretical risk.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **Over-resection / concavity** | Prevented by mild under-correction.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

## Combined / sequenced procedures

Monsplasty may be performed alone or combined with abdominoplasty or panniculectomy, according to the abdominal deformity and patient preference.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Most common combinations<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>:

- **With abdominoplasty or panniculectomy.** Hamdi and colleagues propose adding monsplasty for El-Khatib grade 2 or higher ptosis based on their nonrandomized cohorts.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **With lower body lift.** SFS-suspension approach (Kitzinger 2014).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **With buried-penis repair.** Pubic contouring is integral to correcting hidden penis in men.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> See [buried-penis-repair](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/buried-penis-repair).
- **With labia majora reduction.** Post-bariatric patients may need correction of both regions.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> Alter's technique stages open labia majora reduction when abdominoplasty or panniculectomy is performed and cautions against simultaneous labial skin excision after prior medial thigh lift because perfusion may be compromised.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> See [labia-majora-reduction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/labia-majora-reduction).
- **With fat grafting to the mons.** When prior over-resection or constitutional volume deficit produces a concave mons. See [fat-grafting-mons](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/fat-grafting-mons).
- **In a reported combined cosmetic series.** A 2013 retrospective cohort combined mons liposuction with labia minora reduction, labia majora fat transfer, laser brightening and optional vaginal tightening; wound dehiscence occurred in 10/124 patients (8.1%).<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

## Limitations

- ACOG 2020 does not name monsplasty; it states that, in general, the safety and effectiveness of elective female genital cosmetic procedures have not been well documented.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- The evidence is small series and cohorts. Hamdi 2023 included 25 prospective and 80 retrospective patients, Bloom 2012 had 31 survey responders of 54 eligible, and Bykowski 2017 compared 20 operated patients with 20 controls at 3 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **No RCTs** have compared liposuction alone, excisional monsplasty, dermal-fascial suspension, and SFS suspension head-to-head.
- Long-term recurrence of ptosis is poorly characterized. Fascial suspension is intended to prevent it, but follow-up was 12–38 months in El-Khatib's series and 6–31 months (mean 16.9) in Kitzinger's SFS series.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Postoperative management

- **Activity restriction.** Furnas and colleagues' expert aftercare for monsplasty/majoraplasty delays demanding high-impact exercise for 6 weeks and saddle activities, including cycling, for at least 8 weeks. Their intercourse and tampon limits are procedure-specific: 4–6 weeks after labiaplasty and 6–8 weeks after perineoplasty or vaginoplasty, depending on healing.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **Wound care.** Compression garment for 4–6 weeks; ice 24–48 h; loose-fitting clothing; antibiotic ointment to incision.
- **Drains.** Suction drains may be placed after larger excisional or combined procedures to limit fluid collection; El-Khatib left them for 2 days, while Simone normally removed them at 4–6 days after abdominoplasty with monsplasty.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[9]](#ref9)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Smoking cessation** preoperatively when feasible; combined procedures and lipo-aided pubic lifts are particularly sensitive to nicotine-driven wound complications. Document recent weight-loss magnitude and rate: in a 2026 retrospective abdominal-body-contouring cohort, larger and faster weight loss were associated with more seroma, without isolating mons surgery.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Follow-up.** 1 week (wound check, drain removal), 2 weeks, 6 weeks (clearance, garment off), 3–6 months (final outcome). Follow-up may be extended to 12 months to detect ptosis recurrence.
- **PRO assessment.** UDI-6, FSFI, and FGSIS at baseline and ≥ 6 months; the mons-appearance rating used by Bloom 2012 can be added.

---

## Key takeaways

- Series report low morbidity and improved patient-reported appearance and function after monsplasty; comparative data are lacking.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Hamdi and colleagues propose monsplasty during abdominoplasty for El-Khatib grade 2 or higher ptosis, based on nonrandomized cohorts with limited follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Functional indications (hygiene, sexual function, urinary symptoms, mobility) are the strongest indications and are documented when insurance coverage is sought.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- Tissue deep to Scarpa's fascia is preserved, and over-resection or concavity is avoided.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
