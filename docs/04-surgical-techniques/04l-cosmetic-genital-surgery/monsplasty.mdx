# Monsplasty (Mons Pubis Reduction / Lift)

**Monsplasty** addresses excess skin, fat, and ptosis of the mons pubis through liposuction, direct excision, dermal-fascial suspension, or a combined approach. It is most commonly performed in massive weight loss (post-bariatric) patients as a component of body contouring, alongside abdominoplasty or panniculectomy. The Hamdi 2023 *JPRAS* technique series and the Saheb-Al-Zamani 2022 *Clin Plast Surg* synthesis are the principal references; in men with hidden penis, mons contouring is part of the buried-penis correction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> For positioning versus other female cosmetic options see [Cosmetic Genital Surgery — Female](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/female-cosmetic).

**Society positioning.** The ACOG 2020 Committee Opinion No. 795 notes that aside from labia minora reduction, the safety and effectiveness of female genital cosmetic procedures have not been well documented; high satisfaction in non-validated reports does not establish clinical effectiveness.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The FIGO 2025 Statement echoes this position. ACOG advises assessment for body dysmorphic disorder when indicated, and referral before surgery when psychological concerns are suspected.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> When monsplasty is performed for functional indications (hygiene, intertrigo, urinary stream, sexual function, mobility), it is no longer purely cosmetic, and those indications are documented.

---

## Indications

Monsplasty is performed for mons pubis enlargement or ptosis that causes the following problems.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- **Aesthetic concerns.** Visible bulging even under clothing.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Functional impairment.** Difficulty with hygiene maintenance, impaired visualization of the genitalia, sexual dysfunction, skin irritation or intertrigo, urinary-stream alteration.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Psychosocial distress.** Embarrassment, reduced self-esteem, impaired QoL.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

The most common population is women after massive weight loss (≥ 50 lbs), particularly post-bariatric, though monsplasty is also performed for age-related changes, obesity, and aesthetic indications. In men, mons excess contributes to buried (hidden) penis, which requires pubic contouring as part of the correction (see [buried-penis-repair](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/buried-penis-repair)).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## El-Khatib classification of mons ptosis

The El-Khatib 2011 classification grades ptosis based on adipose deposit and degree of skin redundancy, guiding surgical planning<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>:

| Grade | Findings |
|---|---|
| **Grade 1** | Mild fullness without significant ptosis |
| **Grade 2** | Moderate ptosis with excess skin and fat |
| **Grade 3** | Severe ptosis with significant tissue redundancy |

**Hamdi 2023** recommends monsplasty as a standard component of abdominoplasty for patients with Grade 2 or higher mons ptosis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgical techniques

Multiple approaches exist, often used in combination, tailored to the degree of deformity<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>.

| Technique | Indication | Key elements |
|---|---|---|
| **Liposuction alone** | Excess fat with good skin quality and minimal ptosis | Standalone or adjunct; preserves skin envelope; risk of contour irregularity if elasticity poor<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Excisional monsplasty (skin / fat excision)** | Significant skin laxity | Direct excision of redundant skin and subcutaneous fat via horizontal or vertical wedge incisions<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Mons-pubis lift with dermal-fascial suspension** | Moderate–severe ptosis where durable suspension is required | Mons tissues elevated; superficial fibrofatty tissue tacked to the rectus fascia (or musculoaponeurotic system) with permanent sutures<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[11]](#ref11)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **Superficial fascial system (SFS) suspension (Kitzinger 2014)** | Massive weight loss; integrated with lower body lift | Mons suspended via the SFS; Pittsburgh Rating Scale scores improved significantly (p = 0.03); 82% rated outcomes good or very good<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Combined approach** | Most common, with concurrent abdominoplasty or panniculectomy | Pubic lift + fat excision / liposuction + fascial tacking in a single session<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |

**Operative principles**<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>:

- Excise or suction pubic fat without creating a concavity; under-correction is preferred to over-correction (irreversible).
- Lift excess skin and stabilize the mons tissues to the underlying fascia.
- Preserve tissue deep to Scarpa's fascia to avoid disrupting vasculature and lymphatic drainage (commonly taught technical point; no comparative data).

## Outcomes

| Endpoint | Result | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| **Body-image satisfaction** | Significant improvement (p &lt; 0.001) | Hamdi 2023<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Sexual function** | Self-reported improvement in 48% (Hamdi, n = 25; p = 0.009) and 51.6% (Bloom, n = 31 responders); FSFI unchanged at 3 months in Bykowski (p = 0.11) | Hamdi 2023; Bloom 2012; Bykowski 2017<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Hygiene** | Improved in 32% (Hamdi) to 61.3% (Bloom) | Hamdi 2023; Bloom 2012<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Visualization of genitalia** | Improved from 25.8% to 100% in one series (p &lt; 0.01) | Bloom 2012<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Aesthetic appearance** | Self-rated mons appearance 3.18 of 10 preoperatively and 8.58 of 10 postoperatively (p &lt; 0.001) | Bloom 2012<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Urinary function** | UDI-6 decreased in the surgical group (n = 20) vs nonsurgical controls (n = 20) at 3 months (p = 0.03); incontinence fell from 22.6% to 12.9% in Bloom's survey responders | Bykowski 2017; Bloom 2012<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Movement dynamics and clothing fit** | Good to very good across cohort | Marques 2012<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |

## Complications

Series report low morbidity and no major complications<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>:

| Complication | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Temporary edema** | Most common minor complication.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Wound dehiscence** | Rare. |
| **Seroma / hematoma** | Drains may mitigate. |
| **Infection** | Rare; one case of infected fascia-suture granuloma requiring removal reported in Kitzinger 2014.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Altered genital sensitivity** | Sensitivity improved in 24% (Hamdi) and 32% (Bloom) of patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Scar concerns** | Low-transverse incision typically concealed by underwear; document scar location preoperatively. |
| **Vascular / lymphatic disruption** | Preserve tissue deep to Scarpa's fascia. |
| **Over-resection / concavity** | Prevented by mild under-correction.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

## Combined / sequenced procedures

Monsplasty is rarely performed in isolation outside isolated liposuction cases. Most common combinations<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>:

- **With abdominoplasty or panniculectomy.** Standard component for Grade 2+ mons ptosis (Hamdi 2023).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **With lower body lift.** SFS-suspension approach (Kitzinger 2014).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **With buried-penis repair.** Pubic contouring is integral to correcting hidden penis in men.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> See [buried-penis-repair](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/buried-penis-repair).
- **With labia majora reduction.** Post-bariatric patients with concurrent ptosis of both regions.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> See [labia-majora-reduction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/labia-majora-reduction).
- **With fat grafting to the mons.** When prior over-resection or constitutional volume deficit produces a concave mons. See [fat-grafting-mons](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/fat-grafting-mons).
- **In genital-beautification packages.** Combined with labiaplasty, labia majora augmentation, brightening and vaginal tightening.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

## Limitations

- ACOG 2020 notes that safety and effectiveness of cosmetic vulvar surgery, including monsplasty, have not been well documented.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- The evidence is small series and cohorts. Hamdi 2023 included 25 prospective and 80 retrospective patients, Bloom 2012 had 31 survey responders of 54 eligible (57.8%), and Bykowski 2017 compared 20 operated patients with 20 controls at 3 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **No RCTs** have compared liposuction alone, excisional monsplasty, dermal-fascial suspension, and SFS suspension head-to-head.
- Long-term recurrence of ptosis is poorly characterized. Fascial suspension is intended to prevent it, but follow-up in the published series is 12–38 months.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

---

## Postoperative management

- **Activity restriction.** Typical institutional regimens (not from controlled data). Avoid intercourse, tampon use, cycling, and strenuous exercise for 4 weeks; with combined or post-bariatric procedures extend to 4–6 weeks.
- **Wound care.** Compression garment for 4–6 weeks; ice 24–48 h; loose-fitting clothing; antibiotic ointment to incision.
- **Drains.** Suction drains are commonly placed for 48–72 h after larger excisional / combined procedures to reduce dead space and seroma risk.
- **Smoking cessation** preoperatively when feasible; combined procedures and lipo-aided pubic lifts are particularly sensitive to nicotine-driven wound complications.
- **Follow-up.** 1 week (wound check, drain removal), 2 weeks, 6 weeks (clearance, garment off), 3–6 months (final outcome). Follow-up may be extended to 12 months to detect ptosis recurrence.
- **PRO assessment.** UDI-6, FSFI, and FGSIS at baseline and ≥ 6 months; the mons-appearance rating used by Bloom 2012 can be added.

---

## Key takeaways

- Series report low morbidity and improved patient-reported appearance and function after monsplasty; comparative data are lacking.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Hamdi 2023 supports incorporating monsplasty as a standard component of abdominoplasty for Grade 2+ mons ptosis (El-Khatib).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Functional indications (hygiene, sexual function, urinary symptoms, mobility) are the strongest indications and are documented when insurance coverage is sought.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- Tissue deep to Scarpa's fascia is preserved, and over-resection or concavity is avoided.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
