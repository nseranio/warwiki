# Glans Augmentation with Hyaluronic Acid

**Glans penis augmentation (GPA) with hyaluronic acid (HA)** is the office injection of cross-linked HA gel into the dermis of the glans, either to enlarge a glans the patient considers small or, in premature ejaculation (PE), to reduce glans sensitivity. Kim, Moon and colleagues in Seoul reported the first human series in 2003: 2 mL of HA gel injected into the glans of 187 men increased maximal glans circumference by about 15 mm at 1 year.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The evidence consists mainly of small single-center series and a few small randomized trials.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The AUA/SMSNA guideline classes bulking-agent injection for PE as experimental, and the ISSM guideline does not recommend glans augmentation with HA for PE.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

Shaft girth enhancement, HA material properties and hyaluronidase reversal are covered on [Hyaluronic Acid Filler — Penile Girth Augmentation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/hyaluronic-acid-filler), which also summarizes the randomized HA-versus-PLA girth trials by [Dae Yul Yang](/docs/roots/surgeons/s-z/dae-yul-yang) and colleagues. Positioning among other male cosmetic procedures is on [Male Cosmetic Genital Surgery](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/male-cosmetic).

---

## Indications and patient selection

- **Cosmetic glans enlargement.** Reported targets are a subjectively small glans, glans–shaft disproportion and poor glans tumescence during erection.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Girth enhancement of the shaft can leave a thick shaft with a relatively small glans. In Kim 2003, 87 of the 187 men had a small glans after dermofat-graft girth enhancement.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Glans after penile prosthesis.** A small glans and lack of glans tumescence after penile prosthesis implantation are described indications for GPA.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> No HA series in prosthesis patients was identified. The reported prosthesis cases used polyacrylamide gel (2 patients, short-lived effect requiring reinjection) or a dermofat graft (Shaeer's glans augmentation).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Premature ejaculation.** A review concluded that patients with lifelong PE can mainly benefit from injectable fillers rather than surgical grafting.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> In a 2016 survey of Korean urologists who performed GPA for PE, 16 of the 22 respondents represented in the indications table (72.7%) reported selecting men who benefited from topical anesthetics, and 13 of 22 (59.1%) reported selecting men after failed pharmacotherapy.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> Most PE trials enrolled circumcised men with lifelong PE, and several excluded men with erectile dysfunction.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

**Screening for penile dysmorphic disorder.** Men requesting cosmetic enlargement often have normal dimensions; see [Small Penis Syndrome](/docs/clinical-conditions/03g-genital-scrotal/small-penis-syndrome). The SMSNA 2024 position statement by [Trost](/docs/roots/surgeons/s-z/landon-trost) and colleagues (expert opinion) requires that penile dysmorphic disorder be ruled out before invasive enhancement.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> For shaft girth, EAU 2026 recommends against HA and other soft-tissue fillers in men with penile dysmorphic disorder (strong).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> For PE, EAU 2026 recommends treating erectile dysfunction, other sexual dysfunction or genitourinary infection first (strong).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Anatomy and rationale

The glans is covered by stratified squamous epithelium over a dense dermis-like connective tissue that blends into the tunica albuginea of the corpus spongiosum. Free nerve endings lie in almost every dermal papilla. Genital end bulbs are present throughout the glans and are most numerous at the corona and near the frenulum. The main branches of the dorsal nerve within the glans lie 3 to 6 mm from the epithelial surface.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

In the preclinical work, HA gel injected into the glans dermis of rabbits and dogs lay in the lamina propria (and in rabbits also the corpus spongiosum). The implants were maintained at 6 months in dogs without foreign-body reaction.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> For PE, the proposed mechanism is a bulking barrier between tactile stimuli and the nerve terminals. Glans vibratory threshold measured by biothesiometer rose after GPA.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> The originators also proposed a contribution from increased self-confidence after glans enlargement, which the studies cannot separate from the sensory effect.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Technique

No standardized protocol exists. The elements below are taken from the published series.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

1. **Anesthesia.** Topical lidocaine–prilocaine (EMLA) applied 30 minutes before injection is tolerated by most patients; a few need local anesthetic injection.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Lidocaine 2% jelly for 30 minutes was used in one randomized trial.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
2. **Product and needle.** The originators used a large-particle HA gel (Perlane) through a 27 G needle into the deep dermis, with a small-particle gel (Restylane) through a 30 G needle to correct surface undulation.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Later trials used 30 G needles with other cross-linked HA products.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
3. **Plane.** Deep dermis of the glans. In the authors' view, injection that is too superficial exaggerates surface undulation and can cause pressure necrosis because glans skin is inelastic, while injection that is too deep may give inadequate augmentation or sensory effect and risks deep vascular compromise.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
4. **Volume.** Most protocols use 2 mL in one session.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> To avoid discoloration or pressure necrosis from initial overinjection, Kim and colleagues recommended 2 mL initially and supplementary injection at 2 weeks.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
5. **Distribution.** The glans surface has small folds from the underlying rete ridges, so the augmented surface is uneven at rest; the minor undulation disappears during erection.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

### Described injection patterns

| Pattern | Description | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Linear threading | Multiple linear passes | Abandoned by the originators: too many punctures, with mucosal tearing, bleeding and leakage<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Fan (Kim 2004) | Needle inserted at one-third of the distance from glans tip to coronal sulcus; gel fanned from that entry | Fewer punctures; ventral frenular area and coronal margin difficult to fill<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Multiple puncture (Abdallah 2012) | Entry points from the proximal third along the coronal sulcus and frenulum, 0.25 mL per point | The authors expected more uniform distribution and described less pain from experience. Injection took longer; bruising was reported in 7 cases. In the 49 completers, complications occurred in 7/26 multiple-puncture and 7/23 fan patients, with no significant IELT difference at 1 or 3 months.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| Two circular levels (Alahwany 2019) | 0.2 mL deep-dermal aliquots, 6 at the corona and 4 midway between corona and meatus, 30 G, 2 mL total | Used in the saline-controlled crossover trial<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[17]](#ref17)</sup> |
| Three circles (Littara 2013) | Three rings 1 cm apart from the glans base, each divided into quarters; 12 deep-dermal injections | Used in a 110-patient series<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| Five puncture (Ibrahim 2024) | Glans divided into five sectors; 0.4 mL per sector, 2 mL total, 30 G | Used in a randomized trial against dapoxetine<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |

No trial has shown one pattern to be more effective than another.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Outcomes

### Cosmetic enlargement

In Kim 2003, net maximal glans circumference increased by 14.93 mm in 100 men with mixed concerns about glans size, penile size or early ejaculation and by 14.78 mm in 87 men with a small glans after dermofat girth grafting, measured by tape at 1 year. More than 50% of the injected volume was judged by the patients to remain in 95% and 100% of the two groups, and 77% and 69% were satisfied.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> A 2024 meta-analysis pooled an increase in glans circumference of 14.82 mm (95% CI 12.75–16.90) across PE studies.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The IELT gain peaks at about 1 month and then attenuates while remaining above baseline in the series that report later visits.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[20]](#ref20)[[9]](#ref9)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In the controlled crossover trial the effect was not present at 1 week; among the 20 men who had improved at 1 month, the median IELT fell from 120 seconds at 1 month to 45 seconds at 9 months, still significantly above the 34-second baseline. Because only responders were followed beyond 1 month, these later values describe the men who benefited, not the whole cohort.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> The only placebo-controlled pooled estimate is 65 seconds at 1 month, much smaller than the uncontrolled pre–post estimate.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In Zhang 2022, men with acquired PE had higher IELT and larger satisfaction gains than men with lifelong PE.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

**Comparison with drug therapy.** In randomized trials summarized by the AUA/SMSNA guideline, on-demand dapoxetine 30 or 60 mg increased IELT 2.5- to 3.0-fold.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Daily paroxetine produced a mean 8.8-fold increase in a 2004 meta-analysis.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[22]](#ref22)</sup> These figures come from different designs and IELT methods and are not a head-to-head comparison with the 2.6-fold median in Alahwany 2019.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> The two randomized trials against dapoxetine both favored HA; both were small (n = 100 analyzed and n = 60).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

## Durability and retreatment

In the 5-year follow-up of 38 of the original 65 HA-only recipients, maximal glans circumference was 15% lower than at 6 months, while the patients' visual estimate of glans size did not change (grade 3.60 versus 3.56).<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> The originators attribute the slow loss to isovolumetric degradation of cross-linked HA and describe supplementation by reinjection when volume is lost.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> A narrative review describes reinjection every 9 to 12 months for PE.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> A second narrative review notes that GPA requires retreatment over time, unlike selective dorsal neurectomy.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> No standardized retreatment interval has been tested.

## Complications

**Pooled rates.** In the PE meta-analysis, adverse events occurred in 91 of 598 patients (15.22%): pain 7.69%, bulla or nodule 4.18% and ecchymosis 3.34%.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In the dapoxetine trial, injection-site pain (14%), bullae (8%) and ecchymosis (6%) resolved within 10 to 15 days.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> In the saline-controlled crossover trial, 6 of 30 men (20%) had adverse effects 1 week after HA (local discomfort in 3, glans ecchymosis in 2 and a blanched papule from too-superficial injection in 1), all resolved by 1 month; saline caused ecchymosis in 1.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> Glans discoloration from swelling usually resolved within 2 weeks in Kim 2003.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Contour.** Poor longevity and surface undulation are the main limitations of HA GPA in a review of glans augmentation complications.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

**Sensation.** The ISSM recommendation rests on possible permanent loss of sexual function.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> In Kim 2004, no sensory loss was reported among 65 men after GPA alone, whereas numbness, paresthesia, neuroma pain or Peyronie's disease occurred in 10 of 74 men after dorsal neurectomy.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> In the survey of Korean urologists (4,344 procedures), respondents recalled glans pain or paresthesia in 36 cases (0.8%), no erectile dysfunction and PE recurrence in 206 (4.7%).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> These figures are recalled by the injecting surgeons, not measured.

**Vascular compromise.** Glans ischemia after HA has been reported. A 29-year-old man developed blackening of the distal glans with a demarcation line one day after 2 mL of HA by multiple puncture (0.2 mL per injection).<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> It reversed after hyaluronidase, aspirin 100 mg, warm saline dressings and nitroglycerin cream, without necrosis.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> A 65-year-old man with glans necrosis and ulceration after HA injection for genital augmentation underwent partial penectomy.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup> The originators state that most glans necrosis they have seen followed non-HA or unpurified fillers or mismanagement of immediate discoloration. With HA, ischemic necrosis can follow injection that is too superficial, too large in volume or intravascular.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

**Other fillers and grafts.** Fillers that hyaluronidase cannot dissolve remove the main safety margin. Pressure necrosis followed overinjection of acellular dermal fragments, which can be neither aspirated nor digested, and complications followed suspected polyacrylamide injection.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Indirect GPA with dermofat grafts or scaffolds placed between the corpus spongiosum and the corporal tips forms hard, painful lumps over time in most patients, and the blunt dissection can cause glans necrosis.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> Paraffin, silicone and other non-medical injectables can cause necrosis and autoamputation; see [Non-Autologous Injectables](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/non-autologous-injectables).<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

### Suspected vascular occlusion

No genital-specific protocol has been published. The sequence below is extrapolated from facial-filler practice; the cited glans ischemia case report describes genital treatment.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)[[29]](#ref29)[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

1. **Recognize.** Facial signs are blanching, livedo reticularis, slow capillary refill and dusky blue-red discoloration, followed days later by blisters and slough.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>
2. **Stop injecting** and start treatment at once.
3. **Hyaluronidase.** The facial consensus recommends high doses (at least 200 U) without a skin test, with further hyaluronidase if there is no improvement within 60 minutes.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup> Moon and colleagues described 75 U intradermally or 150 U for filler lumpiness; those doses were not validated for glans ischemia.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> The glans ischemia case report does not establish a genital dosing regimen.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>
4. **Adjuncts.** Warm compresses, massage and oral aspirin; topical nitroglycerin paste is used by some experts but is controversial.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)[[28]](#ref28)</sup>
5. **Follow up daily.** In the facial consensus, patients diagnosed and treated within 24 hours usually had the best outcomes.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup>

Hyaluronidase use for filler dissolution is off-label; product details are on the [girth page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/hyaluronic-acid-filler#reversibility-with-hyaluronidase).

## Regulatory status and society positions

- **FDA.** HA dermal fillers have product-specific approvals for facial and hand indications. The FDA recommends against injectable fillers for body contouring and enhancement. Glans injection is off-label.<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup>
- **AUA/SMSNA (2020 guideline, published 2022), Statement 15.** "Clinicians should inform patients that surgical management (including injection of bulking agents) for premature ejaculation should be considered experimental and only be used in the context of an ethical board-approved clinical trial" (Expert Opinion). The discussion notes that surgery could cause permanent loss of penile sensation and that the Alahwany trial was small and single-center.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **ISSM (2014 update).** Selective dorsal nerve neurotomy or glans penis augmentation with HA gel "may be associated with permanent loss of sexual function and is not recommended in the management of PE."<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The originators of GPA called this a misinterpretation of their data, since no permanent complications were reported after GPA alone.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **EAU (2026).** Use HA injection with caution as a treatment option for PE compared with more established modalities (weak). The summary of evidence states that HA injections decrease penile sensitivity (level 2b). The text warns that the procedure may cause serious complications and that more safety studies are needed before it can be recommended.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **SMSNA (2024).** The cosmetic penile enhancement position statement (expert opinion) requires penile dysmorphic disorder to be ruled out before invasive treatment. For temporary HA and PLA girth fillers, limited data suggest potential cosmetic benefit and an acceptable safety profile; the society urges clinicians to study safety and efficacy under IRB-approved protocols.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **BAUS AGUS (2026).** Its consensus recommends against absorbable injectable fillers for elective penile augmentation in adults without structural or functional penile abnormalities because high-quality supporting evidence is absent.<sup>[[33]](#ref33)</sup>
- **Reviews.** A 2022 treatment review and a 2024 narrative review did not recommend surgical treatment for PE because safety and efficacy data were limited; the EAU 2026 guideline above gives HA injection a cautious weak recommendation.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)[[23]](#ref23)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Evidence gaps

- The meta-analysis included 4 RCTs, of which only 2 were placebo-controlled, and heterogeneity was high (I² 82–83%).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Products, volumes, injection patterns and retreatment intervals are not standardized.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- IELT methods differ between studies. Retrospective reporting, questionnaires and open designs give far more variable ejaculation times than stopwatch measurement.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- Follow-up is mostly 12 months or less; the only 5-year data come from 38 men.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- Sensory outcomes are rarely measured objectively. Vascular compromise has been reported in case reports<sup>[[25]](#ref25)[[26]](#ref26)</sup> and in a retrospective PE series, whose authors recorded regional vascular embolism in 2/31 and skin necrosis in 1/31 glans-injection recipients.<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup>
- No HA data exist for glans augmentation after penile prosthesis.
No trial cited by the AUA/SMSNA guideline compares HA with topical anesthetics, which the guideline lists among first-line PE pharmacotherapies (Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
