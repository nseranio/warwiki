# Polylactic Acid Filler — Penile Girth Augmentation

**Polylactic acid (PLA) filler** is a biostimulatory injectable used for penile girth enhancement. Unlike [hyaluronic acid (HA)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/hyaluronic-acid-filler), which gives immediate volume, PLA is proposed to induce endogenous neocollagenesis, producing a more gradual augmentation that may last longer.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> It is the second most studied injectable filler for penile augmentation after HA, with the most rigorous comparative data from a series of multicenter RCTs.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> For positioning versus other male cosmetic options see [Cosmetic Genital Surgery — Male](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/male-cosmetic).

**Society positioning.** The SMSNA 2024 expert-opinion statement describes limited evidence of possible cosmetic benefit with temporary HA and PLA fillers, urges IRB-approved safety and efficacy analyses when they are offered, and strongly recommends against permanent filler materials.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> It also states that body dysmorphic disorder must be ruled out before invasive treatments.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> For adults without structural or functional penile abnormalities, the 2026 BAUS consensus recommends against absorbable fillers because high-quality supporting evidence is absent.<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup> The 2026 EAU guideline weakly recommends soft-tissue fillers for penile girth enhancement in selected men but strongly recommends against their use in men with penile dysmorphic disorder.<sup>[[33]](#ref33)</sup> Sculptra (PLA filler) is FDA-approved for HIV-associated facial lipoatrophy and certain facial wrinkles; penile use is off-label.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)[[28]](#ref28)</sup>

---

## Mechanism — biostimulatory vs volumetric

PLA is a synthetic, biocompatible, biodegradable polymer that functions as a biostimulatory scaffold rather than a conventional space-filling filler.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

Dermal cell, human 3D skin-model and aged-animal skin studies suggest the following responses to PLA formulations; the cited studies did not examine penile tissue.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

1. **Subclinical foreign body response.** PLA microparticles elicit a controlled inflammatory tissue response with M2 macrophage polarization.
2. **Fibroblast activation.** Macrophage-mediated upregulation of TGF-β1, TGF-β2, IL-1β, and CXCL6 activates resident fibroblasts.
3. **Neocollagenesis.** Stimulated fibroblasts produce type I and type III collagen, creating new volume through autologous tissue rather than exogenous material.
4. **Gradual degradation.** PLA microparticles metabolized to CO₂ and H₂O via the Krebs cycle and expelled through the respiratory system; the newly formed collagen persists.

This mechanism explains the distinct temporal profile: gradual onset of augmentation but potentially longer-lasting results, since the volume is composed of the patient's own collagen rather than an exogenous gel that undergoes enzymatic degradation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## FDA approval and regulatory status

Sculptra (poly-L-lactic acid) was FDA-approved in 2004 for HIV-associated facial lipoatrophy and in 2009 for cosmetic correction of nasolabial-fold contour deficiencies and other facial wrinkles in immunocompetent patients.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)[[27]](#ref27)[[28]](#ref28)</sup> The 18-month penile RCT used PowerFill, a different PLA formulation; penile use has no US FDA indication.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> In facial applications, therapeutic effects last approximately 2 years.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Clinical evidence

The penile-PLA evidence includes a 23-man prospective series and four multicenter, patient- and evaluator-blinded HA-versus-PLA RCTs, three led by Yang and one by Ahn.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

### Yang 2017 — foundational PLA monotherapy

23 healthy men, mean injected volume 20 mL.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- Proximal, mid- and distal-shaft circumference increased by mean +2.2, +2.7, +2.7 cm at 3 months (each p &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Among 21 of 23 men assessed at 18 months, mean girth remained above baseline; the 3-to-18-month difference was not statistically significant (p &gt; 0.05).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- VAS satisfaction rose from 51.6 at baseline to 64.8 at 3 months and 74.3 at 6 months (each p &lt; 0.05).
- Six mild, transient adverse events occurred in 5 subjects; no serious adverse events were reported.

### Key comparative findings

- **Early augmentation:** HA &gt; PLA at 4 weeks (p &lt; 0.001); HA's volumetric effect is immediate while PLA's collagen-stimulating mechanism is gradual.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Convergence over time:** the difference narrowed and was no longer significant at 48 weeks (p = 0.075). Resorption of HA and gradual collagen stimulation by PLA are the proposed explanations; neither was measured.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **At 18 months:** both fillers maintained augmentation above baseline in one RCT, with no statistically significant between-group girth difference.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> A 2023 meta-analysis of four small trials (283 participants) found greater pooled girth gain with HA; pain and inflammation rates did not differ significantly.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>
- **Satisfaction.** The 24-week RCT found no between-group difference in changes from baseline in appearance or sexual-life satisfaction, or in Beliefs about Penis Size Scale scores, at 24 weeks.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> In the 18-month RCT, the change from baseline in appearance satisfaction differed between groups at 3 months (p = 0.028), but not at 18 months (p = 0.114); PLA appearance satisfaction declined from 6 to 18 months (p = 0.039), with 46/67 assessed at 18 months.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> A four-trial meta-analysis found greater sexual satisfaction with HA at 12 weeks (p = 0.0004).<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>
- **Psychological benefits:** both fillers significantly reduced BPSS scores (5.3–7.8 points, p &lt; 0.05).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Durability — comparative evidence through 18 months

Durability is the proposed distinction between PLA and HA, and the penile data do not yet confirm it beyond 18 months:

- **PLA:** a 23-man prospective series (21 assessed at 18 months) and a separate 67-man RCT (46 assessed at 18 months) followed penile girth through 18 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> In the RCT, mean gain declined by 0.51 cm (22.1%) from its 1-month peak; this within-group change was not statistically significant (p = 0.123), and 46/67 participants completed 18-month follow-up.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **HA:** flaccid girth gain peaked at 1 month and progressively attenuated, retaining approximately 70–75% of that peak gain at 12 months among 38 completers.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

By the proposed biostimulatory mechanism, newly formed collagen persists after the PLA microparticles degrade. Facial-application data report PLLA effects lasting up to 2 years or longer.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> Whether this same durability extends beyond 18 months in penile tissue is unknown; no longer-term penile-augmentation data exist.

## Safety

Adverse-event rates after penile PLA injection were similar to HA in the randomized trials.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[3]](#ref3)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- **PLA injection-related AE rates were 5.9–14.3%** across four RCTs with 24-week to 18-month follow-up; reported events were mild, but two PLA recipients in one 24-week trial still had AEs at trial end.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[25]](#ref25)</sup>
- **No serious adverse events** were reported in the randomized trials through 18 months; a later three-case penile PLA-based-filler report described delayed granulomas, with surgical excision in 2/3 patients.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
- No statistically significant difference in AE rates versus HA (p = 0.245).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### Reported AEs in penile PLA injection

- Injection-site discomfort / pain.
- Transient edema.
- **Subcutaneous nodules** may be palpable without being visible; contour deformity has also been observed. A 2025 nonrandomized, single-surgeon cohort reported nodules or penile deformities at 24 weeks in 7/134 PLA recipients (5.2%), 2/125 HA recipients (1.6%), and 3/42 PMMA recipients (7.2%); the three-group comparison was not significant (p = 0.107).<sup>[[35]](#ref35)</sup>
- Bruising.

### Considerations from facial-PLA literature (potentially applicable)

- **Papules and nodules.** Most commonly discussed PLA-specific complication. In facial applications, arise several weeks after injection, generally palpable but non-visible, typically resolve spontaneously. Result from suboptimal product reconstitution or placement; minimized by improved technique.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **True inflammatory granulomas.** A 2014 review quoted 0.01–0.1% for true inflammatory granulomas after injectable PLLA.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> The review describes these reactions as potentially systemic; they may appear months to years after injection and may persist or grow.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> Treatment: intralesional steroids and antimetabolites (5-fluorouracil).<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Late-onset immune-mediated reactions.** Alijotas-Reig 2009 case series of 10 non-HIV patients: mean latency 19.2 months (range 6–60) to onset of delayed adverse effects (tender inflammatory nodules, facial edema). After a mean 50.2 months of follow-up (range 38–78): 5/10 in remission, 4/10 with recurrent episodes, and 1/10 lost to follow-up.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Wortsman 2025 international ultrasound study.** Among 240 selected patients with ultrasound-confirmed filler granulomas, 18.8% had received PLLA (versus 50.4% HA). Without exposure denominators, these proportions cannot estimate incidence or compare material-specific risk.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

**Non-reversibility: distinction from HA.** Unlike HA (which can be dissolved with [hyaluronidase](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/hyaluronic-acid-filler)), PLA cannot be enzymatically reversed.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> If nodules, asymmetry, or overcorrection occur, management options are limited to observation, intralesional corticosteroids, 5-fluorouracil, or surgical excision.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> Non-reversibility makes precise injection technique and patient selection more important for PLA than for HA. Final result is not fully apparent until weeks to months after injection, which makes overcorrection more difficult to predict.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Injection technique

The specific PLA injection technique for penile augmentation is less well-described than for HA. General principles from the RCTs and facial-application literature<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[3]](#ref3)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>:

- **Volume:** mean approximately 20 mL.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Plane:** In the 18-month PowerFill RCT, filler was placed between the dartos and Buck's fascia by fanning from penile-base entry sites.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Reconstitution.** PowerFill is supplied as a powder; the 18-month penile RCT reconstituted it with 7 mL of saline immediately before injection.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Facial Sculptra sources discuss higher-volume dilution and hydration before injection for that formulation; those protocols were not tested for penile PowerFill.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- **Post-injection care:** the cited 2014 facial PLLA consensus recommends massaging the injected area for a few minutes after treatment and leaves continued self-massage to physician discretion.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> A separate 2023 facial PLLA consensus describes optional “5-5-5” self-massage (5 minutes, 5 times daily, for 5 days).<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup> The 18-month penile RCT instructed gentle shaft massage, an elastic support bandage for one day, and abstinence from intercourse for one month; the facial schedule was not its protocol.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Treatment sessions:** facial PLA is often administered over 2–4 sessions spaced 4–6 weeks apart to allow gradual collagen buildup and avoid overcorrection. The penile-augmentation studies used a single injection session.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[19]](#ref19)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations of current evidence

- **Single research group.** Nearly all penile-PLA data come from Yang et al., limiting generalizability.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **High dropout rates.** Up to 43.1% in the 48-week RCT.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **No data beyond 18 months** for penile augmentation; the theoretical advantage of longer durability remains unproven in this specific application.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **No standardized penile injection protocol.** Reconstitution volumes, dilution ratios, and injection techniques are not standardized for penile use.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- **Nodule or granuloma risk in penile tissue is unknown.** Penile tissue has unique biomechanical properties (repeated stretching, compression, friction) that may affect PLA microparticle behavior differently than facial tissue.
- **Non-reversibility** limits the safety margin compared to HA, particularly in an off-label application without established guidelines.
- **Overall low quality of evidence.** Manfredi 2022 narrative review noted 89.7% of penile-girth-enhancement studies are not RCTs.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

## Where PLA fits — clinical decision-making

**Patient selection.** Exclude penile dysmorphic disorder before invasive augmentation; the 18-month PLA trial also excluded men with body dysmorphic disorder.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> In eligible men, PLA may be considered when they:

- Understand that comparative durability remains uncertain: in one RCT, the loss of peak girth gain from 1 to 18 months was 22.1% with PLA versus 43.6% with HA (p = 0.015), but the change in mean flaccid girth from baseline did not differ significantly between groups at 18 months and retreatment was not studied.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Accept the gradual onset of augmentation (weeks to months rather than immediate).
- Understand and accept non-reversibility.

**HA may be chosen when patients:**

- Desire immediate visible results.
- Value the safety net of reversibility with hyaluronidase.
- Are undergoing penile augmentation for the first time and wish to "trial" the effect before committing to a non-reversible option.

In the 18-month randomized trial, both fillers increased girth and satisfaction; the between-group girth difference at 18 months was not statistically significant, adverse events were mild, and no serious adverse events were reported.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> A separate randomized trial followed men for 24 weeks.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
