# Female Cosmetic Genital Surgery (FGCS)

**Female genital cosmetic surgery (FGCS)** is the elective, aesthetic end of female genital surgery. It includes labiaplasty, clitoral hoodoplasty, labia majora augmentation, monsplasty, surgical vaginoplasty or energy-based devices, hymenoplasty, and regenerative and fat-grafting techniques. Reconstructive operations for trauma, oncologic resection, lichen sclerosus, FGM/C, or congenital anomaly live at [04e Genital Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction); gender-affirming pathway surgery lives at [04k Gender-Affirming Surgery](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery).

> **Society positions and evidence-quality caveat.** The ACOG 2020 Committee Opinion states that procedures to alter sexual appearance or function, other than those done for clinical indications, are not medically indicated, pose substantial risk, and have not had their safety and effectiveness established. The FIGO 2025 Statement holds that it is ethically inappropriate for obstetrician-gynecologists to recommend, perform or refer patients for cosmetic genital procedures in women without structural or functional abnormalities.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The FDA 2018 safety communication warned against energy-based devices for "vaginal rejuvenation" and cosmetic indications; no such device is FDA-cleared for these uses.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The SMSNA 2024 position statements on cosmetic penile enhancement are expert opinion and note the limited research on these procedures.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> Screening for **body dysmorphic disorder (BDD)** comes first in every consultation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Decision Framework

Professional societies advise caution about these procedures (see the box above). The outcome literature reports high satisfaction, but it consists mainly of retrospective series and meta-analyses of them.

- **Labiaplasty meta-analyses.** Pooled satisfaction was 99% (95% CI 97–99%) in Escandón 2022 (46 studies, 3,804 patients) and 94% (95% CI 93–95%) in Géczi 2024 (53 studies). Pooled dehiscence was 5.9% in Géczi 2024.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Symptoms.** In a prospective series of 62 patients (82% trim, 18% wedge), the mean number of symptoms fell from 6.5 of 11 to 0.23, and 93.5% were symptom-free at a mean of 13.3 months (Sorice-Virk 2020).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Psychological outcomes.** In a prospective controlled study (29 labiaplasty patients, 22 controls), genital appearance dissatisfaction fell, but general psychological well-being did not change. Higher preoperative psychological distress was associated with lower satisfaction (p = 0.001) (Sharp 2016).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Mixed genital procedures.** A retrospective multicenter study of 258 women (341 procedures, including vaginoplasty and perineoplasty) reported 91.6% satisfaction at 6–42 months (Goodman 2010).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Clitoral hood.** In a series of 789 patients treated by a zone-based classification, satisfaction was 95.7%, complications 4.3% and revision 1.9% (Liu 2022).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Vaginal laxity.** In a systematic review, energy-based devices improved sexual function in observational studies but not in randomized trials (Pereira 2024).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

### Mandatory Pre-Procedural Assessment

| Assessment | Action |
|---|---|
| **Counseling on normal anatomic variation** | Reassure that vulvar size, shape, and color vary widely and are further modified by puberty, aging, childbirth, and menopause. Reassurance about normal anatomy is the first step (ACOG 2020).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Distinguish functional vs purely cosmetic indication** | Document specific symptoms (chafing, tugging, pain with exercise / intercourse, hygiene). In a retrospective series of 414 women, 52.4% reported functional impairment, 20.3% aesthetic concerns and 27.3% both.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> In Sorice-Virk 2020 (n = 62), patients averaged 6.5 of 11 symptoms preoperatively.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **BDD screening** | Use a validated tool (BDDQ-AS or BDD-YBOCS). If positive, refer for psychiatric evaluation before surgery.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> BDD affects about 2.5% of US adults, and its prevalence is higher among patients seeking cosmetic surgery.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Psychological-distress assessment** | Higher preoperative distress was associated with lower postoperative satisfaction (Sharp 2016, n = 29, p = 0.001).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| **Informed consent** | Discuss investigational nature per ACOG / FIGO; potential complications (pain / bleeding / infection / scarring / adhesions / altered sensation / dyspareunia / reoperation); lack of high-quality evidence for most procedures. |

### Identify the Clinical Goal

| Patient Concern | Primary Procedure Category | Evidence Level |
|---|---|---|
| **Labia minora protrusion (functional ± aesthetic)** | Labiaplasty (trim, wedge or de-epithelialization) | Moderate (meta-analyses of predominantly retrospective series) |
| **Clitoral-hood redundancy** | Clitoral hoodoplasty (usually combined with labiaplasty) | Low–Moderate (large case series; Liu 2022 classification) |
| **Labia majora atrophy / deflation** | Fat grafting to labia majora | Low (case series) |
| **Labia majora excess (post-massive weight loss)** | Labia majora reduction (excision) | Very Low (case reports) |
| **Mons pubis ptosis / excess** | Monsplasty (excision ± liposuction ± fat grafting) | Very Low (case series) |
| **Vulvovaginal atrophy (GSM)** | Microfat / nanofat grafting; or energy-based devices via the canonical [Vaginal Laser Therapy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/vaginal-laser-therapy) page | Low–Moderate |
| **Hymen restoration** | Hymenoplasty | Very Low (single retrospective series) |
| **G-spot enhancement** | G-spot amplification (HA injection) | Virtually none; safety and effectiveness not established |

### Technique Selection by Procedure Category

#### Labiaplasty: Trim vs Wedge vs De-Epithelialization

| Factor | Trim Resection | Wedge Resection | De-Epithelialization |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best for** | Irregular / darkened edge; all morphologies | Central protrusion; preserve natural edge / color transition | Mild hypertrophy; preserve volume |
| **Pooled satisfaction (Géczi 2024)** | 96% (edge resection) | 96% | 97% (5 studies; 95% CI 85–99%) |
| **Pooled dehiscence** | Not among the highest | 3% (Escandón 2022) to 8% (Géczi 2024) | Not among the highest |
| **Nicotine or cocaine use** | Favored<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | Not favored<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | Not addressed in the algorithm |
| **Edge appearance** | Scar along free margin | Preserves natural edge | Preserves natural edge |
| **Surgeon experience needed** | Lower | Higher | Moderate |
| **Decision rule** | Zahedi 2023 algorithm: the patient wants the darkened or irregular edge removed<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | Zahedi 2023 algorithm: the patient wants the natural edge and color transition preserved<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | Mild hypertrophy with a wish to preserve volume (not part of the Zahedi algorithm) |

#### Energy-Based Devices for GSM / "Rejuvenation"

Energy-based devices (fractional CO₂, Er:YAG, RF ± PEMF) are not FDA-cleared for cosmetic vaginal "rejuvenation" or for GSM (FDA 2018 Safety Communication). In the double-blind, sham-controlled Li 2021 *JAMA* RCT (85 postmenopausal women), fractional CO₂ laser did not significantly improve vaginal symptoms at 12 months.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> Uncontrolled series report benefit, and the discrepancy may reflect a placebo effect. A systematic review of vaginal-laxity treatments found improved sexual function in observational studies but not in randomized trials.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> FIGO 2025 states that research data are insufficient to support the safety and effectiveness of laser devices for GSM or cosmetic purposes,<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> and ESSM 2020 reached a similar conclusion. Cost is high ($1,800–9,000 per CO₂ course) and not insurance-covered. For full cross-indication evidence (GSM / SUI / lichen sclerosus / vaginal laxity / breast-cancer survivors) see the canonical [Vaginal Laser Therapy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/vaginal-laser-therapy) page.

### Combined Procedures

Multiple procedures are frequently performed simultaneously to achieve a harmonious result. Combined procedures increase operative time, anesthetic exposure and cumulative complication risk, and patients are counseled accordingly.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

- **Labiaplasty and clitoral hoodoplasty.** This is the most common combination. Concomitant hoodoplasty is done to avoid residual hood redundancy after isolated labiaplasty (Liu 2022; Eserdağ 2021 inverted-Y; Xia 2021 three-step composite).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[16]](#ref16)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- **Labiaplasty and labia majora augmentation.** This addresses the labia majora–to–minora ratio and allows more conservative labial reduction. In Hersant 2018 (n = 21), one patient had a small edge separation and 81% were satisfied or very satisfied.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- **"Genital beautification" packages.** These combine labia minora reduction, labia majora augmentation and mons liposuction, with or without laser brightening or vaginal tightening (Cihantimur 2013, n = 124, wound dehiscence in 8.1%; Toplu 2021; Cihantimur 2021 360 fat transfer, n = 97).<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

### Special Decision Branch — Clitoral Hoodoplasty

Frequently performed concurrently with labiaplasty. The Liu 2022 *Aesthet Plast Surg* classification divides the hood into a central zone (C) and a lateral zone (L) and defines five types (standard, central hypertrophy, lateral hypertrophy, composite hypertrophy, special). It guides technique selection.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

| Anatomy | Technique | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Vertical and / or horizontal central redundancy** | Inverted-Y plasty (Eserdağ 2021 n = 63)<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> | 96.9% satisfaction at 2 mo |
| **Horizontal redundancy** | Bilateral triangular skin resection | Per Liu classification |
| **Vertical redundancy** | Inverted horizontal V-shaped resection | Per Liu classification |
| **Composite (lateral hood + labia minora)** | Three-step excision (Xia 2021 n = 136)<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> or two-part excision (Duan 2026 n = 68)<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> | Xia: 95.5% satisfaction at 3 mo, complications 4.4%, revision 3.7%. Duan: complications 7.4%, revision 1.5%, GAS scores improved |
| **Liu 2022 classification-based (any anatomy)** | Tailored technique per zone<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | 95.7% satisfaction; complications 4.3%; revision 1.9% (n = 789) |

**Safety note.** The clitoris must be carefully identified and protected during all hoodoplasty procedures to avoid injury to the dorsal nerve of the clitoris, which can cause permanent loss of sensation.

### Postoperative Management

- **Activity restriction**: avoid intercourse, tampon use, and strenuous exercise for 4–6 weeks.
- **Wound care**: sitz baths, topical antibiotic ointment, loose-fitting clothing.
- **Ice application**: first 48–72 hours to reduce edema.
- **Follow-up cadence**: 1 week (wound check), 6 weeks (clearance for activity), 3–6 months (final outcome assessment).
- **PRO assessment**: FSFI at baseline and 6 months postoperatively; FGSIS or GAS at baseline and 6 months.
- **Maintenance counseling**: HA filler will resorb over time; energy-based device maintenance sessions may be required.

### Complication Management

| Complication | Incidence | Management |
|---|---|---|
| **Wound dehiscence** | 3–8% (highest pooled rates with wedge and laser)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | Conservative; secondary closure if needed |
| **Asymmetry** | 1.4–6.3%<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | Observation (may improve with edema resolution); revision if persistent |
| **Hematoma** | 0.5–8% (highest pooled incidence with W-shape resection)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | Observation if small; drainage if expanding |
| **Infection** | Rare | Antibiotics; wound care |
| **Flap necrosis (wedge)** | Very rare (3 cases in a meta-analysis of 3,804 patients)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | Debridement; secondary healing or revision |
| **Altered sensation** | Reported but not quantified | Usually transient; permanent loss rare |
| **Over-resection** | Rare | Revision with local flaps or grafts; prevention depends on conservative resection |
| **Patient dissatisfaction despite normal outcome** | 1.6–8.9%<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> | Re-evaluate for BDD, refer for psychiatric evaluation and avoid repeat procedures |
