# V-I Penoscrotal Reconfiguration (Bagnara)

The **V-I penoscrotal reconfiguration** is a flap-free technique for selected children with congenital webbed penis (CWP). An inverted-V skin incision, skin mobilization off dartos, and midline I closure lower the web and recreate the penoscrotal angle without tissue excision or a designed transposition flap.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Bagnara et al. (2024) described it in a retrospective series of 21 children, and Z-plasty is the comparator technique discussed on this page.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> For the broader webbing-correction toolkit see [Penoscrotal-Web Correction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/penoscrotal-web-correction); for the male cosmetic decision framework see the [Male Cosmetic Genital Surgery atlas page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/male-cosmetic).

---

## Background

Congenital webbed penis is grouped with buried and trapped penis as a concealed or inconspicuous penis, and it belongs to the broader group of penoscrotal positional anomalies.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> In the Bagnara retrospective series (n = 21), CWP was isolated in 24%, associated with phimosis in 52%, hypospadias in 14%, and both in 10%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**El-Koutby severity grading** (Bagnara series distribution):<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Grade | Description | n (%) |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Mild — scrotal skin to proximal shaft | 5 (24%) |
| **2** | Moderate — scrotal skin to mid-shaft | 5 (24%) |
| **3** | Severe — scrotal skin to distal shaft / coronal sulcus | 11 (52%) |

---

## Indications

| Setting | Best for / indications |
|---|---|
| **Congenital webbed penis** | Bagnara et al. treated grades 1–3 in children with intact foreskin, including mild glanular or coronal hypospadias; acquired webbing, prior circumcision, and other penile malformations were excluded. The authors advocate childhood correction, but prevention of adult sexual or psychological problems was not measured.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **CWP + phimosis** | In 13/21 children (62%) with phimosis, 12 underwent circumcision and one dorsal preputioplasty after V-I reconfiguration.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **CWP + hypospadias** | Five of 21 children (24%; three with isolated hypospadias and two with both hypospadias and phimosis) underwent concurrent distal hypospadias repair.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Adult aesthetic webbing** | Not studied. The Thomas & Navia algorithm proposes a penoscrotal junction Z-plasty for adult webbing, based on 11 mostly case-report articles.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |

---

## Principle

Bagnara et al. mark an inverted V at the penile–scrotal skin boundary, incise the skin, detach skin and subcutaneous tissue from dartos with bipolar dissection, slide the skin triangle toward the scrotum, and close the cutaneous edges in a longitudinal midline I.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> No flap is designed, no tissue is excised, and no geometric transposition is required; the technique relies on dartos-band release and the native elasticity of penoscrotal skin.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Stepwise Operative Protocol<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

1. **Positioning / anesthesia.** Supine; all 21 procedures in the index series were performed under a caudal block.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
2. **Mark and test the V.** Place the penis on gentle stretch. Mark the penile–scrotal skin boundary as an inverted V of about 45–60° and mark the scrotal raphe. Place midline traction stitches at the V apex and lower raphe, then temporarily approximate the V limbs to check the new angle, ventral skin tension, and sleeve compression before incision.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
3. **V-incision.** Incise the skin along the marked inverted V, then enter the skin–dartos plane for bipolar dissection.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
4. **Skin mobilization.** Use bipolar dissection to separate skin and subcutaneous tissue from dartos, then slide the triangular skin segment toward the scrotum.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
5. **Reconfigure to I-closure.** Advance the released scrotal skin inferiorly and approximate the wound edges along a single vertical midline, converting the V-defect into a straight ventral midline scar.
6. **Closure.** Approximate the cutaneous margins in the midline with interrupted 4-0 or 5-0 absorbable poliglecaprone 25 monofilament sutures; address any dog-ears.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
7. **Concurrent procedures.** After V-I reconfiguration, address coexisting phimosis with circumcision or dorsal preputioplasty when indicated. The reported concomitant hypospadias repairs were limited to distal forms.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Technical points:** no designed flap transposition, no tissue excision, and no complex geometry. Check ventral skin tension and sleeve compression before incision; the described correction depends on mobilizing skin off dartos, native skin elasticity, and tension-free midline closure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Outcomes (Bagnara 2024, n = 21)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Postoperative complications | 0/21 reported through 12 months |
| Redo surgery | **0%** |
| Parent satisfaction score 4 (Likert) | 81% (17/21) |
| Parent satisfaction score 3 (Likert) | 19% (4/21) |
| Cosmetic outcome | Excellent in all cases |
| Follow-up schedule | 2 wk, 1 mo, 6 mo, 12 mo — physical + psychological assessment at each visit |

The series included a psychological assessment at each follow-up visit; the instrument is not described in the abstract.

|---|---|---|---|
| Complexity | Simple — no flap design | Moderate — precise flap geometry | Simple — raphe incision |
| Flap transposition | None | Required | None |
| Risk of flap-tip necrosis | None | Present | None |
| Tissue excision | None | Minimal | None |
| Reported postoperative events | 0/21 in the V-I series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 1/100 suture-line separation requiring secondary healing and 1/100 hypertrophic scar across mixed pediatric indications (overlap not specified).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | None reported among 11 webbed-penis cases; 2 mild lymphatic-stasis cases occurred in the buried-penis group.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Follow-up outcome | No redo surgery reported in the V-I series; follow-up visits scheduled through 12 mo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | All patients completing follow-up (97/100) had stable corrections at final evaluation, 1–2 yr.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | No recurrence among 11 webbed-penis cases; all patients observed for at least 1 yr.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Scar orientation | Linear vertical midline | Oblique / reoriented | Along scrotal raphe |
| Lengthening mechanism | Tethering release and elastic redistribution | Geometric flap transposition (a 60-degree Z-plasty has a theoretical 75% scar-length gain; actual gain may be lower).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | Tethering release via raphe |
| Severe (Grade 3) webbing | Included (52% of series).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Not graded by El-Koutby in the cited series | Not graded in the cited series |
| Evidence level | Level IV (single series, n = 21).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Level IV (mixed-indication series, n = 100).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | Level IV (n = 11 webbed-penis cases within a mixed concealed-penis series).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |

## Advantages and Limitations

**Advantages.** The authors report a simple technique without flap design, tissue preservation, use across all El-Koutby grades including Grade 3, and no complications or redo surgery in the index series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Absence of flap-related complications follows from the lack of a flap; reproducibility outside the originating center has not been shown.

**Limitations.**

- Single-center, single-series evidence (n = 21); no independent replication or head-to-head comparison with Z-plasty.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Pediatric only; not validated for adult aesthetic webbing, where the Thomas & Navia algorithm proposes Z-plasty.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- No follow-up beyond 12 months; durability through puberty or adulthood unknown.
- Correction depends on tissue release and elasticity, without the defined geometric gain of Z-plasty (a theoretical 75% scar-length gain for a 60-degree Z-plasty; actual gain may be lower).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> It may be less effective in scarred or inelastic tissue (expected from the mechanism, not reported).
- Parent-reported outcomes only; no validated patient-reported instruments.

---

## Postoperative Care

- Follow-up at 2 weeks, 1 month, 6 months, and 12 months, with both physical examination and psychological assessment at each visit.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Absorbable sutures; no removal required.
- Activity restrictions appropriate to the pediatric population.

---

## Positioning in the Surgical Armamentarium

V-I reconfiguration is a flap-free option for congenital webbed penis. Other non-Z-plasty options include the Borsellino scrotal-raphe approach (no recurrence in 11 webbed-penis cases at ≥ 1 year) and various degloving-based penoplasty techniques.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> Álvarez Vega 2025 reported 100 pediatric Z-plasty procedures across several indications (41 hypospadias revisions, 28 primary hypospadias repairs, 10 chordee corrections, 21 others) with 98% primary flap healing.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The Thomas & Navia algorithm proposes Z-plasty for adult penoscrotal webbing, where V-I has not been evaluated.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
