# Central Wedge Resection Labiaplasty

The **central wedge labiaplasty** removes a V-shaped (or pie-slice) full-thickness segment from the most protuberant portion of the labium minus, then reapproximates the anterior and posterior labial segments, preserving the native free edge with its natural color, contour, and texture. First described by Alter in 1998, with the extended ("hockey-stick") modification that incorporates a lateral clitoral-hood reduction published in *Plast Reconstr Surg* in 2008, it is one of the two most commonly used labiaplasty techniques, alongside the trim.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> For other female cosmetic options, see [Cosmetic Genital Surgery: Female](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/female-cosmetic).

**Society positioning.** ACOG Committee Opinion No. 795 (2020) states that procedures to alter sexual appearance or function, other than those performed for clinical indications, are not medically indicated, pose substantial risk, and have no established safety or effectiveness. It advises counseling on the lack of high-quality data and on the risks of pain, bleeding, infection, scarring, adhesions, altered sensation, dyspareunia and reoperation, and assessment for body dysmorphic disorder (BDD) when indicated.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The FIGO 2025 statement holds that it is ethically inappropriate for obstetrician-gynecologists to recommend, perform or refer patients for cosmetic genital surgery.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> BDD screening and counseling on normal anatomic variation belong at the start of every consultation. For adolescents under 18, ACOG Committee Opinion No. 686 states that labiaplasty in girls younger than 18 years should be considered only for significant congenital malformation, persistent symptoms the physician believes are caused directly by labial anatomy, or both.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Definition and concept

The central wedge excises a full-thickness V or pie-slice segment from the widest portion of the labium. The apex points toward the base (interlabial sulcus); the two arms of the V meet the free edge. The remaining anterior and posterior labial segments are then sutured together, shortening the labium while preserving the native free edge in continuity.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The defining trade-off versus the trim is edge handling: the wedge preserves the native edge; the trim removes it.

---

## Indications

The wedge technique suits patients in whom one or more of the following apply<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>:

- **Good-quality labial edge.** Normal color and smooth contour, when the patient wants the natural margin preserved.
- **Focal central protrusion.** The redundancy is concentrated centrally rather than diffusely along the entire labium.
- **Concurrent lateral clitoral-hood redundancy.** Addressed simultaneously via the extended ("hockey-stick") wedge that carries the outer arm of the V anteriorly into the lateral clitoral hood.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Non-smokers.** Wedge closure depends on flap vascularity. In one retrospective cohort (n = 40), all three dehiscences followed wedge resection in active smokers.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> The Zahedi algorithm treats nicotine or cocaine use as a reason to prefer the trim.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

**Zahedi 2023 algorithm.** The algorithm favors a wedge for good edge quality, focal central protrusion and edge-preservation goals, and a trim for a hyperpigmented or irregular edge, diffuse protrusion, or active nicotine or cocaine use.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Surgical technique

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| **1. Anesthesia** | Local with epinephrine, with or without sedation, or general. In a small single-surgeon retrospective series (n = 32), outcomes were comparable between local and general anesthesia, so the choice can be patient-led.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **2. Marking** | A V- or pie-slice wedge is marked at the most protuberant portion of the labium with the apex toward the interlabial sulcus and the two arms meeting the free edge. Wedge width determines the degree of reduction. Mark conservatively so the anterior and posterior segments approximate without tension. |
| **3. Hockey-stick extension (when indicated)** | The lateral arm of the V is curved anteriorly and laterally to extend along the lateral labium and into the **lateral clitoral hood**, allowing simultaneous hood reduction. Almost all patients in Alter's series had some lateral hood excision.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Triana describes isolated labial reduction without addressing concurrent hood excess as leaving disharmony in the area.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| **4. Excision** | The wedge is excised **full-thickness** through both medial and lateral mucosal surfaces and the intervening tissue. |
| **5. Hemostasis** | Bipolar electrocautery; suture ligation as needed. |
| **6. Multilayer closure** | A two- or three-layer closure (deep absorbable sutures and superficial skin closure) is preferred. Single-layer closure was associated with dehiscence in a retrospective series (p = 0.050).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Typical material is 4-0 or 5-0 polyglactin or chromic gut. Anterior and posterior labial segments are reapproximated to reconstitute the labial edge in continuity. |
| **7. Contralateral side** | Repeat with deliberate intraoperative comparison for symmetry. |

## Variants and modifications

| Variant | Description | Key feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Central wedge (Alter 1998)** | Simple V-shaped full-thickness excision from the most protuberant portion | Preserves edge; no hood reduction<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Extended ("hockey-stick") central wedge (Alter 2008)** | Lateral arm of the V curves anteriorly into the lateral clitoral hood | Simultaneous labia minora and lateral hood reduction<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Inferior wedge with superior pedicle flap** | Wedge taken inferiorly with the labium based on a superior pedicle | Reproducible; reliable vascularity in selected anatomies<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Modified wedge with non-parallel closure (Qiang 2021)** | Angled incisions creating non-parallel closure lines | Aimed at reducing scar contracture and dehiscence; case-control, n = 119<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Three-step composite excision (Xia 2021)** | Sequential resection: clitoral hood → labial wedge → junction triangle | Systematic approach to combined hood and labia reduction (n = 136; 95.5% overall satisfaction at 3 months)<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Upper- vs lower-pedicle flap (Yang 2020)** | Pedicle direction selected by hypertrophy pattern (length-dominant vs width-dominant) | Individualized to morphology<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Modified wedge for composite reduction (Shi 2026)** | Wedge with concurrent clitoral-hood reduction in a single procedure | Retrospective, n = 738; 99.2% aesthetic satisfaction; 96.2% overall satisfaction; delayed healing 3.3%; perceived asymmetry 1.4%<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |

## Outcomes

### Aesthetic and patient-reported outcomes

- **Pooled satisfaction** across labiaplasty techniques is 94% to 99% in meta-analyses of predominantly observational studies. Neither meta-analysis identifies a technique that is clearly superior on satisfaction alone.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Alter 2008 single-surgeon series (n = 407).** Almost all patients had some lateral clitoral hood excision with the hockey-stick extension. Reoperation rate was 2.9% (12/407). Among the 166 questionnaire respondents, mean satisfaction was 9.2 of 10, 93% reported improved self-esteem, 71% improved sex life and 95% improved discomfort, and 98% would undergo the surgery again. The significant complication rate was 4%.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Shi 2026 modified wedge for composite reduction (retrospective, n = 738; mean follow-up 1.9 months).** Preoperative symptoms improved in 99.3%; aesthetic satisfaction 99.2%; overall satisfaction 96.2%; delayed healing 3.3%; perceived asymmetry 1.4%.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Ucar 2025 retrospective cohort (n = 40).** Wedge produced greater improvement than trim in the FSFI subdomains of arousal, orgasm and satisfaction, and trim produced greater improvement in FGSIS scores.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Minikowski 2025 RCT (n = 48).** Wedge versus trim showed no significant difference in genital self-image improvement, BDD-symptom reduction, or complications at 6 months.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Zulfikaroglu 2026 sexual-function meta-analysis.** Six FSFI studies, mostly uncontrolled pre-post cohorts, showed a moderate improvement (SMD 0.52; 95% CI 0.38–0.65) across techniques. No technique showed clear superiority (low certainty).<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

## Complications

### Dehiscence: the dominant wedge-specific risk

Wound dehiscence is the principal disadvantage of the wedge and the main complication that distinguishes it from the trim:

- **Escandón 2022 systematic review and meta-analysis (46 studies, 3,804 patients).** The highest pooled dehiscence rates were with laser-assisted labiaplasty (5%; 95% CI 2–8%) and wedge resection (3%; 95% CI 1–5%).<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Géczi 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis.** Pooled wedge dehiscence was 8% (95% CI 5–13%), elevated relative to other techniques.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Köle 2024 Turkish multicenter retrospective study (n = 2,594; data from surgeon interviews).** Wedge complication rate 3.0% (26/753) versus trim 0.5% (7/1,323); complete dehiscence was the most common complication overall (29% of complications) and was most frequent after wedge (16 cases).<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

### Risk factors for wedge dehiscence (Sinnott 2020)

In a retrospective series of 77 patients treated by six surgeons in one group practice and followed for a mean of 37.4 months, postoperative asymmetry or redundancy occurred in 12 (revision in 10) and dehiscence in 12 (revision in 9). Risk factors for dehiscence were<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>:

- Single-layer wound closure (p = 0.050).
- Concurrent mons liposuction (p = 0.011; performed in 4 patients).

Smoking was not among the Sinnott risk factors. In Ucar 2025, all three dehiscences occurred in active smokers.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### Other complications

| Complication | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Flap necrosis** | Reported after wedge resection. Two of three flap-necrosis cases in a meta-analysis of 3,804 patients followed wedge resection; the third technique was not specified.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| **Hematoma** | Uncommon; meticulous hemostasis at the closure layer. One hematoma requiring evacuation in the Sinnott 2020 series.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **Infection** | Infrequent; none in the Nwaoz 2021 series.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **Asymmetry / residual redundancy** | The most common reason for revision in the Sinnott 2020 series.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **Scar contracture** | Addressed by modified non-parallel closure (Qiang 2021).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Decreased sensation** | One case in the Sinnott 2020 series.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **Dyspareunia** | One case in the Sinnott 2020 series.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |

## Wedge vs trim: comparative summary

| Parameter | Wedge resection | Trim (edge) resection |
|---|---|---|
| **Native edge** | Preserved | Removed |
| **Best for** | Focal central protrusion; good edge quality; concurrent lateral hood redundancy | Hyperpigmented / irregular edge; diffuse protrusion; smokers |
| **Aesthetic satisfaction (FGSIS)** | Significant improvement; lower than trim in Ucar 2025; equivalent in Minikowski 2025 RCT<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[16]](#ref16)</sup> | Significant improvement<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Sexual function (FSFI)** | Greater improvement in arousal, orgasm, satisfaction subdomains<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | Significant improvement |
| **BDD-symptom reduction (RCT)** | Equivalent to trim<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> | Equivalent to wedge<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| **Dehiscence (meta)** | 3% to 8% pooled<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> | Not among the techniques with elevated pooled dehiscence<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| **Multicenter complication rate** | 3.0% (26/753)<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | 0.5% (7/1,323)<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| **Flap necrosis** | Reported (rare)<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> | Not applicable |
| **Smokers / cocaine users** | Dehiscences clustered in smokers in one cohort<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | Favored by the Zahedi algorithm<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Scar location** | Hidden on medial / lateral surface | Along new free margin |
| **Surgeon experience needed** | Higher (precise design and multilayer closure) | Lower |
| **Reoperation (Alter 2008, 407 pts)** | 2.9%<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | Variable |

## Postoperative management

- **Activity restriction.** Intercourse, tampon use, cycling and strenuous exercise are usually avoided for 4–6 weeks (common practice; the sources do not specify an interval). Early activity stresses the wedge closure.
- **Wound care.** Sitz baths starting 24–48 h postoperatively, topical antibiotic ointment, loose-fitting clothing.
- **Edema control.** Ice / cold compresses for 48–72 h; elevation.
- **Smoking cessation.** Counsel cessation before surgery. If the patient cannot stop, the Zahedi algorithm favors the trim.
- **Follow-up.** 1 week (wound check, watch for early dehiscence), 2 weeks, 6 weeks (clearance), 3–6 months (final outcome).
- **PRO assessment.** FSFI and FGSIS at baseline and at 6 months or later.
- **Counseling on edema and final result.** The final aesthetic result is assessed at about 3–6 months, when edema has resolved.

## Technical pearls for reducing dehiscence

Drawn from Sinnott 2020, the meta-analyses and operative practice<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>:

- **Multilayer closure.** Single-layer closure was associated with dehiscence in Sinnott 2020.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Tension-free approximation.** Design the wedge to avoid an over-wide excision that leaves the closure under tension. If the planned reduction would need a wide wedge, consider staged reduction or a different technique.
- **Mons liposuction.** Avoid it concurrently when feasible; it was a risk factor for dehiscence in Sinnott 2020.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Nicotine and cocaine use.** Screen preoperatively. If use is active, the algorithm favors the trim; alternatively, defer surgery until cessation.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Non-parallel closure lines** (Qiang 2021) have been described to reduce scar contracture and dehiscence.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Meticulous hemostasis.** Hematoma at the closure layer drives breakdown.
- **Postoperative restriction counseling.** Premature return to intercourse, cycling or strenuous exercise stresses the closure.
