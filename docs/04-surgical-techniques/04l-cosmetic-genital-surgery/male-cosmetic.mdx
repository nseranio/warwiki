# Male Cosmetic Genital Surgery

Male cosmetic genital surgery is the elective or aesthetic end of male genital surgery: augmentation phalloplasty, girth enhancement, suspensory-ligament division, suprapubic lipectomy, Penuma, and scrotal aesthetic procedures. Reconstructive operations for trauma, oncologic resection, lichen sclerosus, FGM/C, or congenital anomaly are covered at [04e Genital Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction); gender-affirming pathway surgery is covered at [04k Gender-Affirming Surgery](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery). For the clinical-conditions framework on small penis syndrome (SPS) or penile dysmorphic disorder (PDD) see [Small Penis Syndrome](/docs/clinical-conditions/03g-genital-scrotal/small-penis-syndrome).

> **Society positions and evidence-quality caveat.** The SMSNA 2024 position statement is expert opinion, limited by sparse research. It covers injectable fillers, suspensory-ligament division, graft-and-flap procedures, silicone sleeve implants and sliding/slicing techniques.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Systematic reviews rate the study quality as low and describe augmentation procedures as investigational.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> For comparison, ACOG Committee Opinion No. 795 (2020) states that procedures to alter sexual appearance or function, other than those done for clinical indications, are not medically indicated.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> FIGO (2025) holds that it is ethically inappropriate for obstetrician-gynecologists to recommend, perform or refer patients for cosmetic genital surgery.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> The FDA 2018 safety communication warned against energy-based devices for "vaginal rejuvenation" or cosmetic indications and reported no cleared device for these uses.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Screening for body dysmorphic disorder (BDD) and penile or genital dysmorphic disorder belongs at the start of every consultation.

## Decision Framework

Planning starts with psychological screening, then objective measurement, then first-line non-invasive therapy, then selected invasive options. The main sources follow.

- **SMSNA 2024** issued expert-opinion position statements on invasive procedures (Trost).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Romero-Otero 2021** (*BJU Int*) systematically reviewed 57 studies in 4,351 men. Study quality was low, and standardized criteria for efficacy, safety and satisfaction were missing.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Vyas 2020** (*Plast Reconstr Surg*) reviewed augmentation phalloplasty in 1,192 patients (16 articles). The pooled complication rate was 14.6%, highest with combined length and girth procedures.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Wessells 1996** (*J Urol*) described 12 men with complications of augmentation done elsewhere, and **Furr 2018** (*J Sex Med*) described 11 referral-center patients with severe complications.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Elist 2018** (single surgeon), **Siegal 2023** (single center) and **Wilson 2022** (multicenter update) report on Penuma, the only FDA-cleared cosmetic penile device (510(k) clearance).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Yang 2019 and 2020** are randomized trials of HA versus PLA fillers.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- **Toussi 2021** (*J Urol*) is a randomized trial of penile traction therapy after prostatectomy.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- **Mansfield 2020, Thomson 2024 and Zhang 2025** inform BDD screening.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

### Mandatory Psychological Screening

| Assessment | Tool | Action if Positive |
|---|---|---|
| **BDD screening** | **BDDQ-AS** (7-item self-report, 1–2 min; adopted by the Thomson 2024 group to meet the Australian regulator's requirement for a validated screen) or **BDD-YBOCS** (used to diagnose BDD in Zhang 2025) | Refer to psychiatry and do not proceed with cosmetic surgery. Cosmetic procedures are reported not to relieve core BDD symptoms and to be associated with persistent dissatisfaction<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[6]](#ref6)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| **Penile dysmorphic disorder / SPS** | MGSIS (Male Genital Self-Image Scale) + objective penile measurement | Counsel on normal anatomy (Veale nomograms); CBT / psychosexual referral if pain remains; **do not equate SPS with surgical indication** |
| **Realistic-expectation assessment** | Clinical interview ± APPSSI questionnaire | Defer surgery if expectations unrealistic; partner / counseling involvement |
| **General prevalence framing** | BDD affects about 2.5% of US adults (Mansfield 2020) and 11.3% of one Chinese plastic-surgery outpatient cohort (n = 531, Zhang 2025)<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> | Expect higher BDD prevalence in self-referring cosmetic-augmentation candidates |

### Objective Assessment

Measure flaccid length, stretched penile length (SPL), erect length, and circumference using standardized technique. Published nomogram means (Veale 2015; up to 15,521 men) are flaccid pendulous length 9.16 cm, stretched length 13.24 cm, erect length 13.12 cm and erect circumference 11.66 cm.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> Micropenis is defined statistically, as a stretched length well below the population mean, and warrants endocrine workup. Most men who request surgical penile enhancement have a normal-sized, fully functional penis (Vardi 2008), and the primary problem is often psychological.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### First-Line Non-Invasive Options

A 2022 narrative review of 14 papers (1,661 patients) found similar length gains from invasive and non-invasive approaches, judged the study quality low, and suggested non-invasive techniques as first-line treatment after psychological evaluation.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

| Patient Concern | First-Line | Expected Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Short penile length (cosmetic)** | [Penile traction therapy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/penile-traction-therapy), 30–90 min/day for 3–6 months. Randomized data exist after prostatectomy and in Peyronie's disease; evidence for cosmetic lengthening in men with normal anatomy is very limited<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup> | Gain in normal anatomy is not established; +1.6 cm stretched length vs +0.3 cm in controls after prostatectomy |
| **Apparent shortening from suprapubic adiposity** | Weight loss, then [cryolipolysis (3 sessions)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/cryolipolysis). Azab 2021 (n = 46, prospective) apparent stretched length 12.1 → 12.88 cm (p &lt; 0.001)<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup> | Variable; depends on fat reduction |
| **Post-prostatectomy length loss** | PTT (RestoreX) ± VED. Toussi 2021 RCT (82 randomized, 6-month data in 55): PTT +1.6 cm vs +0.3 cm in controls (p &lt; 0.01)<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> | Statistically significant length preservation in this indication |
| **Psychological distress about normal-size penis** | CBT / psychosexual therapy | Addresses BDD / SPS symptoms without procedural risk |

### Invasive-Procedure Selection by Clinical Goal

| Clinical Goal | First-Line | Alternative(s) | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Girth enhancement (~70.6% of patients)** | **Hyaluronic-acid (HA) filler injection.** Reversible (hyaluronidase) and office-based. Yang 2019 and 2020 randomized trials compared HA with PLA, and both increased girth;<sup>[[19]](#ref19)[[20]](#ref20)</sup> Zhang 2022 (n = 38) reported +2.44 cm flaccid girth at 12 months;<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup> Boiko 2023 (n = 132) reported +1.7 cm midshaft girth, and psychosocial gains did not correlate with size change<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup> | **PLA filler** (collagen-stimulating; not reversible; girth gain was greater with HA at 4 weeks in Yang 2019, with no significant difference at 48 weeks, and did not differ between HA and PLA over 18 months in Yang 2020);<sup>[[19]](#ref19)[[20]](#ref20)</sup> **[Penuma silicone sleeve](#)** (the only FDA-cleared device; Elist 2018, single surgeon, n = 400: 56.7% midshaft girth increase and 3% device removal;<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> Siegal 2023, single center, n = 49: 52% flaccid length increase at 6 months<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>); **autologous fat injection** (counsel on unpredictable resorption and on nodules, seen in 7 of 12 patients in the Wessells 1996 referral-center series)<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | **[Non-autologous injectables (silicone, paraffin, Vaseline, PMMA)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/non-autologous-injectables)** — strongly discouraged; siliconomas / migration / radical-excision risk |
| **Length enhancement (true)** | **Suspensory-ligament division (SLD) ± V-Y plasty.** Flaccid length gains of 1–4 cm are reported, and erect-length gain is minimal or absent; experienced surgeons only | None — sliding / slicing penile-disassembly techniques are **PD-specific**, not cosmetic; see [Sliding & Slicing Techniques (Peyronie's section)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease/sliding-slicing-techniques) | SLD without a plan to limit retraction (scrotalization in 4 of 12 patients in Wessells 1996);<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> SLD in a patient who does not understand that erect-state gain is minimal or absent |
| **Apparent shortening (suprapubic lipodystrophy)** | **Suprapubic liposuction** (Ghanem 2017, n = 10: significant increase in flaccid and stretched length at 3 months)<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup> | Open suprapubic lipectomy | Surgical lipectomy in patient unwilling to address weight regain |
| **Penoscrotal webbing (apparent shortening)** | **[Penoscrotal-Web Correction (Z-plasty)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/penoscrotal-web-correction)** at the penoscrotal junction. The Álvarez Vega 2025 series is pediatric (100 consecutive Z-plasty procedures for mixed indications including penoscrotal webbing): 98% primary healing, and 97% of patients had stable correction at 1–2 years<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> | Combined SLD + suprapubic lipectomy + Z-plasty for maximal apparent length; ventral phalloplasty with IPP (Miranda-Sousa 2007, implant patients: 36 of 43 reported some increase in perceived length after web release)<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup> | Web correction without addressing suprapubic lipodystrophy when present; V-Y plasty in patients with diabetes (higher dehiscence risk has been reported) |
| **Scrotal laxity / aesthetic concerns** | **[Reduction scrotoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/reduction-scrotoplasty)** (Thomas 2021 *Aesthet Plast Surg* systematic review and proposed algorithm; 11 eligible articles, mostly case reports or technique descriptions;<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> vertical midline scrotal-skin resection ± Z-plasty for penoscrotal webbing). See [Scrotal Reconstruction Atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/scrotal-reconstruction) for the full reconstructive scrotal toolkit | Energy-based devices (RF / laser); no published scrotal-skin clinical evidence was identified | Aesthetic-only intervention without addressing buried-penis or lymphedema disease when present |
| **Patient with refractory dissatisfaction after normal outcome** | Re-evaluate for BDD, refer to psychiatry, and avoid repeat procedures | Mental-health-led care + CBT | Surgeon-shopping for repeat augmentation |

### Procedure-Specific Sub-Comparisons

#### Girth: HA vs Penuma vs Autologous Fat

| Factor | HA Filler | Penuma (FDA-Cleared) | Autologous Fat |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reversibility** | Reversible (hyaluronidase) | Removable (device extraction; ~3% removal rate) | Irreversible (fat-resorption unpredictable) |
| **Durability** | Temporary (repeat injections needed) | Permanent unless removed | Variable (resorption is unpredictable) |
| **Evidence quality** | Best of the three (randomized HA-versus-PLA trials, Yang 2019 and 2020;<sup>[[19]](#ref19)[[20]](#ref20)</sup> 2023 meta-analysis of 4 studies, n = 283<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>) | Moderate (Elist 2018 single-surgeon n = 400; Siegal 2023 single-center n = 49) | Lower (mostly retrospective; Wessells 1996 referral-center series: nodules in 7 of 12, sexual dysfunction in 4 of 12) |
| **Complication severity** | Mild, transient in most series | Moderate (seroma 4.8%, scar 4.5%, infection 3.3% in Elist 2018) | Potentially severe (nodules, deformity, necrosis, infection) |
| **Setting** | Office-based | Operating room | Operating room |

A 2025 review of complications after penile enhancement found that HA, applied with standardized low-volume protocols, generally has lower complication rates than PLA, PMMA, silicone or self-injected non-medical materials. Typical HA problems are migration, nodules, Tyndall effect, phimosis and infection.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> A 2023 meta-analysis of four HA-versus-PLA studies (283 men) found greater girth enhancement with HA (P = 0.01).<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

#### Length: PTT vs Suprapubic Lipectomy vs SLD

| Factor | PTT | Suprapubic Lipectomy | SLD + V-Y |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Anatomic basis** | None — relies on tissue remodeling | Suprapubic lipodystrophy (true anatomic cause of apparent shortening) | Normal anatomy + desire for length |
| **Invasiveness** | Non-invasive | Minimal–moderate | Moderate |
| **Erect-length gain** | Modest | None (apparent only) | Minimal–none |
| **Flaccid-length gain** | +1–2 cm | Variable (depends on fat volume) | +1–4 cm |
| **Risk of intercourse instability** | None | None | **Yes** — loss of suspensory support |
| **Compliance requirement** | High (daily × months) | None post-op | None post-op |

### Postoperative Management

- **PTT post-SLD.** Traction is commonly advised to limit retraction and scar contracture and to maintain length gains.
- **Compression dressing.** Needed after fat injection, Penuma, and HST to minimize seroma and edema.
- **Sexual-activity restriction.** Typically 4–6 weeks post-surgery.
- **Standardized follow-up measurements.** Flaccid, SPL, erect and circumference measurements at 1, 3, 6 and 12 mo using the same technique as preoperative.
- **HA or PLA maintenance injections.** Plan for repeat dosing as filler resorbs.
- **Weight management.** Weight regain negates results after suprapubic lipectomy or liposuction.

### Evidence Hierarchy and SMSNA 2024 Recommendations

| Procedure | SMSNA Position | Evidence Level | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Penile traction therapy** | Not addressed (the SMSNA statement covered only injection and surgical procedures)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Randomized data after prostatectomy and in Peyronie's disease; limited for cosmetic use<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup> | Reasonable first step for length concerns |
| **HA / PLA filler injection** | Further study needed | Moderate (randomized HA-versus-PLA trials) | Reasonable option for girth with appropriate counseling |
| **Penuma silicone implant** | Only FDA-cleared cosmetic device; early data are favorable | Moderate (single-surgeon series, single-center series, multicenter update) | Acceptable with experienced surgeon and informed consent |
| **Suspensory-ligament division** | May provide modest flaccid gains; risk of instability | Low (retrospective series) | Caution; experienced surgeons only; mandatory anti-retraction technique |
| **Autologous fat injection** | Significant complication risk | Low (retrospective; high complication rates at referral centers) | Approach with caution; counsel extensively |
| **[Non-autologous injectables (silicone / paraffin / Vaseline / PMMA)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/non-autologous-injectables)** | Strongly discouraged | N/A — complication reports only | **Contraindicated** |
