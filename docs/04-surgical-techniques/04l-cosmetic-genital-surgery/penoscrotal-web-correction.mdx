# Penoscrotal-Web Correction (Ventral Phalloplasty)

**Penoscrotal-web correction** releases the web of scrotal skin extending onto the ventral penile shaft and can increase visible penile length and define the penoscrotal junction; the procedure is intended to address complaints such as difficulty with condom fit or intercourse.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Miranda-Sousa and colleagues describe it as "simple, safe, and reproducible." It can be performed as a standalone cosmetic procedure or as an adjunct to penile-prosthesis implantation, suspensory-ligament release, or other genital aesthetic surgeries.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For the broader male cosmetic-genital-surgery decision framework see the [Male Cosmetic Genital Surgery atlas page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/male-cosmetic). For the related but distinct skin-resection procedure see [Reduction Scrotoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/reduction-scrotoplasty). For the foundational geometric flap principles see [Z-Plasty](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/z-plasty) and [V-Y Advancement](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps/v-y-advancement).

## I. Definition and Anatomy of Penoscrotal Webbing

**Penoscrotal webbing (PSW)**, also called "webbed penis", is a condition in which scrotal skin inserts abnormally high on the ventral penile shaft, creating a web-like membrane that tethers the ventral penis to the scrotum. This obscures the ventral penile shaft, shortens the visible penile length, and blurs the normal penoscrotal angle.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

**Normal anatomy.** The penoscrotal junction is a well-defined angle where ventral penile skin meets anterior scrotal skin. Normally this junction sits at or near the base of the penile shaft, creating a clear demarcation between penis and scrotum.

**Webbed anatomy.** In PSW, the scrotal skin extends distally along the ventral penile shaft, sometimes reaching the mid-shaft or even the coronal sulcus in severe cases. The web consists of scrotal skin, dartos fascia, and subcutaneous tissue that are continuous with the ventral penile skin rather than forming a distinct junction.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

**Pathophysiology.** PSW can be:<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

- **Congenital.** Developmental anomaly from abnormal differentiation of the penoscrotal raphe; may occur in isolation or with hypospadias, chordee, or phimosis. Congenital webbed penis (CWP) sits in the spectrum of "concealed penis" disorders alongside buried penis (phallus concealed in prepubic fat) and trapped penis (phallus trapped by scar tissue after circumcision).
- **Acquired.** May develop after circumcision (particularly aggressive ventral skin removal), after penile surgery, or as part of age-related scrotal laxity.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Iatrogenic.** Post-circumcision trapped penis is a well-recognized entity where scar contracture creates a secondary web.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## II. Classification — El-Koutby Grading System

The Bagnara 2024 V-I reconfiguration study graded congenital webbed penis with the El-Koutby classification.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- **Grade 1.** Mild; scrotal skin extends slightly onto the proximal ventral shaft.
- **Grade 2.** Moderate; scrotal skin extends to the mid-shaft.
- **Grade 3.** Severe; scrotal skin extends to the distal shaft or coronal sulcus.

In the Bagnara series, 52% were Grade 3, 24% Grade 2, and 24% Grade 1; most patients in this pediatric series had significant webbing.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

No standardized grading system exists for adult-onset or acquired PSW. Thomas & Navia 2021 propose that a "bothersome scrotum" hangs &gt; 1–2 cm below the penile tip and may be associated with persistent webbing, but this is not a validated classification.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## III. Indications

### A. Cosmetic / aesthetic<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

- **Shortened visible penile length.** The web obscures the ventral shaft, making the penis appear shorter than it actually is. Correction "unmasks" the ventral shaft and increases visible length without lengthening the corpora cavernosa.
- **Poor genital cosmesis.** Blurred penoscrotal junction.
- **Psychological distress.** Webbed penis and concealed penis are among the most common pathologies in adolescents seeking genital cosmetic surgery, with significant improvement in genital self-evaluation scores postoperatively. Overweight is associated with worse preoperative genital self-evaluation scores.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Genital rejuvenation.** Increasingly sought as part of comprehensive male aesthetic genital surgery.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### B. Functional<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

- Difficulty with condom application: the web interferes with proper placement and retention.
- Ventral penile curvature during erection: tethering effect of the web.
- Difficulty with intercourse: mechanical interference.
- Hygiene difficulties: skin folds at the penoscrotal junction trap moisture and debris.

### C. Adjunctive to other procedures<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

- **Penile-prosthesis implantation.** Most common context. In the Miranda-Sousa comparison with clinical records of patients who had no web release, 84% (31 of 37) complained of penile shortening after prosthesis placement, and 84% (36 of 43) of those with web release reported increased penile length; the release group included 39 inflatable and four semirigid implants.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Suspensory-ligament release.** Web correction complements [SLR](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/suspensory-ligament-division) by addressing the ventral component of penile concealment while SLR addresses the dorsal or pubic component.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Suprapubic lipectomy.** Combined with web correction for comprehensive genital aesthetic optimization (see [Suprapubic Lipectomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/suprapubic-lipectomy)).<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

## IV. Surgical Techniques

### A. Z-plasty at the penoscrotal junction

Thomas & Navia propose Z-plasty at the penoscrotal junction for penoscrotal webbing in their algorithm, which rests on a systematic review of 11 articles, mostly case reports and technique descriptions.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

**Principle.** Z-plasty transposes two triangular flaps to break up the linear web, redistribute tissue tension, lengthen the ventral penile skin, and reorient the central scar axis. A standard 60° Z-plasty lengthens the central limb by approximately 75% (theoretical √3 ≈ 73%; clinically reported 50–70%) and reorients the central axis by 90°.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)[[24]](#ref24)[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

**Geometry of flap angle, lengthening and reorientation:**<sup>[[17]](#ref17)[[24]](#ref24)[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

| Flap angle | Theoretical lengthening | Reorientation of central limb |
|---|---|---|
| 30° | 25% | 45° <sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> |
| 45° | 50% | 60° <sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> |
| **60° (standard)** | **75%** | **90°** <sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> |
| 75° | 100% | 105° |
| 90° | 120% | 120° |

Angles of 60–75° balance planimetric elongation against tension distribution; ≤30° can paradoxically produce shortening in practice. Keeping the two peripheral limbs identical in length and angle is needed; mismatched flaps cause dog-ears and increase the risk of tip necrosis.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)[[24]](#ref24)[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

**Stepwise operative protocol.**<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[17]](#ref17)[[18]](#ref18)[[19]](#ref19)[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

1. **Positioning / anesthesia.** Supine; local ± sedation or general.
2. **Identify the web.** Place the penis on gentle stretch to define the V-shaped tethering on the ventral shaft.
3. **Design.** Central limb along the line of maximal contracture (ventral midline of the web); two peripheral limbs of equal length at 60° from each end of the central limb, oriented in opposite directions: one onto ventral penile shaft, one onto scrotal skin.
4. **Incise.** Full-thickness through skin and superficial dartos along all three limbs; preserve urethra and corpus spongiosum.
5. **Elevate the two triangular flaps** in the subdartos plane with adequate thickness to preserve perfusion.
6. **Release tethering dartos bands** to fully unmask the ventral shaft.
7. **Transpose** the two flaps, which converts the vertical web into a transverse or oblique orientation, lengthening ventral skin and recreating the penoscrotal angle.
8. **Layered closure.** Dartos with absorbable braided suture; skin with 4-0 or 5-0 absorbable (chromic or polyglactin rapide).
9. **Dressing.** Light compression; scrotal support.

**Variations.**<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[26]](#ref26)[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

| Variation | Description | Best indication |
|---|---|---|
| **Classic single Z-plasty** | Standard 60° Z at the penoscrotal junction | Mild–moderate webbing |
| **Multiple (serial) Z-plasties** | Series of smaller Z's linked along the web | Longer webs needing distributed lengthening |
| **Double-opposing Z-plasty** | Two Z's in opposing directions sharing a central limb | Limited adjacent tissue; provides depth |
| **Five-flap Z-plasty** | Modified design with five flaps for maximal lengthening | Severe contracture / webbing<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup> |

**Outcomes: Álvarez Vega 2025 (100 Z-plasty procedures).** This pediatric series covered mixed penile and scrotal indications (41 hypospadias revisions, 28 primary hypospadias repairs, 10 chordee corrections and 21 other reconstructions), so the results are not specific to webbing.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

- Primary skin-flap healing in 98% of 100 mixed pediatric Z-plasty procedures.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- One case (1%) of suture-line separation, healed by secondary intention.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- One case (1%) of hypertrophic scar, managed with local steroid.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- Stable corrections with minimal visible scarring in the 97% of patients with complete follow-up.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- The authors call Z-plasty "highly effective, versatile, and safe" for pediatric genital reconstruction.

**Flap perfusion.** Flap necrosis or sloughing can complicate Z-plasty; maintaining adequate flap thickness and tension-free transposition are operative principles.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> Qiu found necrosis associations in 600 traumatic flap-like wounds after emergency debridement, but that study does not quantify risk or prove prevention for planned penoscrotal Z-plasty.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>

### B. V-Y advancement plasty

**Principle.** V-shaped incision at the penoscrotal junction; flap is advanced distally and the wound is closed in a Y configuration. This effectively lowers the scrotal insertion point.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

**Technique.**<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

1. V-shaped incision designed at the penoscrotal junction with apex pointing toward the scrotum.
2. V-flap elevated and advanced distally toward the penile tip.
3. Donor site closed in a Y configuration.
4. Advancement effectively lengthens the ventral penile skin and lowers the scrotal attachment.

**Concern: dehiscence risk.** In Gupta 2019 (n = 103 scrotoplasty + IPP), V-Y plasty was independently associated with wound dehiscence (OR 4.9; 95% CI 1.2–8.6, p = 0.003) versus other scrotoplasty techniques.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

### C. Ventral phalloplasty ("check mark" incision) — Caso & Carrion 2008

Specifically described for use during penile-prosthesis implantation.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

1. The penoscrotal web is defined and marked.
2. A "check mark" incision is made: an oblique incision at the penoscrotal junction with excision of a wedge of scrotal tissue.
3. The incision provides exposure for prosthesis cylinder, pump, and reservoir placement.
4. Wound closure performed longitudinally, converting the oblique or transverse defect into a longitudinal closure.
5. This effectively lowers the scrotal insertion and exposes more ventral penile shaft.

The authors described this as "a modified extension of surgeries described in the pediatric literature for webbed penis" that can be performed standalone or in concert with penile-prosthesis implantation.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### D. V-I penoscrotal reconfiguration — Bagnara 2024

A newer technique specifically for congenital webbed penis.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

1. V-shaped incision at the penoscrotal junction.
2. Flap advanced and closed in a linear (I) configuration rather than a Y.
3. Simplifies closure compared to V-Y plasty while achieving the same tissue redistribution.

**Outcomes (n = 21).**<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- **No postoperative complications were reported among 21 children** at scheduled assessments through 12 months.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- No redo surgeries were reported among 21 children through the scheduled 12-month assessment.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- Parent-rated satisfaction score of 4 in 81% (17/21) and 3 in 19% (4/21).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- The authors advocate correction in childhood to prevent psychological and sexual issues.

### E. Longitudinal ventral-raphe release with scrotal descent (Chen 2012)

Chen and colleagues incised the ventral median raphe longitudinally, released adherent scrotal tissue from the spongiosum and shaft, lowered the scrotal skin, and closed the incision. They used an inverted-Y modification for a broad web.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

**Chen 2012 (n = 12 patients, ages 14–23).**<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- Operation time 20 min to 1 hr.
- Mean blood loss under 50 mL.
- All 12 patients in Chen’s series had satisfactory cosmetic results at 1–3 months.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- No severe complications; follow-up was 1 to 3 months.

### F. Scrotal-raphe approach (Borsellino 2007)<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- Incision along the scrotal raphe (midline of the scrotum).
- Penile shaft exteriorized and the penoscrotal angle reconstructed through this scrotal incision.
- No flaps, grafts or ventral Z-plasty were used; all 11 webbed penises were phimotic and required circumcision.
- No webbed penis recurred among 11 children (0/11); buried penis recurred in 3/56 (5.3%) and trapped penis in 2/20 (10%) after at least 1 year of follow-up.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- The authors report that the approach "simplified the complete exteriorization of the penile shaft, with easy bleeding control."

### G. Concealed-penis repair with penoscrotal-angle reconstruction (Xu 2015)<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

For concealed penis (buried, webbed, or trapped subtypes). Pairs complete penile degloving with a longitudinal ventral incision at the penoscrotal junction (access and dead-space drainage) and tunica-albuginea-to-proximal-dartos fixation to reconstruct the penoscrotal angle and prevent retraction; the authors present the longitudinal incision and Z-plasty as interchangeable within the same framework. Xu reported 41 concealed-penis repairs using either a longitudinal incision or Z-plasty: correction succeeded in all, with a median 2.1-cm flaccid-length gain and 6-month to 2-year follow-up; the abstract gives neither incision-specific results nor a complication count.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> See the dedicated page: [Longitudinal Incision + Penoscrotal-Angle Reconstruction (Xu)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/xu-longitudinal-penoplasty).

## VI. Penoscrotal-Web Correction Combined With Penile Prosthesis

Direct web-release outcome data come mainly from short-term, nonrandomized prosthesis series. The two randomized prosthesis studies below tested different or bundled lengthening procedures and do not establish a separate web-release effect.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[14]](#ref14)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

### Miranda-Sousa 2007 (n = 80)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- Forty-three patients underwent penile-prosthesis placement plus web release (39 inflatable and four semirigid devices); 37 patients without web release formed a comparison group assessed from clinical records.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- All 80 patients in the comparison had a high ventral scrotal insertion (penoscrotal web).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **With web release.** At 3 months, 36/43 (84%) perceived increased penile length, 5/43 (12%) no change, and 2/43 (5%) decreased length.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Without web release.** Clinical records showed 31/37 (84%) complained of penile shortening and 1/37 (3%) reported increased length.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **42/43 (98%) reported good overall satisfaction with the surgery** at 3 months in the web-release group.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Complications with implant plus web release: wound hematoma 2/43 (4.7%) and focal superficial dehiscence 3/43 (7.0%).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Added operative time with implant plus web release was approximately 12 minutes (mean 11.7 min).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Authors' verdict: "Release of penoscrotal web is a simple, safe, and reproducible procedure that can enhance patient perception of penile length and further improve satisfaction."

### Abdelwahab 2025 RCT: combined maneuvers (n = 61)<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

The randomized comparison tested a four-component lengthening package during malleable prosthesis placement; it did not isolate the contribution of ventral phalloplasty. Length and satisfaction outcomes were assessed at 3 months.

- Group A (n = 31): malleable penile prosthesis with SLR, lower penopubic Z-plasty, and dorsal and ventral phalloplasty.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- Group B (n = 30): malleable penile prosthesis alone through a ventral midline penoscrotal incision.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- Mean functional and visible penile lengths were greater in Group A (p &lt; 0.001), and the EDITS resting-state score was higher (55.2% vs 0.0%, p &lt; 0.001) at 3 months.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

### Aboul Fotouh El Gharably 2022 RCT: adjacent lengthening evidence, not web-release evidence (n = 61)<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

This trial tested dorsal/pubic lengthening maneuvers with malleable prosthesis placement; it cannot estimate the effect of penoscrotal-web correction.

- Group A (n = 31): malleable penile prosthesis with SLR and pubic lipectomy via penopubic Z-plasty.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- Group B (n = 30): conventional malleable penile prosthesis through a penoscrotal incision.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- Visible penile-length gain: +2.5 cm (IQR 1–3.5) in Group A versus 0 cm (IQR −1 to 0) in Group B (p &lt; 0.001).
- Median operative time was 170 min in Group A and 97.5 min in Group B (p &lt; 0.001).

### Mokhless 2010 (n = 9 adolescents post-circumcision)<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

- Z-plasty of penoscrotal web with SLR and suprapubic-fat liposuction.
- Functional penile length increased by +18–30 mm (mean 23 ± 4 mm) in the erect state at 3 mo.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- No postoperative complications occurred.
- All nine patients in this series had normally developed corpora cavernosa.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

### Spyropoulos 2005 (n = 11 combined augmentation phalloplasty)<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

- Penile lengthening (suprapubic skin advancement and ligamentolysis) ± dermal-fat graft ± panniculectomy or lipectomy.
- Mean flaccid-stretched length gain +1.6 cm; mean circumference gain +2.3 cm at the base and +2.6 cm subcoronally.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- **91% of the 11 selected men reported significant (20–53%) improvement** in sexual self-esteem and functioning.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

## VII. Complications

Generally a low-morbidity procedure.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[10]](#ref10)[[18]](#ref18)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

| Complication | Incidence | Risk factors | Management |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wound dehiscence** | 15/103 (14.6%) after scrotoplasty with IPP<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>; 1/100 suture-line separation after mixed pediatric Z-plasty<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | **Diabetes (OR 6.1)**; **V-Y plasty (OR 4.9)** in the IPP scrotoplasty cohort<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> | Conservative (mild); suture reinforcement (moderate); OR washout (major, 1/103) |
| Hematoma | 2/43 (4.7%) with prosthesis plus web release<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | Anticoagulation; inadequate hemostasis | Observation vs drainage |
| Superficial wound dehiscence | 3/43 (7.0%) with prosthesis plus web release<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | V-Y technique associated with dehiscence in a separate IPP scrotoplasty cohort<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>; tension on closure | Wound care; usually heals secondarily |
| Hypertrophic scar | 1/100 (1%) in mixed pediatric genital Z-plasty procedures<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | Individual scarring tendency | Local steroid injection |
| Edema, glans numbness or instability after combined pubic lengthening | In a malleable-prosthesis trial of SLR plus lipectomy via penopubic Z-plasty: 24/31 (77.4%), 3/31 (9.7%) and 3/31 (9.7%), respectively; these are not web-correction rates.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> | Combined pubic dissection | Manage according to the specific injury and operation |
| Recurrence of webbing | 0/11 webbed children at at least one year in Borsellino; this does not establish a zero recurrence risk.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | Not established for adult web correction | Assess the anatomy before revision |

**Risk factors: Gupta 2019.**<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

- **Diabetes.** Strongest predictor of wound dehiscence (OR 6.1; 95% CI 1.5–25.0; p = 0.013).<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- **V-Y plasty technique** independently associated with dehiscence (OR 4.9; 95% CI 1.2–8.6; p = 0.003).<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- Age, Peyronie's disease, hypertension, smoking, and device manufacturer were not associated.
- Authors recommend that diabetic patients be specifically counseled about increased wound-complication risk.

## VIII. Psychological Impact and Patient Selection

### Adolescent genital dissatisfaction — Zampieri 2022 (n = 98)<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

- Webbed penis and penile curvature were the most frequent pathologies in adolescents seeking genital cosmetic surgery.
- Patients with concealed penis and webbed penis showed the best postoperative improvement in genital self-evaluation scores.
- Overweight associated with worse preoperative scores.
- Authors' verdict: "Cosmetic genital surgery should be considered even in male adolescents."

### Adult genital self-image — Hustad 2022 (n = 3,503)<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

- **33.8%** of all individuals in the online survey reported dissatisfaction with genital appearance.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- **5.5% of males** had severely low genital self-image (2 SD below mean).<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- **11.3% of males** had considered cosmetic genital surgery.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- Larger penis size predicted higher genital self-image, but avoidance or safety-seeking behaviors and openness toward surgery predicted lower self-image; psychological factors play a significant role beyond anatomy.

### Long-term outcomes of concealed-penis repair — Herndon 2003<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

- In Herndon’s parent telephone survey after 14 infant/toddler concealed-penis repairs (mean 41.7 months), parents reported improved appearance (100%), hygiene (87%), and accessibility (86%).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- In Herndon’s four-adolescent parent telephone-survey subgroup, 50% reported improved appearance, 50% improved hygiene, and 75% improved penile accessibility at a mean 38.8 months after surgery.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- Despite less favorable adolescent results, all four adolescent parents in Herndon’s telephone survey would still recommend the surgery to a friend with the same problem.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- The series had 14 toddlers and 4 adolescents. The small adolescent group is consistent with better results after earlier correction, which is the basis of the Bagnara recommendation for childhood correction of CWP.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## IX. Standalone Cosmetic Penoscrotal-Web Correction — Practical Considerations

**Patient selection.**<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

- Patients with visible PSW causing cosmetic dissatisfaction, functional complaints (condom difficulty, ventral curvature), or psychological distress.
- Distinguish webbed penis from buried penis (which requires more extensive surgery including escutcheonectomy) and from penile dysmorphic disorder (which may benefit from psychological intervention rather than surgery).
- The SMSNA 2024 statement requires that body dysmorphic disorder be ruled out before invasive treatment, using structured assessment such as the BDD Questionnaire or symptom scale together with professional psychological evaluation.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
- No standardized diagnostic criteria; clinical assessment is subjective.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

**Preoperative counseling.**<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- Goals of surgery should be clearly defined: (1) expose the glans and coronal sulcus; (2) match penile-skin length dorsally and ventrally; (3) provide a normal penoscrotal junction.
- Patients should understand that web correction increases visible penile length but does not increase actual corporal length.
- Expected length gain is primarily perceptual; the ventral shaft that was previously hidden by the web becomes visible.

**Anesthesia.** Local anesthesia with sedation for standalone procedures, or general anesthesia when combined with other procedures.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

**Operative time.** 20 minutes to 1 hour for standalone web correction; adds approximately 12 minutes when combined with IPP.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

**Postoperative care** (typical institutional regimen).

- Supportive underwear for 2–4 weeks.
- Activity restriction (no sexual activity) for 4–6 weeks.
- Wound monitoring, particularly in diabetic patients.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

### CPT coding

- **CPT 55180.** Scrotoplasty, complicated; appropriate for penoscrotal Z-plasty given the flap-transposition component.
- When performed concurrently with penile-prosthesis implantation, code separately from the prosthesis-placement codes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## X. Technique Selection Algorithm

Based on Thomas & Navia 2021 and the available evidence.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

**Penoscrotal webbing alone (no scrotomegaly).**

→ **Z-plasty at the penoscrotal junction**: proposed by the Thomas & Navia algorithm.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> It breaks up the linear scar and redistributes tissue tension, and a mixed pediatric series reported 2% minor complications.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

**Penoscrotal webbing + scrotomegaly.**

→ **Z-plasty at the penoscrotal junction plus vertical midline scrotal-skin resection**: addresses both components simultaneously. See [Reduction Scrotoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/reduction-scrotoplasty) for the skin-resection deep-dive.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

**Combined with penile prosthesis.**

→ **Ventral phalloplasty** ("check mark" incision or V-Y plasty): provides exposure for prosthesis placement while correcting the web. In Gupta’s retrospective IPP series, V-Y plasty was associated with higher dehiscence odds (OR 4.9), and diabetes was a separate predictor (OR 6.1).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

→ In patients with diabetes, counsel about higher wound-dehiscence risk when adding scrotoplasty to prosthesis placement. Gupta reported an association with V-Y plasty, but did not establish that Z-plasty lowers that risk.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

**Congenital webbed penis (pediatric / adolescent).**

→ **V-I penoscrotal reconfiguration** was described in 21 children, with no complications or redo procedures reported through the scheduled 12-month follow-up; comparative safety and adult outcomes are unknown.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

→ **Z-plasty**: adaptable to severity; 98% primary healing in a mixed pediatric series.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

→ **Scrotal-raphe approach**: avoids flaps and grafts; no recurrence in 11 webbed penises.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## XI. Evidence Summary

| Feature | Current evidence |
|---|---|
| **Level of evidence** | Level IV (case series, technique descriptions); 2 RCTs in IPP context |
| **Systematic reviews** | 2: Thomas & Navia 2021; Schifano 2022 |
| **RCTs isolating web correction** | Neither cited randomized prosthesis trial isolates a web-correction effect: the 2022 trial tested pubic maneuvers without web release, and the 2025 trial bundled ventral phalloplasty with other procedures.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| **Largest Z-plasty series** | Álvarez Vega 2025 (100 procedures, pediatric, mixed penile and scrotal indications) |
| **Largest IPP + scrotoplasty series** | Gupta 2019 (n = 103 scrotoplasties using multiple techniques with initial IPP insertion)<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> |
| **Validated outcome measures** | None specific to cosmetic web correction |
| **Standardized classification** | El-Koutby (congenital only); no adult classification |
| **Position statements** | SMSNA 2024 addresses fillers, suspensory-ligament division, graft-and-flap procedures, silicone sleeves and sliding/slicing techniques; penoscrotal web correction is not among its listed topics |

---

## XII. Clinical Summary

Penoscrotal-web correction is a technically straightforward procedure with low reported morbidity in small series. The strongest evidence comes from the penile-prosthesis literature, where ventral phalloplasty was associated with reversal of the perception of penile shortening (84% complained of shortening without web release in a record-based comparison group, and 84% reported increased length with web release).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> For standalone cosmetic correction, the Thomas & Navia algorithm proposes Z-plasty at the penoscrotal junction, and a mixed pediatric series reported 98% primary flap healing.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> V-Y plasty is used cautiously in diabetic patients because it was associated with nearly 5-fold higher odds of dehiscence.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> The V-I reconfiguration was described as a simple closure; in a retrospective series of 21 children, no postoperative complications were reported with assessments through 12 months, while comparative safety remains unknown.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> The evidence base remains limited, dominated by case series and technique descriptions, and no validated outcome measures, standardized adult classification systems, or large prospective standalone cosmetic-correction studies exist.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
