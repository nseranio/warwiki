# Cosmetic Genital Surgery

Cosmetic genital surgery is elective, aesthetic surgery. It is distinct from reconstructive operations for trauma, oncologic resection, lichen sclerosus, FGM/C or congenital anomaly (see [04e Genital Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction)) and from gender-affirming surgery (see [04k Gender-Affirming Surgery](/docs/surgical-techniques/04k-gender-affirming-surgery)). The atlas is divided by the two patient cohorts that drive technique selection: **male cosmetic** (augmentation phalloplasty, girth enhancement, suspensory-ligament division, suprapubic lipectomy, Penuma, scrotal aesthetic procedures) and **female cosmetic (FGCS)** (labiaplasty, clitoral hoodoplasty, labia majora augmentation, monsplasty, surgical vaginoplasty or energy-based devices, hymenoplasty, regenerative and fat-grafting techniques). Each sub-page hosts its own Decision Framework and Treatment Database. For the clinical-conditions framework on small penis syndrome (SPS) / penile dysmorphic disorder (PDD) see [Small Penis Syndrome](/docs/clinical-conditions/03g-genital-scrotal/small-penis-syndrome).

> **Society positions and evidence quality.** The SMSNA 2024 position statement (expert opinion) recommends against graft-and-flap penile augmentation outside a research protocol, recommends silicone sleeve implants only with IRB oversight, long-term safety endpoints and no patient cost, and restricts sliding/slicing techniques to IRB-supervised research by experienced prosthetic surgeons, and states that data on temporary HA and PLA fillers are limited.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> It requires that penile dysmorphic disorder be ruled out before invasive enhancement.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The EAU 2026 guideline strongly recommends against grafts, biodegradable scaffolds and subcutaneous penile implants for girth enhancement, and against HA, other soft-tissue fillers or autologous fat injection in men with PDD.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> It gives a weak recommendation for those injections in eligible men without PDD.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> ACOG Committee Opinion 795 (2020) states that female genital cosmetic procedures are not medically indicated without a clinical indication and that their safety and effectiveness have not been established. It advises assessment for **body dysmorphic disorder (BDD)** if indicated.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The FIGO 2025 statement asserts that it is ethically inappropriate for obstetrician-gynecologists to recommend, perform or refer patients for cosmetic genital surgery.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The FDA 2018 safety communication warned against energy-based devices for "vaginal rejuvenation" and vaginal cosmetic procedures, for which the agency had not cleared or approved such devices.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
