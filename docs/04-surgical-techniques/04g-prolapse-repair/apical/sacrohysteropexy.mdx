# Sacrohysteropexy

**Sacrohysteropexy** is an abdominal (open, laparoscopic, or robotic) uterus-preserving apical repair in which graft material supports the cervix/uterus and is fixed at the sacrum. It is the uterine-preserving analogue of sacrocolpopexy, but it is not a mesh-free vaginal hysteropexy and its results should not be transferred to those operations.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For the vault operation, see [Sacrocolpopexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrocolpopexy). For mesh-free vaginal uterine preservation, see [Sacrospinous Hysteropexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrospinous-hysteropexy) and [Manchester-Fothergill Procedure](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/manchester-fothergill).

---

## Selection and counseling

Sacrohysteropexy may be considered when a patient with symptomatic apical prolapse wishes to retain her uterus and has had appropriate evaluation of abnormal bleeding, cervical screening, and uterine pathology. Preference for uterine preservation alone does not establish suitability, and evidence for pregnancy after the procedure is sparse.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Counsel separately about the abdominal dissection and permanent implanted mesh, including mesh exposure/erosion, pain, bowel or vascular injury, recurrent prolapse, and possible reoperation. Reported frequencies vary substantially with the graft, extent of vaginal dissection, concomitant surgery, study design, and duration of follow-up; a single pooled percentage is not an individual forecast.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Operative concept

The operation requires sacral-promontory dissection, graft attachment to the cervix/uterus and/or vagina, sacral fixation, and closure of peritoneum over the graft. Exact graft configuration, plane of dissection, fixation material, and management of associated compartment defects are technique- and surgeon-specific. This overview intentionally does not prescribe unverified universal suture counts, landmark distances, or device choices; those decisions require formal training and current institutional practice.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Comparative evidence

### LAVA trial: laparoscopic sacrohysteropexy versus vaginal sacrospinous hysteropexy

LAVA compared two different uterus-preserving operations—laparoscopic mesh sacrohysteropexy and vaginal native-tissue sacrospinous hysteropexy—in women with stage 2 or greater uterine prolapse. The trial reported noninferiority for its 12-month composite surgical-failure outcome. Its short follow-up, specific techniques, and different adverse-symptom profiles mean that the result is not proof of long-term equivalence or a general preference for either route.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

### Systematic-review context

The 2023 Cochrane review found low- to moderate-certainty evidence for several apical-prolapse comparisons and emphasized that operations, mesh products, outcome definitions, and follow-up vary. In its vaginal-versus-sacral hysteropexy comparison, differences in awareness of prolapse, repeat surgery, recurrent prolapse, and several adverse outcomes were uncertain or small; it did not establish a universal best uterus-preserving operation.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Keep other trials distinct

SUPeR is sometimes cited in hysteropexy discussions, but it tested **transvaginal polypropylene mesh sacrospinous hysteropexy**, not laparoscopic sacrohysteropexy. SAVE-U tested **native-tissue vaginal sacrospinous hysteropexy** against vaginal hysterectomy plus USLS. Neither trial directly answers the LAVA comparison or substitutes for individualized mesh counseling.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Follow-up

Continue cervical screening when appropriate, investigate new bleeding, and reassess recurrent bulge, urinary, bowel, or pain symptoms. Studies of long-term mesh outcomes are observational and should inform counseling without being presented as a guarantee of safety or durability.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Key principles

- Sacrohysteropexy is a mesh-augmented abdominal operation; it is distinct from native-tissue hysteropexy.
- Mesh counseling should address both procedure-specific benefit uncertainty and potentially serious but uncommon complications.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- LAVA supports short-term noninferiority for its specified comparison, not a universal long-term equivalence claim.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Do not use SUPeR or SAVE-U as if they were direct sacrohysteropexy trials.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
