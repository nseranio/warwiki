# Pectopexy

**Pectopexy** (also called **pectocolpopexy** or **pectocervicopexy**) is an apical-prolapse repair that suspends the vaginal vault or cervix to the **bilateral iliopectineal (Cooper's) ligaments** with mesh or suture, rather than the sacral promontory. Banerjee and Noé's original technique was published online in **2010** and in the **2011** print volume. Its route **avoids presacral dissection**. Avoiding that dissection is an anatomical difference, not proof that every other operative or postoperative risk is lower.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

For the sacral-fixation alternative, see [Sacrocolpopexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrocolpopexy). For the uterus-preserving sacral variant, see [Sacrohysteropexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrohysteropexy). For the broader prolapse decision framework, see [Prolapse Repair](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair).

---

## Definition and Rationale

The **iliopectineal ligament** (Cooper's ligament) is a strong, well-defined anatomic structure familiar to surgeons from hernia repair. Pectopexy uses it as a bilateral fixation anchor for apical suspension.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Potential advantages and trade-offs compared with sacrocolpopexy depend on the chosen variant and patient. The following are **anatomic or study-specific observations**, not universal comparative benefits:

- **Avoids presacral dissection** — does not expose the promontory and its presacral vessels/nerve plexus in the way sacrocolpopexy does; other pelvic structures remain at risk.
- **Avoids the rectosigmoid mesentery** in a standard anterior approach; a lower bowel-dysfunction rate is not guaranteed for an individual patient.
- **Anterior pelvic field** may simplify access in selected anatomy; one single-surgeon series found a learning-curve turning point at case 12, not a general competency threshold.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Bilateral fixation** gives a different support vector, but a more physiologic or superior suspension has not been established.
- **Operative time** was shorter in several reported comparisons; interpretation depends on case mix and concomitant surgery.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

---

## Indications and Patient Selection

Pectopexy has been studied in women with **POP-Q stage ≥ 2 apical prolapse** (uterovaginal or post-hysterectomy vault prolapse).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Situations in which its non-sacral route may be considered include:

- Patients where **presacral dissection is high risk** — prior pelvic radiation, sacral anomalies, severe adhesions, deep pelvis.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Obese patients** — an anterior route has been described, but comparative access or outcome advantage in obesity is not established by the cited small technique reports.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Patients who desire **uterine preservation** (pectouteropexy / pectohysteropexy).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- Settings where procedure time matters, after considering the entire planned operation and alternatives.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Surgical Technique — Illustrative Laparoscopic Pectopexy

The descriptions below retain historical and published variants. Port sites, mesh shape, fixation and concomitant repairs are **not interchangeable universal steps**; the Chuang cohort used a 3 × 23 cm DynaMesh-PRS strip, V-Loc at the cervix/vault and either Ethibond sutures or **absorbable** AbsorbaTack at the pectineal ligaments.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Product-specific instructions and the chosen operative anatomy govern use.

### Positioning and ports<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

- Dorsal lithotomy with hip extension and knee flexion; **45° Trendelenburg**.
- One **10-mm umbilical optical trocar** + three 5-mm accessory trocars (two placed 2 cm above and medial to the ASIS bilaterally; one 5 cm below the umbilicus).

### Step-by-step

1. **Bilateral identification and exposure of the iliopectineal ligaments.** Begin on one side (typically left). Incise peritoneum in the center of a **V-shaped area** bordered laterally by the **round ligament** and medially by the **obliterated umbilical artery (medial umbilical ligament)**. Dissect soft tissue to expose the **iliopectineal ligament** **medial to the external iliac vessels** in the Chuang description; it is not an S2 sacral landmark. Repeat contralaterally.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
   - **Critical structures to preserve**: nearby external iliac vessels and obturator neurovascular structures; confirm their relationship in the individual field.
2. **Vaginal / cervical dissection.** If hysterectomy planned, perform supracervical hysterectomy at this point. Dissect the **vesicovaginal space** anteriorly to expose the anterior vaginal wall / cervical stump. A vaginal retractor or assistant's finger tents the vaginal wall for identification.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
3. **Mesh fixation to vaginal wall / cervix.** Suture the central portion of the mesh to the **anterior vaginal wall and / or cervical stump**. In one reported strip technique, V-Loc was used at the cervix/vault; another inverted-T variant extends anteriorly along the vesicovaginal septum for concurrent cystocele. **Tacks and sutures are different fixation devices**, not interchangeable labels.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
4. **Bilateral mesh fixation to the iliopectineal ligaments.** Bring the lateral arms to Cooper's ligaments bilaterally; the Chuang series used **two interrupted Ethibond sutures or 2–3 absorbable AbsorbaTack tacks per side**. Other described materials and devices require their own source and instructions. Elevate the vaginal apex without excessive traction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
5. **Peritonization.** The illustrated variant closes peritoneum over the mesh bilaterally with running absorbable suture (e.g., Vicryl 2-0), separating intraperitoneal bowel from the implant.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
6. **Concomitant procedures (as needed).**
   - **Burch colposuspension** for concurrent SUI — the space of Retzius is already exposed.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
   - Anterior / posterior colporrhaphy.
   - Supracervical hysterectomy when indicated.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

### Mesh configurations

| Configuration | Dimensions | Material | Vaginal / cervical fixation | Lateral fixation | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Horizontal strip (Noé/Chuang variants)** | 3 × 15 cm in the Noé long-term cohort; 3 × 23 cm DynaMesh-PRS in Chuang's series | PVDF or polypropylene according to study/device | Central portion to vaginal cuff or cervix; fixation pattern varies | Bilateral iliopectineal ligaments, **not an S2 sacral level** | Apical prolapse with study-specific concomitant repair<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[16]](#ref16)[[19]](#ref19)[[20]](#ref20)</sup> |
| **T-shaped mesh** | 8 × 15 cm sheet cut to a T in one description | Polypropylene | Short (vertical) arm to anterior vaginal wall + cervical stump | Two lateral (horizontal) arms to bilateral Cooper's ligaments | One pectopexy configuration for apical + anterior prolapse<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Inverted T-mesh** | Custom cut | PVDF or polypropylene | Anterior arm extended along the vesicovaginal septum + cervix | Bilateral iliopectineal ligaments | Advanced uterine + anterior prolapse<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |

**Y-shaped mesh belongs to a different named route in the cited reports.** Chen 2018 and Li 2021 describe transvaginal single-port **sacral-promontory** fixation, with a separate anterior attachment to the descending pubic ramus; they do **not** demonstrate a Y-shaped, bilateral-Cooper's-ligament pectopexy. Keep their 93-case feasibility outcome in that sacral reconstruction context, not as a pectopexy success estimate.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)[[24]](#ref24)</sup> See [Sacrocolpopexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrocolpopexy).

**Material choice.** The 2025 pectopexy scoping review found polypropylene mesh in **46.7% of included studies**, not 46.7% of patients or proof of one material's superiority; PVDF appears in other reported variants.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The Kavallaris/Zygouris polypropylene-versus-PVDF comparison concerns **sacrocolpopexy**, so it cannot establish pectopexy handling, erosion or outcome advantages.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> In uterus-preserving pectopexy, anterior versus posterior cervical strip placement was examined in a separate cohort; its scope should not be generalized to all mesh shapes.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

**Preparation and delivery.** A mesh strip may be rolled for trocar introduction and unfolded intracorporeally; published dimensions, trocar choice and edge finishing are **variant/device-specific**, not determined by the cited sacrocolpopexy material comparison. Avoid assigning Chen/Li's Y-mesh preparation or 93-case result to pectopexy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[21]](#ref21)[[23]](#ref23)[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

A **cadaveric biomechanical study** found no significant difference in ultimate load or failure displacement between the tested continuous and interrupted sutures (see [Biomechanical fixation note](#biomechanical-fixation-note)). These laboratory findings do not establish clinical equivalence or mandate a fixation method.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

### Biomechanical fixation note

In **eight fresh cadavers** (16 ligaments, 33 material-specific tests), the tested mesh + single interrupted suture had ultimate load **35 ± 12 N**, versus **48 ± 7 N** with continuous suture; the difference was not statistically significant. The authors found the tested mesh limiting in their setup. **This is bench evidence**, not clinical equivalence or a universal one-suture fixation rule.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Mesh-Free (Non-Mesh) Variants

| Variant | Technique | Outcomes |
|---|---|---|
| **Laparoscopic Non-Mesh Cerclage Pectopexy (LNMCP)** — Zhang / Lyu | Permanent cervical cerclage sutures (non-absorbable) embedded along the **round ligament** bilaterally to the iliopectineal ligament | In 16 reported cases, OR time was 67.8 ± 15.5 min and EBL 73.1 ± 51.1 mL; **100% objective and 93.8% subjective success** were reported, without a comparative arm.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **LNMCP — uterine-preserving variant (LNMCPUP)** | Uterine preservation while retaining cerclage approach | In 14 cases (mean follow-up 19.71 ± 15.87 months), reported objective success was 100% and subjective success 92.86%; there was no comparative arm.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **Meshless pectopexy (Salman's modification)** | Monofilament non-absorbable sutures (no mesh) to suspend uterus to bilateral iliopectineal ligaments | Single-group series of 40 women with stage 3–4 prolapse: mean C-point change **−8.21 ± 3.03 cm at one year**; reported apical recurrence 7.9%.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |

## LESS and Robotic Pectopexy

| Variant | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Laparoendoscopic Single-Site (LESS) pectopexy** | A narrated single-patient video report used a single umbilical site and reported an ~80-minute operation. Its feasibility report does not establish an obesity-specific advantage.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Robot-assisted pectouteropexy** | A uterus-preserving robotic case report in an obese patient describes visualization and maneuverability advantages; it does not quantify a comparative operating-time benefit.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |

## Learning Curve

In one **single-surgeon retrospective** comparison of 18 initial pectopexies and 21 later-experience sacrocolpopexies, a CUSUM turning point occurred at the **12th pectopexy case**. The groups differed in age and had short, unequal follow-up; this is a local operative-time learning curve, **not** a general training threshold or proof that the procedure is easier for all surgeons.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Outcomes and Efficacy

The figures below come from **different techniques, populations, endpoints and follow-up windows**; they are not pooled patient-level predictions or a single pectopexy success rate. The 2025 scoping review identified only four randomized trials among 30 studies and substantial outcome heterogeneity.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

| Endpoint | Result |
|---|---|
| Objective anatomical success | 16/16 and 14/14 reported in the separate non-mesh cerclage series; these small cohorts do not provide a pooled pectopexy success rate<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| Subjective success | 93.8% of 16 cases and 92.86% of 14 cases in the separate non-mesh cerclage series, using their own definitions<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| Apical recurrence | 0–7.9% in selected short-term reports; Noé separately reported 8.0% in a longer combined-repair cohort<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[7]](#ref7)[[14]](#ref14)[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| 10-yr recurrence-free survival (median) | **9.34 yr**<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| No apical re-fixation in Noé's combined-repair cohort | **94.9%** (42/832 re-fixed); distinct from all apical recurrence<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| Reoperation rate (10-yr) | 9.7%<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| OR time (illustrative LESS case) | Approximately 80 min in one published video case; not a standard procedure time<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| OR time (Chuang pectopexy cohort) | 182.9 ± 27.2 min in 18 early cases with other procedures frequently performed<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Patient-reported outcomes (including PFDI-20, PFIQ-7 and PISQ-12) | Improvement reported in selected series; instruments and follow-up differ, and the scoping review found heterogeneous reporting.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)[[11]](#ref11)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |

## Comparative Outcomes

### vs Sacrocolpopexy (2025 meta-analyses + comparative studies)

Lin's 2025 review pooled ten randomized and nonrandomized comparative studies (764 patients). Chuang's separate 18-versus-21 retrospective series had different ages and unequal mean follow-up (7.2 versus 16.2 months). A null test or pooled average does not establish equivalent long-term efficacy or a procedure-level guarantee.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

| Parameter | Pectopexy | Sacrocolpopexy | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operative time | **34.4 min shorter** in Lin's pooled estimate | — | 95% CI −47.12 to −21.69 min; p < 0.01<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Blood loss | 13.34 mL less in Lin's pooled estimate | — | 95% CI −21.37 to −5.32 mL; p < 0.01<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Hospital stay | 0.15 d shorter in Lin's pooled estimate | — | 95% CI −0.26 to −0.04 d; p < 0.01<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Postoperative bowel dysfunction | Lin reports fewer events with pectopexy | — | The abstract's RR 1.09 does not explain its comparison coding; avoid a quantitative patient-risk estimate from it alone<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Postoperative low back pain | 0/18 in Chuang's pectopexy group | 4/21 in its sacrocolpopexy group | p = 0.11; small, non-significant comparison<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Prolapse recurrence | No difference detected in Lin's pooled comparison | — | Not proof of equivalence<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Anatomical outcomes | No difference detected in Lin's pooled comparison | — | Not proof of equivalence<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Satisfaction | No difference detected in Lin's pooled comparison | — | Not proof of equivalence<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Sexual function | No difference detected in Lin's pooled comparison | — | No demonstrated superiority<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Quality of life | No difference detected in Lin's pooled comparison | — | Study instruments and follow-up differ<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |

**Caveat**: Yang 2023 reported **higher urinary symptom recurrence after pectopexy** (13.7% vs 5.0%, OR 3.1, p = 0.032) — warrants attention.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

### vs Sacrospinous Ligament Fixation

The cited pectopexy-versus-SSLF comparison reported operative time **151.8 versus 69 minutes** and a vaginal-length difference, but its procedure selection and concomitant repairs limit causal interpretation. Do not treat an observed anterior-compartment association as a proven protective effect.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

### vs High Uterosacral Ligament Suspension (HUSLS)

Peng’s retrospective one-year comparison included 23 uterine-preserving pectopexies, 78 pectopexies with hysterectomy, and 69 HUSLS procedures with hysterectomy. The reported anatomical and sexual-function differences are associations in selected groups with different uterine status and concomitant surgery, not proof of pectopexy superiority.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### Three-way comparison (Sánchez-Ferrer 2025, n = 180)

Sacropexy vs pectopexy vs lateral suspension — an observational cohort found **no detected difference** in its surgical-failure endpoint and shorter operative times for the non-sacral groups. This does **not** establish equivalence or isolate procedure effects from selection and concomitant surgery.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

| Postoperative constipation / bowel dysfunction | Direction favored pectopexy in some comparative syntheses; Chuang's 0/18 versus 4/21 difference was not significant (p = 0.11), so no individual risk reduction is established<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Postoperative low back pain | 0/18 versus 4/21 in Chuang's small, non-significant comparison (p = 0.11), **not an absence-of-risk claim**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Presacral hemorrhage | **N/A** — no sacral dissection |

## Long-Term Durability — 10-Year Data

The long-term **single-center retrospective series** (Noé 2026, n = 832) involved laparoscopic pectopexy **with defect-oriented native-tissue repairs**; its estimates are not isolated pectopexy effects or an across-technique forecast:<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

- **Median recurrence-free survival**: 9.34 yr (IQR 7.36–10.60).
- **Median reoperation-free survival**: 9.56 yr (IQR 7.68–10.71).
- **87.9% of observed events within first 4 years** in this cohort; the authors support attention through that interval, not a universal minimum-surveillance mandate.
- **42/832 (5.05%) underwent apical re-fixation**; this differs from any apical recurrence, reported separately as 8.0%.
- **Lateral defect severity** was associated with recurrence in the cohort; this is not a validated individual prediction rule.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

## Biomechanical Considerations

Cadaveric biomechanical testing of the iliopectineal ligament:<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

- Mesh + single interrupted suture: **35 ± 12 N**.
- Mesh + continuous suture: 48 ± 7 N (NS).
- The **tested mesh was the limiting factor** in this cadaver setup; no clinical fixation method is established as equivalent or adequate by this experiment alone.

---

## Limitations and Evidence Gaps

- Most evidence is from **observational studies and small RCTs** — only 4 RCTs (13.3%) among 30 eligible studies in the most recent scoping review.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Studies are dominated by **Asian centers** (66.7%) and tertiary referral practice (86.7%).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **PROMs underreported** — no studies reported recurrence of prolapse symptoms as a primary outcome.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Long-term data > 10 years remain limited.
- **Higher urinary-symptom recurrence** in one comparative study warrants further investigation.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

---

## Key Principles

- Pectopexy uses **bilateral Cooper's-ligament fixation** as an alternative to sacrocolpopexy — same mesh-augmented apical-suspension principle, different anchor.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Avoids presacral dissection**; this removes the sacral-promontory dissection step, not all vascular or neurologic risk.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Shorter operative time** in several comparisons (Lin pooled mean difference −34.4 minutes); the 12-case CUSUM turning point came from one initial 18-patient, single-surgeon series.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Bowel and back-pain findings vary by cohort**; Chuang's 0/18 versus 4/21 observations were non-significant and do not prove absence of risk.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Critical anatomic relationship: the **iliopectineal ligament is medial to the external iliac vessels** in the Chuang technique; preserve the vessels and obturator neurovascular structures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Single-suture fixation** was tested in cadavers, not proven clinically equivalent to continuous fixation.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- In Noé's **832-patient combined-repair cohort**, 42 underwent apical re-fixation, and 87.9% of observed events occurred within four years; do not equate this with an isolated pectopexy success rate.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Higher urinary-symptom recurrence vs sacrocolpopexy** in one comparative cohort — counsel and follow accordingly.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- **Mesh-free and meshless variants** (cerclage pectopexy, Salman's modification) are emerging options for mesh-averse patients.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
