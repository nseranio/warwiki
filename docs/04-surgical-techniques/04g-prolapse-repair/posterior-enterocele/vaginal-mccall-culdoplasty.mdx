# McCall Culdoplasty

**McCall culdoplasty** is a native-tissue vaginal apical suspension performed at the time of hysterectomy to **prevent or treat vaginal vault prolapse and enterocele** by incorporating the uterosacral ligaments into the closure of the vaginal cuff and obliterating the cul-de-sac. The original technique is performed **vaginally at vaginal hysterectomy**; an **abdominal variant** is described at the time of abdominal hysterectomy, with identical principles. Both share the same anatomy and intent — only the access differs.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For the broader prolapse-repair atlas, see [Prolapse Repair](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair). For Moschcowitz / Halban variants targeting cul-de-sac obliteration without USL plication, see [Moschcowitz Procedure](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/moschcowitz-procedure) and [Halban Culdoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/halban-culdoplasty). For high USL suspension as a stand-alone apical operation, see [Uterosacral Ligament Suspension](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/uterosacral-ligament-suspension).

---

## Indications

- **Apical support and enterocele prevention** at the time of **vaginal hysterectomy** — the dominant use case; standard adjunct.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Apical support and enterocele prevention** at the time of **abdominal hysterectomy** — same operation performed via the abdominal route.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Treatment of established vault prolapse or enterocele**, in addition to prophylaxis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Advanced (stage 3–4) and lesser degrees** of uterovaginal prolapse — comparable success rates across severity in the Alas 2018 cohort.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Prophylactic application has also been described at **mini-laparoscopic hysterectomy**.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Surgical Technique

### Vaginal McCall (original)

1. After the uterus is removed during vaginal hysterectomy, **identify the uterosacral ligaments bilaterally**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
2. Place **one to two sutures** incorporating the uterosacral ligaments into the closure of the peritoneum and upper vagina, suspending the cuff to the USLs and obliterating the cul-de-sac.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
3. **Anterior or posterior colporrhaphy** is often performed concurrently as compartment-specific support requires.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
4. **Intraoperative cystoscopy** is recommended to confirm bilateral ureteral patency before final closure.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### Abdominal McCall (variant)

The same anatomic principles applied at the end of an abdominal hysterectomy: USL plication with culdesac obliteration. Used to incorporate apical support into the same operative session as abdominal hysterectomy and prevent future enterocele formation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Modified McCall variants

| Variant | Description | Outcome highlight |
|---|---|---|
| **Extraperitoneal high McCall** (Zilberlicht 2021) | Sutures placed extraperitoneally, higher and more lateral into the uterosacral / cardinal ligaments; achieves higher cuff suspension<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | — |
| **Modified McCall with extended dissection** (Ettore 2024) | Dissection of USLs to the ischial spines; ligaments shortened and attached to the vaginal apex and to both rectovaginal and vesicovaginal fasciae | **Total vaginal length 8.3 vs 6.4 cm**; higher patient satisfaction<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Double ligament suspension (DLS)** (Parisi 2020) | Cuff suspended to **both USLs and adnexal peduncles** | **Recurrence 5.9% vs 32.3%** vs traditional McCall<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |

## Outcomes

McCall culdoplasty is a durable native-tissue apical operation across modern series.

| Endpoint | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Objective vault support success | **97.1%** at mean 2.8 yr | Bushra 2021 (n = 490)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Subjective success | 94.1% | Bushra 2021<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Reoperation for vault recurrence | **1.0%** | Bushra 2021<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Long-term vault prolapse recurrence | **1%** at median 8.9 yr (2/200) | Schiavi 2018<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| Advanced (POP-Q ≥ 3) success | 76.3% vs 68.5% for lesser stages (p = 0.36) | Alas 2018<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Anterior compartment recurrence (cystocele) | 6–18% — most common failure site | Alas 2018, Bushra 2021<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |

A 2025 systematic review identified McCall culdoplasty among the **techniques associated with lower recurrence** for post-hysterectomy vault prolapse.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

## Comparison With Other Apical Techniques

| Technique | Apical recurrence | Vaginal length | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Traditional McCall** | 1–2.6% | Shorter (6.1–6.4 cm) | Simplicity, speed; standard adjunct to vaginal hysterectomy<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Modified McCall** | 2.5–14% | Longer (8.3 cm) | Better anatomy preservation<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Shull (high USLS)** | 1–1.4% | ~8 mm longer | Comparable to modified McCall; preserves vaginal length and may favor sexual function<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Sacrospinous fixation** | Variable | Maintained | Avoids ureteral risk; different axis<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Sacrocolpopexy** | Lower (~38% composite failure at 5 yr in SUPeR vs ~53% NTR) | Maintained | Mesh-augmented; durability benchmark<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |

McCall and Shull suspension show **no clinically significant differences** in anatomical outcomes, complication rates, or reoperation rates; Shull tends to preserve more vaginal length and may favor sexual function.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Complications

| Complication | Rate / note |
|---|---|
| **Ureteral kinking** | ~**2.9%** — the principal clinically significant complication; typically requires intraoperative release of the McCall suture. **Intraoperative cystoscopy is recommended** to confirm patency<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Ureteral injury | 0–1%<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| Bladder injury | Rare<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| Vaginal shortening | Recognized limitation of the traditional technique vs Shull or extended-dissection modifications<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)[[9]](#ref9)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Anterior compartment (cystocele) recurrence | 6–18% — the most common failure site<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |

## Key Principles

- McCall culdoplasty is the **standard apical adjunct at hysterectomy** for prevention or treatment of vault prolapse and enterocele — applicable via **either vaginal or abdominal access** with identical principles.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Native tissue, durable** — long-term vault prolapse recurrence ~1%; reoperation ~1%.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Comparable durability** across primary and advanced uterovaginal prolapse (Alas 2018).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Anterior compartment recurrence** (cystocele) is the dominant failure site — assess and address anterior support concurrently.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Intraoperative cystoscopy is mandatory** — ureteral kinking ~2.9% is the principal clinically significant complication.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Modifications that extend USL dissection toward the ischial spines** preserve more vaginal length and may improve recurrence (Ettore 2024, Parisi 2020 DLS).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Native tissue repair generally has higher composite failure** than sacrocolpopexy at 5 yr (SUPeR ~53% vs ~38%) — frame counseling accordingly.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
