# McCall Culdoplasty

**McCall culdoplasty** is a native-tissue apical-support and cul-de-sac-obliteration technique most often performed at vaginal hysterectomy for uterovaginal prolapse. Sutures incorporate the uterosacral support complex, posterior vaginal cuff and cul-de-sac peritoneum; technique descriptions differ in the number and course of internal and external sutures.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> Uterosacral fixation at abdominal or laparoscopic hysterectomy shares an anatomic goal but is not automatically the same studied operation or outcome population.

For the broader prolapse-repair atlas, see [Prolapse Repair](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair). For other cul-de-sac closure techniques, see [Moschcowitz Procedure](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/moschcowitz-procedure) and [Halban Culdoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/halban-culdoplasty). In the randomized comparison, the vaginal Moschcowitz-type procedure also approximated the uterosacral-cardinal complex in the midline; McCall additionally elevated the posterior apex.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> For high USL suspension as a stand-alone apical operation, see [Uterosacral Ligament Suspension](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/uterosacral-ligament-suspension). For McCall's 1957 description, see the [History of Prolapse Surgery & Urogynecology](/docs/roots/history/prolapse-urogynecology#mccall-culdoplasty-1957).

## Indications

- **Apical support and enterocele prevention or repair** during vaginal hysterectomy for uterovaginal prolapse. The 2025 ISGE guideline recommends McCall culdoplasty for stage II uterovaginal prolapse (Grade 1B); for stage III or IV prolapse, it recommends considering sacrospinous fixation, sacrocolpopexy or alternatives such as pectopexy (Grade 1B).<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> The original technique and most McCall outcome series concern vaginal hysterectomy.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Advanced uterovaginal prolapse** is not an automatic exclusion. In one retrospective cohort, 38 women with exteriorized advanced prolapse and 273 with lesser prolapse had no statistically detected overall-success difference, but the advanced group was small and anterior failure was more frequent.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> In a separate 68-patient randomized trial of stage III or greater uterine prolapse treated with vaginal hysterectomy, prolapse recurrence occurred in 12/35 after McCall versus 3/33 after vaginally assisted laparoscopic sacrocolpopexy at mean follow-up of approximately 26 months.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> The comparator and small sample limit transfer to other sacrocolpopexy routes or McCall modifications.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **Support at minimally invasive hysterectomy** has been described with vaginal-approach McCall after mini-laparoscopic hysterectomy and with a distinct laparoscopic uterosacral-plication technique.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- For established post-hysterectomy vault prolapse, select and counsel among dedicated apical repairs rather than assuming a hysterectomy-adjunct series estimates treatment success; see [Uterosacral Ligament Suspension](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/uterosacral-ligament-suspension) and the [Prolapse Repair atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair).

## Surgical Technique

### Vaginal McCall at hysterectomy

1. After vaginal hysterectomy, identify the uterosacral support and posterior cuff bilaterally and assess the enterocele or cul-de-sac defect.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
2. Place internal McCall plication sutures through the pararectal or uterosacral support across the cul-de-sac; a described external suture also incorporates posterior vaginal wall and peritoneum. The operative series cited here (Niblock) used one or two internal sutures of 0 monofilament absorbable material, each passed deep into the left pararectal fascia, across the front of the sigmoid colon and deep into the right pararectal fascia, plus a more cephalad external suture. Suture counts vary between descriptions.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
3. Tie the suspension or cul-de-sac closure without excessive cuff shortening or ureteral traction. Address anterior or posterior wall defects with compartment-specific repair when indicated; many outcome cohorts included these concomitant operations.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
4. **Ureteral patency.** Check bilateral ureteral patency with intraoperative cystoscopy after suspension sutures are tied. A compromised efflux pattern warrants assessment and, when suture-related, release or repositioning before completion. Both a published McCall series and the 490-case modified-McCall cohort detected intraoperative ureteral obstruction or kinking.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

### Abdominal McCall (variant)

At abdominal or laparoscopic hysterectomy, the uterosacral-cardinal support can be incorporated into cuff closure or plicated for apical support. Wall described a modified McCall culdoplasty during abdominal hysterectomy.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> In a 50-patient pilot randomized trial at total laparoscopic hysterectomy for benign indications, adding McCall to standard cuff closure showed no detected difference in vaginal apex position at 12 months.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> Gencdal performed McCall through the vaginal route after mini-laparoscopic hysterectomy, and Niblock compared vaginal McCall with laparoscopic uterosacral plication.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> Exposure, suture path and outcome evidence differ; specify the actual route-adapted operation in the operative record.

### Modified McCall variants

| Variant | Description | Reported outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Extraperitoneal high McCall** (Zilberlicht 2021) | Sutures placed extraperitoneally, higher and more laterally into the uterosacral-cardinal complex to resuspend the cuff; the reported operation also closed large levator-plate hiatal defects when present.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | In a longitudinal series of 176 women with stage 3–4 uterovaginal prolapse, any-compartment stage II or greater recurrence occurred in 25/176 (14%) at mean 19.4 months; 6/176 (3%) underwent another operation, all for enterocele recurrence<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |

Karmakar and Dwyer describe their exposure and fixation sequence for this extraperitoneal high variant at vaginal hysterectomy: after identifying and ligating the uterosacral/cardinal complex, it is dissected free laterally to its mid-portion to allow higher suture placement, while a Breisky-Navratil retractor lateralizes the bladder and ureters out of the operative field. A 1-0 Vicryl suture passes through the posterior vaginal wall and the mid-portion of the uterosacral/cardinal complex on both sides and is tied in the midline; a second pass through the vaginal vault re-supports and closes it, and the last 2-0 PDS suture from the anterior repair ties the complex at the vault to suspend the anterior and apical compartments. This sequence is specific to the chapter authors' modification.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

| Variant | Description | Reported outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Modified McCall with extended dissection** (Ettore 2024) | USLs dissected to the ischial spines and shortened; at that level, 0 PDS sutures transfix each USL medial to lateral (the authors’ stated precaution against ureteral injury). The Douglas pouch is closed high, and suspension sutures attach to the vaginal apex and duplicated anterior and posterior fasciae.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | In a prospective observational comparison, mean vaginal length 8.3 vs 6.4 cm at 12 months; any-compartment stage II or greater recurrence was 2/79 (2.5%) vs 5/75 (6.7%) among patients with complete 12-month follow-up (85 modified vs 86 traditional initially enrolled; the comparison group was an earlier cohort), described by the authors as a tendency toward lower recurrence<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Double ligament suspension (DLS)** (Parisi 2020) | Cuff suspended to both USLs and adnexal peduncles | In a retrospective 34-versus-34 comparison, stage ≥II recurrence was 2/34 with DLS vs 11/34 with traditional McCall at mean 22–23 months; further treatment was 1/34 vs 2/34<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |

## Outcomes

Several observational cohorts report low apical failure after McCall techniques, but success definitions, concomitant repairs and follow-up differ; these percentages are study results and are not a common predicted rate for every McCall variant.

| Endpoint | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Posterior enterocele after vaginal hysterectomy | 2/32 McCall-type, 10/33 vaginal Moschcowitz-type, 13/33 peritoneal closure only at three years | Cruikshank/Kovac 1999 randomized comparison of these three prophylactic closures; not a modern post-hysterectomy vault-repair trial<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| Objective vault support / subjective success | 97.1% / 94.1% at mean 2.8 years | Bushra 2021; single-surgeon, retrospective modified-McCall cohort, n = 490<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Reoperation for vault recurrence | 1.0% | Same Bushra cohort; distinct from anatomic or composite recurrence<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Vault prolapse after hysterectomy | 2/200 at median 8.9 years | Schiavi 2018 retrospective modified-McCall group; Shull group also 2/214<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| Overall success in advanced versus lesser prolapse | 76.3% of 38 vs 68.5% of 273 at ≥1 year, p = 0.3553 | Alas 2018 retrospective cohort<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Anterior-compartment failure in advanced versus lesser prolapse | 18.4% vs 6.2% at ≥1 year | Same Alas cohort; this is not a pooled McCall recurrence range<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |

A 2025 systematic review discussed McCall among preventive approaches, but its four included studies (497 women) were heterogeneous and summarized qualitatively; it cannot establish a McCall-specific rank or a head-to-head recurrence advantage.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

## Comparison With Other Apical Techniques

| Comparison | What the cited study observed | Counseling limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Modified McCall vs Shull suspension** | Schiavi's retrospective groups had 2/200 vs 2/214 vault prolapses at median 8.9 years; vaginal length shortened more with McCall, while sexual-function scores improved more with Shull.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | Not a randomized equivalence result; concomitant repairs and selection matter. |
| **Modified McCall vs Shull suspension** | Spelzini's retrospective comparison reported roughly 8 mm longer total vaginal length with Shull and no statistically detected anatomic-outcome difference.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | Surgeon selection by age/sexual activity limits causal inference. |
| **McCall vs high USLS (Shull)** | Novara's retrospective 155 McCall / 69 Shull cohort recorded objective cuff recurrence 4/155 vs 1/69 at median 15 vs 13 months.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | Surgeon familiarity determined technique. |
| **Traditional McCall vs double-ligament modification** | Parisi found stage ≥II recurrence 11/34 vs 2/34 and longer vaginal length with DLS.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | Small, nonrandomized, ~2-year comparison; recurrence is not the same as retreatment. |
| **Vaginal McCall vs laparoscopic uterosacral plication** | Niblock's retrospective comparison (two surgeons) recorded post-hysterectomy vault prolapse in 0/70 after vaginal hysterectomy with McCall vs 12/73 after total laparoscopic hysterectomy with uterosacral plication.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> | Different hysterectomy routes and plication technique; follow-up duration is not comparable across arms and selection is uncontrolled. |
| **Vaginal McCall vs vNOTES high USLS** | Arkan reported 6/46 vs 1/46 cuff prolapses at 12 months and mean length 8.2 vs 9.0 cm.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> | Small prospective cohort; access, visualization and apical technique differ together. |

For a post-hysterectomy vault comparison, the ASPIRe trial studied native-tissue repair (mostly sacrospinous fixation, some USLS) against sacrocolpopexy and transvaginal mesh; it did not test McCall culdoplasty. Its modeled 36-month composite failure was 43% with native repair and 28% with sacrocolpopexy.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> That endpoint includes anatomy and symptoms or retreatment, not simply recurrent vault prolapse, and cannot be substituted for any McCall row above.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> The Cochrane reviews provide wider prolapse context and do not give McCall-specific operative instructions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Complications

| Complication | Rate / note |
|---|---|
| **Ureteral kinking/obstruction** | 2.9% in Bushra's 490 modified-McCall operations; one intraoperative suture release after cystoscopic obstruction in a separate 70-case McCall series. Check patency after suspension sutures.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| **Ureteral or bladder injury** | Arkan's 46-patient McCall arm reported one ureteral and one bladder injury; this is a small selected cohort, not a general 0–1% McCall risk estimate.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Postoperative urinary retention** | Niblock reported 6/70 after vaginal hysterectomy with McCall, each managed with intermittent self-catheterization. Most patients also underwent wall repair, so this is a cohort rate, not an isolated McCall risk.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| **Postoperative hemorrhage** | In the same cohort, 2/70 returned to theatre for laparotomy within 24 hours for intra-abdominal bleeding. Hysterectomy and concomitant repairs limit attribution to the culdoplasty alone.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| **Vaginal shortening / sexual function** | Greater length reduction with modified McCall than Shull in Schiavi's retrospective comparison; procedure selection and concomitant repairs limit attribution.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Other-compartment recurrence** | Alas found more anterior failure in the advanced subgroup; posterior and anterior defects warrant separate assessment rather than assuming cuff support corrects them.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |

## Key Principles

- The best-described McCall use is apical support and cul-de-sac closure at vaginal hysterectomy. Describe abdominal or laparoscopic adaptations by their actual suture path and do not import vaginal-series outcomes.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- Check bilateral ureteral patency after suspension sutures; published series document suture-related obstruction detected intraoperatively.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- Modified-McCall cohorts report favorable vault results alongside anterior or posterior failure.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- Extended-dissection and double-ligament variants may preserve more vaginal length or improve anatomy in their cohorts.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- ASPIRe is a post-hysterectomy vault trial, not a McCall or SUPeR trial. Use its composite endpoint only when counseling about the procedures and population it actually studied.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
