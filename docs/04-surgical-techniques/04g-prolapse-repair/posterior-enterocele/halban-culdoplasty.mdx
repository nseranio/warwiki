# Halban Culdoplasty

**Halban culdoplasty** is a historical cul-de-sac-obliteration approach often described with anteroposterior (sagittal) sutures between posterior vaginal/peritoneal and anterior rectal surfaces. It differs conceptually from the concentric Moschcowitz approach and from McCall culdoplasty, which includes apical-support elements. Names and technical details vary across reports; Halban is not a standardized standalone apical suspension.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For the concentric alternative, see [Moschcowitz Procedure](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/moschcowitz-procedure). For the vaginal-hysterectomy alternative, see [McCall Culdoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/mccall-culdoplasty). For apical mesh suspension, see [Sacrocolpopexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrocolpopexy).

## What the evidence can distinguish

The directly identified 1999 randomized study did **not** include a Halban arm. It compared a vaginal Moschcowitz-type closure, McCall-type culdoplasty, and peritoneal closure at vaginal hysterectomy; McCall had fewer reported posterior-superior prolapse/enterocele events at three years. That result cannot be used to assign Halban an exact success rate, to prove it equivalent to Moschcowitz, or to rank its ureteral safety.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In a small prospective series, sacrocolpopexy combined with pouch-of-Douglas obliteration and posterior mesh extension had no recorded vault or enterocele recurrence at mean 26 months, but recurrent/de novo rectocele and altered defecatory symptoms occurred. The combined intervention cannot establish an independent benefit, preferred culdoplasty orientation, or a mandatory adjunct at sacrocolpopexy.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Evidence boundaries

| Question | What can be said | What cannot be concluded |
|---|---|---|
| Procedure identity | Descriptions use a sagittal/anteroposterior cul-de-sac-closure concept. | A universal suture count, material, bite depth, route, or anatomic plane. |
| Comparison with Moschcowitz | Both labels refer to cul-de-sac-obliteration approaches, with different described suture orientations. | That Halban is equivalent, safer, or has a lower ureteral-injury rate. |
| Comparison with McCall | The cited trial favors McCall over its studied vaginal Moschcowitz-type closure. | That Halban is inferior to McCall by a known amount or that this result applies to all routes. |
| Use with sacrocolpopexy | Cul-de-sac obliteration has appeared in combined posterior-compartment repairs. | That it should always be added or independently prevents enterocele/defecatory symptoms. |

## Operative safety

This is not a step-by-step operative protocol. Whether to close the cul-de-sac, how to protect adjacent urinary and bowel structures, and whether to perform urinary-tract assessment must be determined by current anatomy, training, associated procedure, and institutional protocol. Historical midline descriptions should not be converted into a guarantee that lateral structures are uninjured or into a fixed safety rule.

## Follow-up

Follow-up should be driven by bulge symptoms, bowel and urinary function, pelvic pain, bleeding/discharge, sexual function, and examination when indicated. New obstructive defecatory symptoms, pain, or posterior/apical prolapse should prompt evaluation rather than assumption about the particular closure technique.

## Key principles

- Halban is a historical sagittal cul-de-sac-closure concept, not a standardized apical-suspension procedure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- There is no directly identified randomized Halban comparison supporting an exact success rate, equivalence claim, or ureteral-risk ranking.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- The combined sacrocolpopexy series cannot show that a specific cul-de-sac closure independently improves outcomes.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Individualized posterior-compartment assessment is more defensible than a universal technical recipe.
