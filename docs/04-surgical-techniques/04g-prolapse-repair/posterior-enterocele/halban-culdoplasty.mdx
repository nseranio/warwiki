# Halban Culdoplasty

The **classic Halban culdoplasty** is a **cul-de-sac obliteration** technique using **sagittal (anteroposterior) sutures** along the pouch of Douglas, approximating the posterior vaginal/peritoneal surface toward anterior rectal or rectosigmoid serosa. Its defining distinction from the Moschcowitz procedure is **sagittal rather than concentric purse-string orientation**. Avoiding lateral pelvic-sidewall bites is an anatomic rationale, not a measured comparative reduction in ureteral injury.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> A procedure called *modified Halban colpopexy* may additionally attach the cuff to uterosacral support; its apical effect must not be attributed to cul-de-sac closure alone.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

For the orthogonal concentric variant, see [Moschcowitz Procedure](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/moschcowitz-procedure). For the apical-elevation alternative performed at vaginal hysterectomy, see [McCall Culdoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/mccall-culdoplasty). For sacrocolpopexy, see [Sacrocolpopexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrocolpopexy).

---

## Historical Background

Attributed to **Josef Halban** (1870–1937), a Viennese gynecologist who described sagittal (front-to-back) cul-de-sac sutures as an alternative to Moschcowitz's concentric purse-strings (1912). The anatomical reasoning is that midline placement avoids lateral pelvic-sidewall bites near the ureteral course; lower comparative injury risk has not been established.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

## Indications

| Setting | Use |
|---|---|
| **Treatment of enterocele** | Obliteration of a deep cul-de-sac that permits herniation of small bowel or sigmoid<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Adjunct at abdominal or laparoscopic sacrocolpopexy** | A described combined technique; the apical suspension and closure are not independently evaluated by its outcome series<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| **Prophylaxis at hysterectomy** (abdominal or vaginal) | Historical application; the Cruikshank 1999 vaginal-hysterectomy trial favored McCall over vaginal Moschcowitz-type repair and simple closure, but did not test Halban<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Adjunct at rectopexy** for rectal prolapse | Historical use<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

## Surgical Technique — Abdominal

Performed via laparotomy or laparoscopy with the patient in Trendelenburg position.

1. **Cul-de-sac exposure.** Pack small bowel and sigmoid cephalad. Assess the enterocele and cul-de-sac depth in relation to the apical and posterior defects; depth alone does not establish symptomatic prolapse.
2. **First (deepest) suture.** Place a **permanent or delayed-absorbable suture** (historical examples include 2-0 silk or braided polyester; these are not a universal prescription) at the **deepest point of the cul-de-sac**, incorporating a **sagittal (front-to-back) bite**:
   - **Posterior vaginal-wall peritoneum** (or vaginal cuff) anteriorly
   - **Seromuscular layer of the anterior rectal wall** (never full thickness) posteriorly
3. **Critical distinction from Moschcowitz.** Sutures are intended to avoid lateral uterosacral-ligament incorporation and remain in the **midline sagittal plane**, approximating posterior vagina to anterior rectum in a series of anteroposterior bites.
4. **Serial parallel sagittal sutures.** Place several interrupted sutures in parallel sagittal rows progressing from deep to superficial. The number and material are adjusted to the actual defect and tissues rather than fixed by comparative evidence.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
5. **Verification.** Confirm complete cul-de-sac obliteration; ureteral patency check is recommended despite the lower theoretical risk.

## Halban vs Moschcowitz vs McCall

| Feature | **Halban** | Moschcowitz | McCall |
|---|---|---|---|
| Suture orientation | **Sagittal (anteroposterior)** | Concentric purse-strings | Transverse / oblique |
| Structures incorporated | Posterior vaginal/peritoneal surface + anterior rectal or rectosigmoid serosa (midline) | Posterior vaginal peritoneum + lateral support/sidewall + anterior rectal serosa (circumferential) | USL-cardinal or pararectal support + posterior vaginal cuff/peritoneum; **rectal serosa is not a routine McCall fixation target** |
| Ureteral considerations | Midline placement avoids lateral bites; comparative risk unestablished | Lateral purse-string bites can kink or incorporate a ureter | USL-cardinal bites require attention to the ureters |
| Apical elevation | Not from **isolated classic closure**; modified Halban *colpopexy* can add USL fixation | Not from isolated cul-de-sac closure | **Yes** — plicates USL-cardinal complex and elevates posterior apex |
| Ligament plication | None in classic closure; modified colpopexy can add USL fixation | Brings USLs to midline | Plicates USL-cardinal complex |
| Enterocele events at 3 yr in Cruikshank 1999 | Not studied | 10/33 (vaginal Moschcowitz-type) | 2/32 |

The trial cannot supply a Halban success rate or establish Halban’s relative efficacy or safety.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Rationale for Sagittal Placement

The rationale is to **avoid lateral suture placement** near the ureters. Ureteral relationships vary with pelvic level, prolapse, and prior surgery; a fixed distance from the uterosacral ligament is not a safe operative landmark. Sagittal placement reduces lateral extension but does not guarantee ureteral protection or establish lower comparative complication rates.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The classic closure alone does **not** provide a separately established apical suspension. A modified Halban *colpopexy* has been described with additional uterosacral-to-cuff fixation; its apical support comes from that added fixation, not from the sagittal cul-de-sac sutures in isolation.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

## Outcomes

### Standalone enterocele prophylaxis

No direct Halban arm was included in the cited randomized comparison. The 3-year results support McCall over vaginal Moschcowitz-type repair and peritoneal closure at vaginal hysterectomy; they cannot be extrapolated into a numerical Halban success rate.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

### As adjunct to sacrocolpopexy

Baessler 2001 enrolled **33 women; 31 returned for follow-up** at 12–48 months (mean 26) after sacrocolpopexy, pouch-of-Douglas obliteration and posterior mesh extension. No vault or enterocele recurrence was observed among those followed. The abstract does **not name a Halban closure**, and the result belongs to the **combined operation**, not an isolated estimate of Halban efficacy.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> A separate laparoscopic sacrocolpopexy series explicitly describes **Halban culdoplasty with sutures in posterior mesh before peritonization**, but likewise cannot isolate a closure benefit.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

## Advantages and Limitations

| Advantage | Limitation |
|---|---|
| Sagittal placement avoids lateral purse-string bites<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | **Classic closure alone provides no apical suspension**; a modified colpopexy can add USL fixation<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| Classic sagittal closure does not require USL fixation | Halban was not tested against McCall in the cited trial; modified *colpopexy* may add USL fixation<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| Described abdominally, laparoscopically, and in vaginal modifications | **Classic closure does not plicate ligaments**; apical weakness requires a separately planned suspension<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| Provides a described method of cul-de-sac obliteration<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | **Limited comparative data** — no direct Halban-versus-Moschcowitz/McCall trial was identified in the cited evidence<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |

---

## Complications

| Complication | Rate / note |
|---|---|
| **Ureteral injury** | Midline placement does not eliminate risk; assess ureteral safety according to the entire repair and intraoperative findings<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Rectal injury | If sutures are placed full-thickness rather than seromuscular |
| Small bowel obstruction | Potential intraperitoneal-closure complication; the cited Moschcowitz case report does not establish a Halban-specific rate |
| Enterocele recurrence | Comparative recurrence versus McCall is not established by the cited trial<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Postoperative constipation / altered defecation | 28% reported altered defecation in Baessler’s combined sacrocolpopexy series; this is not a Halban-specific complication rate<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |

## Vaginal Modification

A vaginal enterocele repair may use related sagittal/fascial closure concepts, but the cited Milani tutorial and Miklos site-specific series do **not** establish an outcome for an isolated, named Halban repair. In the Miklos series, all 17 women also underwent USL vault suspension and 15 had posterior colporrhaphy.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> A descriptive Halban-type sequence is:

- After enterocele sac identification and excision, visualize peritoneal edges.
- Place interrupted sutures in a **sagittal direction** approximating posterior vaginal wall fascia to anterior rectal wall fascia.
- Tie sutures to obliterate the cul-de-sac from below.
- Often combined with **site-specific fascial defect repair**, reapproximating pubocervical and rectovaginal fascia to close the hernial port.

---

## Laparoscopic Adaptation

Readily adaptable to laparoscopic surgery. Sagittal suture placement is technically straightforward laparoscopically; midline orientation avoids lateral dissection near the ureters. Some laparoscopic enterocele repairs labeled "modified Moschcowitz" actually employ a sagittal (Halban-type) suture orientation with running suture from posterior vaginal fascia to anterior rectal wall.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

---

## Current Clinical Role

The Halban culdoplasty occupies a **niche but relevant role**:

- An option when sagittal cul-de-sac closure suits the anatomy; its ureteral-safety rationale is anatomical rather than comparative trial evidence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Described adjunct to sacrocolpopexy** for cul-de-sac obliteration when sagittal placement is preferred; the directly described combined laparoscopic series is historical, not proof that closure is required in every present-day sacrocolpopexy.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **McCall provides apical elevation** as well as cul-de-sac closure; Halban itself is not an apical suspension.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Classic closure is not a standalone apical suspension** — when apical support is needed, add an actual suspension (sacrocolpopexy, sacrospinous fixation or USL fixation); distinguish modified Halban *colpopexy* that includes such fixation.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

---

## Key Principles

- Halban culdoplasty obliterates the cul-de-sac with **sagittal interrupted sutures** from posterior vaginal peritoneum to anterior rectal serosa — midline only, no lateral extension.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Midline orientation avoids lateral bites**, but a lower ureteral-injury rate has not been established.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- Place **several sagittal interrupted sutures** from deep to superficial; incorporate **seromuscular** rectum (not full thickness) and posterior vaginal peritoneum / fascia; exact count and material depend on the repair.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Classic closure alone does not supply a separately established apical suspension**; modified Halban *colpopexy* can include USL fixation.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Do not assign Halban the Moschcowitz trial result**; the cited randomized study did not evaluate Halban.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- In a described laparoscopic sacrocolpopexy sequence, **Halban sutures were placed in posterior mesh before peritonization**; sequence is operation-specific rather than a universal rule.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Discuss bowel symptoms in the context of the complete repair**; Baessler’s 28% altered-defecation figure came from combined sacrocolpopexy, not isolated Halban culdoplasty.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
