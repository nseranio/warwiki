# Moschcowitz Procedure

The **Moschcowitz procedure** is a cul-de-sac obliteration technique, traditionally using purse-string sutures to reduce a deep posterior peritoneal space associated with enterocele. It is historically related to rectal-prolapse surgery and has been adapted to vaginal and abdominal prolapse operations. Its precise contemporary role, configuration, and use with other repairs vary; it is not a universal adjunct to sacrocolpopexy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For the sagittal alternative, see [Halban Culdoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/halban-culdoplasty). For the vaginal-hysterectomy alternative, see [McCall Culdoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/mccall-culdoplasty). For apical mesh suspension, see [Sacrocolpopexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrocolpopexy).

## Evidence that distinguishes the procedures

The strongest directly identified comparison is a historical randomized trial at vaginal hysterectomy. One hundred women were randomized to a vaginal Moschcowitz-type closure, McCall-type culdoplasty, or peritoneal closure. At three years, posterior-superior prolapse/enterocele was reported in 2/32 McCall, 10/33 Moschcowitz-type, and 13/33 peritoneal-closure participants. This supports McCall over the studied vaginal Moschcowitz-type approach **in that setting**; it does not establish the best abdominal or laparoscopic enterocele operation, or a universal result for all variants.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In a prospective 33-person series, sacrocolpopexy combined with pouch-of-Douglas obliteration and posterior mesh extension had no recorded vault-prolapse or enterocele recurrence during a mean 26-month follow-up. The same cohort had recurrent/de novo rectocele and altered defecatory symptoms; the combined procedure cannot isolate an effect of cul-de-sac obliteration or establish a mandatory adjunct.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Evidence boundaries

| Question | What can be said | What cannot be concluded |
|---|---|---|
| Vaginal-hysterectomy prophylaxis | The 1999 randomized study favored McCall-type culdoplasty over its studied vaginal Moschcowitz-type closure at three years. | That every Moschcowitz, Halban, or peritoneal-closure variant has the same outcome, or that the result applies to abdominal sacrocolpopexy. |
| Abdominal use | Cul-de-sac obliteration has been reported as part of combined sacrocolpopexy/posterior-compartment repairs. | That it should always be added, prevents every enterocele, or independently improves bowel function. |
| Technique | Purse-string cul-de-sac closure is the defining historical concept. | A validated number of sutures, depth of bite, anatomic distance, suture material, cystoscopy rule, or mesh-extension protocol. |
| Complications | Pelvic surgery requires attention to surrounding bowel, urinary tract, vascular, and neural structures. | A procedure-specific complication rate or a claim that one orientation eliminates ureteral or bowel risk. |

## Operative safety

This is not a step-by-step operative protocol. In a specific patient, the need for cul-de-sac closure, surgical route, extent of dissection, suture technique, urinary-tract assessment, and postoperative surveillance should follow current training, anatomy, institutional protocol, and the associated prolapse procedure.

Avoid converting historical descriptions into a universal recommendation. The small contemporary-accessible evidence base does not validate fixed concentric-suture counts, routine rectal-wall incorporation, a required cystoscopy protocol, or a fixed order relative to mesh peritonization.

## Follow-up

Follow-up should be driven by bulge symptoms, bowel and urinary function, pelvic pain, bleeding/discharge, sexual function, and examination when indicated. New obstructive defecatory symptoms, pain, or suspected recurrent posterior/apical prolapse should prompt assessment rather than attribution to one component of a combined reconstruction.

## Key principles

- Moschcowitz describes a historical purse-string cul-de-sac-obliteration concept; variants and associated repairs differ.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- At vaginal hysterectomy, the identified randomized trial favored the studied McCall technique over the studied vaginal Moschcowitz-type closure at three years.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Evidence from a combined sacrocolpopexy series cannot prove that cul-de-sac obliteration is independently necessary or beneficial.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Individualized planning and careful evaluation of posterior-compartment symptoms remain more defensible than a universal technical recipe.
