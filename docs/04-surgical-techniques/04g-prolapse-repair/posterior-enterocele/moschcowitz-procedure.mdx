# Moschcowitz Procedure

The **Moschcowitz procedure** is a **cul-de-sac obliteration** technique using **concentric purse-string sutures** in the pouch of Douglas. It was described by **Alexis V. Moschcowitz in 1912** for rectal prolapse, based on a sliding-hernia theory. Abdominal and vaginal variants have since been used for enterocele repair or prevention, including historical adjunctive use at sacrocolpopexy. The cited reports do not establish how frequently it is used in current practice or that it is required during sacrocolpopexy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

For the orthogonal sagittal alternative, see [Halban Culdoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/halban-culdoplasty). For the McCall culdoplasty performed at vaginal hysterectomy, see [McCall Culdoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/mccall-culdoplasty). For sacrocolpopexy, see [Sacrocolpopexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrocolpopexy).

## Indications

| Setting | Use |
|---|---|
| **Adjunct at abdominal sacrocolpopexy** | Reported use — cul-de-sac obliteration to prevent postoperative enterocele<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **Treatment of enterocele** | Obliteration of a deep cul-de-sac allowing herniation of small bowel or sigmoid between vagina and rectum<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Prophylaxis at vaginal hysterectomy** (vaginal Moschcowitz-type modification) | Historical option; the cited three-arm trial found fewer posterior enteroceles after McCall-type repair at 3 years<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Adjunct at abdominal rectopexy** for rectal prolapse | Historical use; contemporary rectal-prolapse operations are selected from several abdominal and perineal approaches rather than this original sliding-hernia repair<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| **Combined pelvic-floor reconstruction** | When sacrocolpopexy is performed with cul-de-sac obliteration and posterior mesh extension<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |

## Surgical Technique — Abdominal

Performed via laparotomy or laparoscopically, with the patient in Trendelenburg and bowel packed out of the pelvis.

1. **Cul-de-sac exposure.** Retract small bowel and sigmoid colon cephalad to expose the pouch of Douglas. Assess actual herniation and apical/posterior support; cul-de-sac depth alone does not establish a symptomatic enterocele.
2. **First (deepest) purse-string.** Place a **permanent or delayed-absorbable suture** (historical examples include 2-0 silk or braided polyester, not a universal material prescription) at the **deepest point** of the cul-de-sac, incorporating in a circumferential bite:
   - **Posterior vaginal-wall peritoneum** (anteriorly)
   - **Uterosacral ligaments** (laterally)
   - **Anterior rectal wall seromuscular layer** (posteriorly) — **never full thickness**
3. **Critical safety relationship — ureters.** Identify the ureteral course for the actual anatomy and keep bites away from it; a fixed distance from the uterosacral ligament is not a safe landmark. Lateral bites can incorporate or kink a ureter. Assess patency according to the entire repair.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
4. **Tie the first purse-string.** Obliterates the deepest portion of the cul-de-sac.
5. **Serial concentric purse-strings.** Place several progressively larger purse-strings more cephalad, each tied sequentially to close the pouch from deep to superficial. Number and extent depend on the defect and tissue, not a fixed comparative-evidence rule. The final suture approximates peritoneal surfaces near the posterior vaginal fornix or cuff.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
6. **Verification.** Confirm complete cul-de-sac obliteration with no residual peritoneal pocket. Inspect the ureteral course and assess **ureteral patency with cystoscopy and bilateral efflux** as appropriate to the repair; visualizing an intact ureter alone does not establish patency.

## Vaginal Moschcowitz-Type Modification

A vaginal adaptation at the time of vaginal hysterectomy:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- After uterus removal, visualize cul-de-sac from below.
- Bring uterosacral-cardinal complex together in the midline.
- Place purse-string or interrupted sutures to close the cul-de-sac and approximate the uterosacral ligaments.

**Key distinction from McCall culdoplasty:** the Moschcowitz-type vaginal procedure emphasizes **cul-de-sac closure**, whereas McCall includes **uterosacral-cardinal plication and posterior-apical elevation**. These are operative differences; the trial did not isolate which component caused its outcome difference.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

---

## Laparoscopic Modification

Cadeddu 1996 description of a laparoscopic adaptation:<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- Three trocars + transvaginal digital manipulation; reduce the enterocele.
- Obliterate the cul-de-sac by **approximating posterior vaginal fascia to the anterior rectal wall** with a running suture.
- Small series (n = 3): no operative morbidity, no enterocele recurrence at mean 10.5 mo follow-up.

## Comparative Outcomes

### Cruikshank & Kovac 1999 — landmark RCT

100 women randomized to Moschcowitz-type vs McCall culdoplasty vs simple peritoneal closure at the time of vaginal hysterectomy. **3-year enterocele prevention**:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Technique at vaginal hysterectomy | Posterior enterocele at 3 years |
|---|---|
| **McCall culdoplasty** | **6%** (2/32) |
| Moschcowitz | 30% (10/33) |
| Simple peritoneal closure | 39% (13/33) |

McCall had the fewest posterior enteroceles. The published abstract reports an **overall three-arm comparison** (χ² = 11.27, 2 degrees of freedom, **p = 0.004**), not separate pairwise p values for McCall against each alternative. These results apply to the studied **vaginal-hysterectomy methods**, not abdominal Moschcowitz or Halban closure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Moschcowitz vs Halban — orientation matters

| | Moschcowitz | Halban |
|---|---|---|
| Suture orientation | **Concentric (purse-string)** | **Sagittal (anteroposterior)** |
| Lateral suture extension | Circumferential bites can reach the lateral ureteral course | Classic sagittal bites stay midline; comparative ureteral-injury risk is unmeasured |
| Apical elevation | Not from isolated closure | Not from isolated classic closure; modified colpopexy can add USL fixation |
| Cul-de-sac obliteration | Effective | Effective |

The **isolated classic closures** are not apical suspensions. A reported *modified Halban colpopexy* adds USL-to-cuff fixation; do not assign that variant's support to sagittal closure alone.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Role as Adjunct to Sacrocolpopexy

One described application is **adjunct cul-de-sac obliteration during abdominal sacrocolpopexy**:<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

- After mesh attachment to vaginal vault and sacral promontory, the cul-de-sac is obliterated with Moschcowitz-type purse-strings before peritonization to prevent postoperative enterocele.
- **Baessler & Schuessler 2001**: 33 women underwent sacrocolpopexy with pouch-of-Douglas obliteration and posterior mesh extension; **31 returned for follow-up** (mean 26 months), with no vault or enterocele recurrence among those followed. The abstract does not name a Moschcowitz closure. These are **combined-operation results**, not an isolated Moschcowitz effect.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Winters 2000**: the authors advocated combining abdominal sacrocolpopexy with enterocele repair and cul-de-sac obliteration. This is a historical operative recommendation, not evidence that every contemporary sacrocolpopexy requires the same closure.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Complications

| Complication | Rate / note |
|---|---|
| **Ureteral injury / kinking** | Lateral purse-string placement can endanger a ureter. Identify its actual course, avoid compromising bites, and assess patency according to the full repair; no comparative injury rate versus Halban is established<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| Small bowel obstruction | Reported delayed after Moschcowitz closure; the cited case report does not establish its frequency<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| Rectal injury | If sutures are placed too deeply through the rectal wall |
| Bleeding | From uterosacral-ligament vessels |
| **Altered defecation** | 28% in Baessler / Schuessler’s combined sacrocolpopexy series; the authors considered possible denervation during rectal mobilization. This is **not** a Moschcowitz-specific rate<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |

## Outcomes Summary

| Setting | Result |
|---|---|
| Standalone enterocele prophylaxis at vaginal hysterectomy | 10/33 posterior enteroceles after vaginal Moschcowitz-type repair versus 2/32 after McCall at 3 years in the cited trial<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Adjunct at abdominal sacrocolpopexy | No enterocele or vault recurrence at mean 26 months in a small combined-operation series; independent closure benefit unestablished<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Primary treatment of rectal prolapse | Historical sliding-hernia operation; modern operation selection includes several abdominal and perineal approaches<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |

## Current Status

The Moschcowitz procedure occupies a **diminished but still relevant** role:

- The studied **vaginal Moschcowitz-type prophylaxis** produced more posterior enteroceles than McCall at 3 years; the trial does not determine current use rates.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- The original **rectal-prolapse role** is historical; contemporary rectal-prolapse repair offers several approaches, not a single mandated replacement.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Described adjunct to sacrocolpopexy** for cul-de-sac obliteration; the cited series do not prove a separately necessary closure or specify a universal modern technique.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- The **laparoscopic modification** remains feasible for MIS enterocele repair.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Circumferential placement warrants **ureteral attention**; sagittal Halban placement avoids lateral bites, but comparative injury rates have not been established.

---

## Key Principles

- The Moschcowitz procedure obliterates the cul-de-sac with **concentric purse-strings** from deep to superficial; its original rectal-prolapse design and later enterocele applications are distinct.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Protect the ureters** by identifying their actual course and avoiding compromising bites; assess patency when indicated by the full repair.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- Incorporate **seromuscular** rectum (never full thickness) and posterior vaginal-wall peritoneum / fascia.
- Use **several concentric purse-strings** deep-to-superficial; no fixed suture count is established by the cited source.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- The cited randomized trial favored **McCall over vaginal Moschcowitz-type repair** at vaginal hysterectomy (2/32 versus 10/33 posterior enteroceles at 3 years).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Halban (sagittal) is the orientation alternative** that avoids lateral bites; a lower ureteral-injury rate has not been measured.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- In historical combined sacrocolpopexy descriptions, cul-de-sac closure can occur before mesh peritonization; do not treat the sequence as mandatory for every repair.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- Be alert to bowel obstruction after any intraperitoneal closure; Baessler’s altered-defecation rate belongs to a **combined** reconstruction, not isolated Moschcowitz closure.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
