# Prolapse Repair

Pelvic organ prolapse (POP) repair encompasses native-tissue, mesh-augmented, and obliterative procedures targeting the anterior, posterior, and apical compartments. Surgical planning must account for compartment-specific anatomy (DeLancey Levels I–III), patient goals including uterine preservation and sexual activity, prior surgery, and mesh history.

> See [POP Overview](/docs/clinical-conditions/03c-pelvic-support/pelvic-organ-prolapse) for pathophysiology, risk stratification, and trial data (OPTIMAL, PEOPLE).

## Decision Framework

Plan around bothersome symptoms, compartment anatomy and patient goals, including uterine preservation, vaginal intercourse, mesh preferences and recovery. Assess and restore apical support when it is deficient; anatomic recurrence, bothersome recurrence and reoperation are different outcomes. In ASPIRe (Menefee 2024), 376 women with *post-hysterectomy vault prolapse* were randomized, and 360 underwent a study operation. Modeled 36-month composite failure was 28% after sacrocolpopexy, 29% after historical transvaginal-mesh repair and 43% after vaginal native-tissue repair. Sacrocolpopexy was superior to native tissue for that endpoint (adjusted HR 0.57; 99% CI 0.33–0.98); the trial's per-protocol mesh-versus-sacrocolpopexy noninferiority result is not equivalence. Vaginal POP mesh products studied there are not marketed in the US. See the [source-scoped interpretation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/prolapse-repair-principles#post-hysterectomy-vault-prolapse-aspire) and [FDA route distinctions](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/implants-and-prosthetics/urogynecologic-surgical-mesh-implants).

Uterine-preserving surgery should be offered as an option to surgical candidates without contraindications, after an individualized benefit-risk discussion of preservation and hysterectomy ([AUGS Clinical Practice Statement 2025](https://doi.org/10.1097/SPV.0000000000001667)). Evidence depends on the specific operation and route; a nonrandomized cohort cannot prove general superiority over hysterectomy. Colpocleisis is a useful obliterative option for a patient who does not intend future penetrative vaginal intercourse; age or frailty alone does not dictate it. See [Principles of Prolapse Repair](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/prolapse-repair-principles) for the Ruffolo/Brennand and continence-source limits.

POP is diagnosed clinically. Document symptoms, POP-Q compartments, urinary emptying and leakage, bowel and sexual function, prior surgery and goals. Discuss observation, supervised pelvic floor muscle training, pessary and surgery. A pessary trial is an option, not a prerequisite for surgery; NICE recommends discussing surgery when nonsurgical management has not helped *or is declined*. Assess occult SUI or voiding dysfunction when the result would change a proposed continence plan; urodynamics is not required for every prolapse operation. See the [assessment and treatment-selection source review](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/prolapse-repair-principles#assessment-and-treatment-selection).

### Treatment Selection by Clinical Scenario

| Clinical Scenario | Potential approach | Alternative(s) | Important caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary uterine prolapse, desires uterine preservation** | [Sacrospinous hysteropexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrospinous-hysteropexy), [Manchester procedure](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/manchester-fothergill) or [sacrohysteropexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrohysteropexy), according to anatomy and route | Uterosacral hysteropexy | Evaluate uterine/cervical pathology and future surveillance; operations are not interchangeable |
| **Primary uterine prolapse, does not desire uterine preservation** | [Vaginal hysterectomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/vaginal-hysterectomy) with planned apical support ([USLS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/uterosacral-ligament-suspension) or [SSLF](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrospinous-ligament-fixation)) | [McCall culdoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/mccall-culdoplasty) at hysterectomy | Do not leave apical support unaddressed after prolapse hysterectomy |
| **Post-hysterectomy vault prolapse, stage II** | Vaginal native-tissue [USLS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/uterosacral-ligament-suspension) or [SSLF](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrospinous-ligament-fixation) | [Sacrocolpopexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrocolpopexy) | Do not select solely from POP-Q stage; discuss mesh and operative trade-offs |
| **Post-hysterectomy vault prolapse, stage III–IV** | [Sacrocolpopexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrocolpopexy) or vaginal native-tissue suspension according to goals, risk and expertise | [USLS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/uterosacral-ligament-suspension) or [SSLF](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrospinous-ligament-fixation) when vaginal repair is preferred | Discuss stage-specific recurrence and mesh harms; no absolute NTR prohibition from a retrospective stage comparison |
| **Anterior compartment prolapse (primary)** | [Anterior colporrhaphy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/anterior-colporrhaphy) with apical support when deficient | [Paravaginal repair](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/paravaginal-repair) for selected lateral defects | Anterior bulge does not by itself prove a lateral defect or mandate apical suspension |
| **Posterior compartment prolapse** | [Posterior colporrhaphy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/posterior-colporrhaphy) | Site-specific repair | NICE recommends repair without mesh; evaluate bowel symptoms and avoid unnecessary levator tightening |
| **Recurrent anterior / apical prolapse after native-tissue repair** | [Sacrocolpopexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrocolpopexy) or individualized repeat native-tissue repair | Procedure choice depends on failure compartment, prior repair and goals | Historical transvaginal POP mesh kits are not marketed in the US |
| **No desire for future penetrative vaginal intercourse** | [Colpocleisis (Le Fort or total)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/colpocleisis) | Individualized reconstructive repair | Explain permanent change in vaginal access/function; do not infer preference from age alone |
| **Sexually active, advanced prolapse, durability prioritized** | [Sacrocolpopexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrocolpopexy), with route-specific uterine-preservation discussion | Vaginal native-tissue suspension | Colpocleisis precludes future penetrative vaginal intercourse; age alone does not determine route |
| **Concomitant occult SUI** | Discuss a concomitant procedure, such as Burch with abdominal sacrocolpopexy or [MUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/retropubic-midurethral-sling) with vaginal repair | Staged approach | Benefits and adverse effects differ by operation; prophylaxis is not automatic in continent patients |
| **Symptomatic, considering conservative care** | Surgical choice remains preference-sensitive | [Pessary](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/prolapse-pessaries), supervised [PFMT](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/pelvic-floor-pt) and lifestyle measures | Surgery may be discussed when nonsurgical care is ineffective or declined; no mandatory pessary trial |

### Apical Suspension Sub-Comparison

| Feature | [USLS (Vaginal)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/uterosacral-ligament-suspension) | [SSLF (Vaginal)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrospinous-ligament-fixation) | [Sacrocolpopexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrocolpopexy) | [Manchester Procedure](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/manchester-fothergill) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Approach** | Vaginal | Vaginal | Laparoscopic / robotic / open | Vaginal |
| **Mechanism** | Apex to uterosacral ligaments | Apex to sacrospinous ligament | Mesh from apex to sacral promontory | Cervical amputation + ligament plication |
| **Evidence anchor** | E-OPTIMAL: modeled first composite failure **61.5% at 5 years** in a cohort with planned concomitant slings | E-OPTIMAL: modeled first composite failure **70.3% at 5 years**; no equivalence demonstrated | ASPIRe: modeled 36-month composite failure **28%** in post-hysterectomy vault prolapse | SAM: uterine-prolapse population, distinct operation and endpoint; do not compare its percentages directly with vault trials |
| **Advanced-stage context** | Recurrence varies by population and endpoint | Recurrence varies by population and endpoint | May offer durability advantages for selected vault prolapse; mesh risks remain | Requires a suitable cervix and uterine-preservation counseling |
| **Mesh use** | No | No | **Yes (type 1 polypropylene)** | No |
| **Key risks** | Ureteral compromise; perform cystoscopy with efflux assessment | Buttock/gluteal pain and hemorrhage | Mesh exposure, visceral injury and route-specific recovery | Cervical stenosis and uterine/cervical surveillance; future pregnancy needs individualized counseling |
| **OR time** | Varies by repair and expertise | Varies by repair and expertise | Varies by route and concomitant repair | Varies by cervical and compartment work |
| **Typical use** | Vaginal native-tissue vault suspension | Vaginal native-tissue vault suspension | Vault prolapse when sacral mesh repair fits patient goals and risk | Uterine prolapse with suitable cervical anatomy after counseling |

### Compartment Stepwise Approach

**Anterior compartment**

1. Assess anterior and apical support; use [anterior colporrhaphy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/anterior-colporrhaphy) for a suitable central defect and restore apical support when deficient.
2. If recurrent, reassess the failed compartment and prior repair; [sacrocolpopexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrocolpopexy) or another native-tissue repair may be considered. Historical vaginal POP-mesh kits are not marketed in the US.

**Posterior compartment**

1. [Posterior colporrhaphy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/posterior-colporrhaphy) (midline plication) ± [perineorrhaphy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/perineorrhaphy)
2. NICE recommends posterior repair without mesh; tailor the repair to bowel symptoms and anatomy.

**Apical compartment**

1. Vaginal native-tissue suspension ([USLS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/uterosacral-ligament-suspension) or [SSLF](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrospinous-ligament-fixation)). Select by anatomy, goals and risks rather than a stage-only rule.
2. Hysteropexy ([sacrospinous](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrospinous-hysteropexy), [Manchester](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/manchester-fothergill), or sacral) if uterine preservation is desired
3. [Sacrocolpopexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrocolpopexy). Discuss for selected advanced or recurrent vault prolapse, including mesh and visceral risks.
4. [Colpocleisis (Le Fort or total)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/colpocleisis) for patients who do not intend future penetrative vaginal intercourse after informed discussion.
