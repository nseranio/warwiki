# Prolapse Repair

Pelvic organ prolapse (POP) repair encompasses native-tissue, mesh-augmented, and obliterative procedures targeting the anterior, posterior, and apical compartments. Surgical planning must account for compartment-specific anatomy (DeLancey Levels I–III), patient goals including uterine preservation and sexual activity, prior surgery, and mesh history.

> See [POP Overview](/docs/clinical-conditions/03c-pelvic-support/pelvic-organ-prolapse) for pathophysiology, risk stratification, and trial data (OPTIMAL, PEOPLE).

## Decision Framework

POP management has shifted toward a **patient-centered, symptom-driven approach** that prioritizes **native-tissue repair (NTR)** as first-line for primary prolapse and reserves mesh augmentation for the situations where its durability advantage is greatest. The single most important principle across every approach is that **adequate apical support must always be addressed** — failure to do so is the most common cause of recurrent prolapse (ACOG PB 214). The **Menefee 2024 RCT** (JAMA Surg, 3-arm: NTR vs SCP vs TVM for vault prolapse) is the contemporary anchor: SCP was superior to NTR (aHR 0.57; p = 0.01) and TVM was noninferior to SCP. **Hysteropexy has emerged as a viable alternative to hysterectomy** with comparable / superior outcomes and reduced perioperative morbidity (Ruffolo 2025 meta n = 2,544; Brennand 2025 prospective cohort n = 321 — apical recurrence 7.5% hysteropexy vs 17.2% hysterectomy). **Colpocleisis** remains the most durable repair (1.8% reoperation rate; Dallas 2021) and should be considered first-line for elderly / frail patients not desiring vaginal function.

POP is a clinical diagnosis. Minimum eval: POP-Q exam (Level C), symptom assessment (bulge, pressure, urinary / bowel / sexual dysfunction), compartment determination (anterior, posterior, apical — usually multicompartment), and assessment for occult SUI with prolapse reduced (cough stress test or urodynamics) in all women with significant apical / anterior prolapse (Level C). All symptomatic patients should be offered **non-surgical options first** (PFMT, vaginal pessary, lifestyle modifications) before surgical reconstruction.

### Treatment Selection by Clinical Scenario

| Clinical Scenario | First-Line Surgical Option | Alternative(s) | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary uterine prolapse, desires uterine preservation** | [Sacrospinous hysteropexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrospinous-hysteropexy) or [Manchester procedure](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/manchester-fothergill) (vaginal); sacrohysteropexy (abdominal) | Uterosacral hysteropexy | Hysterectomy without apical support |
| **Primary uterine prolapse, does not desire uterine preservation** | [Vaginal hysterectomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/vaginal-hysterectomy) + apical suspension ([USLS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/uterosacral-ligament-suspension) or [SSLF](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrospinous-ligament-fixation)) | [McCall's culdoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/vaginal-mccall-culdoplasty) at hysterectomy | Hysterectomy alone (apical suspension required, Level B) |
| **Post-hysterectomy vault prolapse, stage II** | Vaginal NTR ([USLS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/uterosacral-ligament-suspension) or [SSLF](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrospinous-ligament-fixation)) | [Sacrocolpopexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrocolpopexy) (if surgeon experienced) | Mesh augmentation rarely needed at stage II (recurrence ~6% NTR vs 5% SCP) |
| **Post-hysterectomy vault prolapse, stage III–IV** | [Sacrocolpopexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrocolpopexy) (laparoscopic / robotic) preferred | Vaginal NTR | NTR alone — USLS recurrence 25.7% vs SCP 7.8% at stage III (Lavelle 2018) |
| **Anterior compartment prolapse (primary)** | [Anterior colporrhaphy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/anterior-colporrhaphy) ± apical suspension | [Vaginal paravaginal repair](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/vaginal-paravaginal-repair) | Isolated anterior repair without apical support |
| **Posterior compartment prolapse** | [Posterior colporrhaphy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/posterior-colporrhaphy) (midline plication) | Site-specific repair | **Mesh / biologic grafts in posterior repair (Level A — does NOT improve outcomes)**; levator plication (dyspareunia risk) |
| **Recurrent anterior / apical prolapse after NTR** | [Sacrocolpopexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrocolpopexy) | Apical-only TVM (where permitted; experienced surgeons only) | Repeat NTR alone in stage III–IV recurrence |
| **Elderly / frail, multiple comorbidities, no desire for vaginal intercourse** | [Le Fort colpocleisis](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/le-fort-colpocleisis) or [total colpocleisis](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/colpocleisis-total) | — | Long reconstructive procedures in this population |
| **Young / sexually active, advanced prolapse, durability prioritized** | [Sacrocolpopexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrocolpopexy) (with or without [hysteropexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrospinous-hysteropexy)) | NTR + apical hysteropexy | Colpocleisis (precludes intercourse) |
| **Concomitant occult SUI** | Add anti-incontinence procedure: Burch at SCP; [MUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/retropubic-midurethral-sling) at vaginal repair | Staged approach | Ignoring occult SUI — high de novo SUI risk after prolapse correction |
| **Symptomatic, not yet failed conservative** | [Pessary](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/prolapse-pessaries) trial + [PFMT](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/pelvic-floor-pt) | Lifestyle modifications | Surgery before non-surgical trial |

### Apical Suspension Sub-Comparison

| Feature | [USLS (Vaginal)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/uterosacral-ligament-suspension) | [SSLF (Vaginal)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrospinous-ligament-fixation) | [Sacrocolpopexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrocolpopexy) | [Manchester Procedure](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/manchester-fothergill) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Approach** | Vaginal | Vaginal | Laparoscopic / robotic / open | Vaginal |
| **Mechanism** | Apex to uterosacral ligaments | Apex to sacrospinous ligament | Mesh from apex to sacral promontory | Cervical amputation + ligament plication |
| **2-yr success** | 64.5% (OPTIMAL) | 63.1% (OPTIMAL) | ~72% / superior to NTR aHR 0.57 (Menefee 2024) | ~93% composite (Enklaar 2023 RCT — **0% reoperation at 2 yr**) |
| **Stage III recurrence** | 25.7% (Lavelle 2018) | Comparable to USLS | **7.8% (Lavelle 2018)** | Low |
| **Mesh use** | No | No | **Yes (type 1 polypropylene)** | No |
| **Key risks** | Ureteral kinking up to 6% — mandatory cystoscopy | Buttock / gluteal pain; hemorrhage | Mesh exposure 3–10.5% (CARE 7-yr); ileus 2.7%; VTE 0.6% | Cervical stenosis (rare); not for future pregnancy |
| **OR time** | Shorter | Shortest | Longest | Short |
| **Best indication** | Primary vault, stage II–III | Primary vault, stage II–III | Stage III–IV; recurrent; young / active | Primary uterine prolapse; completed childbearing |

### Compartment Stepwise Approach

**Anterior compartment**

1. [Anterior colporrhaphy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/anterior-colporrhaphy) — always with concurrent apical support
2. If recurrent → [sacrocolpopexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrocolpopexy) or apical-only transvaginal mesh (where permitted)

**Posterior compartment**

1. [Posterior colporrhaphy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/posterior-colporrhaphy) (midline plication) ± [perineorrhaphy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/perineorrhaphy)
2. Mesh / grafts **NOT** recommended (Level A — no outcome benefit)

**Apical compartment**

1. Vaginal NTR ([USLS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/uterosacral-ligament-suspension) or [SSLF](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrospinous-ligament-fixation)) — for stage II, primary prolapse
2. Hysteropexy ([sacrospinous](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrospinous-hysteropexy), [Manchester](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/manchester-fothergill), or sacral) — if uterine preservation desired
3. [Sacrocolpopexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrocolpopexy) — for stage III–IV, recurrent prolapse, or when durability is prioritized
4. [Colpocleisis](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/colpocleisis-total) / [Le Fort](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/le-fort-colpocleisis) — for elderly / frail patients not desiring vaginal function
