# Colpocleisis (Le Fort and Total)

**Colpocleisis** is an obliterative vaginal operation for **advanced pelvic organ prolapse (POP)**, primarily in elderly women who **no longer desire vaginal intercourse**. It corrects prolapse by repositioning pelvic viscera and **closing off the vaginal canal** — partially (Le Fort, uterus retained) or totally (post-hysterectomy colpectomy). The operation is short, low-morbidity, and produces among the **highest success rates of any POP repair**.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For the broader prolapse decision framework, see [Prolapse Repair](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair). For the McCall culdoplasty performed at concomitant hysterectomy in selected colpocleisis cases, see [McCall Culdoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/mccall-culdoplasty). Concurrent SUI procedures are covered in the [Female Stress Incontinence Database](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/female-sui/female-stress-incontinence-database).

---

## Definitions

The operation has several variants — same indication, same outcomes, different technical detail driven by whether the uterus is in situ.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Variant | Uterus | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Le Fort (partial) colpocleisis** | In situ | Strips of epithelium denuded from anterior and posterior vaginal walls, then sutured together; **lateral channels preserved** for drainage of cervical secretions<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Total colpocleisis (colpectomy)** | Absent (post-hysterectomy) | Entire vaginal epithelium denuded; serial purse-string sutures invert the vagina<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Rouhier's colpocleisis** | Removed at the same operation | Vaginal hysterectomy first, followed by total-style obliterative closure<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |

The intended endpoint is a well-approximated, obliterated vagina **~3 cm deep and ~1 cm wide**. **Levator myorrhaphy and perineorrhaphy** are typically performed concurrently to reinforce the repair.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Indications and Patient Selection

ACOG PB 214 lists colpocleisis as appropriate for women with POP who have **significant comorbidities, wish to avoid hysterectomy, and no longer desire vaginal coital function**.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

| Criterion | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Stage** | Stage III–IV; > 90% of published series have stage ≥ 3<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Age** | Mean 69–84 yr in published series<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Coital function** | **Patient (and partner if applicable) must accept that the procedure is irreversible and eliminates vaginal intercourse**<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Frailty** | Dominant predictor of perioperative complications — better than age alone<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **Anesthetic** | Can be performed under **local anesthesia + IV sedation** in selected patients<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |

Shorter operative time, smaller anesthetic burden, and lower perioperative morbidity make colpocleisis particularly suited to medically frail patients vs reconstructive alternatives.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Preoperative Evaluation

- **Occult SUI.** All women with significant apical or anterior prolapse should undergo cough stress test or urodynamic testing **with prolapse reduced** — prolapse may mask SUI by kinking the urethra.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Cervical and endometrial evaluation (Le Fort only).** Document normal cervical cytology, HPV testing, and endometrial sampling preoperatively because the uterus will be inaccessible postoperatively.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Frailty assessment.** Frail patients have significantly higher overall (23.3% vs 10.3%) and serious (5.0% vs 1.8%) complication rates than non-frail.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Surgical Technique

### Shared technical principles

- **Hydrodissection** with lidocaine + epinephrine (or dilute vasopressin) submucosally — facilitates dissection, provides hemostasis, and develops the epithelial-muscularis plane.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Push-spread technique** with Metzenbaum scissors — push and spread the tissue plane rather than cutting blindly; minimizes bleeding and inadvertent injury.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Allis clamps with rubber bands on hemostats** for retraction in the confined field.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Critical landmark**: dissection must **not pass distal to the urethrovesical junction** — risks urethral angulation and postoperative voiding dysfunction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Meticulous hemostasis** at every layer — eliminates dead space and prevents hematoma.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Transverse epithelial closure** of the introitus is standard.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

### Le Fort (partial) colpocleisis — step-by-step

The uterine-sparing operation; creates a **midline septum** by suturing denuded anterior and posterior walls together with **lateral drainage channels** for cervical secretions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

1. **Hydrodissection.** Submucosal lidocaine + epinephrine (or pituitrin / vasopressin) into anterior and posterior walls.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
2. **Mark and denude epithelium.** Electrosurgery to mark **rectangular patches** centered on the midline anteriorly and posteriorly, leaving lateral epithelial strips intact (these become the drainage channels).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
3. **Excise sharply** with the push-spread technique. **Stop proximal to the urethrovesical junction.**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
4. **Proximal closure (cervical inversion).** Re-approximate the proximal margins of the anterior and posterior denuded rectangles with absorbable sutures, **inverting the cervix** back into the canal.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
5. **Bilateral lateral channels.** Suture the lateral margins of the anterior and posterior rectangles together on each side, creating **bilateral tunnels**. Channels should admit **one finger** to confirm patency.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
   - **Vessel-loop technique** (Dessie 2015): a blue vessel loop placed across the cervix and threaded through each lateral channel during suturing — pulled through after closure to confirm patency.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
   - Alternatively, a **urinary catheter** placed within the lateral tunnels during suturing.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
6. **Serial plication.** Plicate anterior and posterior denuded muscularis surfaces together with **serial rows of interrupted or figure-of-eight absorbable sutures**, progressively reducing the prolapse from proximal to distal — each row inverts the walls further, telescoping the prolapse into the pelvis.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
7. **Distal epithelial closure.** Bring distal vaginal epithelial edges together; close transversely with absorbable suture.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

### Total colpocleisis (colpectomy) — step-by-step

For post-hysterectomy vault prolapse — the entire vaginal epithelium is removed circumferentially.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

1. **Hydrodissection.** Circumferential lidocaine + epinephrine submucosally.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
2. **Quadrant epithelial denudation.** Sharp dissection in four quadrants (anterior, posterior, right lateral, left lateral). For ulcerated mucosa, the friction of a sharp-edged knife on remaining non-eroded areas can be used.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
3. **Serial purse-string reduction.** **Concentric purse-string sutures** of absorbable material from the apex (cuff) distally; each tied down sequentially to invert the vault and obliterate dead space. Place purse strings closely to eliminate potential spaces.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
4. **Vaginal epithelial closure.** Close remaining edges transversely.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
5. **Ulcerated prolapse variant.** Two mirror-image triangles excised from anterior and posterior walls; the resulting diamond-shaped incision is closed **vertically** to narrow the introitus.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Variant techniques

| Variant | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Rouhier's colpocleisis** (Constantin 2019) | Vaginal hysterectomy first, followed by total-style circumferential denudation and serial purse-string reduction; time-saving and reproducible when hysterectomy is indicated<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| **Modified Le Fort (Lv & Rong 2021)** | Anterior and posterior **midline incisions** rather than rectangular excision; lateral flaps to expose bladder/rectal fascia; purse-string sutures push viscera back; excess wall trimmed; result is a **solid longitudinal central septum** with most vaginal-wall tissue preserved<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| **Standardized purse-string (Bern, Hoehn 2025)** | A single standardized purse-string approach applicable to both Le Fort and total — designed to be easy to learn and reproducible. n = 88: **16% overall recurrence; 7% in patients without prior surgery**; reoperation 9.2%<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |

### Concurrent procedures

- **Levator myorrhaphy.** Sutures through the levator-ani muscles bilaterally to narrow the genital hiatus.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Aggressive perineorrhaphy.** Reconstructs the perineal body and further narrows the hiatus, reducing risk of recurrent posterior wall prolapse — recommended in Le Fort.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Anti-incontinence procedure.** Retropubic synthetic midurethral sling for occult SUI is placed after the obliterative portion is complete, before perineorrhaphy.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Concomitant Procedures — When and What

### Hysterectomy — generally not required

Adding vaginal hysterectomy to colpocleisis is associated with **longer operative time** (144 vs ~110 min), **higher EBL** (253 vs ~140 mL), greater postoperative pain, and longer hospital stay — **without clear prolapse benefit** in most patients with procidentia.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[4]](#ref4)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> Reserve hysterectomy for documented uterine pathology.

### Anti-incontinence procedure

- **Suburethral plication or midurethral sling** is often recommended to decrease postoperative SUI risk.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- After concomitant midurethral sling at colpocleisis: **86.8–94% continence**, with **0–14% sling revision** for retention.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Evidence regarding **prophylactic** anti-incontinence procedures for occult SUI remains conflicting.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Outcomes and Efficacy

| Endpoint | Result |
|---|---|
| **Anatomic success** | **92.5–100%** (no prolapse beyond hymen 87.5–100%)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Subjective success** | 77.5–100%<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Reoperation for prolapse** | **0–1.8%**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Durability vs reconstructive repair** | More durable — failure 1.8% vs 3.5% (p < 0.01)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Patient satisfaction** | 81–99% satisfied / very satisfied<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Regret rate** | **0–13.8% overall; 0–12.9% specific to loss of coital ability**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |

A 208-patient cohort with mean ~5-year follow-up reported **no anatomical recurrence**, 98.6% satisfaction, and 0.96% regret.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> Significant improvements in PFDI-20, POPDI, and UDI scores are consistent across series.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Complications

| Complication | Rate / note |
|---|---|
| Overall perioperative complication rate | **6.8–11.1%**<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **UTI** | Most common — 4.3–34.7% depending on definition<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| Persistent urinary incontinence | ~30% in patients with **pre-existing** incontinence; de novo incontinence rare<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Urinary retention | 3.8%<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| Bowel injury | 0–2.7% (related to concomitant procedures)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Urinary tract injury | 0–9.1% (related to concomitant procedures)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| VTE | Rare; associated with concomitant hysterectomy<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| ICU admission | 2.8%<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Mortality** | 0.15–1.3%<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |

### Predictors of persistent urinary incontinence<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

- **Preoperative urodynamic stress incontinence** — OR 7.5
- **BMI ≥ 30** — OR 5.0

## Quality of Life

Studies consistently show **substantial QoL improvements without alteration in body image**.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> One series reported median PFDI-20 scores improving from **78.1 → 16.6** (p < 0.001).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Counseling Points

- The procedure is **irreversible** and eliminates vaginal intercourse.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Preoperative urodynamics** identifies occult SUI and informs concomitant anti-incontinence planning.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **For Le Fort**, complete cervical-cancer screening and endometrial evaluation preoperatively — the uterus will be inaccessible.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Hysterectomy is not mandatory** and adds operative morbidity without clear benefit in most cases.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Regret rates remain consistently low** despite irreversibility — appropriate counseling and patient selection drive this outcome.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)[[5]](#ref5)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

---

## Key Principles

- Colpocleisis is the **most durable POP repair** with the **shortest operative time and lowest morbidity** of the obliterative options — first-line for elderly / frail patients without future intercourse goals.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Le Fort** preserves the uterus; **total colpocleisis** is for post-hysterectomy vault prolapse; **Rouhier's** combines vaginal hysterectomy with obliterative closure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- Target endpoint: **~3 cm deep × 1 cm wide** obliterated vagina with adequate lateral channels (Le Fort).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Never dissect distal to the urethrovesical junction** — the principal anatomic safety landmark.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Hydrodissection + push-spread + meticulous hemostasis + transverse closure** are the shared technical pearls.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Hysterectomy is generally not required** and adds morbidity without prolapse benefit.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Pre-existing SUI persists in ~30%**; **occult SUI** drives the case for concomitant midurethral sling — continence rates 86.8–94% with sling.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Frailty** drives complication risk far more than age alone — assess and counsel accordingly.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Regret rates are low** (0–13.8%) — appropriate selection and counseling are protective.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[5]](#ref5)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
