# Prolapse Pessaries

Vaginal pessaries are a nonsurgical option for symptomatic pelvic organ prolapse (POP), alone or with pelvic floor muscle training (PFMT). They are discussed alongside observation, PFMT and surgery according to symptoms and patient goals. NICE considers at least 16 weeks of supervised PFMT a first option for symptomatic stage 1–2 POP, not a mandatory pessary trial before surgery.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> The IUC review found reported fitting success ranging from about 41% to 97%, depending on the definition and follow-up interval; initial fitting, continued use and symptom improvement are different outcomes.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

This page is the canonical [04g Prolapse Repair](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair) entry for prolapse-reducing pessaries. For the pessary used to treat stress urinary incontinence specifically (the knob-style continence pessary that compresses the urethra against the pubic symphysis), see the dedicated [Continence Pessary](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/continence-pessary) page.

---

## Types of Pessaries

Pessaries are broadly categorized into two groups:<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

| Group | Devices | Typical role |
|---|---|---|
| **Support pessaries** | Ring, ring with support, Shaatz, Smith-Hodge with support | First-line attempt; the ring is the usual first choice |
| **Space-filling pessaries** | Gellhorn, donut, cube, Marland with support | Advanced prolapse or when support pessaries fail to retain |

All modern pessaries are made of silicone or plastic and come in multiple sizes.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

---

## Fitting and Selection

Fitting often starts with a ring pessary. A Gellhorn or other space-filling design is considered when a support pessary does not stay in place. No single type or prolapse stage guarantees fitting success, and more than one fitting may be needed.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> Fitting considerations include the nature and extent of prolapse, vaginal size, cognitive status, manual dexterity, and sexual activity level. A properly fitted pessary allows a finger to be swept between the pessary and the vaginal walls.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

**Predictors of unsuccessful fitting** include previous POP surgery, previous hysterectomy, short vaginal length, wide genital hiatus, and posterior compartment involvement.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Efficacy

A meta-analysis of eight studies (627 patients, pre-treatment versus post-treatment scores, mostly about 3 months of follow-up) reported improvement in validated quality-of-life scores after pessary use: PFDI-20 (mean change −46.1), PFIQ-7 (mean change −36.0) and POPIQ-7 (mean change −16.3).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> In the Cochrane review, one trial (260 women) found that a pessary combined with pelvic floor muscle training (PFMT) probably improves prolapse symptoms and prolapse-specific quality of life at 12 months more than PFMT alone (moderate-certainty evidence; NNT = 3 for symptom improvement in the AFP summary).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> The same review is uncertain whether a pessary differs from PFMT alone or from no treatment, and pessaries may carry more adverse events than PFMT.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> In a prospective cohort of 50 women, POP-Q measurements fell in all measured compartments (Ba, Bp and C) after at least 4 months of ring use, assessed 72 hours after removal.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

The IUC review reports improvement in other symptoms after successful fitting:<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

| Symptom | Improvement rate |
|---|---|
| Obstructive voiding | 40–97% |
| Urinary urgency | 38% |
| Urgency incontinence | 29–77% |
| Stress incontinence | 9–45% |

## Pessary vs. Surgery

A randomized trial (van der Vaart et al., *JAMA* 2022) assigned 440 women with symptomatic POP; one was excluded after randomization. Its 24-month comparison tested an initial pessary strategy after successful fitting, not a universal pessary-versus-surgery effect in every patient:<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

- Subjective improvement: 76.3% (pessary) versus 81.5% (surgery)
- The difference of −6.1% did not meet the prespecified noninferiority margin of −10%, and noninferiority of pessary to surgery was not established
- Both groups showed similar improvements in PFDI-20 and PFIQ-7 total scores

Continued use matters: 118 of 218 participants assigned to pessary crossed over to surgery during follow-up, and only 74 of 173 pessary-arm primary-outcome respondents were still using one at 24 months. These are distinct denominators and should not be presented as a general discontinuation rate for all fitted patients.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Follow-Up and Management

Follow-up is individualized by pessary type, ability to self-manage and risk of complications. NICE advises removal at least every 6 months and a six-month pessary-clinic appointment for those at risk because they cannot manage their own care; the IUC review describes a range of practice intervals rather than one universal schedule.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

| Patient profile | Follow-up cadence |
|---|---|
| **Self-manages** (removes / reinserts independently) | Agree a review and symptom-triggered contact plan; ensure removal at least every 6 months under NICE guidance, with an individualized inspection schedule. Annual clinical review is one reported practice, not a universal ACOG requirement. |
| **Unable to self-manage** | Arrange clinic-based removal and vaginal inspection; NICE specifies a six-month appointment for those at risk, with shorter intervals when device type, symptoms or tissue findings warrant it. |

Self-management training is offered when feasible. In TOPSY, it was an alternative to clinic-based care without a clear 18-month prolapse-specific quality-of-life difference; many in the clinic group also removed the device themselves.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> Vaginal estrogen is an option for genitourinary symptoms associated with menopause, but it has not been shown to improve pessary continuation: a 2025 placebo-controlled trial after successful ring fitting found no improvement in its 12-month continuation-plus-symptom endpoint, though some local adverse events were reduced.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

## Complications

Most complications are minor and manageable:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- **Vaginal discharge.** This is the most common side effect (up to 40% of users).
- **Vaginal erosion or ulceration.** Reported rates are 2–9%. Erosion is managed by removing the pessary for 2–4 weeks with or without topical estrogen.
- **Stress urinary incontinence after insertion.** This developed in 12 of 140 (8.6%) older women in one tailored-protocol cohort.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Vaginal bleeding.** Bleeding may occur, especially with erosion.
- **Discomfort or pain.** These are common reasons for discontinuation.

**Neglected pessary syndrome.**

Rare but serious complications (vesicovaginal or rectovaginal fistula, bowel obstruction, pessary impaction, and vaginal cancer) have usually been reported in older women with neglected pessary maintenance; this is not an exclusive-risk rule.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Caregivers of patients with cognitive impairment need to know that the pessary requires regular changes.

---

## Special Populations

| Population | Consideration |
|---|---|
| **Pregnancy** | ACOG recommends considering a pessary for symptomatic POP in women who wish to become pregnant<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Older / frail** | Used in poor surgical candidates. In a single-center cohort of 140 women aged ≥65 yr managed with a tailored protocol, 5 (3.6%) eventually had prolapse surgery<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Dementia** | Caregivers are counseled on regular pessary changes to prevent neglected-pessary complications<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |

---

## Emerging Innovations

Recent developments include 3D-printed custom pessaries, self-removable designs and hormone-releasing pessaries, which are intended to improve comfort and adherence and to reduce complications.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
