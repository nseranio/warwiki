# Anterior Colporrhaphy

Anterior colporrhaphy is an established native-tissue transvaginal repair for anterior vaginal wall prolapse (cystocele). The FDA ordered manufacturers to stop selling transvaginal mesh devices for pelvic organ prolapse (POP) in the US in April 2019; this did not prohibit native-tissue repair, SUI slings or transabdominal POP mesh.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

---

## Definition and Indications

Anterior colporrhaphy addresses prolapse of the bladder (cystocele) or urethra into the anterior vaginal wall. The indication is bothersome prolapse with shared treatment choice, not a fixed POP-Q stage or a requirement to fail every nonsurgical option. NICE NG123 offers surgery when nonsurgical treatment has not helped or has been declined, with discussion of [pessary](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/prolapse-pessaries), pelvic-floor training and other relevant choices.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> NICE recommends anterior repair without mesh for anterior vaginal wall prolapse.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> Assess the apex and other compartments before choosing an isolated anterior repair.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

---

## Surgical Technique

The procedure can be performed under regional or general anesthesia:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

1. **Midline incision** in the vaginal mucosa overlying the bladder and urethra
2. **Dissection** in a plane directly below the vagina exposes the damaged pubocervical (fibromuscular) layer supporting the bladder
3. The fascia is plicated in the midline with delayed-absorbable or permanent sutures
4. Excessive vaginal skin may be trimmed
5. The vaginal skin is closed
6. Other sites of prolapse are repaired as required

### Variations

| Variation | Description |
|---|---|
| **Ultralateral colporrhaphy** | Plication extended laterally for broader fascial coverage |
| **Double-layered anterior colporrhaphy (DAC)** | Continuous suture followed by traditional interrupted sutures<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **[Paravaginal repair](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/paravaginal-repair)** | Reattachment of lateral vaginal fascia to the arcus tendineus fascia pelvis (white line) |

## Outcomes and Success Rates

Success rates vary substantially depending on the definition used:

| Definition | Success rate |
|---|---|
| Clinically relevant (no prolapse beyond the hymen, no bulge symptoms and no retreatment) | 88% (66/75 evaluable women) at 1 year, from a 114-person trial of three anterior-repair techniques including a polyglactin-mesh arm; this is not an isolated native-tissue colporrhaphy rate<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Strict anatomic (POP-Q stage 0 or 1) | 13.2% at mean 74.1 mo (68 women analyzed; 70 of 137 invited attended)<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Subjective success (absence of bulge symptoms) | 68.7% at mean 74.1 mo<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Patient satisfaction | 98.5% were satisfied; 66.1% of women with objective recurrence had no prolapse symptoms<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |

In a retrospective series of double-layered anterior colporrhaphy with concomitant vaginal hysterectomy, 49 of the 60 women examined (81.7%) had anatomic cure (prolapse below stage 2) at a median of 19.3 months.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> In the Altman randomized trial, the composite of POP-Q stage 0 or 1 and no bulge symptoms was met by 34.5% of women after colporrhaphy at 1 year.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

---

## Recurrence — The Central Challenge

Recurrence is the primary limitation of anterior colporrhaphy. A 2026 Cochrane review of 41 RCTs (4,531 women) found:<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

| Comparator | Recurrence outcome |
|---|---|
| **vs. biological graft** | Native tissue repair likely increases recurrent anterior prolapse (RR 1.53, 95% CI 1.19–1.97; moderate certainty); little to no difference in prolapse awareness, repeat surgery, or dyspareunia |
| **vs. transvaginal permanent mesh** | Native tissue repair is followed by more prolapse awareness (RR 1.77, moderate certainty), more recurrence (RR 3.21, low certainty) and more repeat surgery for prolapse (RR 2.17, moderate certainty); dyspareunia is similar and bladder injuries are fewer |

Anatomic recurrence, prolapse awareness and repeat surgery are different endpoints. Historical transvaginal permanent-mesh trial results do not make those POP devices a current US treatment option.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

---

## Suture Type

Whether suture type meaningfully changes patient outcomes remains uncertain. A published multicenter RCT protocol compares non-absorbable with absorbable sutures; its remodeling hypothesis is not an outcome result.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

---

## Complications

Safety estimates depend on what other procedures were done. Mothes et al. studied 438 women who all had both anterior and posterior colporrhaphy; many also had apical fixation or hysterectomy. Its event rates are not risks of isolated anterior colporrhaphy.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> The Altman randomized comparison separately informs selected mesh-versus-native-tissue events.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

| Complication | Reported source and scope |
|---|---|
| Intraoperative bladder or rectal injury | One bladder lesion (0.23%) and one rectal lesion (0.23%) in the combined anterior/posterior repair cohort; the Altman anterior-repair arm reported 0.5% bladder perforation<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Urinary tract infection | 7.8% in the combined-repair cohort<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| Elevated postoperative residual | 5.5% in the combined-repair cohort<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| Hemorrhage requiring reoperation | 0.9% in the combined-repair cohort<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| New stress urinary incontinence | 6.3% in the Altman native-tissue arm versus 12.3% in its mesh arm; this is not a universal anterior-repair incidence<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Clavien-Dindo grade III | 1.8% in the combined-repair cohort<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |

No grade IV or V complications were reported in that 438-person combined-procedure assessment. Counsel about bleeding, infection, bladder injury, voiding dysfunction, pain, dyspareunia and recurrent prolapse according to the individual's planned operations; de novo urgency after anterior colporrhaphy was not quantified in the cited sources.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Anterior Colporrhaphy vs. Transvaginal Mesh — Regulatory Context

In the Altman et al. *NEJM* 2011 randomized trial (389 women), transvaginal mesh had higher composite success at 1 year (60.8% versus 34.5%), with:<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

- Longer operative times
- More hemorrhage
- Higher bladder perforation (3.5% versus 0.5%, p = 0.07)
- More new SUI (12.3% versus 6.3%, p = 0.05)
- Surgical reintervention for mesh exposure in 3.2% of 186 mesh patients

> *"As compared with anterior colporrhaphy, use of a standardized, trocar-guided mesh kit for cystocele repair resulted in higher short-term rates of successful treatment but also in higher rates of surgical complications and postoperative adverse events."*
> — Altman D, et al. *NEJM* 2011

Subsequently, the FDA ordered manufacturers to stop selling transvaginal mesh for POP in the US in April 2019 because they had not shown a reasonable assurance of safety and effectiveness. No such device is currently marketed in the US. The order does not apply to SUI slings or transabdominal mesh ([sacrocolpopexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrocolpopexy)). Older guidance that discussed mesh in selected patients does not describe a current US device option.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

For deeper coverage of mesh-augmented repair indications, complications, and the regulatory timeline, see [Mesh & Graft-Augmented Prolapse Repair](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/mesh-graft-augmented-repairs).

## The Critical Role of Concomitant Apical Support

Many women with anterior wall prolapse also have apical descent. Evaluate both; repair a clinically relevant apical defect rather than assuming isolated anterior plication will support the apex. A large Medicare analysis found an association between concomitant apical support and less later prolapse surgery, but it was observational and cannot by itself prove a causal effect.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

In a 2,756-woman Medicare vaginal-repair cohort that included anterior-only, posterior-only and combined repairs, the anterior-only subgroups had these cumulative ten-year reoperation rates:<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

| Group | Cumulative reoperation rate at 10 years |
|---|---|
| Isolated anterior repair | 20.2% |
| Anterior repair + concomitant apical suspension | 11.6% (p &lt; 0.01) |

A separate statewide hysterectomy analysis reported colpopexy in 24% of POP hysterectomies. That is a different population and denominator from the Medicare cohort.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

## Concomitant Anti-Incontinence Surgery

The OPUS trial randomized 337 women without stress-incontinence symptoms undergoing vaginal prolapse surgery; it was not an isolated anterior-colporrhaphy incidence study. At 3 months, urinary incontinence or treatment occurred in 23.6% with a prophylactic sling versus 49.4% with sham. At 12 months, urinary incontinence allowing for subsequent treatment was 27.3% versus 43.0%, respectively. Bladder perforation, urinary tract infection, bleeding and incomplete emptying were more frequent with a sling.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> OPUS supports a discussion of benefits and harms; it does not support automatic sling placement. Guidelines differ: NICE NG123 advises against surgery to prevent incontinence in women without incontinence (1.8.3), while AUA/SUFU 2023 states that clinicians may perform an incontinence procedure at the time of prolapse repair (Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C) and advises shared decision-making.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

Characterize baseline stress, urgency and mixed symptoms before that decision; a general incontinence evaluation is distinct from predicting postoperative SUI for this repair.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> Burch retropubic colposuspension has a separate evidence base as an anti-incontinence operation and should not be confused with anterior colporrhaphy or used to estimate this repair's complication rates.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

## Long-Term Outcomes

In one cohort followed to a mean of 74 months, patient satisfaction was high despite a low anatomic success rate.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> A retrospective comparison of colporrhaphy, xenograft and mesh at a mean of 95 months (109 women) found no significant difference in objective or subjective outcomes, and mesh was associated with more complications.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> In a retrospective study of 181 women, 5-year objective cure was 64.4% after anterior colporrhaphy with sacrospinous fixation versus 89.1% after anterior-apical mesh (Surelift), with minor complications in both groups.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

## Summary of Key Comparisons

| Comparison | Prolapse awareness | Anatomic recurrence | Repeat surgery | Dyspareunia | Key complications |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **AC vs. Biological Graft** | No difference (RR 1.20)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | Higher with AC (RR 1.53)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | No difference (RR 0.99) | No difference | Similar safety profiles |
| **AC vs. historical transvaginal permanent mesh** | Higher with AC (RR 1.77)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | Higher with AC (RR 3.21)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | Higher with AC (RR 2.17)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | No clear difference | Mesh exposure and bladder injury matter; event rates depend on device/study, and POP mesh is not marketed in the US<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)[[20]](#ref20)</sup> |
| **AC + apical suspension vs. AC alone (Medicare observational)** | Not reported | Not established by this claim | 11.6% vs. 20.2% at 10 yr (observational association)<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> | Not reported | Procedure mix and confounding apply |

## Current Practice Recommendations

Practice points:

- Offer surgery for bothersome prolapse when nonsurgical treatment has not helped or is declined, after discussing alternatives, goals and operative risk (NICE NG123 1.8.1, 1.8.2).<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- Assess apical support and address a coexisting clinically relevant defect; the Medicare association is observational.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- Discuss symptom, retreatment and anatomic outcomes separately; biological grafts and historical vaginal mesh show different trade-offs in the anterior Cochrane review.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Do not present transvaginal POP mesh as a current US option; distinguish SUI slings and abdominal POP mesh.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- Discuss occult or de novo SUI and a concomitant anti-incontinence procedure using the actual trial endpoints, patient preference and relevant local guideline; avoid routine prophylactic sling language.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
