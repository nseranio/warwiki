# Kelly Plication

The **Kelly plication** (also called the **Kelly-Kennedy plication** or **Kelly suture**) is one of the oldest surgical procedures for stress urinary incontinence (SUI), first described by Howard A. Kelly in the early 20th century. It is the bladder-neck-buttressing component of the anterior colporrhaphy and involves placing sutures in the periurethral tissue and pubocervical fascia to support and elevate the bladder neck.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> While historically important, it has been largely supplanted by midurethral slings and Burch colposuspension due to inferior long-term continence outcomes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

This page is the canonical [04g Prolapse Repair](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair) entry for the Kelly plication. For modern anti-incontinence procedures with superior long-term outcomes, see the [Female SUI database](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/female-sui/female-stress-incontinence-database), particularly [Burch colposuspension](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/burch-colposuspension), [retropubic midurethral sling](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/retropubic-midurethral-sling), and [autologous fascial pubovaginal sling](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/autologous-pubovaginal-sling).

---

## Definition and Technique

The Kelly plication is performed transvaginally as part of or alongside an [anterior colporrhaphy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/anterior-colporrhaphy):<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

1. **Midline incision** in the anterior vaginal wall overlying the bladder and urethra
2. The vaginal mucosa is dissected off the underlying periurethral tissue and pubocervical fascia, extending from below the urethra to just anterior to the cervix
3. One to three sutures ("Kelly sutures") are placed in the periurethral tissue and pubocervical fascia to support and elevate the bladder neck
4. The fascia is plicated in the midline, narrowing the bladder neck and proximal urethra
5. Excess vaginal tissue may be trimmed, and the vaginal mucosa is closed

The procedure aims to lift and support the urethrovesical junction to restore the anatomic position needed for continence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

---

## Mechanism of Action

The Kelly plication works by buttressing the bladder neck and increasing abdominal pressure transmission to the urethra. When successful, all three classic procedures (Kelly, Burch, Pereyra) significantly increase abdominal pressure transmission to the urethra on urodynamic testing.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> However, the Kelly plication does not reposition the bladder neck into a high, supported retropubic position, a limitation that contributes to its higher failure rate compared with retropubic procedures.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Historical Comparative Series

The following observational reports used different populations and follow-up intervals. Their percentages are not short-term randomized estimates or directly comparable treatment effects:<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

| Study | n | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Park & Miller 1988**<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | 680 women across three procedures, observed annually for up to 10 yr | **More than 3 yr** after surgery, Kelly **69%** and Marshall–Marchetti–Krantz (MMK) **66%** corrective; both exceeded original Pereyra in this cohort. No 1-year 69% estimate is reported in the abstract. |
| **Beck 1991**<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | 519 anterior colporrhaphies over 25 yr; **194** patients treated for genuine SUI | A Kelly-Kennedy-type technique was associated with **75% cure** versus **94%** when modified with vaginal retropubic urethropexy in the genuine-SUI subgroup. The abstract does not give randomized allocation or a common follow-up interval. |

## Efficacy — Long-Term

Long-term outcomes reveal the central weakness of the Kelly plication, a progressive decline in continence rates:<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

**Bergman & Elia 1995, 5-year RCT.** 127 women were randomized, 107 evaluated at 1 year and 93 at 5 years. Objective success among those evaluated at 5 years was:<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

| Procedure | 5-yr objective success | Drop from yr 1 → yr 5 |
|---|---|---|
| **Kelly plication** | **37%** | −26% |
| Pereyra needle suspension | 43% | −22% |
| **Burch colposuspension** | **82%** | **−7%** |

At the same 5-year assessment, 30% of the evaluated Kelly group had a negative cotton-swab test, compared with 46% for Pereyra and 91% for Burch, an anatomic finding within this trial, not a universal rate.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> In Park & Miller's separate observational cohort, success declined with longer observation; more than 3 years after surgery the Kelly and MMK figures were 69% and 66%, respectively.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Kelly Plication vs. Other Anti-Incontinence Procedures

Cross-study percentages are not a valid head-to-head ranking. In SISTEr, autologous PVS achieved 66% SUI-specific success but 47% strict overall success at 24 months; the Burch estimates were 49% and 38%. These are cumulative estimates in one randomized comparison, not universal cure rates for every technique.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> Compare the dedicated [Burch](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/burch-colposuspension), [MUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/female-slings-suspensions) and [PVS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/autologous-pubovaginal-sling) pages for population, endpoint, durability and complications.

## Cochrane Review Evidence

The Cochrane review on anterior vaginal repair for urinary incontinence (Glazener 2017; 10 trials, 1,012 women) concluded:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- Anterior vaginal repair (including Kelly plication) was less effective than open abdominal retropubic suspension (mostly Burch, not exclusively): patient-reported failure 97/259 (38%) versus 57/327 (17%) at 1–5 yr (RR 2.29, 95% CI 1.70–3.08)
- The difference persisted beyond 5 yr: 49/128 (38%) versus 31/145 (21%) failure (RR 2.02, 95% CI 1.36–3.01)
- **Repeat incontinence surgery** was more frequent after anterior repair in three trials: 25/107 (23%) versus 4/164 (2%) (RR 8.87, 95% CI 3.28–23.94)
- Fewer women had prolapse after anterior repair (RR 0.24), but this did not translate into fewer prolapse operations

> This review found no evidence that anterior repair was better than its comparators for SUI, and favored open retropubic suspension on the reported continence endpoints. The authors suggested restricting anterior repair for SUI to women unsuitable for alternatives; current NICE guidance is more explicit against offering anterior colporrhaphy as SUI treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

---

## Network Meta-Analysis Ranking

A BMJ network meta-analysis of 175 RCTs with 21,598 women (Imamura 2019) reported relative rankings for the cure outcome using SUCRA. These ranking scores are not cure percentages or individual probabilities of success:<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

1. Traditional sling: 89.4%
2. Retropubic midurethral sling: 89.1%
3. Open colposuspension: 76.7%
4. Transobturator MUS: 64.1%

Anterior vaginal repair or Kelly plication was not among the top-ranked procedures, consistent with its inferior long-term outcomes.

## Complications and Morbidity

Historical anterior-colporrhaphy series reported the following harms. They do not establish a comparative safety ranking against contemporary injectables, retropubic procedures or slings:<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

| Complication | Rate |
|---|---|
| Significant morbidity (excluding incontinence) | **1%** in 25-yr / 519-case series<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| New-onset detrusor instability | **6% across surgeries** in Beck's series; higher after incontinence than prolapse surgery<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| De novo incontinence after prolapse surgery | **11% in one subgroup** of Beck's series, not the entire 519-case cohort<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Repeat operations | More likely to fail than primary repairs |
| Mesh-related complications | None (native-tissue procedure) |

## Current Role and Indications

The Kelly plication is no longer considered a first-line anti-incontinence procedure. Current guidelines and expert consensus position it as follows:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

- **[Midurethral slings](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/retropubic-midurethral-sling)** are the most commonly performed and most extensively studied anti-incontinence procedure, with documented short- and long-term efficacy<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- [Burch colposuspension](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/burch-colposuspension) and [autologous fascial pubovaginal slings](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/autologous-pubovaginal-sling) are the primary mesh-free alternatives<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **NICE NG123 advises against anterior colporrhaphy as a treatment for SUI.** This is distinct from an anterior repair performed for symptomatic prolapse. Frailty, mesh avoidance or concomitant prolapse do not by themselves establish Kelly plication as a recommended continence operation; discuss conservative options and established continence procedures instead.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

---

## Emerging Alternatives — Pubourethral Ligament Plication (PLP)

**Pubourethral ligament plication (PLP) is a distinct operation, not a modern Kelly plication.** A 2026 prospective, nonrandomized cohort compared 48 PLP recipients with 92 transobturator-tape (TOT) recipients at 6 months. Pad-count-defined objective success was 77.1% for PLP versus 88.0% for TOT (p &gt; 0.05); preoperative symptoms were more severe in the TOT group. PLP took less operative time, and the report found no mesh-related complications with PLP versus an 8.7% complication rate with TOT. The short follow-up, group imbalance and nonrandomized design do not establish equivalence or long-term comparative safety.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

---

## Historical Significance

The Kelly plication holds an important place in the history of urogynecology as one of the first described surgical treatments for female SUI. It established the principle that bladder-neck support is necessary for continence and paved the way for the development of retropubic suspensions, needle suspensions, and ultimately midurethral slings. While its role has diminished, the concept of periurethral tissue plication remains embedded in modern anterior colporrhaphy technique.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
