# Intracorporeal Urinary Diversion (ICUD)

**ICUD** means constructing the diversion within the abdomen using robotic or laparoscopic instruments. It is a route of construction for a conduit, neobladder or selected cutaneous reservoir. It is most often performed during robot-assisted radical cystectomy, and the ileal conduit predominates: in a 170-patient stapled-anastomosis series 75.8% of diversions were ileal conduits, 21% neobladders and 2.3% continent pouches.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> Specimen extraction and stoma formation still require incisions. Bowel selection, perfusion, storage pressure and outlet suitability remain central; see [Urinary Diversion Principles](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion/urinary-diversion-principles).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Evidence That Changes Counseling

### Robotic Cystectomy with ICUD versus Open Surgery

The multicenter iROC randomized trial enrolled 338 patients; 317 underwent cystectomy and 305 contributed the primary outcome. Median days alive and outside hospital during the first 90 days were 82 with robotic surgery/ICUD versus 80 with open surgery, with an adjusted difference of 2.2 days (95% CI 0.50–3.85). Most operated patients received an ileal conduit. Early recovery advantages were modest, and their clinical importance requires discussion with the patient.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

This compares the whole robotic-with-ICUD pathway against open cystectomy. It does not isolate the effect of intracorporeal versus extracorporeal reconstruction after the same robotic cystectomy, and it does not prove that a particular neobladder design is superior.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

A separate 116-patient randomized trial, reported by Mastroianni with three-year follow-up, found fewer perioperative transfusions with robotic surgery/ICUD: absolute risk reduction 19 percentage points (95% CI 2–36). It did not detect a difference in complications or three-year oncologic outcomes; these secondary results are not proof of equivalence. Robotic surgery cost more. Neither trial supports promising every patient fewer complications or better continence.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### ICUD versus ECUD after Robotic Cystectomy

The 2021 systematic review included 12 studies and 3,067 patients, reporting lower blood loss and transfusion with ICUD but no significant overall or major-complication difference in the pooled short- and mid-term analyses. ICUD was associated with lower estimated blood loss (mean difference −102 mL), lower transfusion (OR 0.36) and higher lymph node yield (mean difference +3.68 nodes). Major complications were lower with ICUD only in the high-volume-center subgroup (short-term OR 0.57, 95% CI 0.37–0.86; mid-term OR 0.66, 95% CI 0.46–0.94). These subgroup associations are not randomized proof that ICUD alone prevents complications.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

Results vary across observational datasets. In the 2,125-patient IRCC study, 1,094 patients (51%) had ICUD. ICUD was associated with shorter operative time (357 versus 400 minutes), less blood loss (300 versus 350 mL) and fewer transfusions (4% versus 19%), but more high-grade complications (13% versus 10%, p = 0.02), which decreased over time. The registry's rise in ICUD use from 9% to 97% describes participating centers during 2005–2015, not worldwide current practice.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> A separate 948-patient institutional comparison (272 open, 301 ICUD, 375 ECUD) associated ICUD with fewer 90-day Clavien III–V complications (16.9% versus 24.8% for ECUD and 26.1% for open, p = 0.015), but case selection, treatment era and center experience limit causal interpretation.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> In a 267-patient single-center analysis with standardized complication reporting (162 ICUD, 105 ECUD), the approach was not an independent predictor of postoperative outcomes, and Clavien ≥2 complications occurred in 35% versus 43% (p = 0.2). A significant interaction with age-adjusted Charlson index (p = 0.04) suggested lower risk with ICUD as comorbidity increased.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

The Asian registry found lower blood loss and shorter stay, with similar overall complication rates; it should not be cited as proving a specific reduction in gastrointestinal complications.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Operative Planning

Plan access for the bowel, ureters, urethral anastomosis and premarked stoma. Reduction of steep Trendelenburg and redocking may facilitate reconstruction, depending on platform and team setup. Use atraumatic handling and preserve the mesenteric vascular supply. Trocar layout, assistant side and the choice of grasper are technique choices, not universal requirements.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[1]](#ref1)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

### Ileal Conduit

1. **Select the segment.** Choose adequate length and reach for the intended stoma and ureteral implantation while preserving terminal-ileal function and mesenteric perfusion.
2. **Isolate and restore bowel continuity.** A stapled side-to-side bowel anastomosis is commonly described. Choose the stapler and cartridge for the actual tissue thickness and device instructions. A vascular load for mesenteric division is not an interchangeable default for bowel-wall stapling.
3. **Check the bowel anastomosis.** Inspect perfusion, lumen, hemostasis and the common-enterotomy closure; avoid mesenteric twist and entrapment.
4. **Bring the ureters to the conduit.** Preserve periureteral vascular tissue and obtain a tension-free course; the left ureter commonly passes beneath the sigmoid mesentery.
5. **Construct the ureteroenteric anastomoses.** Individual Bricker or conjoined Wallace implantation according to anatomy and the reconstructive plan, with stenting as indicated.
6. **Mature the stoma.** Deliver a well-perfused conduit to the marked site without excessive tension, compression or angulation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

A 170-patient stapled-anastomosis series demonstrates a described workflow, not that a robotic stapler is mandatory or that its exact suturing method prevents ileus. Device and cartridge details are in the [staplers reference](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/staplers) and the [robotic stapler](/docs/foundations/tools/instruments/robotic/robotic-stapler) page.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

### Orthotopic Neobladder

The Piramide systematic atlas identified nine intracorporeal configurations: Studer, Hautmann, Y-shaped, U-shaped, Bordeaux, Pyramid, Shell, Florence (FIRN) and Padua. It found no direct comparative trial establishing the best design. Select a configuration the team can construct reliably while maintaining a suitable outlet and compliant storage.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The USC technique for the intracorporeal ileal neobladder proceeds in this order: isolation of 65 cm of small bowel, small bowel anastomosis, detubularization, suture of the posterior wall, neobladder-urethral anastomosis with cross-folding of the pouch, and uretero-enteric anastomosis. Operative time fell from 450 to 360 minutes as the technique was refined.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> In a Studer-type reconstruction, the afferent ileal chimney remains tubular; detubularize only the reservoir-forming portion. Construct the posterior plate, ensure a tension-free dependent urethral anastomosis, and fold/close the reservoir according to the chosen technique. The sequence, bowel length, suture and anastomotic design vary; do not mix steps from different named neobladders indiscriminately.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

Partly stapled neobladders are described, but short operative times or single-series continence do not establish their superiority. In the original 45-patient Padua series, substantial complications were reported during 180-day follow-up, emphasizing that technical feasibility and recovery burden are separate outcomes. Median operative time was 305 minutes; 2-year daytime and nighttime continence were 73.3% and 55.5%.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> In a multi-institutional cohort of 410 patients with intracorporeal neobladder, Clavien III–IV complications occurred in 15% within 30 days and 21% within 90 days. Kaplan-Meier 24-month recurrence-free, cancer-specific and overall survival were 78%, 88% and 86%.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

## Bricker versus Wallace

Al-Nader's study screened 740 patients and compared 209 propensity-matched patients per group. Per-patient stricture rates were 12% with Bricker and 14.4% with Wallace (p=0.56), but bilateral strictures occurred in one versus 15 patients, respectively. This is an observational signal about the consequences of a conjoined anastomosis, not proof that one method is always preferable.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

Kouba's older single-surgeon comparison reported 7/187 versus 0/184 ureters with strictures; those are per-ureter, not per-patient, denominators. In a 96-patient robotic intracorporeal conduit series (72 Bricker, 24 Wallace), strictures, complications and readmissions did not differ significantly.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> Small series with no events do not establish zero risk. Nonsignificance does not establish equivalence. Choose the reconstruction according to ureteral length, perfusion, geometry and disease.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

## Learning and Follow-up

Proficiency cannot be assigned from a universal case count. Published learning curves vary by prior experience, endpoint, diversion type and case selection.

- One surgeon's 203-case series (85 conduits, 118 neobladders) reported acceptable operative quality after 30 consecutive cases, while console time plateaued around case 140 (conduit 60, neobladder 80).<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- In a single-surgeon series of 53 conduits, median operating time fell from 415 to 361 minutes and blood loss from 400 to 200 mL. Early cases were selected for lower complexity, with more complex cases handled by open surgery.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- In a 100-patient intracorporeal neobladder series, cumulative-sum analysis found an operative-time plateau (more than three consecutive procedures under 300 minutes) after 20 cases; hemoglobin drop, positive margins and node count did not change significantly along the curve.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- For intracorporeal neobladder at Karolinska, the first surgeon's median operating time fell from 565 to 345 minutes (47 patients), and conversion to open surgery fell from 30% in the first group of ten to zero in later groups. The second surgeon's change (413 to 385 minutes) was not significant.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- IRCC data showed that high-grade complications after ICUD decreased with institutional experience.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

Structured mentoring, team consistency and auditing complications are the practical measures; the series do not support declaring competence after a fixed 20–30 operations.

Follow the actual diversion's long-term needs: drainage/emptying, renal function, upper-tract imaging, metabolic and nutritional effects, stones and stricture. An intracorporeal route does not remove these risks. For established strictures, see [Ureteroenteric Anastomotic Revision](/docs/surgical-techniques/04d-upper-tract-reconstruction/reimplantation/ureteroenteric-anastomotic-revision).
