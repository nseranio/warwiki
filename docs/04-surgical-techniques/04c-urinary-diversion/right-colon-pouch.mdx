# Right Colon Pouch

**Right-colon and ileocecal reservoirs** are a family of reconstructions. Some empty through a catheterizable skin stoma; others connect to the urethra. Reservoir shape, retained ileum, outlet mechanism and ureteral implantation determine their different functional and revision risks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

See [Urinary Diversion Principles](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion/urinary-diversion-principles) for patient selection. A large-capacity colon pouch is not automatically low pressure or continent, and it may still require intermittent catheterization.

## Why the Right Colon

The cecum and ascending colon provide a large, distensible segment that becomes a low-pressure reservoir when it is detubularized. The ileocecal valve region can be reinforced or the terminal ileum tapered to form a catheterizable outlet, as in the Indiana, Florida and Miami designs. The appendix can be embedded submucosally as an outlet: in the original Mainz appendix-stoma report of 13 patients, closure pressure exceeded 80 cm H₂O and one stoma stenosis needed reoperation.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The colonic wall accepts a standard submucosal tunnel for antireflux ureteral implantation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Operative Principles

Detubularization and reconfiguration aim to provide compliant storage. Preserve the ileocolic or colonic mesenteric supply and confirm that the selected segment reaches its outlet without twisting or tension. The native ileocecal valve may contribute to a reinforced ileal outlet, but it is not a universal continence mechanism for every pouch.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

Distinguish three separate constructions in the operative record:

1. **Reservoir:** bowel segment, length and configuration, including any undetubularized inlet limb.
2. **Outlet:** tapered ileum, appendiceal flap-valve channel, intussuscepted nipple, another reconstructed channel, or urethral anastomosis.
3. **Ureteral implantation:** individual or conjoined implantation, refluxing or antireflux design, and any stents.

An antireflux tunnel must preserve perfusion and avoid obstruction. Pouch name alone does not identify its implantation technique. In Wiesner's long-term Mainz I comparison, strictures occurred in 59/809 renal units with submucosal implantation and 3/74 renal units with an extramural tunnel; these unequal observational groups do not establish a universal preferred method.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### What the Larger Follow-up Reports Show

The Mainz experience of more than 800 patients reported 92.8% overall continence over a mean follow-up of 7.6 years. Stomal stenosis occurred in 23.5% of appendix stomas and 15.3% of ileal nipples. Stones occurred in 10.8% of reservoirs with an ileal nipple and 5.6% with an appendix stoma. Eleven patients (eight appendix, three nipple) needed reoperation for ischemic degeneration of the continence mechanism. These subgroup comparisons are retrospective, not randomized.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> In the companion ureteral report, serum creatinine was 1.6 mg/dL or lower in 97% to 98% of patients at last follow-up.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

The pediatric Mainz series operated on 70 patients and followed 65, assessing 118 renal units; 113 units were stable or improved. Complete continence was 97%, and revisions were needed for stoma stenosis in 23% and pouch calculi in 15%. These are renal-unit outcomes in selected followed patients.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

The Florida pouch uses a detubularized right-colon reservoir with a tapered ileal limb reinforced at the ileocecal valve and directly anastomosed ureters. The report began with 179 patients, but its long-term analysis included 74 patients followed for a mean of 133 months; the other 105 had died or were lost, so late function reflects survivor selection and is not follow-up of all 179. Outlet incontinence occurred in 6.7%, stomal stenosis in 4% and reservoir stones in 5.4%. Ureteral obstruction followed 6.3% of primary reimplantations and 28.4% of irradiated ureters (p = 0.02). Severe acidosis developed in 5.5% and low vitamin B12 in 4%. Metabolic alterations occurred in 58% of patients converted from another diversion, compared with 6.4% of the others (p = 0.0001).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

Using standardized complication reporting, Myers followed 53 right-colon pouch patients for a mean of 30 months: 22% had major complications (Clavien-Dindo grade 3 or higher) within 90 days, 45% were readmitted within 90 days, 53% underwent a reoperation and 32% a major reoperation during follow-up. Diabetes was associated with more complications and reoperation, and higher body mass index and prior abdominal surgery with readmission. The series was too small to establish causal risk factors.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Miami Pouch

The Miami pouch uses the terminal 15 cm of ileum and the right colon. The distal ileum is tapered over a 14 Fr catheter, the ileocecal valve is reinforced with three circumferential pursestring silk sutures, and the ureters are anastomosed to the colon without a tunnel. In the original 75-patient report (follow-up 7 to 69 months), continence was 98.6% and ureterocolonic anastomotic success was 90%. Early complications occurred in 26% and late complications in 23%, and there were five perioperative deaths.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

The 2021 Baboudjian series included 15 patients, with median follow-up of 39 months. Thirteen were continent and two reported nighttime leakage with catheterization intervals of 5 to 7 hours. Two patients had a Clavien III complication within 30 days. No efferent-tube stenosis or catheterization difficulty was reported. The series is too small to estimate complication rates.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

The Salom report included 90 patients treated from 1988–2002, predominantly in an exenteration or radiation setting. “Fifteen years' experience” describes the program's accrual period rather than fifteen-year follow-up of each patient. Ninety-one percent had received prior radiation. Continence was 93%. Reservoir-related complications affected 53%, most commonly urinary infection (40%), ureteral stricture (20%) and difficult catheterization (18%), and conservative treatment resolved more than 80% of them. Postoperative death occurred in 11%. The selection and extent of pelvic surgery limit comparison with routine cystectomy cohorts.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Orthotopic Right-colon or Ileocolonic Reservoirs

Orthotopic reconstruction requires a suitable urethra or outlet and willingness to use catheterization if emptying is inadequate. Colon-based and ileal reservoirs cannot be ranked solely by continence rates from separate series.

- **Le Bag:** a stapled ileocolonic orthotopic design with several modifications; see the [dedicated page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion/le-bag) for construction and the actual continence definitions.
- **B-bladder:** a Le Bag modification with a Studer-type ileal chimney for the ureteral anastomoses, reported in 29 patients with median follow-up of 3.4 years. Late recurrent urinary infection occurred in 17% and uretero-neobladder stricture in 3%.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **D'Orazio right-colonic neobladder:** detubularized, remodeled right colon with an intact cecal segment, reported in 38 patients operated between 1993 and 2004 (the eleven years describe accrual, not follow-up of each patient). Continence came from a low-pressure reservoir of more than 600 mL and haustral contractions of the intact segment that eject urine toward the detubularized portion. Voiding used abdominal tension and mass contractions of the intact segment above 100 cm H₂O. Daytime continence was immediate in 37 of 38 (all by 30 days), nighttime continence was total and immediate in 36 (92%), and mean maximum flow exceeded 26 mL/s. One patient needed clean intermittent catheterization. Single-center results of this kind do not establish an advantage over compliant ileal reservoirs.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

The Bedük comparison included 19 ileocecal (Mainz) and 36 ileal (Abol-Enein and Ghoneim) neobladder patients. Only 39 underwent the 12-month urodynamic evaluation. Follow-up differed and the groups were small and nonrandomized.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

| Parameter | Ileocecal | Ileal |
|---|---|---|
| Capacity | 426 ± 34 mL | 442 ± 27 mL |
| Maximum flow | 19.6 ± 3.7 mL/s | 16 ± 6.1 mL/s |
| Residual volume | 37 ± 8.2 mL | 45 ± 7.1 mL |
| Daytime incontinence | 5.3% | 5.5% |
| Nighttime incontinence (twice weekly or more) | 21% | 8.4% |
| Ureterointestinal stenosis | 10.5% | 5.7% |
| Pyelonephritis | 15.8% | 13.8% |

Urodynamic parameters did not differ significantly.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

The Cochrane review of intestinal reconstruction found only five randomized trials involving 355 participants, of small size and moderate or poor quality. It found no evidence that bladder replacement was better than conduit diversion after cystectomy for cancer, and no daytime or nocturnal continence difference between ileocolonic or ileocecal and ileal replacement in a two-trial meta-analysis. One small trial suggested less nocturnal incontinence with ileum, of uncertain clinical significance.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

## Choosing the Outlet and Comparing Quality of Life

An appendiceal channel, tapered ileal limb and reconstructed neo-appendix have different reach and revision issues. Cheng's 58-patient comparison contained 33 Indiana, 15 appendico-umbilical and 10 neo-appendico-umbilical diversions, with baseline age and comorbidity and treatment differences. After a median follow-up of 21 months, the appendico-umbilical and neo-appendico-umbilical groups had more high-grade diversion-related complications than the Indiana group, and the neo-appendico group had more stoma complications (70% vs 30%). Most patients in all groups rated quality of life as good to excellent. The groups were not comparable, so the study does not rank channels.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

A gender-matched comparison of 45 Mainz pouch I (ileal nipple) and 45 ileal-conduit pairs found no difference in stool incontinence. Mainz pouch patients more often reported increased stool frequency (53% vs 31%), softer stool (60% vs 13%) and diarrhea (62% vs 20%), and less constipation (4% vs 22%). They also reported impaired overall postoperative quality of life more often (51% vs 29%, p = 0.024). Changes in bowel habit belong in preoperative counseling. Selection also weighs catheterization burden, stoma preference and the possibility of revision.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

## Follow-up and Late Complications

Follow storage and emptying, catheter access, continence, mucus and stone problems, renal function, electrolytes including bicarbonate, and upper-tract imaging. The frequency of investigations should reflect renal reserve, prior obstruction, biochemical abnormalities and symptoms; a fixed annual pouchogram or arterial blood gas is not necessary for every stable patient.

Ileal resection and urine-exposed bowel create risks of B12 deficiency and hyperchloremic acidosis. In the Mainz metabolic follow-up study, 94 of 458 original patients were examined after a median of nine years; B12 supplementation was recommended in 32% because levels were low-normal or low; no patient had megaloblastic anemia, funicular myelosis or osteoporosis. A total of 37% were still taking sodium/potassium citrate to prevent acidosis. Bone density was measured in only 18 patients.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

A retrospective comparison of 155 patients included only 20 ileocecal pouches, alongside 107 conduits and 28 ileal neobladders. Renal decline did not differ significantly between the diversions. The small ileocecal group limits this comparison.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

Promptly assess new hematuria, recurrent symptomatic infection, difficult catheterization, flank pain, hydronephrosis or worsening renal function. Acute abdominal pain with reduced reservoir drainage or systemic illness raises concern for obstruction or perforation. Medication and irrigation details are maintained in the [mucus](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/urinary-diversion-specific/mucus-management), [B12](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/urinary-diversion-specific/vitamin-b12-supplementation) and [alkali](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/urinary-diversion-specific/urinary-acidifiers) companion pages.
