# Vescica Ileale Padovana (VIP) / Padua Ileal Bladder (PIB)

The **Vescica Ileale Padovana (VIP)**, or **Padua ileal bladder**, is a detubularized and reconfigured orthotopic ileal reservoir described by Pagano, Artibani and colleagues. The original 1990 report included 16 patients; later open, robotic and partly stapled modifications use different construction sequences and ureteral anastomoses.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

VIP is one of several neobladder configurations. The 2024 systematic review of robotic intracorporeal neobladders identified nine types and found no randomized trials or cohort studies comparing them. No configuration has shown superior functional results over Studer, Hautmann or the others.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Historical Development

| Publication | Milestone |

| 2009 | Ferriero and Simone (Rome) report early and late urodynamics in 46 men followed to 48 months<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| 2010 | Novara and Artibani report validated-questionnaire outcomes in 113 patients at a median of 44 months<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |

| 2019 | Cacciamani (Padova) report robot-assisted VIP (ra-VIP), reproducing the open principles intracorporeally<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| 2023 | Tuderti (Rome) report 251 patients treated with robotic cystectomy and intracorporeal diversion<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| 2024 | Tuderti reports a 10-year learning-curve analysis of 200 patients<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |

## Anatomy and Construction

Detubularization interrupts coordinated ileal contractions; reshaping creates a rounded reservoir with a dependent urethral outlet. The biological pouch can still contract, and neither its shape nor a favorable pressure measurement guarantees safe storage or complete emptying.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

The classic VIP has no Studer-type afferent limb. The original authors' operative chapter describes a detubularized reservoir, a dependent funnel for urethral connection and Le Duc ureteral implantation, with a later serous-lined extramural tunnel modification. An added afferent limb belongs to a particular modified design.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

### Operative framework

1. Select well-perfused ileum that reaches the retained urethra without tension; the original-author chapter describes 40–60 cm, with appropriate terminal ileum preserved.
2. Isolate the segment, restore bowel continuity and open the reservoir bowel along the antimesenteric border.
3. Form the dependent outlet or funnel and reconfigure the opened bowel into the posterior wall and rounded reservoir.
4. Perform the planned ureteral implantation, preserving ureteral blood supply and avoiding angulation or constriction.
5. Connect the dependent reservoir to the urethra, complete closure and confirm drainage and watertightness.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

For the serous-lined extramural variant, adjacent bowel surfaces form the trough; the ureter is anastomosed to intestinal mucosa and the edges close over it. This is not a mucosal Le Duc sulcus and does not require inventing a separate Studer afferent limb. See [Mansoura Neobladder](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion/mansoura-neobladder) for the detailed tunnel discussion.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

Robotic VIP and Rome's partly stapled PIB are adaptations of these principles. Their dimensions, anastomoses and outcomes differ. Steps and results are reported here by the specific modification.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Selection and Postoperative Care

Use the same selection principles as other orthotopic bowel reservoirs: suitable bowel, adequate kidney and liver function, a usable sphincter or outlet, oncologic suitability of the retained urethra and the ability to manage emptying or catheterization. A negative urethral margin must be verified. Age alone is not an absolute contraindication.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

Plan pouch drainage, mucus care, leak assessment and catheter or stent removal around the actual reconstruction and healing. After catheter removal, teach scheduled emptying, outlet relaxation, recognition of retention and intermittent catheterization when needed. Published low-pressure results do not replace residual-urine and upper-tract assessment.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Functional Outcomes

### Original open experience

Pagano's initial 16 patients had 4–18 months of follow-up (mean ten months). Fourteen (87%) were continent by day and 13 (81%) reported dry sleep for 6–7 hours. Capacity was 400–650 mL and mean pressure at capacity was 17 cmH₂O, but half had pressure waves of 30–50 cmH₂O at a threshold volume of 250 mL. Reflux was absent, and patients emptied by abdominal straining and perineal relaxation. One ureteroileal and two urethroileal strictures were reported. This is an early cohort with short follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Patient-reported function

Novara 2010 evaluated 113 patients alive and disease-free at approximately 44 months with the AUA Symptom Index, ICIQ-UI SF and IIEF-5. Sixteen (13%) used intermittent catheterization. Among the 97 voiding patients, lower urinary tract symptoms were mild in 48.5%, moderate in 40.2% and severe in 11.3%. Twenty patients (17.7%) were fully continent, and 31.9%, 35.4% and 15% had slight, moderate and severe incontinence. Roughly 90% by day and 80% by night used no pad or only a safety pad. The safety-pad category is broader than dryness. About 20% of the men were potent (IIEF-5 ≥ 17).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

ASA class (OR 9.0, p = 0.03) and BMI (OR 1.5, p = 0.023) independently predicted the need for catheterization, and age at surgery predicted symptom severity. The authors concluded that functional outcomes may be significantly overestimated without patient-completed questionnaires. The cohort was cross-sectional, from a single center and limited to disease-free survivors.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

### Change over time

Ferriero's urodynamic study began with 46 men; eight died of cancer before long-term assessment. Cystometric capacity and urethral closing pressure did not change between 12 and 48 months. End-filling pressure (p = 0.0005), pressure at peak flow (p = 0.0007) and residual urine (p = 0.013) were lower at 48 months. Involuntary contractions were more frequent (p = 0.0012) with comparable amplitude (p = 0.084). Daytime continence was 96% at 12 months and 90% at 48 months; nighttime continence was 60% at 48 months. The authors concluded that ileal tone persists despite detubularization and probably limits enlargement of the reservoir.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Robotic Series and Experience

| Report | Population and interpretation |
|---|---|
| Cacciamani 2019 ra-VIP | 15 consecutive patients (2015–2017). Median operating time was 390 minutes (IQR 284–470), estimated blood loss 300 mL and hospital stay 17 days, with no conversions. Six patients had major complications within 90 days. Daytime continence at 12 months was 62%; 14 of 15 were alive at a mean follow-up of 17 months. The report establishes early feasibility.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Simone 2018 partly stapled PIB | 45 consecutive patients operated on during 2012–2014. Median operating time was 305 minutes, blood loss 210 mL and stay 9 days, with no transfusion or conversion. Overall complications rose from 44.4% perioperatively and 57.8% by 30 days to 77.8% by 180 days; severe complications were 17.8%, 17.8% and 35.5% at those intervals. Two-year daytime and nighttime continence were 73.3% and 55.5%, and two-year disease-free, cancer-specific and overall survival were 72.5%, 82.3% and 82.4%. The later interval captures additional events and is not directly comparable with a 30- or 90-day series.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Tuderti 2023 | 251 patients underwent robotic cystectomy with intracorporeal diversion (2012–2020); 192 had an orthotopic neobladder. One-year continence estimates were 78.6% by day and 48.3% by night, and the trifecta rate was 64.1% in the neobladder group. Five-year disease-free, cancer-specific and overall survival were 66.5%, 65.4% and 61.5% for the whole cohort, which includes non-neobladder diversions.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| Tuderti 2024 learning-curve study | 200 patients treated during 2013–2022, each with at least one year of follow-up. It examined changes in one-year outcomes across the program's decade, not ten-year recovery in every patient.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |

The learning-curve study defined daytime continence as no pads, while nighttime continence allowed one safety pad with wetness of no more than 50 mL. Hospital stay (p = 0.002) and 30-day complications (p = 0.04) fell over the decade, and trifecta achievement improved (p < 0.001). The annual average percentage change in the one-year continence probability was 11.45% by day (p < 0.001) and 10.05% by night (p = 0.009). Case sequence independently predicted daytime (HR 1.008; p < 0.001) and nighttime continence (HR 1.004; p = 0.03). The authors could not identify a case-volume plateau, and experience coincided with other changes over time.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

In the 2023 Tuderti series, age (HR 0.98; p = 0.03), female sex (HR 0.57; p = 0.008) and Clavien grade ≥ III complications (HR 0.55; p = 0.03) were independent predictors of daytime continence.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Late Complications and Renal Function

Presicce 2021 studied 210 patients: 167 PIB and 43 ileal conduits, with mean follow-up of 30 months. Its overall 14% stricture and 52% renal-decline figures should not be labeled PIB-only outcomes. The published subgroup counts were:<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

| Outcome during follow-up | PIB group, n = 167 |
|---|---:|
| Ureteroileal stricture | 25 (15%) |
| At least 20% eGFR reduction | 75 (45%) |
| Symptomatic UTI | 63 (38%) |
| Stones | 17 (10%) |
| Incisional hernia | 35 (21%) |

Across all 210 patients, lymphocele occurred in 10%, bowel occlusion in 2% and systemic events (metabolic acidosis and major cardiovascular events) in 1%. Among the 43 conduit patients, who were older and had more comorbidity, 5 (12%) had ureteroileal stricture, 34 (79%) had an eGFR reduction of at least 20% and none had stones. The authors describe the results as in line with historical open series; definitions, eligibility, follow-up and anastomotic methods differ between series, and between-diversion differences here are descriptive.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

Investigate obstruction, infection, poor emptying and other contributors when kidney function deteriorates. A technically patent or refluxing anastomosis does not eliminate the need for long-term renal and upper-tract surveillance.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Metabolic and Bone Health

Giannini's observational study compared 25 male VIP patients with 16 healthy controls. It found lower bicarbonate and lower bone density at selected femoral sites. It did not test lifelong alkali treatment or establish that every VIP patient needs indefinite supplementation or a fixed bone-density schedule.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

Monitor electrolytes, bicarbonate and kidney function, and treat acidosis according to the actual findings and clinical context. Assess bone health according to acidosis, renal function and other risk factors. Measure vitamin B12 annually after bowel diversion. A low rate of coded acidosis complications in a surgical series is not the same as systematically measured biochemical prevalence.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

See [Vitamin B12 supplementation](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/urinary-diversion-specific/vitamin-b12-supplementation), [Urinary acidifiers & alkalinizers](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/urinary-diversion-specific/urinary-acidifiers), and [Mucus management](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/urinary-diversion-specific/mucus-management).

## Reconstruction in Women

Tuderti's 2020 report included 11 highly selected women (cT ≤ 2 N0 M0, no tumor in the bladder neck, trigone or urethra at transurethral resection) undergoing sex-sparing cystectomy with PIB. Median operating time was 255 minutes and median stay 7 days. Four women (36.3%) had low-grade complications and none had high-grade complications. At a median follow-up of 28 months, none had new-onset CKD stage 3b. The one-year daytime and nighttime continence values of 90.9% and 86.4% were Kaplan–Meier recovery estimates, not simple percentages of eleven observations. Three women used intermittent catheterization twice daily; one developed a neobladder stone and one required ureteral reimplantation.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

Quality of life and physical and emotional functioning improved significantly over time (p ≤ 0.04). Urinary symptoms and sexual function worsened at three months and recovered significantly by one year. Eight of 11 women (72.7%) were sexually active at one year, but several sexual-function scores remained significantly below baseline. A small nonrandomized comparison with substantially older patients undergoing standard surgery cannot establish that the preservation technique causes superior function or equivalent cancer control. Counsel about both leakage and retention, and base organ preservation on oncologic eligibility.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Comparison with Other Reservoirs

The Di Maida 2022 comparison enrolled 100 patients treated with robotic cystectomy and the Florence robotic intracorporeal neobladder (FloRIN), but only 69 had complete functional data and 28 had complete urodynamic data. Among the 69, 52 (75.4%) reached daytime continence (28, or 40.6%, with no pads) and 45 (65.2%) reached nighttime continence. Compared with a contemporary open VIP series from the same institution, only neobladder compliance differed on urodynamics (higher with FloRIN, p = 0.03). Urgency (p = 0.01), psychological status (p = 0.02) and physical self-acceptance (p = 0.02) scores favored FloRIN. The differences reflect both the approach (robotic vs open) and the reservoir construction. They do not isolate the effect of the pouch.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

Zhu 2025 compared an anti-reflux ileum valve-pouch, a design the authors relate to VIP, with a modified Studer pouch in 127 patients at two centers (66 and 61 before matching; 42 per group after 1:1 propensity matching). Median follow-up was 44.5 and 35.5 months. Operating time, cancer-specific survival, overall survival and total complications did not differ. Rehospitalization for urinary tract infection was less frequent with the valve-pouch (p = 0.039), and decreased eGFR at 12 months was less frequent (p = 0.031). The valve-pouch is not the classic VIP, and the study does not test VIP itself.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

Similarly, Parekh's historical 100-patient series contained only 18 Padua reservoirs among five reconstruction types. It was an institutional experience, not a randomized multicenter equivalence trial.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

The larger review of intracorporeal techniques found insufficient direct comparative evidence to favor one configuration. Choose with attention to the patient's goals, anatomy, self-care capability and the team's experience, without promising that every named reservoir has identical pressures, continence or renal outcomes.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Long-Term Surveillance

Continue functional assessment beyond the intensive cancer-surveillance period.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

- **Kidney and metabolic function:** creatinine and eGFR, electrolytes and bicarbonate. AUA recommends laboratory assessment every 3–6 months for the first 2–3 years after treatment and then annually, adjusted for clinical need.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **Upper tract and emptying:** imaging as indicated for obstruction, stones or deterioration; review residual urine, continence, catheterization and mucus management.
- **Nutrition:** annual B12, with additional evaluation for unexplained anemia, neurologic symptoms, diarrhea or bone-health concerns.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Retained urothelium:** risk-appropriate cancer follow-up, including monitoring of the retained urethra.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

See [Renal function and metabolic surveillance](/docs/evaluation/laboratory-studies/renal-function-metabolic-surveillance).
