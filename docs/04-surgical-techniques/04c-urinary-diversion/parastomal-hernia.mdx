# Parastomal Hernia After Urinary Diversion

**Parastomal hernia (PSH)** is the **most common long-term stomal complication** after urinary diversion. After radical cystectomy with ileal conduit, **20–30% of patients develop a radiologic PSH within 2 years**, ~31% of those become symptomatic, and roughly 15% require surgical repair.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Repair carries **high recurrence rates (17–52%)** regardless of technique and is complicated by the unique vulnerabilities of urinary stomas — UTI, mesh exposure to urine, conduit mesentery, and frequent concomitant ureteroenteric stricture or ventral hernia.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

This page is canonical for urinary-stoma PSH. Cross-linked from [Continent Catheterizable Channels Principles](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/catheterizable-channels) — PSH also affects continent cutaneous channels (Indiana / Kock / Mitrofanoff) and is discussed below.

---

## Epidemiology and Natural History

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Radiologic PSH at 1 yr | 12–20%<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Radiologic PSH at 2 yr | 22–30%<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Radiologic PSH at 3 yr | 32%<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Pooled clinical incidence (systematic review) | 14.4%<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| Symptomatic PSH (of those with radiologic PSH) | 31%<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Requiring surgical repair | 15–45% of symptomatic patients<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Median time to PSH development | 8–13 mo<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Risk plateau | After year 3<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |

Approximately **26%** of patients with radiologic PSH will progress (radiologic upgrading or need for surgery), with median time to progression of **12 months**.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Risk Factors

| Risk factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Obesity / higher BMI** | OR 2.8 (BMI ≥30); HR 1.07 per unit BMI<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Fascial defect ≥24–30 mm** | OR 5.23 for defect ≥30 mm<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Female sex** | HR 1.86<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Diabetes** | HR 1.81<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **COPD** | HR 1.78<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **Prior laparotomy** | Adjusted HR 1.98<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Longer operative time** | OR 1.25<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Lower postop eGFR** | OR 2.17<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Poor nutritional status (NRI ≤83)** | AUC 0.731<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Postoperative UTI** | Independent predictor<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |

No significant difference between **open vs MIS cystectomy** or **intracorporeal vs extracorporeal conduit** in most series.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Why Urinary Stoma PSH Is Different

Urinary stoma PSH carries challenges fecal stoma PSH does not:<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[9]](#ref9)[[6]](#ref6)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- **Urinary complications dominate** the postoperative course — ureteroenteric stricture, UTI, urine leak in up to 17–33%.
- **Conduit mesentery must be preserved** during dissection, limiting mobilization and mesh-configuration options.
- **Concomitant ureteroenteric stricture** may require simultaneous repair.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Mesh-infection risk** is heightened by constant exposure to urine — mesh extraction was needed in **8.6%** in one ileal-conduit series.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Neurogenic-bladder patients** with continent catheterizable channels have weak abdominal-wall musculature.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Stoma-appliance adherence** is critical — a large hernia sac stretches overlying skin, often forcing resiting.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Up to 50% of ileal conduit PSH** are associated with a concomitant midline incisional hernia, requiring simultaneous repair.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

---

## Conservative Management

Small, reducible, asymptomatic PSH:<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

- Hernia belt / support garment.
- Stoma-appliance optimization (convex pouching, belt attachment).
- Weight management and avoidance of heavy lifting.

Elective surgical repair is reserved for **significant pouching difficulties, pain, recurrent obstruction, or incarceration**.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

---

## Prevention — Prophylactic Mesh at Index Surgery

The role of prophylactic mesh at the time of ileal conduit creation is **controversial** with conflicting RCTs.

| Study | Design | Mesh type | Position | PSH (mesh) | PSH (no mesh) | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Liedberg 2020 (ISRCTN 95093825)<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> | RCT, n = 242 | Lightweight synthetic | Sublay (retromuscular) | 11% clinical at 24 mo | 23% | HR 0.45 (p = 0.02) — **favors mesh** |
| Djaladat 2024 (PUBMIC)<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> | RCT, n = 146 | Biologic (FlexHD) | Sublay intraperitoneal | 31% radiologic at 2 yr | 38% | No significant difference |
| Donahue 2025 (MSKCC)<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> | RCT, n = 178 | Semi-absorbable (Ultrapro) | Sublay retromuscular | 47% radiologic at 24 mo | 33% | No benefit; mesh arm trended worse |

A **network meta-analysis** (25 studies across all stoma types) found prophylactic mesh significantly reduced PSH beyond 6 mo (OR 0.43, 95% CI 0.33–0.58), with the **retromuscular approach** lowest.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> A **Cochrane review** concluded that mesh placement is safe and reduces PSH, especially with the open extraperitoneal (sublay) technique.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

The two most recent urology-specific RCTs (PUBMIC, MSKCC) failed to show benefit — leaving the evidence equivocal for ileal conduits specifically. The discrepancy may relate to **mesh type** (biologic and semi-absorbable performed worse than lightweight synthetic in the sublay position) and **technique**.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[15]](#ref15)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

A **cost-effectiveness analysis** found prophylactic mesh cost-effective ($2,115 / QALY gained), sensitive to mesh-infection probability.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

### Novel stoma-construction technique

Tanaka et al. limit the fascial passage to **≤2.4 cm**, create an oblique passage through the rectus, and separately fix the anterior and posterior rectus sheaths to the conduit. Radiologic PSH dropped from **19.6% to 3.5%** (p = 0.011) **without mesh**.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

## Indications for Surgical Repair

- Symptomatic hernia: pain, pouching difficulties, cosmetic distress.
- Recurrent bowel obstruction or incarceration / strangulation (emergency).
- Difficulty with stoma-appliance adherence.
- Concurrent need for other abdominal surgery.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

The ASCRS guidelines recommend **mesh reinforcement** for PSH repair (Grade 1C) — primary suture repair carries 46–78% recurrence vs ~20% with mesh.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

---

## Mesh-Repair Techniques

| Technique | Configuration | Recurrence in ileal-conduit series |
|---|---|---|
| **Keyhole** | Mesh with aperture for the stoma limb | **35–52%** — highest among mesh techniques.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[13]](#ref13)[[6]](#ref6)[[21]](#ref21)</sup> |
| **Sugarbaker** | Intact mesh underlay; stoma exits laterally under mesh edge | **10–21.5%** — generally lowest.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[13]](#ref13)[[6]](#ref6)[[21]](#ref21)</sup> |
| **Sandwich** | Two mesh sheets above and below fascia; stoma passes through | 13.5% in one prospective randomized study.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **3-D mesh implant (IPST)** | Purpose-made 3D alloplastic mesh via small open incision | 7.4% at median 29 mo (n = 40); 1 wound infection, no removals.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> |

The only **RCT comparing Sugarbaker vs keyhole** in the retromuscular position (Maskal, JAMA Surg 2024, n = 150) found **no significant difference at 2 yr** — Sugarbaker 17% vs keyhole 24% (adjusted RR 0.87, 95% CI 0.42–1.69). No difference in mesh-related complications, wound morbidity, QoL, or decision regret.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Mesh position

| Position | Recurrence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Preperitoneal** | **6.5%** | Lowest in one large series<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Onlay | 25% | Accessible but higher recurrence<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Intraperitoneal | 36.4% | Most common in MIS repairs<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Retrorectus | 17–24% (RCT) | Conflicting smaller-series data<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |

### Mesh type

- **Permanent synthetic** (polypropylene, polyester) — most commonly used; overall morbidity 24.9%, SSI 3.8%, mesh infection 1.7%.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Biologic** — recurrence ~15.7–18% at 3.8 yr; not superior to synthetic for elective repair.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Synthetic resorbable** — limited data; used in some prophylactic series.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

## Open vs Minimally Invasive Repair

| Parameter | Open | Laparoscopic / Robotic |
|---|---|---|
| OR time | Longer | Shorter (p \<0.001)<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> |
| Hospital stay | 6–7 d | 1–5 d (p \<0.001)<sup>[[24]](#ref24)[[25]](#ref25)</sup> |
| Wound complications | 18.6% | Similar or lower<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> |
| SSI | Higher | RR 0.37–0.63 (p = 0.02–0.03)<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> |
| Mortality | Higher | RR 0.18 (p = 0.0009)<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> |
| Recurrence | 17–21% | Similar (no significant difference)<sup>[[24]](#ref24)[[25]](#ref25)</sup> |

ASCRS allows MIS repair in selected patients (Grade 1C). Open is favored for **larger defects** and when **stoma resiting** is needed.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> A 2025 meta-analysis confirmed laparoscopic repair shortens LOS by ~4 d, lowers SSI and mortality, with no difference in recurrence.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

### Robotic-assisted repair

- **Xu 2021** — 4 patients robotic-assisted (3 Da Vinci SP). Median LOS **1 day**, no intraoperative complications, **no recurrences** at median 18.3 mo. Concurrent ureteroenteric stricture repair, panniculectomy, and abdominal-wall reconstruction performed simultaneously.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Dewulf 2022** — 15 patients, 10/15 robotic. Median LOS 5 d. One recurrence (6.7%) at d 66. One-third developed postop UTI.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

The robotic platform handles adhesiolysis in a hostile abdomen, allows simultaneous ureteroenteric stricture repair, and provides the dexterity needed for complex mesh placement around the conduit mesentery.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Stoma Resiting vs In Situ Repair

Resiting has **fallen out of favor** because hernia recurs at the new site:<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- Holland series — recurrence after resiting with mesh **34.8%** vs **17.3%** for in-situ mesh repair.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- All recurrences after resiting were at the new stoma site, independent of prophylactic mesh use.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

Resiting remains necessary when overlying skin is too damaged for appliance adherence or when body habitus has changed substantially.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

**Laparoscopic stoma resiting** for both continent channels and incontinent diversions has been described — channel dropped into the abdomen, hernia repaired with mesh, channel brought out through a laparoscopic port site. Continence and patency **100%** at 2 yr.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Continent Cutaneous Diversion PSH

PSH also complicates [Indiana pouch](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion/indiana-pouch), [Kock pouch](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion/kock-pouch), and [Mitrofanoff appendicovesicostomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/appendicovesicostomy):<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[26]](#ref26)[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

- **Helal 1997 (n = 21).** **61.9%** had simultaneous urinary incontinence — the hernia disrupts the continence mechanism. Repair: transabdominal takedown, hernia closure (Marlex if defect \>6 cm), revision of the anti-incontinence segment, and stoma resiting. **89.5% success at 23.4 mo**; incontinence corrected in 100% of affected patients.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>
- **Stout 2021** — laparoscopic repair in 2 continent catheterizable channels and 2 incontinent diversions. **100% continence and patency** at 2 yr.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Mitrofanoff channels** — PSH reported but uncommon (1/31 in one pediatric series). Small caliber of the appendiceal channel makes a smaller fascial defect.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

Key considerations for continent-diversion PSH: **address the continence mechanism simultaneously** (often disrupted by the hernia) and **preserve the mesenteric blood supply** to the catheterizable channel during dissection.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Complications of PSH Repair

| Complication | Incidence |
|---|---|
| UTI | 17–33% (most common in ileal-conduit PSH repair)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| Wound complications | 18.6% (SSI, seroma, dehiscence)<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> |
| Mesh infection requiring removal | 1.7–8.6% (higher with urinary stomas)<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| Stoma stenosis | 7.4%<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| Bowel obstruction | Rare (mesh or adhesive)<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Recurrence | **17–52%** by technique; keyhole highest<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Clavien ≥III | 7.8–12.9%<sup>[[24]](#ref24)[[28]](#ref28)</sup> |
| 90-day mortality | 0–6% (higher in elderly, open repair with resiting)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[28]](#ref28)</sup> |

---

## Recurrent PSH

Recurrent PSH after initial repair is common and uniquely difficult:<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- Roussel series — **22.9%** of patients required reoperation for recurrence (30.7% keyhole, 18.2% Sugarbaker).
- At reoperation, **50% could not receive synthetic mesh** because of difficult adhesiolysis, leading to suture-only repair — which then required **a third surgery in 37.5%**.
- Authors concluded that both keyhole and Sugarbaker may be inadequate for ileal-conduit PSH and that new approaches need to be explored.

---

## Treatment Algorithm

1. **Asymptomatic / minimally symptomatic** — conservative management with hernia belt and stoma-appliance optimization.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
2. **Symptomatic, small defect, no concomitant ventral hernia** — MIS (laparoscopic or robotic) with Sugarbaker or sandwich; or 3-D mesh implant via small open incision.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[24]](#ref24)[[11]](#ref11)[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
3. **Symptomatic, large defect, concomitant ventral hernia** — open retromuscular repair with posterior component separation (transversus abdominis release) and mesh; Sugarbaker or keyhole — no clear superiority at 2 yr.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)[[30]](#ref30)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
4. **Stoma requiring resiting** (skin damage, body-habitus change) — open or laparoscopic resiting with mesh reinforcement at new site; counsel re: high recurrence.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[13]](#ref13)[[5]](#ref5)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
5. **Concurrent ureteroenteric stricture** — robotic approach allows simultaneous PSH repair and ureteroenteric reimplantation.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
6. **Continent diversion PSH** — address continence mechanism simultaneously; consider stoma resiting with anti-incontinence revision.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>
7. **Emergency (incarceration / strangulation)** — emergent laparotomy with hernia reduction, bowel assessment, and repair.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
