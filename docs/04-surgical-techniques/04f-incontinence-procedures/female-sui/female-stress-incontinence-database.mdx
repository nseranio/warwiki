# Female Stress Urinary Incontinence

Female stress urinary incontinence (SUI) results from urethral hypermobility, intrinsic sphincter deficiency (ISD), or both. Treatment selection is guided by incontinence severity, degree of hypermobility vs. ISD, prior pelvic surgery or radiation, mesh eligibility, and patient goals. Behavioral and conservative measures are always first-line; surgical intervention is reserved for patients who have failed or declined conservative management.

---

## Decision Framework

**Pelvic floor muscle training (PFMT) is the undisputed first-line therapy** across all major guidelines (ACOG 2015, AUA / SUFU 2023, Cochrane). Surgery is reserved for patients who fail or decline conservative management. The 2023 AUA / SUFU guideline update conditionally recommends **all three midurethral-sling approaches — retropubic, transobturator, and single-incision** — for uncomplicated SUI; the SIMS RCT (NEJM 2022, n = 596) established noninferiority of single-incision mini-slings to standard MUS through 36 months. **Autologous fascial slings have re-emerged** as a mesh-free alternative with equivalent cure rates (Grigoryan 2024 meta) and zero mesh-erosion risk. **Bulkamid (polyacrylamide hydrogel)** has emerged as the preferred bulking agent — 67% cure / improvement at 7 years (Brosche 2021) — filling an important niche for elderly / frail patients and those declining mesh.

Minimum evaluation per ACOG / AUGS: history, urinalysis, exam with cough stress test, urethral-mobility assessment, and post-void residual. **Multichannel urodynamics are not required before primary surgery for uncomplicated SUI** but are indicated for complicated SUI (mixed symptoms, prior failed surgery, neurogenic bladder, PVR &gt; 150 mL, prolapse beyond the hymen).

### Treatment Selection by Clinical Scenario

| Clinical Scenario | First-Line Surgical Option | Alternative(s) | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Uncomplicated SUI with urethral hypermobility** | [Retropubic MUS (TVT)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/retropubic-midurethral-sling) | [Transobturator MUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/transobturator-midurethral-sling); [Single-incision mini-sling](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/single-incision-mini-sling) | — |
| **Patient prefers to avoid synthetic mesh** | [Autologous fascial pubovaginal sling](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/autologous-pubovaginal-sling) | [Burch colposuspension](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/burch-colposuspension); urethral bulking | Mesh-based MUS |
| **Patient prefers minimally-invasive option / declines anesthesia** | [Urethral bulking (Bulkamid)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/urethral-bulking-agents) — office-based | [Single-incision mini-sling](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/single-incision-mini-sling) under local | — |
| **Elderly / frail patient with multiple comorbidities** | [Urethral bulking (Bulkamid)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/urethral-bulking-agents) | [Continence pessary](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/continence-pessary) | High-morbidity surgery |
| **SUI without urethral hypermobility (ISD, fixed urethra)** | [Autologous fascial PVS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/autologous-pubovaginal-sling) | [Urethral bulking](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/urethral-bulking-agents) | TMUS (less effective in ISD) |
| **Concomitant pelvic organ prolapse (open / laparoscopic SCP)** | MUS + prolapse repair, **or** [Burch at time of sacrocolpopexy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/burch-colposuspension) | Autologous PVS at time of vaginal repair | — |
| **Recurrent SUI after prior sling** | Re-evaluate (urodynamics + ISD assessment) → repeat bulking, repeat sling, [autologous PVS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/autologous-pubovaginal-sling), or [AUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) | — | Reflexive repeat MUS without workup |
| **Severe ISD after failed prior surgery** | [Female AUS (AMS-800)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) | Obstructing autologous PVS | Standard MUS (likely to fail) |
| **Devastated outlet, all options exhausted** | Bladder-neck closure + [continent catheterizable channel (Mitrofanoff)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/catheterizable-channels) | [Urinary diversion](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion) | Continued failed reconstruction |
| **Postmenopausal with GSM-driven irritative overlay** | Add [vaginal estrogen](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/hormonal-therapies/vaginal-topical-estrogen) before deciding on procedure | — | [Energy-based vaginal laser](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/vaginal-laser-therapy) (low-certainty Cochrane evidence; FDA warning) |

### MUS Sub-Comparison

When a midurethral sling is selected, three approaches differ in profile:

| Feature | [Retropubic (RMUS)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/retropubic-midurethral-sling) | [Transobturator (TMUS)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/transobturator-midurethral-sling) | [Single-Incision (SIS)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/single-incision-mini-sling) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cure rate (Imamura BMJ 2019 SUCRA)** | Highest (89.1%) | Moderate (64.1%) | Noninferior to MUS at 36 mo (SIMS) |
| **Bladder perforation** | Higher | Lower | Lowest |
| **Groin / thigh pain** | Lower | Higher | Lower (short-term) |
| **Voiding dysfunction** | Moderate | Lower | Similar |
| **Local-anesthesia placement** | Rarely | Rarely | **Yes** |
| **Long-term data (&gt; 5 yr)** | Extensive | Extensive | Limited (Level B) |
| **Mesh exposure** | ~1–5% | ~1–5% | 3.3% (SIMS) |
| **Dyspareunia** | Low | Low | Higher (11.7% vs 4.8%) |

### Stepwise Escalation Ladder

1. **Conservative therapy** — [supervised PFMT](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/pelvic-floor-pt) ± biofeedback ± dynamic lumbopelvic stabilization, [weight loss](/docs/clinical-conditions/03a-storage-incontinence/sui-female) (BMI ≥ 25; Level A), behavioral / fluid / caffeine modifications
2. **[Continence pessary](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/continence-pessary)** — bridge to surgery or for patients preferring non-surgical management
3. **[Urethral bulking (Bulkamid)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/urethral-bulking-agents)** — office-based, repeatable; preferred for elderly / frail / mesh-averse
4. **Midurethral sling** ([RMUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/retropubic-midurethral-sling) / [TMUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/transobturator-midurethral-sling) / [SIS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/single-incision-mini-sling)) — gold-standard surgical
5. **[Autologous fascial PVS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/autologous-pubovaginal-sling)** — when mesh declined / contraindicated, ISD-predominant, or concomitant urethral reconstruction
6. **[Burch colposuspension](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/burch-colposuspension)** — typically at time of concomitant abdominal / laparoscopic pelvic surgery
7. **Repeat sling or bulking** — for recurrent SUI after workup
8. **[Artificial Urinary Sphincter (AUS)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter)** — severe ISD after failed surgery (80% continence per Peyronnet 2019 meta of 964 women; revision ~15%, explantation ~13%)
9. **Bladder-neck closure / urinary diversion** — last resort
