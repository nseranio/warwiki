# OAB & Urgency Urinary Incontinence

Overactive bladder (OAB) and urgency urinary incontinence (UUI) are managed across a menu of treatment categories rather than a strict stepwise ladder. The [AUA/SUFU 2024 guideline](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/nau.25532) permits minimally invasive treatment without mandatory prior behavioral or drug trials through shared decision-making; this is an Expert Opinion option, not a requirement to start with a procedure. Reconstructive urologists are typically involved at the procedural and surgical end of this pathway.

---

## Decision Framework

The guideline describes incontinence management, behavioral therapy, optimization of comorbidities, noninvasive therapy, pharmacologic therapy, minimally invasive therapy, invasive therapy and indwelling catheters. Discuss absorbent products, other containment options and barrier creams with all patients who have UUI (AUA/SUFU Expert Opinion).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Bladder training is offered to all patients with OAB (AUA/SUFU 2024 Strong Recommendation, Grade A), as are behavioral therapies (Clinical Principle).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> β3-agonists or antimuscarinics are oral options, with side-effect and cognitive-risk counseling. SNM, percutaneous tibial nerve stimulation (PTNS) and intradetrusor botulinum toxin should be offered after inadequate response or intolerance to behavioral or drug therapy (Moderate Recommendation, Grade A, for these named modalities); earlier procedural access without those trials is separately permitted through shared decision-making (Expert Opinion). Augmentation or diversion is reserved for severely impacted patients after other options fail. **Minimum initial office evaluation** is symptom-focused history, physical examination and urinalysis; PVR, questionnaires and a voiding diary are selective aids, not universal requirements. Urodynamics, cystoscopy and imaging are not routine initial tests. See [guideline statements 1–6 and 23–31](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/nau.25532).

The [EAU 2026 non-neurogenic female LUTS guideline](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/non-neurogenic-female-luts) is more stepwise. It recommends bladder training first-line (Strong); anticholinergics (extended-release preferred) or β3 agonists for women who fail conservative management (Strong), with early review (Strong); onabotulinumtoxinA 100 U for OAB/UUI refractory to conservative or drug treatment (Strong); SNM for UUI refractory to anticholinergic therapy (Strong); and PTNS as an option (Strong). It advises caution with long-term anticholinergics in older women, particularly with cognitive risk, and assessing anticholinergic burden (both Strong); it reports oxybutynin as likely to worsen cognition in older women (level 2) and darifenacin, fesoterodine, solifenacin and trospium as not shown to do so in short-term studies (level 1b). Vaginal laser is not offered for OAB outside research (Strong); augmentation cystoplasty is offered only after all other options fail (Weak), with lifelong follow-up.

### Treatment Selection by Clinical Scenario

| Clinical Scenario | Possible choice | Alternative(s) | Key Considerations |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mild OAB, prefers non-pharmacologic** | [Behavioral therapy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/behavioral-therapy-incontinence) + [PFMT](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/pelvic-floor-pt) ± TTNS | Bladder training alone | Adherence-dependent; few adverse effects with behavioral and noninvasive therapies per the [AUA/SUFU 2024 guideline](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/nau.25532) |
| **OAB / UUI, initial pharmacotherapy** | [β-3 agonist (vibegron or mirabegron)](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/storage-oab/beta3-agonists) | [Antimuscarinic](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/storage-oab/anticholinergics) | A β3-agonist trial is typically preferred before an antimuscarinic when cognitive risk matters; choose by patient factors. Use antimuscarinics with extreme caution in narrow-angle glaucoma, impaired gastric emptying, or a history of urinary retention (AUA/SUFU Clinical Principle).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> **US-labeled vibegron dose is 75 mg once daily**, not 100 mg. See the [current US prescribing information](https://www.gemtesa.com/files/gemtesa-prescribing-information.pdf). |
| **Inadequate response to oral monotherapy** | Combine a β3 agonist and an antimuscarinic (mirabegron 50 mg plus solifenacin 5 mg studied) | Switch drug or class | AUA/SUFU permits different-class combination (Conditional Recommendation, Grade B).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> SYNERGY II randomized wet-OAB patients to combination or either monotherapy for 12 months (treatment-emergent adverse events: 49% combination, 41% mirabegron, 44% solifenacin).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> BESIDE added mirabegron after inadequate response to solifenacin 5 mg; combination beat solifenacin 5 mg and was noninferior to 10 mg on key secondary endpoints, with superiority over 10 mg for daily micturitions.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The panel generalized the class-combination principle. Monitor anticholinergic burden. |
| **Older adult or cognitive concerns** | [β-3 agonist](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/storage-oab/beta3-agonists) often preferred | If an antimuscarinic is chosen, consider lower-CNS-penetration agents such as **trospium**, while counseling about anticholinergic exposure and monitoring cognition | Trospium **poorly** crosses the blood-brain barrier but is not proven dementia-risk-free; observational drug-specific rankings conflict. The Malcher 2022 **class-exposure** aOR was 1.48 at &gt;365 defined daily doses, not a tolterodine-specific estimate.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Patient prefers to avoid daily medication** | [Intradetrusor onabotulinumtoxinA 100 U](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/intradetrusor-botox) **or** [SNM](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/sacral-neuromodulation) **or** [iTNM](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/implantable-tibial-nerve-stimulation) | [PTNS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/percutaneous-tibial-nerve-stimulation) | May be offered without mandatory earlier therapy after counseling; this is the guideline's **Expert Opinion** early-access option, not a blanket first-line ranking.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> PTNS has been studied in treatment-naive patients in a single-arm study and in a randomized comparison with tolterodine.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> US labels differ: [BOTOX](https://www.rxabbvie.com/pdf/botox_pi.pdf) for OAB requires prior anticholinergic inadequate response or intolerance; [eCoin](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf20/P200036D.pdf) requires inadequate response or intolerance to conservative treatment or a successful PTNS trial; [Altaviva](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfPMA/pma.cfm?id=P240011) requires failure or intolerance of conservative treatment. [Revi](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/DEN220073.pdf) has no mandatory prior-treatment-failure condition, but its FDA summary contraindicates use in men with BPH or other lower urinary tract obstruction and in pregnancy; clinician discretion under guidelines still applies. Coverage may differ. |
| **Inadequate response or intolerance** | [BTX 100 U](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/intradetrusor-botox) **or** [SNM](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/sacral-neuromodulation) **or** [tibial nerve stimulation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/percutaneous-tibial-nerve-stimulation), including an implantable option in selected patients | [iTNM](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/implantable-tibial-nerve-stimulation) or office PTNS according to device eligibility and treatment burden | AUA's **Moderate Recommendation (Grade A evidence)** is to offer the treatment families after inadequate response/intolerance.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Refractory to BTX and neuromodulation** | [Augmentation cystoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/bladder-augmentation) | — | Last surgical resort; assess willingness and ability for CIC given the high postoperative risk, and plan lifelong surveillance ([EAU 2026 female LUTS](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/non-neurogenic-female-luts/chapter/disease-management), Strong). |
| **Severely impacted OAB after all other therapies fail** | [Urinary diversion](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion) | Chronic indwelling catheter only when OAB therapies are contraindicated, ineffective or no longer desired | Diversion has major surgical morbidity; chronic catheterization requires separate harm counseling.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Male with OAB + BPH** | [Vibegron 75 mg](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/storage-oab/beta3-agonists)<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> added to ongoing BPH pharmacotherapy when using its specific US BPH indication | [Mirabegron](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/storage-oab/beta3-agonists) | The [GEMTESA US label](https://www.gemtesa.com/files/gemtesa-prescribing-information.pdf) specifies men on BPH drug therapy; Study 3005 used an alpha-blocker with or without a 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor. [AUA/SUFU Statement 33](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/nau.25532)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> separately permits β3-agonist monotherapy. |

### Minimally-Invasive Sub-Comparison

When pharmacotherapy fails or is declined, the choice among BTX, SNM, iTNM, and PTNS involves distinct trade-offs. The figures below come from different studies and populations and are not head-to-head rankings:

| Feature | [Intradetrusor BTX 100 U](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/intradetrusor-botox) | [Sacral Neuromodulation (SNM)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/sacral-neuromodulation) | [Implantable TNS (iTNM)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/implantable-tibial-nerve-stimulation) | [PTNS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/percutaneous-tibial-nerve-stimulation) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Reported response** | Improves UUI in trials of 100 U; not ranked against every device here | ≥50% UUI-episode response 71.8% (weighted single-arm average among permanently implanted SNM patients; trial-phase nonresponders were excluded where tested, [Amundsen 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39476308/)<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>); 5-year composite OAB therapeutic success was 67% (modified completers) or 82% (completers) in [Siegel 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28709886/)<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>; 272 of 340 test-phase completers were implanted, and success included UUI or urgency-frequency response or normalization to fewer than eight voids/day | ≥50% UUI-episode response 71.3% (weighted single-arm average, [Amundsen 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39476308/)<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>; OAB-symptom response 79.4% vs 73.9% for SNM); 79% of 97 women assessed at 24 months after 151 implants in the single-arm pivotal OASIS Revi study (≥50% fewer UUI episodes on a 3-day diary; [Heesakkers 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39585851/)<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>) | 68% pooled success in [Wang 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32681345/)<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> meta-analysis |
| **Duration** | Repeat injections typically every 3–12 mo (AUA/SUFU); often ~6–9 mo | Continuous; generator life varies by model and settings (Axonics R20 is rated for at least 20 years)<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | Scheduled, device-specific stimulation: [eCoin (FDA Model UUI manual)](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf20/P200036D.pdf) has a nonrechargeable implant; [Revi (FDA DEN220073)](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/DEN220073.pdf) uses an externally powered, battery-free implant; [Altaviva (FDA Model P7850N guide)](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf24/P240011C.pdf) has a rechargeable implant | Ongoing maintenance sessions |
| **Reversibility** | Wears off | Device removal | Device removal | Fully reversible |
| **Trial phase needed** | No | **Yes** ([PNE](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/percutaneous-nerve-evaluation) / staged implant) | **No** | No |
| **Key risks** | UTI and retention; **6.5% initiated CIC** versus 0.4% placebo in pooled US-label OAB trials<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>; catheterization planning is required | Implant-site pain, lead issues and revision/removal risk; rates vary by device and cohort | Device-specific implant and follow-up risks; not proven identical to SNM | Usually low procedural burden, but repeated visits |
| **Office burden** | Repeat injections, typically every 3–12 mo | Low after implant | Low (home-based) | **High** — weekly + maintenance |
| **MRI compatibility** | N/A | Verify model-specific MRI conditions | eCoin: the implanted lower leg must remain outside the scanner and the device at least 20 cm from the bore edge under [FDA Model UUI labeling](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf20/P200036D.pdf)<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>; verify separate Revi and Altaviva conditions | N/A |

The [BOTOX US label](https://www.rxabbvie.com/pdf/botox_pi.pdf) specifies 100 U as the recommended and maximum adult OAB intradetrusor dose and reports CIC initiation after 100-U treatment in 36/552 (6.5%) versus 2/542 (0.4%) with placebo (Table 10).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Among patients who initiated CIC for urinary retention after BOTOX 100 U in the label trials (36/552), median catheterization lasted 63 days.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Measure baseline PVR before injection.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Intradetrusor BOTOX is contraindicated in patients with UTI or with urinary retention or PVR &gt;200 mL who do not routinely perform CIC; treat only patients willing and able to initiate catheterization if needed.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The guideline advises caution with a baseline PVR above the BTX trial exclusion thresholds, which varied from 100 to 200 mL, and calls for PVR measurement if symptoms fail to improve or worsen.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> The US label directs PVR assessment within two weeks for patients not already catheterizing and periodically as medically appropriate through 12 weeks.

The [2024 AUA/SUFU guideline](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/nau.25532) offers tibial nerve stimulation without a device-specific recommendation; the [eCoin](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf20/P200036D.pdf) and [Revi](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/DEN220073.pdf) pivotal efficacy studies were single-arm. In a sham-controlled Intibia trial of adults with UUI, 86/137 active participants (62.8%) versus 28/67 sham participants (41.8%) exceeded the OABq-SF symptom-bother minimum clinically important difference at three months (p = 0.0046); the primary UUI-episode responder analysis was reserved for a separate report.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Antimuscarinic Cognitive-Risk Considerations

In the [Malcher 2022 case-control study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35686842/), greater combined OAB-anticholinergic exposure was associated with dementia (aOR 1.07 at 1–90 defined daily doses, 1.29 at 91–365, and 1.48 at &gt;365).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> In Danish registry data, anticholinergic bladder drug use was associated with more dementia diagnoses than nonuse (IRR 1.44, 95% CI 1.40–1.48), but fewer than mirabegron use in a separate new-user comparison (IRR 0.82, 95% CI 0.74–0.92); residual confounding remains possible.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> The [AUA/SUFU guideline](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/nau.25532) recommends discussing possible cognitive risk with patients taking or prescribed antimuscarinics. The [pharmacology hub](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/storage-oab/anticholinergics) holds agent-specific details.

| Consideration | Agents | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Lower expected CNS penetration, not proven dementia-safe** | **Trospium** (quaternary amine); **Fesoterodine** | Trospium poorly crosses the BBB; the guideline names fesoterodine, darifenacin and trospium as theoretically lower-risk on permeability grounds; observational dementia signals differ across cohorts. Neither agent is established as universally risk-free. |
| **Other agent-specific evidence** | Darifenacin, tolterodine ER, solifenacin | CNS penetration, short-term cognitive outcomes and observational dementia associations differ. |
| **Greater concern** | **Oxybutynin**, especially chronic oral use | Higher CNS penetration and observational dementia signals warrant particular caution; route and cumulative exposure matter. |

### Stepwise Escalation (Optional / When Patient Prefers a Graduated Approach)

The 2024 guideline eliminates mandatory step therapy, but many patients still prefer a graduated approach:

1. **Behavioral therapies:** bladder training, fluid, caffeine and alcohol modification, urgency suppression, [weight loss](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/weight-loss-incontinence)
2. **Non-invasive therapies:** [PFMT](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/pelvic-floor-pt) ± biofeedback, TTNS, transvaginal electrical stimulation
3. **Pharmacotherapy:** choose a [β3 agonist](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/storage-oab/beta3-agonists) or [antimuscarinic](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/storage-oab/anticholinergics) by adverse-effect profile and preference; after inadequate response, switch medication within or between classes, or combine different classes.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
4. **Minimally invasive:** [BTX 100 U](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/intradetrusor-botox)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>, [SNM](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/sacral-neuromodulation), [iTNM](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/implantable-tibial-nerve-stimulation), or [PTNS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/percutaneous-tibial-nerve-stimulation)
5. **[Augmentation cystoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/bladder-augmentation)**
6. **[Urinary diversion](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion)** or indwelling catheter
