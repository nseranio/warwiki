# Male Stress Urinary Incontinence

Male stress urinary incontinence (SUI) most commonly results from sphincteric insufficiency following radical prostatectomy, radiation therapy, TURP, or trauma to the external urethral sphincter. Treatment selection depends on incontinence severity (pad count, pad weight), prior radiation, urethral health, patient dexterity, and preference. Options range from conservative (pelvic floor rehabilitation, penile compression) to surgical (slings, adjustable devices, AUS).

---

## Decision Framework

**PFMT is the universally recommended first-line therapy** for post-prostatectomy incontinence (PPI) across all major guidelines (EAU, AUA / SUFU, BAUS, ICI, CUA); network meta-analysis ranks **electrical stimulation + biofeedback + PFMT** highest for objective and patient-reported outcomes (Zhao 2025). Surgery should generally be deferred until **≥ 12 months** post-prostatectomy to allow spontaneous recovery, with urodynamics and cystoscopy before any surgical intervention to confirm stress-only incontinence and exclude bladder-neck contracture or stricture.

The **AUS (AMS-800) remains the gold standard** for moderate-to-severe SUI (AUSCO 2025: 94% &gt; 50% pad-weight reduction, 60% zero pad use at 12 months) — but reintervention-free survival is **71% / 57% / 40% at 2 / 5 / 10 years** (Lenfant 2025, n = 8,475). The **MASTER RCT (n = 380, Abrams 2021)** established that the male transobturator sling is **noninferior to AUS at 12 months** for primary continence — making fixed slings (AdVance / AdVance XP) a reasonable first-line surgical option for mild-to-moderate non-radiated SUI. **ATOMS** has emerged as a versatile adjustable option and a proven second-line device after failed fixed slings (Queissert 2021). **Prior radiation consistently predicts worse outcomes** across all sling types and shifts the decision toward AUS. Unlike female SUI, **urethral bulking agents have a very limited evidence base in men** (Toia 2019: 8 studies, dry rates 0–83%) and are reserved for highly selected patients.

### Treatment Selection by Clinical Scenario

| Clinical Scenario | First-Line Surgical Option | Alternative(s) | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mild SUI (1–2 PPD), no prior radiation** | [Fixed sling — AdVance / AdVance XP](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/advance-sling) | [ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling) (adjustable); [ProACT](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/proact-balloons) | — |
| **Moderate SUI (3–5 PPD), no prior radiation** | [AUS (AMS-800)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) preferred | [ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling) or [AdVance XP](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/advance-sling) (Sacco 2021: AUS 94.3% vs sling 68.6% at 12 mo) | Fixed sling alone in radiation history |
| **Severe SUI (&gt; 5 PPD)** | [AUS (AMS-800)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) | — | Fixed sling; bulking agents |
| **Mild-moderate SUI, poor surgical candidate or declines implant** | [ProACT periurethral balloons](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/proact-balloons) (60% dry / 82% improved) | [Urethral bulking](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/urethral-bulking-agents) (niche role; limited male data) | — |
| **SUI with prior pelvic radiation** | [AUS (AMS-800)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) preferred | [ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling) (62.5% vs 87.9% non-radiated continence) | Fixed sling — radiation is a strong failure predictor |
| **Recurrent SUI after failed fixed sling** | [ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling) (Queissert 2021: 76.1% social continence at 42.5 mo after failed AdVance) | [AUS (AMS-800)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) | Repeat fixed sling |
| **Recurrent SUI after failed AUS** | AUS revision / replacement; salvage with **tandem cuff** | Larger-cuff revision (urethral atrophy is the most common nonmechanical failure — 56.5% of revisions; Bentellis 2021) | — |
| **Concomitant SUI + erectile dysfunction** | Simultaneous [AUS + penile prosthesis](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction) (synchronous or staged) | [Sling + penile prosthesis](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/male-urethral-slings) | — |
| **Mixed incontinence (SUI + urgency / OAB)** | Optimize OAB component first via the [OAB / UUI pathway](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/oab-uui/oab-uui-database) (anticholinergic / β-3 / botulinum toxin / SNM) → reassess SUI | If pure SUI persists after OAB control → standard surgical pathway | — |
| **End-stage urethra after recurrent AUS erosion, accepts chronic SPT** | [Perineal permanent urethral ligation (PUL)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/permanent-urethral-ligation) | [Bladder-neck closure](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/outlet-continence/bladder-neck-closure) + [continent catheterizable channel](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/catheterizable-channels) | Repeat cuff placement in a non-salvageable urethra |
| **Devastated outlet, bladder or SPT pathway also unacceptable** | [Bladder-neck closure](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/outlet-continence/bladder-neck-closure) + [continent catheterizable channel](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/catheterizable-channels) | [Urinary diversion](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion) | Continued failed reconstruction |

### Sling / Adjustable Device Sub-Comparison

| Feature | Fixed Sling ([AdVance XP](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/advance-sling)) | Adjustable ([ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling)) | Adjustable ([Argus](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/argus-sling)) | [ProACT Balloons](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/proact-balloons) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mechanism** | Sphincter repositioning | Cushion compression + adjustable | Suburethral compression + adjustable | Periurethral balloon compression |
| **Best indication** | Mild-moderate, non-radiated | Mild-moderate; second-line after failed sling | All severities | Mild-moderate, non-radiated |
| **Dryness rate** | ~60–84% | 68% (Angulo 2019 meta) / 80% (Doiron 2019) | 72% (Bochove-Overgaauw) | 60% (Larson 2019 meta) |
| **Postop adjustability** | No | **Yes (scrotal port)** | **Yes (suprapubic regulator)** | **Yes (scrotal port)** |
| **Explant rate** | Low | 5% | 55% (mostly Clavien I–II) | 24% |
| **Prior-radiation tolerance** | Poor | Reduced (62.5% vs 87.9%) | Limited data | Limited data |
| **Use after failed sling** | Not recommended | **Yes — proven second-line** | Limited data | Limited data |

### AUS vs Sling Snapshot

- **MASTER RCT** (Abrams 2021, n = 380): male transobturator sling **noninferior to AUS** at 12 mo (87.0% vs 84.2% incontinence rates). Sling had fewer serious adverse events (6 vs 13).
- **AUSCO trial** (Kaufman 2025, n = 115, 17 sites): 94% &gt; 50% pad-weight reduction, 60% zero pad use, 7.8% 12-month revision rate.
- **Sacco 2021 propensity match** (moderate PPI): AUS 94.3% vs fixed sling 68.6% continence at 12 mo — AUS clearly superior in moderate disease.
- **Geretto 2023 propensity match** (AUS vs ATOMS): AUS dryness 44.9% vs 22.5%, but more high-grade complications and reinterventions; **5-year device survival favors ATOMS** (82% vs 67%).
- **Lenfant 2025** national database (n = 8,475): AUS reintervention-free 71% / 57% / 40% at 2 / 5 / 10 yr — durability is the principal long-term concern.

### Stepwise Escalation Ladder

1. **Conservative therapy** — [PFMT](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/pelvic-floor-pt) ± biofeedback ± [electrical stimulation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/pelvic-floor-electrical-stimulation); lifestyle modifications; minimum 6–12 months
2. **Containment** — [penile clamp](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/penile-clamp), [condom catheter](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/condom-catheters), pads (combination strategy preferred)
3. **[Urethral bulking](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/urethral-bulking-agents)** — niche role, mild SUI in poor surgical candidates
4. **Fixed male sling** ([AdVance / AdVance XP](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/advance-sling); [Virtue](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/virtue-sling)) — mild-to-moderate SUI without radiation
5. **Periurethral balloons** ([ProACT](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/proact-balloons)) or **adjustable sling** ([ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling), [Argus](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/argus-sling), [REMEEX](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/remeex-mrs)) — alternative to fixed sling or as second-line after failed fixed sling
6. **[AUS (AMS-800)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter)** — moderate-to-severe SUI, irradiated patients, or after failed sling
7. **AUS revision / tandem cuff** — for failed AUS (urethral atrophy or mechanical failure)
8. **[Perineal permanent urethral ligation (PUL)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/permanent-urethral-ligation)** — end-stage urethra after failed / eroded AUS when chronic SPT drainage is acceptable
9. **[Bladder-neck closure](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/outlet-continence/bladder-neck-closure) / urinary diversion** — last resort when PUL is unsuitable or the bladder / drainage plan also requires reconstruction

For **mixed incontinence with an OAB / urgency component**, optimize the OAB pathway first (see [OAB & UUI database](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/oab-uui/oab-uui-database) — anticholinergics / β-3 / botulinum toxin / SNM) and then reassess SUI severity. Pharmacotherapy is **not effective for pure male SUI** and is not part of this ladder.
