# Male Stress Urinary Incontinence

Male stress urinary incontinence (SUI) most commonly results from sphincteric insufficiency following radical prostatectomy, radiation therapy, TURP, or trauma to the external urethral sphincter. Treatment selection depends on incontinence severity (pad count, pad weight), prior radiation, urethral health, patient dexterity, and preference. Options range from conservative (pelvic floor rehabilitation, penile compression) to surgical (slings, adjustable devices, AUS).

---

## Decision Framework

**Pelvic-floor muscle training (PFMT)** is an early conservative option after prostate treatment, with benefits for the pace of recovery; the incremental value of electrical stimulation is less certain and should not be treated as a required component.[1,2] Surgery is commonly considered after recovery has had time to occur, but 12 months is not an absolute minimum: the AUA/GURS/SUFU guideline allows surgery from six months for bothersome incontinence that is not improving despite conservative treatment, and recommends offering it at one year. For SUI surgery after prostate treatment, AUA/GURS/SUFU recommends cystourethroscopy (Expert Opinion) to identify stricture, bladder-neck contracture and bladder pathology that can change or abort the operation; it permits urodynamics when this may clarify diagnosis or counseling (Conditional, Grade C).[1] For men with urinary incontinence, EAU 2026 strongly recommends a validated symptom score, bladder diary, pad-weight test and post-void residual measurement and weakly recommends invasive urodynamics when invasive treatment is considered.[2]

AUA/GURS/SUFU 2024 recommends discussing AUS for mild-to-severe SUI (Strong), describes it as the most predictable and reliable option, and recommends counseling that effectiveness likely declines over time and reoperation is common.[1] In the single-arm AUSCO trial, 91/97 (94%) achieved ≥50% pad-weight reduction and 61/101 (60%) reported zero pad use at 12 months.[4] In a French first-implant cohort of 8,475 men, reintervention-free survival was 71%, 57% and 40% at 2, 5 and 10 years, respectively (Lenfant 2025).[5] In the MASTER RCT (n = 380), a fixed male sling met its prespecified 12-month noninferiority margin against AUS on a strict any-leakage endpoint, but 87.0% versus 84.2% still reported some incontinence, and several secondary symptom and satisfaction outcomes favored AUS.[3] At 24 months the sling was still noninferior, and further surgery was needed in 11% of sling versus 2% of AUS recipients.[13] Fixed slings remain an option for selected mild-to-moderate non-radiated SUI; severe leakage and prior radiation are associated with poorer sling results.[1,2] A 2025 systematic review of 62 cohorts (5,696 men after prostate surgery) graded the associations of irradiation, greater pad use and greater 24-hour urine loss with fixed-sling failure as high certainty, and those of prior urethral stricture, previous incontinence therapy, detrusor overactivity, higher MSIGS score and an unfavorable repositioning test as moderate certainty.[16] ATOMS is an adjustable option, including after failed fixed slings in observational series, but head-to-head evidence has not established superior outcomes from adjustability.[2,6] Male urethral bulking has limited, heterogeneous evidence and a niche role; the guideline gives a strong recommendation to counsel that efficacy is low and cure is rare.[1,2]

### Treatment Selection by Clinical Scenario

The pad-per-day bands below are practical orientation, not universal guideline cutoffs: pad weight, degree of bother, radiation history, urethral condition and patient goals can move the choice.[1,2] Percentages from different cohorts should not be read as direct head-to-head device rankings.

| Clinical Scenario | First-Line Surgical Option | Alternative(s) | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mild SUI (1–2 PPD), no prior radiation** | Discuss [fixed sling — AdVance / AdVance XP](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/advance-sling) or [AUS (AMS-800)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) (AUA Moderate and Strong recommendations, respectively)[1] | [ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling) (adjustable); [ProACT](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/proact-balloons) | — |
| **Moderate SUI (3–4 PPD), no prior radiation** | [AUS (AMS-800)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) preferred | [ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling) or [AdVance XP](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/advance-sling) (Sacco matched cohort, in which the sling was a TiLOOP Male retrourethral transobturator sling: 0–1 PPD at 12 mo, AUS 94.3% vs sling 68.6%)[7] | Fixed sling alone in radiation history |

| **Mild-moderate SUI, unsuitable for or declines major surgery** | [Containment options](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/absorbent-products); discuss limited-evidence [urethral bulking](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/urethral-bulking-agents) if appropriate | [ProACT periurethral balloons](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/proact-balloons) only if the patient accepts an implanted device and its adjustment/revision risks, without prior pelvic radiation[1,2] | Presenting ProACT as a non-implant or nonsurgical option |
| **SUI with prior pelvic radiation** | [AUS (AMS-800)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) preferred; counsel that radiation is associated with higher AUS erosion and explantation risk.[1,2] | [ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling) (Doiron multicenter series: continence 62.5% after prior radiation vs 87.9% without)[10] | Fixed sling — radiation is a strong failure predictor |
| **Recurrent SUI after failed fixed sling** | Reassess severity, urethral health and cause, then recommend [AUS (AMS-800)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) (AUA/GURS/SUFU 2024, moderate recommendation)[1] | [ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling) in selected patients (Queissert retrospective cohort: 76.1% social continence at mean 42.5 mo after failed AdVance)[6] | Automatic repeat fixed sling |
| **Recurrent SUI after failed AUS** | Evaluate mechanical failure, erosion, tissue/urethral status and bladder function, then discuss AUS revision or replacement (AUA/GURS/SUFU 2024, strong recommendation)[1] | Cuff or component strategies, including selected tandem-cuff salvage, depend on the failure mechanism; see the [AUS page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) | Assuming every recurrence is urethral atrophy |
| **Concomitant SUI + erectile dysfunction** | [AUS + penile prosthesis](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction), concomitant or staged (conditional recommendation; counsel that combined surgery lengthens the operation and may carry a higher reoperation risk)[1] | [Sling + penile prosthesis](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/male-urethral-slings) | — |
| **Mixed incontinence (SUI + urgency / OAB)** | Characterize the stress and urgency contributions; address bothersome urgency via the [OAB / UUI pathway](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/oab-uui/oab-uui-database) while planning SUI treatment | Reassess both symptom components and patient priorities after each intervention | Assuming urgency must resolve completely before SUI treatment |
| **End-stage urethra after recurrent AUS erosion, accepts chronic SPT** | [Perineal permanent urethral ligation (PUL)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/permanent-urethral-ligation) | [Bladder-neck closure](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/outlet-continence/bladder-neck-closure) + [continent catheterizable channel](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/catheterizable-channels) | Repeat cuff placement in a non-salvageable urethra |
| **Devastated outlet, bladder or SPT pathway also unacceptable** | [Bladder-neck closure](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/outlet-continence/bladder-neck-closure) + [continent catheterizable channel](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/catheterizable-channels) | [Urinary diversion](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion) | Continued failed reconstruction |

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mechanism** | Sphincter repositioning | Cushion compression + adjustable | Suburethral compression + adjustable | Periurethral balloon compression |
| **Best indication** | Selected mild-moderate, non-radiated | Selected cases, including observational salvage after failed sling | Selected cases; limited heterogeneous data | Selected non-radiated cases; high revision burden |
| **Reported continence or success (definitions and follow-up vary)** | ~62% cure pooled across male-sling cohorts, range 34–91% (guideline review; cure definitions vary)[1] | 68% using 0–1 safety pad/day in observational meta-analysis (mean ATOMS follow-up 20.8 mo)[8]; 80% study-defined continence at median 9 mo in a 160-man cohort[10] | 72% composite success (cured or improved; 68/95) at median 27 mo, not a dry-rate estimate[11] | 60.2% study-defined dryness in 19 studies (1,264 men; mean follow-up 3.6 years)[12]; 55.1% using 0–1 pads/day in an updated 18-study synthesis (1,570 men; mean follow-up 34.7 months)[17] |
| **Postop adjustability** | No | **Yes (scrotal port)** | **Yes (silicone washer tensioning)**[15] | **Yes (scrotal port)** |
| **Explant rate** | Low in selected cohorts; erosion is exceedingly rare[1] | ~5% in the Angulo ATOMS-vs-ProACT meta-analysis[8] | Model- and study-dependent; counsel on erosion, pain and explant risk | ~24% in the same meta-analysis[8]; 27% all-cause (range 7–55%) in the guideline review[1] |
| **Prior-radiation tolerance** | Reduced; radiation accelerates failure across sling types[1] | Reduced (62.5% vs 87.9%)[10] | Limited data | Limited data |
| **Use after failed sling** | Selected repeat cases require individual assessment | Reported in observational series; not proven superior to AUS | Limited data | Limited data |

### AUS vs Sling Snapshot

- **MASTER RCT** (Abrams 2021, n = 380): male sling met the 12-month noninferiority criterion (87.0% versus 84.2% remained incontinent under a strict any-leakage definition; continence-success risk difference −3.6 percentage points, sling minus AUS, 95% CI −11.6 to 4.6, within the noninferiority margin; the abstract prints the same difference with a positive sign); the ICIQ-UI SF mean difference at 12 months was 1.4 (95% CI 0.2 to 2.6) in the 2021 article versus 1.30 (95% CI 0.11 to 2.49, p = 0.032) in the full HTA report — the same comparison, both favoring AUS.[3,14] In the 2021 article, 6 sling and 13 AUS recipients had serious adverse events; the full HTA report counts 8 of 180 sling and 13 of 175 AUS recipients (15 AUS events because one man had three), a difference that was uncertain.[14] At 24 months, further surgery occurred in 20 sling (11%) versus 4 AUS (2%) recipients; the ICIQ-UI SF mean difference was 1.4 (95% CI 0.2 to 2.6) favoring AUS.[13]
- **AUSCO trial** (Kaufman, 115 implanted at 17 sites): in this single-arm study, 91/97 (94%) achieved ≥50% pad-weight reduction and 61/101 (60%) reported zero pad use at 12 months; 9/115 (7.8%) underwent device revision (mechanical malfunction 3, erosion 2, none for infection) and 15% had a serious adverse event.[4]
- **Sacco 2021 propensity match** (moderate PPI): AUS 94.3% versus fixed sling 68.6% using 0–1 pad/day at 12 mo in 35 matched men per arm; observational selection and outcome definitions limit a universal superiority claim.[7]
- **Geretto 2023 propensity match** (AUS versus ATOMS, retrospective, 49 per arm): AUS dryness 44.9% versus 22.5%, but more high-grade complications and reinterventions; Estimated 60-month freedom from explantation was 82% with ATOMS versus 67% with AUS (p = 0.03); freedom from any reintervention was 67% versus 53% (p = 0.11).[9]
- **Lenfant 2025** French first-implant national database (n = 8,475): AUS reintervention-free survival 71% / 57% / 40% at 2 / 5 / 10 yr; this endpoint includes removal or replacement, not only mechanical device failure.[5]

### Treatment Options and Salvage

- **Conservative therapy.** [PFMT](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/pelvic-floor-pt) and appropriate behavioral measures; biofeedback or [electrical stimulation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/pelvic-floor-electrical-stimulation) are selective adjuncts, not requirements. Reassess recovery and bother over time; surgery may be considered before 12 months in selected non-improving cases.[1,2]
- **Containment.** [penile clamp](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/penile-clamp), [condom catheter](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/condom-catheters), pads (combination strategy preferred)
- **[AUS (AMS-800)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter).** Mild-to-severe SUI; preferred for irradiated patients and after failed sling
- **Fixed male sling** ([AdVance / AdVance XP](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/advance-sling); [Virtue](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/virtue-sling): mild-to-moderate SUI without radiation
- **Periurethral balloons** ([ProACT](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/proact-balloons)) or adjustable sling ([ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling), [Argus](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/argus-sling), [REMEEX](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/remeex-mrs): selected alternatives with device-specific evidence, adjustment and explantation trade-offs; the AUA/GURS/SUFU guideline limits adjustable balloons to non-radiated men (conditional recommendation) and EAU advises against ProACT after pelvic radiation.[1,2]
- **[Urethral bulking](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/urethral-bulking-agents).** Niche role, mild SUI in poor surgical candidates
- **AUS reassessment / individualized revision.** Identify erosion, infection, mechanical failure, tissue/urethral changes and bladder dysfunction before selecting a salvage method
- **[Perineal permanent urethral ligation (PUL)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/permanent-urethral-ligation).** End-stage urethra after failed / eroded AUS when chronic SPT drainage is acceptable
- **[Bladder-neck closure](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/outlet-continence/bladder-neck-closure) / urinary diversion.** Last resort when PUL is unsuitable or the bladder / drainage plan also requires reconstruction

For mixed incontinence with an OAB / urgency component, assess both components and treat bothersome urgency through the [OAB & UUI database](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/oab-uui/oab-uui-database) while planning SUI management; reassess after each step. OAB pharmacotherapy targets urgency, not pure sphincteric male SUI.
