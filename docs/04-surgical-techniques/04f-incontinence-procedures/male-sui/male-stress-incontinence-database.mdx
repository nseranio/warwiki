# Male Stress Urinary Incontinence

Male stress urinary incontinence (SUI) most commonly results from sphincteric insufficiency following radical prostatectomy, radiation therapy, TURP, or trauma to the external urethral sphincter. Treatment selection depends on incontinence severity (pad count, pad weight), prior radiation, urethral health, patient dexterity, and preference. Options range from conservative (pelvic floor rehabilitation, penile compression) to surgical (slings, adjustable devices, AUS).

---

## Decision Framework

**Pelvic-floor muscle training (PFMT)** is an early conservative option after prostate treatment, with benefits for the pace of recovery; the incremental value of electrical stimulation is less certain and should not be treated as a required component.[1,2] Surgery is commonly considered after recovery has had time to occur, but **12 months is not an absolute minimum**: the AUA/GURS/SUFU guideline allows surgery from six months for bothersome incontinence that is not improving despite conservative treatment, and recommends offering it at one year. Preoperative cystourethroscopy is recommended; urodynamics can be used when the diagnosis or management choice remains uncertain rather than routinely required for every patient.[1]

The **AUS (AMS-800)** is a standard option for moderate-to-severe SUI (AUSCO: 94% [91/97] achieved ≥50% pad-weight reduction and 60% [61/101] reported zero pad use at 12 months in a single-arm trial), with later reintervention a material trade-off (Lenfant 2025: 71% / 57% / 40% reintervention-free survival at 2 / 5 / 10 years in a French first-implant cohort of 8,475).[4,5] In the **MASTER RCT** (n = 380), a fixed male sling met its prespecified 12-month noninferiority margin against AUS on a **strict any-leakage endpoint**, but **87.0% vs 84.2% still reported some incontinence**, and several secondary symptom/satisfaction outcomes favored AUS.[3] Fixed slings remain an option for selected mild-to-moderate non-radiated SUI; severe leakage and prior radiation predict poorer sling results.[1,2] **ATOMS** is an adjustable option, including after failed fixed slings in observational series, but head-to-head evidence has not established superior outcomes from adjustability.[2,6] Male urethral bulking has limited, heterogeneous evidence and a niche role.[2]

### Treatment Selection by Clinical Scenario

The pad-per-day bands below are practical orientation, **not universal guideline cutoffs**: pad weight, degree of bother, radiation history, urethral condition and patient goals can move the choice.[1,2] Percentages from different cohorts should not be read as direct head-to-head device rankings.

|---|---|---|---|
| **Mild SUI (1–2 PPD), no prior radiation** | [Fixed sling — AdVance / AdVance XP](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/advance-sling) | [ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling) (adjustable); [ProACT](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/proact-balloons) | — |
| **Moderate SUI (3–5 PPD), no prior radiation** | [AUS (AMS-800)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) preferred | [ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling) or [AdVance XP](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/advance-sling) (Sacco matched cohort, in which the sling was a TiLOOP Male retrourethral transobturator sling: 0–1 PPD at 12 mo, AUS 94.3% vs sling 68.6%)[7] | Fixed sling alone in radiation history |
| **Severe SUI (&gt; 5 PPD)** | [AUS (AMS-800)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) | — | Fixed sling; bulking agents |
| **Mild-moderate SUI, unsuitable for or declines major surgery** | [Containment options](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/absorbent-products); discuss limited-evidence [urethral bulking](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/urethral-bulking-agents) if appropriate | [ProACT periurethral balloons](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/proact-balloons) only if the patient accepts an implanted device and its adjustment/revision risks, without prior pelvic radiation[1,2] | Presenting ProACT as a non-implant or nonsurgical option |
| **SUI with prior pelvic radiation** | [AUS (AMS-800)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) preferred | [ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling) (Doiron multicenter series: continence 62.5% after prior radiation vs 87.9% without)[10] | Fixed sling — radiation is a strong failure predictor |

| **Recurrent SUI after failed AUS** | Evaluate mechanical failure, erosion, tissue/urethral status and bladder function before choosing revision or replacement | Cuff or component strategies, including selected tandem-cuff salvage, depend on the failure mechanism; see the [AUS page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) | Assuming every recurrence is urethral atrophy |
| **Concomitant SUI + erectile dysfunction** | Simultaneous [AUS + penile prosthesis](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction) (synchronous or staged) | [Sling + penile prosthesis](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/male-urethral-slings) | — |
| **Mixed incontinence (SUI + urgency / OAB)** | Characterize the stress and urgency contributions; address bothersome urgency via the [OAB / UUI pathway](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/oab-uui/oab-uui-database) while planning SUI treatment | Reassess both symptom components and patient priorities after each intervention | Assuming urgency must resolve completely before SUI treatment |
| **End-stage urethra after recurrent AUS erosion, accepts chronic SPT** | [Perineal permanent urethral ligation (PUL)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/permanent-urethral-ligation) | [Bladder-neck closure](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/outlet-continence/bladder-neck-closure) + [continent catheterizable channel](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/catheterizable-channels) | Repeat cuff placement in a non-salvageable urethra |
| **Devastated outlet, bladder or SPT pathway also unacceptable** | [Bladder-neck closure](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/outlet-continence/bladder-neck-closure) + [continent catheterizable channel](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/catheterizable-channels) | [Urinary diversion](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion) | Continued failed reconstruction |

### Sling / Adjustable Device Sub-Comparison

| Feature | Fixed Sling ([AdVance XP](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/advance-sling)) | Adjustable ([ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling)) | Adjustable ([Argus](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/argus-sling)) | [ProACT Balloons](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/proact-balloons) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mechanism** | Sphincter repositioning | Cushion compression + adjustable | Suburethral compression + adjustable | Periurethral balloon compression |
| **Best indication** | Selected mild-moderate, non-radiated | Selected cases, including observational salvage after failed sling | Selected cases; limited heterogeneous data | Selected non-radiated cases; high revision burden |
| **Dryness rate** | ~60–84% | 68% (Angulo 2019 meta)[8] / 80% (Doiron 2019)[10] | 72% (Bochove-Overgaauw)[11] | 60% (Larson 2019 meta)[12] |
| **Postop adjustability** | No | **Yes (scrotal port)** | **Yes (suprapubic regulator)** | **Yes (scrotal port)** |
| **Explant rate** | Low in selected cohorts | ~5% in the Angulo ATOMS-vs-ProACT meta-analysis[8] | Model- and study-dependent; counsel on erosion, pain and explant risk | ~24% in the same meta-analysis[8] |
| **Prior-radiation tolerance** | Poor | Reduced (62.5% vs 87.9%)[10] | Limited data | Limited data |
| **Use after failed sling** | Selected repeat cases require individual assessment | Reported in observational series; not proven superior to AUS | Limited data | Limited data |

### AUS vs Sling Snapshot

- **MASTER RCT** (Abrams 2021, n = 380): male sling met the 12-month **noninferiority** criterion (87.0% vs 84.2% remained incontinent under a strict any-leakage definition); secondary symptoms/satisfaction often favored AUS. Six sling recipients versus 11 AUS recipients experienced serious adverse events (13 AUS events because one person had three); the between-group rate difference was uncertain.[3]
- **AUSCO trial** (Kaufman, 115 implanted at 17 sites): in this single-arm study, 91/97 (94%) achieved ≥50% pad-weight reduction and 61/101 (60%) reported zero pad use at 12 months; 9/115 (7.8%) underwent device revision (mechanical malfunction 3, erosion 2, none for infection) and 15% had a serious adverse event.[4]
- **Sacco 2021 propensity match** (moderate PPI): AUS 94.3% vs fixed sling 68.6% using 0–1 pad/day at 12 mo in 35 matched men per arm; observational selection and outcome definitions limit a universal superiority claim.[7]
- **Geretto 2023 propensity match** (AUS vs ATOMS, retrospective, 49 per arm): AUS dryness 44.9% vs 22.5%, but more high-grade complications and reinterventions; **60-month device survival favors ATOMS** (82% vs 67%).[9]
- **Lenfant 2025** French first-implant national database (n = 8,475): AUS reintervention-free survival 71% / 57% / 40% at 2 / 5 / 10 yr; this endpoint includes removal or replacement, not only mechanical device failure.[5]

### Stepwise Escalation Ladder

1. **Conservative therapy** — [PFMT](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/pelvic-floor-pt) and appropriate behavioral measures; biofeedback or [electrical stimulation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/pelvic-floor-electrical-stimulation) are selective adjuncts, not requirements. Reassess recovery and bother over time; surgery may be considered before 12 months in selected non-improving cases.[1,2]
2. **Containment** — [penile clamp](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/penile-clamp), [condom catheter](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/condom-catheters), pads (combination strategy preferred)
3. **[Urethral bulking](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/urethral-bulking-agents)** — niche role, mild SUI in poor surgical candidates
4. **Fixed male sling** ([AdVance / AdVance XP](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/advance-sling); [Virtue](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/virtue-sling)) — mild-to-moderate SUI without radiation
5. **Periurethral balloons** ([ProACT](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/proact-balloons)) or **adjustable sling** ([ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling), [Argus](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/argus-sling), [REMEEX](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/remeex-mrs)) — selected alternatives with device-specific evidence, adjustment and explantation trade-offs; ProACT is not an option after pelvic radiation under EAU guidance.[2]
6. **[AUS (AMS-800)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter)** — moderate-to-severe SUI, irradiated patients, or after failed sling
7. **AUS reassessment / individualized revision** — identify erosion, infection, mechanical failure, tissue/urethral changes and bladder dysfunction before selecting a salvage method
8. **[Perineal permanent urethral ligation (PUL)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/permanent-urethral-ligation)** — end-stage urethra after failed / eroded AUS when chronic SPT drainage is acceptable
9. **[Bladder-neck closure](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/outlet-continence/bladder-neck-closure) / urinary diversion** — last resort when PUL is unsuitable or the bladder / drainage plan also requires reconstruction

For **mixed incontinence with an OAB / urgency component**, assess both components and treat bothersome urgency through the [OAB & UUI database](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/oab-uui/oab-uui-database) while planning SUI management; reassess after each step. OAB pharmacotherapy targets urgency, not pure sphincteric male SUI.
