# Male Stress Urinary Incontinence

Male stress urinary incontinence (SUI) most commonly results from sphincteric insufficiency following radical prostatectomy, radiation therapy, TURP, or trauma to the external urethral sphincter. Treatment selection depends on incontinence severity (pad count, pad weight), prior radiation, urethral health, patient dexterity, and preference. For men with SUI after prostate treatment and prior urethral reconstruction, AUA/GURS/SUFU says AUS may be offered with counseling that complication rates are higher (Conditional, Grade C); the guideline considers slings ineffective after most posterior or anterior urethral reconstructions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Options range from conservative (pelvic floor rehabilitation, penile compression) to surgical (slings, adjustable devices, AUS).

---

## Decision Framework

**Pelvic-floor muscle training (PFMT)** is an early conservative option after prostate treatment, with benefits for the pace of recovery; the incremental value of electrical stimulation is less certain and it is not required.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Surgery is commonly considered after recovery has had time to occur, but 12 months is not an absolute minimum: the AUA/GURS/SUFU guideline allows surgery from six months for bothersome incontinence that is not improving despite conservative treatment and recommends offering it at one year. For SUI surgery after prostate treatment, AUA/GURS/SUFU recommends cystourethroscopy (Expert Opinion) to identify stricture, bladder-neck contracture and bladder pathology that can change or abort the operation; it permits urodynamics when this may clarify diagnosis or counseling (Conditional, Grade C).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Treat symptomatic vesicourethral anastomotic stenosis or bladder-neck contracture before continence surgery (Clinical Principle); after VUAS treatment, repeat cystourethroscopy at least 4–6 weeks later to document improvement and stability.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> For men with urinary incontinence, EAU 2026 strongly recommends a validated symptom score, bladder diary, pad-weight test and post-void residual measurement and weakly recommends invasive urodynamics when invasive treatment is considered.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

AUA/GURS/SUFU 2024 recommends discussing AUS for mild-to-severe SUI (Strong), describes it as the most predictable and reliable option, and recommends counseling that effectiveness likely declines over time and reoperation is common.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Before implantation, confirm that the patient has the physical and cognitive ability to operate the AUS pump (Clinical Principle).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In the single-arm AUSCO trial, 91/97 (94%) achieved ≥50% pad-weight reduction and 61/101 (60%) reported zero pad use at 12 months after device activation.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> In a French first-implant cohort of 8,475 men, reintervention-free survival was 71%, 57% and 40% at 2, 5 and 10 years, respectively (Lenfant 2025).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> In the MASTER RCT (n = 380), a fixed male sling met its prespecified 12-month noninferiority margin against AUS on a strict any-leakage endpoint, but 87.0% versus 84.2% still reported some incontinence, and several secondary symptom and satisfaction outcomes favored AUS.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> At 24 months, the trial's secondary continence analysis met noninferiority under the strict no-leakage definition (21/140 sling versus 22/148 AUS) but not under the less-strict definition (44/140 versus 51/148).<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> Further surgery occurred in 20 sling (11%) versus 4 AUS (2%) recipients.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> Fixed slings remain an option for selected mild-to-moderate non-radiated SUI; severe leakage and prior radiation are associated with poorer sling results.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> EAU also advises counseling that prior transurethral surgery may worsen nonadjustable sling outcomes (Weak).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> A 2025 systematic review of 62 cohorts (5,696 men after prostate surgery) graded the associations of irradiation, greater pad use and greater 24-hour urine loss with fixed-sling failure as high certainty, and those of prior urethral stricture, previous incontinence therapy, detrusor overactivity, higher MSIGS score and an unfavorable repositioning test as moderate certainty.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> ATOMS is an adjustable option, including after failed fixed slings in observational series, but head-to-head evidence has not established superior outcomes from adjustability.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Male urethral bulking has limited, heterogeneous evidence and a niche role; the guideline gives a strong recommendation to counsel that efficacy is low and cure is rare.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Treatment Selection by Clinical Scenario

The pad-per-day bands below are practical orientation; pad weight, degree of bother, radiation history, urethral condition and patient goals can change the choice.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Device percentages from different cohorts lack direct head-to-head comparison. EAU recommends offering AUS or ProACT implantation only in expert centres (Weak).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### AUS vs Sling Snapshot

- **MASTER RCT** (Abrams 2021, n = 380): male sling met the 12-month noninferiority criterion (87.0% versus 84.2% remained incontinent under a strict any-leakage definition; continence-success risk difference −3.6 percentage points, sling minus AUS, 95% CI −11.6 to 4.6, within the noninferiority margin; the abstract prints the same difference with a positive sign); the ICIQ-UI SF mean difference at 12 months was 1.4 (95% CI 0.2 to 2.6) in the 2021 article versus 1.30 (95% CI 0.11 to 2.49, p = 0.032) in the full HTA report — the same comparison, both favoring AUS.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[14]](#ref14)</sup> In the 2021 article, 6 sling and 13 AUS recipients had serious adverse events; the full HTA report counts 8 of 180 sling and 13 of 175 AUS recipients (15 AUS events because one man had three), a difference that was uncertain.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> At 24 months, further surgery occurred in 20 sling (11%) versus 4 AUS (2%) recipients; the ICIQ-UI SF mean difference was 1.4 (95% CI 0.2 to 2.6) favoring AUS.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **AUSCO trial** (Kaufman, 115 implanted at 17 sites): in this single-arm study, 91/97 (94%) achieved ≥50% pad-weight reduction and 61/101 (60%) reported zero pad use at 12 months after device activation; 9/115 (7.8%) underwent device revision (mechanical malfunction 3, erosion 2, none for infection) and 15% had a serious adverse event.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Sacco 2021 propensity match** (moderate PPI): AUS 94.3% versus fixed sling 68.6% using 0–1 pad/day at 12 mo in 35 matched men per arm; observational selection and outcome definitions limit a universal superiority claim.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Geretto 2023 propensity match** (AUS versus ATOMS, retrospective, 49 per arm): AUS dryness 44.9% versus 22.5%, but more high-grade complications and reinterventions; Estimated 60-month freedom from explantation was 82% with ATOMS versus 67% with AUS (p = 0.03); freedom from any reintervention was 67% versus 53% (p = 0.11).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Lenfant 2025** French first-implant national database (n = 8,475): AUS reintervention-free survival 71% / 57% / 40% at 2 / 5 / 10 yr; this endpoint includes removal or replacement, not only mechanical device failure.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### Treatment Options and Salvage

- **Conservative therapy.** [PFMT](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/pelvic-floor-pt) and appropriate behavioral measures; biofeedback or [electrical stimulation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/pelvic-floor-electrical-stimulation) are selective adjuncts, not requirements. Reassess recovery and bother over time; for bothersome SUI after prostate treatment that is not improving despite conservative therapy, surgery may be offered as early as six months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Containment.** [penile clamp](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/penile-clamp), [condom catheter](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/condom-catheters), pads (combination strategy preferred)
- **[AUS (AMS-800)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter).** Mild-to-severe SUI; preferred for irradiated patients and after failed sling
- **Fixed male sling** ([AdVance / AdVance XP](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/advance-sling); [Virtue](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/virtue-sling): mild-to-moderate SUI without radiation
- **Periurethral balloons** ([ProACT](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/proact-balloons)) or adjustable sling ([ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling), [Argus](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/argus-sling), [REMEEX](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/remeex-mrs)): selected alternatives with device-specific evidence, adjustment and explantation trade-offs; the AUA/GURS/SUFU guideline limits adjustable balloons to non-radiated men (Conditional, Grade C) and EAU advises against ProACT after pelvic radiation (Weak).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The FDA ProACT physician IFU indicates the device for adult men with intrinsic-sphincter-deficiency SUI lasting at least 12 months after radical prostatectomy or TURP despite conservative therapy; contraindications include incontinence due to detrusor overactivity, reduced bladder compliance, and postvoid residual &gt;100 mL.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> In the FDA pivotal cohort, bladder or urethral perforation occurred during implantation in 19/123 men (15.4%).<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **[Urethral bulking](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/urethral-bulking-agents).** Niche role, mild SUI in poor surgical candidates
- **AUS reassessment / individualized revision.** Identify erosion, infection, mechanical failure, tissue/urethral changes and bladder dysfunction before selecting a salvage method
- **[Perineal permanent urethral ligation (PUL)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/permanent-urethral-ligation).** End-stage urethra after failed / eroded AUS when chronic SPT drainage is acceptable
- **[Bladder-neck closure](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/outlet-continence/bladder-neck-closure) / urinary diversion.** Last resort when PUL is unsuitable or the bladder / drainage plan also requires reconstruction

For mixed incontinence with an OAB / urgency component, assess both components and treat bothersome urgency through the [OAB & UUI database](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/oab-uui/oab-uui-database) while planning SUI management; reassess after each step. OAB pharmacotherapy targets urgency, not pure sphincteric male SUI.
