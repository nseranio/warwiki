# REMEEX — Male Readjustable Sling (MRS)

The **Remeex / MRS** (Neomedic; first described Sousa-Escandón 2004) is an early adjustable male sling. A suprapubic mechanical regulator (**varitensor**) changes tension through an external manipulator. The initial postoperative adjustment and later readjustment differ. After the external manipulator is detached, a later adjustment requires a minor procedure to reconnect it.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

For positioning versus other male slings, see [Male Urethral Slings — chooser](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/male-urethral-slings).

> **US regulatory status:** The original Male Remeex System received FDA 510(k) clearance in 2006 for male stress urinary incontinence; that record does not establish clearance for the current Remeex System II.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> Reported use includes Europe and parts of Asia. [ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling) is another adjustable option. Only indirect cohort comparisons between the two are available.

---

## Device design

- Monofilament polypropylene mesh sling beneath the bulbar urethra.
- Two monofilament traction threads pass retropubically (analogous to a retropubic female TVT trajectory) to the suprapubic region.
- **Varitensor.** Permanent subcutaneous regulator over the rectus fascia approximately 2 cm above the pubis. The manipulator is used for initial tensioning and is then detached. Later readjustment requires reconnection in a minor procedure.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- Retropubic passage carries a bladder-perforation risk like that of retropubic female MUS, and bowel injury has been reported.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The current generation is **Remeex System II**.

---

## Surgical technique

1. Lithotomy; perineal incision; expose bulbar urethra.
2. Polypropylene mesh sling positioned beneath the bulbar urethra.
3. Pass the retropubic needles from perineum to suprapubic region; after both passes, perform cystourethroscopy to confirm urethrovesical integrity before drawing the sling traction threads through.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
4. **Varitensor** implanted subcutaneously over the rectus fascia approximately 2 cm above the pubis.
5. Implant the sling without final tension. The manufacturer's product description places the first tension adjustment 24–48 hours postoperatively, with the patient standing and performing the maneuvers that provoke leakage. Intraoperative positioning or preliminary tensioning described in published variants is a separate step.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
6. **Cystoscopy** to rule out bladder perforation from retropubic needle passage.

After the first adjustment, confirm spontaneous voiding and check postvoid residual before detaching the external manipulator.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Later readjustment follows a minor procedure to reconnect it. Access, anesthesia and timing follow the model IFU. The manufacturer describes readjustment as possible at any time, and the 2007 European series performed it under local anesthesia.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## REMEEX vs ATOMS — indirect meta-analysis

The Angulo 2021 meta-analysis pooled 29 observational studies without a direct comparison. Results favored ATOMS for dryness, improvement and explantation. Complications did not differ significantly. Selection, follow-up and reporting differed between cohorts:<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

| Outcome | ATOMS | REMEEX | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dryness | **69.3%** | 53.4% | 0.008 |
| Improvement | **90.8%** | 80.2% | 0.007 |
| Complication | 18.9% | 35.8% | 0.096 (NS) |
| Explantation | **5.5%** | 13.9% | **0.027** |

Differences persisted when restricted to current-generation devices (ATOMS SSP versus Remeex System II).

## Complications

- **Intraoperative bladder perforation:** five of 51 men (9.8%) in Sousa-Escandón 2007, described as uneventful by the authors; the retropubic route is analogous to retropubic female MUS.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Bowel injury.** Chiu 2022 reported 2/18 (11.1%, Clavien-Dindo IIIb) during sling implantation. Both slings were removed immediately after signs of bowel injury appeared, within two days of implantation. One patient then recovered with antibiotics and conservative care; the other required operative repair of two sigmoid and one ileal perforation. All patients had undergone transperitoneal RP with the peritoneum not routinely closed, and the authors proposed that bowel migration into the retropubic space contributed. This small single-center rate is not a general incidence estimate.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Postoperative urinary retention occurred in 3/18 men (16.7%) in Chiu's cohort; urethral sounding or temporary catheterization was required.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Urethral erosion approximately 2%; varitensor infection approximately 4% requiring removal; perineal hematoma 5.9%.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In Chiu's separate 18-man cohort, 3/18 (16.7%) had mesh infection treated with antibiotics without mesh removal.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Pooled explantation was 13.9%, compared with 5.5% for ATOMS (p = 0.027).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Predictors of failure

- **Prior pelvic radiotherapy.** Kim 2016 reported an odds ratio of 8.4 for failure (64 men, 2 centers), and concluded that MRS suits radiation-naive men with mild incontinence.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Higher baseline severity.** OR 1.41 per pad (Kim 2016).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Severity-related complete-dry gradient.** Non-severe 57.9% versus severe 23.1% (Altan 2017, p = 0.029), a different adjustable perineal sling. Total success did not differ significantly (78.9% vs 57.7%, p = 0.135).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

---

## Salvage

When REMEEX fails, AUS is the most common salvage: 12 of 18 surgical failures in Kim 2016 received subsequent AUS.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Current positioning

Remeex offers postoperative adjustment through a suprapubic regulator. In a 2026 prospective two-center cohort, 55% of 66 men analyzed after exclusions for death or loss to follow-up achieved social continence (0–1 pad/day) at a mean 48 months; 89% underwent at least one postoperative adjustment and 6% had explantation; the authors conclude that midterm effectiveness is satisfactory in selected men with mild-to-moderate post-prostatectomy SUI.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> The 2026 EAU guideline rates the evidence for adjustable male slings as limited (LE 3), reports no evidence that adjustability adds benefit (LE 3), and makes no recommendation for adjustable slings; it recommends offering AUS to men with moderate-to-severe SUI (Strong).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> Counseling covers retropubic implantation risks, adjustment procedures and alternatives. The observational ATOMS comparison informs counseling; it did not assess treatment sequence.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
