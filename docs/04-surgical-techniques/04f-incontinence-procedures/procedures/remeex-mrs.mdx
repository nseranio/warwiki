# REMEEX — Male Readjustable Sling (MRS)

The **Remeex / MRS** (Neomedic; first described Sousa-Escandón 2004) is the earliest adjustable male sling and the only one with **truly non-invasive percutaneous re-tensioning** — the suprapubic mechanical regulator (**varitensor**) is activated by an **external screwdriver-like manipulator** under local anesthesia, with no incision or port access required.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For positioning vs other male slings, see [Male Urethral Slings — chooser](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/male-urethral-slings).

> **Not FDA-approved in the US**; remains in use primarily in Europe and parts of Asia. Largely superseded by [ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling) in centers with access to both.

---

## Device design

- Monofilament polypropylene mesh sling beneath the bulbar urethra.
- Two monofilament traction threads pass **retropubically** (analogous to a retropubic female TVT trajectory) to the suprapubic region.
- **Varitensor** — permanent subcutaneous regulator over the rectus fascia ~ 2 cm above the pubis. Tension is increased or decreased percutaneously with the external manipulator at any time post-op.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- The retropubic passage carries the same **bladder-perforation risk** seen with retropubic female MUS — and rare bowel injury.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The current generation is **Remeex System II**.

---

## Surgical technique

1. Lithotomy; perineal incision; expose bulbar urethra.
2. Polypropylene mesh sling positioned beneath the bulbar urethra.
3. Traction threads passed **retropubically** using needles from perineum to suprapubic region.
4. **Varitensor** implanted subcutaneously over the rectus fascia ~ 2 cm above the pubis.
5. **Intraoperative regulation** — sling tension adjusted with the external manipulator while performing a cough stress test or filling cystometry to achieve continence without obstruction.
6. **Cystoscopy** to rule out bladder perforation from retropubic needle passage.

Postoperative readjustment is performed under local anesthesia by accessing the varitensor percutaneously with the external manipulator. This can be done at any time — early postoperatively or months to years later.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Outcomes

| Study | N | Follow-up | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sousa-Escandón 2004 (pilot)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 6 | 18 mo | 83% continent; all satisfied |
| Sousa-Escandón 2007 (European MC)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | 51 | 32 mo | **64.7% cured / 19.6% improved (84.3% combined)**; 86% required ≥ 1 readjustment 1–4 mo postop |
| Kim 2016 (2-center)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | 64 | 46 mo | **71.9% success**; pads 3.42 → 0.84 (p &lt; 0.001) |
| Márquez-Sánchez 2021<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | 47 | 33.5 mo | **89.4% objective effectiveness across all severity strata** (mild 85.7% / moderate 90.9% / severe 88.9%); 34% needed readjustment; device survival 95.7% |
| Chiu 2022<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | 18 | — | 61.1% success; **2 / 18 (11.1%) bowel injury** during retropubic passage |
| Altan 2017<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | 45 | 48 mo | 66.7% total success (37.8% completely dry); non-severe 57.9% vs severe 23.1% complete dry (p = 0.029) |
| **Angulo 2021 SR/meta vs ATOMS**<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | 1,919 (29 studies) | — | **REMEEX dryness 53.4% / improvement 80.2%** — significantly worse than ATOMS on every endpoint |

## REMEEX vs ATOMS — head-to-head meta

The **Angulo 2021 SR/meta** is the definitive comparison:<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

| Outcome | ATOMS | REMEEX | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dryness | **69.3%** | 53.4% | 0.008 |
| Improvement | **90.8%** | 80.2% | 0.007 |
| Complication | 18.9% | 35.8% | 0.096 (NS) |
| Explantation | **5.5%** | 13.9% | **0.027** |

Differences persisted when restricted to current-generation devices (ATOMS SSP vs Remeex System II).

---

## Complications

- **Intraoperative bladder perforation 9.8%** (Sousa-Escandón 2007 EMC) — analogous to retropubic female MUS; usually managed conservatively.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Bowel injury** — 11.1% in Chiu 2022 (2/18) during retropubic needle passage; rare but serious.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Urethral erosion ~ 2%; varitensor infection ~ 4% requiring removal; perineal hematoma 5.9%.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Pooled explantation 13.9% — meaningfully higher than ATOMS (5.5%, p = 0.027).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

---

## Predictors of failure

- **Prior pelvic radiotherapy** — Kim 2016 OR **8.4** for failure, among the highest reported for any male sling.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Higher baseline severity** — OR 1.41 per pad (Kim 2016).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Severity-related complete-dry gradient** — non-severe 57.9% vs severe 23.1% (Altan 2017, p = 0.029) despite comparable overall success.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

---

## Salvage

When REMEEX fails, **AUS is the most common salvage** — 12 / 18 surgical failures in Kim 2016 received subsequent AUS.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Current positioning

Remeex occupies a **niche role** today. Its conceptual advantage — the only truly non-invasive percutaneous re-tensioning mechanism — is offset by retropubic-passage risks (bladder / bowel injury) absent from transobturator devices, lower dryness rates, and higher explantation than ATOMS. May be considered in settings where ATOMS is unavailable.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
