# REMEEX — Male Readjustable Sling (MRS)

The **Remeex / MRS** (Neomedic; first described Sousa-Escandón 2004) is an early adjustable male sling. A suprapubic mechanical regulator (**varitensor**) changes tension through an external manipulator. The initial postoperative adjustment and later readjustment are different procedures: after the external manipulator is detached, a later adjustment requires a minor procedure to reconnect it.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

For positioning versus other male slings, see [Male Urethral Slings — chooser](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/male-urethral-slings).

> **Not FDA-approved in the US**; reported use includes Europe and parts of Asia. [ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling) is another adjustable option; indirect cohort comparisons do not establish a universal preferred sequence.

---

## Device design

- Monofilament polypropylene mesh sling beneath the bulbar urethra.
- Two monofilament traction threads pass retropubically (analogous to a retropubic female TVT trajectory) to the suprapubic region.
- **Varitensor.** Permanent subcutaneous regulator over the rectus fascia approximately 2 cm above the pubis. The manipulator is used for initial tensioning and detached afterward; later readjustment requires reconnection in a minor procedure.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- The retropubic passage carries the same bladder-perforation risk seen with retropubic female MUS, and rare bowel injury.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The current generation is **Remeex System II**.

---

## Surgical technique

1. Lithotomy; perineal incision; expose bulbar urethra.
2. Polypropylene mesh sling positioned beneath the bulbar urethra.
3. Traction threads passed retropubically using needles from perineum to suprapubic region.
4. **Varitensor** implanted subcutaneously over the rectus fascia approximately 2 cm above the pubis.
5. Implant initially without final tension; the manufacturer's product description places the first tension adjustment 24–48 hours postoperatively, with the patient standing and straining. Intraoperative positioning or preliminary tensioning in published variants should not be conflated with this initial adjustment.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
6. **Cystoscopy** to rule out bladder perforation from retropubic needle passage.

After the first adjustment, the external manipulator is detached. Subsequent readjustment may be performed later after a minor procedure to reconnect it; follow the applicable model IFU for access, anesthesia and timing.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Outcomes

| Study | N | Follow-up | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sousa-Escandón 2004 (pilot)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | 6 | 18 mo | 83% continent; all satisfied |
| Sousa-Escandón 2007 (European MC)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | 51 | 32 mo | **64.7% cured / 19.6% improved (84.3% combined)**; 86% required ≥ 1 readjustment 1–4 mo postop |
| Kim 2016 (2-center)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | 64 | 46 mo | **71.9% success**; pads 3.42 → 0.84 (p &lt; 0.001) |
| Márquez-Sánchez 2021<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | 47 | 33.5 mo | **89.4% objective effectiveness across all severity strata** (mild 85.7% / moderate 90.9% / severe 88.9%); 34% needed readjustment; device survival 95.7% |
| Chiu 2022<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | 18 | 18.4 mo mean (14 median) | 61.1% success; **2 / 18 (11.1%) bowel injury** during retropubic passage in this single-center cohort after transperitoneal RP |
| Altan 2017<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | 45 | 48 mo | 66.7% total success (37.8% completely dry); non-severe 57.9% vs severe 23.1% complete dry (p = 0.029) |
| **Angulo 2021 SR/meta vs ATOMS**<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | 1,919 (29 studies) | — | **REMEEX dryness 53.4% / improvement 80.2%** — indirect observational estimates, with substantial heterogeneity |

## REMEEX vs ATOMS — indirect meta-analysis

The Angulo 2021 meta-analysis pooled 29 observational studies without a direct comparison. Results favored ATOMS for dryness, improvement and explantation, but complications did not differ significantly. Selection, follow-up and reporting differed between cohorts; this does not establish superiority for every patient:<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

| Outcome | ATOMS | REMEEX | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dryness | **69.3%** | 53.4% | 0.008 |
| Improvement | **90.8%** | 80.2% | 0.007 |
| Complication | 18.9% | 35.8% | 0.096 (NS) |
| Explantation | **5.5%** | 13.9% | **0.027** |

Differences persisted when restricted to current-generation devices (ATOMS SSP versus Remeex System II).

## Complications

- **Intraoperative bladder perforation 9.8%** (Sousa-Escandón 2007 EMC); analogous to retropubic female MUS, usually managed conservatively.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Bowel injury.** Chiu 2022 reported 2/18 (11.1%) during retropubic needle passage, including one patient requiring bowel repair. All patients had undergone transperitoneal RP with peritoneum not routinely closed; authors proposed altered retropubic anatomy as a possible contributor. This small single-center rate is not a general device incidence estimate.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Urethral erosion approximately 2%; varitensor infection approximately 4% requiring removal; perineal hematoma 5.9%.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Pooled explantation 13.9% — meaningfully higher than ATOMS (5.5%, p = 0.027).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

---

## Predictors of failure

- **Prior pelvic radiotherapy.** Kim 2016 OR 8.4 for failure, among the highest reported for any male sling.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Higher baseline severity.** OR 1.41 per pad (Kim 2016).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Severity-related complete-dry gradient.** Non-severe 57.9% versus severe 23.1% (Altan 2017, p = 0.029) despite comparable overall success.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

---

## Salvage

When REMEEX fails, AUS is the most common salvage: 12 of 18 surgical failures in Kim 2016 received subsequent AUS.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Current positioning

Remeex offers postoperative adjustment through a suprapubic regulator. Discuss the retropubic implantation risks, adjustment procedures and alternatives. The observational comparison above can inform counseling, but should not be treated as proof of a universal ATOMS-first sequence.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
