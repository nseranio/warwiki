## How to Use This Hub

Neuromodulation is a family of treatments that share electrical modulation of bladder, bowel, pelvic-floor and afferent sensory circuits. The operations differ sharply in invasiveness, maintenance burden, reversibility and patient selection.

| Clinical problem | Main neuromodulation option |
|---|---|
| Urgency urinary incontinence / OAB | PTNS, implantable tibial stimulation or SNM according to preference and eligibility. The AUA/SUFU 2024 guideline recommends SNM, PTNS and/or intradetrusor botulinum toxin (a non-neuromodulation option) after inadequate response to or intolerable effects from pharmacotherapy or behavioral therapy (Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade A)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Non-obstructive urinary retention | After excluding obstruction and addressing conservative options, consider SNM. The EAU 2026 female LUTS guideline recommends offering SNM to women with underactive bladder refractory to conservative management (Strong).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Fecal incontinence | SNM is the implanted option; two sham-controlled randomized trials of PTNS (CONFIDeNT, NOTABLe) did not show a significant benefit over sham<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| Patient wants the least invasive option | PTNS / TTNS, accepting maintenance burden |
| Patient wants implanted therapy | SNM after a successful test phase, or an appropriately indicated implantable tibial device without an SNM-style test phase. Counsel that direct comparative efficacy and durability data for these implants are lacking.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |

## Key Distinctions

- **SNM** targets the S3 sacral nerve root with an implanted lead and pulse generator. It requires a test phase, device programming, and long-term device surveillance.
- **PNE** is not definitive treatment. It is a temporary screening procedure used to decide whether permanent SNM implantation is justified.
- **PTNS** stimulates the posterior tibial nerve, usually in the office, and avoids an implanted sacral device but requires induction and maintenance sessions.
- **Pudendal nerve stimulation** is an investigational peripheral target for refractory urgency-frequency, urgency incontinence and nonobstructive retention, not an established alternative to SNM. In a small single-blind, randomized crossover trial (n = 30) that implanted both an S3 lead and a pudendal lead and tested each for 7 days, 24 of 30 patients responded; overall symptom improvement was 63% with pudendal stimulation versus 46% with sacral stimulation (P = 0.02), and among the 24 responders, 79.2% chose the pudendal lead as superior.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> This is short-term, exploratory evidence and does not establish long-term benefit or a routine clinical pathway.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> For lead placement, FDA status and the separate pudendal-neuralgia literature, see [Pudendal Neuromodulation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/pudendal-neuromodulation).
- **Implantable tibial systems** stimulate near the ankle with distinct batteries or wearables and FDA indications; an individual implant's MRI and other device precautions must be checked separately. The AUA/SUFU 2024 guideline states that clinicians may offer minimally invasive therapies without requiring trials of behavioral, non-invasive or pharmacologic management (Expert Opinion). AUA/SUFU permits flexible sequencing.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Practical Counseling

Preoperative counseling asks whether the patient can accept the maintenance model. Some patients prefer PTNS office visits to an implant, and others prefer implanted SNM to repeated visits. A failed PTNS trial does not necessarily predict SNM failure. The target and stimulation strategy differ.

For device hardware, see the neuromodulation-device pages under [Tools / Biomaterials](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/neuromodulation-devices/ecoin), including eCoin, Axonics, and Medtronic InterStim profiles.
