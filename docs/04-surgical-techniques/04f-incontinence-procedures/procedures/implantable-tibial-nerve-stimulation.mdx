# Implantable Tibial Nerve Stimulation (iTNM)

Implantable tibial nerve stimulation (iTNM) targets the posterior tibial nerve through a lower-leg implant rather than repeated [office PTNS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/percutaneous-tibial-nerve-stimulation). The at-home maintenance model differs by device: eCoin stimulates automatically, Revi requires an external wearable, and Medtronic Altaviva is rechargeable. These devices avoid routine in-office PTNS induction/maintenance visits but require implantation, system-specific precautions and follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[18]](#ref18)[[19]](#ref19)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

This page is the canonical implantable tibial-nerve stimulation target for the OAB database. It is intentionally separate from PTNS because the procedure, durability, maintenance burden, and device counseling differ substantially. Mechanism-of-action and efficacy fundamentals shared with PTNS are not duplicated here — see the [PTNS page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/percutaneous-tibial-nerve-stimulation) for the underlying neuromodulation rationale and the SUmiT pivotal sham-controlled foundation.

---

## Rationale for Implantable Devices

The primary limitations of PTNS — the burden of weekly in-office visits, short-lived effects requiring indefinite maintenance, and limited insurance coverage — have driven the development of implantable alternatives. Real-world data show that **over 40% of PTNS maintenance patients discontinue therapy due to logistic reasons and physical strain** rather than treatment failure.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Implantable devices address these barriers by providing continuous or scheduled at-home stimulation through a permanent implant, with the added potential for dynamic, patient-centered programming.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## FDA-Authorized Devices

### eCoin® (Valencia Technologies)

The eCoin is a nickel-sized, coin-shaped, fully implanted, primary battery-powered device placed in the lower leg above the fascia over the tibial nerve. It delivers automated, pre-programmed, low-duty-cycle stimulation without requiring any external wearable for daily therapy.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- **Implantation:** Performed under local anesthesia in approximately 20 minutes; placed suprafascially over the tibial nerve in the lower leg and activated 4 weeks post-implantation.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Power source:** Internal primary (non-rechargeable) battery
- **Stimulation:** Automatic, intermittent low-duty cycle; no patient interaction required for daily therapy delivery<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **FDA status:** PMA-approved for UUI after intolerance of or inadequate response to more conservative treatments, or after a successful PTNS trial; the precise label matters even though the AUA guideline permits individualized selection.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

### Revi® (BlueWind Medical, formerly RENOVA™)

The Revi is a leadless, miniature, battery-free implant placed subfascially near the tibial nerve. It is powered wirelessly by an external, battery-operated ankle wearable that the patient controls, allowing individually tailored stimulation parameters.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

- **Implantation:** Subfascial placement near the tibial nerve; median operative time approximately 34 minutes in early studies<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Power source:** Battery-free implant; powered externally by a wearable ankle device
- **Stimulation:** Patient-controlled via the external wearable, enabling personalized therapy sessions at home<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **FDA status:** FDA-cleared Revi labeling is for urgency incontinence alone or with urinary urgency, without an explicit prior-conservative-treatment failure condition; clinician assessment and model contraindications still apply.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

### Altaviva™ (Medtronic, formerly investigational ITNM)

Altaviva is a rechargeable, implanted tibial stimulator. Its TITAN 2 trial appears below. The FDA **approved** Altaviva in September 2025 for UUI in patients who failed or could not tolerate more conservative treatments; it should not be described only as investigational or assigned Revi's broader label.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

### Protect PNS (Uro Medical Corp.) and Others

A percutaneous implantable pulse generator (pIPG) with integrated quadripolar electrodes has been studied in a pilot trial. It is implanted in the office under local anesthesia using a retrograde approach and activated via an external wireless transmitter. Early data show feasibility and safety, though larger studies are needed.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

A newer implantable tibial nerve stimulator has completed a first-in-human feasibility study (n=10), showing 80% responder rates at 13 weeks with no serious adverse events.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

## Clinical Evidence Summary

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **eCoin** | Feasibility<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | 46 | 12 mo | 65%; 26% dry | 1 SAE (wound care, resolved) |
| **eCoin** | Pivotal FDA ITT<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> | 133 | 48 wk of stimulation | 68% | Device-related harms require review of the FDA event tables; no cross-device rate inference |
| **eCoin** | Pivotal extension<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | 72 | 96 wk | 78%; 22% dry | No serious or unanticipated AEs |
| **Revi** | OASIS pivotal<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | 151 | 6 mo | 78% | No device / procedure-related SAEs |
| **Revi** | OASIS 2-year<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | 97 | 24 mo | 79% | No device / procedure-related SAEs; 97% satisfaction |

These rows are **different single-arm cohorts and endpoints**, not head-to-head device efficacy or safety comparisons. The 96-week eCoin and 36-month Revi follow-up rows involve fewer observed participants than the pivotal cohorts; a higher later percentage does not establish increasing efficacy.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

## Key Pivotal Trials

### OASIS Study (Revi)

The pivotal OASIS trial enrolled **151 women** with UUI. The primary endpoint — ≥50% reduction in UUI episodes at 6 months — was met by **78%** of participants. This response was durable through 3 years (79% at 36 months). Quality-of-life improvements were clinically significant across all OAB-q domains. Notably, **95% reported therapeutic satisfaction** and **82% reported feeling "much better" or "very much better"** at 3 years. No device- or procedure-related serious adverse events occurred through 36 months.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

### eCoin Pivotal Trial

The FDA pivotal study included **133 implanted/attempted-implant participants in the ITT population**, with **68%** achieving ≥50% reduction in UUI episodes after 48 weeks of stimulation. A smaller observed extension cohort reported **78%** responders at 96 weeks and **22%** dry; it should not be read as an improvement in the same complete denominator or as a controlled comparison. Medication-use and extension safety details require the full original report.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

### TITAN 2 (Medtronic ITNM)

This pivotal IDE study enrolled **126 subjects** implanted with the Medtronic leadless, rechargeable ITNM system. At 6 months, **59%** achieved ≥50% improvement in UUI episodes/day (primary endpoint met, p &lt; 0.001 vs performance goal). Twenty percent experienced adverse device effects through 12 months; one serious ADE resolved without sequelae.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

## Comparison with Sacral Neuromodulation (SNM)

A systematic review of separate iTNM and [SNM](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/sacral-neuromodulation) studies (20 studies, 1,766 patients) reported pooled UUI responder proportions of 71.3% and 71.8%, respectively. **These indirect single-arm proportions do not establish equivalent efficacy or comparable adverse-event risk**: selection, trial phase, follow-up and endpoint definitions differ. The studied tibial implants generally did not use an SNM-style temporary test phase.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

---

## Safety Profile

Safety and MRI conditions are **device-specific**; do not substitute PTNS or one implant's labeling for another:

- **eCoin:** The original FDA physician labeling contraindicates implantation in specified poor lower-leg surgical candidates (including open wound, local vascular disease/edema and local infection) and patients unable to operate the magnet controller if unwanted stimulation occurs. The implant is **MR conditional only under its labeled conditions**, including keeping it at least 20 cm outside the MRI bore at 1.5/3 T; the controller magnet is MR unsafe. Diathermy is prohibited. Confirm any later PMA supplement/label revision.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **Revi:** The manufacturer lists inability to operate the system, obstructive BPH or other lower urinary tract obstruction, metallic implants within about 20 cm (8 in) of the implant site, nerve damage affecting treatment, lower-leg wound-healing risks (open wounds, edema, venous or arterial disease) and pregnancy among contraindications. The implant has conditional MRI requirements; external wearable/programmer components are MR unsafe. Full current model labeling governs screening and scanning.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- **Altaviva:** FDA's original PMA label limits the indication to UUI after failure/intolerance of conservative therapies; its current implant/MRI manuals and recent PMA supplements must be checked before device-specific counseling.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

Observed study harms (different denominators and ascertainment):

- **eCoin:** Most adverse events relate to wound healing and infection at the implant site. In the pivotal trial, 16% experienced device-related events through 52 weeks, mostly minor.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The FDA labeling tabulates device- or procedure-related events, including exploratory procedure-related events, in 22% of 133 implanted patients (25 non-serious, 4 serious; infections were the most common serious category). No serious or unanticipated AEs were reported in the 2-year extension.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **Revi:** No device- or procedure-related serious adverse events through 3 years of follow-up in the OASIS study.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> The earlier 36-patient feasibility study reported implant site pain (14%), suspected infection (22%) and procedural wound complications (8%); one system was removed for inflammation.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Medtronic ITNM:** 20% adverse device effects through 12 months; one serious ADE that resolved.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

## Advantages Over PTNS

- **Eliminates in-office visit burden** — at-home therapy delivery<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Improved compliance** — removes the primary reason for PTNS discontinuation<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Longer observed follow-up** — selected eCoin and Revi cohorts report responses through 2–3 years; lack of a randomized PTNS comparator prevents concluding that implants eliminate wearing-off or are more durable<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Patient-controlled therapy** (Revi) — allows individualized stimulation parameters<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **No SNM-style temporary test phase in the studied implant pathways** — confirm the individual device's indication and preimplant evaluation; eCoin labeling also allows eligibility after a successful PTNS trial<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

---

## Guideline Positioning

The **AUA/SUFU 2024 guideline** lists implantable tibial nerve stimulation among the minimally invasive OAB therapies (Table 3), but its named recommendation for patients with inadequate response to or intolerance of drugs or behavioral therapy covers sacral neuromodulation, percutaneous tibial nerve stimulation and intradetrusor onabotulinumtoxinA rather than implantable devices. Clinicians may offer minimally invasive therapies, in the context of shared decision-making, **without requiring prior trials of behavioral, non-invasive or pharmacologic management**.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

---

## Limitations and Considerations

- All pivotal trials to date are **single-arm, open-label** studies without sham controls, though the underlying mechanism is supported by sham-controlled PTNS trials<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **No head-to-head randomized trials** comparing iTNM devices to each other, to SNM, or to onabotulinumtoxinA<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- Long-term data beyond 3 years remain limited
- The eCoin uses a **non-rechargeable primary battery**, raising questions about device longevity and potential need for replacement
- MRI compatibility varies by device and should be confirmed before implantation
- Cost-effectiveness data are still emerging
