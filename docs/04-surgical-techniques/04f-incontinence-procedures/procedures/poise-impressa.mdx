# Poise Impressa

**Poise Impressa** was marketed as a single-use, tampon-like, disposable intravaginal bladder-support device sold over the counter for the nonsurgical management of female stress urinary incontinence (SUI). It supports the bladder neck and urethra to increase urethral resistance during increases in intra-abdominal pressure (cough, sneeze, exercise), is sized using a starter kit (sizes 1–3), and is inserted by the patient at home; no provider fitting is required.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Poise states that Impressa products have been discontinued; the device specifications and trial results below describe a discontinued product.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

For the umbrella article on intravaginal continence devices and the broader pessary class, see [Continence Pessary](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/continence-pessary). For the female SUI ladder, see the [Female Stress Incontinence Database](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/female-sui/female-stress-incontinence-database). For women with SUI, the EAU guideline recommends supervised intensive pelvic floor muscle training for at least three months as first-line therapy; see [Pelvic Floor PT](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/pelvic-floor-pt).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

---

## How It Works

Impressa is inserted vaginally like a tampon. Once seated, it elevates and supports the bladder neck and proximal urethra, increasing urethral resistance to leak during stress maneuvers. It is not a pelvic-organ-prolapse pessary and does not provide vaginal-vault support.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Class** | Disposable intravaginal continence device (DICD) |
| **Sizes** | 1, 2, 3 — starter / sizing kit available |
| **Wear time** | Up to 12 hours total in a 24-hour period (Poise instructions)<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **Use** | **Single use** — discard after wear |
| **Distribution** | **Over the counter**; no prescription required<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Fitting** | Self-fit at home using the sizing kit; no in-office fitting needed<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |

---

## Indications

- **Female stress urinary incontinence.** Leakage on cough, sneeze, exertion.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Situational SUI.** Especially exercise- or activity-related leakage where on-demand control is desired.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Patients who prefer a non-pharmacologic, noninvasive option, or who wish to avoid or delay surgery.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Patients unlikely to adhere to behavioral or pelvic-floor-muscle-training programs.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

It was intended for SUI, not urgency or overflow leakage; Poise specifically excluded mixed urinary incontinence.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

---

## Contraindications and Precautions

Poise instructions say not to use Impressa under age 21; during pregnancy or possible pregnancy; within three months of childbirth or vaginal surgery; after unexpected vaginal bleeding within six months; with painful urination or vaginal infection symptoms; or with diagnosed severe vaginal atrophy. Do not wear it during menses or intercourse. Remove it and contact a doctor immediately for fever of 102°F with any listed systemic symptom because toxic shock syndrome is possible.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> Poise also advised removal and clinician consultation for blood spots, vaginal soreness or discomfort, infection symptoms, or increased leakage.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Poise also advised asking a doctor before use when intercourse or tampon insertion is painful, or a tampon-like device is difficult to retain; discomfort or no reduction in leakage after 14 days warrants evaluation.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> The Nekkanti trial exclusions listed below are research criteria, not the manufacturer's complete use restrictions.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

- Pregnancy
- Active urinary tract infection
- Postmenopausal bleeding (un-evaluated)
- Neurogenic bladder
- Urinary retention
- Prolapse
- Prior SUI surgery or prior treatment with a continence pessary or device

## Clinical Evidence

The Impressa-specific evidence base is limited; the contemporary expert reviews position it within the broader literature on intravaginal continence devices.

### Nekkanti 2022 RCT — Impressa vs continence pessary

A multicenter randomized trial compared Impressa (a disposable intravaginal continence device) with a fitted continence pessary at 4 weeks. Treatment success was based on the Patient Global Impression of Improvement. Fifty women were enrolled, 35 completed a fitting, and 22 completed 4-week follow-up:<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

| Endpoint | Impressa | Continence pessary | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Treatment success at 4 weeks | 75% (9/12) | 80% (8/10) | 1.0 |
| Improvement on questionnaire measures | Improved on all questionnaires | Improved, except lower sexual function scores at 4 weeks | — |
| Serious adverse events | 0 | 0 | — |
| Use of additional therapies over 6 mo | Higher | Lower | — |

The trial was stopped early for slow enrollment (50 of a planned 138 participants) and was underpowered. It cannot establish equivalence. Only 34% of enrolled women completed 6-month follow-up.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### Expert reviews

- **JAMA review (Lukacz 2017).** OTC vaginal-insert devices such as Impressa "may provide an alternative noninvasive treatment, although comparative data are lacking."<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **NEJM review (Wu 2021).** Impressa is described as a commercially available option, but "additional comparative effectiveness studies are needed."<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Cochrane overview (Todhunter-Brown 2022).** Mechanical devices including disposable intravaginal devices are within scope of conservative SUI therapy; certainty of benefit is modest.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Place in the SUI Treatment Algorithm

| Tier | Option |
|---|---|
| **First-line** | Pelvic floor muscle training (about half of women with stress-predominant UI are satisfied 1 year after starting) ± behavioral / lifestyle (weight loss, fluid / caffeine modulation)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Other nonsurgical options** | Fitted continence pessary or Uresta; Poise Impressa was a historical OTC option that has been discontinued.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Procedural options** | Urethral bulking, midurethral sling, autologous fascial PVS, or Burch colposuspension, selected through shared decision-making; bulking is less effective than the listed operations and repeat injections may be required.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |

Before discontinuation, Impressa was a nonsurgical vaginal-support option alongside fitted continence pessaries and PFMT. The 2024 CUA guideline recommends offering pessaries and other vaginal devices to suitable women with SUI (Weak recommendation; moderate-quality evidence).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> ACOG positions pessary-class therapy as appropriate for patients who wish to avoid surgery, are not likely to adhere to PFMT, or want immediate situational symptom control.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Comparison with Other Intravaginal Continence Devices

| Device | Reusable | Provider fit needed | OTC | Wear time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Poise Impressa (discontinued)** | No (single use) | No — patient self-fits | Historically OTC; no longer marketed<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | Up to 12 h total in 24 h<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **Uresta** | Yes (up to 12 mo)<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> | Patient self-selects and fits | **Yes — OTC** | Daily use; remove at bedtime<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Fitted continence pessary** (ring/dish with knob) | Yes | **Yes — provider fitting** | No | Long-term with periodic checks |

When sold, Impressa required no prescription or provider fitting, but repeated single-use purchases could cost more than reusable devices; it is now discontinued.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

---

## Limitations

- **Single-use cost** accumulates with regular use, disadvantageous versus reusable Uresta or fitted continence pessaries for patients with daily SUI.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **No large well-powered RCT** specifically against sham or no treatment; the available comparative trial was underpowered.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Symptomatic relief.** The device manages leakage only while in place and does not treat underlying pelvic-floor or urethral-support pathology.

---

## Key Principles

- Before discontinuation, Impressa was a single-use, OTC, self-fitted intravaginal continence device for female SUI, sized using a starter kit (sizes 1–3) and worn up to 12 hours total in a 24-hour period.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- Best suited for patients with situational or activity-related SUI, those preferring noninvasive options, or those unlikely to adhere to PFMT.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- The Nekkanti 2022 RCT found no significant difference in 4-week treatment success versus a fitted continence pessary (75% versus 80%, p = 1.0) but was underpowered (n = 50 of planned 138; 22 completed 4-week follow-up).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Impressa is a nonsurgical vaginal insert; fitted continence pessaries and PFMT are other nonsurgical options, while urethral bulking and surgery are procedural options.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> EAU recommends supervised intensive PFMT for at least three months as first-line SUI therapy (Strong).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Historical OTC access.** Impressa was sold without a prescription before it was discontinued.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Comparative effectiveness data remain limited; JAMA and NEJM reviews both call for larger trials.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Poise excluded urge and mixed urinary incontinence; Impressa was not indicated for overflow incontinence.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> The Nekkanti trial excluded pregnancy, active UTI, neurogenic bladder, urinary retention and prolapse.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
