# ProACT — Adjustable Continence Therapy (Periurethral Balloons)

The **ProACT (Adjustable Continence Therapy)** system uses paired periurethral balloons for male SUI. FDA approval covers adult men with intrinsic-sphincter-deficiency SUI lasting at least 12 months after radical prostatectomy or TURP despite conservative therapy.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> See the [device page](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/prosthetics/proact-balloons) for labeling, contraindications and handling. The indication follows the FDA label. Older systematic reviews describe earlier and broader use.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For positioning versus other minimally invasive male SUI options, see [Male Urethral Slings — chooser](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/male-urethral-slings).

> ProACT is a periurethral balloon system, not a sling. It acts by focal compression at the bladder neck. It does not reposition the urethra or compress the bulbar urethra from below.

---

## Device design and mechanism

Two silicone balloons placed bilaterally at the bladder neck augment urethral coaptation through titrated focal compression that stops short of circumferential occlusion. Each balloon connects via a conduit to a titanium port placed in the scrotum, allowing percutaneous volume adjustment under local anesthesia in clinic.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Surgical technique

- The FDA-posted physician IFU specifies general or spinal anesthesia, cystourethroscopy and fluoroscopic placement. TRUS-guided placement, which may use local anesthesia, has been reported in observational cohorts. It is a published variant and differs from the FDA-posted implantation sequence. Model-specific training and the supplied IFU apply.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- The IFU describes up to 1.5 mL initial fill per balloon, with no therapeutic adjustment before six weeks; thereafter, adjustments are considered about every four weeks, up to 1 mL per balloon per visit, and never more than 8.0 mL in either balloon. Only the specified 23-gauge needle is used for adjustments. For retention, the IFU describes deflating each balloon in 0.5 mL increments until voiding resumes.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- Perforation and retention rates vary by cohort and definition. The FDA-posted safety table records bladder or urethral perforation in 19/123 (15.4%) and urinary retention in 19/123 (15.4%) of implanted participants through 24 months. A TRUS-guided series reported lower rates: bladder perforation 2/79 (2.5%) and transient retention 1/79 (1.2%).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

## Efficacy

The systematic reviews below synthesize mainly observational cohorts. No randomized comparison is available.

| Study | Design / n | Follow-up | Key outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Larson 2019 meta**<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | SR/meta of 19 studies, n = 1,264 | Mean 3.6 yr | Pads/day **4.0 → 1.1**; **dry rate 60.2%**; dry-or-improved 81.9%; I-QOL +30.8 |
| **Tricard 2023 meta**<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | Updated SR/meta, 18 studies, n = 1,570 | Mean ~ 3 yr | **Social continence 53%** using 0–1 pads/day; this permits a safety pad |
| **Nash 2019 (FDA pivotal)**<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | Prospective; 68 of 123 implanted completed 4-yr follow-up | 4 yr | 24-h pad weight **293 → 73 g**; 1 procedure-related serious AE |
| **Finazzi Agrò 2019 multicenter**<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | National Italian, n = 240 | 24 mo (152 pts to 5 yr) | **29.6% dry / 37.5% improved** (67.1% combined); maintained at 5 yr |
| **Nestler 2019 long-term**<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | Single-center, n = 134 | Median 118 mo | **82.6% initial success**; 77.8% success after repeat implantation |

### Efficacy by severity

In the Gregori TRUS-guided series (62 patients completing adjustment), dry rates were 85% for mild, 63.6% for moderate and 33.3% for severe SUI.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The 4-year pivotal study reported pad-weight reductions across all severity levels.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> These are cohort findings and not eligibility cutoffs. AUA 2024 allows offering balloons across mild through severe SUI in non-radiated patients.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

---

## Complications and durability

Overall complication rate 22.5–31.2% across series.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> A 2025 systematic review of 1,607 patients catalogued 752 adverse events. The percentages below use the 752 events as the denominator:<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- **Mechanical or balloon failure:** 30%
- **Device migration or malposition:** 21%
- **Device erosion:** 16%

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Explantation rate | **24–26.5%**<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| Reoperation rate | **22.7–34%**<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Working device at 1 / 2 / 3 yr | **76% / 61% / 58%**<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |

Reintervention is a counseling issue. In Angulo 2019, working-device estimates for ATOMS were 92%, 85% and 81% at 1, 2 and 3 years. That was an indirect comparison of separate observational cohorts with differing follow-up.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

---

## Negative predictive factors

- **Prior pelvic radiotherapy** is the most consistent negative predictor; success or dryness was about 35–46% in irradiated patients and about 66–68% in non-irradiated patients, and urethral erosion was significantly more frequent after radiotherapy (p = 0.005 in Rouprêt 2011). Multiple reviews advise against use in irradiated patients.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Baseline severity.** Severe SUI was associated with a lower success rate in Ricard 2022 (p = 0.033). Severity-specific results vary between series and do not define an eligibility threshold.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

---

## Salvage role after failed sling

**Munier 2020** (n = 26 with persistent SUI after a prior sling failure): 66.7% continence and 96.3% improvement with ProACT, This retrospective two-center series supports feasibility as a salvage option when [AUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) is undesired or contraindicated.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

---

## Comparative evidence — ATOMS-vs-ProACT meta

The Angulo 2019 meta-analysis pooled 41 observational studies (3,059 patients), with no direct comparative trial. Pooled estimates favored ATOMS for several outcomes. Unequal follow-up, heterogeneity and selection differences limit causal interpretation. Overall complications did not differ significantly (17% versus 26%, p = 0.07):<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

| Endpoint | ATOMS | ProACT | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dryness | 68% | 55% | 0.01 |
| Improvement | 91% | 80% | 0.007 |
| Satisfaction | 87% | 56% | 0.002 |
| Explantation | 5% | 24% | &lt; 0.001 |
| Working device 3 yr | 81% | 58% | — |

## Current positioning

AUA/GURS/SUFU 2024 permits offering adjustable balloons to non-radiated men with mild through severe SUI after prostate treatment (conditional recommendation, Grade C). Counseling covers AUS and other alternatives, manual dexterity, adjustment visits and revision risk. After radiotherapy, the guideline favors AUS over balloons or male slings.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> In one series, 29.4% of ProACT patients ultimately required an AUS, so the balloons may be a step in the treatment sequence and not a final procedure.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
