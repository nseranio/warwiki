# Adjustable Continence Devices

Each adjustable male continence device now has its own canonical page. For the comparative chooser and decision matrix, see [Male Urethral Slings — chooser](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/male-urethral-slings).

Adjustability is attractive because post-prostatectomy SUI is not static: edema resolves, compression relaxes, radiation tissues change, and patient tolerance for obstruction varies. The tradeoff is that adjustable devices add device-specific failure modes — ports, washers, balloons, re-tensioning mechanisms, erosion, migration, and explantation.

The EAU guideline finds **limited evidence for adjustable male slings and no demonstrated benefit from adjustability over fixed slings**; it cautions that explantation may be more frequent on indirect comparisons. Selection below is a discussion aid, not a proven device ranking. See the [EAU male-incontinence section](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/management-of-non-neurogenic-male-luts/chapter/disease-management).

## Practical Positioning

| Scenario | Best fit from existing WARWIKI pages |
|---|---|
| Mild-to-moderate non-radiated SUI, residual sphincter function | Fixed sling or ATOMS depending severity and repositioning test |
| Moderate-to-severe SUI | AUS remains the reference standard; ATOMS is the main adjustable alternative |
| Failed AdVance / fixed sling | Consider AUS or an adjustable device such as ATOMS; observational salvage cohorts do not prove one is superior |
| Patient wants a minimally invasive adjustable option and is non-radiated | ProACT may be considered, with durability counseling |
| Prior pelvic radiation | AUS is usually favored; ATOMS is the main non-AUS adjustable option, with lower success than non-radiated cohorts |

## High-Yield Differences

- **ATOMS** is used in multiple non-US settings: transobturator, ventral compression and port adjustment. Comparative observational studies are subject to selection and device-generation differences.
- **Argus** has reported urethral erosion, pain and explantation in some cohorts; these do not establish a universal highest-risk ranking among devices.
- **REMEEX** permits suprapubic re-tensioning, while its retropubic passage entails bladder or bowel-injury risk.
- **ProACT** is not a sling. It is a periurethral balloon system at the bladder neck, best kept to carefully selected non-radiated mild-to-moderate cases.
