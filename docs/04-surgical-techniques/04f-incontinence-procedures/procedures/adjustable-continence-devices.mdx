# Adjustable Continence Devices

Each adjustable male continence device now has its own canonical page. For the comparative chooser and decision matrix, see [Male Urethral Slings — chooser](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/male-urethral-slings).

Adjustability is attractive because post-prostatectomy SUI is not static. Edema resolves, compression relaxes, irradiated tissues change, and patient tolerance for obstruction varies. Adjustable devices add device-specific failure modes: ports, washers, balloons, re-tensioning mechanisms, erosion, migration and explantation.

Comparative evidence is limited. In a multicenter cohort of 294 fixed and 176 adjustable male slings (not randomized; adjustable slings were used more often in irradiated and more severe patients), functional outcomes did not differ, and infection (2.3% vs 0%) and pain (11.3% vs 1.7%) were more frequent with adjustable slings.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The AUA/GURS/SUFU guideline (2024) recommends that clinicians discuss the AUS with men who have mild-to-severe SUI after prostate treatment (Strong Recommendation), discuss male slings for mild-to-moderate SUI (Moderate Recommendation), and may offer adjustable balloon devices to non-radiated men (Conditional Recommendation).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The selection notes below are a discussion aid and not a device ranking.

## Practical Positioning

| Scenario | Best fit from existing WARWIKI pages |
|---|---|
| Mild-to-moderate non-radiated SUI, residual sphincter function | Fixed sling or ATOMS depending severity and repositioning test |
| Moderate-to-severe SUI | AUS is the guideline-supported option; ATOMS is the main adjustable sling alternative |
| Failed AdVance / fixed sling | The AUA guideline recommends an AUS after sling failure (Moderate); ATOMS has been reported as a salvage option, and observational cohorts do not prove one is superior |
| Patient wants a minimally invasive adjustable option and is non-radiated | ProACT may be offered (AUA Conditional Recommendation), with durability counseling |
| Prior pelvic radiation | The AUA guideline recommends offering an AUS over a sling or adjustable balloon (Moderate); ATOMS results are lower than in non-radiated cohorts |

## High-Yield Differences

- **ATOMS** is used mainly outside the US. It is a transobturator device with ventral compression and port adjustment. Comparative observational studies are subject to selection and device-generation differences.
- **Argus** has reported urethral erosion, pain and explantation in some cohorts. These reports do not rank it as the highest-risk device.
- **REMEEX** permits suprapubic re-tensioning, and its retropubic passage carries a risk of bladder or bowel injury.
- **ProACT** is a periurethral balloon system at the bladder neck and not a sling. The AUA guideline allows it for non-radiated men with mild-to-severe SUI (Conditional Recommendation).
