# Adjustable Continence Devices

Adjustable continence devices are implantable systems that provide post-operatively titratable urethral resistance for male stress urinary incontinence. Unlike a [fixed male sling](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/male-urethral-slings) or the [artificial urinary sphincter (AUS)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter), these devices have a percutaneous port that allows non-operative volume adjustment in clinic. The category includes:

- **[ProACT](#proact)** — bilateral periurethral balloons (FDA-approved 2015)
- **[ATOMS](#atoms)** — submucosal cushion + scrotal port (the strongest second-line device after failed AdVance)
- **[Argus](#argus)** — silicone-foam suburethral cushion + readjustable suprapubic columns
- **[REMEEX](#remeex)** — readjustable suburethral sling with suprapubic mechanical regulator

The shared rationale across the category is that **urethral compliance and patient continence requirements drift over time** — particularly after radiation, atrophy, or weight change — and a device that can be tightened or loosened without re-operation has a durability advantage over a fixed system. None of these devices replaces the [AUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) as the gold standard for moderate-to-severe SUI, but they fill important gaps for poor AUS candidates, patients with limited dexterity, mild-to-moderate disease, and salvage after a failed fixed sling.

## ProACT

The **ProACT (Adjustable Continence Therapy)** system is a minimally invasive periurethral balloon device **FDA-approved in 2015** for male SUI, primarily post-prostatectomy. First introduced in 2000.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### Device design and mechanism

Two **silicone balloons placed bilaterally at the bladder neck** augment urethral coaptation through titrated focal compression — *not* circumferential occlusion. Each balloon connects via a conduit to a **titanium port placed in the scrotum**, allowing percutaneous volume adjustment under local anesthesia in clinic.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Surgical technique

- General or local anesthesia.
- Implantation is guided by **fluoroscopy, transrectal ultrasound (TRUS), or flexible cystoscopy**. TRUS-guided placement has shown comparable success and complication rates to fluoroscopic technique.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Balloons begin at low fill volume and are progressively titrated in clinic over multiple sessions to achieve continence without retention.
- Intraoperative bladder perforation occurs in ~2.5% of cases; transient retention in ~1.2%.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Efficacy

All evidence is observational — **no randomized controlled trials exist**.

| Study | Design / n | Follow-up | Key outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Larson 2019 meta**<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | SR/meta of 19 studies, n = 1,264 | Mean 3.6 yr | Pads/day **4.0 → 1.1**; **dry rate 60.2%**; dry-or-improved 81.9%; I-QOL +30.8 |
| **Tricard 2023 meta**<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | Updated SR/meta, 18 studies, n = 1,570 | Mean ~3 yr | **Dry rate 53%** by strict 0–1 PPD definition |
| **Nash 2019 (FDA pivotal)**<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | Prospective, n = 68 | 4 yr | 24-h pad weight **293 → 73 g**; 1 procedure-related serious AE |
| **Finazzi Agrò 2019 multicenter**<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | National Italian, n = 240 | 24 mo (152 pts to 5 yr) | **29.6% dry / 37.5% improved** (67.1% combined); maintained at 5 yr |
| **Nestler 2019 long-term**<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | Single-center, n = 134 | Median 118 mo | **82.6% initial success**; 77.8% success after repeat implantation |

### Efficacy by severity

Outcomes are stratified — **mild 85% dry / moderate 63.6% / severe 33.3%** in the Gregori TRUS-guided series.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Severe SUI is consistently associated with lower dryness across studies.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### Complications and durability

Overall complication rate **22.5–31.2%** across series.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> A 2025 SR of 1,607 patients catalogued 752 adverse events — most common categories:<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- **Mechanical / balloon failure** — 30%
- **Device migration / malposition** — 21%
- **Device erosion** — 16%

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Explantation rate | **24–26.5%**<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| Reoperation rate | **22.7–34%**<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Working device at 1 / 2 / 3 yr | **76% / 61% / 58%**<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |

Device longevity is the principal long-term concern. Working-device rates compare unfavorably to ATOMS (92% / 85% / 81% at 1 / 2 / 3 yr in the same Angulo 2019 meta).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

### Negative predictive factors

- **Prior pelvic radiotherapy** is the most consistent negative predictor — dryness ~35–46% irradiated vs ~66–68% non-irradiated, and significantly higher urethral-erosion rates (p = 0.005). Multiple reviews advise against use in irradiated patients.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Severe baseline incontinence** consistently predicts lower dryness.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

### Salvage role after failed sling

Munier 2020 — n = 26 with persistent SUI after a prior sling failure: **66.7% continence and 96.3% improvement** with ProACT, supporting feasibility as a salvage option when [AUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) is undesired or contraindicated.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

### Current positioning

ProACT is best suited for **non-irradiated men with mild-to-moderate post-prostatectomy SUI** who are not ideal AUS candidates (limited manual dexterity, declined implant) or who prefer a minimally invasive, adjustable, and reversible option.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Notably, **~29% of ProACT patients in one series ultimately required AUS** — counsel that it is a step on the ladder, not necessarily a definitive endpoint.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## ATOMS

The **ATOMS (Adjustable Transobturator Male System)** is a cushion-based adjustable suburethral device with a silicone-covered scrotal port (current SSP generation) for postop volume titration. The deep-dive — three device generations (IP / SP / SSP), full outcomes table including Esquinas 2019 SR/meta of 1,393 patients, Angulo 2020 long-term Iberian (86.7% sustained dry at 8 yr in initially-dry patients), Giammò 2023 device-survival data (87.9% at 60 mo), comparative AUS / ProACT data, salvage roles after failed AdVance and after failed initial ATOMS, and predictors of failure — lives on the [Male Urethral Slings — ATOMS section](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/male-urethral-slings#atoms--adjustable-transobturator-male-system).

Quick summary:

- **Mechanism:** adjustable ventral compression of the bulbar urethra against the urogenital diaphragm — distinct from AdVance repositioning.
- **Esquinas 2019 SR/meta (1,393 pts, 20 studies):** 67% pooled dry / 90% improved.
- **Angulo 2019 meta (ATOMS vs ProACT)**<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> — dryness 68% vs 55% (p = 0.01); satisfaction 87% vs 56%; explantation 5% vs 24%; working device at 1 / 2 / 3 yr **92% / 85% / 81%**.
- **Best second-line after failed AdVance** (Queissert 2021: 76.1% social continence at 42.5 mo).
- **Reduced — but still meaningful — efficacy with prior radiation** (Angulo 2023 multicenter of 223 irradiated patients: 42.6% dry, 84% satisfied).
- **Not currently FDA-approved in the US**; widely used in Europe / Canada / Australia / NZ.

## Argus

Suburethral adjustable sling with silicone-foam pad and readjustable suprapubic columns.

- **Bochove-Overgaauw 2011** — 72% success at 27 mo across all severity strata (92% mild / 67% moderate / 67% severe). Complication rate 55%, mostly Clavien I–II. Higher complication burden than ATOMS but uniquely usable in severe disease.

---

## REMEEX

Readjustable suburethral sling with a **suprapubic mechanical regulator** that allows postop tension adjustment via an external manipulator (the regulator is then removed once continence is stable).

- Limited multicenter data; mostly single-center European series. Most useful when postop tension adjustment is anticipated and the surgeon prefers a sling-form over a balloon.

## Cross-Device Comparison

| Feature | [ProACT](#proact) | [ATOMS](#atoms) | [Argus](#argus) | [REMEEX](#remeex) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | Periurethral balloon compression | Cushion compression + adjustable | Suburethral compression + adjustable | Suburethral sling + adjustable |
| Best indication | Mild–mod, non-radiated, AUS-averse | Mild–mod; **second-line after failed sling** | All severities | Postop adjustability anticipated |
| Adjustment route | Scrotal port | Scrotal port | Suprapubic columns | Suprapubic regulator (then removed) |
| Dryness | 53–60% (Larson / Tricard meta) | 68% (Angulo meta) / 80% (Doiron) | 72% (Bochove-Overgaauw) | Limited data |
| Working device 3 yr | 58% | 81% | Limited data | Limited data |
| Explant rate | 24–26.5% | 5% | 55% (mostly low-grade) | Limited data |
| Prior-radiation tolerance | **Poor** — avoid | Reduced (62.5% vs 87.9%) | Limited data | Limited data |
| Use after failed fixed sling | Salvage option (Munier 66.7%) | **Proven second-line** (Queissert 76.1%) | Limited data | Limited data |

## Where Adjustable Devices Sit in the Male SUI Pathway

Within the [Male SUI ladder](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/male-sui/male-stress-incontinence-database):

- After failed conservative therapy and below the [AUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) gold standard.
- ProACT and ATOMS are **alternatives** to a [fixed male sling](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/male-urethral-slings) in the mild-to-moderate non-radiated patient.
- ATOMS is the **best second-line device** after a failed AdVance.
- All four devices are **inferior to AUS** for moderate-to-severe disease — the AUSCO 2025 trial 94% &gt; 50% pad-weight reduction is the comparator that adjustable devices do not match.
- **Avoid in irradiated patients** for ProACT (most strongly evidenced negative predictor); ATOMS is reduced but not contraindicated.
