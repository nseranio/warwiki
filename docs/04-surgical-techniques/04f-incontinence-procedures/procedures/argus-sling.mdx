# Argus — Adjustable Suburethral Compression Sling

The **Argus sling** (Promedon, Córdoba, Argentina; first described Romano 2006) is an adjustable, compressive suburethral sling that exists in two variants: the Argus Classic (retropubic) and the Argus T (transobturator). Its defining feature is mechanical postoperative adjustability via radio-opaque silicone washers; re-tensioning requires a minor surgical procedure (unlike [ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling), which adjusts via percutaneous port).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> 

For positioning versus other male slings, see [Male Urethral Slings — chooser](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/male-urethral-slings). 

> Argus cohorts report meaningful erosion, pain and explantation risks with variation by center and route. Erosion is not unique to Argus, and indirect comparisons cannot establish that it has the highest complication rate of every contemporary sling.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> 

---

## Device design

- Radio-opaque silicone foam cushion pad (42 × 26 × 9 mm) for soft bulbar compression. 
- Pad attached to two silicone columns formed by multiple conical elements.
- Columns pass to the abdominal wall (Classic) or through the obturator foramina (Argus T).
- **Radio-opaque washers** at the exit points regulate and maintain tension; fluoroscopy can verify position during follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Argus Classic vs Argus T

The Argus T was introduced after the Classic to avoid retropubic-passage complications (bladder perforation, vascular injury). The largest comparative analysis (Loertzer 2020, n = 106, 44-mo median) found a trend favoring the Classic:<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> 

- Dry rates 33.3% (Classic) versus 11.8% (T), p = 0.114 
- 24-h pad test postop 71 versus 160 g, p = 0.066 
- Explantation 14.0% versus 23.3%, p = 0.371 
- **Inguinal pain significantly higher with Argus T** (p = 0.033)
- Kaplan-Meier device survival favored Classic (NS)

---

## Surgical technique

- Lithotomy; perineal incision.
- Cushion positioned beneath the bulbar urethra.
- Columns passed bilaterally, retropubically (Classic) or through the obturator foramina (Argus T), using needles. 
- Washers placed and adjusted intraoperatively to achieve the desired urethral compression.
- Mean OR time approximately 49 min (range 28–105).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> 
- **Postoperative readjustment requires a minor surgical procedure** to reposition the washers; needed in 38.6–42.9% of patients.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Outcomes

| Study | Variant | N | Follow-up | Dry / success | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Romano 2006 (Phase III)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Classic | 48 | 7.5 mo | **73% dry / 10% improved** | ICIQ-SF 19.2 → 4.0 |
| Hübner 2011<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | Classic | 101 | 2.1 yr | **79.2% dry** (pad 0–1 g) | I-QoL 28.8 → 63.2 (p &lt; 0.001) |
| Bochove-Overgaauw 2011<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | Classic | 100 | 27 mo | 72% overall (**92% mild / 67% mod / 67% severe**) | Observational severity subgroups; not proof of comparative efficacy |
| Bauer 2015<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | T | 42 | 28.8 mo | **61.9% cure** | Effective even after radiotherapy |
| Siracusano 2017 (multicenter)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | T | **182** | 22 mo | **86.2%** (95% mild / 78% mod / 70% severe) | 61.2% success in irradiated; adjustment in 42.9% |
| Casteleijn 2021 (long-term)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | T | 78 | **3.2 yr (up to 6.1)** | 63.6% immediate dry → **53.3% dry at 5 yr** | 79.6% &gt; 50% improvement at 5 yr; reimplant only 2.6% |
| Loertzer 2020<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | Classic vs T | 106 | 44 mo | 33.3% vs 11.8% (strict zero-pad) | Classic favored |

## Complications — the central caveat

Some Argus cohorts report substantial complications and explantation, with variation by center and implant route; the ranges below are not head-to-head risk estimates against fixed slings. 

| Complication | Reported range |
|---|---|
| Overall complication rate | 14.3% (multicenter T) → 55–83% (single-center series) |
| Explantation | 14.0–35% (Classic) / 2.6–23.3% (T) |
| Urethral erosion | **3–10%** in cited Classic cohorts; possible with other implants too |
| Acute urinary retention | 15–35% (Classic) |
| Significant perineal pain | 27% (Dalpiaz) / inguinal pain higher with T |
| Intraoperative urethral perforation | ~ 6% in some series |
| Bowel injury | Potential risk of retropubic needle passage; no reliable Argus-specific rate established here |

The most concerning data come from Dalpiaz 2011 (n = 29 Argus Classic, 35-mo median): 83% complication rate, 35% AUR, 35% sling removal (urethral erosion, infection, dislocation, retention or pain), 27% significant perineal pain requiring continuous analgesia, 17% dry, 72% dissatisfied, leading the authors to conclude that "significant changes are warranted in the sling system and in the implantation technique."<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Hübner 2011 reported 15.8% explantation, with 6 of 16 explantations occurring within the first 22 cases, a possible learning-curve signal.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> 

---

## Predictors of failure

- **Severity.** Strong gradient (mild 92–95%, moderate 67–78%, severe 67–70%).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> 
- **Prior pelvic radiotherapy.** Success drops to approximately 61% irradiated.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> 
- **Preoperative urinary loss ≥ 250 g.** Significantly worse outcomes.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> 
- **Center volume and learning curve.** Loertzer 2020 required &gt; 150 prior implantations for inclusion; Hübner 2011 explant signal concentrated in early cases.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> 

---

## Salvage after Argus failure

In the Hübner 101-pt series, 13 of 16 explanted patients received successful subsequent treatment: 7 [AUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) and 5 sling reimplantation.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> 

---

## Current positioning

Argus offers postoperative washer readjustment, but cohort reports describe substantial pain, erosion and explantation in some settings. [ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling) offers a different, port-based adjustment mechanism; nonrandomized comparisons do not establish consistent superiority across centers. For moderate-to-severe SUI, discuss the [AUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) as the guideline-supported reference option, alongside patient-specific implant risks and local expertise. The EAU guideline finds no demonstrated added benefit from adjustable over fixed slings and does not recommend a particular adjustable device.
