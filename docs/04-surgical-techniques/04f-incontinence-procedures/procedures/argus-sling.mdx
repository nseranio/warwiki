# Argus — Adjustable Suburethral Compression Sling

The **Argus sling** (Promedon, Córdoba, Argentina; first reported by Romano in 2006) is an adjustable, compressive suburethral sling with two variants: the Argus Classic (retropubic) and the Argus T (transobturator). Postoperative adjustment is mechanical, using radio-opaque silicone washers, and re-tensioning requires a minor surgical procedure. [ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling), by contrast, is adjusted percutaneously through a port.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

For positioning versus other male slings, see [Male Urethral Slings — chooser](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/male-urethral-slings).

> Argus cohorts report erosion, pain and explantation, with variation by center and route. Erosion also occurs with other implants, and indirect comparisons cannot establish that Argus has the highest complication rate of any contemporary sling.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

---

## Device design

- A radio-opaque silicone foam cushion pad (42 × 26 × 9 mm) provides soft bulbar compression.
- The pad is attached to two silicone columns formed by multiple conical elements.
- The columns pass to the abdominal wall (Classic) or through the obturator foramina (Argus T).
- Radio-opaque washers at the exit points regulate and maintain tension, and fluoroscopy can verify position during follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Argus Classic vs Argus T

The Argus T was introduced after the Classic to avoid retropubic-passage complications (bladder perforation, vascular injury). The largest comparative analysis (Loertzer 2020; 74 Classic and 32 T, median follow-up 44 months) found nonsignificant trends favoring the Classic:<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- Dry rates were 33.3% (Classic) versus 11.8% (T), p = 0.114.
- The postoperative 24-hour pad test was 71 versus 160 g, p = 0.066.
- Explantation was 14.0% versus 23.3%, p = 0.371.
- Inguinal pain was significantly more frequent with the Argus T (p = 0.033).
- Kaplan-Meier device survival favored the Classic (p = 0.198).
- In multivariable analysis the Argus T was not an independent predictor of lower success (OR 0.587, 95% CI 0.166–2.076).

---

## Surgical technique

- Lithotomy; perineal incision.
- Position the cushion beneath the bulbar urethra.
- Pass the columns bilaterally with needles, retropubically (Classic) or through the obturator foramina (Argus T).
- Place the washers and adjust them intraoperatively to reach the desired urethral compression.
- Mean operative time was approximately 49 min (range 28–105) in the Hübner series.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Postoperative readjustment repositions the washers in a minor surgical procedure. It was needed in 38.6% (Hübner) to 42.9% (Siracusano) of patients.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Complications — the central caveat

Some Argus cohorts report substantial complications and explantation, with variation by center and implant route. The ranges below are not head-to-head risk estimates against fixed slings.

| Complication | Reported range |
|---|---|
| Overall complication rate | 14.3% (multicenter T) → 55–83% (single-center series) |
| Explantation | 14.0–35% (Classic) / 9.3–23.3% (T); reimplantation 2.6% in the Casteleijn T cohort |
| Urethral erosion | **3–10%** in cited Classic cohorts; possible with other implants too |
| Acute urinary retention | 15–35% (Classic) |
| Significant perineal pain | 27% requiring continuous analgesics (Dalpiaz) / inguinal pain more frequent with T |
| Intraoperative urethral perforation | ~ 6% in some series |
| Bowel injury | Potential risk of retropubic needle passage; no reliable Argus-specific rate established here |

The most concerning data come from Dalpiaz 2011 (n = 29, median follow-up 35 months). Twenty-four men (83%) had a complication, 35% had acute urinary retention, and 35% had the sling removed (urethral erosion, infection, dislocation, retention or pain). Twenty-seven percent had significant perineal pain requiring continuous oral analgesics, 17% were dry and 72% were dissatisfied. The authors concluded that "significant changes are warranted in the sling system and in the implantation technique."<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Hübner 2011 reported 15.8% explantation, with 6 of 16 explantations occurring within the first 22 cases, a possible learning-curve signal.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Predictors of failure

- **Severity.** Success falls with severity (mild 92–95%, moderate 67–78%, severe 67–70%).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Prior pelvic radiotherapy.** Success was approximately 61% in irradiated men.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Preoperative urinary loss.** Men with a loss of 250 g or more had significantly less improvement than men with less than 250 g.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Center volume and learning curve.** Loertzer 2020 came from centers with more than 150 prior implantations, and the Hübner 2011 explants were concentrated in early cases.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Salvage after Argus failure

In the Hübner 101-patient series, 13 of 16 explanted patients later received successful treatment (7 [AUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) and 5 sling reimplantation), and 4 were lost to follow-up.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Current positioning

The Argus allows postoperative washer readjustment, but cohort reports describe substantial pain, erosion and explantation in some settings. [ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling) uses a port-based adjustment mechanism, and nonrandomized comparisons do not establish consistent superiority across centers. In a multicenter cohort of 294 fixed and 176 adjustable male slings (not randomized; adjustable slings were used more often in irradiated and more severe patients), functional outcomes did not differ, and infection (2.3% vs 0%) and pain (11.3% vs 1.7%) were more frequent with adjustable slings.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> The AUA/GURS/SUFU guideline (2024) recommends that clinicians discuss the [AUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) with men who have mild-to-severe SUI after prostate treatment (Strong Recommendation) and offer an AUS over a sling after radiotherapy (Moderate Recommendation).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
