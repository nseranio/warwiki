# Argus — Adjustable Suburethral Compression Sling

The **Argus sling** (Promedon, Córdoba, Argentina; first described Romano 2006) is an adjustable, **compressive** suburethral sling that exists in two variants — the **Argus Classic (retropubic)** and the **Argus T (transobturator)**. Its defining feature is **mechanical postoperative adjustability via radio-opaque silicone washers**; re-tensioning requires a minor surgical procedure (unlike [ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling), which adjusts via percutaneous port).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For positioning vs other male slings, see [Male Urethral Slings — chooser](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/male-urethral-slings).

> Argus carries the **highest complication burden of any modern male sling** — particularly a unique urethral-erosion signal and high explantation rates. Outcomes vary dramatically by center experience.

---

## Device design

- Radio-opaque **silicone foam cushion pad** (42 × 26 × 9 mm) for soft bulbar compression.
- Pad attached to two silicone columns formed by multiple conical elements.
- Columns pass to the abdominal wall (Classic) or through the obturator foramina (Argus T).
- **Radio-opaque washers** at the exit points regulate and maintain tension; fluoroscopy can verify position during follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Argus Classic vs Argus T

The Argus T was introduced after the Classic to avoid retropubic-passage complications (bladder perforation, vascular injury). The largest comparative analysis (**Loertzer 2020**, n = 106, 44-mo median) found a **trend favoring the Classic**:<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- Dry rates 33.3% (Classic) vs 11.8% (T), p = 0.114
- 24-h pad test postop 71 vs 160 g, p = 0.066
- Explantation 14.0% vs 23.3%, p = 0.371
- **Inguinal pain significantly higher with Argus T** (p = 0.033)
- Kaplan-Meier device survival favored Classic (NS)

---

## Surgical technique

- Lithotomy; perineal incision.
- Cushion positioned beneath the bulbar urethra.
- Columns passed bilaterally — retropubically (Classic) or through the obturator foramina (Argus T) — using needles.
- Washers placed and adjusted intraoperatively to achieve the desired urethral compression.
- Mean OR time **~ 49 min** (range 28–105).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Postoperative readjustment requires a minor surgical procedure** to reposition the washers; needed in 38.6–42.9% of patients.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Outcomes

| Study | Variant | N | Follow-up | Dry / success | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Romano 2006 (Phase III)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> | Classic | 48 | 7.5 mo | **73% dry / 10% improved** | ICIQ-SF 19.2 → 4.0 |
| Hübner 2011<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | Classic | 101 | 2.1 yr | **79.2% dry** (pad 0–1 g) | I-QoL 28.8 → 63.2 (p &lt; 0.001) |
| Bochove-Overgaauw 2011<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | Classic | 100 | 27 mo | 72% overall (**92% mild / 67% mod / 67% severe**) | All-severity efficacy |
| Bauer 2015<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | T | 42 | 28.8 mo | **61.9% cure** | Effective even after radiotherapy |
| Siracusano 2017 (multicenter)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | T | **182** | 22 mo | **86.2%** (95% mild / 78% mod / 70% severe) | 61.2% success in irradiated; adjustment in 42.9% |
| Casteleijn 2021 (long-term)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | T | 78 | **3.2 yr (up to 6.1)** | 63.6% immediate dry → **53.3% dry at 5 yr** | 79.6% &gt; 50% improvement at 5 yr; reimplant only 2.6% |
| Loertzer 2020<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | Classic vs T | 106 | 44 mo | 33.3% vs 11.8% (strict zero-pad) | Classic favored |

## Complications — the central caveat

Argus carries a **notably higher complication burden than non-adjustable male slings**, and outcomes vary dramatically by center experience.

| Complication | Reported range |
|---|---|
| Overall complication rate | 14.3% (multicenter T) → 55–83% (single-center series) |
| Explantation | 14.0–35% (Classic) / 2.6–23.3% (T) |
| Urethral erosion | **3–10%** (Classic) — unique among modern male slings |
| Acute urinary retention | 15–35% (Classic) |
| Significant perineal pain | 27% (Dalpiaz) / inguinal pain higher with T |
| Intraoperative urethral perforation | ~ 6% in some series |
| Bowel perforation | 2/18 in one early Argus Classic series<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

The most concerning data come from **Dalpiaz 2011** (n = 29 Argus Classic, 35-mo median): **83% complication rate**, 35% AUR, 35% sling removal (urethral erosion / infection / dislocation / retention / pain), 27% significant perineal pain requiring continuous analgesia, ureteral reimplantation in one patient for ureteral erosion from a dislocated sling, 17% dry, **72% dissatisfied** — leading the authors to conclude that "significant changes are warranted in the sling system and in the implantation technique."<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Hübner 2011 also reported a 15.8% explantation rate, with **6 of 16 explantations occurring within the first 22 cases** — a strong learning-curve signal.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Predictors of failure

- **Severity** — strong gradient (mild 92–95%, moderate 67–78%, severe 67–70%).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Prior pelvic radiotherapy** — success drops to ~ 61% irradiated.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Preoperative urinary loss ≥ 250 g** — significantly worse outcomes.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Center volume / learning curve** — Loertzer 2020 required &gt; 150 prior implantations for inclusion; Hübner 2011 explant signal concentrated in early cases.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Salvage after Argus failure

In the Hübner 101-pt series, 13 of 16 explanted patients received successful subsequent treatment — **7 [AUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) and 5 sling reimplantation**.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Current positioning

The Argus occupies a **diminishing role** in contemporary male SUI care. Its theoretical advantage (adjustability across all severity strata) is offset by the **highest complication burden of any modern male sling** — particularly the unique urethral-erosion signal, high explantation rates, and significant perineal pain. The [ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling) has largely **superseded the Argus** in many European centers because it offers easier office-based adjustability (percutaneous port vs surgical washer repositioning), lower complication rates, and more consistent center-to-center outcomes. The Argus remains in use primarily in Europe and South America, with the strongest case in moderate-to-severe SUI when [AUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) is unavailable or contraindicated.
