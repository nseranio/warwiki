# Argus — Adjustable Suburethral Compression Sling

The **Argus sling** (Promedon, Córdoba, Argentina; first reported by Romano in 2006) is an adjustable, compressive suburethral sling with two variants: the Argus Classic (retropubic) and the Argus T (transobturator). Postoperative adjustment is mechanical, using radio-opaque silicone washers, and re-tensioning requires a minor surgical procedure. [ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling), by contrast, is adjusted percutaneously through a port.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

For positioning versus other male slings, see [Male Urethral Slings — chooser](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/male-urethral-slings).

Promedon lists untreated urinary tract infection, anticoagulant therapy, urinary tract obstruction, renal insufficiency and autoimmune disease affecting connective tissue as main contraindications to Argus implantation. Confirm applicability against the current model-specific instructions for use.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

> Argus cohorts report erosion, pain and explantation, with variation by center and route. Erosion also occurs with other implants; indirect comparisons cannot rank Argus against contemporary slings by complication rate.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

---

## Device design

- A radio-opaque silicone foam cushion pad (42 × 26 × 9 mm) provides soft bulbar compression.
- The pad is attached to two silicone columns formed by multiple conical elements.
- The columns pass to the abdominal wall (Classic) or through the obturator foramina (Argus T).
- Radio-opaque washers at the exit points regulate and maintain tension, and fluoroscopy can verify position during follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Argus Classic vs Argus T

The Argus T was introduced after the Classic to avoid retropubic-passage complications (bladder perforation, vascular injury). The largest comparative analysis (Loertzer 2020; 74 Classic and 32 T, median follow-up 44 months) found nonsignificant trends favoring the Classic:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- Dry rates were 33.3% (Classic) versus 11.8% (T), p = 0.114.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- The postoperative 24-hour pad test was 71 versus 160 g, p = 0.066.
- Explantation was 14.0% versus 23.3%, p = 0.371.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Inguinal pain was significantly more frequent with the Argus T (p = 0.033).
- Kaplan-Meier device survival favored the Classic (p = 0.198).
- In multivariable analysis the Argus T was not an independent predictor of lower success (OR 0.587, 95% CI 0.166–2.076).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgical technique

- Lithotomy; perineal incision.
- For Classic, Promedon's 2013 quick guide instructs preservation of the bulbospongiosus muscle and central tendon, with the cushion against the intact muscle at the bulbar urethra before applying tension.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> The Argus T technique also leaves the bulbospongiosus intact and positions the sling over it.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- Pass the columns bilaterally with needles, retropubically (Classic) or through the obturator foramina (Argus T). For Classic, Promedon's 2013 quick guide directs the needles close to the posterior pubic surface and requires control cystoscopy after both passes to exclude bladder perforation. A retropubic Argus series also describes cystoscopic inspection of the urethra for injury.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> Promedon’s 2013 quick guide targets retrograde adjustment pressure 10–15 cm H2O above the untensioned baseline without overtightening;<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> its 2017 ARGUS IFU recommends an adjusted pressure of 30–40 cm H2O.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- Place the washers and adjust them intraoperatively to reach the desired urethral compression.
- Mean operative time was approximately 49 min (range 28–105) in the Hübner series.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Postoperative readjustment repositions the washers in a minor surgical procedure. It was needed in 38.6% (Hübner) to 42.9% (Siracusano) of patients.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Complications — the central caveat

Some Argus cohorts report substantial complications and explantation, with variation by center and implant route. The ranges below are not head-to-head risk estimates against fixed slings.

| Complication | Reported range |
|---|---|
| Any reported complication or adverse event | Argus T: 14.3% postoperative complications in Siracusano (median 22 months)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>; 50/78 (64.1%) in Casteleijn within six months (25/78 acute retention, 27/78 early perineal pain)<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>; and 51% in Carvalho during five-year follow-up<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>. Classic: 55% in Bochove (median 27 months)<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> and 83% (24/29) in Dalpiaz (median 35 months)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>. Event definitions and follow-up differ. |
| Explantation | 14.0–35% (Classic)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> / 9.3–23.3% (T)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>; reimplantation 2.6% in the Casteleijn T cohort<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| Erosion or infection requiring sling removal | Romano's original Classic cohort had 3/48 urethral erosions requiring removal at mean 7.5 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> At mean 45 months, 9/47 slings had been removed: six for erosion (four urethral, one bladder, one abdominal wall) and three for infection.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> Dalpiaz reported urethral erosion in 3/29 Classic recipients.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> Erosion can also occur with AUS.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| Acute urinary retention | 15–35% (Classic)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Perineal / inguinal pain | 27% requiring continuous analgesics (Dalpiaz)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> / inguinal pain more frequent with T<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Intraoperative urethral perforation | 6% (3/48; Romano Classic cohort)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Bladder or external iliac-vein injury (Classic) | In one 41-man retropubic Argus series, bladder perforation occurred in 5/41 (12.2%) and external iliac-vein injury in 2/41 (4.9%); one man had both injuries.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| Bowel injury | Potential risk of retropubic needle passage; no reliable Argus-specific rate established here |

The most concerning data come from Dalpiaz 2011 (n = 29, median follow-up 35 months).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> Twenty-four men (83%) had a complication, 35% had acute urinary retention, and 35% had the sling removed (urethral erosion, infection, dislocation, retention or pain).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> Twenty-seven percent had significant perineal pain requiring continuous oral analgesics, 17% were dry and 72% were dissatisfied.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> The authors concluded that "significant changes are warranted in the sling system and in the implantation technique."<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> Hübner 2011 reported 15.8% explantation, with 6 of 16 explantations occurring within the first 22 cases, a possible learning-curve signal.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Predictors of failure

- **Severity.** Success falls with severity (mild 92–95%, moderate 67–78%, severe 67–70%).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Prior pelvic radiotherapy.** Study-defined composite success (cured or improved) was 61.2% in men with prior radiotherapy.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> In a separate 41-man retropubic Classic series with five irradiated men, prior radiotherapy was associated with intraoperative complications in univariable analysis (OR 16.5, 95% CI 1.93–140.85).<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> Both external iliac-vein injuries occurred in men with prior radiotherapy and urethrotomy.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Preoperative urinary loss.** Men with a loss of 250 g or more had significantly less improvement than men with less than 250 g.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Center experience.** Loertzer enrolled patients from centers with more than 150 prior Argus implantations but did not compare outcomes by center volume. In Hübner's series, 6 of 16 explantations occurred within the first 22 cases, a possible learning-curve signal.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Salvage after Argus failure

In the Hübner 101-patient series, 16 slings were explanted; the abstract describes later treatment with seven [AUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) implants and five sling reimplantations.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> For persistent or recurrent SUI after a sling, the AUA/GURS/SUFU 2024 guideline recommends AUS (Moderate Recommendation).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

---

## Current positioning

The Argus allows postoperative washer readjustment, but cohort reports describe substantial pain, erosion and explantation in some settings. [ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling) uses a port-based adjustment mechanism. In a 22-man randomized Argus T versus AdVance trial (11 per arm; nine Argus T patients assessed for cure criteria at 18 months), 24-hour pad weight fell significantly within the Argus T arm at 18 months, but between-arm cure criteria did not differ significantly.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> Among the 11 Argus T recipients, three had transient retention requiring catheterization, four had prolonged pain (one underwent sling removal), and three had crural silicone-column extrusions; these counts can overlap. The trial was too small to establish comparative durability or uncommon harms.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> In a multicenter cohort of 294 fixed and 176 adjustable male slings (not randomized; adjustable slings were used more often in irradiated and more severe patients), functional questionnaire outcomes did not differ among follow-up respondents (126 fixed, 78 adjustable), and infection (2.3% vs 0%) and pain (11.9% vs 1.7%) were more frequent with adjustable slings (pooled Argus and ATOMS).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> The AUA/GURS/SUFU guideline (2024) recommends discussing the [AUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) for mild-to-severe SUI (Strong Recommendation), advises against routinely implanting male slings for severe SUI (Moderate Recommendation), and recommends an AUS over a sling after radiotherapy (Moderate Recommendation).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> The EAU 2026 male LUTS guideline assigns level 3 evidence to adjustable male-sling efficacy and makes no recommendation for adjustable slings because a benefit from adjustability over other slings has not been demonstrated.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
