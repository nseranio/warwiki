# Virtue Quadratic Male Sling

The **Virtue quadratic male sling** (Coloplast, Humlebaek, Denmark) is a four-arm polypropylene mesh sling combining transobturator and prepubic approaches to treat post-prostatectomy stress urinary incontinence. Its two arm pairs are intended to combine urethral relocation and compression; a categorical claim that no other marketed sling can do so is not established by the cited studies.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

For positioning vs other male slings, see [Male Urethral Slings — chooser](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/male-urethral-slings).

---

## Mechanism — incremental RLPP buildup

The "quadratic" name reflects the four arms: two transobturator and two prepubic.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- **Transobturator arms.** Ventral urethral elevation (analogous to AdVance repositioning).
- **Prepubic arms.** Urethral compression against the genitourinary diaphragm, without bone screws or retropubic needle passage.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Comiter 2012 measured retrograde leak point pressure (RLPP) after each technical step and demonstrated incremental contributions:

| Step | RLPP (cm H₂O) |
|---|---|
| Baseline | 33.4 |
| After transobturator tensioning | 43.3 |
| After prepubic tensioning | 55.8 |
| After final fixation | **68.8** |

Each step was significantly higher than the preceding one.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Evolution: unfixed vs fixed technique

The initial Virtue lacked a fixation mechanism and produced disappointing results. The multinational trial comparing unfixed versus fixation showed better outcomes with fixation:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

| Cohort | 12-mo subjective + objective success | Pad-weight reduction |
|---|---|---|
| **Unfixed** | 41.9% | 51.1% |
| **Fixation** | **70.9% subjective / 79.2% objective** | **88.3%** |

Fixation is now the standard technique. Efficacy was similar regardless of baseline severity in the fixation cohort.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Surgical technique

- Lithotomy; perineal incision over the bulbar urethra.
- Two transobturator arms through the obturator foramen for urethral elevation.
- Two prepubic arms anterior to the pubic bone for urethral compression.
- Intraoperative RLPP measurement confirms adequate urethral resistance.
- **Fixation step.** The original unfixed design produced 41.9% success versus 79.2% with fixation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Outcomes

The Virtue-specific evidence base is observational, without a direct randomized Virtue-versus-AUS trial. Results vary across cohorts with different endpoints and follow-up; the European prospective outcomes and the Mayo long-term series should not be treated as a head-to-head comparison.

| Study | N | Follow-up | Cure | Improvement | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Comiter 2014 (fixation)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | — | 12 mo | — | 70.9% subj / 79.2% obj | 88.3% pad-weight reduction; efficacy across all severity strata |
| Ferro 2017<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | 29 | 36 mo | — | Pad weight 128.6 → 2.5 g; pads/day 2 → 0; ICIQ-SF 14.3 → 0.9 | Italian prospective, predominantly mild SUI; all 17 complications Clavien I; PGI-I median 1 |
| Roumeguère 2022<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | 117 | 36 mo | **19%** | 35% objective / 34% subjective | Published abstract: strict cure &lt;1.3 g on 24-h test; ICIQ-UI-SF 15 → 9; predictors BMI, PVR, nocturia and ICIQ; grade III complications 5.1%, including four revisions. |
| **McCall 2016 (Mayo)**<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | 31 | **55 mo (median)** | — | **68% failure**; pads 3 → 2 only | **22% explanted; 20% required AUS; 7% chronic pain; radiation predicted failure (p = 0.02). Authors abandoned the device.** |
| Hogewoning 2017<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | 8 | 12 mo | 4 / 8 continent | 2 / 8 improved | Small post-TURP series; very limited evidence for that indication |

---

## The long-term controversy

The Virtue evidence base is sharply divided. Short-to-medium-term European data (Ferro / Comiter / Roumeguère) show meaningful improvement and acceptable safety; the Mayo Clinic long-term experience (McCall 2016) reported a 68% failure rate at 55-month median follow-up with 22% explantation and 20% subsequent AUS requirement, leading the authors to abandon the device.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

Reasons for the different cohorts' results are unclear: center experience, fixation technique, patient mix, endpoint definitions and follow-up may contribute. A 19% strict cure at 36 months and 79.2% objective success at 12 months with fixation use different definitions and populations; their difference is not a measured decline within one cohort. Prior radiation warrants caution given Mayo's association with failure and broader male-sling evidence.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

Unlike the AdVance, baseline severity has not consistently predicted outcome; fixation-cohort efficacy was similar across mild, moderate, and severe SUI.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

---

## Complications

- **Mesh erosion or exposure is a possible complication** under the manufacturer's brief safety statement. A zero-event report in a particular series does not establish zero risk across the device's use.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- Explantation rates 0% (multinational fixation trial) to 22% (Mayo long-term).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Chronic pain approximately 7% in McCall 2016.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- 5.1% Clavien grade III in the Roumeguère abstract, including four sling revisions; grade II OAB symptoms (10.3%) and pain (2.9%) were reported.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- No prolonged urinary retention or severe AEs in the multinational fixation trial.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Comparison with AUS

Not directly compared in any RCT. In the Mayo series, 20% of Virtue patients ultimately required AUS placement.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

---

## Current positioning

The Virtue occupies a controversial position in the male SUI armamentarium. The dual mechanism is attractive, but cohort-specific outcomes raise reproducibility and durability questions. The European multicenter 19% strict cure at 36 months and the Mayo 68% composite failure at 55-month median follow-up use different definitions and are not directly comparable.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

The device may be considered for post-prostatectomy SUI across severity levels, particularly for patients who are not candidates for AUS, but the evidence base is limited and conflicting. Prior radiation should be a relative contraindication. Patients should be counseled that the long-term durability question is unresolved.
