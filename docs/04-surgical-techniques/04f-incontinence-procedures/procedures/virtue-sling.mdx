# Virtue Quadratic Male Sling

The **Virtue quadratic male sling** (Coloplast, Humlebaek, Denmark) is a four-arm polypropylene mesh sling combining transobturator and prepubic approaches to treat post-prostatectomy stress urinary incontinence. Its two arm pairs are designed to provide both urethral relocation and urethral compression.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

For positioning relative to other male slings, see [Male Urethral Slings — chooser](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/male-urethral-slings).

---

## Mechanism: incremental RLPP buildup

The "quadratic" name reflects the four arms: two transobturator and two prepubic.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- **Transobturator arms.** Ventral urethral elevation (analogous to AdVance repositioning).
- **Prepubic arms.** Urethral compression against the genitourinary diaphragm, without bone screws or retropubic needle passage.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Comiter 2012 measured retrograde leak point pressure (RLPP) by perfusion sphincterometry in 22 consecutive men after each technical step. Mean RLPP rose at each step:

| Step | RLPP (cm H₂O) |
|---|---|
| Baseline | 33.4 |
| After transobturator tensioning | 43.3 |
| After prepubic tensioning | 55.8 |
| After final fixation | **68.8** |

Each mean value was significantly higher than the preceding one.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Evolution: unfixed vs fixed technique

The initial Virtue lacked a fixation mechanism and produced disappointing results. In the multinational prospective trials, the fixation cohort had better 12-month outcomes than the initial unfixed cohort:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

| Cohort | 12-mo success | Median pad-weight reduction |
|---|---|---|
| **Unfixed** | 41.9% (subjective and objective) | 51.1% |
| **Fixation** | **70.9% subjective / 79.2% objective** | **88.3%** |

Fixation is the current technique. In the fixation cohort, efficacy was similar regardless of baseline incontinence. Objective success was a greater than 50% decrease in 24-hour pad weight, and subjective success was a Patient Global Impression of Improvement score of "much" or "very much" better.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Surgical technique

- Lithotomy; perineal incision over the bulbar urethra.
- Two transobturator arms through the obturator foramen for urethral elevation.
- Two prepubic arms anterior to the pubic bone for urethral compression.
- Intraoperative RLPP measurement confirms adequate urethral resistance.
- **Fixation step.** The original unfixed design produced 41.9% success, versus 70.9% subjective and 79.2% objective success with fixation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Outcomes

The Virtue-specific evidence base is observational, without a direct randomized Virtue-versus-AUS trial. Results vary across cohorts with different endpoints and follow-up; the European prospective outcomes and the Mayo long-term series should not be treated as a head-to-head comparison.

| Study | N | Follow-up | Cure | Improvement | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Comiter 2014 (fixation)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | — | 12 mo | — | 70.9% subj / 79.2% obj | 88.3% pad-weight reduction; efficacy across all severity strata |
| Ferro 2017<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | 29 | 36 mo | — | Pad weight 128.6 → 2.5 g; pads/day 2 → 0; ICIQ-SF 14.3 → 0.9 (12-mo values, stable at 36 mo) | Italian prospective, predominantly mild SUI (72.4% used 1–2 pads/day); all 17 complications Clavien I; PGI-I median 1 |
| Roumeguère 2022<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | 117 | 36 mo | **19%** | 35% objective / 34% subjective | Published abstract: strict cure &lt;1.3 g on 24-h test; ICIQ-UI-SF 15 → 9; predictors BMI, PVR, nocturia and ICIQ; grade III complications 5.1%, including four revisions; no explantations. |
| McCall 2016 (Mayo)<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | 31 | 55 mo (median) | — | 68% failure (retrospective; failure = no reduction in pad use, explant or AUS); median pads 3 → 2 | 22% explanted; 20% required AUS; 7% chronic pain; failure more likely after external beam radiation (p = 0.02). The authors abandoned the device. |
| Hogewoning 2017<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | 8 | 12 mo | 4 / 8 continent | 2 / 8 improved | Small post-TURP series; very limited evidence for that indication |

## The long-term controversy

The Virtue evidence is divided. Short-to-medium-term prospective data (Ferro, Comiter, Roumeguère) report improvement and acceptable safety. The retrospective Mayo series (McCall 2016) reported a 68% failure rate at 55-month median follow-up, with 22% explantation and 20% subsequent AUS, and the authors abandoned the device.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

Reasons for the different cohorts' results are unclear: center experience, fixation technique, patient mix, endpoint definitions and follow-up may contribute. A 19% strict cure at 36 months and 79.2% objective success at 12 months with fixation use different definitions and populations; their difference is not a measured decline within one cohort. Prior radiation warrants caution because it was associated with failure in the Mayo series, and a meta-analysis of male slings found lower odds of success (OR 0.68) and higher odds of explantation (OR 2.93) after pelvic radiotherapy.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

Baseline severity did not predict outcome in the fixation cohort or at 36 months in the European series.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> The AUA 2024 guideline nonetheless advises against routinely implanting male slings in severe SUI.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

---

## Complications

- **Mesh erosion or exposure** is listed as a possible complication in the manufacturer's brief safety statement.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- Explantation was 0% in the European prospective series and 22% in the Mayo series.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Chronic pain occurred in 2 of 31 patients (7%) in McCall 2016.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- 5.1% Clavien grade III in the Roumeguère abstract, including four sling revisions; grade II OAB symptoms (10.3%) and pain (2.9%) were reported.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- No prolonged urinary retention or severe AEs in the multinational fixation trial.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Comparison with AUS

No randomized trial has compared Virtue with AUS. In the Mayo series, 20% of Virtue patients ultimately required AUS placement.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

---

## Current positioning

Virtue outcomes differ widely between cohorts, and durability is unresolved. The European multicenter 19% strict cure at 36 months and the Mayo 68% composite failure at 55-month median follow-up use different definitions and are not directly comparable.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

The AUA 2024 guideline on incontinence after prostate treatment recommends discussing male slings for mild to moderate SUI (Moderate Recommendation), advises against routine sling implantation in severe SUI (Moderate Recommendation), and recommends offering an AUS over a sling or adjustable balloon after radiotherapy (Moderate Recommendation). These statements apply to male slings as a class; the guideline does not single out the Virtue.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> Virtue-specific evidence is limited and conflicting, so patients are counseled that long-term durability is unresolved.
