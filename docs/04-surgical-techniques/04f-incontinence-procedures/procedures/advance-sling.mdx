# AdVance / AdVance XP — Retrourethral Transobturator Male Sling

The **AdVance male sling** (originally American Medical Systems, now Boston Scientific) is a retrourethral transobturator polypropylene mesh sling for mild-to-moderate post-prostatectomy stress urinary incontinence (PPI). It was first described by Rehder &amp; Gozzi in 2007 and is among the most extensively studied male slings. The second-generation AdVance XP (2010) added changes to the sling design and a new needle shape.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For positioning versus other male slings and the AUS, see [Male Urethral Slings — chooser](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/male-urethral-slings). 

---

## Mechanism

**Functional, non-obstructive.** The AdVance repositions the lax and descended supporting structures of the external sphincter back to their pre-prostatectomy position, restoring functional urethral length and the coaptive zone.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> A dynamic-MRI study lengthened the vesicourethral-anastomosis-to-bulbar-urethra distance at rest from 1.27 cm before the sling to 1.53 cm after it (17 men after sling). Continent post-prostatectomy controls measured 1.92 cm; the difference from controls was significant before the sling (P = 0.0018) and no longer significant after it (P = 0.09).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

The AdVance augments the residual sphincter and does not replace it, so residual sphincter function must be present.

---

## AdVance vs AdVance XP

Hüsch 2018 compared 109 AdVance and 185 AdVance XP slings (retrospective complication data, prospective questionnaires). Functional outcomes (ICIQ-SF, PGI, I-QoL, pad usage) did not differ between the two generations. Postoperative urinary retention was significantly more frequent with the XP (p = 0.042).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

---

## Patient selection — the "repositioning test"

Performed during flexible cystoscopy preoperatively. Upward perineal pressure should improve coaptation of the membranous urethra and restore a visible coaptive zone of ≥ 1 cm. Patients without visible sphincter contractions or with a negative repositioning test are excluded.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> In the Soljanik series, weak residual sphincter function with incomplete sphincter closure carried an OR of 29.0 for failure.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

Additional standard selection criteria:

- Continent at night and at rest
- No prior incontinence surgery (relative)
- 24-hour pad weight &lt; 400 g was an inclusion criterion in the Collado cohort, in which preoperative pad weight predicted outcome<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Surgical technique — step by step

1. Lithotomy and empty bladder; a catheter may aid urethral identification but is not required by the AdVance XP instructions
2. **Perineal incision** 3–4 cm over the bulbar urethra, midway between scrotum and anus
3. Dissect through bulbospongiosus to expose the bulbar urethra 
4. Mobilize urethra from the central tendon posteriorly
5. AdVance XP helical needle passers enter outside-in through small incisions over each obturator foramen toward a finger guarding the urethra in the perineal incision. Do not deploy the needle from perineum toward obturator; the manufacturer explicitly warns against that direction. 
6. Attach the sling at the perineal needle tip and withdraw each passer back along its insertion path, drawing the sleeved mesh through the transobturator route without twisting it
7. Mesh positioned retrourethrally behind the bulbar urethra, creating a hammock 
8. Tension applied to reposition the membranous urethra proximally; the goal is relocation, not compression 
9. Secure the central mesh to the corpus spongiosum proximally and distally with adequate bites at least two mesh pores from the edge; the AdVance XP instructions recommend resorbable tack sutures, not mandatory non-absorbable fixation 
10. Flexible cystoscopy may confirm coaptation after tensioning; assess for injury or obstruction as clinically indicated 
11. Close the bulbospongiosus, perineal and obturator incisions; catheter use and removal depend on the surgeon and postoperative voiding assessment
12. Operative time and discharge vary by patient and center.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

These steps follow the AdVance XP manufacturer instructions, rather than prescribing a route or fixation method for every other sling.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

## Outcomes

The evidence base consists of observational studies plus the MASTER randomized trial.

### Short-to-medium term (1–3 yr)

| Study | N | Follow-up | Cure | Cure + improved | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bauer 2009<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> | 124 | 12 mo | 51.4% | 77.1% | Prospective single-center |
| Cornu 2009<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | 102 | 13 mo (median) | 62.7% | 80.4% | Prior radiation predicted higher failure (p = 0.039) |
| Rehder 2012<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> | 156 | 3 yr | — | 76.8% | Stable from 12 mo to 3 yr |

### MASTER RCT (sling vs AUS)

The MASTER trial randomized 380 men with urodynamic stress incontinence after prostate surgery at 27 UK centers to a male transobturator sling (n = 190) or an AUS (n = 190).<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

- The primary outcome was self-reported incontinence at 12 months (an ICIQ-UI SF composite). The sling was noninferior to the AUS: 87.0% versus 84.2% remained incontinent by this strict definition (134/154 versus 133/158; risk difference 3.6%, 95% CI −11.6 to 4.6; noninferiority p = 0.003).
- The ICIQ-UI SF improved in both groups, with a slightly greater improvement with the AUS (mean difference 1.4, p = 0.02).
- Serious adverse events numbered 6 with the sling and 13 with the AUS (one AUS recipient had three).
- Every secondary outcome that reached statistical significance favored the AUS.
- The sling was more cost-effective (99% probability at a £30,000 per QALY threshold), with a slightly lower QALY gain.
- At 24 months the sling remained noninferior (risk difference −0.006, 95% CI −0.092 to 0.080). Further surgery was needed in 20 men (11%) after a sling and 4 (2%) after an AUS.<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup>

A propensity-score-matched study of 70 men with moderate incontinence (3–5 pads/day) compared the AUS with a retrourethral transobturator sling (TiLOOP Male, not AdVance). The AUS was superior: 94.3% versus 68.6% achieved at least "much improved" on the PGI-I at 12 months without further surgery or explantation (p &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> In a retrospective single-surgeon series, treatment failure (more than 1 pad per day or a further procedure) occurred less often with the AUS than with the AdVance sling in moderate SUI (success 80% versus 63%, p = 0.02). Sling success was 78% in mild SUI.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

## Key points on durability

- Cure rates in the two largest long-term series were 44.7% at a mean of 56 months and 51% at a median of 52 months, with 70–76% improved. The Australian cohort described a trend toward declining continence beyond 4 years.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> Two other cohorts reported stable results at 48 months (71.7% cured in a selected XP cohort; 77% cured at a median of 49 months).<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- Preoperative SUI severity is the strongest predictor of long-term success in these series. Collado Serra 2013 found a 0.4% decrease in cure rate per 1 g increase in preoperative 24-hour pad weight.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- Some cohorts report no erosion or explantation. The manufacturer lists erosion, extrusion, infection and retention among possible adverse events.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

---

## Complications

In the prospective Bauer 2010 series (n = 230, median follow-up 17 months) the overall complication rate was 23.9%:<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

- Transient acute urinary retention was the main complication (21.3% in this series; 12.9% in the earlier 124-patient series).<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> The AUA guideline states that retention typically resolves within one week.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup>
- Two slings were explanted (0.9%), one for misplacement and one for symphysitis attributed to Guillain-Barré syndrome.

In a retrospective multicenter review of 127 men (MacAskill 2021), approximately 23% had early postoperative pain. Most cases resolved within 4 weeks, and 2.3% (3 men) had chronic pain (&gt; 3 months) that resolved within 1 year.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

Erectile function (IIEF-5) and voiding parameters (IPSS, Qmax, PVR) were not significantly affected in the cited cohorts.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Reintervention rate approximately 10–12% in the cited series.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> Erosion was uncommon in reports, but remains a listed device risk.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

## Predictors of failure

| Risk factor | Effect | Strength of evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Prior pelvic radiation** | OR 0.66–0.68 for success; approximately 3× higher odds of explantation and infection | Meta-analysis of unadjusted odds ratios<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>; small retrospective series<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> |
| **Severe preoperative SUI (≥ 5 PPD)** | Significantly lower cure / success | Multiple prospective<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| **Detrusor overactivity** | Independent predictor of mid-term failure | Prospective<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> |
| **Obesity (BMI &gt; 30)** | OR 7.9 for failure | Multiple series<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **Prior bladder-neck stenosis** | OR 2.6 for failure | Prospective<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **Prior urethral stricture surgery** | Higher failure (p = 0.013) | Prospective<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> |
| **Weak residual sphincter / negative repositioning test** | OR 29.0 for failure | Prospective<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **No sling tunnelling, or ≤ 4 resorbable fixation stitches** | OR 22.6 and OR 8.4 for failure | Prospective<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **Prior incontinence surgery** | Lower continence, improvement, satisfaction | Multiple series<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup> |

Prior radiation is the most consistent negative predictor. In the Ghaffar 2023 meta-analysis, irradiated patients had an OR of 0.68 for success (95% CI 0.53–0.87). In Torrey 2013 (37 patients, retrospective), none of 7 irradiated men were pad-free versus 63.3% of 30 non-irradiated men.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

## Salvage

A failed AdVance sling does not preclude subsequent [AUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) implantation.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup> For persistent or recurrent SUI after a sling, the AUA/GURS/SUFU guideline recommends an AUS (Moderate Recommendation).<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup> [ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling) has also been reported as a salvage option: in a retrospective multicenter cohort (n = 88, mean follow-up 42.5 months), 76.1% achieved social continence.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup> That series does not establish superiority to salvage AUS.

---

## Current positioning

The AdVance XP is used mainly in non-irradiated men with mild-to-moderate PPI (≤ 3 PPD or 24-hour pad weight &lt; 400 g) and a positive repositioning test.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[11]](#ref11)[[19]](#ref19)</sup> The AUA/GURS/SUFU guideline (2024) says clinicians should discuss male slings for mild-to-moderate SUI after prostate treatment (Moderate Recommendation), should not routinely implant them in severe SUI (Moderate Recommendation), and should offer an AUS over a sling after radiotherapy (Moderate Recommendation).<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup> The sling is minimally invasive, works by repositioning rather than obstruction, leaves the AUS option open, and has low reported erosion and explantation rates. Its limitations are lower efficacy than the AUS in moderate SUI, poorer results after radiation, and a possible decline in continence beyond 4 years.
