Urinary incontinence management is organized by **clinical phenotype**: treatment selection, surgical technique and device options for stress, urgency and mixed presentations in male and female patients. The relevant guidance differs by phenotype. The 2024 AUA/SUFU OAB guideline, the 2023 AUA/SUFU female SUI guideline and the 2019 AUA/GURS/SUFU incontinence-after-prostate-treatment guideline (amended 2024) are not interchangeable.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

For SUI after prostate treatment, the 2024 amended guideline recommends that clinicians discuss the artificial urinary sphincter (AUS) for mild to severe SUI (Strong Recommendation) and male slings for mild to moderate SUI (Moderate Recommendation). It recommends AUS over slings or adjustable balloons after radiotherapy (Moderate Recommendation).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In the MASTER trial (n = 380), the sling was noninferior to AUS on the primary endpoint of patient-reported incontinence at 12 months (87.0% vs 84.2% still incontinent by the strict definition). Secondary outcomes favored AUS.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The three searchable databases below are organized by phenotype rather than technique. Each links from a clinical entry point (female SUI, male SUI, OAB / UUI) to the full procedural toolkit and then to the technique pages for individual slings, neuromodulation, behavioral therapy and devices. Escalation from conservative options, including pelvic-floor training and behavioral strategies, depends on phenotype, bother, severity, prior radiation, urethral health, comorbidity and patient preference.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
