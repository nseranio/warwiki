Urinary incontinence management organized by **clinical phenotype** — treatment selection, surgical technique, and device options for stress, urgency, and mixed presentations across male and female patients. The relevant guidance differs by phenotype: the **2024 AUA/SUFU OAB guideline**, **2023 AUA/SUFU female SUI guideline**, and **2019 AUA/GURS/SUFU incontinence-after-prostate-treatment guideline amended in 2024** are not interchangeable. For post-prostate-treatment SUI, male slings are options to discuss in selected mild-to-moderate cases, while AUS is a major option for moderate-to-severe cases; the MASTER trial's strict primary endpoint showed noninferiority for sling, but secondary symptom and satisfaction results favored AUS.[1–3]

The three searchable databases below are organized by **phenotype rather than technique** — each links from a clinical entry-point (female SUI, male SUI, OAB / UUI) to the full procedural toolkit, then cross-links to the technique deep-dives on individual sling, neuromodulation, behavioral, and device pages. Conservative options (including pelvic-floor training or behavioral strategies where appropriate) are important, but escalation is individualized by phenotype, bother, severity, prior radiation, urethral health, comorbidity and preference.[1,2]
