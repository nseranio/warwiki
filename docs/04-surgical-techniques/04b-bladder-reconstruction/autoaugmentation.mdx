# Bladder Autoaugmentation (Detrusorectomy / Detrusor Myotomy)

**Bladder autoaugmentation** (also called **detrusor myotomy** or **detrusorectomy**) increases bladder capacity and compliance by excising or incising the detrusor muscle over the dome while leaving the underlying urothelium intact, aiming to create an epithelial diverticulum with improved storage capacity and compliance, without incorporating bowel into the urinary tract.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> First described by Cartwright and Snow in 1989, it was developed to avoid the metabolic, mucus-related, and malignancy complications inherent to enterocystoplasty.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

EAU 2026 includes detrusor myectomy as an option in selected neuro-urological patients, while recommending intestinal augmentation for refractory low compliance and/or neurogenic detrusor overactivity. Autoaugmentation has inconsistent long-term results; persistent unsafe pressures require timely reassessment rather than waiting for an assumed delayed benefit.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

## Indications

Refractory low-capacity, poorly compliant, or hyperreflexic bladder (CIC and anticholinergics failed). Common conditions: neurogenic bladder (myelomeningocele, SCI, tethered cord, sacral agenesis), posterior urethral valves, bladder exstrophy.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

Particularly considered when:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- The patient or family wishes to avoid bowel interposition and its metabolic and mucosal complications.
- The patient has renal insufficiency (where enterocystoplasty acidosis would be poorly tolerated).
- A simpler, extraperitoneal procedure that preserves the option for future enterocystoplasty is desired.
- The patient has no anterior abdominal-wall anomalies (important for the rectus muscle backing technique).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Contraindications

- **Severely fibrotic or thickened bladder.** Urothelium may not prolapse adequately.
- **Anterior abdominal-wall anomalies** (omphalocele, gastroschisis) preclude rectus-muscle backing.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **No reliable emptying plan.** Catheterization may still be required. A simultaneous catheterizable channel is not itself a contraindication; combined operations have been reported.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **Severely small bladder capacity** where the achievable degree of augmentation is insufficient.

---

## Surgical Techniques

### 1. Detrusorectomy (Cartwright–Snow)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- Extraperitoneal or transperitoneal approach.
- Detrusor muscle over the entire dome is excised; bladder epithelium is preserved.
- Urothelium prolapses through the defect and forms a large epithelial bulge or diverticulum.
- Storage improvement is variable and must be confirmed; myotomy incises muscle, whereas myectomy or detrusorectomy removes it.

### 2. Detrusorectomy with rectus muscle backing (Perovic)<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

Addresses the problem of urothelial retraction and shrinkage after simple myotomy.

- Completely extraperitoneal approach (inferior midline or transverse incision).
- Detrusorectomy involves the entire upper half of the bladder.
- Both rectus abdominis muscles dissected from their anterior and posterior sheaths.
- Prolapsed urothelium sutured to the rectus muscles at multiple points to prevent retraction.
- Rectus muscles provide structural backing; may facilitate voluntary emptying.
- Long-term results (median follow-up 134 mo): median bladder capacity 419 mL (range 296–552); 14/23 patients voluntary voiding without PVR.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### 3. Autoaugmentation with demucosalized bowel overlay (composite cystoplasty)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

Hybrid combining autoaugmentation with a demucosalized seromuscular bowel patch placed over the exposed urothelium.

- **Demucosalized gastric patch (Nguyen–Mitchell):** in 11 pediatric patients, capacity increased from 109 to 236 mL and compliance from 3 to 9 mL/cmH₂O; no metabolic complications; mucus-free urine.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Demucosalized intestinal patch (Lima nonsecretory cystoplasty):** in 129 augmentations in 123 patients (104 sigmoid, 25 ileal), capacity increased by 329% and compliance 7-fold; no mucus, no perforation, no neoplasia; failure 10.1%.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Mucosal-regrowth evidence:** in an animal study, regrowth occurred in 4/5 sheep when muscularis mucosae and submucosa were preserved, versus none after deeper removal. A related 10-child clinical series reported no detected regrowth. This is not an 80% human risk estimate or proof that regrowth cannot occur.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- A silicone balloon conformer in place 2 weeks postop may prevent mucosal shrinkage (8/10 continent with a conformer versus 1/12 without in a small comparative series; not established routine care).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

### 4. Seromuscular enterocystoplasty (SE)<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

Detrusorectomy combined with a seromuscular bowel patch (preserving submucosa). In a 10-child series after spinal trauma, mean capacity increased from 83 to 320 mL and end-filling intravesical pressure fell from 48 to 21 cm H₂O at three months; 9/10 used Valsalva voiding and none were incontinent. This historical observation is not a recommendation to use abdominal straining: emptying pressures and upper-tract safety require assessment.

### 5. Laparoscopic autoaugmentation<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

- Extraperitoneal retropubic approach is technically feasible.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- KTP-laser-assisted laparoscopic detrusorotomy showed poor durability, and both patients ultimately required enterocystoplasty.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- A single laparoscopic case for traumatic SCI reported a capacity of 510 mL and continence at 6 mo.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- These very small reports establish feasibility, not comparative recovery or durability benefits.

**Possible temporal pattern (not guaranteed):** transient decrease or no change in bladder capacity during the first 3 months, followed by significant and sustained increase beginning approximately 5 months postoperatively; compliance continues to improve for up to 5 years. Low-dose anticholinergics may accelerate the therapeutic effect.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Tradeoffs Compared with Enterocystoplasty

| Feature | Autoaugmentation | Enterocystoplasty |
|---|---|---|
| Bowel-mediated metabolic complications | Avoided when no bowel mucosa is incorporated; unrelated metabolic disease remains possible | Hyperchloremic acidosis, B₁₂ deficiency |
| Mucus production | None | Significant |
| Malignancy risk | Avoids an entero-urinary junction; does not eliminate baseline bladder-cancer risk | Long-latency risk reported; estimates vary by population |
| Bowel morbidity | Avoids bowel resection in pure autoaugmentation; risk depends on access and concomitant surgery | Resection and adhesions introduce bowel morbidity |
| Surgical complexity | Simple, extraperitoneal | Major intraperitoneal |
| Operative time | Shorter | Longer |
| Preserves future options | Does not preclude enterocystoplasty | — |
| Degree of augmentation | Less predictable, smaller increase | Greater, more reliable |
| Durability | Variable; may deteriorate | More durable |

---

## Complications

- **Urothelial retraction and fibrosis.** Primary cause of failure; rationale for rectus-muscle backing and demucosalized bowel overlay modifications.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- **Bladder perforation.** Rare; reported particularly when combined with [AUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) (1 case in Stöhrer's series of 50). A reliable comparative perforation rate is not established.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Increased post-void residual.** Most patients require CIC; the diverticulum does not contract effectively.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Delayed functional improvement.** 1–6 months (occasionally \>1 year); requires patient motivation and patience.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Failure and secondary surgery.** Clinical failure, urodynamic failure, and conversion to enterocystoplasty are different outcomes. For example, MacNeily reported 12/17 clinical failures, but only four subsequent enterocystoplasties. The heterogeneous series do not establish a reliable ranking of techniques.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[10]](#ref10)[[17]](#ref17)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

---

## Controversial Durability

- **Negative long-term data:** MacNeily reported 71% of 17 myelomeningocele patients were clinical failures at median 75 mo; 14/15 (93%) considered urodynamic failures; progressive hydronephrosis in 5; enterocystoplasty in 4. Concluded that autoaugmentation "cannot be endorsed for the management of congenital neuropathic bladder."<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- **Positive long-term data:** Hansen (n=25, 6.8 yr): sustained significant increases in capacity and compliance; normal renal function in all but 1; continence 72%.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Djordjevic (n=23, 134 mo): continued significant capacity increase (median 419 mL); capacity improvement in all, with nine still using CIC; this uncontrolled series does not prove zero long-term failure risk.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Critical review** by Gurocak: the clinical outcome of autoaugmentation "does not appear to be durable" versus enterocystoplasty, but acknowledges definite advantages of low morbidity and absence of bowel-related side effects. Patient selection is the most important factor for success.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

---

## Patient Selection — Keys to Success

Best candidates for autoaugmentation:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

- Detrusor hyperreflexia (overactivity) rather than pure fibrotic low compliance.
- Adequate baseline bladder capacity (the procedure improves compliance more reliably than capacity).
- No anterior abdominal-wall anomalies (for rectus backing).
- Motivated to wait for potentially delayed functional improvement.
- Willing to perform CIC postoperatively.
- Renal insufficiency that makes enterocystoplasty metabolically risky.
- Prefers a procedure that avoids bowel incorporation that preserves the option for future enterocystoplasty.

---

## Technique Selection

- **Simple detrusor myotomy / myectomy:** less extensive than bowel augmentation, but durability is variable; selection should account for current pressures, upper-tract risk, and the consequences of waiting for a response.
- **Detrusorectomy with rectus muscle backing:** favorable long-term results in a selected 23-patient series; superiority over other techniques is unproven. The earlier 7- and 19-patient publications overlap with this cohort and should not be added as independent evidence.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Autoaugmentation with demucosalized bowel overlay:** combines the metabolic advantages of autoaugmentation with the structural support of a bowel patch; the cited Lima series comprises 129 augmentations in 123 patients using both sigmoid and ileum.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Silicone balloon conformer:** may improve outcomes of simple autoaugmentation by preventing early mucosal shrinkage.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
