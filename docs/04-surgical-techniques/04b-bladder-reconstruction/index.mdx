# Bladder Augmentation & Catheterizable Channels

Bladder reconstruction includes augmentation cystoplasty for the hostile low-capacity bladder, continent catheterizable channels (CCC) for patients who cannot catheterize per urethra reliably or prefer an easier abdominal catheterization route, and ileovesicostomy as an incontinent low-pressure outlet.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> For salvage of bladder-neck contracture and vesicourethral anastomotic stenosis (BNC / VUAS) and for bladder-neck closure, see the dedicated [Bladder Neck Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction) page.

## Decision Framework

Augmentation is an option for poor compliance or refractory neurogenic detrusor overactivity when less-invasive treatment has failed or is not tolerated. AUA/SUFU states that clinicians may offer augmentation cystoplasty to select NLUTD patients who are refractory to, or intolerant of, less invasive therapies for detrusor overactivity and/or poor bladder compliance (Statement 51, Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C), and EAU recommends offering it for low compliance or refractory neurogenic detrusor overactivity (strong).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> A catheterizable channel addresses access for emptying and can be constructed with or without augmentation. Neither wheelchair use nor a desire for abdominal access alone establishes a need for augmentation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Plan three separate components: storage, access for emptying and outlet continence. Assess each on its own merits, then decide whether a combined or staged reconstruction is appropriate. Detrusor leak point pressure is not a stand-alone test of sphincter adequacy or a universal safe-storage threshold. A dry outlet must not conceal unsafe reservoir pressure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

| Clinical problem | Reconstruction to consider |
|---|---|
| Low compliance or refractory neurogenic detrusor overactivity | [Augmentation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/bladder-augmentation), with an achievable catheterization plan if needed. |
| Severely impacted idiopathic OAB after all other therapies fail | In carefully selected patients, consider [augmentation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/bladder-augmentation) or [urinary diversion](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion) after counseling about surgical morbidity and possible intermittent catheterization (AUA/SUFU Statement 30, Expert Opinion).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Difficult urethral catheterization with otherwise suitable storage | [Catheterizable channel](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/catheterizable-channels); augmentation is added only for a reservoir indication. |
| Neurogenic stress incontinence | Confirm suitable storage and assess sex, anatomy, hand function and the catheterization plan. EAU recommends an autologous sling first line for women able to self-catheterize (strong), a synthetic sling as an alternative for selected women able to self-catheterize (weak), AUS for selected women at experienced centers (weak), and AUS for men (strong).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> AUA/SUFU states that clinicians should offer slings (Statement 45, Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C) and may offer an artificial urinary sphincter (Statement 46, Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C) to select NLUTD patients with stress incontinence and acceptable bladder storage parameters. An AUS is not restricted to patients who void spontaneously.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Devastated outlet | Selected [bladder-neck closure](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/outlet-continence/bladder-neck-closure) with a reliable alternative drainage route: CCC, permanent suprapubic drainage or selected diversion. AUA/SUFU states that clinicians may offer closure with concomitant bladder drainage to select NLUTD patients with refractory stress incontinence (Statement 47, Expert Opinion).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Refractory bladder-centric IC/BPS or rare end-stage small fibrotic bladder | Carefully selected [supratrigonal cystectomy with augmentation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/supratrigonal-cystectomy) or diversion after other therapies have failed (AUA Statement 22: Option, Evidence Strength Grade C); pain relief is not guaranteed even after cystectomy.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| Radiation injury, tuberculosis or other fibrotic bladder | Treat the underlying disease and assess remaining tissue, renal function and upper tracts before choosing augmentation or diversion. No single segment or capacity cutoff applies to every patient. |
| Cannot sustain catheterization | Consider [cutaneous vesicostomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/cutaneous-vesicostomy), selected [ileovesicostomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/ileovesicostomy) or another [diversion](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion). Counsel about leakage, obstruction and repeat procedures. AUA/SUFU advises offering diversion when other options have failed or are inappropriate (Statement 54, Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |

### Reservoir and segment selection

The [augmentation principles](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/augmentation-principles) page covers bowel physiology and follow-up. The Cochrane review's segment comparisons address bladder replacement and diversion, not augmentation.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Small observational augmentation comparisons favor ileum for some urodynamic outcomes.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

| Option | Selection points |
|---|---|
| [Ileocystoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/ileocystoplasty) | Common bowel augmentation; consider acidosis, mucus, stones, bowel morbidity and lifelong follow-up. |
| [Sigmoid](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/sigmoid-cystoplasty) or [ileocecal cystoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/ileocecal-cystoplasty) | Alternatives selected for anatomy and the overall reconstruction. |
| [Gastrocystoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/gastrocystoplasty) | Uncommon selected or salvage option. Kidney impairment or prior radiation does not automatically make it preferable; hypochloremic alkalosis, hematuria-dysuria and malignancy are concerns. |
| [Ureterocystoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/ureterocystoplasty) | Requires adequate suitable dilated ureter and a renal-preservation plan. Bowel-free does not mean surveillance-free. |
| [Autoaugmentation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/autoaugmentation) or [SCLU](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/seromuscular-colocystoplasty) | Selected alternatives with variable durability and possible later bowel augmentation. |

### Channel selection

The [channel principles](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/catheterizable-channels) and individual pages distinguish primary continence, continued use and revision.

| Channel | Selection points |
|---|---|
| [Appendicovesicostomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/appendicovesicostomy) | Often preferred when appendix length, caliber and blood supply are suitable. |
| [Yang-Monti / double Monti / Casale](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/yang-monti) | Alternatives when the appendix is unavailable or inadequate; select length and routing carefully. |
| [Tubularized bladder flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/tubularized-bladder-flap) | Requires suitable native bladder tissue and capacity; there is no mandatory TBF-before-Monti sequence. |
| [Hemi-Kock](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/hemi-kock) | Selected ileal augmentation and nipple-valve channel; valve revision is only part of the total intervention burden. |
| [Indiana augmentation / CCIC](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/indiana-augmentation-cystoplasty) | Combined augmentation and catheterizable outlet using ileocecal anatomy; the outlet mechanism does not guarantee ureteral antireflux. |

### Outlet selection

Use the [incontinence procedures](/docs/surgical-techniques/incontinence) and [bladder-neck reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction) pages for procedure-specific details. For urethral bulking agents, AUA/SUFU states that clinicians may offer them to NLUTD patients with stress incontinence but must counsel that efficacy is modest and cure is rare (Statement 44, Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C). Sling, AUS and closure decisions require safe storage and a dependable long-term emptying plan.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
