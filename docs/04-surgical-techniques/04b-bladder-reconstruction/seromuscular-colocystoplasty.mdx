# Seromuscular Colocystoplasty Lined With Urothelium (SCLU)

**Seromuscular colocystoplasty lined with urothelium (SCLU)** is a form of [augmentation cystoplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/bladder-augmentation) that uses a demucosalized colonic segment placed over an [autoaugmented bladder](/docs/surgical-techniques/04b-bladder-reconstruction/autoaugmentation), allowing the native urothelium to line the augmented reservoir while the colonic seromuscular layer provides structural support. Developed by González and colleagues in the early 1990s, SCLU was designed to achieve the capacity and compliance benefits of enterocystoplasty while aiming to reduce bowel-mucosa-related metabolic exchange and mucus. SCLU may still carry risks of stones, perforation, bowel complications, and malignancy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Concept and Rationale

Standard enterocystoplasty's central problem is intestinal mucosa in contact with urine: metabolic derangements, mucus, stones, long-term malignancy risk. Earlier attempts to simply strip mucosa from bowel and use the seromuscular layer caused fibrosis and contraction. SCLU combines two principles to solve this:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

1. **Detrusorectomy (autoaugmentation):** detrusor excised from the dome, exposing the underlying urothelium, which prolapses outward.
2. **Seromuscular colonic patch.** Demucosalized sigmoid colon placed over the prolapsed urothelium, providing a supporting layer intended to reduce urothelial retraction and fibrosis.

The result is a composite augmentation: luminal surface is urothelium (from native bladder); outer wall is colonic seromuscular tissue.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Experimental Foundation

The canine model (Buson, González 1994) established findings:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- When the intestinal submucosa was preserved on the seromuscular patch, there was no fibrosis and capacity was preserved.
- When the submucosa was not preserved, there was moderate contraction with subepithelial fibrosis and capacity loss.
- In animals with previously reduced bladder capacity, SCLU effectively augmented.
- Seromuscular patches remained viable and well-vascularized.
- Native bladder urothelium and lamina propria were preserved beneath the patch. This differs from earlier rat experiments that used the serosal surface as a luminal lining.

In the de Badiola 1991 rat model, urothelial growth on the seromuscular colonic patch was first noted at 5 days; histology in 40 rats showed no fibrosis or inflammation, and none of 22 patches measured at sacrifice had shrunk. The 40 rats were examined 5–80 days after surgery.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

These animal studies supported preserving submucosa in the original SCLU technique. Other demucosalized constructs use different dissection planes to reduce mucosal regrowth; operative steps vary by construct.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgical Technique

### Step 1 — Detrusorectomy

A generous detrusorectomy over the bladder dome, excising the detrusor muscle while preserving the underlying urothelium and lamina propria. The urothelium prolapses through the muscular defect, creating a large epithelial bulge.

### Step 2 — Sigmoid colon harvest and demucosalization

- 15–20 cm of sigmoid colon isolated on its vascular pedicle.
- Bowel continuity restored by primary anastomosis.
- Colonic segment detubularized along its antimesenteric border.
- The original SCLU method preserves intestinal submucosa and muscularis mucosae while removing the epithelial lining. Demucosalization depth is technique-specific: deeper removal has been studied to reduce residual crypt regrowth, but may affect patch integrity. Confirm the selected technique rather than merging conflicting dissection planes.

### Step 3 — Composite anastomosis

- The vascularized patch supports the exposed native urothelium. The original operative description should determine patch orientation; the serosa-as-lumen rat experiment is not an instruction to invert a clinical SCLU patch.
- Edges of the seromuscular patch sutured to the edges of the detrusor defect.
- Preserve and appose the native urothelial layer; mucosal injury or separation can compromise the construct. González and Ludwikowski describe maintaining bladder distension at about 20 cm H₂O during the first postoperative week to promote coaptation, provided the bladder mucosa is watertight.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

### Step 4 — Concomitant procedures

- **[AUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter)** is frequently implanted simultaneously or has been placed previously as one selected outlet strategy.
- Bladder-neck procedures or slings as needed. González and Ludwikowski cautioned that simultaneous appendicovesicostomy or ureteroneocystostomy can breach the native urothelium needed for watertight first-week patch coaptation.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

### Avoiding hourglass deformity<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- Early in the experience, hourglass deformity developed in 22% of patients.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Addressed by ensuring the seromuscular patch is sutured well below the level of the detrusorectomy edge, creating a wider anastomosis.
- The González AUS series described modifications intended to avoid this complication.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Indications

SCLU shares the general indications for augmentation but is specifically considered when the surgeon wishes to avoid incorporating intestinal mucosa into the urinary tract:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

- Neurogenic bladder (myelomeningocele, SCI) with low compliance and/or overactivity refractory to conservative management.
- Bladder exstrophy and cloacal exstrophy.
- Posterior urethral valves.
- **Renal insufficiency**, where metabolic acidosis from standard enterocystoplasty would be poorly tolerated.
- Patients with combined reservoir and outlet dysfunction; small series describe SCLU with an AUS, without establishing superiority over alternative reconstructions.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

---

## Critical Prerequisites for Success

### 1. Adequate bladder outlet resistance

Selected series associate adequate outlet resistance with maintained patch apposition. **Abdominal leak point pressure (ALPP)** is not storage detrusor pressure, and these findings do not justify maintaining an unsafe high-pressure reservoir. Jung 2012 retrospectively evaluated 33 patients over a mean of 6.0 years:<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- Among 17 patients with simultaneous anti-incontinence surgery, none in the ALPP 40–60 cm H₂O group required further surgery.
- Four of ten with ALPP below 40 cm H₂O required additional surgery. This is not a reported augmentation-failure rate or a validated universal pressure threshold. Two of 16 patients without anti-incontinence surgery eventually required continence surgery, and the authors recommended an ALPP of 60 cm H₂O or less as an indication for simultaneous anti-incontinence surgery.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

In González’s retrospective pediatric series, 15 patients underwent SCLU without a concomitant bladder procedure (most had an AUS); none required repeat augmentation, and bladder capacity measured at 30 cm H₂O increased from 60% to 100% of expected capacity for age.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> By contrast, 3/5 patients who underwent creation of a continent catheterizable channel at the time of SCLU required repeat augmentation.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

In González and Ludwikowski's five-patient simultaneous-channel subgroup, the three patients who required repeat augmentation also had prolonged postoperative urinary leaks; this association cannot establish the mechanism of failure.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[13]](#ref13)</sup> A functioning continent channel is designed to resist leakage; it should not be described as inherently creating a low-pressure leak point. The small comparison cannot isolate the mechanism.

### 2. Intact bladder mucosa

SCLU should be primarily considered in patients without prior bladder mucosal injury; previous surgery that has damaged the urothelium may compromise its ability to prolapse and line the seromuscular patch.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Synergistic Role of the AUS

González 2002 in 27 children specifically combined SCLU with AUS:<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

- In this retrospective series, 24/27 (89%) achieved study-defined continence (dry between catheterizations or voiding without pads) at a mean follow-up of 1.7 years; six hourglass corrections and two AUS removals were also reported.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Bladder capacity, safe capacity for age, and end-filling pressure all improved significantly.
- No significant upper-tract changes were reported, but two AUS erosions required device removal.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Outlet resistance may support patch apposition, but the study does not prove AUS is necessary for adhesion.
- The authors favored SCLU for selected patients with adverse storage changes after AUS; this is a center-specific conclusion.

González concluded that SCLU is most effective in patients who already have an AUS or who undergo simultaneous AUS implantation. The 2009 authors advised against a simultaneous catheterizable channel after three failures among five patients. This small-series caution is specific to SCLU, not to other augmentation techniques.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Outcomes

The reported SCLU series are predominantly pediatric (mean age at surgery 7.5–11.1 years in several cohorts); these series do not provide adult-specific effect estimates.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

| Series | n | Follow-up | Capacity ↑ | Continence | Failure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| González 1995<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | 16 | Mean 12 mo | 2.4× (139 → 335 mL; paired n = 14) | 13/16 dry day and night | 2/16 reaugmented |
| Jednak 2000<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | 32 | Mean 1.6 yr | 1.8× total / 2.4× safe (urodynamic n = 28, mean 1.5 yr) | 71% after initial procedure; 81% after secondary procedures | 4/32 (12.5%) augmentation failure |
| González (with AUS) 2002<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | 27 | Mean 1.7 yr | Significant | 24/27 (89%) continent (dry between catheterizations or voiding without pads) | 1/27 (3.7%) augmentation failure |
| González 2009<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | 20 | Mean 53 mo | Capacity at 30 cm H₂O: 60 → 100% expected for age (Group 1, n = 15, no simultaneous bladder procedure) | All 20 patients continent | 0/15 Group 1; 3/5 Group 2 (with channel) required repeat augmentation |
| Jung 2012<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | 33 | Mean 6.0 yr | 2.96× | 13/33 stopped anticholinergics at 47.3 mo; not a continence rate | Additional outlet surgery occurred; see subgroup results above |

## Evidence Compared with Standard Enterocystoplasty

| Feature | SCLU | Standard Enterocystoplasty |
|---|---|---|
| Metabolic disturbances | Reduced bowel-mucosa exposure; none reported in selected small series | Metabolic exchange requires monitoring |
| Mucus production | Not clinically significant at short-term follow-up in Jednak's series | Common; irrigations are often required |
| Bladder perforation | None observed in cited small cohorts; risk is not proven zero | Recognized long-term risk varying by population |
| Bowel obstruction | None observed in selected cohorts; bowel surgery still carries risk | Recognized bowel and adhesion morbidity |
| Bladder stones | 1/20 in Shekarriz<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>; 2/32 in Jednak<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | More frequent with continent stomas and in bladder or cloacal exstrophy (32% in exstrophy in Shekarriz's mixed cohort)<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Malignancy risk | Not eliminated; baseline disease and possible intestinal regrowth remain relevant | Long-latency risk; estimates depend on population and follow-up |
| Capacity increase | 1.8–2.96× | No significant difference in achieved capacity in one retrospective comparison |
| Surgical complexity | Higher (demucosalization + detrusorectomy) | Standard |

Shekarriz’s retrospective 133-patient comparison included only 20 SCLU patients; none had perforation or SBO during that follow-up. Its authors favored SCLU/sigmoid, but this does not establish superiority.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> Bandi et al.’s retrospective comparison (26 SCLU; 32 conventional augmentations) found no statistically significant difference in achieved capacity, subjective continence, or several subsequent surgery outcomes; both groups remained at risk of stones, reflux, and augmentation failure.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> In a later pediatric retrospective cohort using ileal or colonic seromuscular patches (n = 37) versus standard cystoplasty (n = 46), mean postoperative capacity was 240 versus 387 mL, compliance was 8.8 versus 26.7 mL/cm H₂O, and end-filling pressure was 44.2 versus 20.7 cm H₂O. Mean group follow-up was 61 versus 36 months; treatment eras and concomitant procedures differed, so these comparisons do not establish a causal effect.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Complications

- **Hourglass deformity.** The most characteristic complication; approximately 22% (7/32 in Jednak<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>; 6/27 in the González AUS series, all six requiring correction<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>). Technical modifications (suturing the patch well below the detrusorectomy edge) were described to reduce hourglass deformity after cases in these early series.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Hydronephrosis and reflux.** In Jednak's series new or increased hydronephrosis was present in 6/62 renal units (10%) and reflux in 9/60 (15%) evaluated.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Colonic mucosal regrowth.** A significant concern. Jednak biopsies showed regrowth in 5/7 (71%)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>; González’s initial series showed urothelium alone in 7/10 biopsies, colonic regrowth in two, and mixed urothelium/colonic mucosa in one<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>. Dewan found regrowth in 4/5 sheep with preserved muscularis mucosae/submucosa, compared with none after deeper removal; ten clinical patients had no detected regrowth.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> This is not an 80% human risk or proof of one universally safe dissection plane.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Augmentation failure and reaugmentation.** Retrospective series report reaugmentation in 2/16 over a mean 12-month cohort follow-up<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> and augmentation failure in 4/32 over a mean 1.6-year cohort follow-up<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>. In a later pediatric cohort using ileal or colonic seromuscular patches, 8/37 patients required reaugmentation over a mean 61-month group follow-up versus 0/46 standard-cystoplasty patients over a mean 36-month group follow-up. The paper does not report a colocystoplasty-only rate, and treatment era, concomitant procedures, and unequal follow-up limit comparison.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> Three of five patients with a simultaneous catheterizable channel required reaugmentation in another small series.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[7]](#ref7)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Patient Selection Summary

For children with spinal dysraphism, the 2026 published summary of the 2025 joint EAU/ESPU/ERN guideline describes seromuscular cystoplasty as a selected-case alternative with variable outcomes.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> The full guideline states that it has not proved as successful as standard intestinal augmentation.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

- Consider selected patients with adequate or surgically achievable outlet resistance, safe storage pressures, and a dependable catheterization route; AUS is one option.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- SCLU can be considered primarily when native bladder mucosa is intact and can prolapse beneath the patch; prior surgery that injured this mucosa may compromise the construct.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Discuss the limited SCLU-specific evidence suggesting increased failure with simultaneous channel construction; avoid presenting the five-patient subgroup as a universal contraindication.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- In renal insufficiency, SCLU may be considered to limit direct bowel-mucosal urine exposure; the small, short-term series do not establish a lower metabolic-complication rate than conventional enterocystoplasty.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- After augmentation for neurogenic dysfunction, arrange lifelong, risk-based follow-up in adults and children, including renal and upper-tract surveillance. The 2026 EAU neuro-urology guideline gives Strong recommendations for regular upper-tract assessment and urodynamic investigation at regular intervals in high-risk patients; CUA/PUC guidance addresses pediatric surveillance. These are general NLUTD recommendations, not SCLU-specific outcome data.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
