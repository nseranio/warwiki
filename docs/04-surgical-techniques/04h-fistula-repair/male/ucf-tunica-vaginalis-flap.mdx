# Tunica Vaginalis Flap for UCF Repair and Reconstruction

A pedicled tunica vaginalis flap (TVF) supplies vascularized tissue over a urethral repair, especially when local penile coverage is scarred or inadequate. It is used as a waterproofing layer for urethrocutaneous fistula (UCF) repair and hypospadias repair, as urethral substitution material, and as corporal patch material for curvature. The authors of a 2016 systematic review proposed it as the first choice in proximal, repeat and fistula repairs. Pooled series do not establish a universal first-choice operation or guarantee closure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Compare [Local Skin Advancement](/docs/surgical-techniques/04h-fistula-repair/male/ucf-simple-closure-skin-flap), [Double Dartos](/docs/surgical-techniques/04h-fistula-repair/male/ucf-double-dartos), [Scrotal Flaps](/docs/surgical-techniques/04h-fistula-repair/male/ucf-scrotal-flaps), [PATIO](/docs/surgical-techniques/04h-fistula-repair/male/ucf-patio-repair) and the [male fistula atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/04h-fistula-repair/male-fistula).

## Anatomy and selection

The tunica vaginalis is a serous membrane derived from the processus vaginalis and lined by a single layer of flattened mesothelial cells. The visceral layer adheres to the testis and epididymis and is not harvested. The parietal layer lines the inner scrotal wall, separated from the scrotal dartos by loose areolar tissue, and is the layer used for the flap. The flap preserves the testis, epididymis, vas and testicular vessels, and its vascular pedicle is maintained with its fascial attachments. It is usually based superiorly toward the external inguinal ring. The cremasteric artery arises from the inferior epigastric artery and anastomoses with the testicular artery, so the flap has a blood supply independent of the penile vessels.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[21]](#ref21)</sup> This donor tissue is separate from penile dartos, but prior inguinal or scrotal surgery can affect availability, dissection and perfusion.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

Assess fistula anatomy, distal obstruction, associated urethral loss, local tissue quality and the donor side before selecting a flap. Previous orchiopexy or hernia repair is a reason for careful planning rather than an automatically proven absolute contraindication. The 2025 randomized trial excluded those patients; it therefore cannot establish safety or feasibility in that subgroup.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

Keep three uses distinct:

- **Interposition:** tissue covers an already closed urethra to separate suture lines.
- **Substitution urethroplasty:** tunica forms part of the urinary lumen, with different healing and stricture risks.
- **Corporal patching:** tunica covers a ventral corporotomy during curvature correction; it is not urethral lining.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

A free tunica graft has no retained pedicle and should not be described as a vascularized flap.

## Operative principles for UCF coverage

1. **Map and evaluate the urethra.** Identify all fistulas and assess the distal lumen. Landau injected povidone-iodine through the meatus to find every fistula and used calibration or cystoscopy to exclude a distal stricture, and he operated with a microscope. Dye injection and magnification are technique-specific choices, not prerequisites for every repair.
2. **Repair the urinary defect.** Close the fistula or perform the required urethral reconstruction. Landau's series used primary closure in 12 children and an onlay island reconstruction in two.
3. **Expose the donor tissue.** Access may use a scrotal incision or the penoscrotal field after penile degloving, depending on the operation. Protect the cord and testicular structures.
4. **Raise the flap with a viable pedicle.** Mobilize the parietal tunica and supporting fascial tissue to achieve adequate reach without compromising perfusion.
5. **Transfer and secure coverage.** A subcutaneous tunnel may be used. Prevent pedicle compression, twist, traction and excessive bulk around the urethra or glans.
6. **Reassess testicular position and closure.** Return the testis to an appropriate position. Landau fixed the testis to the scrotum, and fixation is included in several described techniques. Confirm recipient and donor perfusion and close without tension.
7. **Choose urinary drainage.** Duration and route follow the underlying repair. The 2–7-day drainage interval in Landau's series is not a universal TVF protocol.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

### Technique variants

- **Tunneled TVF (Routh).** Sixteen boys with UCF after failed tubularized incised plate repair had a tunneled flap. All had a subcoronal fistula, eight had additional shaft fistulas (up to four), and 12 had one to four failed previous closures. Mean operating time was 45 minutes, with no complications and no recurrence at a mean of 18 months.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Traction-assisted dissection (Ochi).** A superficial incision is made 3 to 5 mm around the fistula orifice, stay sutures are placed in the outer edge, and only the epidermis is dissected under traction for 7 to 10 mm, which spares underlying connective tissue and perfusion. The inner skin is trimmed, the fistula is closed with 7/0 absorbable interrupted sutures, and a pedicled external spermatic fascia or TVF flap is brought through a subcutaneous tunnel. The catheter was removed the next day.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Free TVF graft (Aldaqadossi).** The tunica is used as a free intermediate layer with no pedicle, so it is a graft and not a flap. It was used in 45 children with recurrent fistula, at least 6 months after the previous repair.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

Orchiopexy does not guarantee prevention of ascent: it was performed in the Ramez trial, yet three TVF patients developed ascent by 12 months. Pedicle length, tissue handling and the remaining cord anatomy still matter.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Histology and biology

The mesothelial lining of the tunica has been shown to convert to a stratified epithelium in animal and human material. In the rabbit onlay model the mesothelium was replaced by a stratified epithelium resembling urothelium with patent urethras. In a second rabbit study a free tunica patch produced one short stricture in 15 plasties, against two in ten preputial patches. A combined dorsal and ventral tunica graft in rabbits integrated fully by 4 weeks and transformed to a pseudostratified urinary epithelium by 12 weeks. In Zargooshi's adults, biopsy in all 13 patients tested showed stratified epithelium indistinguishable from native urethra.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[22]](#ref22)[[23]](#ref23)</sup> The tissue carries no hair follicles, so intraluminal hair growth is not a concern, unlike scrotal skin. Epithelial coverage is not proof of a clinically superior tissue substitute (see the urethroplasty section below).

## Evidence for repairing an established fistula

| Source | Reported outcome | Important scope |
|---|---|---|
| Landau 2003 | Fourteen children with previously failed UCF repairs; no recurrence or stricture at mean 44 months. | Selected uncontrolled series; no observed testicular complication does not establish zero risk.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Routh 2006 | Sixteen children with UCF after TIP; no recurrence at mean 18 months. | Twelve had previous failed fistula closures; four were undergoing their first fistula repair.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| Muruganandham 2010 | Thirteen patients with large or multiple fistulas received tunneled TVF; no recurrence in the study's follow-up. | Technique allocation depended on size/complexity, so the other treatment groups are not comparable controls.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Ochi 2015 | Thirty-five fistulas in 26 patients; all repaired without recurrence at mean 7.4 years. | Coverage used **external spermatic fascia or TVF**. This is not an exclusively TVF cohort or 35 independent patients.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| Aldaqadossi 2020 | Forty-three of 45 recurrent fistula repairs succeeded initially. | Used a **free tunica vaginalis graft**, not a pedicled flap. The reported clinic schedule is not a measured follow-up duration.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |

The 2023 UCF-repair review reported 94.3% pooled success for tunica vaginalis; heterogeneity and selection preclude a reliable head-to-head ranking. Its authors concluded that tunica vaginalis and scrotal flaps gave the best results but that no technique could be called ideal.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In Fahmy's 2016 review, fistula-closure recurrence was 5/97 with TVF versus 11/90 with dartos (P = .39). The frequently quoted 6.4% versus 18.6% comparison concerns repeat hypospadias cases, a different group; neither comparison establishes causal superiority for every recurrent UCF.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Prevention during primary hypospadias repair

Current EAU pediatric guidance notes that tunica vaginalis may yield better results than dartos and recommends double coverage if dartos is used. Redo reconstruction is individualized. These statements should not be converted into one obligatory flap hierarchy across primary distal, proximal, recurrent and adult fistula surgery.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

Fahmy's 2016 review (51 reports, 4,550 patients) reported fistula rates in primary repair of distal hypospadias of 0.6% with double-layer dartos (5/855), 2.0% with tunica vaginalis (5/244) and 5.1% with single-layer dartos (156/3077, P = 0.004 for double versus single). Single-layer dartos in proximal hypospadias gave 8.8% (9/102). Among repeat cases, fistula incidence was 6.4% with tunica vaginalis (3/47) and 18.6% with dartos (26/140, P = 0.020). On this basis the authors proposed double dartos for distal hypospadias and tunica vaginalis as first choice in proximal, repeat and fistula-repair cases.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Smaller comparative series point the same way. In 49 patients with coronal to penoscrotal hypospadias, fistula occurred in 0 of 29 with a tunica vaginalis wrap and 3 of 20 (15%) with dartos, without magnification.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup> Amukele reported that adding either a dartos or a tunica vaginalis flap lowered the fistula rate in proximal hypospadias from 17% to 1.8%.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

Three 2026 meta-analyses address primary tubularized incised plate repair. A meta-analysis of four randomized trials (209 patients) reported UCF in 9.28% with tunica vaginalis and 30.36% with dartos (risk ratio 0.37, 95% CI 0.19 to 0.70). Its fragility index was 7, and the authors called for more trials.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> A meta-analysis of ten comparative studies reported lower risks of fistula (risk ratio 0.27), wound infection, dehiscence and skin necrosis with tunica vaginalis, and no difference in meatal stenosis, stricture or torsion.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> A network meta-analysis of 22 randomized trials (1,994 children) found that the double dartos flap ranked best for reducing fistula and that no intervention was definitively superior.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup> In a single-center series of 425 distal and midshaft repairs, fistula occurred in 6.7% with and 7.3% without a second layer (P = 0.8), and the authors attributed prevention to the urethral suture more than to the layer.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

In the 2025 Ramez trial, 88 children were randomized and 84 analyzed after losses to follow-up. Fistulas occurred in 2/41 TVF versus 9/43 dartos repairs (P = .029). Median operative time was 145 versus 100 minutes. Three TVF patients developed testicular ascent and one penile torque; those small counts are not universal rates. Dartos was dorsal or ventral according to the surgeon, so this was not specifically a double-dartos comparison.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

The trial's total HOSE score favored TVF, but HOSE includes fistula status; it is not an isolated measure of appearance. Total PPPS did not differ significantly. Small sample size, one center and limited follow-up temper the findings.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

The 2025 Pezzoli review found heterogeneous results in primary distal or midpenile TIP and could not perform a reliable comprehensive meta-analysis. Earlier Tam data combined 38 de-epithelialized preputial flaps with 14 TVFs in the modified group, while Amukele combined dartos and TVF coverage. Their favorable group outcomes cannot be assigned to TVF alone.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

## Substitution urethroplasty: limited and configuration-dependent evidence

Histological epithelial coverage after tunica-based reconstruction is not proof of a clinically superior tissue substitute. Zargooshi's 20-adult hypospadias series used several onlay or tube combinations; 13 underwent biopsy showing stratified epithelial lining. Three patients developed urethral narrowing, with associated meatal problems in two, and required subsequent treatment.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

Results have not been uniform: Joseph and Pérez reported narrowing in three of five evaluable patients in each of two onlay configurations. Liu's 2024 technical report describes pedicled tunica onlay for lichen-sclerosus strictures over a 19-year experience of 86 cases, performed under spinal anesthesia. It does not establish superiority or equivalence to oral mucosa. Such reports should not be treated as a routine alternative pathway for every long stricture.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

In Theodorescu's rabbit experiment, eight tubular replacements contracted while 16 onlay animals available for long-term assessment had patent urethras. This supports a concern about configuration in that model, not a universal human “never tube” rule.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> More directly relevant to staging, Harper's six highly reoperative patients developed substantial fibrosis when tunica was exposed as the dorsal component of a staged urethroplasty. An exposed tunica stage should not be adopted routinely from evidence about covered interposition.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

## Corporal patching for curvature

Tunica flap or free graft has also been used to cover ventral corporal lengthening. In Braga's retrospective series of 38 boys with curvature above 45 degrees, recurrent curvature occurred in one of 23 patients (4.3%) receiving a tunica flap alone and in four of nine (44.4%) receiving lyophilized dura (P = 0.02). Straight erections were documented in 30 of 35 (85.7%) at a median of 5.3 years, and many children had not reached adulthood. Hayashi reported parental observations of straight erections in 15 boys, with operative reassessment in only seven. Neither study proves superiority of vascularized flaps over all free grafts.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

Ritchey reported one recurrent curvature among 19 free-tunica-graft recipients, with follow-up of 1–5 years. This is a different operation and outcome from UCF closure. Assess the degree and source of curvature and plan follow-up through growth. EAU pediatric guidance considers curvature of 30 degrees or more to warrant correction, and a fixed 45-degree threshold from one series is not the sole indication for corporal grafting. The same guidance notes increasing evidence that corporoplasty with a graft should not be combined with urethral graft reconstruction.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

## Where the tunica vaginalis flap fits

The following summarizes the sources above and is an operative framework, not a guideline recommendation.

- **Primary distal hypospadias.** Double-layer dartos gave the lowest fistula rate in the pooled review, and TVF was less often chosen.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Primary midpenile and proximal hypospadias.** Tunica vaginalis gave lower fistula rates than single-layer dartos in comparative series and meta-analyses, at the cost of longer operating time and a small risk of ascent.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[11]](#ref11)[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
- **Recurrent UCF.** Tunica vaginalis is a common choice when penile dartos is scarred, and EAU guidance notes it may yield better results than dartos.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- **Prior inguinal or scrotal surgery.** Consider scrotal dartos or another tissue, since these patients were excluded from the randomized trial.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Severe curvature.** TVF is one option for ventral corporeal lengthening in proximal hypospadias.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### Technical points

- Harvest the parietal layer only and keep the superior pedicle.
- Make a subcutaneous tunnel wide enough to avoid pedicle compression and narrow enough to prevent dead space.
- Fix the testis to the scrotum to limit ascent, as Landau did.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- Offset the urethral, flap and skin closures.
- Use the flap as an onlay and not as a tube.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Zargooshi used the contralateral tunica as a blanket wrap in 15 of 20 patients when more coverage was needed.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Limit external exposure. Tunica used as an exposed dorsal component of a staged urethroplasty fibrosed.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

## Counseling and follow-up

Discuss recurrent fistula, urethral or meatal narrowing, penile tethering or torque, testicular ascent, scrotal hematoma, infection and possible injury to donor structures. Absence of a rare event in a small series is not proof that it cannot occur. Fixation, pedicle planning and atraumatic dissection reduce avoidable problems but do not eliminate risk.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

Follow both the urinary repair and donor site. Evaluate recurrent leakage, a weak stream, penile deviation and altered testicular position. Urinary drainage, activity restrictions and timing of repeat repair should follow tissue recovery and the full reconstruction rather than a single mandatory interval drawn from one series.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
