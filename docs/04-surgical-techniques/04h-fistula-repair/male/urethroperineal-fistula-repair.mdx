# Urethroperineal Fistula Repair

A **urethroperineal fistula** connects the urethra to perineal skin. Establish whether it is an isolated skin tract, an accessory congenital urethral channel, or part of a fistula involving the rectum, an abscess, or a reconstructed urinary tract. These conditions require different operations; rectourethral fistula closure rates and diversion protocols cannot be applied to all of them.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For bowel communication, see [Transperineal RUF Repair](/docs/surgical-techniques/04h-fistula-repair/male/transperineal-ruf-repair) and the [male fistula treatment atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/04h-fistula-repair/male-fistula).

## Etiology and Classification

| Pattern | Consequence for planning |
|---|---|
| **Congenital posterior urethroperineal fistula** | Differentiate it from Y-type urethral duplication and rectourethral fistula before sacrificing any channel. The normal functional urethra must be demonstrated.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Congenital anterior urethrocutaneous fistula** | Assess the distal urethra, meatus, curvature and associated hypospadias; repair is anatomy dependent.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Acquired urethrocutaneous tract** | Look for distal obstruction, urethral stones, infection, prior injury or surgery, and compromised local tissue.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **After transmasculine genital reconstruction** | Define the reconstructed urethra and look for coexisting stricture or vaginal remnant; isolated skin closure may leave the underlying problem untreated.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **Associated bowel disease or rectal communication** | Plan with colorectal surgery. A perineal opening can be the external component of a rectourethral fistula, including in Crohn's disease.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |

The “25 reported cases” description belongs to a 2014 congenital posterior fistula report, not a current incidence estimate. Likewise, pelvic-fracture urethral injury rates do not measure the incidence of urethroperineal fistula.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Diagnosis and Preoperative Evaluation

Urine leaks from a perineal opening. Recurrent infection, pneumaturia or urine passed per rectum suggests a rectal component; in one urorectal series the presenting symptoms were rectal urine leakage (48%), pneumaturia (24%) and recurrent infections (21%).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> A congenital posterior tract typically drains during micturition with otherwise normal voiding.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

Document urinary leakage, voiding symptoms, infections, prior urethral procedures, radiation, bowel symptoms and baseline continence. Examine the opening and surrounding skin, tissue quality and genital anatomy. Painful swelling or purulent drainage requires assessment for an abscess and source control.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

RUG and, when needed, VCUG map a stricture, proximal urethra and fistulous connection. Endoscopy adds information about the internal opening, stones, a stenosis and the bladder outlet; combined antegrade and retrograde assessment is useful in selected posterior injuries. MRI is an adjunct when complex posterior anatomy or associated pelvic pathology remains uncertain, rather than a mandatory test for every cutaneous opening.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

For a congenital posterior tract, radiographic and endoscopic confirmation of a usable orthotopic channel is particularly important. A published misdiagnosis as a rectourethral fistula illustrates why an external opening alone cannot determine the operation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Principles of Repair

1. **Refer early to a surgeon experienced in fistula repair.** In a systematic review of urogenital fistulas, the first attempt at repair was the most likely to succeed.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> In the Guo series of pelvic-fracture urethrorectal fistulas, all 22 patients without a previous repair were cured, compared with 7 of 10 with a previous failed repair.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
2. **Plan diversion for complex or posterior fistulas.** In the urorectal series cited below, patients with posterior fistulas had fecal diversion before repair, with a median interval of 6 months in the Khouri cohort, and most had a suprapubic catheter.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> An isolated urethra-to-skin tract does not by itself need a colostomy.
3. **Interpose vascularized tissue in complex, radiated or recurrent repairs.** The tissue options are listed below.
4. **Avoid overlapping suture lines.** Close the urethra and skin separately and place tissue between them.
5. **Treat distal obstruction.** A concurrent stricture keeps the repair under pressure and is addressed with the fistula. Strictures, fistulas and vaginal remnants often coexist after transmasculine reconstruction.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Congenital UPF: Surgical Approach

In reported congenital posterior fistulas, the dorsal urethra is the normal functional channel. Excision of the ventral accessory channel through a perineal approach is simple and curative, and fulguration of the accessory tract is an alternative.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> This holds only after radiographic and endoscopic confirmation that the dorsal urethra is normal. Excision or fulguration of the ventral tract must not be extrapolated to a Y-type duplication in which that channel supplies necessary drainage. Refer uncertain anatomy to an experienced pediatric reconstructive team. The evidence consists of small series and case reports.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

Congenital anterior fistulas are a separate problem. Depending on the urethral defect and associated abnormalities, repair ranges from local closure to formal urethroplasty, and no single maneuver is universally curative.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Acquired UPF: Surgical Planning

- **Control infection and provide reliable urinary drainage.** An abscess, obstructing calculus or diseased urethral segment may require drainage, debridement or staged reconstruction rather than immediate closure of the skin opening.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Address associated obstruction and tissue loss.** Closure, urethroplasty and any interposition are selected according to the actual defect and available tissue. In reconstructed genital anatomy, coordinate treatment of a stricture or symptomatic remnant with fistula management.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Separate urinary closure from the skin repair when feasible.** Vascularized coverage helps in difficult repairs, but evidence for a particular flap does not transfer between penile UCF, perineal skin fistula and RUF. A dartos turnover flap has been described for anterior urethrocutaneous fistulas after hypospadias repair (nine of ten healed without complication in one series).<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> A three-patient series of recurrent UCF after metoidioplasty described a free rectus fascia interposition graft, which is not a vascularized muscle flap.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Use a bowel-fistula pathway when the rectum is involved.** Fecal diversion and its timing are determined by the bowel injury, contamination and planned repair.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For extensive anterior urethral disease, the reconstruction discussion may include [perineal urethrostomy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/blandy-perineal-urethrostomy). It is an accepted endpoint when reconstruction is not feasible or not wanted. Its suitability depends on the proximal urethra and outlet, and it is not a solution for radiation-damaged posterior fistulas.

### Evidence from urethrorectal fistula repair

Most operative data for posterior perineal fistulas come from urethrorectal and rectourethral fistula series. These fistulas communicate with bowel, and the results cannot be pooled with isolated urethra-to-skin tracts. They are the closest published guide to technique when a perineal opening is part of a bowel fistula.

| Series | Population and approach | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Guo 2017 | 32 pelvic-fracture urethral defects with urethrorectal fistula; transperineal urethroplasty with gracilis interposition, with or without inferior pubectomy | 29/32 (91%) at mean 33 months. Perineal anastomosis with corporeal separation succeeded in 17/18; with inferior pubectomy, 12/14.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| Xu 2010 | 31 traumatic posterior urethral strictures with urethrorectal fistula; perineal (4), inferior pubectomy (18), transpubic-perineal (9) | One-stage success in 4/4, 16/18 and 7/9 respectively. Bulbospongiosus and dartos flaps were interposed in 22 patients.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| Hou 2025 | 36 men with traumatic posterior urethral stenosis and urethrorectal fistula or anterior rectal wall weakness; bulbospongiosus flap | Overall success 94.4% at mean 31 months. Functional scores did not differ from a matched group without the flap.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> |
| Wang 2024 | 40 pelvic-fracture urethrorectal fistulas; transperineal anastomotic urethroplasty with proximal transection | 36/40 (90%) at median 45 months. Fecal diversion preceded urethroplasty in all.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| Kaufman 2016 | 98 radiated or non-radiated rectourethral fistulas; anterior transperineal repair, muscle flap, selective buccal graft onlay | Closure with one operation in 48/49 non-radiated (98%) and 42/49 radiated (86%). Ninety-day complications were 2% and 24%.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |

### Tissue interposition options

- **Gracilis muscle.** It is the flap used most often in complex and radiated urorectal fistulas. It was placed in 21 of 23 men (91%) in the Khouri cohort.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Bulbospongiosus muscle.** Described for traumatic posterior urethral stenosis with urethrorectal fistula.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- **Dartos pedicle flap.** Used for anterior urethrocutaneous fistulas, particularly after hypospadias repair.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Rectus abdominis or VRAM.** Reserved for the most complex defects. In 12 patients with recurrent complex urogenital fistulas, gracilis flaps needed less operating time than VRAM flaps (3 h 11 min against 5 h 31 min).<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- **Rectus fascia graft.** Described for recurrent UCF after metoidioplasty.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Buccal mucosal graft.** Used when urethral tissue is insufficient for primary anastomosis. In the Kaufman series it was applied selectively as an onlay.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- **Fibrin sealant patch (TachoSil).** Direct transperineal repair reinforced with the patch closed four of five fistulas after radical prostatectomy at median 35.5 months.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

### Concurrent urethroplasty

Posterior urethral stenosis or defect may accompany the fistula. In the Khouri series, 23 of 130 men (18%) had simultaneous posterior urethroplasty. It was an anastomotic repair in 18 (78%) and a buccal mucosal graft in 5 (22%). Twenty men (87%) had fistula closure at median 55.7 months.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

## Outcomes and Follow-Up

Counsel using studies that match the patient's anatomy and etiology. No pooled success estimate applies to all urethroperineal fistulas. Published RUF series report closure, bowel restoration, continence and urethral patency as separate outcomes; use those results when counseling patients with rectal involvement. In the Kaufman series, gastrointestinal continuity was restored in 45/48 non-radiated and 30/46 radiated patients.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

In a systematic review of urogenital fistulas of all types, spontaneous closure with catheter drainage occurred in up to 15%, and conservative management was more likely to succeed in non-radiated fistulas.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> In 29 men after prostate cancer treatment who had open urorectal fistula repair, the 5-year recurrence-free survival was 96% and the 5-year survival free of any disease-related reintervention was 75%. Patient satisfaction was high and decisional regret negligible.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

Catheter duration and imaging follow the actual urethral repair. When urethroplasty is performed, EAU guidance supports assessing for extravasation before catheter removal, and a significant persistent leak warrants continued drainage and reassessment. Prolonged antibiotics solely because a catheter remains in place have not shown benefit as routine urethroplasty prophylaxis. Treat an established infection according to cultures and its clinical context.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

After anterior urethroplasty, a survey of 142 reconstructive surgeons found a urethral catheter left for 2 to 3 weeks by 58.5% and for 3 to 4 weeks by 30.1%, contrast imaging at catheter removal by 67.6%, and objective follow-up (uroflowmetry, postvoid residual, cystoscopy) at 2 to 6 months. This describes practice, not a protocol.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> In the Samplaski series of transperineal gracilis repairs, suprapubic catheters were removed after 6 weeks and diversion was reversed at a median of 17.5 weeks (range 12 to 28).<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

Follow-up assesses recurrent leakage, wound healing, infection, emptying, urethral patency and continence. Bowel continuity is relevant only when a bowel fistula or diversion was part of the treatment.

### Complications

- **Urinary incontinence** is the most common long-term sequela after complex urorectal repair. It was reported by 14 of 23 men (61%) in the Khouri series, of whom 7 (30% of the cohort) had an artificial urinary sphincter, and by 9 of 12 (75%) in the Samplaski series, with 2 reporting it most of the time.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
- **Erectile dysfunction** was reported in 75% after repair of pelvic-fracture urethrorectal fistulas.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **Recurrent fistula** occurred in 1 of 13 (Samplaski), 3 of 23 (Khouri) and 1 of 32 (Guo) after gracilis repairs, in populations that differ by etiology and prior repair.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
- **Urethral stricture** at the anastomosis was managed with internal urethrotomy or repeat urethroplasty in the Guo and Xu series.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Fecal incontinence** occurred in 3 of 13 in the Samplaski series; fecal outcomes were generally better than urinary outcomes.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
- **Wound complications** in the Wang series were hematoma, epididymo-orchitis, infection, delayed healing and numbness.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
