# Simple Closure with Skin Advancement Flap for Urethrocutaneous Fistula

The **simple closure with skin advancement flap** technique for urethrocutaneous fistula (UCF) repair achieves a **95.7% success rate** for simple fistulas, significantly outperforming simple closure alone (71–79%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The landmark series by **Santangelo, Rushton, and Belman** at Children's National Medical Center — spanning 99 patients and 94 repairs — remains the most comprehensive outcome analysis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For alternative UCF approaches see the [male fistula treatment atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/04h-fistula-repair/male-fistula).

---

## Concept and Rationale

Two-layer coverage: the urethral defect is closed first, then a second tissue layer (de-epithelialized or full-thickness skin advancement flap) is placed over the urethral closure to **separate the suture lines** and provide a vascularized waterproofing barrier.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

This addresses the primary cause of recurrence in simple closure — **overlapping suture lines** between urethral and skin closures, which create a direct pathway for fistula recurrence.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

---

## Indications

- **Small-to-moderate fistulas** (typically ≤ 4 mm) at any penile location<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Coronal fistulas** — particularly well-suited<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Midshaft fistulas** — best suited for the de-epithelialized "pants over vest" variant<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Both **first-time and recurrent** fistulas, provided adequate local tissue is available<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Optimal** for large (&gt; 4 mm) and coronal fistulas, where simple closure alone has the highest recurrence — 78% of recurrences were coronal in one series<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Surgical Technique

### Variant 1 — De-Epithelialized "Pants Over Vest" Flap (preferred for midshaft)

1. **Fistula excision** — circumscribing incision; dissect tract to its base; excise completely<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
2. **Urethral layer ("vest") closure** — invert inner skin edges and close with fine absorbable suture (6-0 or 7-0) using an inverting subcuticular stitch<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
3. **De-epithelialization** — mark a flap of adjacent penile skin; remove epithelium while preserving underlying dermis and dartos fascia; **keep base attached (hinged)** to maintain vascular pedicle<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
4. **Flap turnover ("pants")** — turn the de-epithelialized flap over the urethral closure; secure with fine absorbable suture; completely covers the urethral suture line<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
5. **Skin closure** — advance remaining penile skin and close over the de-epithelialized flap as the third layer; **final skin suture line offset from urethral closure**<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

**Belman midshaft series:** **100% success in 19 midshaft fistulas**; 68% performed as nondiverted outpatient procedures.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

### Variant 2 — Full-Thickness Skin Advancement Flap (preferred for coronal)

1. **Fistula excision** — circumscribe and excise tract to urethral base<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
2. **Urethral closure** — close defect with fine absorbable suture in a single layer<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
3. **Skin advancement** — mobilize a full-thickness skin flap from adjacent penile shaft; advance distally over the urethral closure; suture in place. Advancement ensures the skin suture line does not overlie the urethral closure<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
4. **Tension-free closure** — if tension is present, a **longitudinal relaxing incision** 0.5–1.5 cm from the wound edge heals secondarily in 7–21 d<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

**Belman coronal series:** **10/11 (91%) success in coronal fistulas**.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Technical Pearls

- **Exclude distal obstruction before any repair** — calibrate the meatus ± cystoscopy. Unrecognized meatal stenosis or stricture is a major cause of recurrence<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Offset suture lines** — the critical principle. The interposed de-epithelialized tissue or advancement flap achieves this separation<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Suture material** — fine absorbable monofilament (6-0 or 7-0 polyglactin / polyglycolic acid)<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Minimize cautery** — excessive electrocautery devascularizes tissue and promotes recurrence<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Adequate tissue mobilization** — tension is the enemy; generous undermining of adjacent tissue is essential<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Tourniquet use** — penile tourniquet improves visualization during dissection; release before final closure to assess hemostasis and perfusion

---

## Postoperative Management

A major advantage: **stents and catheters generally not required** for simple repairs.

- Santangelo / Belman: stents not left postoperatively in simple cases; repairs routinely performed as **outpatient procedures**<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- Geltzeiler & Belman: 68% midshaft and 91% coronal repairs as **nondiverted outpatient procedures**<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- For **large (&gt; 4 mm) or multiple fistulas**, suprapubic diversion is recommended — recurrence **16% with diversion vs 45% without**<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- When a catheter is used, indwelling urethral catheter for **3–4 d**<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- Prophylactic antibiotics commonly given perioperatively (limited evidence for benefit)
- Anticholinergics (oxybutynin) to reduce bladder spasms if a catheter is in place

## Outcomes

| Technique | n | Success | Fistula location |
|---|---|---|---|
| **De-epithelialized "pants over vest" (midshaft)** | 19 | **100%** | Midshaft<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Skin advancement flap (coronal)** | 11 | **91%** | Coronal<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **De-epithelialized or full-thickness advancement (simple)** | 69 | **95.7%** | Mixed<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **De-epithelialized or full-thickness advancement (all, incl. complex)** | 94 | **93.6%** | Mixed<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **"Pants over vest" layered repair (Cimador 2003)** | 32 | **94%** first attempt | Shaft<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Simple closure alone (comparator)** | 39–42 | 71–74% | Mixed<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |

Layered repair (94%) vs simple closure (74%) — **statistically significant**.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## De-Epithelialized Turnover Dartos Flap — Related Variant

**Ahuja modification** — combines de-epithelialization with a dartos-based turnover flap. Circumscribe fistula, close urethral layer with inverting sutures, raise a de-epithelialized flap with underlying dartos fascia on a hinged pedicle, turn over the closure. Long skin flap from shaft or scrotum is then approximated over this layer.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

**100% success in 10 patients (ages 4–25).** Particularly useful when local dartos tissue is available and well-vascularized.

## Comparison with Other Waterproofing Techniques

| Technique | Pooled success | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Skin-based flaps (heterogeneous)** | 54.5% pooled across all studies (Choudhury 2023 meta) | Heavily influenced by older series<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **De-epithelialized advancement flap (refined Belman / Santangelo)** | **&gt; 90%** with meticulous technique | First-time, small-to-moderate fistulas<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Tunica vaginalis flap** | **94.3%** | Recurrent or complex fistulas; scarred local tissue<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Scrotal dartos flap** | **94.6%** | Mid-shaft / proximal fistulas with scarred local tissue<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **PATIO repair** | ~83% | Coronal / subcoronal fistulas<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |

The refined de-epithelialized advancement flap technique (Belman / Santangelo) consistently achieves **&gt; 90%** when performed with meticulous technique.

---

## When to Choose This Technique vs Alternatives

- **Simple closure + skin advancement flap** — first-time, small-to-moderate fistulas with adequate local penile skin, particularly **coronal and midshaft** locations<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Tunica vaginalis flap** — recurrent fistulas or fibrotic local penile tissue from prior surgery<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Scrotal dartos flap** — mid-shaft and proximal fistulas with scarred local tissue<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **PATIO repair** — technically straightforward option for coronal / subcoronal fistulas (~83%)<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

---

## Key Takeaways

- **95.7% success** for simple fistulas with meticulous technique (Santangelo / Belman).
- Two technique variants: **"pants over vest" de-epithelialized flap for midshaft** (100% in Belman), **full-thickness advancement flap for coronal** (91% in Belman).
- **Stents / diversion not routinely required** for simple repairs — most performed as outpatient procedures.
- **Exclude distal obstruction** before any repair; **offset suture lines**; **minimize cautery**; **mobilize generously for tension-free closure**.
- For large (&gt; 4 mm) or multiple fistulas: suprapubic diversion reduces recurrence from 45% to 16%.
