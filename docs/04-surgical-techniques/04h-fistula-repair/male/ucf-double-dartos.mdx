# Double Dartos Flap for Urethral Coverage and UCF Repair

A **double dartos flap** provides two vascularized tissue layers over a urethral suture line. Most comparative evidence concerns prevention of urethrocutaneous fistula (UCF) during primary pediatric hypospadias repair, especially distal tubularized incised plate urethroplasty (TIP). Those low fistula rates should not be presented as the success rate of repairing an established, recurrent or adult fistula.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

For established fistulas, compare [Simple Closure and Skin Advancement](/docs/surgical-techniques/04h-fistula-repair/male/ucf-simple-closure-skin-flap), [Tunica Vaginalis Flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04h-fistula-repair/male/ucf-tunica-vaginalis-flap), [Scrotal Flaps](/docs/surgical-techniques/04h-fistula-repair/male/ucf-scrotal-flaps) and [PATIO Repair](/docs/surgical-techniques/04h-fistula-repair/male/ucf-patio-repair). See the [urethral reconstruction atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/urethral-reconstruction) when a fistula accompanies stricture, breakdown or a larger urethral defect.

## Selection

Dartos coverage is useful when enough healthy local tissue can be mobilized without compromising penile skin perfusion, constricting the neourethra or placing the glans closure under tension. Prior circumcision, scarring, previous flap harvest and ventral skin deficiency affect the available options; the number of layers alone does not determine the quality of a repair.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

The current EAU pediatric hypospadias chapter states that tunica vaginalis may yield better results than dartos, and recommends a double layer when dartos is used. This is guidance about urethroplasty coverage, not a declaration that double dartos is the best repair for every UCF. Redo hypospadias reconstruction remains individualized.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

Before closing an established fistula, evaluate the distal urethra and meatus, tissue quality, associated dehiscence and the need for additional urethral reconstruction. Healthy local dartos may be suitable; extensive scarring or deficient tissue may favor another vascularized layer. A recurrent or proximal fistula is not an automatic contraindication to dartos, but primary distal TIP results are an unreliable estimate of its outcome in that setting.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Operative principles

Dorsal preputial and ventral dartos techniques differ. A common dorsal approach harvests a vascularized flap during penile degloving, divides it into two adequately perfused portions and brings them over the urethral closure from opposite sides. Ventral dartos can also be configured as double coverage when tissue permits.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

Three published variants illustrate the range.

- **Dorsal double dartos (Kamal).** During TIP urethroplasty the dissected dorsal dartos flap is bisected vertically into two pedicled wings. Each wing is rotated laterally from either side of the glans to cover the neourethra ventrally in two overlapping layers. The symmetric rotation is the stated reason that glanular torsion did not occur in the double-flap group.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Ventral dartos (Cimador).** A single or double ventral dartos flap is raised from the ventral penile skin instead of a dorsal preputial flap, which avoids dorsal dissection. In this randomized comparison the double ventral flap had the lowest complication rate, and the authors proposed it as first-line coverage for distal urethroplasty.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Double dorsal preputial (Mekki).** The dorsal preputial dartos is harvested as a broad flap and divided into two flaps that cover the neourethra in two layers.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

1. **Preserve blood supply.** Separate dartos from the skin carefully, maintaining viable pedicles and adequate skin vascularity. Do not create two layers by excessively thinning or devascularizing an inadequate flap.
2. **Complete the urethral repair.** Confirm that the reconstructed lumen and underlying closure are satisfactory before coverage. Interposition does not compensate for obstruction or a poorly constructed urethroplasty.
3. **Transfer without twist or traction.** Position each component to cover the relevant urethral suture line. Avoid tethering that rotates the penis or compromises the pedicle.
4. **Secure separate coverage.** Fix the layers gently with fine absorbable sutures as appropriate, without narrowing the urethra or creating excessive bulk beneath the glans.
5. **Reassess the entire closure.** Confirm penile alignment, skin and flap perfusion, and tension-free glans and skin approximation. If the available dartos cannot meet these goals, reconsider the coverage choice.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

This is a principles outline, not a substitute for the selected urethroplasty or fistula-closure technique. “Double dartos coverage” and a “two-layer urethral closure” describe different structures.

## Evidence for preventing a first postoperative fistula

| Source | Finding | Important limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Fahmy 2016 systematic review | Distal-hypospadias series reported fistula in 5/855 (0.6%) with double dartos and 156/3,077 (5.1%) with single dartos (P = 0.004), and 5/244 (2.0%) with tunica vaginalis. Single-layer dartos in proximal hypospadias: 9/102 (8.8%). | These are pooled groups from heterogeneous studies, not one randomized comparison. Selection, technique and follow-up differ.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| Kamal 2005 | Ninety-six TIP repairs: two fistulas among 54 single-flap repairs and none among 42 double-flap repairs. | Mild glanular torsion occurred in 49 of 54 and moderate torsion in 5 of 54 single-flap patients, and in none of the double-flap group. That frequency is specific to this single-flap technique and is not the incidence of torsion after every single-dartos repair.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| Cimador 2013 | One hundred thirty children were randomized to dorsal or ventral coverage strategies, with several variants used within these groups; only one fistula was reported after double ventral coverage. Overall fistula rate was 14.9%, distributed about equally between groups; glans dehiscence occurred in 5, four of them after a single ventral flap. | The abstract does not provide that subgroup's denominator or establish a separately randomized comparison of every flap variant.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| Mekki 2024 | A chronological single-surgeon series compared 51 earlier single-dartos with 54 later double-dartos repairs; no fistula was reported in the double-dartos group. | Nonrandomized, with experience and time-period confounding. The article gives inconsistent single-group fistula counts, so a precise comparative effect is not reproduced here. Neither group had penile torsion. The abstract reports 5 fistulas in the single-flap group and none in the double-flap group (P = 0.024).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| Pezzoli 2025 systematic review | Forty studies of primary distal or midpenile TIP generally supported vascularized coverage. Double-layer coverage, especially double dorsal dartos, gave fistula rates of 0% to 12% in distal hypospadias. In midpenile hypospadias, single-layer dorsal dartos gave 12.5% to 36.4% (tunica vaginalis 0% to 3.1%) and double-layer coverage reduced fistula to under 5%. | Redo repairs and established UCF repair were excluded; heterogeneity prevented a reliable comprehensive meta-analysis. Meatal stenosis, skin viability and glans closure remain relevant.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |

The historical Cheng series of 514 primary Snodgrass repairs by six surgeons combined a two-layer urethral closure with vascularized dartos coverage: no fistulas in 414 distal cases and 3 in 100 midshaft or proximal cases, with an overall complication rate under 1%. Its favorable results cannot isolate the benefit of double dartos, and the phrase “two layers” should not be reassigned to the flap.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

In Braga's 153 consecutive distal TIP repairs by one surgeon, absence of dartos coverage was associated with a higher fistula rate after adjustment for stenting. Without a stent the rate was 37% without dartos and 9% with it (P = 0.002; OR 0.16); with a stent it was 25% and 2% (P = 0.12). Stenting modified the association.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

### Tunica vaginalis comparison

A 2025 randomized study allocated 88 children to dartos or tunica vaginalis coverage; 84 were analyzed after four losses. Fistulas occurred in 9/43 dartos versus 2/41 tunica vaginalis repairs at the reported assessments. Fistula rates were 20.9% and 4.9% (P = 0.029). Median operative time was 100 minutes for dartos and 145 for tunica vaginalis, one tunica vaginalis patient had penile torque, and three developed testicular ascent (P = 0.071). Dartos could be dorsal or ventral according to the surgeon; this was not a specifically double-dartos-versus-tunica trial. Its small sample and limited follow-up prevent a universal ranking of coverage options.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

The 2016 review by Fahmy also compared coverage in repeat repairs: fistula occurred in 3/47 (6.4%) with tunica vaginalis and 26/140 (18.6%) with dartos (P = 0.020). Its authors concluded that a double dartos layer should be considered for all distal hypospadias and that tunica vaginalis should be the first choice in proximal, repeat and fistula-repair cases.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Evidence for closing an established fistula

The 2023 review of UCF repairs included 2,886 patients from 71 studies. It reported 81% success for double dartos, with different crude proportions for other techniques. These estimates concern fistula repair, unlike the much lower fistula incidence after selected primary distal TIP repairs. Differences between the pooled techniques cannot establish causal superiority because patient selection and reconstructive complexity vary.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

Similarly, the 2016 review's comparison of dartos with tunica vaginalis for recurrence after fistula closure did not demonstrate a statistically significant difference: 11/90 and 5/97, respectively (P = .39). The review's success rates for other techniques were 94.3% for tunica vaginalis, 94.6% for scrotal flaps, 78.8% for single dartos and 93.5% for PATIO, and its authors did not label any technique ideal.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> The 2016 comparison does not prove equivalence. A healthy, appropriately selected flap and correction of associated urethral problems matter more than applying a pooled percentage to an individual patient.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Scrotal dartos as a related technique

A scrotal dartos flap is harvested from the scrotal wall rather than the penile shaft, so it brings tissue from outside the scarred penile field. Muruganandham used a flip flap with scrotal dartos wrapping for 2 to 4 mm fistulas: 2 of 21 recurred, with no scrotal or testicular complications. Churchill described the flap as fibroadipose tissue between the scrotal skin and tunica vaginalis with its pedicle at the penoscrotal angle, reaching the distal shaft without tension; in 8 children, mostly repeat hypospadias repairs, cosmesis was rated "excellent" in 5 of 6 evaluable repairs and no fistula occurred after repeat hypospadias surgery, but one fistula developed after staged repair for anterior urethral valves. In Shankar's 113 children, recurrent fistulas were covered with tunica vaginalis or scrotal dartos from the third repair onward, with success in five of six.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

## Follow-up and counseling

Counsel about recurrent fistula, meatal or urethral narrowing, skin or glans breakdown, torsion and the possibility of further reconstruction. Double coverage does not eliminate these complications. Drainage, dressings and activity restrictions follow the underlying repair and local protocol; there is no universal catheter duration established by the double-dartos evidence.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

Assess healing and the urinary stream, with uroflowmetry and further investigation when age, symptoms or the reconstruction warrant them. Late obstruction and other complications can arise after hypospadias surgery, so early fistula closure alone is an incomplete outcome.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
