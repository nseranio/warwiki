# Robotic-Assisted VVF Repair

Robotic-assisted vesicovaginal fistula (VVF) repair has been performed since **2005** and is now supported by the **EAU Robotic Urology Section (ERUS) consensus** as a safe technique with excellent success rates and very low morbidity.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The most recent systematic review (Tavares 2026, 14 studies, 206 patients) reports **97% success** (recurrence 2.9%), combined mean operative time **169 ± 70 min**, blood loss **14–90 mL**, overall complication rate **4.4%** (major 0.97%), and hospital stay under 5 days.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

This page is the platform-level overview. For approach-specific atlases see [Extravesical VVF Repair](/docs/surgical-techniques/04h-fistula-repair/female/extravesical-vvf-repair) and [O'Conor (Transvesical) VVF Repair](/docs/surgical-techniques/04h-fistula-repair/female/oconor-vvf-repair). For the broader robotic platform context see [Robotic Reconstructive Applications](/docs/foundations/tools/technology/robotics/reconstructive-applications) and [Single-Port Robotics](/docs/foundations/tools/technology/robotics/single-port).

## EAU / ERUS Consensus (2020)

The most authoritative guidance:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### Preoperative

- Establish the **size, number, location, and etiology** of the VVF.
- Preoperative marking of the fistula by **guidewire or ureteral catheter**.
- Placement of a **protective ureteral JJ stent**.

### Approach

- An **extravesical robotic approach usually provides a good anatomic view** for adequate and wide dissection of the vesicovaginal space, and bladder + vaginal mobilization.
- **Careful sharp dissection** of fistula edges.
- **Tension-free bladder closure** is of utmost importance.
- **Tissue interposition seems to be beneficial**.

### Postop

- **Indwelling catheter ~10 days**.

### Bottom line

Robotic assistance facilitates dissection of the vesicovaginal space, harvesting of a well-vascularized tissue flap, and tension-free closure of the bladder with **low morbidity** for surgery in the deep pelvis with delicate anatomical structures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Dayan-Schwartz 12-Step Technique (2026)

A systematic 12-step robotic **extravesical** repair using the da Vinci system, demonstrated even in a **post-oncologic-surgery patient on carboplatin / paclitaxel / dostarlimab** for stage IIIC1 endometrial carcinoma. Repair was conducted **6 weeks after primary surgery and adjuvant chemo+immunotherapy**, during a treatment-free interval.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

1. **Cystoscopy and fistula identification.**
2. **Ureteral catheterization / stenting.**
3. **Patient positioning and port placement** — dorsal lithotomy; transperitoneal robotic ports.
4. **Vaginal probe placement** — tents up the vaginal cuff for identification from above.
5. **Adhesiolysis.**
6. **Peritoneal incision and vesicovaginal-space entry** — over the posterior bladder wall.
7. **Wide vesicovaginal-space dissection** — well beyond fistula margins.
8. **Fistula-tract excision** — circumscribe and sharply excise with surrounding fibrotic tissue.
9. **Vaginal-wall closure** — absorbable sutures.
10. **Tissue interposition** — vascularized flap between suture lines.
11. **Bladder closure** — layered, tension-free, absorbable.
12. **Integrity testing and catheter placement** — retrograde dye fill; Foley placed.

Outcome: completed entirely robotically; nephrostomies removed; Foley 14 d; **discharged POD 1**; closure confirmed at 6 mo with no recurrence, voiding dysfunction, or need for additional diversion.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Other Stepwise Robotic Techniques

### Sundaram & Hemal 2006 — first robotic VVF series (transvesical, 10 steps)

n = 5; **100% success**; mean OR 233 min (range 150–330); EBL minimal:<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

1. Vaginoscopy.
2. Cystoscopy.
3. Bilateral ureteral catheterization.
4. Robotic port placement.
5. Peritoneoscopy.
6. Adhesiolysis.
7. Cystotomy in **"reverse tennis racquet"** fashion encircling the fistula.
8. Excision and freshening of fistula margins after complete bladder-vagina separation.
9. Vaginal opening closed **horizontally**, bladder **vertically** with interrupted Vicryl.
10. Omental interposition between suture lines.

### Bora 2017 — modified transvesical (4-port limited cystotomy)

n = 30; **93.3% success**; mean OR 133 ± 48 min; median EBL 50 mL; no suprapubic catheter; median LOS 7.5 d; 36.7% complex VVFs. Used **V-Loc barbed sutures** for bladder closure.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

### Nóbrega 2020 — robotic repair after radiation (6-step)

Patient with prior pelvic radiation and multiple abdominal surgeries — traditionally managed by laparotomy:<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

1. Careful bowel adhesiolysis (multiple intestinal segments).
2. Cystotomy with vaginal-probe guidance.
3. Fistulectomy by monopolar scissors after ureteral-orifice visualization.
4. Dissection of the **retropubic space of Retzius** to relieve bladder tension.
5. Transverse coaptation of vaginal-side raw surfaces; bladder closed in 2 layers with minimal tension.
6. Transurethral methylene blue instillation.

### Occhino & Linder 2019 — robotic transvesical mini-cystotomy salvage

Used after two prior failed repairs (one vaginal, one abdominal with bladder bivalving and omental flap):<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

1. Port placement.
2. Mobilization of the **space of Retzius**.
3. Intentional **mini-cystotomy** (smaller than full bivalving).
4. Camera and working arms advanced **into the bladder**.
5. Fistula identified and circumferentially mobilized.
6. Three-layer absorbable closure with care to avoid the ureters.

Cystogram at 4 wk confirmed resolution. Particularly useful when previous surgical planes have been used in prior failed repairs.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Robotic vs Open vs Laparoscopic

The most direct comparison — Wang 2026, retrospective cohort of 78 transabdominal VVF repairs at a single tertiary center:<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

| Parameter | Open (n=31) | Lap (n=31) | Robotic (n=16) | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| OR time | Longest | Intermediate | Shortest | 0.014 |
| Blood loss | Greatest | Intermediate | Least | 0.001 |
| Success | Comparable | Comparable | Comparable | NS |
| Interposition flap use | Most frequent | Intermediate | Least frequent | — |
| Surgical approach as predictor of success | No | No | No | NS |

Surgical approach was **not** an independent predictor of closure rates; MIS was associated with better perioperative outcomes. Strongest predictors of failure: **radiation-induced fistulae** (p = 0.031) and **trigonal location** (32% failure vs 6% non-trigonal; p = 0.004).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

### Robotic-specific advantages

**Over open**

- Reduced OR time (p = 0.014) and blood loss (p \<0.05).<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- Shorter LOS (median 1 d in several robotic series vs 3–7 d open).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- 3D HD magnified deep-pelvic view; less postop pain; faster recovery.
- No suprapubic catheter needed in many robotic series.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Comparable or superior success: 97% robotic vs 93.95% open in a 25-yr comparative study.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

**Over laparoscopic**

- **Wristed (EndoWrist) instruments** — overcome rigid-laparoscopic limitations for deep-pelvic suturing.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Tremor filtration** — critical for precise suturing in confined space.
- Improved ergonomics; reduced surgeon fatigue.
- Significantly **shorter learning curve** for intracorporeal suturing.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- 3D vs 2D laparoscopic view.

### Limitations

- **Cost** — significantly higher than lap or open.
- **Availability** — not universal, particularly in LMICs where obstetric VVF is most prevalent.
- **No haptic feedback** (partially compensated visually).
- **Setup / docking time**.
- **Limited evidence base** — no RCTs; all data from retrospective case series.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Concurrent Procedures — the "One-Stop" Operation

A major robotic advantage. Chandna 2020 — 73 robotic VVF repairs (33 complex) — documented:<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

- **Boari flap ureteric reimplantation** for concomitant ureteric stricture (n = 2).
- **Ureteric neocystostomy** for ureteral involvement (n = 1).
- **Burch colposuspension** for concurrent SUI (n = 1).
- **Vaginal-flap incorporation** — novel technique for tissue-deficient fistulae (n = 1).

Agrawal 2015 — 3 ureteral reimplantations and 1 planned sigmoid colectomy concurrently with robotic VVF repair, with longer OR (up to 457 min) but **100% success** and median LOS 1 d.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> Laparoscopic Boari-flap ureteroneocystostomy has also been combined with VVF repair for concomitant vesicovaginal and ureterovaginal fistulae.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

## Suture Techniques

### V-Loc barbed sutures

- **Self-anchoring**; eliminates intracorporeal knot-tying; consistent tension; reduces OR time.
- Bora 2017 used V-Loc for bladder closure in 30-patient robotic series (93.3% success).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Wang used 2-0 V-Loc for single-layer through-and-through cystotomy closure.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>
- Javali used 3-0 V-Loc for single-layer bladder closure in a simplified laparoscopic approach (n = 22, 100% success, mean OR 75 min).<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
- **Caution.** More long-term data needed; concern that exposed barbs may nidus stones if intravesical.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### Standard

Interrupted or running 3-0 Vicryl remains the most commonly used material across robotic series.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

## Interposition Flap Options in Robotic Repair

| Flap | Description | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Omental** | Pedicled flap from greater omentum (gastroepiploic) | Large, well-vascularized; excellent bulk; most commonly used | Mobilization from transverse colon; ileus risk; unavailable after omentectomy |
| **Peritoneal** | Pelvic peritoneum harvested as a flap | Minimal morbidity; created robotically; no extra dissection | Less bulk than omentum |
| **Rainbow-shaped peritoneal (Yang)** | Crescent-shaped flap from pelvic sidewall | **100%** in 15 patients; avoids omental harvest; OR 137 min<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> | Single-center data |
| **Sigmoid epiploicae** | Epiploic appendages of sigmoid colon | Readily available; minimal dissection | Small volume; limited data |
| **Bladder adventitia** | Adventitial layer of bladder wall | No additional harvest | Minimal bulk |
| **TachoSil** | Collagen-fibrinogen-thrombin patch (4 × 4 cm × 2 layers) | No tissue harvest; straightforward; **100%** in 16 patients<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> | Synthetic; cost; limited data |
| **No flap** | Three-layer closure without interposition | Simplest, shortest OR; **98%** in Miklos series | No additional barrier |

## Robotic Repair for Complex and Recurrent VVFs

### Complex VVFs — Chandna 2020<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

- **33 complex VVFs** of 73 total robotic — the **largest series of complex robotic VVF repairs**.
- 63.1% recurrent; 90% supratrigonal; 3 multiple fistulae.
- **93.9% success** (31/33); 2 recurrences requiring further repair.
- Median follow-up 35 mo.
- Concurrent procedures performed in several patients (Boari flap, Burch, ureteral reimplant).
- Authors framed robotic repair as a **"one-stop procedure"** for complex VVFs.

### Recurrent VVFs

- The Tavares SR identified two case series specifically on recurrent VVF reporting **100% success with no complications or recurrences**, low blood loss, and shorter OR — supporting safety and efficacy in complex cases.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- Multi-institutional Kidd 2021 — 4/22 (18%) had prior failed repair attempts; overall **91% success**.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

## Learning Curve and Practical Considerations

- The steep learning curve of laparoscopic intracorporeal suturing — the principal limitation of laparoscopic VVF repair — is **significantly mitigated** by robotic assistance through wristed instruments, 3D visualization, and tremor filtration.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- For VVF repair generally (any approach), a learning curve of **≥20 cases** is needed to achieve \>80% success.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- The best chance of success is the **first surgical attempt** with an approach the surgeon is familiar with — supersedes platform choice.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- VVF is a rare condition in developed countries — multi-institutional collaboration and referral to high-volume centers are recommended.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Setup

- **4-port transperitoneal**: 12-mm camera (umbilical / supraumbilical) + three 8-mm robotic arms ± 12-mm assistant port.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **da Vinci Xi** allows more flexible port placement than Si.
- **Dorsal lithotomy** + Allen stirrups + steep **Trendelenburg** — enables simultaneous abdominal and vaginal access.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
