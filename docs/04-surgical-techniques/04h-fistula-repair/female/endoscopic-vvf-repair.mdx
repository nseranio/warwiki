# Endoscopic Management of Vesicovaginal Fistula

Endoscopic management of vesicovaginal fistula (VVF) covers a spectrum of minimally invasive, endoscopy-guided techniques, from purely cystoscopic interventions (fulguration, laser ablation, tissue-adhesive injection) for small fistulae to advanced laparoendoscopic and transvesicoscopic suture repairs for more complex cases.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

For the underlying clinical evaluation, classification, and decision algorithm, see the [vesicovaginal fistula clinical page](/docs/clinical-conditions/03f-fistulas/in-females/vesicovaginal). For repair-route selection across the female-fistula spectrum, see the [Female Fistula Repair atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/04h-fistula-repair/female-fistula).

---

## Cystoscopy in Diagnosis and Preoperative Planning

Cystoscopy in VVF evaluation allows direct visualization of the bladder-side fistula orifice, assessment of size and location relative to the ureteral orifices and trigone, and evaluation of surrounding-tissue quality. A **combined vaginoscopy-cystoscopy** technique with simultaneous dual-image (picture-in-picture) display has been described to improve fistula identification and surgical planning in diagnostically challenging cases.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The EAU Robotic Urology Section consensus recommends preoperative cystoscopic marking of the fistula with a guidewire or ureteral catheter to guide subsequent repair.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Endoscopic Fulguration (Electrocoagulation)

A purely endoscopic option reported in selected small VVFs; the best-known series included defects ≤3.5 mm, a study selection limit rather than a validated universal cutoff.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

### Technique

- Cystoscopic insertion of a Bugbee electrode into the fistula tract.
- Electrocoagulation of the epithelial lining.
- Continuous Foley drainage for 2–3 weeks postoperatively.
- Anticholinergics to suppress detrusor contraction during healing.

### Outcomes

- Stovsky series (n = 15, all fistulae ≤3.5 mm): 75% primary-treatment success (9/12) and 2/3 after failed open repair; 11/15 (73%) overall.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- A five-patient series of defects less than 7 mm reported closure in 4/5; its small uncontrolled sample does not establish a reliable treatment effect.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

Best suited as initial treatment for selected very-small fistulae, and as a salvage for small residual fistulae after failed surgical repair.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Laser Ablation / Laser Welding

| Modality | Setting | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Laser welding: one Nd:YAG, seven holmium:YAG cases** | Single-center n = 8, mean fistula 3 mm (range 2–4 mm) | 7/8 dry after catheter removal; mean follow-up 47 months (range 2–110). One procedure was abandoned for bleeding.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Holmium ablation + concurrent vaginal repair** | Single case report | Cystoscopic laser ablates the bladder-side opening while transvaginal excision addresses the vaginal side.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Laser dissection + pneumovesical laparoscopic closure** | Single case after colpectomy in a transmasculine patient | Demonstrated feasibility in that anatomy; comparative tissue-injury or efficacy advantages have not been established.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |

These small reports support feasibility in selected cases, not established safety or effectiveness for every fistula ≤4 mm.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

---

## Tissue Adhesive Injection (Fibrin Glue / Cyanoacrylate / PRP)

| Approach | Outcome |
|---|---|
| **Retrograde endoscopic fibrin glue** | 6/8 successes in a mixed urinary-pathology series: seven fistulas of different types (only two VVF) and one caliceal diverticulum. This is not a VVF-specific 75% success rate.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| **Tissue-adhesive review (84 women; 81 VVF)** | Fourteen heterogeneous reports, summarized narratively: 12/84 failures/recurrences. Mean follow-up was about 11.5 months; no controlled comparison established superiority of a sealant. Reported complications included septic pelvic thrombosis.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **Autologous PRP injection + platelet-rich-fibrin glue interposition** | Cystoscopic de-epithelialization of the fistula edges, peri-fistula PRP injection, fibrin glue in the tract. 91.7% (11/12) clinical cure at 6 mo.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |

Sealants and platelet products remain selectively used options with limited evidence. The review included defects of 0.1–3.9 cm; it does not validate a universal 1-cm threshold or establish the preferred treatment for patients at high operative risk.

## Transurethral NOTES

A transurethral endoscopic suture repair using barbed suture has been described for VVFs after hysterectomy for morbidly adherent placenta. In 3 patients, 2 achieved complete resolution; the third had reduction in fistula size and required subsequent laparotomy. This three-case report establishes feasibility only; it does not establish a preferred initial approach.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

---

## Transvesicoscopic Repair

Direct placement of laparoscopic ports into the bladder (vesicoscopy) for intravesical visualization and repair:

- Combined vaginal and vesicoscopic repair for complex VVF (n = 9): 89% success at median 30 mo. The approach supplements the vaginal route for fistulae not suitable for pure vaginal repair, and pairs the laparoscopic urologist with the vaginal surgeon.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- Robot-assisted transvesical repair via mini cystotomy has been described, particularly when prior surgical planes have been used in failed repairs.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

---

## V-NOTES (Vaginal Natural Orifice Transluminal Endoscopic Surgery)

For apical VVFs, V-NOTES provides endoscopic visualization through the vaginal route. In a 17-patient modified-technique series, 88.2% success (15/17), with both failures cured on second repair. Mean OR 104 min, EBL 10.5 mL, mean LOS 3.3 d.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> See [vNOTES & Robotic vNOTES](/docs/foundations/tools/technology/robotics/vnotes) for the broader platform context.

---

## Laparoscopic and Robotic-Assisted Endoscopic Repair

Laparoscopic and robotic repairs are not purely cystoscopic. Reports include:

- Reported closure is 80–100% across selected laparoscopic and robotic series; no randomized comparison establishes equivalence to open repair.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- The 2015 review reported 95.9% transvesical and 98.0% extravesical closure across heterogeneous reports, almost entirely case reports/series. These are not randomized comparative rates.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- Laparoendoscopic single-site surgery (LESS) has feasibility reports, including five VVF cases and a mixed GU-fistula cohort. The reported shorter stay or analgesic use does not establish comparative superiority.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- In a 78-patient retrospective abdominal-repair cohort, minimally invasive groups had shorter operations and less blood loss. Prior repair history and timing differed by group; approach was not an independent closure predictor. This is an association within selected groups.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

---

## Patient Selection and Limits of the Evidence

Select the intervention using defect size, tissue quality, ureteral proximity, access, prior radiation or repair and patient goals. Case-series cutoffs do not establish a universal algorithm. A predictor measured after abdominal surgical repair should not be transferred to tiny fistulas treated with fulguration or laser.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

The 2026 Cochrane item cited below is a protocol for a future review, not completed comparative evidence. Much of the existing endoscopic literature consists of uncontrolled case reports or small series, so an absence of reported complications or a high closure percentage should not be called established safety or equivalence.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

---

## Common Principles Across Endoscopic Approaches

For reconstructive suture repairs, the common goals are:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

- Tension-free, watertight closure.
- Well-vascularized tissue.
- Adequate mobilization.
- Protection of the ureteral orifices.
- Postoperative drainage tailored to the repair and tissue. The ERUS robotic consensus suggested about ten days; small endoscopic series often used two to three weeks. See [source-specific drainage guidance](/docs/surgical-techniques/04h-fistula-repair/fistula-repair-principles#diversion-and-stenting).
- Plan the first attempt carefully and refer when the necessary reconstructive expertise is unavailable; prior failure prompts reassessment rather than an automatic procedure sequence.
