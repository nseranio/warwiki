# IMPROVE: Perioperative Vaginal Estrogen with Native-Tissue Apical Prolapse Repair

<TrialBottomLine id="improve" />

**IMPROVE** was a three-center, double-masked superiority trial in 199 postmenopausal women planning native-tissue vaginal apical repair that compared perioperative vaginal conjugated estrogen cream with identical placebo and found no improvement in its prespecified 12-month composite failure endpoint (model-estimated failure 19% versus 9%; adjusted HR 1.97, 95% CI 0.92–4.22; P = 0.08).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="improve" />

## Design

Eligible patients had symptomatic anterior and/or apical descent at least to the hymen and were planning transvaginal native-tissue repair. Vaginal atrophy symptoms were not required. Current smokers, patients using hormonal treatments affecting the vagina, and those with prior apical repair or prolapse mesh were excluded. Participants, surgeons and outcome assessors were masked to cream assignment; the apical suspension route was chosen by the surgeon.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Failure meant any anterior or posterior wall descent beyond the hymen, apical descent beyond one-third of total vaginal length, a bothersome bulge, or further prolapse treatment by surgery or requested pessary. The comparison used time to first failure in the 93 operated and assessed participants per arm. The trial tested superiority at a two-sided alpha of 0.05. Its 12-month percentages are model estimates from interval-censored time-to-event analysis, rather than event counts divided by the operated group sizes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

There were 20 composite failure events with estrogen and 10 with placebo. Anatomical descent was the most frequent failure component. In a secondary patient-reported measure, 88/89 (98.9%) estrogen recipients and 84/86 (97.7%) placebo recipients rated themselves much or very much better at 12 months (P = 0.62).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

At operation, masked surgeons rated apical tissue quality 3.6 versus 3.3 on a 1-to-5 scale (mean difference 0.3 points, 95% CI 0.06–0.56; P = 0.02). Among participants with bothersome atrophy symptoms at baseline, the adjusted score for the most bothersome symptom at 12 months was 1.06 after estrogen and 1.62 after placebo; the between-group difference in change was −0.87 points (95% CI −1.67 to −0.07; P = 0.003).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

At 36 months, 164/186 operated participants supplied follow-up data. Model-estimated composite failure was 32.6% with estrogen and 26.8% with placebo (adjusted HR 1.55, 95% CI 0.90–2.66; P = 0.11). Of respondents, 75/80 (93.8%) and 72/76 (94.7%) reported being much or very much better, including 51 of 55 participants classified as surgical failures.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Patients inserted 1 g of conjugated estrogen cream (0.625 mg/g) or placebo nightly for 2 weeks, then twice weekly to complete at least 5 total weeks before surgery. Cream resumed at discharge and continued twice weekly through 12 postoperative months. By diary, 77% of estrogen recipients and 74% of placebo recipients used at least half of scheduled cream through that period.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The standardized apical repair used uterosacral or sacrospinous ligament suspension with permanent and delayed absorbable monofilament sutures. Vaginal hysterectomy was performed when the uterus remained. Of the 93 operated patients per group, uterosacral suspension was performed in 62 versus 75 and sacrospinous suspension in 29 versus 17. Concomitant anterior repair occurred in 82 versus 73, posterior repair in 61 versus 59, and midurethral sling in 53 versus 53.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

After the assigned cream ended, participants could use vaginal estrogen for symptoms. At 36 months, 40/82 (48.8%) originally assigned estrogen and 47/78 (60.3%) originally assigned placebo reported vaginal estrogen use.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Intraoperative bladder perforation was reported in 3/93 (3.2%) estrogen recipients and 6/93 (6.5%) placebo recipients; ureteral injury in 5/93 (5.4%) and 7/93 (7.5%). At least one postoperative urinary tract infection occurred in 18/93 (19.4%) and 23/93 (24.7%) through 12 months (P = 0.38).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

After postoperative month 6, granulation tissue was found in 16/90 (17.8%) versus 7/87 (8.0%), and suture exposure in 14/90 (15.6%) versus 15/87 (17.2%). Neither finding led to repeat operation in the report.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The modified intention-to-treat analysis excluded randomized participants who did not undergo surgery, and the failure composite included apical descent before the apex reached the hymen. Atrophy outcomes used a study-designed, nonvalidated questionnaire. The primary failure estimate had a wide confidence interval. After the assigned cream stopped at 12 months, vaginal estrogen use crossed between original groups, complicating interpretation of the later comparison.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
