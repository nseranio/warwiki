# Tolterodine ER pivotal trial: Once-Daily Extended-Release Tolterodine for Overactive Bladder

<TrialBottomLine id="tolterodine-er" />

The **tolterodine ER pivotal trial** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized comparison of once-daily tolterodine ER, twice-daily tolterodine IR, and placebo in 1,529 adults with overactive bladder and urge incontinence; both active formulations reduced weekly urge-incontinence episodes more than placebo at 12 weeks, while ER produced a larger reported reduction and less dry mouth than IR.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="tolterodine-er" />

## Design

Participants had at least eight micturitions per 24 hours and at least five urge-incontinence episodes per week; 81% were women.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The quality-of-life report specifies symptoms lasting at least six months and excludes pregnancy, other bladder dysfunction, and illnesses affecting urinary output. A one- to two-week run-in confirmed diary eligibility. Randomization assigned 507 participants to a 4 mg ER capsule once daily, 514 to a 2 mg IR tablet twice daily, and 508 to placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Treatment lasted 12 weeks with double-dummy masking, no dose adjustment, and acceptable pill-count compliance in approximately 95% of participants.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The primary comparison was change in mean urge-incontinence episodes per week, assessed in the intention-to-treat population with last observation carried forward; proportional changes were described with medians. Safety assessment included adverse events, laboratory tests, and withdrawals; 154 participants aged at least 65 years had electrocardiography. The study was conducted at 167 centers.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

Mean weekly urge-incontinence episodes decreased by 11.8 with ER, 10.6 with IR, and 6.9 with placebo (ER versus placebo, 97.5% CI for the adjusted difference −7.2 to −2.5, P = 0.0001; IR versus placebo, 97.5% CI −6.0 to −1.3, P = 0.0005). The diary analysis included 507 ER, 514 IR, and 507 placebo participants; 508 had been randomized to placebo. Median percentage reductions from baseline were 71%, 60%, and 33%, respectively; the ER-versus-IR median percentage-change analysis gave an 18% relative improvement (P &lt; 0.05). Both formulations also reduced voluntary micturitions (mean changes −1.8, −1.7, and −1.2 per day) and pad use (−0.5, −0.5, and −0.2 per day) and increased mean voided volume (+34, +29, and +14 mL per micturition) compared with placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the open-label extension, 1,077 participants entered 12 months of ER 4 mg daily regardless of their original assignment; 1,075 received treatment, and 759/1,075 (70.6%) completed it. Among the 759 completers, median reductions from the original randomized baseline at 12 months were 83.1% for weekly incontinence episodes and 21.3% for daily micturitions; median voided volume increased 25.4%. Bladder condition improved by patient report in 75% and urgency in 51% of completers. Among treated participants, 107/1,075 discontinued for adverse events and 108/1,075 for lack of efficacy; more than 85% of completers took at least 75% of medication by pill count.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The prespecified quality-of-life analysis compared the ER and placebo arms. At 12 weeks, with KHQ translations available for 487 ER and 488 placebo participants, mean King's Health Questionnaire incontinence-impact scores changed by −15.68 with ER and −8.86 with placebo (adjusted mean difference −6.75; P = 0.001); role-limitations scores changed by −17.93 and −10.26 (adjusted mean difference −7.36; P = 0.001). These were the report's two prespecified primary quality-of-life domains. The generic SF-36 detected no between-group difference.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Dry mouth of any severity occurred in 118/505 (23%) ER, 156/512 (30%) IR, and 39/507 (8%) placebo recipients; severe dry mouth occurred in 1.8% of the ER safety population.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Constipation occurred in 30/505 (6%), 35/512 (7%), and 22/507 (4%), respectively; urinary tract infection occurred in 16/505 (3%), 13/512 (3%), and 20/507 (4%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Adverse events led to withdrawal in 27/507 (5%), 28/514 (5%), and 33/508 (6%) randomized participants, respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Serious adverse events occurred in 7, 12, and 18 participants, respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> During the open-label extension, dry mouth affected 139/1,075 (12.9%), urinary tract infection 44/1,075 (4.1%), and acute urinary retention 2/1,075 (0.19%); 79/1,075 (7.3%) had serious adverse events.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

The randomized comparison lasted 12 weeks. The extension enrolled volunteers who completed the randomized phase and treated everyone with ER, with no comparison group. The quality-of-life analysis compared ER with placebo and omitted the IR arm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The FDA approved Detrol LA (tolterodine extended-release capsules) on December 22, 2000, for overactive bladder with symptoms of urge urinary incontinence, urgency, and frequency.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
