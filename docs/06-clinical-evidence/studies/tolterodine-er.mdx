# Tolterodine ER pivotal trial: Once-Daily Extended-Release Tolterodine for Overactive Bladder

<TrialBottomLine id="tolterodine-er" />

The **tolterodine ER pivotal trial** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized comparison of once-daily tolterodine ER, twice-daily tolterodine IR, and placebo in 1,529 adults with overactive bladder and urge incontinence; both active formulations reduced weekly urge-incontinence episodes more than placebo at 12 weeks, while ER produced a larger reported reduction and less dry mouth than IR.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="tolterodine-er" />

## Design

Participants had at least eight micturitions per 24 hours and at least five urge-incontinence episodes per week; 81% were women.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The quality-of-life report specifies symptoms lasting at least six months and excludes pregnancy, other bladder dysfunction, and illnesses affecting urinary output. A one- to two-week run-in confirmed diary eligibility. Randomization assigned 507 participants to a 4 mg ER capsule once daily, 514 to a 2 mg IR tablet twice daily, and 508 to placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Treatment lasted 12 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The primary comparison was change in mean urge-incontinence episodes per week, assessed with micturition diaries; proportional changes were described with medians. Safety assessment included adverse events, electrocardiograms, laboratory tests, and withdrawals. The study was conducted at 167 centers.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Results

Mean weekly urge-incontinence episodes decreased more with ER than placebo (P = 0.0001) and with IR than placebo (P = 0.0005). Median percentage reductions from baseline were 71% with ER, 60% with IR, and 33% with placebo. The authors reported an 18% greater reduction with ER than IR (P &lt; 0.05). Both formulations also improved micturition frequency, pad use, and volume voided per micturition compared with placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the open-label extension, 1,077 of 1,377 randomized-phase completers entered 12 months of ER treatment regardless of their original assignment, and 71% of entrants completed it. Median changes from baseline at 12 months were −83% for weekly incontinence episodes and −21% for daily micturitions.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The prespecified quality-of-life analysis compared the ER and placebo arms. At 12 weeks, with KHQ translations available for 487 ER and 488 placebo participants, mean King's Health Questionnaire incontinence-impact scores changed by −15.68 with ER and −8.86 with placebo (adjusted mean difference −6.75; P = 0.001); role-limitations scores changed by −17.93 and −10.26 (adjusted mean difference −7.36; P = 0.001). These were the report's two prespecified primary quality-of-life domains. The generic SF-36 detected no between-group difference.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Adverse events

Dry mouth of any severity occurred in 23% of the ER arm, 30% of the IR arm, and 8% of the placebo arm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Severe dry mouth occurred in 1.8% of the ER arm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Withdrawal rates were described as comparable across the three arms. During the open-label extension, dry mouth was reported in 12.9% of participants.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The randomized comparison lasted 12 weeks. The extension enrolled volunteers who completed the randomized phase and treated everyone with ER, with no comparison group. The quality-of-life analysis compared ER with placebo and omitted the IR arm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The FDA approved Detrol LA (tolterodine extended-release capsules) on December 22, 2000, for overactive bladder with symptoms of urge urinary incontinence, urgency, and frequency.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
