# ReCOUP: Activity Restriction Recommendations and Outcomes After Reconstructive Pelvic Surgery

<TrialBottomLine id="recoup" />

**ReCOUP** was a two-center, double-masked superiority trial in women undergoing reconstructive surgery for stage II or greater prolapse that compared liberal postoperative activity instructions with three months of lifting and strenuous-exercise restrictions; at three months, 44/45 (97.8%) versus 47/50 (94.0%) were satisfied with surgery (P = .619), so the primary superiority comparison did not meet its threshold.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="recoup" />

## Design

Women with stage II or greater prolapse were enrolled at Northwestern University and the University of Chicago before minimally invasive apical repair, including colpocleisis. They had completed childbearing, were ambulatory, and could complete study materials in English. Five board-certified female pelvic medicine and reconstructive surgeons performed the operations. Allocation used site-stratified permuted blocks and sealed opaque envelopes. A nurse or advanced practice nurse delivered the assigned instructions; participants, the surgical team, and outcome assessors were masked to allocation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The primary endpoint was the patient's answer at three months to a five-point satisfaction question about the result of prolapse surgery; “completely” or “mostly” satisfied counted as satisfied. Anatomic support by POP-Q stage, pelvic floor symptom distress, and activity were secondary measures. The study planned to detect a 27-percentage-point difference in satisfaction; its multiplicity-adjusted significance threshold was P &lt; .03. The intervention compared *instructions*, while patients chose their actual activity levels.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The primary report gave an odds ratio of 0.36 for satisfaction with restricted versus liberal instructions (95% CI 0.036–3.55). The Pelvic Floor Distress Inventory total score at three months was 23.0 ± 26.3 with liberal instructions and 41.1 ± 40.7 with restrictions (P = .017); lower scores indicate less symptom distress. POP-Q stage 0/I support occurred in 32/45 (71.1%) and 35/50 (70.0%), respectively (P = .607).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the planned one-year analysis, 83/95 women completed functional assessment and 77/95 completed both functional and anatomic assessment. Satisfaction was 33/38 (86.8%) with liberal and 43/45 (95.6%) with restricted instructions. Composite anatomic failure occurred in 3/34 (8.8%) and 3/43 (7.0%). The composite counted prolapse beyond the hymen, apical descent greater than one third of vaginal length, or retreatment for prolapse. Reoperation for recurrent prolapse occurred in 1/34 (2.9%) and 2/43 (4.7%); operations included sacrocolpopexies and one posterior colporrhaphy.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The liberal group was told to resume lifting, running, aerobics, sit-ups, and other high-impact activity at its own pace. The restricted group was told to avoid lifting over 10 lb and high-impact activity for 12 weeks. Walking and stairs were allowed in both groups; both received the same six-week restriction on vaginal intercourse, tampons, and douching. Instructions were given before surgery and again at discharge.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Of the 95 women assessed at three months, 58 (61.1%) underwent minimally invasive sacrocolpopexy: 25/45 (55.6%) in the liberal group and 33/50 (66.0%) in the restricted group. The remaining procedures included vaginal suspension, colpocleisis, and one abdominal sacrocolpopexy. Concomitant hysterectomy was performed in 67/95 (70.5%) and incontinence surgery in 47/95 (49.5%). Women in the two groups reported similar strenuous activity at three months despite different instructions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Intraoperative complications were reported in 3/45 (6.7%) women assigned liberal instructions and 3/50 (6.0%) assigned restrictions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The primary report's surgical table did not classify the complications. Recurrent prolapse requiring revision surgery is reported with the one-year outcomes above.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The primary endpoint was measured in 95 of 108 enrolled women. The study's sample size was planned around a large satisfaction difference, whereas observed satisfaction was high in both arms. Actual activity was self-reported and similar between groups, limiting the contrast between assigned instructions. At one year, complete anatomic assessment was available for 34/45 liberal and 43/50 restricted participants; the analysis had limited power to detect smaller differences in recurrence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUGS 2025 clinical consensus statement stated that current evidence does not support different activity levels based on short-term outcomes after prolapse surgery with or without mesh <Rec>(100% agreement)</Rec>, although the studies were not powered to determine outcome differences between native-tissue and mesh-reinforced repairs. It also stated that current evidence does not support routine weightlifting restrictions after pelvic reconstructive surgery for most patients <Rec>(90% agreement)</Rec>. The paper reports Delphi agreement rather than a graded recommendation strength.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
