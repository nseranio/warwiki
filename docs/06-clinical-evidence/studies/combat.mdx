# CombAT: Dutasteride, Tamsulosin or Both for Symptomatic BPH With Prostatic Enlargement

<TrialBottomLine id="combat" />

**CombAT** (Combination of Avodart and Tamsulosin) was a multicenter, double-blind, parallel-group randomized trial of dutasteride plus tamsulosin versus either drug alone in 4,844 men with symptomatic BPH, a prostate of 30 mL or more and PSA 1.5 to 10 ng/mL, with time to first acute urinary retention (AUR) or BPH-related surgery over 4 years as the clinical-outcome primary endpoint.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Combination therapy reduced that endpoint versus tamsulosin (4.2% versus 11.9%; relative risk 0.34, 95% CI 0.26 to 0.45) but not significantly versus dutasteride alone (5.2%; 0.80, 95% CI 0.58 to 1.11).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="combat" />

## Design

Men were 50 or older with a clinical diagnosis of BPH, an IPSS of 12 or more, a transrectal-ultrasound prostate volume of 30 mL or more, serum PSA 1.5 to 10 ng/mL, Qmax above 5 and up to 15 mL/s, and a voided volume of at least 125 mL.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Prostate cancer and previous prostatic surgery were exclusion criteria. A 4-week single-blind placebo run-in preceded randomization to dutasteride 0.5 mg, tamsulosin 0.4 mg or both once daily, with matching placebos, for 4 years.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> About 52% of the men had earlier exposure to a 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor (5-ARI) or an alpha-blocker.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The protocol set two separate primary endpoints: change in IPSS at 2 years and time to first AUR or BPH-related surgery at 4 years.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> BPH clinical progression was a secondary endpoint defined as IPSS deterioration of 4 points or more on two consecutive visits, AUR, BPH-related incontinence, recurrent BPH-related urinary tract infection or BPH-related renal insufficiency.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Qmax and prostate volume were also secondary endpoints.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Results

AUR or BPH-related surgery occurred in 67/1,610 men (4.2%) on combination therapy, 84/1,623 (5.2%) on dutasteride and 191/1,611 (11.9%) on tamsulosin.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The relative risk versus tamsulosin was 0.34 (95% CI 0.26 to 0.45; log-rank P &lt; 0.001); versus dutasteride it was 0.80 (95% CI 0.58 to 1.11; P = 0.18), so the difference versus dutasteride was not significant.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Counting the first occurrence of each event separately, AUR occurred in 36 (2.2%), 44 (2.7%) and 109 (6.8%) men and BPH-related surgery in 38 (2.4%), 56 (3.5%) and 126 (7.8%), respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

A first BPH clinical progression event occurred in 203/1,610 men (12.6%) on combination therapy, 289/1,623 (17.8%) on dutasteride and 347/1,611 (21.5%) on tamsulosin; the report states combination therapy was significantly superior to both monotherapies.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

At the 2-year primary time point, the adjusted mean IPSS change was -6.2 points with combination therapy, -4.9 with dutasteride and -4.3 with tamsulosin.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The differences were -1.3 points versus dutasteride (95% CI -1.69 to -0.86) and -1.8 versus tamsulosin (95% CI -2.23 to -1.40), both P &lt; 0.001; the advantage over dutasteride began at month 3 and over tamsulosin at month 9.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> At 4 years the changes were -6.3, -5.3 and -3.8 points, with differences of -0.96 (95% CI -1.40 to -0.52) versus dutasteride and -2.5 (95% CI -2.96 to -2.07) versus tamsulosin, both P &lt; 0.001.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The AUA guideline's review of the trial describes no clinically important difference in symptoms or quality of life between combination therapy and dutasteride alone.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

Qmax rose by 2.4, 1.9 and 0.9 mL/s at 24 months and by 2.4, 2.0 and 0.7 mL/s at 48 months; the label states that the additional improvement over dutasteride was no longer significant at month 48.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Prostate volume at 48 months changed by -27.3% with combination therapy, -28.0% with dutasteride and +4.6% with tamsulosin.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

In post hoc baseline-subgroup analyses, combination therapy lowered AUR or BPH-related surgery versus tamsulosin in men with baseline prostate volume above 42 mL, in every PSA subgroup and in all other subgroups analyzed.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> In a separate analysis of 4-year IPSS storage and voiding subscores, combination therapy was better than dutasteride in men with a baseline prostate volume of 30 to under 58 mL but not at 58 mL or more.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

BPH-related surgery was a component of the primary endpoint: 38 men on combination therapy, 56 on dutasteride and 126 on tamsulosin had a first BPH-related operation during the 4 years.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Baseline mean age was 66 years, mean IPSS about 16.4, mean prostate volume about 55 mL and mean Qmax about 10.7 mL/s; 88% of the men were white.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Four years of double-blind treatment were completed by 1,113/1,610 men (69.1%) on combination therapy, 1,093/1,623 (67.3%) on dutasteride and 989/1,611 (61.4%) on tamsulosin; adverse events led 211 (13.1%), 185 (11.4%) and 210 (13.0%) to stop.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Ejaculation disorders were reported by 11% on combination therapy, 2% on dutasteride and 4% on tamsulosin; the incidence with combination therapy was 7.8% in the first 6 months and fell to 1.6% in months 7 to 12.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The label notes that these sexual adverse reactions may persist after treatment stops.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Erectile dysfunction of any cause was reported by 167/1,610 (10.4%), 147/1,623 (9.1%) and 109/1,611 (6.8%).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The composite term cardiac failure occurred in 12/1,610 men (0.7%) on combination therapy, 2/1,623 (0.1%) on dutasteride and 9/1,611 (0.6%) on tamsulosin; the label states that the clinical significance of the imbalance is unknown and that no causal relationship has been established.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Serious adverse events occurred in 303 (18.8%), 339 (20.9%) and 348 (21.6%).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Withdrawal because of adverse reactions was 6% with combination therapy and 4% in each monotherapy arm, with erectile dysfunction the leading reason in every arm.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

There was no placebo arm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Completion of 4 years of treatment differed by arm (69%, 67% and 61%).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The sponsor was GlaxoSmithKline.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="combat" />

MTOPS randomized 3,047 men with an IPSS of 8 or more to placebo, doxazosin, finasteride or both; combination therapy gave better clinical outcomes than doxazosin alone and similar outcomes to finasteride.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> CombAT used dutasteride with tamsulosin, required an IPSS of 12 or more, a prostate of 30 mL or more and PSA of 1.5 ng/mL or more, and had no placebo arm; combination therapy was superior to tamsulosin but not significantly to dutasteride for AUR or BPH-related surgery.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

- **AUA (Management of Lower Urinary Tract Symptoms Attributed to Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia, 2026), statement 24.** In patients with LUTS/BPH and an estimated prostate volume &gt;30 cc and/or PSA &gt;1.5 ng/mL, clinicians should offer a 5-ARI in combination with an alpha-blocker as a treatment option <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **EAU (Non-Neurogenic Male LUTS, update March 2026).** Offer combination treatment with an alpha-1-blocker and a 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor to men with moderate-to-severe LUTS and an increased risk of disease progression (for example prostate volume &gt;40 mL) <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **FDA (June 14, 2010).** JALYN, the fixed-dose capsule of dutasteride 0.5 mg and tamsulosin 0.4 mg, was approved for symptomatic BPH in men with an enlarged prostate; the label states it is not approved for prostate cancer prevention.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
