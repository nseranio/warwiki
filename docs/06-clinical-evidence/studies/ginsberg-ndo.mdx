# Ginsberg NDO: Phase 3 OnabotulinumtoxinA for Urinary Incontinence from Neurogenic Detrusor Overactivity

<TrialBottomLine id="ginsberg-ndo" />

**Ginsberg NDO** was a 52-week international, multicenter, double-blind randomized trial of intradetrusor onabotulinumtoxinA 200 U, 300 U or placebo in 416 patients with multiple sclerosis or spinal cord injury and urinary incontinence from neurogenic detrusor overactivity, with the change in weekly incontinence episodes at week 6 as the primary endpoint. Both doses reduced weekly episodes by 21 and 23 versus 9 with placebo (each P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="ginsberg-ndo" />

## Design

Eligible patients had urinary incontinence from neurogenic detrusor overactivity with 14 or more episodes per week; 227 had multiple sclerosis and 189 had spinal cord injury. The groups comprised 135 patients on 200 U, 132 on 300 U and 149 on placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Allergan sponsored the trial, registered as NCT00311376 (study 191622-515).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The label describes the patients in the two registration trials as having an inadequate response to or intolerance of at least one anticholinergic.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The primary endpoint was the change from baseline in mean weekly urinary incontinence episodes at week 6. Secondary endpoints were maximum cystometric capacity, maximum detrusor pressure during the first involuntary detrusor contraction and the Incontinence Quality of Life (I-QOL) total score.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At week 6, 200 U reduced mean weekly incontinence by 21 episodes and 300 U by 23, against 9 with placebo (each P &lt; 0.001). Maximum cystometric capacity, maximum detrusor pressure at the first involuntary contraction and the I-QOL total score improved significantly more than with placebo for both doses (each P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Median time to patient request for re-treatment was 256 days with 200 U, 254 days with 300 U and 92 days with placebo. The trial identified no clinically relevant benefit in efficacy or duration of the 300 U dose over 200 U.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the pooled interim analysis, 387, 336, 241, 113 and 46 patients received treatment cycles 1 through 5, respectively.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> At week 6 after cycles 1 through 5, the 200 U group had 22.7, 23.3, 23.1, 25.3 and 31.9 fewer weekly incontinence episodes than at baseline, respectively.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Across those five cycles, 73% to 94% had at least a 50% reduction and 36% to 55% had no incontinence episodes at week 6.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> With repeat treatment, mean volume per void increased by more than 130 mL and quality of life improved across cycles 1 through 5.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

Repeat 200-U treatment reduced daily incontinence by 3.2 to 4.1 episodes at week 6 across six cycles in the pooled, open-label extension; 388 toxin-treated patients had mean follow-up of 3.6 years. More than 83% had at least a 50% reduction and 43% to 56% became dry across cycles. Volume per void nearly doubled, and I-QOL rose 27.1 to 33.5 points (11-point minimally important difference). Median time to retreatment request was 9.0 months in the 200-U-only group; 300-U results were similar.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

Among 227 patients who completed four years, pooled week-6 reductions were 3.5, 3.5, 3.8 and 3.8 episodes per day by year from a baseline of 4.3. The pooled proportions with at least 50% reduction were 87.6% to 92.1%, and 46.3% to 53.2% became dry. The 200-U-only and 300-to-200-U groups had median times to retreatment request of 9.2 and 9.0 months; 29/112 (26%) and 31/95 (32.6%) had effects of at least 12 months, respectively. Pooled I-QOL gains were 30.8 to 32.2 points across years, and patients received 1.4 to 1.5 treatments yearly.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The trial compared 200 U and 300 U with placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The labeled adult neurogenic dose is 200 U, injected cystoscopically into the detrusor, avoiding the trigone, as 30 injections of 1 mL (about 6.7 U) about 1 cm apart and about 2 mm deep.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The bladder is instilled with enough saline for visualization without overdistension, a final 1 mL of saline clears the needle, and the patient is observed for at least 30 minutes.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The label requires no urinary tract infection at treatment, prophylactic antibiotics (except aminoglycosides) from 1 to 3 days before to 1 to 3 days after the procedure, and discontinuation of antiplatelet therapy at least 3 days before. Repeat injection is considered when the effect wanes and no sooner than 12 weeks after the previous injection.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

In the extension, 396 entered after completing a parent trial and 388 received toxin. Each injection used 30 cystoscopic 1-mL detrusor injections sparing the trigone. A 2011 amendment changed later doses to 200 U for all patients; retreatment required a request, at least 12 weeks since the prior injection and at least one incontinence episode within three days.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Of 396 entrants, 240 (60.6%) were followed for at least four years; the completer analysis included 227/388 (58.5%) toxin-treated patients.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Adverse events

Urinary tract infection and urinary retention were the most common adverse events. Among patients not catheterizing at baseline, 10% on placebo, 35% on 200 U and 42% on 300 U began clean intermittent catheterization for retention.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In the pooled extension, first-time catheterization after 200 U occurred in 26/88 (29.5%), 2/58 (3.4%) and 3/50 (6.0%) of prior nonusers in cycles 1 to 3; the corresponding 300-U rates were 43.0%, 15.0% and 4.8%, with none starting in cycles 4 to 6.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Within 12 weeks of the first toxin dose, urinary tract infection occurred in 56/203 (27.6%) at 200 U and 57/185 (30.8%) at 300 U; retention occurred in 41/203 (20.2%) and 42/185 (22.7%).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Neutralizing antibodies developed in 8/381 (2.1%), including 3/200 (1.5%) receiving the approved 200-U dose.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Among four-year completers, first-time catheterization was 37/85 (43.5%) in year 1 and 0/36 in year 4; urinary tract infection occurred after 73/340 (21.5%) year-1 treatments and 43/228 (18.9%) year-4 treatments.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Retention occurred after 45/340 (13.2%) and 7/228 (3.1%) treatments, respectively; neither long-term report identified a new safety signal.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> In the interim analysis through cycle 5, urinary tract infection and urinary retention were the most common adverse events, with no change in the adverse-event profile over time.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

The label reports pooled data from the two placebo-controlled trials. Within 12 weeks, urinary tract infection occurred in 24% with 200 U versus 17% with placebo, urinary retention in 17% versus 3% and hematuria in 4% versus 3%. Among patients not catheterizing at baseline, 30.6% (33/108) catheterized for retention with 200 U versus 6.7% (7/104) with placebo; the rate was 31% (27/86) versus 5% (4/88) in multiple sclerosis and 27% (6/22) versus 19% (3/16) in spinal cord injury. Autonomic dysreflexia occurred in 1.5% with 200 U versus 0.4% with placebo.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The AUA/SUFU guideline reports annualized multiple sclerosis exacerbation rates in this trial of 0.22 with placebo, 0.14 with 200 U and 0.37 with 300 U.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Limitations

The comparator was placebo; the trial did not compare onabotulinumtoxinA with an anticholinergic, with augmentation cystoplasty or with a lower dose. The 100 U dose was later tested separately in the label's post-approval study of noncatheterizing patients with multiple sclerosis (100 U, n = 66; placebo, n = 78).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The primary endpoint was measured at week 6, and the re-treatment endpoint was time to patient request.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The open-label extension enrolled completers of the parent trials, and the four-year analysis selected 227 patients who completed follow-up.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="ginsberg-ndo" />

The replicate trial of Cruz enrolled 275 patients (multiple sclerosis 154, spinal cord injury 121) and used 30 injections that avoided the trigone. At week 6, 200 U and 300 U reduced weekly episodes by 21.8 and 19.4 versus 13.2 with placebo (P &lt; 0.01), and, among patients not using catheterization at baseline, 12%, 30% and 42% on placebo, 200 U and 300 U began catheterization after treatment.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> Both trials found no clinically relevant efficacy or duration advantage for 300 U.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

- **AUA/SUFU (2021).** In patients with neurogenic lower urinary tract dysfunction from spinal cord injury or multiple sclerosis who are refractory to oral medications, clinicians should recommend onabotulinumtoxinA to improve bladder storage parameters, decrease incontinence episodes and improve quality of life measures <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade A)</Rec>. The supporting text lists this trial among the randomized trials behind the statement and finds no efficacy difference between 200 U and 300 U. For patients who void spontaneously, clinicians must discuss the risk of urinary retention and the potential need for intermittent catheterization before selecting botulinum toxin therapy <Rec>(Clinical Principle)</Rec>.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **EAU Neuro-Urology (limited update, March 2026).** Use botulinum toxin A injections in the detrusor to reduce neurogenic detrusor overactivity if antimuscarinic therapy is ineffective <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>. The text cites this trial for repeat injections without loss of efficacy.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **FDA (August 24, 2011).** OnabotulinumtoxinA approved for adults with urinary incontinence due to detrusor overactivity associated with a neurologic condition (for example spinal cord injury or multiple sclerosis) who have an inadequate response to or are intolerant of an anticholinergic.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
