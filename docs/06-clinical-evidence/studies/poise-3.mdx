# POISE-3: Tranexamic Acid in Patients Undergoing Noncardiac Surgery

<TrialBottomLine id="poise-3" />

**POISE-3** was a multicenter randomized trial of intravenous tranexamic acid versus placebo in 9,535 patients undergoing noncardiac surgery with bleeding and cardiovascular risk factors. Tranexamic acid lowered the 30-day bleeding composite, while cardiovascular noninferiority was not established.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="poise-3" />

## Design

The tranexamic-acid comparison was conducted within a partial factorial trial that also assigned perioperative blood-pressure strategies. Participants were at least 45 years old and undergoing inpatient noncardiac surgery; creatinine clearance below 30 ml/min or long-term dialysis excluded participation. Its bleeding endpoint combined life-threatening bleeding, major bleeding, and bleeding into a critical organ. The cardiovascular endpoint combined myocardial injury after noncardiac surgery, nonhemorrhagic stroke, peripheral arterial thrombosis, and symptomatic proximal venous thromboembolism (VTE). Cardiovascular noninferiority required the upper bound of the one-sided 97.5% hazard-ratio confidence interval to be below 1.125 and one-sided P &lt; 0.025.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At 30 days, the intention-to-treat bleeding composite occurred in 433/4,757 (9.1%) with tranexamic acid and 561/4,778 (11.7%) with placebo (HR 0.76, 95% CI 0.67–0.87; absolute risk difference −2.6 percentage points, 95% CI −3.8 to −1.4; P &lt; 0.001 for superiority). In the per-protocol population receiving both doses, the cardiovascular composite occurred in 649/4,581 (14.2%) and 639/4,601 (13.9%), respectively (HR 1.02, 95% CI 0.92–1.14; one-sided noninferiority P = 0.04); its one-sided upper confidence bound was 1.14. The intention-to-treat sensitivity analysis gave HR 1.03 (95% CI 0.92–1.14).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Major bleeding by 30 days occurred in 363/4,757 (7.6%) with tranexamic acid versus 496/4,778 (10.4%) with placebo (HR 0.72, 95% CI 0.63–0.83). Transfusion of at least one unit of packed red cells occurred in 449/4,757 (9.4%) versus 574/4,778 (12.0%) (OR 0.77, 95% CI 0.68–0.88). These secondary and tertiary estimates were not adjusted for multiple comparisons.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In a prespecified analysis of 1,124 urologic participants, the 30-day bleeding composite occurred in 45/556 (8.1%) and 62/568 (10.9%) (HR 0.73, 95% CI 0.50–1.07). Major bleeding occurred in 34/556 (6.1%) and 54/568 (9.5%) (HR 0.63, 95% CI 0.41–0.97). The cardiovascular composite occurred in 67/556 (12.1%) and 62/568 (10.9%) (HR 1.12, 95% CI 0.79–1.58). The analysis found no interaction by surgical approach, cancer status, or recent antithrombotic use.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Patients received a 1-g intravenous bolus of tranexamic acid or placebo at both the start and end of surgery; 96.3% of each group received both assigned doses.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The urologic subgroup included 489 laparoscopic or robotic, 360 open, 244 transurethral, and 31 percutaneous operations.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

In the primary trial, serious adverse events occurred in 263/4,757 (5.5%) receiving tranexamic acid and 242/4,778 (5.1%) receiving placebo (P = 0.16). Nonhemorrhagic stroke occurred in 24/4,757 versus 16/4,778, and symptomatic proximal VTE in 32/4,757 versus 28/4,778. Seizure occurred in 10/4,757 (0.2%) versus 3/4,778 (0.1%) (HR 3.35, 95% CI 0.92–12.20); infection in 499/4,757 (10.5%) versus 487/4,778 (10.2%) (HR 1.03, 95% CI 0.91–1.17).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The urologic analysis reported myocardial injury after noncardiac surgery in 62/556 versus 58/568 patients, nonhemorrhagic stroke in 2/556 versus 2/568, and symptomatic proximal VTE in 3/556 versus 3/568.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

Follow-up for these outcomes was 30 days. Recruitment stopped after 9,535 of 10,000 planned randomizations because of a pandemic-related financial deficit; the decision was made without knowledge of trial results. The urologic analysis included few stroke and VTE events, leaving wide confidence intervals for its cardiovascular composite.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
