# Menefee 2011 (OARS): Colporrhaphy Compared With Mesh or Graft-Reinforced Vaginal Paravaginal Repair for Anterior Vaginal Wall Prolapse: A Randomized Controlled Trial

<TrialBottomLine id="menefee-paravaginal" />

**Menefee 2011 (OARS)** was a two-center, participant- and assessor-blinded randomized trial in women with symptomatic stage II or greater anterior prolapse, comparing anterior colporrhaphy with freehand porcine-dermis graft or polypropylene-mesh vaginal paravaginal repair; at two years, anatomic failure was 58%, 46%, and 18%, respectively, while composite failure did not differ significantly.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="menefee-paravaginal" />

## Design

Women desiring surgery were randomized to the three repairs. Four fellowship-trained female pelvic medicine and reconstructive surgeons supervised or performed operations at two teaching centers. Participants and the examiners assessing POP-Q outcomes were blinded; the surgeon knew the assigned repair. Concomitant continence and apical or posterior procedures were permitted.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The primary endpoint was anterior anatomic failure, defined as POP-Q stage II or greater, corresponding to Ba at −1 cm or greater. Composite failure required both a reported vaginal bulge and stage II-or-greater anterior prolapse. The trial was sized to detect an absolute 40-percentage-point difference in anatomic success between reinforced repair and colporrhaphy, with 33 participants planned per arm to account for dropout. It was not powered for differences in subjective outcomes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Seventy-eight of 99 randomized women completed at least two years of follow-up. Mesh had less anatomic failure than colporrhaphy (P = 0.002) and porcine graft (P = 0.015); graft and colporrhaphy did not differ significantly (P = 0.430). When all missing outcomes were counted as failures, anatomic failure was 36% with mesh, 55% with porcine graft, and 69% with colporrhaphy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Among participants assessed at two years, composite failure occurred in 4% of the mesh arm (one case), 12% of the porcine-graft arm (three cases), and 13% of the colporrhaphy arm (three cases), without a significant between-group difference. Prolapse and urinary symptom scores improved in each group without significant between-group differences in change; sexual-function scores likewise showed no significant difference in change.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Native-tissue colporrhaphy used interrupted 2-0 polydioxanone sutures for midline plication, followed by 2-0 polyglactin epithelial closure and vaginal packing. In the reinforced arms, the surgeon dissected laterally to the ischial spine and arcus tendineus fascia pelvis. Three 0-polypropylene sutures on each side secured a freehand trapezoidal polypropylene mesh or porcine-dermis graft to the arcus. Typical graft width was 6–8 cm distally and 12 cm proximally, with 4–6 cm length. The reinforcement had no trocar arms and was not attached to the apical suspension. Apical suspension, when indicated, was vaginal or laparoscopic.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Concomitant hysterectomy was performed in 40%, midurethral sling in 56%, and an apical procedure in 67% of participants. Two surgeons performed most operations; fewer than 4% were performed by the other two surgeons.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Mesh erosion occurred in 14% of the mesh arm (five cases), compared with porcine-graft erosion in 4% of the graft arm (one case; P = 0.413). Two mesh erosions required operating-room excision; three asymptomatic mesh erosions were treated with vaginal estrogen. The porcine-graft erosion healed with vaginal estrogen. Both reoperations for recurrent anterior prolapse occurred after porcine-graft repair.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Seven women sexually active without dyspareunia at baseline reported new dyspareunia: two after mesh, two after graft, and three after colporrhaphy. The investigators reported no intraoperative bladder or urethral injuries and no serious adverse events.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The small trial had 79% follow-up at a minimum of two years. Frequent concomitant procedures complicate attribution of symptom improvement to the anterior repair. Most operations were performed by two surgeons. The anatomic endpoint counted stage II descent as failure, while the more symptom-dependent composite endpoint had few events.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
