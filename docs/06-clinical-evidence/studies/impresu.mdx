# ImpresU: Methenamine Hippurate for Recurrent Urinary Tract Infection in Older Women

<TrialBottomLine id="impresu" />

**ImpresU** was a multinational, triple-blind, phase 4 randomized trial comparing methenamine hippurate with placebo in women aged at least 70 years with recurrent urinary tract infection (UTI). During six months of treatment, the methenamine arm had fewer antibiotic-treated UTI episodes (87 versus 111; adjusted incidence rate ratio [IRR] 0.75, 95% CI 0.57–1.0; P = 0.049).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="impresu" />

## Design

General practices in Norway, Sweden, Poland and the Netherlands recruited women with at least two antibiotic courses for UTI in the preceding six months or at least three in the preceding twelve months. Nursing-home residents were eligible if managed by a general practice. Country-stratified block randomization assigned 144 women to methenamine and 145 to matching placebo. Tablets looked identical, and participants, clinicians, researchers and the statistician remained unaware of allocation through analysis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The primary outcome was the number of antibiotic courses taken for symptomatic UTI during treatment. A symptom-free interval of at least 14 days separated episodes. Acute infection care remained with usual health services; the research team checked participants' reports against general-practice records. The main modified intention-to-treat (mITT) analysis included 281 women with complete baseline data, imputed missing outcome data and used an adjusted negative binomial model. Per-protocol and complete-case analyses tested the result's sensitivity.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The primary mITT analysis included 140 methenamine and 141 placebo participants. The per-protocol sensitivity analysis of 217 participants gave IRR 0.68 (95% CI 0.49–0.97; P = 0.031); the complete-case analysis of 271 gave IRR 0.77 (95% CI 0.58–1.0; P = 0.078). The latter did not meet conventional statistical significance.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

During the six months after study medication stopped, the former methenamine group had 133 antibiotic-treated UTI episodes versus 82 in the former placebo group (adjusted IRR 1.7, 95% CI 1.3–2.3; P &lt; 0.001). The article reported no between-group difference in UTI symptom severity or episode duration.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

A subsequent descriptive analysis examined urine cultures and antibiotic prescribing from the same study. Cultures were obtained in 178 of 399 symptomatic UTI episodes (45%). Among cultured *E. coli* isolates, 57 came from the methenamine arm and 50 from placebo. Resistance proportions, adjusted for baseline resistance, did not differ significantly by allocation for the antibiotics tested.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Participants took methenamine hippurate 1 g or a matching placebo tablet twice daily for six months, then received no study drug for six months. Unused tablets were counted; 26 of 144 methenamine participants and 28 of 145 placebo participants took less than 80% of their assigned medication. During the study, 7 methenamine participants and 13 placebo participants started prophylactic UTI antibiotics.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Fourteen serious adverse events were reported, seven in each arm. Investigators considered all unrelated or unlikely to be related to study medication; six were related or possibly related to UTI, three per arm. Bladder irritation was the only side effect reported more often with methenamine in the main article.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Recruitment ended earlier than planned because of pandemic-related delays and approaching study-drug expiry. The primary P value was 0.049 and the complete-case analysis gave P = 0.078. The primary endpoint counted antibiotic courses for symptomatic UTI rather than culture-confirmed episodes. Follow-up contacts were less frequent after study medication stopped. In the later microbiology analysis, only 45% of symptomatic episodes had a urine culture, with collection varying by country.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
