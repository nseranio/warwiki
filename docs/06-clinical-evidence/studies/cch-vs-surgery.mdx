# CCH versus surgery: Collagenase and Penile Reconstruction for Peyronie's Disease

<TrialBottomLine id="cch-vs-surgery" />

**CCH versus surgery** was a single-center randomized trial in 40 men with Peyronie's disease comparing collagenase *Clostridium histolyticum* (CCH) with penile plication or incision and grafting, with RestoreX traction and sildenafil in both arms; at three months surgery corrected more curvature, while CCH preserved more length and sensation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="cch-vs-surgery" />

## Design

Eligible men had curvature of at least 30° and erections adequate for intercourse, with or without a phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitor. Prior CCH or surgery and moderate or severe plaque calcification were exclusions. Allocation was stratified by baseline composite curvature. One clinician delivered both treatments, and the clinician measuring postoperative curvature and length was blinded to baseline measurements. Curvature was the sum of two goniometer planes after a pharmacologically induced erection; flaccid penile length was measured from the pubic symphysis to the corona.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The five primary outcomes were overall satisfaction, patient-reported change in erectile function, penile sensation and length, and change in the International Index of Erectile Function erectile-function domain (IIEF-EFD). The report presented separate tests for these outcomes without a multiplicity-adjusted hierarchy; categorical P values compare the full response distributions. Two men withdrew after randomization, leaving 19 treated men per arm with three-month follow-up; some questionnaire items had fewer CCH responses.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At three months, 50.0% of CCH respondents and 21.1% of surgery respondents were very satisfied (P = 0.08). Erectile function was better or unchanged in 100% versus 68.4% (P = 0.03), sensation was unchanged in 75.0% versus 10.5% (P &lt; 0.001), and subjective length was longer or unchanged in 87.5% versus 15.8% (P &lt; 0.0001). Median IIEF-EFD change was +1.5 versus +2.5 points (P = 0.91). These primary responses favored CCH on three measures; satisfaction and IIEF-EFD did not differ significantly.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For secondary objective outcomes, median composite curvature improved 53.9% after CCH versus 83.7% after surgery (P &lt; 0.01), while median flaccid coronal length changed +1.0 versus -0.5 cm (P &lt; 0.01). Median Peyronie's Disease Questionnaire symptom-bother score fell by 6.5 points in each arm (P = 0.87).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The one-year meeting abstract reported data for 28 men: 36% versus 14% were very satisfied, median length change was +1.0 versus -0.13 cm, and median curvature improvement was 55% versus 82%.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> At two years, 29 men had questionnaire data; very satisfied responses were 62% versus 25%, and reported curvature recurrence was 8% versus 56%.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> At three years, 23 men had questionnaire data; 54% versus 8% reported being very satisfied.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Objective curvature and length were not measured at the two- or three-year visits.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

CCH was injected into the erect penis at the point of maximal curvature: two 0.9-mg injections diluted to 0.8 mL, one day apart, followed by office modeling and home straightening and counter-bending. Men could receive up to four injection series. Surgical choice was made by the treating surgeon: 18 of 19 men underwent plication and one underwent plaque incision and grafting. Six surgical patients also received an extratunical graft for residual indentation or hourglass deformity.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Plication used permanent 2-0 Ethibond sutures opposite the curve, with neurovascular-bundle mobilization and partial-thickness tunical incisions to bury knots. The incision graft was autologous testicular tunica vaginalis; extratunical grafts used TachoSil. Traction began after surgery and was prescribed to both groups for 30–60 minutes daily. Reported mean daily use was 31.3 minutes after CCH and 46.6 minutes after surgery (P = 0.02).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

At three months, persistent patient-reported adverse events occurred in 50.0% of CCH respondents and 89.5% of surgery respondents, totaling 13 versus 50 events (P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Penile pain persisted in 6.3% versus 63.2% (P &lt; 0.001), and loss of penile sensation in 18.8% versus 57.9% (P = 0.02).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> One surgical patient underwent circumcision for persistent phimosis after a ventral incision; the authors classified this as a Clavien-Dindo grade IIIa complication. The three-year abstract reported current pain in 8% versus 42% (P = 0.04).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Limitations

Treatment and questionnaire assessment were unblinded, and all procedures came from one specialist. The randomized comparison was small and predominantly compared CCH with plication. CCH questionnaire response counts were lower than the treated count for several primary measures. The longer-term reports are meeting abstracts with attrition and, at the two- and three-year visits, questionnaire outcomes without objective penile measurements. The RestoreX inventor's royalty and equity interests and industry grant support were disclosed in the primary report.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU Sexual and Reproductive Health guideline, limited update March 2026, cites this randomized comparison in its CCH discussion. It recommends intralesional CCH for men with dorsal or lateral curvature greater than 30° who request nonsurgical treatment <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>, and recommends assessing length, curvature, erectile function and expectations before surgery <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
