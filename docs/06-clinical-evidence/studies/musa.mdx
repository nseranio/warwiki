# MUSA: Midurethral Sling vs OnabotulinumtoxinA in Females With Urinary Incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="musa" />

**MUSA** was a multicenter, assessor-masked randomized superiority trial in women with bothersome stress and urgency incontinence after conservative and oral treatment, comparing intradetrusor onabotulinumtoxinA 100 U with a synthetic mesh midurethral sling; onabotulinumtoxinA did not meet the superiority threshold for total Urogenital Distress Inventory (UDI) improvement at six months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="musa" />

## Design

Eligibility required moderate or severe bother from each incontinence component, a positive cough stress test, and at least four urgency incontinence episodes on a three-day diary. Prior sling, anterior or apical prolapse at or beyond the hymen, planned anterior or apical prolapse surgery, and elevated postvoid residual were exclusions. Outcome assessors were masked; participants and surgeons were not. Of 150 randomized women, 140 underwent an assigned procedure and 137 treated women with postbaseline data entered the primary analysis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The primary endpoint was the model-estimated change from baseline in the UDI total score at six months. This patient-reported measure combines irritative, stress, and obstructive symptom domains; lower scores mean less bother. The trial tested two-sided superiority at P &lt; .05. Its planning estimate of a clinically important total-score difference was 26.1 points. Stress and irritative UDI subscores were secondary outcomes. Additional treatment, including crossover, was permitted after six months, making the 12-month comparison exploratory.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At six months, the estimated mean UDI total-score change was −66.8 points after onabotulinumtoxinA and −84.9 after sling, an onabotulinumtoxinA-minus-sling mean difference of 18.1 points (95% CI −4.6 to 40.7; P=.12). The prespecified superiority endpoint was not met.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The sling group had greater improvement in the secondary UDI stress score (−45.2 versus −25.1 points; mean difference 20.1, 95% CI 8.4 to 31.9; P &lt; .001). Six-month irritative-score change was −32.9 with onabotulinumtoxinA and −27.4 with sling (mean difference −5.5, 95% CI −15.3 to 4.3; P=.27). At 12 months, after additional treatment was allowed, the exploratory total-score difference was 24.4 points (95% CI 1.1 to 47.7; P=.04), below the published 26.1-point threshold used in planning.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Investigators used full-length retropubic or transobturator polypropylene midurethral slings; single-incision slings were excluded. OnabotulinumtoxinA was administered intradetrusor in an office or operating room. A second injection could be given between months three and six. By six months, 9/71 (12.7%) injection recipients had a second injection; by 12 months, 20/71 (28.2%) had. After crossover became available, 20/66 (30.3%) women assigned sling received onabotulinumtoxinA and 11/71 (15.5%) assigned injection received a sling by 12 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

In the safety population, urinary tract infection through 12 months occurred in 20/72 (27.8%) injection recipients and 20/68 (29.4%) sling recipients; recurrent infection occurred in 5/72 (6.9%) and 12/68 (17.6%). No injection recipient went home with a catheter after the initial procedure, compared with 8/68 (11.8%) sling recipients. Catheter placement or self-catheterization starting or continuing at least two weeks after treatment occurred in 2/72 (2.8%) and 1/68 (1.5%). Two sling recipients had vaginal mesh exposure (2/68, 2.9%), and one underwent sling removal or revision (1/68, 1.5%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Participants and surgeons knew the assigned procedure. The primary analysis excluded randomized women who were not treated or lacked postbaseline efficacy data. The six-month primary comparison captures an initial procedure, while permitted repeat injection and subsequent crossover affect longer follow-up. Retropubic and transobturator slings were pooled, so the trial does not estimate route-specific effects.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
