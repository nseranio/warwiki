# POTABA: Potassium Paraaminobenzoate for Early Peyronie's Disease

<TrialBottomLine id="potaba" />

**POTABA** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized trial in men with early, noncalcified Peyronie's disease that found higher composite plaque or curvature response with oral potassium paraaminobenzoate than with placebo among study completers.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="potaba" />

## Design

The trial enrolled 103 previously untreated men with disease duration &lt;12 months and noncalcified plaques. Fifty-one were assigned to potassium paraaminobenzoate and 52 to placebo. Response meant plaque-size regression and/or penile-curvature reduction of at least 30%. The main analysis included the 75 men who completed treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Among the 75 completers, composite response was 74.3% with potassium paraaminobenzoate and 50.0% with placebo (P = 0.016). Mean plaque area changed from 259 to 142 mm² and from 259 to 233 mm², respectively, over 12 months (between-group P = 0.042). The abstract reported no relevant difference in improvement of pre-existing curvature (P = 0.066). The abstract described a protective effect against curvature deterioration with potassium paraaminobenzoate. Curvature deteriorated significantly in 32.5% of placebo cases (P &lt; 0.001). Pain decreased in 82.6% and 77.3%, without a significant between-group difference.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The oral regimen was 3 g four times daily (12 g/day) for 12 months. Seventy-five of 103 randomized men completed the study and formed the main valid-case analysis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

No severe adverse events were reported.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The main analysis used only 75 of 103 randomized men. The composite response could be met through plaque regression without improvement in pre-existing curvature.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA 2015 Peyronie's disease guideline placed potassium aminobenzoate among “Other Treatments,” judged its efficacy unproven, and found <Rec>(insufficient evidence currently exists to support even a Conditional Recommendation)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The CUA 2018 guideline permits considering potassium para-aminobenzoate alone or in multimodal care while communicating limitations in efficacy data and costs <Rec>(Level 3 evidence, Grade C recommendation)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
