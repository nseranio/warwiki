# POTABA: Potassium Paraaminobenzoate for Early Peyronie's Disease

<TrialBottomLine id="potaba" />

**POTABA** was an 11-center, double-blind randomized trial in men with early, noncalcified Peyronie's disease that found higher composite plaque or curvature response with oral potassium paraaminobenzoate than with placebo among study completers.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="potaba" />

## Design

The trial enrolled 103 previously untreated men with disease duration no more than 12 months and noncalcified plaques. Fifty-one were assigned to potassium paraaminobenzoate and 52 to placebo. Diabetes, compensated nephropathy, chronic gastric or bowel disease, and absent response to an intracavernosal injection test were exclusion criteria. Response meant complete resolution or plaque-size reduction and/or penile-curvature reduction of at least 30%. The protocol set α = 0.05 for response-rate superiority and planned at least 46 completers per arm; the main analysis included 75 men (35 treated, 40 placebo).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Composite response at 12 months favored potassium paraaminobenzoate among completers: 26/35 (74.3%) versus 20/40 (50.0%) with placebo (P = 0.016), meeting the prespecified 0.05 threshold. Baseline-adjusted mean plaque area changed from 259 to 142 mm² and from 259 to 233 mm², respectively, over 12 months (between-group P = 0.042). Pre-existing curvature decreased in 19/30 (63.3%) and 19/32 (59.4%), without a significant difference in improvement (P = 0.066). Curvature newly developed or worsened in 1/35 (2.9%) versus 13/40 (32.5%; P = 0.001); among initially straight men, new curvature developed in 0/5 versus 6/8 (75.0%; P = 0.021). Pain decreased in 82.6% and 77.3%, without a significant between-group difference.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The oral regimen was 3 g of powder four times daily (12 g/day) for 12 months. Plaques were measured with calipers on the stretched flaccid penis; curvature was assessed from standardized erection photographs after intracavernosal prostaglandin E1 and manual stimulation. Seventy-five of 103 randomized men completed the study and formed the main valid-case analysis; another 9/51 treated men and 8/52 placebo recipients were excluded for irregular drug intake.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

No severe adverse events or clinically significant laboratory abnormalities were reported. Side effects led to withdrawal in 7/51 treated men (13.7%) and 4/52 placebo recipients (7.7%); gastrointestinal disorders accounted for 4/51 (7.8%) and 4/52 (7.7%), respectively. One treated man each withdrew for pruritus, fever, or chills.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The main analysis used 75 of 103 randomized men, fewer than the planned 46 completers per arm. The composite response could be met through plaque regression without improvement in pre-existing curvature.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA 2015 Peyronie's disease guideline placed potassium aminobenzoate among “Other Treatments,” judged its efficacy unproven, and found <Rec>(insufficient evidence currently exists to support even a Conditional Recommendation)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The CUA 2018 guideline permits considering potassium para-aminobenzoate alone or in multimodal care while communicating limitations in efficacy data and costs <Rec>(Level 3 evidence, Grade C recommendation)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
