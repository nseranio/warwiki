# iROC: Intracorporeal Robot-Assisted Radical Cystectomy Versus Open Radical Cystectomy

<TrialBottomLine id="iroc" />

**iROC** was a multicenter, unblinded, phase 3 randomized trial in adults with nonmetastatic bladder cancer suitable for either approach; robot-assisted cystectomy with intracorporeal urinary diversion increased days alive and out of hospital during the first 90 days compared with open cystectomy (median 82 versus 80 days; adjusted difference 2.2 days, 95% CI 0.50–3.85).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="iroc" />

## Design

Nine UK cancer centers randomized 338 patients equally by center and planned diversion type (ileal conduit versus continent diversion/neobladder). Eligibility allowed urothelial, squamous, adenocarcinoma, or variant histology with node status up to N1 and required fitness for both operations. Prior abdominal or pelvic surgery or radiotherapy that made either approach unsuitable excluded patients. Surgeons required trial accreditation; robotic operators had completed at least 30 cystectomies as sole operators, and both groups used enhanced recovery pathways.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The primary endpoint combined the initial admission, readmissions, and death into days alive and out of hospital during the first 90 days. The analysis adjusted for center and diversion type; superiority used a two-sided P &lt; 0.05 threshold. Secondary comparisons were exploratory because the trial tested multiple outcomes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The primary endpoint was available for 305 of 317 operated patients, analyzed by randomized group, and met the prespecified superiority test (P = 0.01). Median days alive and out of hospital were 82 (IQR 76–84) with robotic surgery and 80 (IQR 72–83) with open surgery. Median hospital stay was 7 (IQR 6–10) versus 8 (IQR 6–14) days.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Generic quality of life favored robotic surgery at five weeks (EQ-5D-5L, n = 130 versus 116; adjusted open-minus-robotic mean difference −0.07, 95% CI −0.11 to −0.03; P = 0.003). Self-reported disability also favored robotic surgery at five weeks (WHODAS 2.0, n = 114 versus 102; adjusted open-minus-robotic mean difference 0.43, 95% CI 0.15–0.73; P = 0.003). These secondary comparisons were exploratory.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Cancer recurrence was 29/161 (18.0%) versus 25/156 (16.0%; HR 0.9, 95% CI 0.53–1.54), and all-cause mortality was 23/161 (14.3%) versus 23/156 (14.7%; HR 1.06, 95% CI 0.60–1.90) at a median 18.4 months of follow-up. Neither exploratory secondary comparison detected a statistically significant difference.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The trial based UK National Health Service economic analysis of 305 patients with available primary-outcome data found an incremental 0.01124 quality-adjusted life-years (95% CI 0.00391–0.01857) at an additional mean cost of £1,124 per patient (95% CI −£576 to £2,824) over 90 days. Its modeled incremental cost-effectiveness ratio was £100,008 per quality-adjusted life-year gained, with a 16.3% probability of cost-effectiveness at a £20,000 threshold. Day-5 health utility was imputed from activity data; robot equipment costs and theatre time were included.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The robotic arm included cystectomy and reconstruction inside the abdomen; open surgery used a lower midline incision. The minimum lymphadenectomy included external iliac, obturator, and internal iliac nodes, extending proximally to the ureteric crossing of the common iliac vessels. For robotic cases, the specimen was removed in a bag through the vagina or an extended port or suprapubic incision. Ileal conduits accounted for 142/161 (88.2%) robotic and 140/156 (89.7%) open operations.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Median operative duration was 4.92 (IQR 4.03–5.70) versus 4.50 (IQR 3.50–5.47) hours, and median blood loss was 200 (IQR 125–375) versus 550 (IQR 400–859) mL. Of those randomized, 161/169 in the robotic arm and 156/169 in the open arm underwent cystectomy, including crossover operations.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Any complication within 12 weeks occurred in 102/161 (63.4%) and 106/156 (67.9%) operated patients. Clavien-Dindo grade III–V complications occurred in 25/151 (16.6%) versus 33/150 (22.0%) patients with grading data. Thromboembolic complications occurred in 3/161 (1.9%) versus 13/156 (8.3%); wound complications in 9/161 (5.6%) versus 27/156 (17.3%); and infections in 38/161 (23.6%) versus 52/156 (33.3%). Postoperative transfusion occurred in 11/158 (7.0%) versus 18/149 (12.1%). Ureteroileal stricture rates were 2.5% among 161 robotic and 0.6% among 156 open operations within the genitourinary complication category. Death within 90 days occurred in 2/161 (1.2%) and 4/156 (2.6%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The unblinded team could influence admission and readmission decisions that contribute to the primary endpoint. Recruitment ended early during the COVID-19 pandemic, which also disrupted in-person follow-up. Accredited surgeons at experienced centers performed the operations. Oncologic follow-up in the primary report was relatively short. The economic analysis used UK costs, assumed robot throughput and equipment life, and modeled early health utility rather than measuring it directly.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="iroc" />

RAZOR tested robotic cystectomy with extracorporeal reconstruction and used cancer recurrence as its primary question; iROC tested an intracorporeal surgical package with early recovery as its primary endpoint.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU 2026 muscle-invasive bladder cancer guideline cites iROC in its robotic cystectomy evidence review. It advises informing patients of the advantages and disadvantages of open and robotic cystectomy <Rec>(Strong)</Rec> and selecting centers by experience rather than technique <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
