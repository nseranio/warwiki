# Eriksen Estring: Estradiol-Releasing Vaginal Ring for Recurrent UTI Prevention

<TrialBottomLine id="eriksen-estring" />

**Eriksen Estring** was a multicenter, open-label randomized trial in postmenopausal women with recurrent symptomatic, bacteriologically confirmed urinary tract infections; Estring prolonged time to first recurrence compared with no estrogen treatment over 36 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="eriksen-estring" />

## Design

The investigators randomized 108 women, 53 to the estradiol ring and 55 to no estrogen. The primary endpoint was time to the first recurrent UTI, compared by a log-rank test of Kaplan–Meier curves. Both intention-to-treat and per-protocol analyses were planned. Secondary assessments included vaginal pH, vaginal and urethral mucosal maturation, and urogenital symptoms. Control participants were followed for 36 weeks or until their first recurrence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The estimated proportion remaining free of UTI at 36 weeks was approximately 45% with Estring and 20% without estrogen (log-rank P = 0.008). These are survival estimates for the primary time-to-event outcome. Estring lowered vaginal pH; vaginal mucosal cells and, to a lesser extent, urethral mucosal cells were more mature in the ring group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Estring was a vaginal silicone ring containing 2 mg of estradiol. Each ring was worn for 12 weeks during the 36-week treatment period.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

No unexpected adverse events were reported.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Open-label allocation and an untreated control could influence symptom reporting and urine-culture capture.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="eriksen-estring" />

Raz and Stamm's earlier randomized trial used intravaginal estriol cream against placebo and likewise found fewer recurrent UTIs; Eriksen tested an estradiol ring against an untreated control.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA/CUA/SUFU recurrent UTI guideline, amended in 2025, recommends vaginal estrogen for perimenopausal and postmenopausal women with recurrent UTI when there is no contraindication <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The AUA/SUFU/AUGS 2025 genitourinary syndrome of menopause guideline recommends local low-dose vaginal estrogen for patients with GSM and recurrent UTI to reduce future infections <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
