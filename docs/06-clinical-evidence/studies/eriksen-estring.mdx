# Eriksen Estring: Estradiol-Releasing Vaginal Ring for Recurrent UTI Prevention

<TrialBottomLine id="eriksen-estring" />

**Eriksen Estring** was a multicenter, open-label randomized trial in women at least two years after menopause with three or more treated UTIs in the previous year, the last bacteriologically confirmed. Estring prolonged time to first symptomatic, bacteriologically confirmed recurrence compared with no estrogen treatment over 36 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="eriksen-estring" />

## Design

The investigators randomized 108 women, 53 to the estradiol ring and 55 to no estrogen. The primary endpoint was time to the first symptomatic, bacteriologically confirmed UTI, compared by a log-rank test of Kaplan–Meier curves. Both intention-to-treat and per-protocol analyses were performed; 9 ring recipients and 5 controls were excluded from the per-protocol analysis of the primary endpoint. Secondary assessments included vaginal pH, vaginal and urethral mucosal maturation, and urogenital symptoms. Control participants were followed for 36 weeks or until their first recurrence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Estring delayed the first recurrent UTI: the Kaplan–Meier estimate of remaining infection-free at 36 weeks was approximately 45% with the ring and 20% without estrogen (log-rank P = 0.008). In the intention-to-treat groups, 27/53 (51%) ring recipients and 44/55 (80%) controls had a recurrence during observation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Vaginal mucosal atrophy resolved by termination in 34/51 (67%) ring recipients and 1/50 (2%) controls with atrophy at baseline (P = 0.001). Vaginal and urethral mucosal maturation differed between groups (both P &lt; .001), and ring-group vaginal pH fell by week 12 (P &lt; .001). Among women with symptoms at baseline, urge incontinence became absent in 50% versus 16% (P = .03), and stress incontinence in 48% versus 11% (P = .003).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Estring was a silicone ring containing 2 mg of 17β-estradiol, inserted into the upper third of the vaginal vault. It was worn continuously for 12 ± 2 weeks, replaced at weeks 12 and 24, and removed at week 36.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Three serious adverse events occurred among 53 ring recipients and one among 55 controls; investigators judged a causal relationship with ring treatment unlikely. After treatment began, 3/53 ring recipients reported vaginal bleeding and 8/53 reported nonphysiologic discharge. Five ring recipients withdrew before completion or first recurrence (3 for local discomfort, 2 by choice), compared with 1 control (by choice); the authors considered both ring-group withdrawals by choice plausibly adverse-event related. Counts of new or worsened events were higher with Estring (P = .039), while the proportion of women with a newly reported adverse event did not differ (P = 1.0).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Open-label allocation and an untreated control could influence symptom reporting. Only the last pre-enrollment UTI required bacteriologic confirmation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="eriksen-estring" />

Raz and Stamm's earlier randomized trial used intravaginal estriol cream against placebo and likewise found fewer recurrent UTIs; Eriksen tested an estradiol ring against an untreated control.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA/CUA/SUFU recurrent UTI guideline, amended in 2025, recommends vaginal estrogen for perimenopausal and postmenopausal women with recurrent UTI when there is no contraindication <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The AUA/SUFU/AUGS 2025 genitourinary syndrome of menopause guideline recommends local low-dose vaginal estrogen for patients with GSM and recurrent UTI to reduce future infections <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
