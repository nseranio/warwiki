# Cai ASB: Antibiotic Treatment of Asymptomatic Bacteriuria in Young Women With Recurrent Urinary Tract Infection

<TrialBottomLine id="cai-asb" />

**Cai ASB** was a single-center, open-label randomized trial of withholding versus treating asymptomatic bacteriuria in young women with recurrent urinary tract infection. Through 12 months, 237/312 (76.0%) analyzed women without treatment and 62/361 (17.2%) given susceptibility-guided antibiotics remained recurrence-free.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="cai-asb" />

## Design

The investigators at an Italian sexually transmitted disease center screened consecutive sexually active women aged 18–40 years who had previously received treatment for symptomatic UTI and were asymptomatic at enrollment. Asymptomatic bacteriuria required the same uropathogen at at least 10^5 CFU/mL in two consecutive midstream cultures. Pregnancy, diabetes, urinary tract abnormalities, stones, retention, catheterization, recent antimicrobial use, prophylactic antibiotics, and vaginitis or cervicitis were exclusions. A computer-generated schedule assigned 330 women to no treatment and 369 to susceptibility-guided treatment; 312 and 361, respectively, entered the reported analysis after losses to follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The prespecified main outcome was the recurrence-free rate at the end of follow-up. Women with symptomatic UTI were treated and censored at each visit; the final-visit risk sets therefore differed from the original analyzed groups. The trial used two-sided P &lt; 0.05 as its significance threshold. Patients and investigators were unblinded because the untreated group received no placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Recurrence-free rates through 12 months favored no treatment: 237/312 (76.0%) versus 62/361 (17.2%) among analyzed women. The Kaplan–Meier recurrence comparison reported RR 2.14 (SE 0.187; P = 0.003). At the final visit, new symptomatic UTI was recorded in 41/278 (14.7%) versus 169/231 (73.2%) women still under observation; the abstract reports the same episode counts over the original analyzed groups as 41/312 (13.1%) versus 169/361 (46.8%). The authors report an RR of 3.17 for final-visit symptomatic recurrence with treatment versus no treatment (95% CI 2.55–3.90; P &lt; 0.0001), which differs from crude ratios using either denominator set.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

At 6 months, new symptomatic episodes occurred in 23/301 (7.6%) versus 98/329 (29.8%) women still under observation (reported RR 1.31, 95% CI 1.21–1.42; P &lt; 0.0001). At 12 months, mean Quality of Well-Being scores were 0.82 ± 0.03 without treatment and 0.51 ± 0.02 with treatment (P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The later observational report found higher resistance to three antibiotics among *E. coli* recovered from symptomatic episodes in the original treated group after a mean 38.8 months: amoxicillin–clavulanate, 1/26 (3.8%) versus 23/93 (24.7%; P = 0.03); trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole, 3/26 (11.5%) versus 32/93 (34.4%; P = 0.01); and ciprofloxacin, 5/26 (19.2%) versus 41/93 (44.1%; P = 0.03). The other tested agents showed no significant between-group difference; the authors reported significant susceptibility differences beginning at 2 years. Among the 550 women retained from the original analyzed cohort, symptomatic recurrence occurred in 97/257 (37.7%) without initial treatment versus 204/293 (69.6%) with treatment (P &lt; 0.001). Mean Quality of Well-Being scores were 0.83 ± 0.05 versus 0.54 ± 0.03 (P &lt; 0.01).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The initial intervention was a urine-culture decision, with no procedure or device. Women assigned to treatment received an antibiotic selected from the antibiogram; fosfomycin trometamol (31.4%), nitrofurantoin (26.8%), co-trimoxazole (19.8%), ciprofloxacin (13.1%), and levofloxacin (8.9%) were used. Symptomatic infections in either group were cultured and treated according to susceptibility. Scheduled cultures occurred at 3, 6, and 12 months in the original trial.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In the longer follow-up, visits occurred every 6 months, no cultures were scheduled for asymptomatic women, and symptomatic episodes were cultured and treated by susceptibility. Among 301 symptomatic women, 85 received fosfomycin trometamol, 59 nitrofurantoin, 37 amoxicillin–clavulanate, 35 co-trimoxazole, and 30 ciprofloxacin.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Pyelonephritis occurred in 1/312 women without bacteriuria treatment and 2/361 treated women during the randomized year; no antimicrobial adverse events were reported.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> During the later observational follow-up, pyelonephritis occurred in 2/257 (0.8%) and 6/293 (2.0%), respectively (P = 0.37), and no antibiotic adverse effects were reported among the 301 women treated for symptomatic UTI.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The original report analyzed 673 of 699 randomized women and did not use placebo. Its reported final-visit symptomatic-recurrence RR differs from crude ratios using either the analyzed-group or final-visit denominators. The later observational resistance analysis retained 550 of those 673 women and included isolates only from women who became symptomatic; the retained groups differed in prior UTI frequency at entry.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

- **AUA/CUA/SUFU (2025 amendment).** Omit surveillance urine testing, including culture, in asymptomatic patients with recurrent UTI <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C)</Rec>; do not treat asymptomatic bacteriuria <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **EAU (2026 limited update).** Do not screen or treat asymptomatic bacteriuria in patients with recurrent urinary tract infections <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
