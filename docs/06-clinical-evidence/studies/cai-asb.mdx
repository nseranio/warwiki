# Cai ASB: Antibiotic Treatment of Asymptomatic Bacteriuria in Young Women With Recurrent Urinary Tract Infection

<TrialBottomLine id="cai-asb" />

**Cai ASB** was a single-center, open-label randomized trial of withholding versus treating asymptomatic bacteriuria in young women with recurrent urinary tract infection; symptomatic recurrence at 12 months occurred in 41/312 (13.1%) analyzed women without treatment and 169/361 (46.8%) given susceptibility-guided antibiotics.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="cai-asb" />

## Design

The investigators at an Italian sexually transmitted disease center screened consecutive sexually active women aged 18–40 years who had previously received treatment for symptomatic UTI and were asymptomatic at enrollment. Asymptomatic bacteriuria required the same uropathogen at at least 10^5 CFU/mL in two consecutive midstream cultures. Pregnancy, diabetes, urinary tract abnormalities, stones, retention, catheterization, recent antimicrobial use, prophylactic antibiotics, and vaginitis or cervicitis were exclusions. A computer-generated schedule assigned 330 women to no treatment and 369 to susceptibility-guided treatment; 312 and 361, respectively, entered the reported analysis after losses to follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The prespecified main outcome was the recurrence-free rate at the end of follow-up. The report presents symptomatic recurrence as its complement. It tested the null hypothesis of no difference between groups and used two-sided significance testing. Patients and investigators were unblinded because the untreated group received no placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At 12 months, symptomatic recurrence was lower without antibiotic treatment. The article reports an RR of 3.17 for treatment versus no treatment (95% CI 2.55–3.90; P &lt; 0.0001). Its reported RR does not match the crude ratio of the published analyzed-group risks, while both group counts and the reported comparison favor no treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The later report followed 550 women from the original analyzed cohort for a mean 38.8 months. In this complete-case observational follow-up, 97/257 (37.7%) women from the untreated group and 204/293 (69.6%) from the treated group had symptomatic recurrence (P &lt; 0.001). Among *E. coli* isolates recovered from symptomatic episodes, resistance was 1/26 versus 23/93 for amoxicillin–clavulanate, 3/26 versus 32/93 for trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole, and 5/26 versus 41/93 for ciprofloxacin; each comparison favored the original untreated group.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The initial intervention was a urine-culture decision, with no procedure or device. Women assigned to treatment received an antibiotic selected from the antibiogram; fosfomycin trometamol (31.4%), nitrofurantoin (26.8%), co-trimoxazole (19.8%), ciprofloxacin (13.1%), and levofloxacin (8.9%) were used.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Symptomatic infections in either group were cultured and treated according to susceptibility. Scheduled cultures occurred at 3, 6, and 12 months in the original trial. In the longer follow-up, visits occurred every 6 months and cultures were obtained for symptomatic episodes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Pyelonephritis occurred in 1/312 women without bacteriuria treatment and 2/361 treated women during the randomized year.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> During the later observational follow-up, it occurred in 2/257 (0.8%) and 6/293 (2.0%), respectively; the latter comparison had P = 0.37.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The original report analyzed 673 of 699 randomized women and did not use placebo. It reports 12-month recurrence percentages using the full analyzed-group denominators, but a different set of percentages in its follow-up narrative and table, without reconciling them. The later resistance analysis retained 550 of those 673 women, compared isolates from women who became symptomatic, and described its design as observational; the retained groups differed in prior UTI frequency at entry.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

- **AUA/CUA/SUFU (2025 amendment).** Omit surveillance urine testing, including culture, in asymptomatic patients with recurrent UTI <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C)</Rec>; do not treat asymptomatic bacteriuria <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **EAU (2026 limited update).** Do not screen or treat asymptomatic bacteriuria in patients with recurrent urinary tract infections <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
