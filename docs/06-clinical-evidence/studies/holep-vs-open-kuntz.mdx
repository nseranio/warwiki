# Kuntz HoLEP versus open prostatectomy: Enucleation for prostates larger than 100 g

<TrialBottomLine id="holep-vs-open-kuntz" />

**The Kuntz–Lehrich trial** randomized 120 urodynamically obstructed men with prostates larger than 100 g to holmium laser enucleation of the prostate (HoLEP) or transvesical open prostatectomy; voiding outcomes improved similarly through six months, while HoLEP reduced blood loss, catheterization, hospitalization and transfusions at the cost of longer operating time.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="holep-vs-open-kuntz" />

## Design

Prostate size was measured by transrectal ultrasonography, and obstruction was confirmed urodynamically. The study assessed AUA symptom score, peak urinary flow and postvoid residual at baseline and at one, three and six months, with assessments through 18 months reported later.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The five-year report used a prostate-volume entry threshold of at least 100 mL, excluded prior prostate or urethral surgery, and specified P=0.05 for comparisons without designating a primary endpoint or equivalence margin.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Results

Through 18 months, both operations significantly improved symptom score, peak flow and residual volume, with no significant between-arm differences at any interval.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> At five years, 42/60 HoLEP and 32/60 open-surgery patients completed assessment: mean AUA symptom score was 3.0 versus 3.0 (P=0.98), peak flow 24.3 versus 24.4 mL/s (P=0.97), and postvoid residual 10.6 versus 5.3 mL (P=0.25).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The later operative report describes HoLEP with an 80–100 W Ho:YAG laser at 2.0 J and 40–50 Hz. Mean operating time was 136 versus 91 minutes, mean hemoglobin loss 1.9 versus 2.8 g/dL, median catheter time one versus six days, and median stay two versus ten days for HoLEP and open surgery, respectively.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The five-year report describes retrograde enucleation from apex to bladder, with electrocautery loop fragmentation of the attached lobes in 50/60 HoLEP patients and mechanical morcellation in 10/60.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Adverse events

Transfusion was required in 0/60 HoLEP and 8/60 open-surgery patients.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The 18-month report recorded complications in 7/60 versus 13/60 patients, secondary apical resection in 2/60 versus 0/60, and bladder-neck contracture in 0/60 versus 2/60.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Through five years, late reoperation occurred in 3/60 (5.0%) versus 4/60 (6.7%; P=1.0): urethral stricture requiring visual urethrotomy in 2/60 versus 1/60 and bladder-neck contracture requiring laser incision in 1/60 versus 3/60.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

The comparison was conducted at one center, and the five-year report does not state blinding or a formal equivalence threshold. At five years, 46/120 randomized men had been lost to follow-up or excluded, including 11 deaths, nine incidental prostate cancers and seven reoperations; 42/60 HoLEP and 32/60 open-surgery patients completed assessment.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU male LUTS guideline (March 2026) cites this five-year trial in its open-prostatectomy evidence review and recommends offering HoLEP to men with moderate-to-severe LUTS/benign prostatic obstruction as an alternative to TURP or open prostatectomy <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
