# Kuntz HoLEP versus open prostatectomy: Enucleation for prostates larger than 100 g

<TrialBottomLine id="holep-vs-open-kuntz" />

**The Kuntz–Lehrich trial** randomized 120 urodynamically obstructed men with prostates larger than 100 g to holmium laser enucleation of the prostate (HoLEP) or transvesical open prostatectomy; voiding outcomes improved similarly through six months, while HoLEP reduced blood loss, catheterization, hospitalization and transfusions at the cost of longer operating time.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="holep-vs-open-kuntz" />

## Design

Prostate size was measured by transrectal ultrasonography, and obstruction was confirmed urodynamically. The study assessed AUA symptom score, peak urinary flow and postvoid residual at baseline and at one, three and six months. The primary abstract does not identify a prespecified primary endpoint or formal between-group margin.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Both operations improved symptom score, peak flow and residual volume significantly at six months; the primary report described no between-arm difference in these measures but did not give endpoint values in its abstract. The five-year assessment included 42 HoLEP and 32 open-surgery patients: mean AUA symptom score was 3.0 in each group, mean peak flow 24.4 mL/s in each, and mean postvoid residual 11 versus 5 mL, respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The later operative report describes HoLEP with an 80–100 W Ho:YAG laser at 2.0 J and 40–50 Hz. Mean operating time was 136 versus 91 minutes, mean hemoglobin loss 1.9 versus 2.8 g/dL, median catheter time one versus six days, and median stay two versus ten days for HoLEP and open surgery, respectively.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Transfusion was required in 0/60 HoLEP and 8/60 open-surgery patients.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The 18-month report recorded complications in 7/60 versus 13/60 patients, secondary apical resection in 2/60 versus 0/60, and bladder-neck contracture in 0/60 versus 2/60.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Five-year reoperation rates were 5% and 6.7%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

The comparison was conducted at one center, and the abstracts do not state blinding or a formal equivalence threshold. At five years, 46/120 randomized men had been lost to follow-up or excluded, leaving unequal assessed groups.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU male LUTS guideline (March 2026) cites this five-year trial in its open-prostatectomy evidence review and recommends offering HoLEP to men with moderate-to-severe LUTS/benign prostatic obstruction as an alternative to TURP or open prostatectomy <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
