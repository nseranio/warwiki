# Christensen transanal irrigation: Transanal Irrigation Versus Conservative Bowel Management in Spinal Cord Injury

<TrialBottomLine id="transanal-irrigation-sci" />

In a multicenter randomized trial of 87 adults with spinal cord injury and neurogenic bowel dysfunction, transanal irrigation improved constipation and fecal incontinence scores compared with conservative bowel management after 10 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="transanal-irrigation-sci" />

## Design

Five specialized European spinal cord injury centers assigned 42 participants to irrigation and 45 to best supportive bowel care without irrigation. The primary assessments were the Cleveland Clinic constipation score (0–30) and St. Mark's fecal incontinence score (0–24), both with lower scores indicating fewer symptoms. The Neurogenic Bowel Dysfunction score (0–47) and symptom-related quality-of-life measures were additional outcomes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At study termination, in the analysis with baseline observations carried forward for missing assessments, mean constipation scores were 10.3 (SD 4.4; n = 39) with irrigation and 13.2 (SD 3.4; n = 41) with conservative care (P = 0.0016). Mean fecal incontinence scores were 5.0 (SD 4.6) and 7.3 (SD 4.0), respectively (P = 0.015). Mean Neurogenic Bowel Dysfunction scores were 10.4 (SD 6.8) and 13.3 (SD 6.4) (P = 0.048).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

A later within-group analysis combined the original irrigation arm with 20 patients who crossed over after conservative care, yielding 62 patients offered 10 weeks of irrigation. Mean changes from baseline were −3.4 points (95% CI −4.6 to −2.2) for constipation, −4.1 (−5.2 to −2.9) for fecal incontinence, and −4.5 (−6.6 to −2.4) for Neurogenic Bowel Dysfunction (all P &lt; 0.0001). Its multivariable analyses found no consistent, readily obtainable predictor of response.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The secondary report describes use of the Peristeen system: a coated rectal balloon catheter, manual pump, control unit and water container. After catheter insertion and balloon inflation, the patient or caregiver slowly instilled lukewarm tap water, deflated and removed the catheter, then evacuated the water and bowel contents. Specialist nurses provided training; water volume, balloon inflation and treatment frequency were adjusted during the first weeks. Oral laxatives or constipating medicines could continue as needed.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

In the pooled irrigation cohort, 17/62 (27.4%) discontinued during the treatment period; seven had no termination assessment. The secondary analysis substituted baseline responses for missing termination responses.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Device problems led to withdrawal in five patients after repeated catheter expulsion, one after water leakage around the catheter, and one after balloon burst. One additional patient withdrew because of an unspecified adverse event.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The randomized comparison lasted 10 weeks. The secondary report pooled patients who received irrigation at different points in the study and used baseline substitution for missing termination responses.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The Consortium for Spinal Cord Medicine's 2021 guideline recommends transanal irrigation for adults with neurogenic bowel dysfunction after spinal cord injury when basic bowel management gives insufficient results <Rec>(Level - I; Strength - A; Agreement - strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
