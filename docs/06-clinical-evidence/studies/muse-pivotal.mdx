# MUSE pivotal: Transurethral Alprostadil for Organic Erectile Dysfunction

<TrialBottomLine id="muse-pivotal" />

**MUSE pivotal** was a double-blind, placebo-controlled randomized home-treatment trial in men with chronic organic erectile dysfunction who had responded to transurethral alprostadil in clinic; active treatment led to successful intercourse at least once during three months more often than placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="muse-pivotal" />

## Design

The study enrolled 1,511 men aged 27 to 88 years with erectile dysfunction from various organic causes. In-clinic testing identified 996 men (65.9%) with an erection sufficient for intercourse; only these responders entered the randomized home-treatment phase. Of them, 961 reported at least one home administration and contributed to the efficacy comparison. The active arm used the dose that had worked in clinic.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Among home-treatment reporters, 299/461 men (64.9%) assigned alprostadil and 93/500 (18.6%) assigned placebo reported successful intercourse at least once over three months (risk difference 46.3 percentage points; P &lt; 0.001). The report also described intercourse after an average of 7 in 10 alprostadil administrations in men responsive to treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In a later retrospective analysis of 384 participants whose erectile dysfunction followed radical prostatectomy, 70.3% had an intercourse-sufficient erection in clinic. Among those given active medication at home, 57.1% reported intercourse at least once; the authors estimated 40.1% overall success by multiplying clinic and home response rates.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The in-clinic sequence tested up to four transurethral doses: 125, 250, 500 and 1,000 micrograms. Men with an intercourse-sufficient response were assigned their effective dose or placebo for home use.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Mild penile pain followed 10.8% of alprostadil treatments. Hypotension occurred in clinic in 3.3% of men receiving alprostadil; hypotension-related symptoms were uncommon at home. The report recorded no priapism or penile fibrosis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Randomization followed a positive dose test, so the home-treatment response rate describes men who had already achieved an intercourse-sufficient erection in clinic. The reported efficacy comparison included 961 of the 996 randomized responders who reported at least one home administration.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA erectile dysfunction guideline (2018) advises informing men about intraurethral alprostadil and its benefits, risks and burdens <Rec>(Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C)</Rec>. For men considering it, the guideline calls for an in-office test <Rec>(Clinical Principle)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The EAU sexual and reproductive health guideline (2026) offers topical or intraurethral alprostadil as an alternative first-line therapy for well-informed patients who cannot or do not want oral vasoactive therapy, decline injections, or prefer a less invasive option <Rec>(Weak)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
