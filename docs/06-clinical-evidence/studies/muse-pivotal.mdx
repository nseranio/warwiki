# MUSE pivotal: Transurethral Alprostadil for Organic Erectile Dysfunction

<TrialBottomLine id="muse-pivotal" />

**MUSE pivotal** was a double-blind, placebo-controlled randomized home-treatment trial in men with chronic organic erectile dysfunction who had responded to transurethral alprostadil in clinic; active treatment led to successful intercourse at least once during three months more often than placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="muse-pivotal" />

## Design

The study enrolled 1,511 men aged 27 to 88 years with erectile dysfunction from various organic causes. In-clinic testing identified 996 men (65.9%) with an erection sufficient for intercourse; only these responders entered the randomized home-treatment phase. The 996 responders were randomized 485 to alprostadil and 511 to placebo; 961 reported at least one home administration and contributed to the efficacy comparison. Of the randomized responders, 873 (87.7%) completed three months. The active arm used the dose that had worked in clinic.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Among home-treatment reporters, 299/461 men (64.9%) assigned alprostadil and 93/500 (18.6%) assigned placebo reported successful intercourse at least once over three months (risk difference 46.3 percentage points; P &lt; 0.001). Intercourse followed 2,485/4,933 (50.4%) alprostadil administrations and 454/4,346 (10.4%) placebo administrations (P &lt; 0.001). Among the 299 active-treatment men reporting intercourse at least once, it followed 2,485/3,593 (69.2%) administrations. Orgasm occurred at least once in 293/461 (63.6%) active-treatment men and 118/500 (23.6%) placebo men (P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The retrospective post-prostatectomy subgroup also favored active treatment at home: 270/384 men (70.3%) had an intercourse-sufficient erection in clinic, and among 263 home-treatment reporters, 72/126 (57.1%) given alprostadil reported intercourse at least once versus 9/137 (6.6%) given placebo (calculated risk difference 50.6 percentage points; P &lt; 0.001). The authors estimated 40.1% overall success by multiplying the clinic and active home response rates.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The in-clinic sequence tested 125 and 250 micrograms on separate visits, then 500 and 1,000 micrograms if needed. Men selected the most comfortable dose producing an intercourse-sufficient erection; 116 selected 125 micrograms, 171 selected 250 micrograms, 302 selected 500 micrograms and 407 selected 1,000 micrograms. The home dose could not be changed. The 3.2-cm applicator stem deposited the pellet in the distal urethra after urination; mean onset was 7 minutes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Post-prostatectomy participants received the drug through an applicator inserted 3 cm into the distal urethra; 66.3% of clinic responders required 500 or 1,000 micrograms. Of 270 randomized subgroup participants, 243 (90%) completed three months of home treatment, with diaries reviewed monthly.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Penile pain, usually mild, during home treatment affected 159/486 (32.7%) alprostadil recipients versus 17/511 (3.3%) placebo recipients and followed 10.8% of active administrations. Minor urethral trauma affected 25/486 (5.1%) versus 5/511 (1.0%); urinary tract infection, 1/486 (0.2%) versus 3/511 (0.6%); and dizziness, 9/486 (1.9%) versus 1/511 (0.2%). Clinic hypotension occurred in 3.3% and syncope in 0.4% of 1,511 men. No home-treatment syncope was reported; priapism and penile fibrosis occurred in neither home arm (0/486 active; 0/511 placebo) or in clinic. One man received both assignments at home and appears in both safety sets.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the post-prostatectomy subgroup, penile pain affected 49/126 (38.9%) active-treatment patients and followed 132/1,373 (9.6%) active administrations, versus 1.5% of 137 placebo patients. Urethral pain or burning affected 23/126 (18.3%) versus 4.4% of 137, respectively (P &lt; 0.001 for each patient comparison). Clinic hypotension occurred in 3/384 (0.8%) post-prostatectomy men versus 47/1,127 (4.2%) men with other organic causes (P &lt; 0.001); this was measured before randomization. On active home treatment, 2.4% of 126 post-prostatectomy men reported dizziness, and none reported syncope, urinary tract infection, fibrosis or priapism.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

Randomization followed a positive dose test, so the home-treatment response rate describes men who had already achieved an intercourse-sufficient erection in clinic. The reported efficacy comparison included 961 of the 996 randomized responders who reported at least one home administration.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA erectile dysfunction guideline (2018) advises informing men about intraurethral alprostadil and its benefits, risks and burdens <Rec>(Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C)</Rec>. For men considering it, the guideline calls for an in-office test <Rec>(Clinical Principle)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The EAU sexual and reproductive health guideline (2026) offers topical or intraurethral alprostadil as an alternative first-line therapy for well-informed patients who cannot or do not want oral vasoactive therapy, decline injections, or prefer a less invasive option <Rec>(Weak)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
