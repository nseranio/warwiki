# SALTO-2: Laparoscopic Sacrocolpopexy Versus Vaginal Sacrospinous Fixation for Vault Prolapse

<TrialBottomLine id="salto-2" />

**SALTO-2** was a multicenter randomized trial with a parallel treatment-preference cohort in women seeking surgery for symptomatic post-hysterectomy vault prolapse. Laparoscopic sacrocolpopexy and vaginal sacrospinous fixation had no statistically significant difference in disease-specific prolapse quality-of-life scores at 12 months in either comparison.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="salto-2" />

## Design

Nine Dutch hospitals enrolled women suitable for either procedure; prior vault-prolapse surgery and contraindications to surgery excluded participation. The randomized comparison allocated 33 women to laparoscopic sacrocolpopexy and 31 to vaginal fixation. Another 115 women selected their procedure and entered the separate prospective cohort. Patients and surgeons knew the assigned operation. Each surgeon had performed at least 25 cases of both procedures, and participating hospitals agreed on operative steps at a masterclass.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The primary endpoint was the Urogenital Distress Inventory (UDI) genital-prolapse domain at 12 months; lower scores indicated less bother. The prespecified clinically relevant between-group difference was 10 points. Apical composite success required no apical prolapse beyond the hymen, bothersome bulge, repeat surgery or pessary treatment. Concomitant prolapse or stress-incontinence surgery was permitted.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

In the randomized comparison, mean UDI genital-prolapse scores were 8.5 (SEM 5.2; 29 assessed) after laparoscopic sacrocolpopexy and 10.9 (SEM 4.5; 30 assessed) after vaginal fixation (P = 0.887). The preference cohort scores were 7.0 (SEM 3.3; 34 assessed) and 9.1 (SEM 3.3; 49 assessed; P = 0.704). A fixed-effect analysis combining both cohorts gave a mean difference of 1.57 points (95% CI −6.18 to 9.32; P = 0.692).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Apical composite success in the randomized trial was 25/28 (89.3%) after laparoscopic repair and 25/29 (86.2%) after vaginal fixation (reported adjusted OR 0.81, 95% CI 0.15–4.50; P = 0.810). In the preference cohort, it was 28/31 (90.3%) and 36/41 (87.8%). Anterior POP-Q stage 2 or higher occurred in 9/31 (29.0%) and 17/30 (56.7%) randomized participants, respectively (reported adjusted OR 2.91, 95% CI 0.97–8.68; P = 0.056). Any reintervention occurred in 8/31 (25.8%) and 7/30 (23.3%); three vaginal-fixation patients underwent laparoscopic sacrocolpopexy for recurrent apical prolapse.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Laparoscopic sacrocolpopexy used a four-port approach, dissection along the vesicovaginal and rectovaginal planes, a Y-shaped polypropylene mesh fixed to both vaginal walls, sacral-promontory attachment, and peritoneal closure. Vaginal fixation exposed the right sacrospinous ligament through a posterior vaginal incision and secured the vault with two nonabsorbable sutures placed 2 cm medial to the ischial spine.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Mean randomized-trial operative time was 130.5 minutes (SEM 5.2) for laparoscopic repair versus 66.1 minutes (SEM 4.0) for vaginal fixation (P &lt; 0.001). Concomitant procedures occurred in 3/33 (9.1%) and 25/31 (80.6%), respectively; anterior and posterior colporrhaphies predominated in the vaginal group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

One bladder injury during laparoscopic repair was recognized and treated at surgery. Two vaginal-fixation patients had hemorrhage exceeding 1000 mL; one was treated with clips and one conservatively. De novo dyspareunia was reported by two laparoscopic and one vaginal-fixation patient at 12 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Patient preference curtailed randomized enrollment at 64 of 106 planned participants. The preference cohort had baseline differences in vault descent and cannot supply randomized evidence. Some enrolled women had vault prolapse below the specified stage threshold but symptomatic prolapse in another compartment. The 12-month observation period limits assessment of later recurrence and mesh complications.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The Dutch prolapse guideline (2026) advises discussing vaginal sacrospinous fixation as the first choice for a first post-hysterectomy vault prolapse; it offers sacrocolpopexy for recurrent vault prolapse after prior fixation or hysterectomy for prolapse. <Rec>(Zwakke aanbeveling voor sacrospinale fixatie bij vaginatopprolaps na hysterectomie.)</Rec><sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
