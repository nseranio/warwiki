# WATER II: Aquablation in Large Prostates

<TrialBottomLine id="water-ii" />

**WATER II** was a prospective, multicenter, single-arm study of Aquablation in 101 men with symptomatic BPH and 80–150 mL prostates; its three-month procedure-related safety event rate was 45.5% against a prespecified 65% ceiling, and mean IPSS fell from 23.2 to 6.7, meeting the efficacy goal.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="water-ii" />

## Design

The 16 participating centers were in Canada and the United States. Eligibility required bothersome LUTS after inadequate medical therapy, a prostate measured by transrectal ultrasound in the study volume range, and reduced maximum urinary flow. Men with a clinically significant urethral stricture, bladder-neck contracture, active urinary infection, or a prolonged indwelling catheter were excluded; prior prostate surgery did not exclude enrollment.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The trial used performance goals drawn from prior TURP outcomes in smaller glands, rather than a concurrent TURP group. The primary efficacy test required noninferiority to a 16-point mean IPSS reduction with a 5-point margin, equivalent to a reduction statistically greater than 11 points; the primary safety test required a three-month procedure-related event rate below 65%. The safety composite counted Clavien–Dindo grade 2 or higher events and grade 1 events causing persistent incontinence, erectile dysfunction, or ejaculatory disorder.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

Both goals were met at three months: mean IPSS fell from 23.2 ± 6.3 to 6.7 ± 5.1 (P &lt; 0.001), and the safety composite occurred in 45.5% (P &lt; 0.001 against the 65% ceiling). Ninety-eight of 101 men attended the three- and six-month visits. At six months, mean IPSS was 5.9 ± 5.4 (reported paired improvement 17.5 points; P &lt; 0.0001), mean Qmax rose from 8.7 to 18.8 mL/s (P &lt; 0.001), and mean post-void residual fell from 131 to 47 mL (P &lt; 0.0001). No tissue-removal reintervention occurred by six months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

At five years, 60 of 101 men completed the scheduled visit. Among participants with five-year measurements, mean IPSS changed from 22.6 ± 6.4 at baseline to 6.8 ± 4.6, with a reported paired mean change of −15.9 points (P &lt; 0.001). Mean Qmax changed from 8.6 ± 3.4 to 17.1 ± 9.8 mL/s, with a reported paired mean change of +9.2 mL/s (P &lt; 0.001). Three of 101 men underwent another BPH procedure; Kaplan–Meier freedom from a secondary procedure at five years was 96.3%.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The surgeon inserted a 24F transurethral handpiece and planned the resection contour under real-time transrectal ultrasound, avoiding the bladder neck, ejaculatory ducts, verumontanum, and sphincter. A robotically executed waterjet followed the contour; larger glands commonly needed two or more passes. Mean time from handpiece placement to catheter placement was 37 minutes; from ultrasound probe insertion to catheter placement it was 55 minutes, including 8 minutes of waterjet treatment. The mean number of passes was 1.8.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Hemostasis used Foley balloon tamponade at the bladder neck in 98 of 101 men and in the prostatic fossa in the other 3; the balloon contained 40–80 mL, with traction and continuous bladder irrigation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> No electrocautery was used for hemostasis in this cohort. Mean hospital stay was 1.6 days among 100 men; 68% were discharged with a catheter, and mean catheter duration among all 101 treated men was 94 hours.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

By six months, 22/101 men (21.8%) had experienced a procedure- or device-related Clavien–Dindo grade 2 event, 14/101 (13.9%) a grade 3 event, and 5/101 (5.0%) a grade 4 event.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> At three months, persistent grade 1 events affected 18/101 men: ejaculatory dysfunction in 13, incontinence in 3, and both in 2.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Six men had a grade 2 urinary tract infection, one had grade 3 urinary retention, and one received an artificial urinary sphincter for grade 3 stress incontinence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Bleeding events affected 8/101 men before discharge and 6/101 within one month afterward; 8/101 received transfusion alone, 3/101 underwent fulguration alone, and 2/101 received both.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The five-year report recorded procedure-related erectile dysfunction in 0% and ejaculatory dysfunction in 15% at six months.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> It reported no transfusion or fulguration after day 30 through month 60, and no urethral stricture or meatal stenosis during follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The single-arm design permits comparison with prespecified performance goals but cannot estimate a treatment difference against TURP or enucleation. Only 60 of the original 101 participants completed the scheduled five-year visit; the trial extension and COVID-19 disruptions contributed to missing late assessments. The trial's balloon-traction hemostasis protocol also differed from later protocols using focal cautery.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="water-ii" />

WATER studied Aquablation against TURP in smaller prostates, while WATER II followed a separate large-gland cohort. Their outcomes should therefore be read as results from different study designs and populations.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA's 2026 BPH guideline says clinicians may offer robotic waterjet treatment for LUTS/BPH in prostates of 80–150 cc <Rec>(Expert Opinion)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
