# Montorsi 1997: Early Intracavernous Alprostadil After Nerve-Sparing Radical Prostatectomy

<TrialBottomLine id="montorsi-1997" />

Early intracavernous alprostadil increased reported spontaneous erection recovery after nerve-sparing radical retropubic prostatectomy in the prospective randomized **Montorsi 1997** trial of 30 preoperatively potent men with localized prostate cancer.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="montorsi-1997" />

## Design

The enrolled men had clinical stage B1 or B2 disease, biopsy Gleason sums of 3–6 (mean 4.2), and PSA below 20 ng/mL. After prostatectomy, 15 men were assigned injections and 15 observation without erectogenic treatment. Six-month assessment combined sexual history and examination with nocturnal erection recording and cavernosal artery color Doppler sonography. The report did not designate a primary endpoint or a prespecified significance threshold.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At six months, 8/12 injection completers (67%) reported spontaneous intercourse-sufficient erections; 3/15 men assigned observation (20%) reported spontaneous erections, most sufficient for intercourse (reported P &lt; 0.01). The comparison used injection completers and all observation-assigned men.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Among the eight injection responders, 7/8 had normal nocturnal erection testing and 8/8 had normal penile Doppler hemodynamics. All three observation responders had normal findings on both tests.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Partial erectile recovery was reported by 4/12 injection completers (33%), who still needed injections for more than half of intercourse attempts, and by 10/15 controls (67%), whose erections were insufficient for most intromission attempts; another 2/15 controls (13%) reported no erectile activity. Cavernous veno-occlusive dysfunction was found in 2/12 injection completers (17%) and 8/15 controls (53%; reported P &lt; 0.01).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Nerve-sparing radical retropubic prostatectomy followed the updated Walsh technique; urinary catheterization lasted 8–16 days (mean 12). Intracavernous alprostadil began at the one-month visit, three times weekly for 12 weeks (36 planned injections), regardless of sexual activity. The mean erection-inducing dose was 8 μg (range 2.5–14 μg). Of 15 assigned men, 12 completed treatment (80%); two stopped for reduced sexual interest and one for a penile nodule. At the end of treatment, eight complete responders still used an injection before sex once every 4.2 encounters (range once every 3–6).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Penile nodules occurred in 2/15 alprostadil-assigned men (13.3%): one caused treatment discontinuation and one followed a cavernous hematoma in a completer. Prolonged erection occurred in 1/15 (6.7%) and was treated with intracavernous epinephrine. Neither complication was reported in the observation arm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The efficacy comparison excluded three injection-arm noncompleters but included all observation-assigned men. The sample was small, preoperative erectile function lacked objective testing, treatment was apparent to participants, and follow-up ended at six months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
