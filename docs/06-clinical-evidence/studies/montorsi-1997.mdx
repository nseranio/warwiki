# Montorsi 1997: Early Intracavernous Alprostadil After Nerve-Sparing Radical Prostatectomy

<TrialBottomLine id="montorsi-1997" />

The prospective randomized **Montorsi 1997** trial compared early intracavernous alprostadil with observation after nerve-sparing radical retropubic prostatectomy in 30 preoperatively potent men with localized prostate cancer and found more spontaneous intercourse-sufficient erections at six months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="montorsi-1997" />

## Design

Eligible men had clinical stage B1 or B2 disease, Gleason sum at least 7, and PSA below 20 ng/mL. After prostatectomy, 15 men were assigned injections and 15 observation without erectogenic treatment. Six-month assessment combined sexual history and examination with nocturnal erection recording and cavernosal artery color Doppler sonography. The clinical outcome was patient-reported spontaneous erection sufficient for satisfactory intercourse.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At six months, 8/12 injection completers (67%) reported spontaneous intercourse-sufficient erections, compared with 3/15 men assigned observation (20%; P &lt; 0.01). The injection percentage uses completers, while the observation percentage uses everyone assigned to that arm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Among the eight injection responders, 7/8 had normal nocturnal erection testing and 8/8 had normal penile Doppler hemodynamics. All three observation responders had normal findings on both tests.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The operation was nerve-sparing radical retropubic prostatectomy. Assigned treatment was intracavernous alprostadil three times weekly for 12 weeks after surgery; 12/15 assigned men completed the schedule (80%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Penile nodules occurred in 2/15 alprostadil-assigned men (13.3%) and prolonged erection in 1/15 (6.7%). Neither complication was reported in the observation arm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The efficacy comparison excluded three injection-arm noncompleters but included all observation-assigned men. The sample was small, treatment was apparent to participants, and follow-up ended at six months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
