# CPCRN ciprofloxacin–tamsulosin: Ciprofloxacin or Tamsulosin in Men with Chronic Prostatitis/Chronic Pelvic Pain Syndrome

<TrialBottomLine id="cpcrn-cipro-tamsulosin" />

The **CPCRN ciprofloxacin–tamsulosin** trial was a multicenter, double-blind, factorial randomized comparison of ciprofloxacin, tamsulosin, both drugs, and placebo in men with refractory, long-standing CP/CPPS; neither drug significantly improved the six-week NIH Chronic Prostatitis Symptom Index (NIH-CPSI) total score in its factorial comparison.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="cpcrn-cipro-tamsulosin" />

## Design

Urology outpatient clinics at ten tertiary North American centers enrolled men referred by urologists. Entry required an NIH-CPSI score of at least 15; participants had a mean symptom duration of 6.2 years and substantial previous treatment. The two-by-two factorial design estimated each drug's main effect by comparing recipients with nonrecipients of that drug, across the four assigned groups. The primary measure was change in total NIH-CPSI score after treatment. Pain, urinary and quality-of-life subscores, global response, and general quality of life were secondary measures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

NIH-CPSI total scores declined modestly in all groups. The ciprofloxacin main-effect comparison did not meet the primary superiority test (P = 0.15), nor did the tamsulosin comparison (P &gt; 0.2). Secondary outcomes also showed no statistically significant between-treatment differences.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The six-week regimens were oral ciprofloxacin 500 mg twice daily, tamsulosin 0.4 mg once daily, both drugs at those doses, or placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Adverse events were assessed as a trial outcome.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Treatment beyond six weeks was not tested. Participants were heavily pretreated and had long-standing symptoms, so response after less prior treatment remains uncertain.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU chronic pelvic pain guideline (2026) cites the trial and recommends an alpha-blocker for primary prostate pain syndrome of less than one year's duration <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>, and a quinolone or tetracycline for at least six weeks in treatment-naive patients with symptoms lasting less than one year <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The AUA male chronic pelvic pain guideline (2025) recommends offering an alpha-blocker to patients with CP/CPPS and voiding symptoms <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
