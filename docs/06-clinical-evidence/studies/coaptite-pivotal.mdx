# Coaptite pivotal: Multicenter Prospective Randomized 52-Week Trial of Calcium Hydroxylapatite Versus Bovine Dermal Collagen for Stress Urinary Incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="coaptite-pivotal" />

**Coaptite pivotal** was a multicenter, single-blind randomized noninferiority trial in 296 treated women with stress incontinence from intrinsic sphincter deficiency and no urethral hypermobility; calcium hydroxylapatite injection met the prespecified noninferiority criterion against bovine dermal collagen for Stamey-grade improvement at 12 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="coaptite-pivotal" />

## Design

Women received urethral sphincter augmentation with Coaptite or Contigen. The primary endpoint was improvement of at least one Stamey grade from baseline. The FDA review specified a noninferiority boundary based on 15% of the control response proportion.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Randomization preceded screening; treatment allocation was therefore imbalanced. Efficacy at 12 months was available for 231/296 treated women.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

Among women with complete follow-up, 83/131 (63.4%) assigned Coaptite and 57/100 (57.0%) assigned collagen improved by at least one Stamey grade (P = 0.34 for the between-group comparison).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The FDA's treated-population analysis with last observation carried forward found 91/158 (57.6%) versus 70/138 (50.7%).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> These are different analysis populations; the FDA judged the prespecified noninferiority criterion met.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Investigators injected under anesthesia and cystoscopic guidance. Either a transurethral or periurethral route was permitted; the initial injection was transurethral in 145/158 (91.8%) Coaptite patients and 123/138 (89.1%) collagen patients.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Repeat treatment was permitted during the first six months, up to five sessions. Across the treated population, 60/158 (38.0%) versus 36/138 (26.1%) received only one injection (P = 0.034).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The journal reported mean total injected volume of 4.0 versus 6.6 mL during the study (P &lt; 0.0001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Among women with complete 12-month data, the FDA reported 4.0 versus 6.8 mL.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

The FDA review reported treatment-related urinary retention in 41% of Coaptite recipients and 33% of collagen recipients.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Two Coaptite recipients had serious tissue dissection, tissue bridges or erosion; the review could not separate device effects from injection technique. The journal abstract reported no systemic adverse events with either product.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The complete-case efficacy analysis excludes treated women without a 12-month assessment. Injection route was left to investigators, and the small periurethral subgroup cannot establish route-specific outcomes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The FDA approved Coaptite on November 10, 2005 for soft-tissue augmentation in adult women with stress urinary incontinence due to intrinsic sphincter deficiency.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
