# BPH6: Prostatic Urethral Lift Versus Transurethral Resection of the Prostate

<TrialBottomLine id="bph6" />

**BPH6** was a ten-center, open-label randomized noninferiority trial in 80 men with BPH-related lower urinary tract symptoms who were candidates for TURP; prostatic urethral lift (PUL) met noninferiority and superiority versus TURP on the 12-month BPH6 composite responder endpoint.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="bph6" />

## Design

Eligible men were at least 50 years old, had an IPSS above 12, Qmax at most 15 mL/s, and prostate volume at most 60 cc. Allocation was concealed until the procedure, but patients and surgeons could not be blinded. The composite responder endpoint joined symptom relief, quality of recovery, preservation of erections and ejaculation, preservation of continence, and avoidance of high-grade complications. Its components used IPSS, SHIM, MSHQ-EjD, Incontinence Severity Index, a recovery visual analogue scale, and Clavien–Dindo grades. Noninferiority was assessed with the lower confidence limit of the between-arm responder difference.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

The primary report found significant symptom relief in both arms. Ejaculatory function preservation and quality of recovery favored PUL (both P &lt; 0.01).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

At one month, 82% of PUL patients and 53% of TURP patients reached a recovery visual analogue score of at least 70/100 (P = 0.008). At 24 months, paired mean IPSS change was −9.2 points with PUL (n=37) and −15.3 with TURP (n=32; between-arm P = 0.004). Paired Qmax change was +5.0 mL/s (n=27) versus +15.8 mL/s (n=27; P = 0.002). These symptom and flow comparisons favored TURP. The two-year report found no statistically significant between-arm difference in IPSS-related quality of life or BPH Impact Index improvement.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

At 24 months, mean MSHQ-EjD function change was +0.3 points with PUL (n=29) versus −4.0 with TURP (n=27; P &lt; 0.001). Ejaculatory bother did not differ significantly. An improvement in general health utility meeting the study's SF-6D threshold occurred in 16/34 PUL patients (47%) and 12/32 TURP patients (37.5%; P = 0.43). The SF-6D threshold had been established in urinary incontinence, then applied to LUTS in this analysis.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

PUL used permanent transurethral UroLift implants to compress the lateral prostatic tissue and enlarge the lumen without tissue resection or thermal injury. Investigators had performed 0–20 PUL procedures before study entry, while each had extensive TURP experience; TURP technique followed each surgeon's usual practice. Patients returned for scheduled assessment through 24 months.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

By 24 months, secondary treatment for recurrent LUTS occurred in 6/44 PUL patients (13.6%) and 2/35 TURP patients (5.7%). This endpoint included repeat PUL, intradetrusor botulinum toxin, laser treatment, or TURP. One PUL implant placed too proximally protruded into the bladder, caused intermittent haematuria, and was removed. One TURP patient left the study after epididymitis requiring an extended hospital stay.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Average continence scores remained stable after PUL; TURP patients had significant worsening at two weeks and three months. The later report found no sustained worsening at subsequent visits.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The primary report had a small sample and no participant blinding. Its six-part BPH6 responder composite was novel and had not been externally validated. The two-year IPSS analysis retained paired observations from 37 PUL and 32 TURP patients, and PUL operators entered with less procedural experience than for TURP.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The **EAU 2026 male LUTS guideline** recommends offering PUL to men with LUTS and benign prostatic obstruction who want to preserve ejaculation, have prostates &lt;70 mL, and have no middle lobe <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>. It recommends counseling that retreatment rates are higher after PUL than after TURP <Rec>(Weak)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
