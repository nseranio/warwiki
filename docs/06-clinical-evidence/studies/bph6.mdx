# BPH6: Prostatic Urethral Lift Versus Transurethral Resection of the Prostate

<TrialBottomLine id="bph6" />

**BPH6** randomized 91 men at ten centers to prostatic urethral lift (PUL) or TURP; 80 were treated and 79 analyzed. PUL met noninferiority and superiority on the 12-month composite endpoint.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="bph6" />

## Design

Eligible men were sexually active, at least 50 years old, had IPSS above 12, Qmax at most 15 mL/s, and prostate volume at most 60 cc; obstructive median lobe and retention were excluded. Allocation was concealed; patients and surgeons were unblinded. The composite required six outcomes: at least 30% IPSS reduction, recovery score at least 70/100 by one month, preserved erections and ejaculation, Incontinence Severity Index at most 4, and no treatment-related Clavien–Dindo grade II+ event.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The noninferiority margin was 10 percentage points.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

PUL had higher 12-month composite response: 23/44 (52.3%) versus 7/35 (20.0%); difference 32 points (95% CI 10–51; superiority P = 0.005; noninferiority P &lt; 0.0001). Eleven randomized men declined treatment; one treated PUL patient was excluded. The reported endpoint used a recovery threshold lowered from 80 to 70/100 and sustained sexual-function assessment instead of one visit.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

TURP produced greater 12-month IPSS reduction: −15.4 (n=32) versus −11.4 points (n=41), difference −4.0 (95% CI −7.7 to −0.4; P = 0.02). Ejaculation was preserved in 100% after PUL versus 61% after TURP (P &lt; 0.0001); recovery reached 70/100 by one month in 82% versus 53% (difference 29 points, 95% CI 6–49; P = 0.008).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

At 24 months, paired mean IPSS change was −9.2 points with PUL (n=37) and −15.3 with TURP (n=32; between-arm P = 0.004). Paired Qmax change was +5.0 mL/s (n=27) versus +15.8 mL/s (n=27; P = 0.002). These symptom and flow comparisons favored TURP. The two-year report found no statistically significant between-arm difference in IPSS-related quality of life or BPH Impact Index improvement.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

At 24 months, mean MSHQ-EjD function change was +0.3 points with PUL (n=29) versus −4.0 with TURP (n=27; P &lt; 0.001). Ejaculatory bother did not differ significantly. An improvement in general health utility meeting the study's SF-6D threshold occurred in 16/34 PUL patients (47%) and 12/32 TURP patients (37.5%; P = 0.43). The SF-6D threshold had been established in urinary incontinence, then applied to LUTS in this analysis.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

PUL used permanent transurethral UroLift implants to compress the lateral prostatic tissue and enlarge the lumen without tissue resection or thermal injury. Investigators had performed 0–20 PUL procedures before study entry, while each had extensive TURP experience; TURP technique followed each surgeon's usual practice. Patients returned for scheduled assessment through 24 months.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

PUL used a mean 4.7 implants; TURP comprised 12 unipolar and 23 bipolar resections. Anesthesia averaged 55 versus 71 minutes, discharge 1.0 versus 1.9 days, return to activity 11 (n=43) versus 17 days (n=32), and catheterization beyond 24 hours 45% versus 74%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Treatment-related Clavien–Dindo grades I, II, and IIIb affected 30/44, 3/44, and 4/44 PUL patients versus 26/35, 4/35, and 5/35 TURP patients. Retention occurred in 4/44 versus 0/35, UTI in 3/44 versus 2/35, and retrograde ejaculation in 0/44 versus 7/35. Reintervention occurred in 3/44 versus 5/35: recurrent LUTS treatment in 3 versus 2, two TURP bleeding interventions, and one TURP urethrotomy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

By 24 months, secondary treatment for recurrent LUTS occurred in 6/44 PUL patients (13.6%) and 2/35 TURP patients (5.7%). This endpoint included repeat PUL, intradetrusor botulinum toxin, laser treatment, or TURP. One PUL implant placed too proximally protruded into the bladder, caused intermittent haematuria, and was removed. One TURP patient left the study after epididymitis requiring an extended hospital stay.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Average continence scores remained stable after PUL; TURP patients had significant worsening at two weeks and three months. The later report found no sustained worsening at subsequent visits.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The primary report had a small sample and no participant blinding. Its six-part BPH6 responder composite was novel and had not been externally validated. The two-year IPSS analysis retained paired observations from 37 PUL and 32 TURP patients, and PUL operators entered with less procedural experience than for TURP.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The **EAU 2026 male LUTS guideline** recommends offering PUL to men with LUTS and benign prostatic obstruction who want to preserve ejaculation, have prostates &lt;70 mL, and have no middle lobe <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>. It recommends counseling that retreatment rates are higher after PUL than after TURP <Rec>(Weak)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
