# CUPIDO 1: Vaginal Prolapse Repair With or Without a Midurethral Sling

<TrialBottomLine id="cupido-1" />

**CUPIDO 1** was an open-label, multicenter randomized trial of vaginal prolapse repair with or without a concomitant midurethral sling in women with symptomatic stage II or greater prolapse and overt stress urinary incontinence (SUI); at 12 months, absence of SUI was 49/63 (78%) with sling and 28/71 (39%) without (RR 1.97, 95% CI 1.44–2.71).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="cupido-1" />

## Design

Fourteen Dutch teaching hospitals enrolled women scheduled for vaginal prolapse surgery whose SUI was reported on the Urogenital Distress Inventory (UDI) or demonstrated by cough stress testing without prolapse reduction. Leakage had to occur more than once weekly and predominate over urgency incontinence. Prior incontinence surgery, isolated posterior prolapse, elevated postvoid residual, and recent prolapse surgery excluded enrollment. The surgeons selected the prolapse repair according to compartment and stage; either retropubic or transobturator sling placement was permitted after the repair. Participants and surgeons were unblinded.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The prespecified 12-month outcomes were UDI-reported absence of any urinary incontinence and of SUI, and receipt of additional SUI or overactive-bladder (OAB) treatment during the first postoperative year. The sample-size calculation used a one-sided test for subjective SUI; analysis was by randomized assignment. Of 138 randomized women, 134 were analyzed after randomization errors and a withdrawal.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Absence of any urinary incontinence was reported by 39/63 (62%) women assigned to sling and 21/71 (30%) controls (RR 2.09, 95% CI 1.39–3.15). Additional SUI treatment was received by 6/63 (10%) and 26/71 (37%), respectively (RR 0.26, 95% CI 0.11–0.59). OAB treatment was received by 12/63 (19%) and 10/71 (14%) (RR 1.35, 95% CI 0.63–2.91). The reported one-sided P values for absence of any incontinence and absence of SUI were each &lt;0.0001.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Objective SUI, a secondary endpoint, was found in 4/25 (16%) versus 14/32 (44%) women with an evaluable cough test (RR 0.37, 95% CI 0.14–0.97). Moderately or greatly bothersome SUI was reported by 5/63 (8%) versus 14/70 (20%); its confidence interval included no difference (RR 0.40, 95% CI 0.15–1.04).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Anterior repair was performed in 58/63 (92%) sling-assigned women and 65/71 (92%) controls; apical repair in 40/63 (63%) and 46/71 (65%); and posterior repair in 26/63 (41%) and 30/71 (42%). Among sling-assigned women, 10 received a retropubic sling, 52 a transobturator sling, and one declined sling placement. One control-assigned woman received a sling at the index operation after duplicate randomization and remained in the control analysis. Treatment for persistent SUI was initiated according to patient and clinician preference: 12/71 (17%) controls subsequently received a midurethral sling, compared with none in the initial-sling arm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Any complication occurred in 33/63 (52%) women assigned to sling and 24/71 (34%) controls (RR 1.55, 95% CI 1.04–2.32). Severe complications occurred in 10/63 (16%) and 4/71 (6%); the difference was not statistically significant (RR 2.82, 95% CI 0.93–8.54). Surgery for a complication occurred in 8/63 (13%) and 3/71 (4%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Incomplete emptying requiring catheterization beyond one week occurred in 14/63 (22%) and 14/71 (20%); two sling patients required catheterization beyond six weeks. Antibiotic-treated urinary infection occurred in 8/63 (13%) and 6/71 (9%). Bladder injury occurred in 2/63 (3%) and 1/71 (1%). One perforation during transobturator sling placement was followed by urethral tape exposure and partial tape removal with a Martius flap. Sling-related reoperations also included excision for vaginal tape extrusion, tape removal for thigh pain, and urethrolysis for obstructive voiding.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Enrollment was low relative to the hospitals' estimated eligible population, and the trial was not powered to resolve the severe-complication difference. The open-label design could influence symptom reporting and decisions about later SUI treatment. Only a subset had an objective cough test at adequate bladder filling. The one-year endpoint may miss later sling procedures or complications after staged sling placement.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU 2026 Non-neurogenic Female LUTS guideline cites CUPIDO 1 among trials in women with preoperative SUI undergoing vaginal prolapse surgery. It advises offering simultaneous prolapse and SUI surgery only after discussing the benefits and risks against prolapse surgery alone <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>, and informing women of the increased adverse-event risk with combined surgery <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
