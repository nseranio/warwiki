# Tadalafil BPH dose-finding trial: Once-Daily Tadalafil for BPH-Associated Lower Urinary Tract Symptoms

<TrialBottomLine id="tadalafil-bph-pivotal" />

The multinational, double-blind dose-finding trial randomized 1,058 men with BPH-associated lower urinary tract symptoms to once-daily tadalafil 2.5, 5, 10, or 20 mg or placebo; all tadalafil doses improved International Prostate Symptom Score (IPSS) more than placebo after 12 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="tadalafil-bph-pivotal" />

## Design

Participants completed a four-week placebo run-in before assignment to one of five parallel groups. Change in total IPSS from baseline to endpoint was the principal efficacy measure. The randomized groups contained 209, 212, 216, and 209 men assigned to ascending tadalafil doses and 212 assigned to placebo. The trial registry identifies the study as phase 2/3 and participant and investigator masked.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The erectile-function analysis selected men who reported ED and expected to remain sexually active with a female partner; it required no minimum number of intercourse attempts.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Results

Least-squares mean IPSS changes were −3.9, −4.9, −5.2, and −5.2 points with ascending tadalafil doses, versus −2.3 points with placebo (2.5 mg: P = 0.015; other doses: P &lt; 0.001). The authors selected 5 mg as the most favorable risk-benefit dose. No dose produced a statistically significant between-group peak-flow improvement.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Erectile function improved in the post hoc BPH/ED subgroup: among 581 included men, 579 contributed efficacy data. Least-squares mean IIEF-EF changes were +5.4 (2.5 mg; n=113), +6.8 (5 mg; n=117), +7.9 (10 mg; n=120), and +8.2 points (20 mg; n=115), versus +2.0 with placebo (n=114; P &lt; 0.001 for each dose).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Among men with baseline IIEF-EF &lt;26, normal function (IIEF-EF at least 26) was reached by 40/117 (34.2%) at 5 mg versus 16/114 (14.0%) with placebo.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> In the post hoc BPH/ED cohort, least-squares mean IPSS change at 5 mg was −4.2 points (n=114) versus −2.1 with placebo (n=113; P = 0.004); all four doses improved IPSS, while Qmax did not differ significantly.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> A separate post hoc uroflowmetry analysis found peak-flow increases of 16% with 5 mg and 12% with placebo, without a significant difference; postvoid residual did not change significantly.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

During a one-year open-label extension, all 427 entrants received tadalafil 5 mg daily. The investigators reported maintenance of the IPSS improvement; 299/427 completed the extension.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Treatment was oral once daily after the placebo run-in. The extension gave 5 mg daily regardless of each participant's original assignment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Adverse events

The initial report described treatment-emergent adverse events as infrequent across tadalafil groups. In the open-label extension, 57.6% of entrants reported a treatment-emergent adverse event and 22/427 (5.2%) discontinued because of one.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

In the BPH/ED subgroup, treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 30/117 (25.6%) men at 5 mg versus 24/115 (20.9%) with placebo; serious adverse events occurred in 1/117 (0.9%) versus 1/115 (0.9%), and adverse-event discontinuation in 6/117 (5.1%) versus 2/115 (1.7%). Dyspepsia occurred in 5/117 (4.3%) versus 0/115; no urinary retention was reported in tadalafil-treated men, and one placebo-treated man had a urinary tract infection.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

The randomized comparison lasted 12 weeks. The extension enrolled 427 prior trial completers, treated everyone with tadalafil, and had no concurrent placebo group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> The BPH/ED analysis was post hoc; the full trial was powered for IPSS, and the report performed multiple subgroup tests.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA 2026 BPH guideline recommends offering tadalafil 5 mg daily to men with LUTS/BPH, with or without erectile dysfunction <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> The FDA approved tadalafil for the signs and symptoms of BPH, and for coexisting ED and BPH, on October 6, 2011.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
