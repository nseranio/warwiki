# Tadalafil BPH dose-finding trial: Once-Daily Tadalafil for BPH-Associated Lower Urinary Tract Symptoms

<TrialBottomLine id="tadalafil-bph-pivotal" />

The multinational, double-blind dose-finding trial randomized 1,058 men with BPH-associated lower urinary tract symptoms to once-daily tadalafil 2.5, 5, 10, or 20 mg or placebo; the prespecified 5 mg comparison met its International Prostate Symptom Score (IPSS) endpoint at 12 weeks, and the other doses also improved IPSS versus placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="tadalafil-bph-pivotal" />

## Design

Participants completed a four-week placebo run-in before assignment to one of five parallel groups. The prespecified primary endpoint was total IPSS change at 12 weeks with 5 mg versus placebo; efficacy analyses used an intent-to-treat set with baseline and at least one postbaseline measure, last observation carried forward, and multiplicity-adjusted ANCOVA.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Men were at least 45 years old with at least six months of LUTS, pre-run-in IPSS of at least 13, Qmax of 4–15 mL/s and screening postvoid residual below 300 mL.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The randomized groups contained 209, 212, 216, and 209 men assigned to ascending tadalafil doses and 212 assigned to placebo. The trial registry identifies the study as phase 2/3 and participant and investigator masked.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The erectile-function analysis selected men who reported ED and expected to remain sexually active with a female partner; it required no minimum number of intercourse attempts.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Results

The prespecified 5 mg comparison improved IPSS by a least-squares mean of 4.87 points (n=212), versus 2.27 points with placebo (n=210), a 2.6-point greater reduction (P &lt; 0.001). Secondary IPSS changes were −3.88 points with 2.5 mg (n=208), −5.17 with 10 mg (n=216), and −5.21 with 20 mg (n=208), versus −2.27 with placebo (2.5 mg: P = 0.015; 10 and 20 mg: P &lt; 0.001). The authors selected 5 mg as the most favorable risk-benefit dose. At 5 mg, the IPSS quality-of-life index changed by −0.86 versus −0.49 points with placebo and improved significantly. Peak urinary flow increased by 1.64 versus 1.24 mL/s with 5 mg and placebo, without a statistically significant between-group difference at any dose.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Erectile function improved in the post hoc BPH/ED subgroup: among 581 included men, 579 contributed efficacy data. Least-squares mean IIEF-EF changes were +5.4 (2.5 mg; n=113), +6.8 (5 mg; n=117), +7.9 (10 mg; n=120), and +8.2 points (20 mg; n=115), versus +2.0 with placebo (n=114; P &lt; 0.001 for each dose).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Among men with baseline IIEF-EF &lt;26, normal function (IIEF-EF at least 26) was reached by 40/117 (34.2%) at 5 mg versus 16/114 (14.0%) with placebo.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> In the post hoc BPH/ED cohort, least-squares mean IPSS change at 5 mg was −4.2 points (n=114) versus −2.1 with placebo (n=113; P = 0.004); all four doses improved IPSS, while Qmax did not differ significantly.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> A separate post hoc uroflowmetry analysis found peak-flow increases of 16% at 5 mg (n=202) and 12% with placebo (n=198), without a significant between-group difference.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Obstructive IPSS subscore improvement was 31% versus 13%; mean postvoid residual changed by +0.2 mL (n=203) versus +0.9 mL (n=202), with no significant tadalafil-associated change.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

In the one-year open-label extension, 427 men received tadalafil 5 mg daily and 299/428 entrants (69.9%) completed treatment. Among 416 men with evaluable IPSS data, mean change from randomized baseline to the last extension assessment was −5.0 ± 6.7 points; the change from week 12 was −0.9 ± 5.7 points. Among 219 sexually active men with ED and evaluable IIEF-EF data, mean change from randomized baseline was +5.9 ± 7.6 points.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Treatment was oral once daily after the placebo run-in; 182/212 (85.9%) assigned to 5 mg and 185/212 (87.3%) assigned to placebo completed 12 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The uroflowmetry analysis used centrally read tracings with prevoid bladder volume of 150–550 mL and voided volume of at least 125 mL.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The extension gave 5 mg daily regardless of each participant's original assignment and prohibited other BPH or ED drugs.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Adverse events

Treatment-emergent adverse events increased across ascending tadalafil doses: 56/209 (26.8%), 65/212 (30.7%), 75/216 (34.7%) and 83/209 (39.7%), versus 45/211 with placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> At 5 mg versus placebo, serious adverse events occurred in 1/212 (0.5%) versus 6/211 (2.8%), and adverse-event discontinuation in 12/212 (5.7%) versus 5/211 (2.4%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Dyspepsia occurred in 10/212 (4.7%) versus 0/211; one placebo recipient required catheterization for urinary retention.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In the open-label extension, treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 246/427 (57.6%), serious events in 20/427 (4.7%), and adverse-event discontinuation in 22/427 (5.2%).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Dyspepsia and gastro-oesophageal reflux each occurred in 17/427 (4.0%), back pain in 16/427 (3.7%), and headache in 13/427 (3.0%).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

In the BPH/ED subgroup, treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 30/117 (25.6%) men at 5 mg versus 24/115 (20.9%) with placebo; serious adverse events occurred in 1/117 (0.9%) versus 1/115 (0.9%), and adverse-event discontinuation in 6/117 (5.1%) versus 2/115 (1.7%). Dyspepsia occurred in 5/117 (4.3%) versus 0/115; no urinary retention was reported in tadalafil-treated men, and one placebo-treated man had a urinary tract infection.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

The randomized comparison lasted 12 weeks. The extension enrolled prior trial completers, treated everyone with tadalafil, and had no concurrent placebo group; efficacy results used men with available postbaseline data.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> The BPH/ED and uroflowmetry analyses were post hoc; the full trial was powered for IPSS, and both reports performed multiple subgroup tests.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA 2026 BPH guideline recommends offering tadalafil 5 mg daily to men with LUTS/BPH, with or without erectile dysfunction <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> The FDA approved tadalafil for the signs and symptoms of BPH, and for coexisting ED and BPH, on October 6, 2011.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
