# Tadalafil once daily for ED: Placebo-Controlled Tadalafil 5-mg and 10-mg Dosing

<TrialBottomLine id="tadalafil-daily-ed" />

Porst and colleagues randomized 268 men with erectile dysfunction to tadalafil 5 mg daily, tadalafil 10 mg daily or placebo for 12 weeks; both tadalafil doses improved IIEF erectile-function scores, successful penetration and intercourse completion versus placebo (all comparisons P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="tadalafil-daily-ed" />

## Design

The multicenter, double-blind trial assigned 54 men to placebo, 109 to tadalafil 5 mg and 105 to tadalafil 10 mg. Men were at least 18 years old, had erectile dysfunction for at least three months and were in monogamous relationships with female partners; prior ineffective sildenafil treatment and prior tadalafil trial participation were exclusions. The three co-primary efficacy measures were change in the IIEF erectile-function domain and change in per-patient mean Sexual Encounter Profile diary responses for penetration (SEP2) and successful completion of intercourse (SEP3). Patient-reported improvement and an endpoint IIEF erectile-function score of 26–30 among men with a baseline score below 26 were secondary measures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Efficacy analyses included randomized participants with a baseline and at least one post-baseline measurement; missing IIEF erectile-function scores were imputed by last observation carried forward. The prespecified primary analysis retained 14 men whose baseline IIEF erectile-function score was already 26–30, and pairwise dose comparisons with placebo used Dunnett-adjusted P values.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Both daily tadalafil doses met all three co-primary efficacy comparisons with placebo. Mean IIEF erectile-function score changed by +9.7 points with 5 mg, +9.4 with 10 mg and +0.9 with placebo, reaching endpoint means of 22.8, 22.8 and 15.0. Successful-penetration responses increased by 36.5, 39.4 and 11.2 percentage points, respectively, reaching per-patient means of 79.4%, 81.2% and 51.7%; successful-intercourse responses increased by 45.5, 50.1 and 13.2 percentage points, reaching 67.2%, 72.8% and 36.7%. Each dose differed from placebo on each co-primary measure (Dunnett-adjusted P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Among men who answered the endpoint Global Assessment Question, 84.5% with 5 mg, 84.6% with 10 mg and 28.3% with placebo reported improved erections. Among men with baseline IIEF erectile-function scores below 26, endpoint scores of 26–30 occurred in 51.5%, 50.5% and 8.3%, respectively (both secondary comparisons P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the later open-label 5-mg extension, mean IIEF erectile-function score was 10.4 points above the original baseline at one year; 208/234 (88.9%) completed that extension.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The same report also included a separate two-year extension of another randomized study.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> A retrospective analysis of men from the one-year extension found that 63/136 (46.3%) who had improved or maintained normal function at the end of treatment still met that definition four weeks after stopping tadalafil.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Treatment delivery

Participants took placebo, tadalafil 5 mg or tadalafil 10 mg around the same time each day during a 12-week study; treatment lasted 12–15 weeks. Compliance, defined as taking at least 70% of doses between visits, was 94.2%, 93.0% and 96.2%, respectively. Tadalafil recipients averaged 6.5 doses per week, placebo recipients 6.6, and 234/268 (87.3%) completed the study.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

In the randomized trial, adverse events led to discontinuation in 2/109 (1.8%) with 5 mg, 6/105 (5.7%) with 10 mg and 1/54 (1.9%) with placebo, or 9/268 (3.4%) overall. Dyspepsia occurred in 6/109 (5.5%), 12/105 (11.4%) and 2/54 (3.7%), respectively; headache in 7/109 (6.4%), 11/105 (10.5%) and 4/54 (7.4%). Back pain occurred in 4/109 (3.7%), 10/105 (9.5%) and 2/54 (3.7%); upper abdominal pain in 3/109 (2.8%), 9/105 (8.6%) and 0/54; and myalgia in 3/109 (2.8%), 7/105 (6.7%) and 0/54.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

One participant in the 5-mg group died with renal cell carcinoma; the investigator judged the death unrelated to study drug. No myocardial infarctions occurred.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The extension report recorded no drug-related serious adverse events across its one- and two-year cohorts.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

Randomized treatment lasted 12–15 weeks and did not compare daily with as-needed tadalafil. The open-label extension included participants from another trial, and its post-treatment analysis selected men who had responded during treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA erectile dysfunction guideline (2018) advises discussing an FDA-approved oral PDE5 inhibitor with men with erectile dysfunction, including benefits and risks, unless contraindicated <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
