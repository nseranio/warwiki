# Hakvoort Catheter Duration Trial: Next-Morning Versus Fifth-Day Removal After Anterior Colporrhaphy

<TrialBottomLine id="hakvoort-catheter" />

The **Hakvoort catheter duration trial** randomized 100 women scheduled for anterior colporrhaphy, with or without posterior repair or vaginal hysterectomy, to transurethral catheter removal the morning after surgery or on postoperative day 5; earlier removal increased recatheterization but reduced positive urine cultures, catheter days, and hospital stay.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="hakvoort-catheter" />

## Design

Experienced gynecologists performed the repairs at a Dutch teaching hospital. A 14 Ch transurethral Foley catheter was placed in the operating theatre immediately after surgery. Patients were assigned on reaching the ward using closed opaque envelopes. Each arm initially had 50 patients. Four in the prolonged-catheter arm and two in the next-morning arm were excluded from analysis: four had preoperative urine findings consistent with infection, and two assigned to prolonged drainage had posterior-only repair.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The main outcomes were repeat catheterization, positive urine culture, mean catheter duration, and mean hospital stay. Urine was cultured before the first catheter removal; the report defined infection as culture growth above 10<sup>5</sup> CFU/mL. Ultrasound measured postvoid residual within 8 hours of removal. A residual of at least 200 mL triggered another transurethral catheter for 3 days and a repeat voiding assessment. Sample-size planning anticipated more recatheterization with early removal and allowed a rate as high as 50% in that arm; the report did not present a formal equivalence test.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Repeat catheterization occurred in 19/48 (40%) after next-morning removal and 4/46 (9%) after fifth-day removal. The reported odds ratio for prolonged versus early removal was 0.15 (95% CI 0.045–0.47). Positive urine cultures occurred in 2/48 (4%) and 18/46 (39.1%), respectively; the reported odds ratio in the same direction was 15 (95% CI 3.2–68.6).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Mean catheter duration was 2.3 versus 5.3 days, a 3.0-day reduction with next-morning removal (P &lt; 0.001). Mean hospital stay was 5.7 versus 7.0 days, a 1.3-day reduction (P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Table 1 records anterior and posterior colporrhaphy in 22 early-removal and 23 prolonged-catheter patients, and those repairs with vaginal hysterectomy in another 18 and 17, respectively. Anterior colporrhaphy alone was recorded in 7 and 6 patients. One early-removal patient lacked a recorded procedure category in that table. Both arms followed the same ultrasound and repeat-catheter protocol after initial catheter removal.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Culture positivity was measured before initial catheter removal; the report did not give symptoms or antibiotic treatment for these patients. After the second catheter was removed, two recatheterized patients in the early-removal arm still had residuals above 200 mL, while all four recatheterized prolonged-catheter patients had residuals below that threshold. The authors reported no comparison of other operative or catheter complications.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The single-center trial analyzed 94 of 100 randomized patients after exclusions. Treatment and postoperative assessment were unblinded. The paper designated four main outcomes without a formal hierarchy, and its sample-size equivalence premise was not followed by a reported equivalence analysis. Urine culture positivity captured bacteriuria without reported symptoms; patient satisfaction and later voiding outcomes were not measured.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
