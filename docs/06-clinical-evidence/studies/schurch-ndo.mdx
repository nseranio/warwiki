# Schurch NDO: Intradetrusor Botulinum Toxin A for Neurogenic Urinary Incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="schurch-ndo" />

**Schurch NDO** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized trial of one intradetrusor injection of onabotulinumtoxinA 200 U, 300 U, or placebo in 59 patients with neurogenic detrusor overactivity who used clean intermittent self-catheterization; both active groups had significant within-group decreases in daily incontinence episodes over 24 weeks, while placebo did not.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="schurch-ndo" />

## Design

Spinal cord injury accounted for 53 participants and multiple sclerosis for six. All had urinary incontinence from neurogenic detrusor overactivity and required clean intermittent self-catheterization. The later analysis of this same cohort states that oral anticholinergic treatment had been inadequate. Patient bladder diaries tracked daily incontinence episodes; maximum cystometric capacity, reflex detrusor volume, maximum detrusor pressure during contraction, and the Incontinence Quality of Life (I-QOL) questionnaire assessed bladder function and patient experience.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

The primary report described significant improvements in urodynamic measures and I-QOL in both toxin groups, beginning at week 2 and still observed at week 24.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In a later analysis of the same randomized cohort, median total I-QOL scores were higher with both doses than with placebo at every assessed visit. At week 24, the 200-U group no longer differed from placebo on the Psychosocial Impact and Social Embarrassment subscales; the 300-U group still differed on both.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The investigators delivered the single treatment cystoscopically as thirty 1-mL injections distributed across the detrusor while sparing the trigone. Participants required intermittent self-catheterization at enrollment.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Adverse events

The primary report stated that no safety concerns arose during the study. The earlier meeting abstract reported no autonomic dysreflexia or systemic events.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

The trial evaluated a single injection over 24 weeks in a small cohort dominated by spinal cord injury. Its reported significance for incontinence episodes was based on within-group change.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

- **AUA/SUFU (2021), NLUTD Statement 40.** Recommend onabotulinumtoxinA for patients with spinal cord injury or multiple sclerosis refractory to oral medication, to improve storage, incontinence, and quality of life <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade A)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **EAU (2026), Neuro-urology §3.4.2.e.7.** Use intradetrusor botulinum toxin A for neurogenic detrusor overactivity when antimuscarinic treatment is ineffective <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
