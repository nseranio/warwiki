# Schurch NDO: Intradetrusor Botulinum Toxin A for Neurogenic Urinary Incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="schurch-ndo" />

**Schurch NDO** randomized 59 patients with neurogenic detrusor overactivity who used clean intermittent self-catheterization to one intradetrusor injection of onabotulinumtoxinA 200 U, 300 U, or placebo. Daily incontinence episodes fell more than with placebo at selected visits through 24 weeks in this multicenter, double-blind trial.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="schurch-ndo" />

## Design

Spinal cord injury accounted for 53 participants and multiple sclerosis for six. All had urinary incontinence from neurogenic detrusor overactivity, used clean intermittent self-catheterization, and had responded inadequately to oral anticholinergics. The 200-U, 300-U, and placebo arms enrolled 19, 19, and 21 patients, respectively. Daily incontinence episodes were the primary efficacy variable; urodynamic measures and the Incontinence Quality of Life (I-QOL) questionnaire assessed bladder function and patient experience. The primary analysis used the intent-to-treat population, imputed missing diary values with the mean of available values, and used two-sided α=0.05 without adjustment for multiple comparisons.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The primary diary endpoint favored toxin at selected visits: against placebo, 300 U differed at week 2 (P=0.015) and week 6 (P=0.047), while 200 U differed at week 24 (P=0.019). At week 24, mean daily incontinence episodes had changed by −1.1 from a baseline of 1.9 with 200 U, by −0.9 from 2.8 with 300 U, and by −0.1 from 3.0 with placebo. At least one incontinence-free diary week occurred in 14/19, 10/19, and 5/21 patients, respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Bladder function and I-QOL also improved with toxin. At week 24, mean maximum detrusor pressure fell by 38.7 cm H₂O with 200 U, 35.5 cm H₂O with 300 U, and 1.4 cm H₂O with placebo; both toxin arms differed from placebo (P &lt; 0.05). Mean total I-QOL change exceeded placebo at every assessed visit from week 2 through week 24.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The later I-QOL analysis included 56 of 59 randomized patients with screening scores. Median total scores at screening were 48.9 with 200 U, 44.3 with 300 U, and 42.6 with placebo. At week 24 they were 86.4 with 200 U and 67.0 with 300 U; both active doses exceeded placebo at every assessed visit (P &lt; 0.05). A 10.4-point increase defined the minimal important difference. Psychosocial Impact improved more with both doses than with placebo at weeks 2 through 18, and Social Embarrassment from week 6 through week 18, and with 200 U also at week 24 (P &lt; 0.05).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

A single 200-U or 300-U dose was diluted to 30 mL and delivered as thirty cystoscopic 1-mL intramuscular detrusor injections, avoiding the base and trigone. Patients received prophylactic antibiotics; general, local, spinal, or no anesthesia was used. One patient assigned to 200 U withdrew before drug administration because of urethral stricture, and another withdrew after week 6 for lack of efficacy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Urinary tract infection occurred in 6/19 patients assigned to 200 U, 4/19 assigned to 300 U, and 3/21 assigned to placebo (P=0.455). Injection-site pain occurred in 0/19, 2/19, and 1/21, respectively; hematuria occurred in 1/19, 1/19, and 0/21. Two serious events, confusion in the 200-U arm and a skin ulcer in the placebo arm, were judged unrelated to treatment. No autonomic dysreflexia or systemic effects were observed.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The trial evaluated one injection over 24 weeks in a small cohort dominated by spinal cord injury. Between-group significance for diary episodes occurred at selected visits, with no multiplicity adjustment. The later I-QOL analysis included 56/59 randomized patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

- **AUA/SUFU (2021), NLUTD Statement 40.** Recommend onabotulinumtoxinA for patients with spinal cord injury or multiple sclerosis refractory to oral medication, to improve storage, incontinence, and quality of life <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade A)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **EAU (2026), Neuro-urology §3.4.2.e.7.** Use intradetrusor botulinum toxin A for neurogenic detrusor overactivity when antimuscarinic treatment is ineffective <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
