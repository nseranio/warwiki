# SUBSoNIC: Subsensory Sacral Neuromodulation for Fecal Incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="subsonic" />

**SUBSoNIC** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized crossover trial of subsensory sacral neuromodulation versus sham stimulation in implanted adults with refractory fecal incontinence; the prespecified comparison of weekly incontinence episodes did not reach statistical significance.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="subsonic" />

## Design

Participants were selected for sacral neuromodulation after multidisciplinary assessment and failure of conservative measures. Test stimulation preceded randomization, and only patients who responded to testing received permanent implants and entered the crossover. The randomized population was aged 18–80 years. Patients with a fistula between the anal and vaginal tracts, full-thickness rectal prolapse, active perianal sepsis, inflammatory bowel disease, overflow incontinence, or a neurological disorder were excluded.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

At sites in the United Kingdom and Ireland, patients received active stimulation followed by sham or the reverse sequence. Each blinded period lasted 16 weeks. The endpoint was the number of fecal incontinence episodes per week recorded in paper diaries during the final four weeks of each period. Participants, implanting surgeons, assessors, and statisticians were blinded; tamper-proof tape concealed the programmer settings. The planned mixed Poisson analysis failed to converge with the small sample, so the primary comparison used a paired t-test in participants with usable diaries from both periods.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Of 39 randomized patients, 16 had complete primary-outcome data. Mean weekly episodes were 2.3 (SD 2.8) during subsensory stimulation and 3.0 (SD 3.7) during sham: mean difference −0.795 episodes per week (95% CI −1.5 to 0.0; P = 0.06). The article's Table 2 rounds the difference to −0.7. A sensitivity analysis imputing blank diary entries as zero when another entry had been completed that day yielded a mean difference of −0.9 (95% CI −1.8 to 0.0; P = 0.04).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Fecal urgency without incontinence averaged 3.2 versus 2.7 episodes per week during active and sham stimulation, respectively (mean difference 0.5; 95% CI −0.4 to 1.4; P = 0.23). In the 19 participants with baseline and 58-week open-label follow-up diaries, mean weekly incontinence episodes were 6.2 (SD 5.9) at baseline and 3.2 (SD 3.3) at follow-up. Stimulation during this cohort phase could be subsensory or suprasensory according to patient preference.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The surgeons implanted an InterStim II pulse generator with a fluoroscopy-guided lead at the S3 or S4 root, accepting lead position when at least three of four electrodes elicited low-voltage contraction of the anal sphincter and pelvic floor, with or without big-toe movement. Active settings used 14 Hz and a 210 μs pulse width; amplitude was lowered just below the habituated sensory threshold. Sham was programmed to 0 or 0.05 V. Reprogramming was scheduled six weeks after each intervention began.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The sequence groups comprised 17 active-then-sham and 22 sham-then-active participants; only 9 and 7, respectively, completed both primary diaries. The study stopped after 39 of 90 planned randomizations when COVID-19 disrupted recruitment, visits, and access to surgery. Nineteen participants completed the open-label follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Ten adverse events were reported across the crossover and cohort phases, including one serious adverse device event that did not require overnight hospitalization.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Early termination and missing crossover diaries left the primary paired analysis with 16 patients. The main analysis and the missing-entry sensitivity analysis produced different significance results. Randomization occurred after a positive test-stimulation phase, and the later cohort comparison was open-label and uncontrolled. The sham device delivered 0.05 V in some participants because of programmer limitations.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="subsonic" />

Earlier implanted cohorts reported symptom change after sacral neuromodulation without a concurrent sham period. SUBSoNIC compared active and sham periods within the same newly implanted patients after test stimulation; its open-label cohort outcome is a separate, within-person follow-up measure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
