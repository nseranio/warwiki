# Tamsulosin US pivotal studies: Modified-release alpha blockade for symptomatic BPH

<TrialBottomLine id="tamsulosin-pivotal" />

The primary multicenter, double-blind phase 3 trial randomized 756 men with symptomatic BPH to tamsulosin 0.4 mg daily, tamsulosin 0.4 mg daily for 1 week then 0.8 mg daily, or placebo; both doses improved AUA symptom score and peak urinary flow over 13 weeks, with more adverse events at the higher dose.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="tamsulosin-pivotal" />

## Design

Change in total AUA symptom score and peak urinary flow (Qmax) were the primary efficacy measures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The investigators also assessed symptom subscales, patient bother, flow response, and orthostatic vital signs. The independent US 93-01 study randomized 735 men after a 4-week single-blind placebo evaluation period and evaluated 0.4 mg daily and a regimen of 0.4 mg daily for 1 week followed by 0.8 mg daily against placebo during 13 weeks of double-blind treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Results

In the primary trial, both tamsulosin doses produced statistically significant improvements in the two primary measures versus placebo. Qmax improvement after the first 0.4 mg dose was evident within 4 to 8 hours.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the independent US 93-01 study, mean AUA symptom score fell by 25% in each tamsulosin group, more than with placebo (P = 0.01). Symptom and flow improvements appeared by the first treatment week.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Of 618 patients who completed the 13-week primary trial, 418 (68%) continued their assigned drug and dose for another 40 weeks in a double-blind extension.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The initial symptom-score gains in both active groups were maintained; Qmax improved significantly from baseline in both tamsulosin groups but not the placebo group.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

In the primary trial, adverse events with 0.4 mg were comparable to placebo and were somewhat more frequent with 0.8 mg.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Positive orthostatic tests were comparable across groups.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In US 93-01, positive orthostatic tests were more frequent in the group assigned to 0.8 mg (still receiving 0.4 mg) than placebo at visit 4 (P = 0.012).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Clinically significant orthostatic hypotension was not observed in the double-blind extension.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The primary randomized treatment period lasted 13 weeks. Only 418 of 618 patients entered its extension, which selected for men willing and able to continue assigned treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
