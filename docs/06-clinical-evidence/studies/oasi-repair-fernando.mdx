# Fernando 2006: Overlap Versus End-to-End Repair of Obstetric Anal Sphincter Injury

<TrialBottomLine id="oasi-repair-fernando" />

**Fernando 2006** randomized 64 women with acute obstetric anal sphincter injury to immediate overlapping or end-to-end external sphincter repair and reported lower fecal incontinence at 12 months after overlap repair.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="oasi-repair-fernando" />

## Design

Eligible tears were grade 3b (more than half the external sphincter thickness), grade 3c (internal sphincter injury), or fourth degree (including anorectal epithelium). The trial assigned 32 women to each repair. Serial questionnaires assessed fecal incontinence at 12 months as the primary outcome; fecal urgency, flatus incontinence, perineal pain, dyspareunia, quality of life, and symptom change were secondary outcomes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At 12 months, fecal incontinence was reported by 0% of the overlap group and 6/25 (24%) of the end-to-end group (reported RR 0.07, 95% CI 0.00–1.21; P = .009). Fecal urgency was reported by 1/27 (3.7%) versus 8/25 (32%) (RR 0.12, 95% CI 0.02–0.86; P = .02). Deterioration of defecatory symptoms during follow-up occurred in 0% versus 4/25 (16%; P = .01). Dyspareunia and quality-of-life outcomes did not differ significantly.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Both groups underwent immediate primary repair after the tear. The randomized surgical distinction was overlapping versus end-to-end approximation of the external anal sphincter.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Perineal pain at 12 months was reported by 0% after overlap and 5/25 (20%) after end-to-end repair (P = .04).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The trial randomized 64 women, while 12-month fecal urgency results included 52. The published relative-risk interval for the primary endpoint includes 1 despite its reported P value of .009. Follow-up ended at 12 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The IUGA 2026 obstetric anal sphincter injury guideline allows either overlapping or end-to-end repair for a full-thickness, full-length external sphincter tear <Rec>(A)</Rec>. It specifies end-to-end repair for partial-thickness external sphincter tears <Rec>(D)</Rec> and separate end-to-end repair of an injured internal sphincter <Rec>(C)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
