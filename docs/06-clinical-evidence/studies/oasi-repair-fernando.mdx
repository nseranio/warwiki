# Fernando 2006: Overlap Versus End-to-End Repair of Obstetric Anal Sphincter Injury

<TrialBottomLine id="oasi-repair-fernando" />

**Fernando 2006** randomized 64 women with acute obstetric anal sphincter injury to immediate overlapping or end-to-end external sphincter repair and reported lower fecal incontinence at 12 months after overlap repair.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="oasi-repair-fernando" />

## Design

Eligible tears were grade 3b (more than half the external sphincter thickness), grade 3c (internal sphincter injury), or fourth degree (including anorectal epithelium); grade 3a and previous third- or fourth-degree tears were excluded. The trial assigned 32 women to each repair by minimization for parity, gestation, and delivery mode. Participants were blinded to the repair method. Serial questionnaires assessed fecal incontinence at 12 months as the primary outcome; fecal urgency, flatus incontinence, perineal pain, dyspareunia, quality of life, and symptom change were secondary outcomes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At 12 months, fecal incontinence was reported by 0/27 of the overlap group and 6/25 (24%) of the end-to-end group (reported RR 0.07, 95% CI 0.00–1.21; P = .009). Fecal urgency was reported by 1/27 (3.7%) versus 8/25 (32%) (RR 0.12, 95% CI 0.02–0.86; P = .02). Deterioration of defecatory symptoms from 6 weeks to 12 months occurred in 0/27 versus 4/25 (16%; P = .01). Flatus incontinence was reported by 4/27 versus 4/25 (RR 0.93, 95% CI 0.26–3.31; P = 1.0), and dyspareunia by 2/27 (7.4%) versus 3/25 (12%) (RR 0.62, 95% CI 0.11–3.39; P = 1.0). Quality-of-life outcomes did not differ significantly.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Both groups underwent primary repair within 3 hours of injury detection by one of two clinicians trained in both techniques. End-to-end repair approximated the external sphincter with 2–3 mattress sutures of 3-0 PDS; overlap repair mobilized the ends and used two suture rows, with division of remaining external sphincter fibers when needed. An injured internal sphincter was repaired separately with interrupted 3-0 PDS. All women received intraoperative intravenous antibiotics, 7 days of oral antibiotics, and 14 days of ispaghula husk and lactulose.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Median operative time was 38 versus 28 minutes (P = .003), and median estimated blood loss was 260 versus 100 mL (P = .05), for overlap versus end-to-end repair.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Perineal pain at 12 months was reported by 0/27 after overlap and 5/25 (20%) after end-to-end repair (RR 0.08, 95% CI 0.00–1.45; P = .04). No woman required transfusion or developed wound dehiscence, fistula, or suture migration.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The trial randomized 64 women, while 52 (27 overlap, 25 end-to-end) returned 12-month questionnaires. The designated intention-to-treat analysis used questionnaire respondents for the 12-month comparison. The published relative-risk interval for the primary endpoint includes 1 despite its reported P value of .009. Follow-up ended at 12 months, and two clinicians performed every repair.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The IUGA 2026 obstetric anal sphincter injury guideline allows either overlapping or end-to-end repair for a full-thickness, full-length external sphincter tear <Rec>(A)</Rec>. It specifies end-to-end repair for partial-thickness external sphincter tears <Rec>(D)</Rec> and separate end-to-end repair of an injured internal sphincter <Rec>(C)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
