# Botros methenamine–trimethoprim trial: Methenamine Hippurate Compared With Trimethoprim for Recurrent UTI Prevention

<TrialBottomLine id="botros-methenamine" />

**The Botros trial** was an open-label randomized comparison of daily methenamine hippurate and trimethoprim in 92 adult women with recurrent culture-positive UTI; culture-proven recurrence by 12 months occurred in 28/43 (65.1%) analyzed women in each arm (P = 1.00).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="botros-methenamine" />

## Design

Eligibility required at least two symptomatic, culture-positive UTIs in the preceding six months or three in the preceding year, with a culture threshold of 10,000 CFU/mL. The mean age was 71.9 years; 86/92 women (93.5%) were postmenopausal and 43/92 (46.7%) used vaginal estrogen. Pregnancy, urinary tract abnormalities, acute pyelonephritis, renal insufficiency or failure, and postcoital prophylaxis were exclusion criteria. The primary outcome was culture-proven recurrence within 12 months of starting prophylaxis. The authors reported an intention-to-treat analysis and a per-protocol analysis accounting for receipt of the alternative drug after randomization.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The primary comparison counted women with a recurrence, whereas the per-protocol analysis regrouped women according to treatment received after randomization. Both entry and outcome definitions required microbiologic documentation. The trial was powered to detect a 23.8 percentage-point difference at a 0.05 significance level; it specified no equivalence margin.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The primary recurrence comparison found no difference among the 86 of 92 randomized women analyzed (P = 1.00). After reassignment by treatment received, recurrence occurred in 30/46 (65.2%) women in the methenamine group and 26/40 (65.0%) in the trimethoprim group (P = 0.98).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Time to first recurrent UTI averaged 119.3 ± 94.1 days with methenamine and 100.7 ± 84.4 days with trimethoprim (P = 0.52). Mean UTI recurrences in the following year were 1.6 ± 1.9 and 1.5 ± 1.7, respectively (P = 0.72).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Methenamine hippurate was prescribed at 1 g twice daily and trimethoprim at 100 mg nightly for six months; recurrence-free participants were encouraged to discontinue, then followed to 12 months. Mean recorded treatment duration was 272.1 ± 18.4 and 279.5 ± 17.5 days, respectively (P = 0.77).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Adherence was measured in 51/92 participants; mean Morisky-8 scores were 6.9 ± 1.6 with methenamine and 7.3 ± 1.1 with trimethoprim (P = 0.44), both in the medium-adherence range. Eleven of 86 analyzed women switched drugs and were reassigned for the per-protocol analysis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Adverse effects led 10/92 women to stop assigned medication. Reported events included diarrhea in 2/45 methenamine and 1/47 trimethoprim recipients; the trimethoprim arm also had rash in 2/47, weakness in 2/47 and *Clostridium difficile* colitis in 1/47. The methenamine arm had abdominal pain and nephrolithiasis in 1/45 each; the latter was thought unrelated.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Trimethoprim resistance appeared in 30/79 (38.0%) recurrent urine cultures with susceptibility data in the methenamine arm and 58/73 (79.5%) in the trimethoprim arm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Treatment was unblinded, and six randomized women are absent from the reported intention-to-treat denominators.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="botros-methenamine" />

ALTAR subsequently tested methenamine against daily low-dose nitrofurantoin, trimethoprim or cefalexin in a separate randomized cohort. Its primary endpoint counted symptomatic, antibiotic-treated UTI episodes per person-year during 12 months, whereas Botros counted women with culture-proven recurrence by 12 months.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA/CUA/SUFU 2025 guideline says clinicians may offer methenamine hippurate for prophylaxis to women with recurrent UTI <Rec>(Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
