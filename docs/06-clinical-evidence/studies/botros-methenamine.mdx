# Botros methenamine–trimethoprim trial: Methenamine Hippurate Compared With Trimethoprim for Recurrent UTI Prevention

<TrialBottomLine id="botros-methenamine" />

**The Botros trial** was an open-label randomized comparison of daily methenamine hippurate and trimethoprim in 92 adult women with recurrent culture-positive UTI; culture-proven recurrence by 12 months occurred in 28/43 (65%) analyzed women in each arm (P = 1.00).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="botros-methenamine" />

## Design

Eligibility required at least two culture-positive UTIs in the preceding six months or three in the preceding year. The primary outcome was culture-proven recurrence within 12 months of starting prophylaxis. The authors reported an intention-to-treat analysis and a per-protocol analysis accounting for receipt of the alternative drug after randomization.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The primary comparison counted women with a recurrence, whereas the per-protocol analysis regrouped women according to treatment received after randomization. Both entry and outcome definitions required microbiologic documentation, which is relevant when comparing this result with trials using symptom-based outcomes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The reported intention-to-treat analysis included 86 of 92 randomized women. In the per-protocol analysis, recurrence occurred in 30/46 (65%) women analyzed in the methenamine group and 26/40 (65%) women analyzed in the trimethoprim group (P = 0.98).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Both groups received daily prophylaxis for a minimum of six months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

The authors described adverse effects as similar between groups.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Treatment was unblinded, and six randomized women are absent from the reported intention-to-treat denominators.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="botros-methenamine" />

ALTAR subsequently tested methenamine against daily low-dose nitrofurantoin, trimethoprim or cefalexin in a separate randomized cohort. Its primary endpoint counted symptomatic, antibiotic-treated UTI episodes per person-year during 12 months, whereas Botros counted women with culture-proven recurrence by 12 months.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA/CUA/SUFU 2025 guideline says clinicians may offer methenamine hippurate for prophylaxis to women with recurrent UTI <Rec>(Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
