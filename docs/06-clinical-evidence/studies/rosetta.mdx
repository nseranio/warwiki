# ROSETTA: Refractory Overactive Bladder—Sacral Neuromodulation Versus Botulinum Toxin Assessment

<TrialBottomLine id="rosetta" />

**ROSETTA** was a nine-center, open-label randomized superiority trial in women with refractory urgency urinary incontinence comparing one cystoscopic 200 U onabotulinumtoxinA injection with staged InterStim sacral neuromodulation; the injection produced a small additional reduction in daily episodes over six months, with more urinary tract infection and intermittent catheterization.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="rosetta" />

## Design

The trial randomized 386 women after at least one supervised behavioral or physical therapy intervention and at least two anticholinergic medications, unless those drugs were intolerable or contraindicated. Entry required at least six urgency-incontinence episodes on a three-day diary. Relevant neurologic disease, previous treatment with either study intervention, and postvoid residual above 150 mL were exclusions. The assigned-treatment groups in the primary report contained 381 eligible women; 364 contributed at least one postbaseline diary to the modified intention-to-treat analysis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Participating implanters and injectors had each performed at least 10 procedures of their assigned type and reviewed standardized technique videos. The primary endpoint was the change from baseline in mean daily urgency-incontinence episodes across monthly three-day diaries over six months. The prespecified superiority test used two-sided alpha 0.05; the study was powered around a two-episode daily difference, while secondary comparisons were exploratory without multiplicity adjustment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

In the 364-woman primary analysis, mean daily episodes fell by 3.9 with onabotulinumtoxinA and 3.3 with sacral neuromodulation across six months: a mean difference in reduction of 0.63 episode per day (95% CI 0.13–1.14; P = 0.01), meeting the prespecified superiority test. Among women with at least four monthly diaries, complete resolution occurred in 35/178 (20%) versus 6/166 (4%); this was an exploratory outcome. Treatment-satisfaction scores were 67.7 versus 59.8 (mean difference 7.8, 95% CI 1.6–14.1).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The planned two-year analysis followed initial clinical responders, with outcome data for 260/298 (87%). Mean daily episode reductions across 24 months were 3.88 with toxin and 3.50 with neuromodulation (mean difference 0.38, 95% CI −0.14 to 0.89; P = 0.15). Complete resolution at month 24 was estimated at 5% in each group. Treatment-satisfaction scores across follow-up remained higher after toxin (68.1 versus 58.9; reported difference for neuromodulation minus toxin −9.1, 95% CI −14.4 to −3.9).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

In the trial-based health-sector economic analysis, mean two-year costs in 2018 US dollars were $7,460 for toxin and $35,680 for neuromodulation, a $28,220 difference. Among participants with QALY data (150 toxin, 134 neuromodulation), adjusted quality-adjusted life-years were 1.41 and 1.39, respectively (difference for neuromodulation minus toxin −0.02, 95% CI −0.09 to 0.05).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Among 187 toxin recipients grouped by UTI within 14 days of injection, mean daily urgency-incontinence episodes changed by −4.29 without early UTI versus −3.74 with early UTI at one month (difference −0.55, 95% CI −2.39 to 1.28; P = 0.55) and by −3.63 versus −2.15 at six months (difference −1.48, 95% CI −3.44 to 0.48; P = 0.14).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> At six months, mean pad use was 1.72 versus 3.30 per day (difference −1.57, 95% CI −3.06 to −0.08; P = 0.04), and Incontinence Impact Questionnaire changes were −29.19 versus −6.12 (difference −23.07, 95% CI −44.02 to −2.12; P = 0.03).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

Nocturnal voids fell in a pooled secondary analysis of 197 ABC and ROSETTA participants with at least two nightly voids, from 2.7 ± 0.4 to 1.6 ± 0.5 overall at six months (41%; P &lt; 0.001). In the ROSETTA subset, 200 U toxin fell from 2.8 ± 1.0 to 1.9 ± 1.2 voids/night (32%; adjusted change −1.1 ± 0.2; cohort n = 63, Table 2 n = 62), and neuromodulation from 3.0 ± 1.1 to 2.0 ± 1.4 (33%; adjusted change −0.9 ± 0.2; n = 54); change across all four cohorts did not differ (P = 0.118). Resolution to at most one nightly void occurred in 23/63 (37%) and 16/54 (30%), respectively (four-cohort P &gt; 0.05).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

For neuromodulation, first-stage lead placement took place in the operating suite under local and monitored anesthesia care. Teams tested sensory and motor responses at each electrode, then permitted program adjustments during a 7–14-day test. A reduction of at least 50% on a three-day diary qualified the patient for permanent generator implantation; nonresponders had the lead removed. Of 169 eligible assigned women who underwent first-stage placement, 142 (84%) responded and 139 received the generator. The toxin arm received a 200 U cystoscopic intradetrusor injection in clinic; 189/192 randomized women received the full dose and 159/192 (83%) met the one-month response definition.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Postinjection intermittent catheterization began for residual above 300 mL, or above 200 mL with incomplete-voiding symptoms.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Through two years, 115/159 (72%) initial toxin responders requested a second injection after a median 350 days; 101 received another 200 U and 14 were reduced to 100 U after prolonged catheterization.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Among implanted initial neuromodulation responders, 81/139 (58%) needed reprogramming.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Through six months, treated urinary tract infection occurred in 66/191 (35%) toxin recipients and 20/178 (11%) neuromodulation recipients. Intermittent catheterization after toxin was required at some visit by 38/191 (20%), including 16/191 (8%) at one month and 4/191 (2%) at six months; median catheterization duration for any reason was 37 days. Neuromodulation revision or removal occurred in 6/178 (3%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Over two years, recurrent UTI among women without baseline recurrent UTI occurred in 40/167 (24%) after toxin and 16/154 (10.4%) after neuromodulation. Catheterization at any point after toxin occurred in 45/189 (24%). Among 139 implanted initial neuromodulation responders, 4 (3%) underwent revision and 12 (8.6%) had device removal; the reported removal reasons were infection, reduced efficacy, patient preference, and pain.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

In a secondary analysis of the 200 U toxin arm, symptomatic culture-positive UTI occurred within 14 days after injection in 10/187 (5.3%) women with adverse event data.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Limitations

Treatments and assessments were open-label. The six-month primary analysis missed roughly 10% of monthly diaries, with more missing in the neuromodulation arm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The two-year efficacy analysis was restricted to initial responders and permitted repeat toxin injections and neuromodulator reprogramming. The economic analysis used trial-era 2018 US costs; its five-year estimates extrapolated two-year outcomes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="rosetta" />

The ABC trial compared a lower toxin dose with anticholinergic medication, whereas ROSETTA enrolled women with refractory symptoms and compared a higher toxin dose directly with staged neuromodulation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

- **AUA/SUFU (2024).** For OAB with inadequate response or intolerable adverse effects from pharmacotherapy or behavioral therapy, offer sacral neuromodulation, tibial nerve stimulation, and/or intradetrusor botulinum toxin <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade A)</Rec>.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **EAU female LUTS guideline (2026).** Offer bladder-wall onabotulinumtoxinA 100 U for OAB or urgency urinary incontinence refractory to conservative or drug treatment <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>; warn about limited response duration, UTI, and possible prolonged intermittent catheterization <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **EAU female LUTS guideline (2026).** Offer sacral nerve stimulation for OAB or urgency urinary incontinence refractory to anticholinergic therapy <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
