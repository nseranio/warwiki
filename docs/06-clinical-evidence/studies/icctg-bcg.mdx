# ICCTG BCG: Intravesical Bacillus Calmette-Guérin for Treatment-Refractory Interstitial Cystitis

<TrialBottomLine id="icctg-bcg" />

**ICCTG BCG** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized trial of six weekly intravesical bacillus Calmette-Guérin (BCG) instillations versus placebo in 265 participants with treatment-refractory interstitial cystitis; the week-34 patient-reported global response comparison did not reach statistical significance.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="icctg-bcg" />

## Design

Participants met National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases research criteria for interstitial cystitis and reported at least moderate pain and urinary frequency for at least six months. Both arms received masked intravesical instillations. The primary endpoint was the patient-reported global response assessment at week 34, supplemented by interstitial cystitis medication use during weeks 31–34. Secondary assessments included a voiding diary, pain, urgency and validated symptom indices.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The global response scale spanned markedly worse to markedly improved. Moderate or marked improvement, with medication use considered, defined response; participants who withdrew without a completed assessment were counted as nonresponders.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

At week 34, global response was 21% with BCG and 12% with placebo (P = 0.062). The primary report described small changes in secondary outcomes, with between-arm differences of borderline statistical significance. Seventeen of the 265 randomized participants withdrew from the randomized study.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Among original nonresponders, 156 elected an open-label BCG course. The global response rate was 18% both in participants originally assigned BCG and in those originally assigned placebo. Eighteen participants withdrew during this extension.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Of 44 week-34 responders identified by clinical-site assessment, 38 entered an observational extension. At week 68, 19/22 (86%) original BCG responders and 12/16 (75%) original placebo responders still reported moderate or marked improvement (P = 0.43). Five withdrawals were retained in those denominators; the follow-up comparison was restricted to initial responders.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The original course used six weekly intravesical instillations. Each assignment used a 50 mL instillation, with mean retention of 1.7 hours for BCG and 1.6 hours for placebo.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Nonresponders could receive another six-week course of open-label BCG; responders entered observation without another study course.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Adverse events

Numerous adverse events occurred in the BCG arm, without a statistically significant difference in overall adverse-event rates between arms. During open-label BCG, most participants reported at least one event, chiefly pain, genitourinary symptoms or gastrointestinal disturbances. The responder extension did not assess adverse events during its observational phase.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

The open-label extension had no concurrent placebo group. The week-68 extension selected only initial responders and included small groups. Clinical-site classification for extension entry differed slightly from the centrally reviewed primary response classification.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA's 2022 interstitial cystitis/bladder pain syndrome guideline states that intravesical BCG should not be offered outside investigational study settings <Rec>(Standard (Evidence Strength: Grade B))</Rec>. Its evidence discussion cites the randomized report and both ICCTG extensions, and describes potentially serious BCG exposure events, including sepsis and death, from bladder-cancer literature.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
