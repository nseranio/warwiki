# ICCTG BCG: Intravesical Bacillus Calmette-Guérin for Treatment-Refractory Interstitial Cystitis

<TrialBottomLine id="icctg-bcg" />

**ICCTG BCG** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized trial of six weekly intravesical bacillus Calmette-Guérin (BCG) instillations versus placebo in 265 participants with treatment-refractory interstitial cystitis; the week-34 patient-reported global response comparison did not reach statistical significance.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="icctg-bcg" />

## Design

Participants met National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases research criteria for interstitial cystitis, reported at least moderate pain and urinary frequency for at least six months, and had failed at least 12 weeks of standard treatment. Both arms received masked intravesical instillations. The primary endpoint was the patient-reported global response assessment at week 34, supplemented by interstitial cystitis medication use during weeks 31–34. Secondary assessments included a voiding diary, pain, urgency and validated symptom indices.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The global response scale spanned markedly worse to markedly improved. Moderate or marked improvement, with medication use considered, defined response; participants who withdrew without a completed assessment were counted as nonresponders.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

At week 34, adjudicated intent-to-treat global response was 27/131 (21%) with BCG and 16/134 (12%) with placebo (P = 0.062), which did not meet the prespecified two-sided alpha of 0.05. Among complete-data participants (at most 120 BCG and 126 placebo), mean pain-score change was −1.4 ± 2.4 with BCG and −0.9 ± 2.1 with placebo (longitudinal-model P = 0.042); secondary comparisons were not adjusted for multiplicity. Seventeen of the 265 randomized participants withdrew before the week-34 assessment (10/131 BCG; 7/134 placebo) and were counted as nonresponders.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Among 205 eligible original nonresponders, 156 (76%) entered the self-selected open-label BCG series. At week 68, its primary efficacy outcome, unmodified global response, occurred in 13/73 (18%) after prior BCG and 15/83 (18%) after prior placebo (P &gt; 0.99). Eighteen of 156 (12%) withdrew during the extension and were counted as nonresponders.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Of 44 week-34 responders identified by clinical-site assessment, 38 entered an observational extension. At week 68, 19/22 (86%) original BCG responders and 12/16 (75%) original placebo responders still reported moderate or marked improvement (P = 0.43). Five withdrawals were retained in those denominators; the follow-up comparison was restricted to initial responders.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The original course used up to six instillations over 6–10 weeks: 1 mL TICE BCG (5 ± 3 × 10^8 colony-forming units per 50 mg protein) in 49 mL saline, or 50 mL saline placebo, with a two-hour target dwell.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Among 260 who received at least one randomized instillation, 98% assigned BCG and 97% assigned placebo completed all six.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The primary report gives mean retention of 1.6 hours for BCG and 1.7 hours for placebo; the later responder follow-up prints the arm values in reverse.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Nonresponders could receive another six-week course of open-label BCG; responders entered observation without another study course.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Among 155 receiving at least one open-label instillation, 76% after prior BCG versus 94% after prior placebo completed all six (P = 0.0023).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Adverse events

Adverse events in the randomized safety set occurred in 123/129 (95%) with BCG and 126/132 (95%) with placebo (P = 0.73); Common Toxicity Criteria grade 3 events occurred in 64/129 (50%) and 63/132 (48%). Hematuria occurred in 45/129 (35%) and 27/132 (20%), respectively. One BCG participant had a grade 4 suprapubic-pain event judged unrelated to treatment. Of 32 serious events in 21 participants, investigators attributed two to treatment: BCG hypersensitivity and placebo-arm UTI/presumed prostatitis from catheterization.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

During open-label BCG, at least one adverse event occurred in 66/73 (90%) after prior BCG and 79/83 (95%) after prior placebo; grade 3 events occurred in 34/73 and 36/83, respectively.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Hematuria occurred in 24/73 (33%) and 16/83 (19%; P = 0.067).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Fifteen serious events were reported, including bladder pain and penile pain judged possibly drug-related; four withdrawals were attributed to adverse events.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The responder extension did not assess adverse events during its observational phase.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The open-label extension had no concurrent placebo group. The week-68 extension selected only initial responders and included small groups. Clinical-site classification for extension entry differed slightly from the centrally reviewed primary response classification.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA's 2022 interstitial cystitis/bladder pain syndrome guideline states that intravesical BCG should not be offered outside investigational study settings <Rec>(Standard (Evidence Strength: Grade B))</Rec>. Its evidence discussion cites the randomized report and both ICCTG extensions, and describes potentially serious BCG exposure events, including sepsis and death, from bladder-cancer literature.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
