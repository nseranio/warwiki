# REVIVE: Avanafil for Erectile Dysfunction

<TrialBottomLine id="avanafil-pivotal" />

**REVIVE** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized trial in 646 men with erectile dysfunction comparing on-demand avanafil 50, 100 or 200 mg with placebo; after 12 weeks, all avanafil doses improved successful penetration, successful intercourse and IIEF erectile-function scores on all three coprimary endpoints (largest reported P = 0.0014).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="avanafil-pivotal" />

## Design

Men had established mild to severe erectile dysfunction and were in a monogamous heterosexual relationship. The registry excluded men with diabetes, erectile dysfunction after prostatectomy or spinal cord injury, prior consistent PDE5-inhibitor failure, nitrate use and significant cardiovascular disease. A four-week untreated run-in preceded randomization; men needed at least four intercourse attempts, a failure rate of at least 50% and an IIEF erectile-function score of 5–25.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> SEP2 measured vaginal penetration, SEP3 measured intercourse completed with an erection, and the IIEF erectile-function domain measured self-reported function. The efficacy analysis included 622 men who received drug and had a posttreatment measure; missing IIEF data used last observation carried forward, while SEP2 and SEP3 data were not imputed.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

The randomized trial's coprimary SEP3 endpoint improved by a mean 41 percentage points with 50 mg and 57 points with either 100 or 200 mg, versus 27 points with placebo (P = 0.0002, P &lt; 0.0001 and P &lt; 0.0001, respectively). SEP2 improved with each dose (P &lt; 0.001 for each); the 100- and 200-mg groups achieved penetration on nearly eight in ten attempts at treatment end. IIEF erectile-function scores also improved with each dose (P = 0.0014 for 50 mg; P &lt; 0.0001 for 100 and 200 mg).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In a post hoc timing analysis of 300 attempts within 15 minutes of dosing, 64%–71% of avanafil-treated attempts ended in successful intercourse, versus 27% of placebo attempts. Among 80 attempts more than six hours after dosing, the corresponding rates were 59%–83% and 25%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the open-label extension of REVIVE and a separate diabetes trial, successful intercourse rates increased from the qualifying trials’ run-ins to extension treatment: among 712 enrollees (686 contributing efficacy data), they rose from 13% to 68% in the 100-mg-only group and from 11% to 66% in the group that received both 100 and 200 mg. Successful penetration rose from 44.1% to 83.3% and from 43.0% to 79.4%, respectively. Mean IIEF erectile-function scores rose from 13.6 to 22.2 and from 11.9 to 22.7, respectively. Among 172 men without a positive SEP3 change from baseline during the first 30 days on 100 mg who escalated, 112 (65.1%) had a positive SEP3 change on 200 mg. Among men attempting intercourse within 15 minutes of a dose, 169/203 (83%) had a successful attempt; the corresponding rate beyond six hours was 48/65 (74%).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Treatment delivery

In REVIVE, study drug was taken about 30 minutes before sexual activity, with at least 12 hours between doses and no food or alcohol restriction. In the extension, 536/712 (75.3%) received both 100 and 200 mg; 492/712 (69.1%) completed the trial.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Mean avanafil exposure was 35.3 weeks, and 153/711 (21.5%) of the safety population received it for at least 52 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Adverse events

In REVIVE's safety population, treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 52/160 (32.5%), 68/161 (42.2%) and 63/162 (38.9%) at 50, 100 and 200 mg, versus 42/161 (26.1%) with placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Headache affected 7/160 (4.4%), 12/161 (7.5%) and 15/162 (9.3%) at the three doses, versus 2/161 (1.2%) with placebo; flushing and nasal congestion were also reported.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Serious adverse events occurred in 1/160, 3/161 and 3/162 avanafil recipients, versus 2/161 placebo recipients; none were judged drug-related.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In the extension safety population, 275/711 (38.7%) experienced a treatment-emergent adverse event, 11/711 (1.5%) a serious adverse event and 20/711 (2.8%) discontinued because of adverse events; no serious event was judged drug-related.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

The randomized comparison lasted 12 weeks and used placebo rather than another PDE5 inhibitor. The extension combined two parent trials, included men with diabetes, allowed dose adjustment and had no placebo group. Enrollment closed early after the FDA exposure targets were met.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA erectile dysfunction guideline (2018) advises informing men with ED about FDA-approved oral PDE5 inhibitors, including avanafil, unless contraindicated <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The FDA approved Stendra (avanafil) for erectile dysfunction on April 27, 2012.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
