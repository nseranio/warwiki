# REVIVE: Avanafil for Erectile Dysfunction

<TrialBottomLine id="avanafil-pivotal" />

**REVIVE** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized trial in 646 men with erectile dysfunction comparing on-demand avanafil 50, 100 or 200 mg with placebo; after 12 weeks, all avanafil doses improved successful penetration, successful intercourse and IIEF erectile-function scores (P ≤ 0.001 for each comparison).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="avanafil-pivotal" />

## Design

Men had established mild to severe erectile dysfunction and were in a monogamous heterosexual relationship. The registry excluded men with diabetes, erectile dysfunction after prostatectomy or spinal cord injury, prior consistent PDE5-inhibitor failure, nitrate use and significant cardiovascular disease. An untreated run-in preceded randomization. SEP2 measured vaginal penetration, SEP3 measured intercourse completed with an erection, and the IIEF erectile-function domain measured self-reported function.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

In a secondary timing analysis of 300 attempts within 15 minutes of dosing, 64%–71% of avanafil-treated attempts ended in successful intercourse, versus 27% of placebo attempts. Among 80 attempts more than six hours after dosing, the corresponding rates were 59%–83% and 25%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The open-label extension enrolled 712 men from REVIVE and a separate diabetes trial; 686 contributed efficacy data. Successful intercourse rose from 13% to 68% in the 100-mg group and from 11% to 66% in the group that escalated to 200 mg. Mean IIEF erectile-function scores rose from 13.6 to 22.2 and from 11.9 to 22.7, respectively.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Adverse events

Headache, flushing and nasal congestion were the commonly reported adverse events with avanafil; no drug-related serious adverse events were reported. In the open-label extension, fewer than 3% discontinued treatment because of adverse events.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

The randomized comparison lasted 12 weeks and used placebo rather than another PDE5 inhibitor. The extension combined two parent trials, included men with diabetes, allowed dose adjustment and had no placebo group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA erectile dysfunction guideline (2018) advises informing men with ED about FDA-approved oral PDE5 inhibitors, including avanafil, unless contraindicated <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The FDA approved Stendra (avanafil) for erectile dysfunction on April 27, 2012.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
