# CPCRN Pregabalin: Pregabalin for Men With Chronic Prostatitis/Chronic Pelvic Pain Syndrome

<TrialBottomLine id="pregabalin-cpps" />

**CPCRN Pregabalin** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized trial of pregabalin versus placebo in men with chronic prostatitis/chronic pelvic pain syndrome; the prespecified six-week NIH-CPSI responder endpoint occurred in 103/218 (47.2%) versus 38/106 (35.8%) and did not meet the superiority threshold (P=.07).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="pregabalin-cpps" />

## Design

The Chronic Prostatitis Collaborative Research Network enrolled men at 10 North American tertiary centers. Eligible participants had pelvic pain during at least 3 of the preceding 6 months and NIH-CPSI total scores of at least 15 at both screening and randomization. Men with calculated creatinine clearance below 60 mL/min/1.73 m² were excluded. Prior CP/CPPS treatment, including prior gabapentin or pregabalin completed before enrollment, was permitted. Randomization was centralized within clinical center; participants and investigators were blinded.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The primary endpoint was a decrease of at least six points in the NIH-CPSI total score at week 6. Men who withdrew before that visit counted as nonresponders. The site-adjusted exact Mantel–Haenszel test used a two-sided significance threshold of P&lt;.05. Global Response Assessment (GRA) response required moderate or marked improvement; withdrawn men also counted as nonresponders for that analysis. Continuous secondary scores were analyzed among men with complete follow-up data.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The site-adjusted difference in primary responder rates was 10.9 percentage points (95% CI −0.0 to 21.8; P=.07). The point estimate favored pregabalin, but the prespecified primary comparison did not reach statistical significance.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For the secondary GRA endpoint, 68/218 (31.2%) assigned to pregabalin versus 20/106 (18.9%) assigned to placebo reported moderate or marked improvement; the site-adjusted difference was 12.0 percentage points (95% CI 2.6 to 21.5; P=.02). Mean NIH-CPSI total scores fell 6.6 versus 4.2 points, with a between-arm mean difference in change of −2.4 points (95% CI −4.1 to −0.6; P=.01). Mean pain-domain scores fell 3.3 versus 2.2 points, a between-arm mean difference in change of −1.0 point (95% CI −2.0 to −0.04; P=.04). These continuous-score findings came from participants with complete follow-up data.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Pregabalin was given orally at 150 mg/day for two weeks, then 300 mg/day for two weeks, and then 600 mg/day for two weeks; men who could not tolerate escalation could remain at the previously tolerated dose. Placebo capsules followed the same schedule. Of 324 randomized men, 313 (96.6%) completed the six-week visit. The highest dose was taken for at least one week by 154/218 (70.6%) in the pregabalin arm and 82/106 (77.4%) in the placebo arm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Neurologic adverse events were reported by 38.5% with pregabalin versus 22.6% with placebo (P=.01). Pain adverse events were reported by 17.4% versus 33.3% (P=.003), with headache the most common pain event. Across both arms, 191/324 (59.0%) reported at least one adverse event; the overall distribution did not differ between arms (P=.40), and no serious adverse events were reported.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Treatment and outcome ascertainment lasted six weeks. Participants generally had longstanding symptoms and could have received earlier CP/CPPS treatment. The trial did not select men by a defined neuropathic-pain phenotype. Continuous secondary outcomes used complete follow-up cases, whereas the primary endpoint and GRA counted withdrawals as nonresponders.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
