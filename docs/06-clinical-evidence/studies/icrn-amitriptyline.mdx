# ICCRN Amitriptyline: Treatment-Naïve Interstitial Cystitis/Painful Bladder Syndrome

<TrialBottomLine id="icrn-amitriptyline" />

**ICCRN Amitriptyline** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized trial in treatment-naïve adults with interstitial cystitis/painful bladder syndrome (IC/BPS) that compared amitriptyline plus education and behavioral modification with placebo plus the same program; amitriptyline did not significantly improve the primary global symptom response at 12 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="icrn-amitriptyline" />

## Design

Participants had bladder pain or discomfort and urinary frequency for at least six weeks, with no prior significant IC/BPS treatment. The investigators excluded active urinary infection, urinary retention, narrow-angle glaucoma, cardiac conduction disorders, and several prior IC/BPS treatments. Randomization was stratified within clinical center. Both arms received instruction on fluid management, bladder training, urge suppression, symptom management, and avoidance of possible dietary triggers.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The primary endpoint was a patient-reported Global Response Assessment at 12 weeks. “Moderately improved” or “markedly improved” counted as response; participants without an assessment after withdrawal counted as nonresponders. The prespecified primary comparison used a two-sided significance threshold of 0.05 and accounted for clinical center. Secondary symptom and voiding-diary changes used participants with data at both time points.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Global response occurred in 74/135 (55%) assigned amitriptyline versus 61/136 (45%) assigned placebo, a risk difference of 10 percentage points (95% CI −2 to 22; P = 0.12). The prespecified primary threshold was not met. Among participants who maintained at least 50 mg/day of assigned study drug through week 12, response occurred in 48/62 (77%) versus 52/98 (53%; P &lt; 0.001). This achieved-dose comparison was exploratory.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the available-case secondary analysis, the frequency symptom score changed by −3.5 in the amitriptyline arm versus −2.6 in the placebo arm (mean difference −0.9, 95% CI −1.5 to −0.3). Mean voids per 24 hours changed by −4.4 versus −2.0 (mean difference −2.4, 95% CI −3.7 to −1.0). These analyses included 111 and 119 participants, respectively, in the two arms.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Study drug started at 10 mg daily, then increased weekly to 25 mg and 50 mg; after three weeks, participants could increase to 75 mg if tolerated and continued their highest tolerated dose through week 12. Only 62/135 (46%) assigned amitriptyline maintained at least 50 mg/day, compared with 98/136 (72%) assigned matching placebo. Treatment-group withdrawals were 23/135 (17%) versus 17/136 (13%); 231/271 (85%) completed the 12-week follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Any adverse event was reported by 119/135 (88%) assigned amitriptyline and 98/136 (72%) assigned placebo. Constitutional symptoms, mainly fatigue or malaise, occurred in 61/135 (45%) versus 42/136 (31%); gastrointestinal symptoms, mainly dry mouth or constipation, in 57/135 (42%) versus 32/136 (24%). Neurologic symptoms, mainly dizziness or somnolence, occurred in 45/135 (33%) versus 29/136 (21%), and renal or genitourinary symptoms in 22/135 (16%) versus 10/136 (7%). Most adverse events were mild or moderate.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The trial assessed 12-week response, and dose tolerance differed between arms. Its education and behavioral program was given to every participant; its independent effect was therefore unmeasured. The achieved-dose response analysis selected participants after randomization.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The **AUA 2022** IC/BPS guideline cites Foster and lists amitriptyline, cimetidine, hydroxyzine, and pentosan polysulfate as oral medications that may be administered, with no hierarchy among them <Rec>(Option (Evidence Strength: Grades B, B, C, and B))</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
