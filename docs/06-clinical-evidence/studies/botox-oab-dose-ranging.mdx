# OnabotulinumtoxinA OAB dose-ranging trial: Intradetrusor dose selection for idiopathic overactive bladder

<TrialBottomLine id="botox-oab-dose-ranging" />

The **onabotulinumtoxinA OAB dose-ranging trial** randomized 313 patients with idiopathic overactive bladder and urgency urinary incontinence inadequately managed with anticholinergics to five intradetrusor doses (50–300 U) or placebo. Week-12 mean reductions in weekly incontinence episodes did not show a clear dose response; cumulative analysis favored doses of at least 100 U, while residual volume and catheterization increased with dose.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="botox-oab-dose-ranging" />

## Design

Entry required at least eight urgency-incontinence episodes per week and eight micturitions per day; detrusor overactivity was not required. A seven-day bladder diary measured the primary variable, change in weekly urgency-incontinence episodes at week 12. The intent-to-treat ANCOVA used an unadjusted P ≤0.05 threshold, with no multiplicity correction. The study randomized patients to placebo or onabotulinumtoxinA 50, 100, 150, 200, or 300 U.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The placebo arm enrolled 44; the active arms together enrolled 269.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

The week-12 mean changes in weekly urgency-incontinence episodes were −17.4 with placebo and −20.7, −18.4, −23.0, −19.6, and −19.4 with 50, 100, 150, 200, and 300 U, respectively. The cumulative rank analysis showed little additional symptom improvement above 150 U. The authors identified 100 U as a possible balance between symptom benefit and residual-volume safety.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The proportion with no urgency-incontinence episodes on the week-12 diary rose from 7/44 (15.9%) with placebo to 17/57 (29.8%), 20/54 (37.0%), 20/49 (40.8%), 27/53 (50.9%), and 32/56 (57.1%) across the ascending active doses.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The urodynamic analysis found week-12 mean maximum cystometric-capacity gains of 49.5 mL with placebo, 101.7 mL with 150 U (P = 0.05), and 130.8 mL with 300 U (P &lt; 0.01); other doses did not differ from placebo. At week 36, 300 U remained different from placebo (+59.3 vs +12.5 mL; P &lt; 0.05). Volume at first involuntary detrusor contraction, measured only in patients with a contraction at baseline and both follow-ups, did not differ from placebo at week 12; 150, 200, and 300 U did at week 36. Clinical symptom changes were similar with and without baseline detrusor overactivity, which was present in 76.0% at baseline.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The exploratory quality-of-life analysis found week-12 mean Incontinence Quality-of-Life Instrument total-score gains of 17.9 points with placebo and 29.8, 32.9, 35.2, 37.1, and 39.7 points across ascending active doses. Total-score improvement with doses of at least 100 U exceeded placebo from weeks 2 through 36; subscale improvement did so through week 24 (unadjusted P &lt; 0.05). At week 12, the King's Health Questionnaire symptom score also improved more than placebo at doses of at least 100 U (P &lt; 0.05). At week 36, the generic SF-36 showed significant between-group differences in four of eight domains at 300 U and one at 200 U.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Treatment was administered cystoscopically in 20 detrusor injections of 0.5 mL each, distributed while avoiding the trigone and dome, under local anesthesia with optional sedation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The trial compared five dose levels within the same placebo-controlled protocol; 272/313 (86.9%) completed 36 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Adverse events

Urinary tract infection and urinary retention were the only adverse events significantly more frequent with onabotulinumtoxinA than placebo. In the safety population, urinary tract infection occurred in 7/43 (16.3%) with placebo and 19/56 (33.9%), 20/55 (36.4%), 22/50 (44.0%), 25/52 (48.1%), and 19/55 (34.5%) across ascending active doses; retention occurred in 1/43 (2.3%) and 5/56 (8.9%), 10/55 (18.2%), 14/50 (28.0%), 12/52 (23.1%), and 14/55 (25.5%), respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> A posttreatment residual of at least 200 mL was recorded as retention after a protocol amendment, regardless of symptoms.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Postvoid residual volume and catheterization increased with dose: PVR-related catheterization occurred in 0/43 with placebo and 3/56 (5.4%), 6/55 (10.9%), 10/50 (20.0%), 11/52 (21.2%), and 9/55 (16.4%) across ascending active doses.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Among patients with posttreatment residual at least 200 mL, 33/55 (60.0%) required catheterization.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Serious adverse events affected 34/311 (10.9%) treated patients; two were drug related.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The realized allocation left 44 placebo recipients across five active-dose comparisons. Catheterization start and stop criteria were not standardized, and urinary tract infections did not require culture confirmation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The quality-of-life analyses were exploratory, some measures were unvalidated, and the trial was not powered for between-group quality-of-life differences.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA/SUFU 2024 idiopathic OAB guideline recommends offering intradetrusor botulinum toxin injection, sacral neuromodulation, and/or percutaneous tibial nerve stimulation after inadequate response or intolerable effects from pharmacotherapy or behavioral therapy <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade A)</Rec>. Its bibliography includes this dose-ranging report.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
