# OnabotulinumtoxinA OAB dose-ranging trial: Intradetrusor dose selection for idiopathic overactive bladder

<TrialBottomLine id="botox-oab-dose-ranging" />

The **onabotulinumtoxinA OAB dose-ranging trial** was a multicenter, double-blind phase 2 randomized comparison of five intradetrusor doses (50–300 U) with placebo in 313 patients with idiopathic overactive bladder and urgency urinary incontinence inadequately managed with anticholinergics; doses of 100 U or greater improved the week-12 incontinence outcome, while residual volume and catheterization increased with dose.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="botox-oab-dose-ranging" />

## Design

Entry required at least eight urgency-incontinence episodes per week and eight micturitions per day. A seven-day bladder diary measured the primary variable, weekly urgency-incontinence episodes, at week 12. The study randomized patients to placebo or onabotulinumtoxinA 50, 100, 150, 200, or 300 U.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The placebo arm enrolled 44; the active arms together enrolled 269.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

Dose-response analysis found little additional symptom improvement above 150 U. The authors identified 100 U as a possible balance between symptom benefit and residual-volume safety.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the later urodynamic analysis, maximum cystometric capacity increased from baseline at week 12 in all five onabotulinumtoxinA dose groups; among patients with an involuntary detrusor contraction recorded at baseline and at weeks 12 and 36, volume at first contraction increased within the 50-, 100-, 200-, and 300-U groups at week 12 but did not differ significantly from placebo; both responses generally diminished by week 36. At week 12, 15.9% of placebo recipients and 29.8%–57.1% across the 50–300 U groups were incontinence-free on diary. Clinical outcomes did not differ according to baseline detrusor overactivity.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The quality-of-life analysis found greater improvement than placebo in the Incontinence Quality-of-Life Instrument total and subscale scores with doses of at least 100 U at visits from week 2 through week 24 (P &lt; 0.05).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Treatment was administered intradetrusor as a single injection procedure. The trial compared five dose levels within the same placebo-controlled protocol.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Adverse events

Urinary tract infection and urinary retention were the only adverse events significantly more frequent with onabotulinumtoxinA than placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Postvoid residual volume and clean intermittent catheterization increased with dose; residuals above 200 mL were more common with doses above 150 U.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The dose-ranging study used an unequal placebo allocation, with 44 placebo recipients across five active-dose comparisons. The quality-of-life analysis included exploratory measures that had not been validated.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA/SUFU 2024 idiopathic OAB guideline recommends offering intradetrusor botulinum toxin injection, sacral neuromodulation, and/or percutaneous tibial nerve stimulation after inadequate response or intolerable effects from pharmacotherapy or behavioral therapy <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade A)</Rec>. Its bibliography includes this dose-ranging report.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
