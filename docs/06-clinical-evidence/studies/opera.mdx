# OPERA: Extended-Release Oxybutynin Versus Tolterodine for Overactive Bladder

<TrialBottomLine id="opera" />

**OPERA** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized comparison of oxybutynin ER and tolterodine ER in 790 women with overactive bladder; weekly urge urinary incontinence improved similarly over 12 weeks, while fewer voids and more complete continence favored oxybutynin.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="opera" />

## Design

At 71 US centers, investigators enrolled women aged 18 years or older reporting 21–60 urge urinary incontinence episodes per week and at least 10 voids per 24 hours; postvoid residual urine above 150 mL was an exclusion. They assigned 391 women to oxybutynin ER 10 mg daily and 399 to tolterodine ER 4 mg daily. Overencapsulated medication was taken once daily for 12 weeks; 339/391 and 357/399 completed treatment. Seven-day urinary diaries captured urge and total incontinence episodes and micturition at baseline and weeks 2, 4, 8, and 12. Final weekly urge urinary incontinence episodes were the primary endpoint, analyzed by baseline-adjusted ANCOVA in 382 and 393 women with at least one on-treatment efficacy assessment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Final weekly urge urinary incontinence episodes fell from 37.1 to 10.8 with oxybutynin and from 36.7 to 11.2 with tolterodine in the 382/393-person efficacy set (last observation carried forward); the primary comparison was not statistically significant. Oxybutynin reduced weekly micturitions by 28.4 versus 25.2 (final means 66.4 versus 71.1; P = 0.003). At the last efficacy assessment, 23.0% versus 16.8% reported no urinary incontinence episodes in their 7-day diary (absolute difference 6.2 percentage points; P = 0.03); 26.7% versus 20.9% reported no urge incontinence episodes (P = 0.06).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Dry mouth affected 116/391 (29.7%) with oxybutynin and 89/399 (22.3%) with tolterodine (P = 0.02); 87 and 69 cases, respectively, were mild.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Adverse events or intercurrent illness led to discontinuation in 20/391 versus 19/399, and urinary tract infection occurred in 20/391 versus 13/399.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Urinary retention or impaired urination occurred in fewer than 5% of either group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> A subsequent OPERA analysis reported dry mouth in 28.1% versus 21.6% (P = 0.039), severe dry mouth in 1.5% versus 0.5%, and withdrawal because of dry mouth in 7/391 versus 4/399 women.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

A separate analysis found reported central nervous system adverse events in 35/391 (9.0%) versus 33/399 (8.3%; P = 0.8001), without a significant between-group difference. All such reported events were mild or moderate; six versus two women discontinued because of a CNS event, and neither group had serious treatment-related adverse events.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

The active comparator design did not include a placebo group. Treatment lasted 12 weeks, and the trial enrolled women with frequent urge incontinence, so it did not assess long-term outcomes or less symptomatic OAB. The CNS analysis counted reported adverse events rather than formal cognitive test performance.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
