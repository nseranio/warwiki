# OPERA: Extended-Release Oxybutynin Versus Tolterodine for Overactive Bladder

<TrialBottomLine id="opera" />

**OPERA** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized comparison of oxybutynin ER and tolterodine ER in 790 women with overactive bladder; weekly urge urinary incontinence improved similarly over 12 weeks, while fewer voids and more complete continence favored oxybutynin.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="opera" />

## Design

At 71 US centers, investigators enrolled women reporting 21–60 urge urinary incontinence episodes per week and at least 10 voids per 24 hours. They assigned 391 women to oxybutynin ER 10 mg daily and 399 to tolterodine ER 4 mg daily. Urinary diaries captured urge and total incontinence episodes and micturition at baseline and weeks 2, 4, 8, and 12. Change in weekly urge urinary incontinence episodes was the primary endpoint.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Both groups had similar improvements in weekly urge urinary incontinence episodes. Oxybutynin reduced micturition frequency more than tolterodine (P = 0.003); among women with an efficacy assessment, 23.0% versus 16.8% reported no urinary incontinence episodes in their last 7-day voiding diary (absolute difference 6.2 percentage points; P = 0.03).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

The primary report found more dry mouth with oxybutynin (P = 0.02), usually mild, and similar discontinuation due to adverse events.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> A subsequent OPERA analysis reported dry mouth in 28.1% versus 21.6% (P = 0.039), severe dry mouth in 1.5% versus 0.5%, and withdrawal because of dry mouth in 7/391 versus 4/399 women.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

A separate analysis found reported central nervous system adverse events in 9% versus 8%, without a significant between-group difference. All such reported events were mild or moderate; neither group had serious treatment-related adverse events.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

The active comparator design did not include a placebo group. Treatment lasted 12 weeks, and the trial enrolled women with frequent urge incontinence, so it did not assess long-term outcomes or less symptomatic OAB. The CNS analysis counted reported adverse events rather than formal cognitive test performance.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
