# OM-89: Oral Escherichia coli Extract for Recurrent Urinary Tract Infection

<TrialBottomLine id="om-89" />

**OM-89** reduced post-baseline UTI rates in a multinational, double-blind randomized trial of women with recurrent UTI: the 12-month ITT means were 0.84 versus 1.28 per woman (placebo-minus-OM-89 difference 0.44, 95% CI 0.15–0.73; p = 0.0026).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="om-89" />

## Design

The study randomized 454 ambulatory women aged 18–65 years, 232 to OM-89 and 222 to placebo; 453 received treatment and entered the ITT analysis. Eligibility required at least three documented UTIs in the previous year and acute symptoms for at least two days with at least 10³ bacteria/mL in urine. A post-baseline acute UTI required at least one week without anti-infectives, a urine count of at least 10³/mL, and at least two of dysuria, frequency, and burning on urination for at least two days. The three prespecified primary efficacy criteria were UTI rate over 12 months, distribution of UTIs per woman, and the proportion with at least one post-baseline UTI.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

All three prespecified ITT efficacy endpoints favored OM-89. Mean post-baseline UTI episodes per woman over 12 months were 0.84 in 231 OM-89 recipients versus 1.28 in 222 placebo recipients (placebo-minus-OM-89 difference 0.44, 95% CI 0.15–0.73; p = 0.0026), a 34% lower rate. At least one recurrence occurred in 93/231 (40.3%) versus 122/222 (55.0%; risk difference −14.7 percentage points; p = 0.001). The distribution of recurrences per woman also favored OM-89 (p = 0.0013): 127/231 (55.0%) versus 93/222 (41.9%) had none, and total post-baseline episodes numbered 185 versus 276.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The secondary mean number of anti-infective prescriptions was 2.44 ± 1.75 with OM-89 versus 2.79 ± 2.07 with placebo (p = 0.005); prescriptions also included treatment for reasons other than UTI. Post-baseline UTI episodes numbered 99 versus 125 in months 1–6 and 86 versus 151 in months 7–12, respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Participants took one capsule of 6 mg lyophilized *E. coli* lysate or matching placebo daily for 90 days, paused treatment for three months, and then took a capsule on the first 10 days of months 7, 8, and 9. No study capsule was given in months 10–12; follow-up continued through month 12. Compliance was checked through returned blister packs, and per-protocol analysis required at least 70% compliance.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Adverse events affected 75/231 (32.5%) OM-89 recipients and 71/222 (32.0%) placebo recipients, with 161 and 192 events, respectively. Serious adverse events numbered 11 versus 4 events; investigators judged none related to study medication.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The placebo comparator did not assess OM-89 against continuous or postcoital antibiotic prophylaxis. Women entered while experiencing an acute, bacteriuria-positive UTI, and the efficacy outcomes counted infections after that baseline episode. The trial was not designed to quantify the booster courses' contribution to the treatment effect.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The European Association of Urology 2026 guideline recommends immunomodulatory prophylaxis to reduce recurrent cystitis in women only in a well-regulated clinical trial <Rec>(Weak)</Rec>. Its evidence summary names OM-89 among the most studied agents and reports no statistically significant agent-specific placebo difference in its cited meta-analysis.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
