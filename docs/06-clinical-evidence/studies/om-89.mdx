# OM-89: Oral Escherichia coli Extract for Recurrent Urinary Tract Infection

<TrialBottomLine id="om-89" />

**OM-89** was a multinational, double-blind randomized trial comparing oral *Escherichia coli* extract with matching placebo in women with recurrent UTI; mean post-baseline UTI rates over 12 months were 0.84 versus 1.28 (p &lt; 0.003).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="om-89" />

## Design

The study randomized 454 women, 232 to OM-89 and 222 to placebo; 453 received treatment. Eligible women had an acute UTI at enrollment with urinalysis showing at least 10³ bacteria/mL. The prespecified efficacy criteria were UTI rates over 12 months, the distribution of UTIs, and the proportion of women with a post-baseline UTI.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Among 231 OM-89 recipients and 222 placebo recipients, 93/231 (40.3%) versus 122/222 (55.0%) had at least one post-baseline UTI (risk difference −14.7 percentage points; p = 0.001). The groups had 185 and 276 post-baseline UTI episodes, respectively. The investigators described the difference in mean UTI rate as a 34% reduction with OM-89.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Participants took one OM-89 or matching placebo capsule daily for 90 days, paused treatment for three months, and then took a capsule on the first 10 days of months 7, 8, and 9. Follow-up continued through month 12.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

The investigators characterized the safety profile as good and consistent with previous OM-89 studies.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The placebo comparator did not assess OM-89 against continuous or postcoital antibiotic prophylaxis. Women entered while experiencing an acute, bacteriuria-positive UTI, and the efficacy outcomes counted infections after that baseline episode.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The European Association of Urology 2026 guideline recommends immunomodulatory prophylaxis to reduce recurrent cystitis in women only in a well-regulated clinical trial <Rec>(Weak)</Rec>. Its evidence summary names OM-89 among the most studied agents and reports no statistically significant agent-specific placebo difference in its cited meta-analysis.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
