# Osman Antireflux Trial: Serous-Lined Extramural Tunnel Versus T-Limb Ileal Procedure

<TrialBottomLine id="osman-antireflux" />

In a prospective randomized trial of 60 patients with invasive bladder cancer undergoing orthotopic ileal substitution, a serous-lined extramural ureteral tunnel produced fewer refluxing renal units on ascending studies than a T-limb ileal antireflux procedure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="osman-antireflux" />

## Design

Opaque sealed envelopes allocated 30 patients (55 renal units) per arm to the two ureteroileal antireflux constructions. Before surgery, patients underwent intravenous urography, radioisotope assessment of glomerular filtration rate (GFR), and technetium-99m dimercaptosuccinic acid imaging for cortical scarring. Evaluable patients had repeat imaging and ascending studies. Randomization was by patient, while reflux, scarring, and stricture were reported by renal unit; the stated significance threshold was P &lt; 0.05.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Reflux was less frequent with the tunnel at mean 6.3 (SD 0.5) versus 7.4 (SD 1.9) months: among 27 patients/49 renal units and 23 patients/45 units with follow-up, ascending studies found reflux in 0/49 (0%) versus 13/45 (29%) units (P &lt; 0.01). Progressive cortical scarring, with or without a &gt;25% reduction in GFR, occurred in 3/49 versus 4/45 units; a &gt;25% GFR reduction occurred in 1/49 versus 4/45 units. Among the 13 refluxing T-limb units, three had a &gt;25% GFR reduction and one developed progressive cortical scarring.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The randomized intervention was the antireflux method used in the ileal substitute: an ileal W neobladder with a serous-lined extramural ureteral tunnel or a T-limb ileal procedure. The two methods were assessed with the same upper-tract imaging at follow-up. For ascending studies, contrast and saline flowed from a container 50 cm above the patient until reflux or maximum pouch capacity, with postero-anterior and oblique images.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Ureteroileal stricture was diagnosed in 1/49 evaluated tunnel units and 1/45 evaluated T-limb units (P = 0.51). Early urinary leak affected 1/30 patients in each arm; the tunnel-arm leak required percutaneous drainage and longer urethral catheterization, while the T-limb leak required temporary percutaneous nephrostomy. Temporary anastomotic oedema obstructed 2/30 T-limb patients and resolved after 4 and 6 weeks of nephrostomy drainage. Three of 60 randomized patients died perioperatively; their arm allocation was not reported.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The follow-up analysis excluded 10 of 60 randomized patients: three died perioperatively, five had oncological failure, and two had drug-induced renal failure. Outcomes were reported by renal unit, with two units potentially contributed by one patient.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
