# Osman Antireflux Trial: Serous-Lined Extramural Tunnel Versus T-Limb Ileal Procedure

<TrialBottomLine id="osman-antireflux" />

In a prospective randomized trial of 60 patients with invasive bladder cancer undergoing orthotopic ileal substitution, a serous-lined extramural ureteral tunnel produced fewer refluxing renal units on ascending studies than a T-limb ileal antireflux procedure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="osman-antireflux" />

## Design

The investigators compared two ureteroileal antireflux constructions. Before surgery, patients underwent intravenous urography, radioisotope assessment of glomerular filtration rate (GFR), and technetium-99m dimercaptosuccinic acid imaging for cortical scarring. Evaluable patients had repeat imaging and ascending studies. Randomization was by patient, while reflux, scarring, and stricture were reported by renal unit.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The follow-up analysis included 27 patients with 49 renal units in the tunnel group and 23 patients with 45 units in the T-limb group. Ascending studies found reflux in 0/49 (0%) versus 13/45 (29%) units (P &lt; 0.01). Progressive cortical scarring, with or without a &gt;25% reduction in GFR, occurred in 3/49 versus 4/45 units. Among the 13 refluxing T-limb units, three had a &gt;25% GFR reduction and one developed progressive cortical scarring.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The randomized intervention was the antireflux method used in the ileal substitute: a serous-lined extramural ureteral tunnel or a T-limb ileal procedure. The two methods were assessed with the same upper-tract imaging at follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Ureteroileal stricture was diagnosed in 1/49 evaluated tunnel units and 1/45 evaluated T-limb units (P = 0.5).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The follow-up analysis excluded 10 of 60 randomized patients. Outcomes were reported by renal unit, with two units potentially contributed by one patient.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
