# ARIA dutasteride trials: Dutasteride for symptomatic benign prostatic hyperplasia

<TrialBottomLine id="aria-dutasteride" />

The pooled, double-blind ARIA trials randomized men with symptomatic enlarged prostates to dutasteride or placebo; at 24 months, dutasteride improved symptoms and peak flow and reduced acute urinary retention and BPH-related surgery.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="aria-dutasteride" />

## Design

The three trials enrolled 4,325 men at least 50 years old with clinical BPH, AUA Symptom Index at least 12, Qmax at most 15 mL/s, transrectal-ultrasound prostate volume at least 30 cm3, and PSA 1.5–10 ng/mL. Men with postvoid residual &gt;250 mL, acute urinary retention within three months, prior prostate surgery, alpha-blocker use within four weeks, or any prior 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor use were excluded. A one-month single-blind placebo lead-in preceded dutasteride 0.5 mg daily (n = 2,167) or placebo (n = 2,158). Treatment and assessment remained double-blind for 24 months; the primary endpoints were AUA Symptom Index change and acute urinary retention.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The pooled trials found greater 24-month symptom improvement with dutasteride: mean AUA Symptom Index changed −4.5 points (21.4%) versus −2.3 points with placebo among at-visit completers (between-group difference −2.2 points; P &lt; 0.001), with pooled separation from month 6. Acute urinary retention occurred in 39/2,167 (1.8%) versus 90/2,158 (4.2%) men, respectively (relative risk 0.43, 95% CI 0.29–0.62; P &lt; 0.001). BPH-related surgery occurred in 47/2,167 (2.2%) versus 89/2,158 (4.1%) (relative risk 0.52, 95% CI 0.37–0.74; P &lt; 0.001). At 24 months, mean Qmax rose 2.2 versus 0.6 mL/s, total prostate volume changed −25.7% versus +1.7%, and serum PSA changed −52.4% versus +15.8% (P &lt; 0.001 for each between-group comparison). After six months of dutasteride treatment, the authors advise doubling measured total PSA for clinical decisions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Men continuing dutasteride had larger symptom, flow and prostate-volume changes at month 48 than men switching from placebo in the open-label extension. Among 2,340 enrollees (1,188 continuing; 1,152 switching to dutasteride 0.5 mg daily), mean AUA Symptom Index change from the original baseline was −6.5 versus −5.6 points (P &lt; 0.001), Qmax change was +2.7 versus +1.9 mL/s (P = 0.042), and total prostate-volume change was −27.3% versus −21.7% (P &lt; 0.001), respectively, in the open-label intent-to-treat population. Symptoms and Qmax improved from month 24 in both groups; the further prostate-volume reduction in the continuous-dutasteride group did not reach significance (P = 0.07).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Crude acute urinary retention incidence through month 48 was 2.4% in the continuous-dutasteride group (original double-blind ITT n = 2,167) and 5.1% in the switch group (n = 2,158); BPH-related surgery incidence was 2.6% and 4.5%, respectively. Both groups received dutasteride after month 24.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Drug-related sexual adverse events were more frequent with dutasteride than placebo over 24 months: impotence occurred in 158/2,167 (7.3%) versus 86/2,158 (4.0%), decreased libido in 91/2,167 (4.2%) versus 46/2,158 (2.1%), ejaculation disorder in 48/2,167 (2.2%) versus 17/2,158 (0.8%), and gynecomastia in 50/2,167 (2.3%) versus 16/2,158 (0.7%) (P &lt; 0.001 for each).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Any adverse event occurred in 77% versus 75%, and a drug-related event in 19% versus 14%, respectively; diagnosed prostate cancer occurred in 24/2,167 (1.1%) versus 42/2,158 (1.9%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> During extension months 24–36, new drug-related impotence occurred in 1.4% of men continuing dutasteride (n = 1,188) and 2.8% of men starting it (n = 1,152); decreased libido occurred in 0.4% and 2.4%, ejaculation disorders in 0.3% and 1.2%, and gynaecomastia in 1.8% and 1.3%, respectively.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Any new drug-related event occurred in 6.4% and 10.5%, respectively.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The 24-month symptom and flow estimates used at-visit completers; of 4,325 randomized men, 2,951 (68.2%) completed the double-blind period.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The open-label extension treated both original groups with dutasteride.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA's 2026 BPH guideline cites the pooled ARIA report in its 5-alpha-reductase-inhibitor discussion. For patients with LUTS/BPH and estimated prostate volume &gt;30 cc and/or PSA &gt;1.5 ng/mL, it recommends offering a 5-ARI for symptom relief <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade A)</Rec> and says clinicians may offer a 5-ARI to reduce the risk of clinical BPH progression <Rec>(Clinical Principle)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The FDA approved dutasteride for symptomatic BPH in men with an enlarged prostate on November 20, 2001.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
