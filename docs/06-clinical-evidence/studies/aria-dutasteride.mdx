# ARIA dutasteride trials: Dutasteride for symptomatic benign prostatic hyperplasia

<TrialBottomLine id="aria-dutasteride" />

The pooled, double-blind ARIA trials randomized men with symptomatic enlarged prostates to dutasteride or placebo; at 24 months, dutasteride improved symptoms and peak flow and reduced acute urinary retention and BPH-related surgery.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="aria-dutasteride" />

## Design

The three trials enrolled 4,325 men with clinical BPH, AUA Symptom Index at least 12, Qmax at most 15 mL/s, transrectal-ultrasound prostate volume at least 30 cm3, and PSA 1.5–10 ng/mL. A one-month single-blind placebo lead-in preceded dutasteride 0.5 mg daily or placebo. Treatment and assessment remained double-blind for 24 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At 24 months, AUA Symptom Index had fallen 4.5 points (21.4%) in the dutasteride group, with significant separation from placebo from month 6. Qmax rose 2.2 mL/s and mean total prostate volume fell 25.7% from baseline. The abstract reports a 57% relative reduction in acute urinary retention and a 48% relative reduction in BPH-related surgery versus placebo; it gives neither arm's event count or absolute risk.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In a pooled extension of all three trials, both groups received dutasteride through month 48. Symptom scores and Qmax improved further from month 24 in both groups; men who received dutasteride throughout had greater symptom and flow improvements and greater prostate-volume reduction at month 48 than men who switched from placebo. Those later groups no longer formed a placebo-controlled comparison.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

The initial report describes dutasteride as well tolerated without adverse-event counts in its abstract. The extension abstract reports no new safety issue and says new drug-related events were reported most often after treatment initiation, then declined over time.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The primary abstract does not give arm-specific event rates, adverse-event rates, or the prespecified endpoint hierarchy. Of 4,325 randomized men, 2,951 (68.2%) completed the double-blind period.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The open-label extension treated both original groups with dutasteride.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA's 2026 BPH guideline cites the pooled ARIA report in its 5-alpha-reductase-inhibitor discussion. For patients with LUTS/BPH and estimated prostate volume &gt;30 cc and/or PSA &gt;1.5 ng/mL, it recommends offering a 5-ARI for symptom relief <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade A)</Rec> and says clinicians may offer a 5-ARI to reduce the risk of clinical BPH progression <Rec>(Clinical Principle)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The FDA approved dutasteride for symptomatic BPH in men with an enlarged prostate on November 20, 2001.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
