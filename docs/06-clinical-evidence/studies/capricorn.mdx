# CAPRICORN: Mirabegron 25 mg or 50 mg Versus Placebo for Overactive Bladder

<TrialBottomLine id="capricorn" />

**CAPRICORN** was a multicenter, double-blind, phase III randomized trial in 1,306 adults with overactive bladder that compared once-daily mirabegron 25 mg or 50 mg with placebo for 12 weeks; both doses produced significantly greater improvement in daily micturitions in the full analysis set and in daily incontinence episodes among the 773 participants in the incontinence full analysis set (FAS-I).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="capricorn" />

## Design

After a two-week single-blind placebo run-in, patients with overactive bladder symptoms for at least three months, at least eight micturitions and three grade 3 or 4 urgency episodes per 24 hours were randomized equally among the three oral treatment groups; predominant stress incontinence was excluded. The registry lists 433 patients assigned to placebo, 433 to mirabegron 25 mg, and 440 to mirabegron 50 mg.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The coprimary endpoints were change from baseline to the final visit in mean daily incontinence episodes and mean daily micturitions. Key secondary endpoints included voided volume, week-4 incontinence and micturitions, and urgency measures; patient-reported outcomes were additional secondary endpoints. Testing proceeded through a stepwise gatekeeping procedure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Both doses met the coprimary endpoints at 12 weeks. In the FAS-I, adjusted daily incontinence episodes fell by 1.36 with 25 mg (n=254), 1.38 with 50 mg (n=257), and 0.96 with placebo (n=262); differences versus placebo were −0.40 (95% CI −0.74 to −0.06; P=0.005) and −0.42 (95% CI −0.76 to −0.08; P=0.001), respectively. In the FAS, adjusted daily micturitions fell by 1.65 with 25 mg (n=410), 1.60 with 50 mg (n=426), and 1.18 with placebo; differences were −0.47 (95% CI −0.82 to −0.13; P=0.007) and −0.42 (95% CI −0.76 to −0.08; P=0.015).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The 50 mg dose met the key secondary endpoint of volume voided per micturition, increasing it by 20.7 mL versus an increase of 12.8 mL with 25 mg; the placebo-adjusted differences were +12.4 mL (95% CI +6.3 to +18.6; P&lt;0.001) and +4.6 mL (95% CI −1.6 to +10.8; P=0.15), respectively. The 25 mg key secondary testing stopped after voided volume. At week 4, 50 mg met the incontinence endpoint (difference −0.51 episodes/day, 95% CI −0.85 to −0.17; P&lt;0.001), but its micturition difference of −0.37 (95% CI −0.71 to −0.03; P=0.035) missed the prespecified 0.025 threshold.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Treatment satisfaction improved versus placebo with both doses: the TS-VAS difference was +0.49 (95% CI +0.07 to +0.91; P=0.024) for 25 mg and +0.83 (95% CI +0.41 to +1.25; P&lt;0.001) for 50 mg. The 50 mg dose also reduced OAB-q symptom bother (difference −2.8, 95% CI −5.3 to −0.3; P=0.028); OAB-q HRQL total score and Patient Perception of Bladder Condition did not differ significantly from placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Once-daily mirabegron 25 mg, 50 mg, or matching placebo was given for 12 weeks; treatment discontinuation occurred in 46/433 (10.6%), 54/440 (12.3%), and 66/433 (15.2%), respectively. A safety visit followed two weeks after treatment ended.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 210/432 (48.6%) with 25 mg, 208/440 (47.3%) with 50 mg, and 217/433 (50.1%) with placebo; severe events occurred in 8/432 (1.9%), 8/440 (1.8%), and 16/433 (3.7%), respectively. Urinary tract infection occurred in 18/432 (4.2%), 21/440 (4.8%), and 10/433 (2.3%), respectively; hypertension preferred-term events occurred in 49/432 (11.3%), 47/440 (10.7%), and 37/433 (8.5%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Urinary retention was reported in 0/432 with 25 mg, 0/440 with 50 mg, and 1/433 with placebo. At the final visit, post-void residual was at least 300 mL in one 50 mg recipient and two placebo recipients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Randomized treatment lasted 12 weeks. The placebo run-in preceded allocation, and the trial compared each active dose with placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="capricorn" />

SCORPIO and ARIES tested mirabegron 50 mg and 100 mg in separate geographic cohorts; CAPRICORN supplied the 25 mg comparison.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup><sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> All three used daily incontinence episodes and micturitions as coprimary endpoints.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup><sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA/SUFU 2024 idiopathic overactive bladder guideline cites this report and recommends offering an antimuscarinic medication or a beta-3 agonist to improve urgency, frequency and/or urgency urinary incontinence <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade A)</Rec>.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
