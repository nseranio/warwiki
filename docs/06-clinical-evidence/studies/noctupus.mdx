# Desmopressin Nocturia Studies: Oral Desmopressin for Nocturia in Men and Women

<TrialBottomLine id="noctupus" />

The multinational, double-blind phase III study randomized 151 men with nocturia and nocturnal urine production exceeding functional bladder capacity, after a response to oral desmopressin titration, to continue desmopressin or receive placebo; after three weeks, 28/83 (34%) versus 2/63 (3%) in the efficacy population had more than a 50% reduction in nightly voids (odds ratio 20, 95% CI 4.0–105.2; P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="noctupus" />

## Design

Men had at least two nocturnal voids and nocturnal urine production exceeding their measured maximum functional bladder capacity. The trial excluded patients with diabetes insipidus, primary polydipsia, multiple sclerosis, urge incontinence, recently initiated treatment for bladder outlet obstruction, fluid or electrolyte imbalance, low baseline sodium, or uncontrolled hypertension. Diaries recorded voiding, fluid intake, sleep times, and dosing. The prespecified primary endpoint was the proportion whose mean nightly void count fell by more than half from screening to the double-blind period; the test was stratified by country.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The separate women's trial enrolled women with at least two nightly voids and a nocturia index above 1; 144/224 exposed women entered the double-blind phase after titration and washout.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The long-term report then followed responders from the preceding short-term studies without a randomized comparator. Its primary objective was safety, with emphasis on serum sodium; nocturnal voids, time to first void, and responder proportion were secondary efficacy outcomes. No formal statistical tests were performed.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Results

In the men's trial, mean nightly voids changed from 3.0 to 1.7 with desmopressin and from 3.2 to 2.7 with placebo. The between-group mean difference in change was −0.8 voids (95% CI −1.10 to −0.58; P &lt; 0.001). The mean first sleep period changed from 2.66 to 4.5 hours and from 2.5 to 2.9 hours, respectively, with a between-group mean difference in change of 1.38 hours (95% CI 0.95–1.80; P &lt; 0.001). Five randomized men lacked efficacy data and were excluded from these analyses.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the women's trial, the prespecified primary endpoint was met: 33/72 (46%) on desmopressin versus 5/70 (7%) in the placebo intention-to-treat population had at least a 50% reduction in mean nightly voids after three weeks (OR 13.4, 95% CI 4.6–39.2; P &lt; 0.0001).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The open-label extension enrolled 132 men and 117 women who had responded in the short-term studies. During treatment, reported mean nightly voids ranged from 1.3 to 1.6 in men and 1.2 to 1.3 in women, against pretreatment baselines of 3.1 and 2.9.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Among patients assessed at month 10, 49/95 men (52%) and 56/85 women (66%) had at least a 50% reduction in nightly voids from baseline.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Mean time to the first void rose from 157 to 288 minutes in men and from 142 to 310 minutes in women by month 12, then shortened after treatment stopped.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

In the men's study, 224 patients started open-label oral desmopressin at bedtime. The dose advanced from 0.1 to 0.2 or 0.4 mg according to nocturnal diuresis and tolerability.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> After a one-week washout, 151 responders were randomized: 86 to their selected desmopressin dose and 65 to placebo. From one hour before bedtime until eight hours after dosing, participants drank only to satisfy thirst and avoided drinks with diuretic effects. In the women's study, 0.1–0.4 mg was titrated at bedtime; 141/144 (98%) completed double-blind treatment, and at least 80% adherence was 72/72 (100%) versus 68/72 (94%).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In the extension, 95/132 men and 87/117 women completed 10 or 12 months of treatment; 115/132 men (87%) and 100/117 women (85%) took at least 80% of study medication.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Adverse events

During the men's double-blind period, adverse events occurred in 15/86 (17%) on desmopressin and 16/65 (25%) on placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> During preceding open-label titration, 10/224 (4%) had serum sodium below 130 mmol/L; six were reported as hyponatremic and three withdrew.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> All hyponatremia cases occurred before randomization. In the women's titration, 14/224 (6%) reported hyponatremia, 13/224 (6%) had sodium below 130 mmol/L, and 7/224 (3%) withdrew.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> During double-blind treatment, adverse events affected 26/72 (36%) versus 25/72 (35%); no below-normal sodium was reported.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Two deaths occurred during titration: one was considered unlikely to be treatment related; the other was mainly attributed to diabetic complications, although an initial causal link to pneumonia and respiratory insufficiency could not be excluded.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

During the open-label extension, any adverse event occurred in 92/132 men (70%) and 90/117 women (77%); serious adverse events occurred in 17/132 (13%) and 9/117 (8%), respectively. Adverse events led 18/132 men (14%) and 12/117 women (10%) to withdraw. Four men (4/132, 3%) and one woman (1/117, 1%) had serious drug-related adverse events; her events included hyponatremia. Hyponatremia occurred in 21/132 men (16%) and 14/117 women (12%): 33/35 cases were asymptomatic with sodium at least 130 mmol/L, while one man and one woman had symptomatic sodium of 125 to below 130 mmol/L. No participant had sodium below 125 mmol/L. Treatment-related urinary tract infection affected 1/132 men (1%) and 3/117 women (3%).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

Randomization occurred only after a pharmacodynamic response to desmopressin during open-label titration, so the randomized effect is specific to selected responders. The blinded comparison lasted three weeks. The later women's trial was a separate cohort, while the extension had no concurrent placebo group and enrolled earlier responders.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The European Association of Urology's 2026 non-neurogenic female LUTS guideline cites the women's trial among its desmopressin studies. It recommends offering desmopressin to women with nocturia from nocturnal polyuria after counselling on benefits and risks, including hyponatremia <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>. It also recommends careful sodium monitoring in older patients and avoiding treatment when baseline sodium is below normal <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
