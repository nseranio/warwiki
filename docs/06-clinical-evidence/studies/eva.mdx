# EVA: Vaginal Oestrogen Therapy for Postmenopausal Women Undergoing Prolapse Surgery

<TrialBottomLine id="eva" />

**EVA** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized trial of perioperative vaginal oestriol cream versus placebo in postmenopausal women undergoing primary native-tissue prolapse repair; more oestriol recipients reported much or very much improvement in prolapse symptoms at 12 months (97/106 [91.5%] versus 83/104 [79.8%]; OR 2.73, 95% CI 1.18–6.28), while composite surgical success did not differ significantly.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="eva" />

## Design

Twenty-two Dutch hospitals enrolled women with POP-Q stage 2 or greater in at least one compartment. Each planned native-tissue operation had to include anterior or posterior colporrhaphy. Previous prolapse surgery in the same compartment, mesh repair, estrogen use in the preceding year, and suspected estrogen-dependent malignancy were exclusions. Gynaecologists or residents screened patients; independent gynaecologists performed follow-up examinations. Randomization was stratified by center, and participants and investigators were masked.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The primary endpoint was a patient rating of “much” or “very much” improved prolapse symptoms on the PGI-I at 12 months. The sample-size plan used a difference greater than 15 percentage points as clinically relevant and a two-sided superiority alpha of 0.05. Secondary composite surgical success required no prolapse beyond the hymen, no bulge symptoms, and no reoperation or additional pessary treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The primary PGI-I comparison met its statistical threshold (P = 0.02). The observed absolute difference was 11.7 percentage points, below the difference used in the clinical relevance and sample-size plan. Among examined participants, composite surgical success was 74/102 (72.5%) with oestriol and 74/96 (77.1%) with placebo (P = 0.46). Cystocele stage 2 or greater was present in 46/108 (42.6%) and 47/103 (45.6%), respectively (P = 0.66); rectocele stage 2 or greater in 26/108 (24.1%) and 15/103 (14.6%; P = 0.08); and apical prolapse stage 2 or greater in 7/107 (6.5%) and 2/104 (1.9%; P = 0.10).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Median PFDI-20 symptom-distress scores were 16.7 and 25.0 (P = 0.03). No discomfort or pain on the EQ-5D-5L was reported by 81/105 (77.1%) and 63/104 (60.6%; OR 2.2, 95% CI 1.2–4.0; P = 0.04). Vaginal pH of 6 or lower was recorded in 81/93 (87.1%) and 63/87 (72.4%; P = 0.01); signs of vaginal atrophy were recorded in 32/101 (31.7%) and 42/94 (44.7%; P = 0.06). Additional treatment for recurrent prolapse before 12 months occurred in 2/110 (1.8%) and 4/107 (3.7%; P = 0.39). Sexual-function scores did not differ significantly.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The assigned cream contained oestriol 1 mg/g or an identical placebo vehicle. Participants applied 0.5 g (0.5 mg oestriol in the active arm) intravaginally each day for the first 2 weeks, then twice weekly. Treatment began 4–6 weeks before surgery and continued until 12 months afterward, with a planned pause for the first 2 postoperative weeks. All operations included anterior or posterior colporrhaphy; 48% also included apical suspension by sacrospinous fixation or Manchester-Fothergill operation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Of 311 randomized women, 293 underwent surgery and started the assigned intervention. At 12 months, 236 completed follow-up, and 210 supplied the primary PGI-I response. The per-protocol analysis included 185 participants (94 oestriol, 91 placebo) who reported never, rarely or occasionally missing cream at 6 months; when questionnaire data were missing, tube weight had to indicate at least 6 months of use. Its results were consistent with the main analysis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Surgery-related complications and adverse events did not differ significantly between groups.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Fifty-seven of the operated participants dropped out; primary-endpoint responses were available for 210 of the 311 randomized women. Attrition exceeded the 10% allowance in the sample-size plan, and the authors attributed part of it to pandemic-related postponements and missed follow-up. Treatment and assessment ended at 12 months, before longer-term prolapse recurrence could be assessed.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="eva" />

EVA required colporrhaphy and used oestriol, with PGI-I improvement as its primary endpoint. IMPROVE studied conjugated estrogen around native-tissue vaginal apical repair and made composite surgical failure its primary endpoint. EVA found a PGI-I difference and no composite success difference; IMPROVE found neither.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
