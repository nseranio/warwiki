# Raz–Stamm estriol: Intravaginal Estriol for Recurrent Urinary Tract Infection

<TrialBottomLine id="raz-stamm-estriol" />

The Raz–Stamm single-center, double-blind randomized trial compared intravaginal estriol cream with placebo in 93 postmenopausal women with recurrent culture-confirmed urinary tract infection and found 0.5 versus 5.9 infections per patient-year during eight months of follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="raz-stamm-estriol" />

## Design

Women referred to an infectious disease clinic in Afula, Israel, had experienced at least three microbiologically confirmed symptomatic infections in the preceding year. Enrollment required a negative urine culture. Exclusions included an anatomical urogenital lesion, indwelling catheter, ongoing oral estrogen, and long-term antimicrobial prophylaxis. The investigators assigned 50 women to estriol and 43 to matched placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The study obtained midstream urine cultures monthly and when symptoms occurred. Symptomatic infection required urinary symptoms, pyuria and a midstream culture with at least 10⁵ CFU/mL; a catheterized specimen was obtained when culture was positive without pyuria. Asymptomatic bacteriuria was observed without antibiotics unless symptoms developed. The report compared annualized infection rates with a Wilcoxon test and infection-free survival with a log-rank test. It did not name a formal primary endpoint or specify an alpha threshold.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The annualized median infection rate favored estriol (P &lt; 0.001). At four months, the Kaplan–Meier estimate of remaining infection-free was 95% (95% CI 88–100%) with estriol and 30% (95% CI 16–46%) with placebo; the full survival curves differed (P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Among women cultured at both later visits, lactobacilli were present after one month in 22/36 (61%) estriol recipients and 0/24 (0%) placebo recipients (P &lt; 0.001). Enterobacteriaceae were present in 11/36 (31%) and 15/24 (63%), respectively (P &lt; 0.005), compared with 24/36 (67%) and 16/24 (67%) before treatment. These flora findings were secondary to the clinical infection comparison.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Estriol was applied intravaginally at 0.5 mg nightly for two weeks, then twice weekly for eight months; placebo followed the same schedule. Participants used a vaginal applicator and kept diaries reviewed at monthly visits. Symptomatic infections were treated with a three-day antimicrobial course. The arms contributed 310 and 225 person-months of observation. Eight-month completion was 36/50 (72%) and 24/43 (56%); recurrent infection prompted systemic antimicrobial prophylaxis and study withdrawal in 0/50 (0%) and 10/43 (23%), respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Local vaginal irritation, burning or itching led to discontinuation in 10/50 (20%) estriol recipients and 4/43 (9%) placebo recipients. The report described these reactions as mild and self-limited and observed no systemic adverse reactions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The short observation period and single-center referral population limit estimates of longer-term infection and adherence. The analysis of vaginal flora used the 36 estriol and 24 placebo recipients who supplied both later cultures. Unequal withdrawal, especially recurrence prompting antibiotic prophylaxis in the placebo arm, complicates comparisons among participants who completed treatment. The trial excluded women with anatomic urogenital lesions, catheters and several other potential complicating factors.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

- **AUA/CUA/SUFU, amended 2025:** recommends vaginal estrogen for peri- and postmenopausal women with recurrent UTI when there is no contraindication <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **AUA/SUFU/AUGS GSM guideline, 2025:** recommends local low-dose vaginal estrogen for patients with GSM and recurrent UTI to reduce future infection risk <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
