# SISTEr: Stress Incontinence Surgical Treatment Efficacy Trial

<TrialBottomLine id="sister" />

**SISTEr** was a nine-center, open-label randomized superiority trial in 655 women with stress-predominant urinary incontinence and urethral hypermobility that compared an autologous rectus fascial pubovaginal sling with Burch colposuspension. At 24 months, the sling improved both overall composite success (47% versus 38%; P = 0.01) and stress-specific success (66% versus 49%; P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="sister" />

## Design

Women had a positive standardized stress test and pure or stress-predominant symptoms. Prior incontinence surgery was allowed. Randomization occurred in the operating room after induction of anesthesia, stratified by clinical center. Participating surgeons used standardized operative elements, including Burch suture type and placement, fascial sling dimensions, antibiotic prophylaxis and cystoscopic bladder assessment. Planned concomitant prolapse operations and hysterectomy were declared before randomization.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Overall success required no self-reported incontinence, pad gain &lt;15 g, no leakage in a three-day diary, a negative cough and Valsalva stress test, and no retreatment, including behavioral or drug treatment. Stress-specific success retained the symptom, stress-test and retreatment criteria but omitted the pad and diary components. Both were prespecified primary outcomes; the reported percentages are Kaplan–Meier estimates of cumulative success, with the treatment groups compared by a site-adjusted log-rank test.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At 24 months, 520/655 women had an assessed outcome or an earlier documented failure. The absolute difference in overall composite success was 9 percentage points in favor of sling (47% versus 38%; P = 0.01); for stress-specific success it was 17 points (66% versus 49%; P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In a secondary analysis of this cohort, preoperative urodynamic stress incontinence showed a nonsignificant association with overall success (odds ratio 2.26, 95% CI 0.99–5.17). Valsalva leak point pressure and detrusor overactivity did not predict either surgical success outcome in the women studied.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

E-SISTEr enrolled 482/655 original participants for observational follow-up. Under its narrower composite of no diary leakage, no reported stress symptoms and no surgical stress-incontinence retreatment, five-year cumulative continence was 30.8% after sling (95% CI 24.7–36.9%) and 24.1% after Burch (95% CI 18.5–29.7%; log-rank P = 0.002). Among five-year satisfaction respondents, 148/179 sling participants (83%) and 126/172 Burch participants (73%) were satisfied (P = 0.04).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The Burch operation suspended anterior vaginal tissue at the bladder neck and proximal urethra with permanent sutures fixed to the iliopectineal ligament. For the sling, surgeons harvested rectus fascia, placed the strip under the proximal urethra through a vaginal incision and brought its permanent suture ends retropubically to the anterior abdominal wall for fixation. The study standardized key dimensions and suture details across its centers.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

All 326 sling assignments and 328/329 Burch assignments received the assigned operation. Concomitant prolapse surgery was performed in 55% of sling and 48% of Burch patients. Mean operative time was 136 versus 138 minutes and mean estimated blood loss 229 versus 238 mL, respectively, including concomitant procedures when performed.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Any adverse event affected 206/326 sling patients (63%) and 156/329 Burch patients (47%), chiefly because urinary tract infection affected 157/326 (48%) versus 105/329 (32%). The trial accepted either culture confirmation or treatment based on clinical suspicion for cystitis. Serious events, defined as modified Dindo grade III–V, occurred in 42/326 (13%) and 32/329 (10%; P = 0.20). Incident cystotomy was reported twice after sling and ten times after Burch; ureteral injury occurred only in two Burch patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Protocol-defined voiding dysfunction—catheter use after six weeks or revision to improve emptying—occurred in 14% after sling and 2% after Burch. Nineteen sling recipients underwent 20 revision procedures for voiding symptoms or retention; no Burch recipient required one. Treated postoperative urge incontinence affected 87/326 (27%) and 65/329 (20%). The excess was mainly persistent urge incontinence (79/326 [24%] versus 59/329 [18%]); new treated cases occurred in 11/326 (3%) and 11/329 (3%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

During E-SISTEr follow-up, adverse events were reported in 22/243 sling participants (9%) and 23/239 Burch participants (10%).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

Patients and clinicians knew the assignment after surgery, and more than half the cohort had another operation. SISTEr enrolled women with urethral hypermobility and stress-predominant symptoms at tertiary centers; 520/655 had a known two-year outcome or earlier failure. Its stringent overall composite counted urgency leakage and any incontinence retreatment as failures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

E-SISTEr changed the composite definition and was an observational extension: participants who were incontinent at two years were more likely to enroll than those who were continent (85.5% versus 52.2%). Its five-year estimates therefore describe the extension cohort under its own endpoint.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

- **AUA/SUFU (Surgical Treatment of Female Stress Urinary Incontinence, 2017; amended 2023), statement 12.** Counsel index patients considering surgery about efficacy and safety of their options, including autologous fascia pubovaginal sling and Burch colposuspension <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade A)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **AUA/SUFU (2017; amended 2023), statement 19.** In patients having concomitant prolapse repair and SUI surgery, clinicians may perform a pubovaginal sling or Burch colposuspension, among other continence procedures <Rec>(Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
