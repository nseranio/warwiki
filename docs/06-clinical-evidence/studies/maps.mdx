# MAPS: Pelvic Floor Training for Incontinence After Prostate Surgery

<TrialBottomLine id="maps" />

**MAPS** comprised two parallel UK randomized trials in 853 men with urinary incontinence six weeks after radical prostatectomy or TURP, comparing therapist-led pelvic floor muscle training (PFMT) with indicated bladder training against standard care; neither trial found a lower rate of self-reported incontinence at 12 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="maps" />

## Design

Men were recruited through 34 UK centres and randomized within separate radical-prostatectomy and TURP trials. Allocation used a remote computer system and was minimized by centre, age and incontinence before surgery. Men and therapists knew the assignment; clerical staff entering the participant questionnaires were blinded. Men already referred for formal PFMT, those scheduled for early radiotherapy and those undergoing channel TURP for advanced cancer were excluded.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Both groups received a lifestyle advice leaflet. Controls could obtain routine NHS care, including non-specialist pelvic floor exercise advice and locally available leaflets. The intervention added a standardized therapist program delivered by a specialist continence physiotherapist or nurse. The primary clinical endpoint was any leakage reported on either the frequency or amount question of the ICIQ-UI Short Form at 12 months. Each trial was powered around a prespecified 15-percentage-point reduction in persistent incontinence. The primary economic measure was incremental cost per quality-adjusted life-year (QALY).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

After radical prostatectomy, 148/196 men (75.5%) in the intervention group and 151/195 (77.4%) in the control group reported incontinence at 12 months: risk difference −1.9 percentage points (95% CI −10 to 6). After TURP, the corresponding figures were 126/194 (64.9%) and 125/203 (61.6%): risk difference 3.4 points (95% CI −6 to 13). Neither trial met its prespecified continence target.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Pad use at 12 months was 63/196 (32%) versus 68/195 (35%) after radical prostatectomy and 24/194 (12%) versus 24/203 (12%) after TURP. Therapist assignment increased self-reported PFMT practice: 128/191 (67%) versus 95/189 (50%) after radical prostatectomy (adjusted RR 1.30, 95% CI 1.09–1.53) and 122/188 (65%) versus 39/193 (20%) after TURP (adjusted RR 3.20, 95% CI 2.37–4.32). Mean NHS costs were £181 higher per patient after radical prostatectomy and £209 higher after TURP, without a QALY advantage in either trial.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The radical-prostatectomy cohort included abdominal, perineal and laparoscopic approaches, with no robotic procedures; the TURP cohort included standard and laser resections, with no HoLEP. These were the index operations, performed before randomization.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The therapist program began with a 60-minute assessment, then three approximately 45-minute visits at about 2, 6 and 12 weeks. Digital anal examination supplied verbal feedback on contraction strength. The home program prescribed three maximum-strength contractions separated by 10-second breaks in lying, sitting and standing positions, twice daily, plus sustained contraction while walking and before activities that provoked leakage. Men with urgency received bladder training and urge-suppression instruction.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

At least one therapist visit was attended by 189/205 men (92%) after radical prostatectomy and 189/220 (86%) after TURP; 175/205 (85%) and 158/220 (72%), respectively, attended the final visit.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

No adverse effects were reported by participants in either trial.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The primary endpoint was participant-reported leakage, without objective pad-weight testing. Participants and therapists were unmasked, and pelvic floor advice remained available in standard care. Randomization occurred after incontinence was identified at six weeks, so the intervention assessed treatment of established postoperative leakage. The economic analysis used UK NHS costs.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU's 2026 male LUTS guideline cites MAPS in its pelvic floor training discussion and recommends offering PFMT alone or with biofeedback and/or electrical stimulation to men undergoing radical prostatectomy to speed recovery from incontinence <Rec>(Weak)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
