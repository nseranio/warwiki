# CONFIDeNT: Percutaneous Tibial Nerve Stimulation Versus Sham for Faecal Incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="confident" />

**CONFIDeNT** was a multicenter, masked randomized trial in adults with faecal incontinence despite conservative treatment that compared percutaneous tibial nerve stimulation (PTNS) with sham electrical stimulation; PTNS did not significantly increase the proportion with at least 50% fewer weekly incontinence episodes after the treatment course.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="confident" />

## Design

The trial randomized 227 adults in 17 UK specialist hospital units. Randomization was stratified by sex and, for women, center. Patients and outcome assessors were masked through the 14-week trial; staff delivering treatment were unmasked. The primary endpoint was at least a 50% reduction in weekly faecal incontinence episodes recorded in bowel diaries after 12 sessions. The analysis used intention to treat with multiple imputation for missing data.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

PTNS did not significantly increase diary-defined response after the treatment course: 39/103 (38%) PTNS and 32/102 (31%) sham patients with complete bowel diaries met the primary response definition, and the imputed intention-to-treat comparison gave an adjusted OR of 1.28 (95% CI 0.72–2.28; P = 0.396).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The per-protocol analysis of 197 patients also found no significant responder difference (OR 1.27, 95% CI 0.67–2.34; P = 0.45).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Two weeks after the 12-week course, mean weekly episode counts were 6.4 with PTNS (n=105) and 9.1 with sham (n=104); the adjusted intention-to-treat difference in change was −2.262 episodes per week (95% CI −4.185 to −0.339; P = 0.021).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The adjusted difference for urge episodes was −1.456 per week (95% CI −2.693 to −0.219; P = 0.021), while passive episodes did not differ (P = 0.228).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> St Mark's Continence Score did not differ (adjusted difference −0.047, 95% CI −1.033 to 0.939; P = 0.925), nor did quality-of-life measures.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The patient-centred outcomes score favored PTNS (mean 7.8, n=87, versus 8.4, n=85; adjusted difference −0.545, 95% CI −1.081 to −0.008; P = 0.047).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Obstructed-defecation symptoms were associated with a lower PTNS response (adjusted within-arm OR 0.38, 95% CI 0.16–0.91; P = 0.029) and a higher sham response (adjusted within-arm OR 3.45, 95% CI 1.31–9.11; P = 0.012) in the post hoc analysis of 205 participants with complete outcome data. The symptoms were difficulty evacuating, excessive straining, regular digitation, or incomplete emptying; in the individual-symptom analysis, sensation of blockage was associated with lower PTNS response (adjusted OR 0.15, 95% CI 0.03–0.75; P = 0.02). Among the 93 participants without these symptoms, 24/49 (49%) assigned PTNS and 8/44 (18%) assigned sham responded (adjusted OR 4.71, 95% CI 1.71–12.93; P = 0.003).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Specialist staff delivered Urgent PC stimulation for 30 minutes weekly over 12 treatments, placing a 34-gauge needle about 2 cm deep, 5 cm above the medial malleolus and 2 cm behind the tibia. PTNS practitioners completed at least three mentored 12-session courses before trial certification.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The sham inserted the needle 2 mm at the same site and delivered stimulation to the lateral forefoot at 10 Hz with a 200-microsecond pulse width.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Treatment was completed by 108/115 PTNS and 107/112 sham participants; 197/227 met the per-protocol requirement of at least 10 treatments within 13 weeks, spaced 5–10 days apart.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Seven of 115 PTNS patients and five of 112 sham patients discontinued treatment; five of these 12 discontinuations were due to weekly visit commitments.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Seven mild treatment-related events were reported in each arm (7 events/115 PTNS participants; 7 events/112 sham participants), including needle-site pain in four PTNS and three sham patients. Bruising at the needle site occurred in two PTNS and one sham participant; needle-site bleeding occurred in two sham participants.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> No serious treatment-related adverse events were reported. One patient in each arm withdrew after an adverse event unrelated to treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Four serious adverse events occurred (2 events/115 PTNS participants; 2 events/112 sham participants); all were unrelated to treatment and resolved.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The randomized assessment ended after the 12-session course, leaving durability and maintenance treatment unmeasured. The responder proportions use complete diaries, while the adjusted comparison imputes missing data. The later obstructive-defecation finding arose from a post hoc subgroup analysis; obstructed defecation was classified by structured symptom interview rather than a score or physiological test.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
