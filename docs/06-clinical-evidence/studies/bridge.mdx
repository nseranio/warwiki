# BRIDGE: Perioperative Bridging Anticoagulation in Patients with Atrial Fibrillation

<TrialBottomLine id="bridge" />

**BRIDGE** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized trial in warfarin-treated adults with atrial fibrillation or flutter who required elective surgery or an invasive procedure; placebo without bridging was noninferior to perioperative dalteparin for arterial thromboembolism and reduced major bleeding at 30 days.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="bridge" />

## Design

The trial randomized 1,884 patients at 108 sites in the United States and Canada: 950 to matching placebo and 934 to dalteparin. All had received warfarin for at least 3 months, had a target INR of 2.0–3.0, and had at least one CHADS2 stroke-risk factor. Mechanical heart valves, stroke or transient ischemic attack within 12 weeks, major bleeding within 6 weeks, creatinine clearance &lt;30 mL/min, and planned cardiac, intracranial or intraspinal surgery were exclusions. The mean CHADS2 score was 2.3.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The primary efficacy endpoint was 30-day arterial thromboembolism: stroke, transient ischemic attack or systemic embolism. The primary safety endpoint was 30-day major bleeding. Outcomes were blindly adjudicated. The prespecified noninferiority test required the upper 95% confidence bound for the arterial-event risk difference (no bridging minus bridging) to be below 1.0 percentage point; major bleeding was tested for superiority.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Among 1,813 patients with outcome data, arterial thromboembolism occurred in 4/918 (0.4%) without bridging and 3/895 (0.3%) with bridging. The risk difference was 0.1 percentage points (95% CI −0.6 to 0.8; P = 0.01 for noninferiority), meeting the prespecified margin. Major bleeding occurred in 12/918 (1.3%) and 29/895 (3.2%), respectively (RR 0.41, 95% CI 0.20–0.78; P = 0.005 for superiority).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Warfarin was stopped 5 days before the procedure and restarted the evening of or day after it. Dalteparin 100 IU/kg subcutaneously twice daily or matching placebo began 3 days before the procedure, with the last preoperative dose given about 24 hours before it. Study drug resumed 12–24 hours after a low-bleeding-risk procedure or 48–72 hours after a high-bleeding-risk procedure and continued until the INR reached at least 2. Investigators made the final bleeding-risk classification and managed antiplatelet therapy at their discretion.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Of 1,884 randomized patients, 1,722 underwent the anticipated procedure. Gastrointestinal procedures accounted for 44.0%, cardiothoracic procedures for 17.2%, and orthopedic procedures for 9.2%. The prespecified classification designated 89.4% as minor. Complete adherence to protocol-specified study drug doses was 86.5% before and 96.5% after the procedure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Minor bleeding occurred in 110/918 (12.0%) without bridging and 187/895 (20.9%) with bridging (P &lt; 0.001). No major bleed was fatal. Myocardial infarction, deep-vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism and death did not differ significantly between groups.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Few participants had CHADS2 scores of 5 or 6, and the study included few major operations with high thrombotic and bleeding risk. The noninferiority margin of 1.0 percentage point was large relative to the observed 0.4% arterial-event rate. The sample-size target was reduced during recruitment after interim assessment showed fewer events than expected. Outcome data were unavailable for 71 randomized patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The American College of Chest Physicians 2022 perioperative antithrombotic guideline cites BRIDGE in its evidence review and recommends against heparin bridging when a patient receiving a vitamin K antagonist for atrial fibrillation requires interruption for elective surgery or a procedure <Rec>(Strong Recommendation, Moderate Certainty of Evidence)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
