# REACTT: Tadalafil Once Daily or On Demand After Bilateral Nerve-Sparing Radical Prostatectomy

<TrialBottomLine id="reactt" />

**REACTT** was a multicenter, double-blind, double-dummy, placebo-controlled randomized trial of tadalafil 5 mg daily or 20 mg on demand versus placebo for 9 months after bilateral nerve-sparing radical prostatectomy in 423 men aged 68 or younger with normal preoperative erectile function. After a 6-week washout, an IIEF-EF score of 22 or higher occurred in 20.9%, 16.9% and 19.1% of the three arms, so neither tadalafil regimen met the primary endpoint of improved unassisted erectile function, although daily tadalafil improved drug-assisted function and reduced penile length loss.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="reactt" />

## Design

Men were enrolled between November 2009 and August 2011 at 50 centers in nine European countries and Canada. Eligible men had organ-confined, non-metastatic prostate cancer (Gleason score 7 or lower, PSA below 10 ng/mL) and a preoperative IIEF-EF score of at least 22, measured after the cancer diagnosis and within 6 weeks before surgery. After surgery they had to have erectile dysfunction, defined by a patient-reported residual erection function score of 3 or less; at randomization 83.9% had severe dysfunction (IIEF-EF 0–10).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The registry record also excluded men with diabetes, previous PDE5 inhibitor use, radiation or hormonal therapy for prostate cancer, or previous prostatic surgery. Of 583 men screened, 423 were randomized.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Treatment began within 6 weeks after surgery with matching tablets: tadalafil 5 mg daily plus placebo on demand, tadalafil 20 mg on demand plus placebo daily, or placebo for both.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> On-demand dosing allowed up to 3 tablets a week and no more than 1 a day.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Nine months of double-blind treatment were followed by a 6-week drug-free washout and then 3 months of open-label tadalafil 5 mg daily in all patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The primary endpoint was the proportion of men with an IIEF-EF score of 22 or higher after the washout, a measure of unassisted function. Secondary measures included IIEF-EF, Sexual Encounter Profile question 3 (SEP-3) and penile length.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The prespecified comparisons were each tadalafil arm against placebo, the planned sample size was 412, and the analysis population excluded one on-demand patient with no post-baseline data.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

After the washout, 20.9% of men previously assigned to daily tadalafil, 16.9% previously assigned to tadalafil on demand and 19.1% previously assigned to placebo had an IIEF-EF score of 22 or higher (odds ratio 1.1, 95% CI 0.6–2.1, P = 0.675 for daily tadalafil; 0.9, 95% CI 0.5–1.7, P = 0.704 for on demand).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The analysis populations were 139, 142 and 141 men. The primary endpoint was not met for either regimen.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

At the end of the 9 months of treatment, while men were still taking the drug, 25.2%, 19.7% and 14.2% had an IIEF-EF score of 22 or higher (odds ratio 2.15, 95% CI 1.16–3.99, P = 0.016 for daily tadalafil versus placebo; on demand versus placebo 1.50, 95% CI 0.79–2.85, P = 0.210).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The mean IIEF-EF improvement exceeded placebo by 2.80 points with daily tadalafil (95% CI 0.76–4.83; P = 0.007) and by 1.59 points with on-demand tadalafil (95% CI −0.41 to 3.60; P = 0.118).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Improvement exceeded the 4-point minimally important difference in both tadalafil arms, and SEP-3 improved significantly over placebo with daily tadalafil only (P = 0.019).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Daily tadalafil reduced the loss of stretched penile length by a least-squares mean of 4.1 mm versus placebo at 9 months (95% CI 0.4–7.8 mm; P = 0.032); on-demand tadalafil had no significant effect. At the end of double-blind treatment, 34.2% of men on daily tadalafil, 50.0% on tadalafil on demand and 56.5% on placebo reported no morning erections; the distribution of Standardized Morning Erection Question responses across arms differed (P = 0.045).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

A prespecified secondary analysis measured time to recovery (IIEF-EF of 22 or higher) during double-blind treatment. Recovery occurred at some point in 29.5% of men on daily tadalafil, 23.9% on tadalafil on demand and 18.4% on placebo; fewer than half recovered in any arm, so the median time could not be estimated. A quarter of men reached recovery by 5.8, 9.0 and 9.3 months, respectively. The hazard ratio against placebo was 1.90 for daily tadalafil (95% CI 1.16–3.12; P = 0.011) and 1.47 for tadalafil on demand (95% CI 0.88–2.47; P = 0.140).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

After 3 months of open-label daily tadalafil in all arms, 32.4%, 33.1% and 27.0% had an IIEF-EF score of 22 or higher, without a significant difference between arms (odds ratio 1.34, 95% CI 0.79–2.28, for the daily arm versus placebo).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> In a post hoc analysis using the stricter standard of returning to the preoperative IIEF-EF category, 22.3% of the daily arm, 11.3% of the on-demand arm and 7.8% of the placebo arm had done so at the end of double-blind treatment.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> The difference did not persist after the washout.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

On the patient-rated EPIC-26 sexual domain, daily tadalafil improved scores by 9.6 points more than placebo at the end of double-blind treatment (95% CI 3.1–16.0; P = 0.004); on-demand tadalafil did not differ significantly, and the daily-arm difference was 3.2 points (95% CI −4.3 to 10.7) after open-label treatment. Treatment satisfaction (EDITS total score, 0–4 scale) was higher than with placebo by 0.33 points for daily tadalafil (95% CI 0.10–0.56; P = 0.005) and 0.23 for on demand (95% CI 0.01–0.45; P = 0.041). In an exploratory age-stratified analysis, EPIC-26 urinary incontinence scores improved with daily tadalafil only in men aged 61 to 68, with an overall treatment effect of 8.3 points (95% CI 0.4–16.1; P = 0.040).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

Among 332 men with complete data, a post hoc decision-tree analysis found higher presurgery sexual desire associated with higher IIEF-EF at the end of double-blind treatment (mean 14.9 with an IIEF item 12 score of 3.5 or higher versus 11.1 below 3.5). In the 198-man branch with high presurgery desire and confidence, mean IIEF-EF at the end of double-blind treatment was 17.6 with daily tadalafil (n = 67) and 14.3 with on-demand tadalafil or placebo pooled (n = 131). After the washout, postsurgery treatment group had no relevant effect in the model.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Surgery was not randomized. Of 422 treated men, 189 had open surgery, 115 robot-assisted laparoscopy, 88 conventional laparoscopy and 30 another approach. In a model adjusted for treatment, country, age group, nerve-sparing score and approach, the odds of an IIEF-EF score of 22 or higher after the washout were about twice as high after robot-assisted laparoscopy as after open surgery (odds ratio 2.42, 95% CI 1.24–4.72; P = 0.029), and time to recovery during double-blind treatment was shorter (hazard ratio 1.92, 95% CI 1.17–3.15; P = 0.010). Conventional laparoscopy showed no favorable effect compared with open surgery.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> The trial was not designed for these analyses.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

Surgeons graded nerve sparing on a 1 to 4 scale for each neurovascular bundle (1 complete preservation, 4 complete resection); a total of 2 was perfect, and men with a total score above 4 were excluded, so most enrolled men had a perfect score.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

In the on-demand arm, mean use was 1.5 tablets (SD 0.95) of 20 mg per week.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Completion of double-blind treatment was 114 of 139 men in the daily arm, 122 of 143 in the on-demand arm and 115 of 141 in the placebo arm.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Adverse events

The registry record reports serious adverse events during double-blind treatment and washout in 2 of 139 men on daily tadalafil, 3 of 143 on tadalafil on demand and 10 of 141 on placebo. Adverse events led to discontinuation of double-blind treatment in 10, 6 and 4 men, respectively, and 50, 52 and 40 men had at least one non-serious event. The most frequently listed non-serious events were headache (5, 11 and 6 men), nasopharyngitis (5, 5 and 3), back pain (5, 5 and 3), dyspepsia (5, 5 and 1) and myalgia (5, 5 and 1). Penile curvature was listed as a non-serious event in one man in each tadalafil arm and none on placebo.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

Fewer than half the men in any arm reached an IIEF-EF score of 22 or higher during treatment.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The washout lasted 6 weeks, and open-label daily tadalafil in all patients ended the placebo comparison at month 10.5.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The population was selected: men aged 68 or younger (mean age 57.9 years) with normal preoperative function, organ-confined cancer of Gleason score 7 or lower and no diabetes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Baseline IIEF-EF was assessed after the cancer diagnosis, when anxiety may affect the score, and the nerve-sparing score was a surgeon-rated measure.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> Surgeon volume and learning curve were not accounted for in the surgical-approach analyses.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

The quality-of-life analyses applied no multiplicity adjustment.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The decision-tree and return-to-baseline analyses were post hoc. Of the men who started double-blind treatment, 25 of 139 (daily), 21 of 143 (on demand) and 26 of 141 (placebo) did not complete it.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The trial was funded by Eli Lilly and Company.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="reactt" />

REACTT measured recovery of unassisted function after drug withdrawal as its primary endpoint, whereas its reports on penile length, time to recovery, EPIC-26 scores and satisfaction measure effects during treatment. An earlier nine-month randomized trial of vardenafil, started within 2 weeks after surgery, likewise improved drug-assisted function on demand without a significant effect on unassisted function after washout.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

- **AUA (Erectile Dysfunction guideline, 2018).** Men who desire preservation of erectile function after radical prostatectomy or radiotherapy should be told that early PDE5 inhibitor use may not improve spontaneous, unassisted erectile function <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C)</Rec>. The guideline's rationale cites REACTT among the randomized trials that did not show improved unassisted function with early PDE5 inhibitor use, and reports a pooled relative risk of 1.0 (95% CI 0.66–1.64) in its meta-analysis of three placebo-controlled trials in men after radical prostatectomy.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **EAU Prostate Cancer guideline (limited update, March 2026).** The text describes the 423-man trial and a 9.6-point greater EPIC sexual domain improvement with daily tadalafil (95% CI 3.1–16.0) at 9 months that vanished after the end of the study, and concludes that, based on discordant results, no clear recommendation is possible, although a trend exists for early use of PDE5 inhibitors after radical prostatectomy. The passage carries no strength rating.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
