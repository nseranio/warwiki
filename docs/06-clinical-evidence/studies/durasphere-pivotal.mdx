# Durasphere pivotal trial: Carbon-Coated Beads Versus Bovine Collagen for Stress Urinary Incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="durasphere-pivotal" />

**The Durasphere pivotal trial** randomized 355 women with stress urinary incontinence due to intrinsic sphincter deficiency to transurethral Durasphere carbon-coated zirconium-oxide beads or bovine collagen; the investigators reported similar continence-grade and pad-weight improvement among 12-month completers, with less material injected in the Durasphere arm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="durasphere-pivotal" />

## Design

The multicenter equivalence study assigned 178 women to Durasphere and 177 to collagen. An abdominal leak-point pressure below 90 cm H2O was required. Patients and research staff conducting evaluations were blinded; injectors knew the assigned material. The primary endpoints were Stamey continence-grade improvement, reduction in standardized one-hour pad-test weight, and safety. The protocol allowed up to five retreatments at intervals of at least seven days.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

Among 235 women completing 12-month follow-up after the first injection, 76/115 (66.1%) in the Durasphere arm and 79/120 (65.8%) in the collagen arm improved by at least one Stamey grade.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Mean pad-weight reduction was 27.9 g (n=113) and 26.4 g (n=117), respectively.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The investigators reported equivalence for both effectiveness outcomes; the article gives P=1.000 and P=0.835 for tests of difference.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

At the different timepoint of one year after the last treatment, at least one-grade improvement was reported for 49/61 (80.3%) Durasphere recipients and 47/68 (69.1%) collagen recipients (P=0.162).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Both agents were injected transurethrally under direct vision at the bladder neck. Durasphere used 1.0-mL prepackaged syringes and an 18-gauge needle. Mean initial injected volume was 4.83 mL with Durasphere and 6.23 mL with collagen (P&lt;0.001); mean total volume including repeat injections was 7.55 versus 9.58 mL (P&lt;0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Mean numbers of injections were 1.69 and 1.55, respectively (P=0.253); repeat injections were patient-driven.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The material studied was carbon-coated zirconium oxide in a beta-glucan carrier.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Among all 355 women followed for a mean 11 months, urgency lasting more than 24 hours, of any attribution, occurred in 44/178 Durasphere recipients (24.7%) versus 21/177 collagen recipients (11.9%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Acute retention lasting more than 24 hours and at most seven days occurred in 30/178 (16.9%) versus 6/177 (3.4%); the article reports P=0.001 for the adverse-event comparison.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Retention resolved in all affected women by day 7 after intermittent self-catheterization. The FDA review recommends confirming spontaneous voiding before discharge and managing retention with catheterization when needed.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The 12-month continence-grade analysis included 115/178 Durasphere and 120/177 collagen recipients; pad-weight groups were smaller.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Equivalence was reported without an equivalence confidence interval or prespecified decision threshold.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The treatment-anchored analysis includes 61 and 68 women.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The FDA approved the original zirconium-oxide Durasphere formulation on September 13, 1999, for adult women with stress urinary incontinence due to intrinsic sphincter deficiency.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
