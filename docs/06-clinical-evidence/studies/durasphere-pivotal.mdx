# Durasphere pivotal trial: Carbon-Coated Beads Versus Bovine Collagen for Stress Urinary Incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="durasphere-pivotal" />

**The Durasphere pivotal trial** randomized 355 women with stress urinary incontinence due to intrinsic sphincter deficiency to transurethral Durasphere carbon-coated zirconium-oxide beads or bovine collagen; the investigators reported comparable improvement in continence grade and pad weight at 12 months after the first injection, with less material injected in the Durasphere arm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="durasphere-pivotal" />

## Design

The multicenter equivalence study assigned 178 women to Durasphere and 177 to collagen. An abdominal leak-point pressure below 90 cm H2O was required. Patients and outcome evaluators were blinded; injectors knew the assigned material. The two primary effectiveness measures were change in Stamey continence grade and standardized pad-test weight at 12 months after initial treatment. The continence-grade calculation used a 15-percentage-point equivalence margin.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

At 12 months after the first injection, 76/115 women (66.1%) in the Durasphere arm and 79/120 (65.8%) in the collagen arm improved by at least one Stamey grade.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Mean pad-weight reduction was 27.9 g (n=113) and 26.4 g (n=117), respectively.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The investigators reported equivalence for both primary outcomes; the FDA tables give P=1.000 and P=0.835 for tests of difference.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

At the different timepoint of one year after the last treatment, at least one-grade improvement was reported for 49/61 (80.3%) Durasphere recipients and 47/68 (69.1%) collagen recipients (P=0.162).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Durasphere was injected through a cystoscope or endoscope under direct vision into the bladder-neck submucosa; the protocol allowed up to four retreatments after the initial treatment. Mean initial injected volume was 4.83 mL with Durasphere and 6.23 mL with collagen (P&lt;0.001).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The material studied was carbon-coated zirconium oxide in a beta-glucan carrier.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Urgency lasting more than 24 hours, of any attribution, occurred in 44/178 Durasphere recipients (24.7%) versus 11.9% of 177 collagen recipients (P=0.002). Acute retention lasting more than 24 hours and at most seven days occurred in 30/178 (16.9%) versus 3.4% of 177 (P&lt;0.001). The FDA review recommends confirming spontaneous voiding before discharge and managing retention with catheterization when needed.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The 12-month endpoint tables include 115/178 and 120/177 treated women for continence grade, and smaller groups for pad weight.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The treatment-anchored analysis includes 61 and 68 women.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The FDA approved the original zirconium-oxide Durasphere formulation on September 13, 1999, for adult women with stress urinary incontinence due to intrinsic sphincter deficiency.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
