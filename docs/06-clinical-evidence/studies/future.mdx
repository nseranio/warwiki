# FUTURE: Invasive Urodynamics Before Treatment for Refractory Overactive Bladder Symptoms

<TrialBottomLine id="future" />

**FUTURE** was a multicenter, open-label superiority randomized trial in women with refractory overactive bladder or urgency-predominant mixed urinary incontinence considering invasive treatment; adding invasive urodynamics to comprehensive clinical assessment did not improve final participant-reported success over assessment alone.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="future" />

## Design

Participants had not responded to conservative management and pharmacological treatment. Predominant stress incontinence, neurogenic bladder, bladder pain syndrome, recent urodynamics, previous botulinum toxin A or sacral neuromodulation for incontinence, and prolapse beyond the introitus were among the exclusions. The trial was conducted in UK secondary and tertiary hospitals. Neither participants nor clinicians were masked.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Both groups had a history, examination, bladder diary, post-void residual scan, and optional noninvasive uroflowmetry. The urodynamics group also underwent cystometry and uroflowmetry, with pressure-flow studies when indicated. Its treatment plan followed the urodynamic diagnosis; the comparison plan followed clinical assessment. An expert panel reviewed a sample of urodynamic traces and gave sites feedback.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The primary endpoint was the participant's Patient Global Impression of Improvement (PGI-I) response of “very much improved” or “much improved” at final follow-up. The planned assessment was at 15 months; women whose treatment was delayed during the COVID-19 pandemic could have a final assessment at 24 months. The trial was powered for superiority, with a prespecified minimum 10-percentage-point advantage in success. The primary analysis used the intention-to-treat population after postrandomization exclusions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The trial randomized 1,103 women; after four postrandomization exclusions, 550 assigned to urodynamics and 549 assigned to assessment alone entered the analysis. At final follow-up, PGI-I success occurred in 117/496 (23.6%) versus 114/503 (22.7%); adjusted odds ratio (OR) 1.12 (95% CI 0.73–1.74; P = 0.60). The superiority endpoint was not met.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

At 3 months, success occurred in 34/417 (8.2%) versus 77/433 (17.8%); adjusted OR 0.28 (95% CI 0.16–0.51; P &lt; 0.0001). In an exploratory subgroup of women receiving botulinum toxin A, success two months after injection was 88/138 (63.8%) versus 99/165 (60.0%); OR 1.17 (95% CI 0.73–1.89; P = 0.52). The trial's symptom and quality-of-life measures improved in both groups without a consistent between-group advantage at final follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The later detailed economic report estimated an additional NHS cost of £463 per participant and an incremental 0.011 quality-adjusted life-years with urodynamics, yielding £42,643 per quality-adjusted life-year gained. At a £20,000 willingness-to-pay threshold, the reported probability of cost-effectiveness was 34%. This report expanded the economic analysis of the same trial population rather than adding clinical follow-up.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Treatment was selected after the assigned diagnostic pathway. Any treatment was received by 467/550 (84.9%) in the urodynamics arm and 479/549 (87.2%) in the assessment-only arm. Among treated women, intradetrusor botulinum toxin A was used in 277/467 (59.3%) versus 343/479 (71.6%); stress incontinence surgery in 16/467 (3.4%) versus 5/479 (1.0%); and sacral neuromodulation in 11/467 (2.4%) versus 8/479 (1.7%). These treatment choices followed the diagnostic strategy; the study randomized assessment pathways, not operative techniques.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

At least one adverse event was reported by 113/550 versus 122/549 (22.2%). Urinary tract infection occurred in 39/550 (7.1%) versus 41/549 (7.5%); clean intermittent self-catheterization was required in 26/550 (4.7%) versus 32/549 (5.8%). These events were recorded across the subsequent treatment pathways, which differed between arms.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Treatment was open label, and the diagnostic strategy changed the distribution and timing of subsequent procedures. Most treated participants received botulinum toxin A, leaving small sacral neuromodulation groups. COVID-19 treatment delays prompted an additional follow-up assessment for some participants. The reported clinical follow-up ended at 15 or 24 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
