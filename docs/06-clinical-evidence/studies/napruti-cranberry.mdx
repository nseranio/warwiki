# NAPRUTI Cranberry: Cranberry Extract Versus Trimethoprim–Sulfamethoxazole for Recurrent UTI Prevention

<TrialBottomLine id="napruti-cranberry" />

**NAPRUTI Cranberry** was a multicenter, double-blind, double-dummy noninferiority trial of cranberry extract capsules versus nightly trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX) in premenopausal women with recurrent UTI. Cranberry failed the prespecified noninferiority test for symptomatic recurrences over 12 months; TMP-SMX increased resistance among indigenous *Escherichia coli*.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="napruti-cranberry" />

## Design

The trial randomized 221 community-dwelling premenopausal women aged at least 18 years who reported at least three symptomatic UTIs in the preceding year: 111 to cranberry and 110 to TMP-SMX. Recruitment spanned primary care and secondary or tertiary centers in the Netherlands. Women with active UTI, recent antibiotic or cranberry use, pregnancy, renal transplant, relevant drug interactions, oral anticoagulant use, or renal stones were excluded. Randomization was concealed and stratified by center and complicating host factors; identical placebos maintained masking.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The prespecified clinical outcomes included mean symptomatic recurrences, the proportion with a recurrence, and time to first recurrence. A clinical recurrence was defined by a woman's report of UTI symptoms, usually dysuria, frequency, or urgency. The resistance outcome measured TMP-SMX susceptibility in fecal and asymptomatic-bacteriuria *E. coli*. Microbiologically confirmed symptomatic UTI required symptoms plus bacteriuria. For mean clinical recurrences, noninferiority required the upper bound of the 95% confidence interval for cranberry minus TMP-SMX to be below 1.3 episodes per woman. Women using a catheter were excluded from clinical recurrence analyses.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The mean number of symptomatic episodes was 4.0 with cranberry and 1.8 with TMP-SMX. The cranberry-minus-TMP-SMX mean difference was 2.2 episodes (95% CI 0.3–4.2; P = 0.02); its upper limit exceeded the 1.3-episode noninferiority margin. These means were adjusted for selective dropout. Kaplan–Meier estimates for at least one symptomatic recurrence were 78.2% and 71.1%, respectively, and median time to first recurrence was 4 versus 8 months (log-rank P = 0.03).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

At one month, TMP-SMX resistance among fecal *E. coli* isolates was 23.7% with cranberry and 86.3% with TMP-SMX; corresponding asymptomatic urinary isolate rates were 28.1% and 90.5%. Resistance in the TMP-SMX group returned to baseline three months after stopping prophylaxis. The secondary mean number of microbiologically confirmed symptomatic episodes was 1.2 versus 0.8 (P = 0.15).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

A later economic analysis of this same cohort used multiple imputation and estimated 4.3 versus 2.7 symptomatic episodes per woman. Mean total cost was €627 with cranberry and €378 with TMP-SMX in 2009 euros, a mean difference of €249 (95% CI €70–€516). The economic calculation did not include downstream costs of antibiotic resistance.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Women received Cran-Max cranberry extract 500 mg twice daily plus a nightly placebo tablet, or TMP-SMX 480 mg nightly plus placebo capsules twice daily, for 12 months. The cranberry extract contained 9.1 mg/g of type A proanthocyanidins, measured by high-performance liquid chromatography. Urinary antibacterial activity was present in 632/722 samples (87.5%) collected during TMP-SMX prophylaxis; cranberry adherence was not directly measured. Median UTI-treatment antibiotic prescriptions during prophylaxis were 1 (IQR 0–2) with cranberry and 0.5 (IQR 0–2) with TMP-SMX.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Among 109 cranberry and 98 TMP-SMX safety recipients, any adverse event occurred in 53/109 (48.6%) and 51/98 (52.0%); adverse events led to withdrawal in 6/109 (5.5%) and 5/98 (5.1%). Serious adverse events occurred in 8/109 (7.3%) and 12/98 (12.2%). One TMP-SMX recipient had treatment-related Stevens–Johnson syndrome and withdrew.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The recurrence means required inverse-probability weighting for selective dropout, and women with more recurrences in the cranberry arm were more likely to withdraw. Clinical recurrence relied on reported symptoms rather than culture confirmation. Cranberry adherence was not directly measured, and the tested extract's proanthocyanidin content does not define an optimal cranberry dose. The economic analysis had incomplete monthly questionnaires and omitted the costs associated with resistance.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="napruti-cranberry" />

The linked NAPRUTI lactobacilli trial used oral probiotics versus TMP-SMX in postmenopausal women; this cranberry comparison enrolled premenopausal women. The trials shared the antibiotic comparator and noninferiority design.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA/CUA/SUFU recurrent UTI guideline, amended in 2025, cites this trial among its cranberry-versus-antibiotic evidence and recommends offering cranberry as a prophylaxis option for women with recurrent UTI <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
