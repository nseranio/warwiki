# Cruz NDO: OnabotulinumtoxinA for Urinary Incontinence Due to Neurogenic Detrusor Overactivity

<TrialBottomLine id="cruz-ndo" />

**Cruz NDO** was a multicenter, double-blind phase 3 randomized trial of intradetrusor onabotulinumtoxinA 200 U, 300 U or placebo in 275 patients with multiple sclerosis or spinal cord injury and incontinence from neurogenic detrusor overactivity; at week 6, weekly incontinence episodes fell by 21.8, 19.4 and 13.2, respectively (each active dose versus placebo, P &lt; 0.01).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="cruz-ndo" />

## Design

The 275 adults had MS (154) or SCI (121), at least 14 weekly incontinence episodes, and inadequate anticholinergic response or intolerance. Arms were 200 U (92), 300 U (91) and placebo (92). The week-6 primary endpoint used intent-to-treat analysis and two-sided alpha 0.025. Secondary endpoints were maximum cystometric capacity, detrusor pressure at first involuntary contraction and Incontinence Quality of Life (I-QOL) score.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Both doses met the primary comparison; benefit appeared by week 2. Complete continence at week 6 was 35/92 (38.0%) with 200 U, 36/91 (39.6%) with 300 U and 7/92 (7.6%) with placebo (P &lt; 0.001). Capacity rose by 157.0, 157.2 and 6.5 mL; among patients with an involuntary detrusor contraction at week 6, detrusor pressure at the first contraction changed by −28.5, −26.9 and +6.4 cm H2O, respectively (each active dose P &lt; 0.001 versus placebo). I-QOL also improved (P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Median time to patient request for retreatment was 42.1 weeks with either active dose versus 13.1 weeks with placebo (P &lt; 0.001). The primary report found no clinically relevant efficacy or duration difference between the 200-U and 300-U doses.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The open-label extension pooled 388 treated patients from both trials (mean follow-up 3.6 years). Its primary efficacy measure, week-6 change in daily incontinence from phase 3 baseline, ranged from −3.2 to −4.1 episodes across six 200-U cycles. Median time to retreatment request was 9.0 months in the 200-U-only cohort; I-QOL increased by 27.1 to 33.5 points against an 11-point minimally important difference.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Among 227 pooled four-year completers in a post-hoc analysis, mean week-6 reductions were 3.5, 3.5, 3.8 and 3.8 episodes per day in years 1 through 4; dose cohorts ranged from 3.4 to 3.9. Complete continence at week 6 ranged from 43.6% to 57.4% across cohorts and years; pooled I-QOL improved by 30.8 to 32.2 points.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Initial treatment used 30 cystoscopic 1-mL trigone-sparing detrusor injections; repeat treatment was allowed from week 12 when weekly incontinence reduction was less than 30%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Extension treatment used 30 cystoscopic 1-mL injections; a 2011 amendment changed subsequent 300-U treatments to 200 U.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Retreatment required patient request, at least 12 weeks since injection, and an incontinence episode within three days. Of 396 entrants, 240 (60.6%) were followed at least four years; 227/388 (58.5%) treated patients completed four years and received a mean 1.4 to 1.5 treatments yearly.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Among completers with completed cycles, median time to retreatment request was 9.2 months (n=112) with 200 U only and 9.0 months (n=95) with 300/200 U.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Adverse events

During the first 12 weeks, urinary tract infection occurred in 20/90 (22.2%) placebo patients, 25/91 (27.5%) with 200 U and 34/89 (38.2%) with 300 U; urinary retention occurred in 3/90 (3.3%), 18/91 (19.8%) and 28/89 (31.5%), respectively. Among baseline nonusers of clean intermittent catheterization (CIC), 5/41 (12.2%), 13/44 (29.5%) and 19/45 (42.2%) started CIC, respectively; infection included asymptomatic positive cultures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the pooled extension, 26/88 (29.5%) prior nonusers started CIC after their first 200-U treatment, versus 43.0% after first 300-U treatment; the 200-U rate fell to 2/58 (3.4%) after cycle 2 and 3/50 (6.0%) after cycle 3. Within 12 weeks of the first onabotulinumtoxinA treatment, urinary tract infection occurred in 56/203 (27.6%) of the 200-U group and 57/185 (30.8%) of the 300-U group; urinary retention occurred in 41/203 (20.2%) and 42/185 (22.7%). These extension groups combine patients from both phase 3 trials.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Among four-year completers, 37/85 (43.5%) who had never used CIC started it in year 1, versus 0/36 in year 4. Within 12 weeks of treatments in years 1 and 4, there were 73 UTI events per 340 treatments (21.5%) and 43 per 228 (18.9%), and 45 urinary-retention events per 340 treatments (13.2%) and 7 per 228 (3.1%), respectively; the study's UTI definition did not require symptoms.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

The randomized comparator was placebo, with the primary endpoint measured at week 6. The extension was open-label, permitted anticholinergic changes, and enrolled completers of either phase 3 trial. The 300-U cohort switched to 200 U after the protocol amendment.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The four-year analysis selected patients who remained in the study; 227/388 (58.5%) treated patients completed it.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Time to retreatment request measured patient-reported need under eligibility rules, not the interval between injections.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="cruz-ndo" />

The independent Ginsberg phase 3 trial used the same two onabotulinumtoxinA doses and placebo in another multiple sclerosis and spinal cord injury cohort. Both trials contributed patients to the pooled extension and four-year completer analysis.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

- **AUA/SUFU (2021).** For neurogenic lower urinary tract dysfunction after spinal cord injury or multiple sclerosis refractory to oral medications, clinicians should recommend onabotulinumtoxinA to improve storage, incontinence and quality of life <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade A)</Rec>. The guideline lists Cruz 2011 among the supporting randomized trials. It also requires discussion of urinary retention and possible intermittent catheterization with patients who void spontaneously <Rec>(Clinical Principle)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **EAU Neuro-Urology (limited update, March 2026).** Use detrusor botulinum toxin A injections to reduce neurogenic detrusor overactivity when antimuscarinic therapy is ineffective <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **FDA (August 24, 2011).** OnabotulinumtoxinA was approved for adults with incontinence due to detrusor overactivity associated with a neurologic condition, such as spinal cord injury or multiple sclerosis, after an inadequate response to or intolerance of an anticholinergic medication.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
