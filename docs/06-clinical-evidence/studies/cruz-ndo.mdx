# Cruz NDO: OnabotulinumtoxinA for Urinary Incontinence Due to Neurogenic Detrusor Overactivity

<TrialBottomLine id="cruz-ndo" />

**Cruz NDO** was a multicenter, double-blind phase 3 randomized trial of intradetrusor onabotulinumtoxinA 200 U, 300 U or placebo in 275 patients with multiple sclerosis or spinal cord injury and incontinence from neurogenic detrusor overactivity; at week 6, weekly incontinence episodes fell by 21.8, 19.4 and 13.2, respectively (each active dose versus placebo, P &lt; 0.01).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="cruz-ndo" />

## Design

Patients had at least 14 urinary incontinence episodes per week; 154 had multiple sclerosis and 121 had spinal cord injury. The 200-U, 300-U and placebo groups enrolled 92, 91 and 92 patients. Change from baseline in weekly incontinence episodes at week 6 was the primary endpoint. The secondary measures were maximum cystometric capacity, maximum detrusor pressure at the first involuntary contraction and Incontinence Quality of Life (I-QOL) total score.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The trial used 30 intradetrusor injections and spared the trigone.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Both active doses met the primary superiority comparison with placebo at week 6. An active-treatment benefit was present by the first posttreatment visit at week 2. At week 6, both doses improved maximum cystometric capacity, detrusor pressure at the first involuntary contraction and I-QOL more than placebo (P &lt; 0.001 for each outcome).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Median time to patient request for retreatment was 42.1 weeks with either active dose versus 13.1 weeks with placebo (P &lt; 0.001). The primary report found no clinically relevant efficacy or duration difference between the 200-U and 300-U doses.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The final open-label extension pooled completers of this trial and its Ginsberg replicate. Among 388 patients who received onabotulinumtoxinA, mean follow-up was 3.6 years. Across six 200-U treatment cycles, mean incontinence fell by 3.2 to 4.1 episodes per day at week 6 after treatment; the overall median time to retreatment request in the 200-U-only cohort was 9.0 months. I-QOL increased by 27.1 to 33.5 points, against an 11-point minimally important difference.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

In a post-hoc analysis of 227 pooled patients who completed four years, the mean week-6 reduction across dose cohorts ranged from 3.4 to 3.9 episodes per day by treatment year. Complete continence at week 6 ranged from 43.6% to 57.4% across the dose cohorts and years.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The initial treatment was delivered as trigone-sparing detrusor injections. In the extension, treatment was cystoscopic with 30 injections of 1 mL each.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The extension initially continued the assigned 200-U or 300-U dose, then switched all participants to 200 U after a 2011 protocol amendment.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Repeat injection required a patient request, at least 12 weeks since the prior injection, and at least one incontinence episode within three days. Of 396 extension entrants, 240 (60.6%) were followed for at least four years; four-year completers received a mean 1.4 to 1.5 treatments per year.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Adverse events

Urinary tract infection and urinary retention were the most frequent adverse events in the randomized trial. Among patients who were not using clean intermittent catheterization (CIC) at baseline, 12% on placebo, 30% on 200 U and 42% on 300 U started CIC after treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the pooled extension, 26/88 (29.5%) prior nonusers started CIC after their first 200-U treatment. Within 12 weeks of the first onabotulinumtoxinA treatment, urinary tract infection occurred in 56/203 (27.6%) of the 200-U group and 57/185 (30.8%) of the 300-U group; urinary retention occurred in 41/203 (20.2%) and 42/185 (22.7%). These extension groups combine patients from both phase 3 trials.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The randomized comparator was placebo, with the primary endpoint measured at week 6. The extension was open-label, permitted anticholinergic changes, and enrolled completers of either phase 3 trial. Its 200-U and 300-U results incorporate crossover to 200 U after the protocol amendment.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The four-year analysis selected patients who remained in the study; 227/388 (58.5%) treated patients completed it.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Time to retreatment request measured patient-reported need under eligibility rules, not the interval between injections.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="cruz-ndo" />

The independent Ginsberg phase 3 trial used the same two onabotulinumtoxinA doses and placebo in another multiple sclerosis and spinal cord injury cohort. Both trials contributed patients to the pooled extension and four-year completer analysis.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

- **AUA/SUFU (2021).** For neurogenic lower urinary tract dysfunction after spinal cord injury or multiple sclerosis refractory to oral medications, clinicians should recommend onabotulinumtoxinA to improve storage, incontinence and quality of life <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade A)</Rec>. The guideline lists Cruz 2011 among the supporting randomized trials. It also requires discussion of urinary retention and possible intermittent catheterization with patients who void spontaneously <Rec>(Clinical Principle)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **EAU Neuro-Urology (limited update, March 2026).** Use detrusor botulinum toxin A injections to reduce neurogenic detrusor overactivity when antimuscarinic therapy is ineffective <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **FDA (August 24, 2011).** OnabotulinumtoxinA was approved for adults with incontinence due to detrusor overactivity associated with a neurologic condition, such as spinal cord injury or multiple sclerosis, after an inadequate response to or intolerance of an anticholinergic medication.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
