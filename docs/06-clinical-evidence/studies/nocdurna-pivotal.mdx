# NOCDURNA pivotal program: Low-Dose Desmopressin ODT for Nocturia in Men and Women

<TrialBottomLine id="nocdurna-pivotal" />

The **NOCDURNA pivotal program** comprised separate multicenter, double-blind randomized trials of desmopressin orally disintegrating tablets (ODT) versus placebo in men and women with at least two nocturnal voids; the tested doses reduced nocturnal voids and increased the odds of a one-third response during three months of treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="nocdurna-pivotal" />

## Design

Weiss and colleagues assigned men to 50 μg, 75 μg, or placebo; Sand and colleagues assigned women to 25 μg or placebo. The full analysis sets comprised 385 men and 261 women. Both studies prespecified change in mean nocturnal voids and the proportion achieving at least a 33% reduction as coprimary endpoints.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The men's study assessed both active doses separately against placebo; the women's study assessed one dose.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

Among men, the placebo-adjusted mean differences in nocturnal void change were −0.37 with 50 μg (P &lt; 0.0001) and −0.41 with 75 μg (P = 0.0003). The corresponding odds ratios for a 33% response were 1.98 (P = 0.0009) and 2.04 (P = 0.0004). Both coprimary comparisons met the superiority test at each dose. Time to first void increased by approximately 40 minutes relative to placebo with either dose.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Among women, the 25-μg dose produced a placebo-adjusted mean difference of −0.22 nocturnal voids (P = 0.028) and a 33% responder odds ratio of 1.85 (P = 0.006), meeting both coprimary tests. Time to first nocturnal void was 49 minutes longer than with placebo at three months (P = 0.003).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Serum sodium fell below 130 mmol/L in two men receiving 50 μg, aged 74 and 79 years, and nine men receiving 75 μg.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The women's report recorded three transient sodium decreases below 130 mmol/L without assigning them to a treatment arm; sodium remained above 125 mmol/L throughout that study.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

Efficacy and sodium safety were assessed over three months. Entry required nocturia frequency rather than confirmed nocturnal polyuria; the later US indication specified nocturnal polyuria.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

EAU's 2026 male LUTS guideline recommends offering desmopressin for nocturnal polyuria in men under 65 <Rec>(Weak)</Rec> and checking sodium at baseline, days three and seven, one month, and periodically thereafter <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> EAU's 2026 non-neurogenic female LUTS guideline recommends offering desmopressin for nocturnal polyuria after counselling about benefits and hyponatraemia <Rec>(Strong)</Rec> and careful sodium monitoring in older patients <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

The FDA approved NOCDURNA on June 21, 2018, for adults with nocturia due to nocturnal polyuria who awaken at least twice nightly to void.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
