# NOCDURNA pivotal program: Low-Dose Desmopressin ODT for Nocturia in Men and Women

<TrialBottomLine id="nocdurna-pivotal" />

The **NOCDURNA pivotal program** comprised separate multicenter, double-blind randomized trials of desmopressin orally disintegrating tablets (ODT) versus placebo in men and women with at least two nocturnal voids; the tested doses reduced nocturnal voids and increased the odds of a one-third response during three months of treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="nocdurna-pivotal" />

## Design

Both studies tested longitudinal change in mean nocturnal voids and 33% responder status as coprimary endpoints: Weiss and colleagues randomized 395 men to 50 μg, 75 μg, or placebo, and Sand and colleagues randomized 268 women to 25 μg or placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The full analysis sets included 385 men and 261 women; 84%–89% of men and 88%–89% of women in those sets had nocturnal polyuria at baseline.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In men, both endpoints had to meet a two-sided 5% superiority test at 75 μg before 50 μg was tested.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

Both doses met the men's coprimary superiority tests. Adjusted mean nocturnal void changes were −1.25 in 119 men at 50 μg, −1.29 in 124 at 75 μg, and −0.88 in 142 on placebo; placebo-adjusted differences were −0.37 (95% CI −0.57 to −0.17; P = 0.0003) and −0.41 (95% CI −0.61 to −0.22; P &lt; 0.0001), respectively. Adjusted 33% responder probabilities were 0.67 at either dose versus 0.50 on placebo, with odds ratios of 1.98 (95% CI 1.32–2.96; P = 0.0009) and 2.04 (P = 0.0004). At month 3, time to first void increased by 111.8 and 115.6 minutes versus 72.9 minutes on placebo, with placebo-adjusted differences of 39.0 minutes (95% CI 11.0–66.9; P = 0.006) and 42.8 minutes (95% CI 15.0–70.5; P = 0.003).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The 25-μg dose met both women's coprimary tests. Adjusted mean nocturnal void changes were −1.46 in 133 women on desmopressin versus −1.24 in 128 on placebo, a difference of −0.22 (95% CI −0.42 to −0.02; P = 0.028). Adjusted 33% responder probabilities were 0.76 versus 0.64, with an odds ratio of 1.85 (95% CI 1.19–2.86; P = 0.006). Time to first nocturnal void was 49 minutes longer than with placebo at three months (P = 0.003).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Treatment delivery

Both trials dosed the ODT one hour before bedtime and instructed participants to empty their bladder before bed and drink only to satisfy thirst. More than 80% of planned doses were taken by 98% of the men's full analysis set and 96% of randomized women; 327/395 men (83%) and 89% of women completed three months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Serum sodium fell below 130 mmol/L in 2/119 men (1.7%) receiving 50 μg, aged 74 and 79 years, and 9/122 (7.4%) receiving 75 μg, versus 0/143 on placebo. Sodium reached 125 mmol/L or less in 2/119 and 4/122 active-dose recipients, respectively. Any treatment-emergent adverse event occurred in 46/119 (38.7%), 49/122 (40.2%), and 58/143 (40.6%); serious adverse events occurred in 4/119 (3.4%), 5/122 (4.1%), and 1/143 (0.7%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In women, sodium fell below 130 mmol/L in 3/135 (2.2%) receiving 25 μg versus 0/126 on placebo; all three values were 126–129 mmol/L and recovered above 130 mmol/L within two to four days. Two of the three women had baseline sodium below 135 mmol/L. Any treatment-emergent adverse event occurred in 60/135 (44.4%) versus 57/126 (45.2%), and an adverse event led to discontinuation in 4/135 (3.0%) versus 1/126 (0.8%). Serious adverse events occurred in 0/135 on desmopressin and 2/126 (1.6%) on placebo.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

Efficacy and sodium safety were assessed over three months. Entry required nocturia frequency rather than confirmed nocturnal polyuria; the later US indication specified nocturnal polyuria.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

EAU's 2026 male LUTS guideline recommends offering desmopressin for nocturnal polyuria in men under 65 <Rec>(Weak)</Rec> and checking sodium at baseline, days three and seven, one month, and periodically thereafter <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> EAU's 2026 non-neurogenic female LUTS guideline recommends offering desmopressin for nocturnal polyuria after counselling about benefits and hyponatraemia <Rec>(Strong)</Rec> and careful sodium monitoring in older patients <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

The FDA approved NOCDURNA on June 21, 2018, for adults with nocturia due to nocturnal polyuria who awaken at least twice nightly to void.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
