# USC-STAR: Studer Pouch versus T-Pouch Orthotopic Ileal Neobladder

<TrialBottomLine id="usc-star" />

**USC-STAR** was a single-center, unmasked randomized trial of the T-pouch, an orthotopic ileal neobladder with an antireflux mechanism, versus the Studer pouch in 484 patients with bladder cancer undergoing radical cystectomy, with the change in estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) from baseline to 3 years as the primary endpoint. Among the 260 patients with 3-year renal data, eGFR fell by 6.6 mL/min/1.73 m² with the T-pouch and 6.4 mL/min/1.73 m² with the Studer pouch (p = 0.35).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="usc-star" />

## Design

Patients with clinically nonmetastatic bladder cancer scheduled for radical cystectomy with a neobladder were randomized to a T-pouch or a Studer ileal orthotopic neobladder between February 2002 and November 2009: 237 to the T-pouch and 247 to the Studer pouch. Baseline characteristics did not differ between the groups. Renal function was calculated with the CKD-EPI equation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The registered hypothesis was that the antireflux mechanism would reduce symptomatic urinary tract infection, upper-tract dilation and long-term loss of renal function.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

Among the 260 patients with 3-year renal data, the mean eGFR decreased by 6.6 mL/min/1.73 m² with the T-pouch and 6.4 mL/min/1.73 m² with the Studer pouch between baseline and 3 years (p = 0.35). In multivariable analysis, neobladder type was not independently associated with 3-year renal function (p = 0.63); baseline eGFR, age and urinary tract obstruction were.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

A questionnaire mailed to men at least 12 months after surgery, drawn from the same randomized cohort, was answered by 68% (mean follow-up 4.5 years, range 1 to 8) and pooled both arms. Among the male respondents, 22.3% used no pads; 47% used at least 1 pad in the daytime, 32.2% used small or mini pads and 22.6% used diapers; 72% used pads at night, 14.7% used small or mini pads and 38.9% used diapers. Mucus leakage was reported by 62.5%, and 9.5% performed clean intermittent self-catheterization, 70.6% of whom started within the first year. Increasing age and diabetes mellitus predicted worse urinary function but did not affect pad use.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Patients were assigned at radical cystectomy to a T-pouch, which includes an antireflux mechanism, or a Studer pouch. Treatment assignment was not masked.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

The cumulative risks of urinary tract infection and of overall late complications did not differ between the groups. The T-pouch was associated with an increased risk of diversion-related secondary surgical procedures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The primary endpoint was renal function at 3 years, and both arms showed a moderate decline.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
