# RICH-ART: Hyperbaric Oxygen for Radiation-Induced Cystitis

<TrialBottomLine id="rich-art" />

**RICH-ART** was an open-label, five-center, phase 2–3 randomized trial of hyperbaric oxygen therapy versus standard care in adults with symptomatic late radiation cystitis after pelvic radiotherapy and found greater improvement in patient-reported EPIC urinary total score with hyperbaric oxygen at 6–8 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="rich-art" />

## Design

Participants had completed pelvic radiotherapy at least six months earlier, had an EPIC urinary total score below 80, and had been referred to a participating hyperbaric clinic. The five Nordic university hospitals enrolled adults aged 18–80 years. Randomization was stratified by sex, time since radiotherapy, and previous invasive pelvic surgery; patients and investigators were unmasked. The primary endpoint was change in EPIC urinary total score from enrollment to visit 4. Standard care permitted other medication and interventions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Patients with ongoing bleeding requiring more than 500 mL of blood transfusion in the preceding four weeks, incontinence requiring a permanent urinary catheter, bladder capacity below 100 mL, bladder fistula, previous hyperbaric oxygen for late radiation injury, or a contraindication to hyperbaric oxygen therapy were excluded.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Among 87 randomized patients, eight withdrew consent immediately after assignment, leaving 41 in the hyperbaric oxygen group and 38 in standard care for the reported intention-to-treat analysis. Visit-4 scores were available for 40 and 35 patients, respectively; mean EPIC urinary total score change was 17.8 versus 7.7 points, a 10.1-point difference (95% CI 2.2–18.1; p=0.013) meeting the superiority test. Median time from randomization to visit 4 was 234 versus 217 days.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Urinary symptom response of at least 9 EPIC points occurred in 29/40 (73%) hyperbaric oxygen recipients and 12/35 (34%) standard-care patients at visit 4; the threshold was defined after the trial began. Cystoscopic bladder grades improved in 25/39 (64%) and 6/34 (18%), respectively (p=0.0012); the statistical analysis plan designated this grade comparison exploratory.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The prespecified five-year analysis pooled patients who ultimately received hyperbaric oxygen, including control participants offered treatment after the initial randomized assessment. In 70 treated patients, the modeled EPIC urinary total score improvement from before treatment was 18.0 points (95% CI 14.2–21.8) at six months and 19.1 points (95% CI 13.3–24.9) at five years. The five-year estimate used repeated-measures modeling; 39 of 70 had an observed score at that visit. Early response, defined as at least a 9-point improvement at six months, occurred in 48/70 (68.6%).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The hyperbaric regimen was 30–40 sessions of 100% oxygen at 240–250 kPa, with 80–90 minutes of oxygen breathing per session, five days per week.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Treatment began within four weeks of baseline and was completed within 60–80 days of the first session; centers used monoplace or multiplace chambers and varied their air breaks. All treated patients followed the predefined protocols without dose adjustment, and 1/41 discontinued treatment because of visual disturbance.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The follow-up report specifies five treatment days per week over 6–8 weeks. Standard care had no protocol restrictions on other medication or interventions. After the randomized phase, control participants were offered hyperbaric oxygen.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Nine of the 70 long-term participants received 20–30 further sessions for recurrent cystitis symptoms at least one year after initial treatment. Retreatments were given at a physician's discretion, without common criteria across centers.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

During initial treatment, 17/41 (41%) hyperbaric oxygen recipients had an adverse event.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Ear pain affected 6/41 (15%), barotrauma 4/41 (10%), and transient myopia 5/41 (12%); 1/41 (2%) required tympanic membrane paracentesis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Among the 41 treated patients, there were seven infection events (six grade 1, one grade 3) and three hematuria events (two grade 1, one grade 3), without a clear relation to hyperbaric oxygen. One standard-care patient died of sepsis and cardiac failure unrelated to the study; only serious events were recorded in that group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The later report details equalization pain, transient myopia, and barotrauma among these events. Adverse events were recorded during treatment, not across the subsequent five-year observation period.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The primary symptom endpoint was patient reported in an unmasked comparison. Eight immediate consent withdrawals were excluded from the reported intention-to-treat analysis. The later report had no untreated long-term control after crossover; 31 of 70 lacked a five-year observation, and follow-up ended six months early for administrative reasons. Its five-year estimate is a modeled within-person change in the treated cohort.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
