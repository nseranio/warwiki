# Tolterodine BOO Safety: Tolterodine in Men With Overactive Bladder and Bladder Outlet Obstruction

<TrialBottomLine id="tolterodine-boo-safety" />

In a double-blind trial of men with overactive bladder, detrusor overactivity and screening bladder outlet obstruction, 12 weeks of tolterodine versus placebo met the prespecified equivalence limits for maximum flow and voiding pressure. Postvoid residual volume rose more with tolterodine.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="tolterodine-boo-safety" />

## Design

The trial enrolled 222 men aged 40 years or older; one never took study drug, leaving 149 on tolterodine 2 mg twice daily and 72 on placebo in the intention-to-treat and safety populations. Entry required frequency, urgency, urodynamic detrusor overactivity and screening BOOI of at least 20; recent retention and postvoid residual greater than 40% of maximum cystometric capacity were excluded. Central review classified 15/221 as unobstructed. Qmax and pdetQmax were the primary endpoints, with equivalence requiring both 95% confidence intervals for between-group median-change differences to lie within ±3 mL/s and ±15 cm H2O, respectively. The study also measured BOOI, filling cystometry, postvoid residual volume and adverse events.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Both primary urodynamic endpoints met equivalence: the between-group median-change difference was −0.7 mL/s for Qmax (95% CI −1.6 to 0.4) and −7 cm H2O for pdetQmax (95% CI −11 to −3; median change −8 with tolterodine versus −1 cm H2O with placebo).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Among the other urodynamic measures, median BOOI changed from 49 to 40 with tolterodine and from 43 to 44 with placebo (treatment difference 6.4 points favoring tolterodine; P = 0.0108). Median volume to first detrusor contraction changed from 163 to 217 mL versus 209 to 178 mL (difference 59 mL, 95% CI 19–100; P = 0.0026), and median maximum cystometric capacity from 260 to 320 mL versus 294 to 285 mL (difference 67 mL, 95% CI 35–103; P = 0.0001). Median within-patient postvoid residual change was +25 versus 0 mL (difference 27 mL, 95% CI 8–50; P = 0.0038). Bladder Contractility Index changed from a median 112 to 107 versus 105 to 110 (difference −10, 95% CI −17 to −3; P = 0.0045), and median voiding efficiency from 91% to 80% versus 90% to 89% (difference −7 percentage points, 95% CI −13 to −1; P = 0.0183).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Acute retention requiring catheterization occurred in 0/149 tolterodine recipients and 1/72 placebo recipients; the placebo case led to drug discontinuation. The broader urinary-retention adverse-event category included 5/149 versus 2/72, and two tolterodine recipients discontinued for high postvoid residual. Urinary tract infection occurred in 6/149 versus 3/72, and dry mouth in 36/149 versus 1/72. Overall adverse events occurred in 87/149 versus 37/72 (P = 0.326).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Treatment lasted 12 weeks and excluded men with recent retention or high residual volume. The primary outcomes were urodynamic measurements; one acute retention event gives little precision for the risk of uncommon retention.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU 2026 guideline on non-neurogenic male LUTS cites this trial in its antimuscarinic safety discussion. It recommends muscarinic receptor antagonists for men with moderate-to-severe predominantly storage LUTS <Rec>(Strong)</Rec> and recommends against antimuscarinic OAB medication when postvoid residual exceeds 150 mL <Rec>(Weak)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
