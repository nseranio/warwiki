# Finazzi-Agrò PTNS versus sham: Percutaneous Tibial Nerve Stimulation for Detrusor Overactivity Incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="ptns-sham-finazzi" />

**Finazzi-Agrò PTNS versus sham** was a double-blind randomized trial of percutaneous tibial nerve stimulation (PTNS) against sham needle treatment in women with detrusor overactivity incontinence after antimuscarinic failure; more PTNS recipients achieved a greater than 50% reduction in urge-incontinence episodes after treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="ptns-sham-finazzi" />

## Design

The investigators randomized 35 women, 18 to PTNS and 17 to sham treatment. The response definition was a greater than 50% reduction in urge-incontinence episodes. Bladder diaries and incontinence quality-of-life scores were collected before and after treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Among completers, 12/17 PTNS recipients (71%) and 0/15 sham recipients (0%) met the response definition (P &lt; 0.001). Incontinence episodes, voiding frequency, voided volume and incontinence quality-of-life scores improved significantly from baseline in the PTNS group but not in the sham group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

PTNS consisted of 12 sessions. The sham procedure placed a 34-gauge needle in the medial gastrocnemius. Sessions in both groups lasted 30 minutes and were given three times weekly.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

One PTNS recipient and two sham recipients did not complete treatment for reasons reported as unrelated to the technique.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Three randomized women did not complete the study, leaving 32 in the responder analysis. Response was assessed after the treatment course. The sham needle was placed in the calf muscle, which made the comparison a needle procedure rather than observation alone.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU 2026 guideline on non-neurogenic female lower urinary tract symptoms cites this trial in its PTNS evidence review and recommends offering posterior tibial nerve stimulation for symptomatic improvement of overactive bladder or urgency urinary incontinence <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
