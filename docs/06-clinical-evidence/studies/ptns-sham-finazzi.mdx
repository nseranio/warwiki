# Finazzi-Agrò PTNS versus sham: Percutaneous Tibial Nerve Stimulation for Detrusor Overactivity Incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="ptns-sham-finazzi" />

**Finazzi-Agrò PTNS versus sham** was a double-blind randomized trial in women with urodynamically confirmed detrusor overactivity incontinence unresponsive to behavioral and rehabilitation therapy or antimuscarinics. After 12 sessions, a 50% or greater reduction in urge-incontinence episodes occurred in 12/17 PTNS completers (71%) and 0/15 sham completers (0%; P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="ptns-sham-finazzi" />

## Design

The investigators randomized 35 women, 18 to PTNS and 17 to sham treatment. The prespecified primary outcome was the proportion with a 50% or greater reduction in urge-incontinence episodes after 12 sessions; the two-sided significance threshold was 0.05. Secondary outcomes were incontinence episodes, micturitions, voided volume and incontinence quality-of-life (I-QoL) score, measured before and after treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The primary responder outcome favored PTNS among the 32 completers: 12/17 (71%) versus 0/15 (0%) with sham (P &lt; 0.001), meeting the prespecified threshold. Mean incontinence episodes per 3 days fell from 4.1 to 1.8 with PTNS (P &lt; 0.001) and from 4.2 to 3.8 with sham (P = 0.394); the treatment-by-time interaction had P &lt; 0.001.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The secondary diary measures also improved with PTNS: mean daily micturitions fell from 13.6 to 9.5 (sham 14.7 to 13.9; interaction P &lt; 0.001), and mean voided volume rose from 150.5 to 186.5 mL (sham 146.0 to 150.4 mL; interaction P = 0.001). Mean I-QoL score rose from 69.6 to 81.3 with PTNS and from 69.5 to 70.6 with sham (interaction P = 0.027).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

PTNS used a 34-gauge needle about 5 cm above the medial malleolus and a calcaneal surface electrode connected to the Urgent PC stimulator (9 V, 20 Hz, 0–10 mA); current was raised to a visible toe response and the highest tolerated level. The sham placed a 34-gauge needle in the medial gastrocnemius, delivered current for about 30 seconds, then left it off. Both groups had 12 sessions of 30 minutes, three times weekly. After three sessions, patients' treatment guesses agreed with assignment in 19/32 cases (approximately 60%; kappa 0.18; P = 0.305).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

No serious side effects were reported in either group; both groups reported occasional transient pain at the stimulation site, without event counts. One of 18 PTNS recipients and two of 17 sham recipients did not complete treatment for personal reasons unrelated to the technique.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Three randomized women did not complete the study, leaving 32 in the responder analysis. Response was assessed after the treatment course. The sham needle was placed in the calf muscle, which made the comparison a needle procedure rather than observation alone.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU 2026 guideline on non-neurogenic female lower urinary tract symptoms cites this trial in its PTNS evidence review and recommends offering posterior tibial nerve stimulation for symptomatic improvement of overactive bladder or urgency urinary incontinence <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
