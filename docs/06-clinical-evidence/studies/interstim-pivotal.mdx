# InterStim pivotal trials: Sacral Nerve Stimulation for Refractory Urinary Urge Incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="interstim-pivotal" />

The Sacral Nerve Stimulation Study Group randomized 76 test-stimulation responders with refractory urinary urge incontinence to immediate InterStim implantation or six months of standard medical therapy with delayed implantation; immediate stimulation improved all three primary diary measures at six months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="interstim-pivotal" />

## Design

The open-label comparison allocated 34 patients to immediate implantation and 42 to delayed implantation. Diary outcomes were daily incontinence episodes, episode severity, and pads or diapers replaced daily. After the randomized period, controls could receive an implant. The immediately implanted group also underwent a stimulation-on versus stimulation-off assessment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The between-group P value was &lt; 0.0001 for each diary measure. Of 34 immediately implanted patients, 16 (47%) were dry and another 10 (29%) had a greater than 50% reduction in episodes. Deactivation at six months returned incontinence to baseline levels; the report describes efficacy through 18 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Separate randomized reports examined the other indications. In 51 urgency-frequency patients, 25 received immediate implantation and 26 delayed implantation; immediate-arm voids per day changed from 16.9 ± 9.7 to 9.3 ± 5.1 and volume per void from 118 ± 74 to 226 ± 124 mL at six months (between-group P &lt; 0.0001 for each).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In the retention report, 68 of 177 enrolled patients were randomized after a successful test: 83% in the immediate group (37 assigned) met its success definition versus 9% in controls (31 assigned) at six months.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Five-year diary success in the uncontrolled worldwide implanted cohort differed by outcome: of 96 patients with urge incontinence, 56/96 (58%) had at least 50% fewer leaks per day and 53/78 (68%) had at least 50% fewer heavy leaks; of 25 with urgency-frequency, 10/25 (40%) had at least 50% fewer voids and 14/25 (56%) had at least 50% more volume per void. Among 31 with retention, 21/31 (68%) had at least 50% lower catheterized volume per catheterization. The paper labels this last count 71%, although 21/31 calculates to 68%.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The primary report selected candidates by temporary test stimulation before permanent implantation. In the urgency-frequency report, testing used S3 and/or S4, and the retention report required greater than 50% symptom improvement during a three- to seven-day percutaneous test.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The five-year cohort received abdominal generator placement in 121/152 cases and buttock placement in 31/152. At five years, 105/152 completed a visit and 87/152 provided a diary; the analysis carried forward the last observation for other missing diaries and counted withdrawals for adverse events or lack of efficacy as failures.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Adverse events

The primary report records pulse-generator site pain in 15.9%, implant-site pain in 19.1%, lead migration in 7.0%, and surgical revision for a complication in 32.5% of implanted patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In the five-year cohort, 102/152 patients (67%) had a device- or therapy-related adverse event; 60/152 (39.5%) required surgery for 110 events, including device exchange in 36/152 (23.7%).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Therapy-related new pain or undesired stimulation affected 41/152 (27.0%), therapy-related pain at PNE or the generator implant site 29/152 (19.1%), and therapy-related PNE or implant-site infection 12/152 (7.9%).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Device-related lead migration affected 5/152 (3.3%) and therapy-related migration 8/152 (5.3%); 16/152 (10.5%) underwent explantation for an adverse event or lack of efficacy.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Limitations

Randomization followed a positive test, so the comparison estimates benefit among responders. Treatment was unblinded, and delayed implantation ended the concurrent comparison at six months. The five-year percentages come from an implanted cohort without a concurrent control, with 87 of 152 patients providing a diary at five years.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU 2026 guideline on non-neurogenic female LUTS recommends offering sacral nerve stimulation for overactive bladder or urgency urinary incontinence refractory to anticholinergic therapy <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>; its evidence discussion cites Schmidt's trial.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> FDA approved the original InterStim system for urinary urge incontinence after failure or intolerance of conservative treatment on September 29, 1997.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
