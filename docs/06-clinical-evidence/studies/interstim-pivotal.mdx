# InterStim pivotal trials: Sacral Nerve Stimulation for Refractory Urinary Urge Incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="interstim-pivotal" />

After a positive test, 98/155 patients with refractory urge incontinence were randomized. Six-month diaries from 76 favored immediate InterStim over delayed implantation with standard therapy on all three primary outcomes (each P &lt; 0.0001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="interstim-pivotal" />

## Design

Eligibility required more than 50% improvement during a three- to seven-day test. Diaries came from 34 immediate and 42 delayed patients. Primary outcomes were daily leaks, severity rank and pad use; interim significance required P &lt; 0.0005. Controls could receive an implant after six months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Daily leaks changed from 9.7 to 2.6 with immediate stimulation versus 9.3 to 11.3 in controls; severity rank from 2.0 to 0.8 versus 1.8 to 2.0; and daily pads from 6.2 to 1.1 versus 5.0 to 6.3 (n = 34 versus 42; each between-group P &lt; 0.0001). In the immediate arm, 16/34 (47%) were dry and 10/34 (29%) had more than 50% fewer leaks. Clinical benefit persisted in 76% of 21 assessed at 18 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

All three primary urgency-frequency measures favored immediate implantation at six months (25 versus 26): daily voids changed from 16.9 to 9.3 versus 15.2 to 15.7, volume per void from 118 to 226 versus 124 to 123 mL, and urgency rank from 2.2 to 1.6 versus 2.4 to 2.3 (25 controls analyzed for rank; P &lt; 0.0001 for daily voids; P = 0.001 for volume per void; P = 0.01 for urgency rank).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Higher volume without worse urgency occurred in 22/25 (88%) versus 8/25 (32%; P = 0.0001).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In the retention report, 68/177 (38.4%) passed screening and were randomized (37 immediate, 31 delayed).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Six-month diaries from 29 and 22 patients showed catheterized residual volume falling from 339 to 49 versus 350 to 319 mL (P &lt; 0.0001); reported success of 83% versus 9% corresponds to 24/29 versus 2/22, and 20/29 (69%) stopped catheterizing.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> At 18 months, 17/24 (71%) succeeded and 14/24 (58%) no longer catheterized.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Five-year diary success in the uncontrolled worldwide implanted cohort differed by outcome: of 96 patients with urge incontinence, 56/96 (58%) had at least 50% fewer leaks per day and 53/78 (68%) had at least 50% fewer heavy leaks; of 25 with urgency-frequency, 10/25 (40%) had at least 50% fewer voids and 14/25 (56%) had at least 50% more volume per void. Among 31 with retention, 21/31 (68%) had at least 50% lower catheterized volume per catheterization. The paper labels this last count 71%, although 21/31 calculates to 68%.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The 1999 study placed an anchored sacral lead and subcutaneous generator, usually in the lower abdomen. Deactivation for at least three days increased mean daily leaks from 2.9 to 9.5 among 52 implanted patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The urgency-frequency study used a three- to seven-day S3 and/or S4 percutaneous test; 22/25 immediate patients completed the six-month deactivation test, one had an explant and two declined.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The retention study required more than 50% test improvement; 48 implanted patients averaged 3.9 reprogrammings over six months (range 1–14).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The five-year cohort received abdominal generator placement in 121/152 cases and buttock placement in 31/152. At five years, 105/152 completed a visit and 87/152 provided a diary; the analysis carried forward the last observation for other missing diaries and counted withdrawals for adverse events or lack of efficacy as failures.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Adverse events

The primary report pooled 157 implants across three indications: generator-site pain 15.9%, implant-site pain 19.1%, lead migration 7.0% and revision in 51/157 (32.5%), including six permanent and four temporary explants.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Data from 219 implants gave 12-month probabilities of generator-site pain 15.3%, new pain 9.0%, lead migration 8.4% and infection 6.1%; revision occurred in 33.3%.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> In the five-year cohort, 102/152 patients (67%) had a device- or therapy-related adverse event; 60/152 (39.5%) required surgery for 110 events, including device exchange in 36/152 (23.7%).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Therapy-related new pain or undesired stimulation affected 41/152 (27.0%), therapy-related pain at PNE or the generator implant site 29/152 (19.1%), and therapy-related PNE or implant-site infection 12/152 (7.9%).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Device-related lead migration affected 5/152 (3.3%) and therapy-related migration 8/152 (5.3%); 16/152 (10.5%) underwent explantation for an adverse event or lack of efficacy.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Limitations

Randomization followed a positive test, and the primary six-month analysis included diaries from 76 of 98 randomized patients. Treatment was unblinded, and delayed implantation ended the concurrent comparison at six months. The five-year percentages come from an implanted cohort without a concurrent control, with 87 of 152 patients providing a diary at five years.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU 2026 guideline on non-neurogenic female LUTS recommends offering sacral nerve stimulation for overactive bladder or urgency urinary incontinence refractory to anticholinergic therapy <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>; its evidence discussion cites Schmidt's trial.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> FDA approved the original InterStim system for urinary urge incontinence after failure or intolerance of conservative treatment on September 29, 1997.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
