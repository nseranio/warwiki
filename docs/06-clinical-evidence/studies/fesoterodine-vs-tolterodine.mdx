# Fesoterodine versus tolterodine ER: Head-to-Head Treatment of Overactive Bladder

<TrialBottomLine id="fesoterodine-vs-tolterodine" />

**Fesoterodine versus tolterodine ER** was a 12-week, double-blind, double-dummy trial that randomized 1,712 adults with overactive bladder to fesoterodine 8 mg, tolterodine ER 4 mg or placebo. In the 1,590-patient full efficacy analysis set, fesoterodine produced a greater reduction in urgency urinary incontinence episodes than either comparator.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="fesoterodine-vs-tolterodine" />

## Design

Eligibility required overactive bladder symptoms for at least 3 months and a baseline 3-day diary showing at least 8 voids and 1 urgency urinary incontinence episode per 24 hours. Participants were allocated 2:2:1. The primary endpoint was change from baseline to week 12 in urgency urinary incontinence episodes per 24 hours. Other diary endpoints included voiding frequency, urgency and mean voided volume; patients also completed the OAB questionnaire, Patient Perception of Bladder Condition and Urgency Perception Scale. Patients with neurologic disease, prior catheter-requiring retention, predominant stress incontinence or lower genitourinary pathology or surgery causing voiding dysfunction were excluded. Efficacy analyses omitted 107 participants from two sites with diary-data irregularities, a decision made before unblinding.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Daily urgency urinary incontinence episodes fell by a Winsorized mean of 1.72 with fesoterodine (n=619), 1.61 with tolterodine ER (n=626) and 1.46 with placebo (n=307) at week 12. The prespecified primary comparison favored fesoterodine versus tolterodine ER (P = 0.017) and placebo (P &lt; 0.001). In a post hoc analysis of participants with baseline incontinence, diary dry rates were 396/619 (64.0%), 358/626 (57.2%) and 138/307 (45.0%), respectively (P = 0.015 and P &lt; 0.001 for the respective fesoterodine comparisons). Mean voided volume increased by 32.9 mL with fesoterodine (n=626), 23.5 mL with tolterodine ER (n=633) and 16.8 mL with placebo (n=313; P = 0.005 for fesoterodine versus tolterodine ER). Daily voids fell by 2.2, 2.1 and 1.5, respectively (n=628, 634 and 313; P = 0.380 for fesoterodine versus tolterodine ER).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

After a 2-week single-blind placebo run-in, the fesoterodine arm received 4 mg each morning for 1 week, then 8 mg for 11 weeks. Tolterodine ER was given at 4 mg each morning for 12 weeks. The tolterodine ER and placebo arms had sham dose escalation to maintain masking. Among treated patients, 88% of 679 on fesoterodine, 92% of 684 on tolterodine ER and 91% of 334 on placebo completed the trial.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

In the treated safety set (fesoterodine n=679, tolterodine ER n=684, placebo n=334), dry mouth occurred in 27.8%, 16.4% and 6.0%, respectively; constipation occurred in 5.4%, 4.1% and 3.0%. Treatment-emergent adverse events led to discontinuation in 6%, 4% and 2%. Urinary tract infection occurred in 2.2%, 1.5% and 0.6%. Nonfatal serious adverse events occurred in 16/679, 9/684 and 6/334; two in the fesoterodine group were considered treatment-related. One 70-year-old man with benign prostatic enlargement required catheterization for urinary retention after 6 days of fesoterodine 4 mg.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The fesoterodine dose increased after the first week, while tolterodine ER remained at a fixed dose. Treatment lasted 12 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU 2026 guideline for non-neurogenic female LUTS cites this trial for a higher dry-mouth rate with fesoterodine 8 mg than tolterodine 4 mg. It recommends early review of efficacy and adverse effects during anticholinergic treatment for overactive bladder <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
