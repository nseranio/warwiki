# Fesoterodine versus tolterodine ER: Head-to-Head Treatment of Overactive Bladder

<TrialBottomLine id="fesoterodine-vs-tolterodine" />

**Fesoterodine versus tolterodine ER** was a 12-week, double-blind, double-dummy randomized trial in 1,590 patients with overactive bladder and urgency urinary incontinence, comparing fesoterodine 8 mg, tolterodine ER 4 mg and placebo; fesoterodine produced a greater reduction in urgency urinary incontinence episodes than either comparator.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="fesoterodine-vs-tolterodine" />

## Design

Eligibility required overactive bladder symptoms for at least 3 months and a baseline 3-day diary showing at least 8 voids and 1 urgency urinary incontinence episode per 24 hours. Participants were allocated 2:2:1. The primary endpoint was change from baseline to week 12 in urgency urinary incontinence episodes per 24 hours. Other diary endpoints included voiding frequency, urgency and mean voided volume; patients also completed the OAB questionnaire, Patient Perception of Bladder Condition and Urgency Perception Scale.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

For the primary endpoint, the fesoterodine comparison was significant against tolterodine ER (P = 0.017) and placebo (P &lt; 0.001). Diary dry rates, defined as no urgency urinary incontinence episodes at endpoint among patients who had at least one at baseline, were 64% with fesoterodine, 57% with tolterodine ER and 45% with placebo (P = 0.015 and P &lt; 0.001 for the respective fesoterodine comparisons). Mean voided volume improved more with fesoterodine than tolterodine ER (P = 0.005).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The fesoterodine arm received 4 mg daily for 1 week, then 8 mg for 11 weeks. Tolterodine ER was given at 4 mg daily. The tolterodine ER and placebo arms had sham dose escalation to maintain masking.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Dry mouth occurred in 28%, 16% and 6% of the fesoterodine, tolterodine ER and placebo groups, respectively; constipation occurred in 5%, 4% and 3%. Treatment-emergent adverse events led to discontinuation in 6%, 4% and 2%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The fesoterodine dose increased after the first week, while tolterodine ER remained at a fixed dose. Treatment lasted 12 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU 2026 guideline for non-neurogenic female LUTS cites this trial for a higher dry-mouth rate with fesoterodine 8 mg than tolterodine 4 mg. It recommends early review of efficacy and adverse effects during anticholinergic treatment for overactive bladder <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
