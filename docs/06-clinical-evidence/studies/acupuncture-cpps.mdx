# Sun acupuncture for CP/CPPS: Acupuncture versus sham for chronic prostatitis/chronic pelvic pain syndrome

<TrialBottomLine id="acupuncture-cpps" />

This multicenter, randomized, sham-controlled trial compared acupuncture with sham acupuncture in 440 men with moderate to severe chronic prostatitis/chronic pelvic pain syndrome (CP/CPPS); after assignment to 20 sessions over 8 weeks, the acupuncture group had more NIH-CPSI responders at both week 8 and week 32.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="acupuncture-cpps" />

## Design

Ten tertiary hospitals in China enrolled men aged 18–50 years with at least three months of pelvic pain in the preceding six months, no evidence of infection, and an NIH-CPSI score of at least 15, irrespective of prior acupuncture exposure. Participants, outcome assessors, and statisticians were blinded; acupuncturists were not. The primary outcome was a reduction of at least 6 points from baseline on the National Institutes of Health Chronic Prostatitis Symptom Index (NIH-CPSI). The prespecified test for sustained efficacy required a significant between-group difference at both week 8, when treatment ended, and week 32, after a further 24 weeks. All 440 randomized men entered the intention-to-treat analysis; those who withdrew without an NIH-CPSI score were counted as nonresponders.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The prespecified responder test favored acupuncture at both time points in the intention-to-treat analysis (220 randomized per arm). At week 8, modeled NIH-CPSI response was 60.6% with acupuncture and 36.8% with sham (adjusted OR 2.6, 95% CI 1.8–4.0; P &lt; 0.001). At week 32, modeled response was 61.5% and 38.3% (adjusted OR 2.6, 95% CI 1.7–3.9; P &lt; 0.001). Post hoc adjusted risk differences were 21.6 percentage points (95% CI 12.8–30.4) and 21.1 points (95% CI 12.2–30.1), respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The secondary NIH-CPSI total score decreased by an adjusted mean of 7.4 versus 4.9 points at week 8 (difference 2.5, 95% CI 1.6–3.4) and 7.4 versus 4.9 points at week 32 (difference 2.6, 95% CI 1.6–3.5). These secondary comparisons were exploratory because no multiplicity adjustment was made.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Each group was assigned twenty 30-minute acupuncture or sham-acupuncture sessions over 8 weeks: three weekly for the first 4 weeks, then two weekly for 4 weeks. Certified acupuncturists had five years of undergraduate training and at least two years of clinical experience. Active needling used bilateral BL33 and BL35 to a depth of 50–60 mm and BL23 and SP6 to 25–30 mm, with manipulation every 10 minutes except at BL33; sham needling used superficial 2–3 mm insertion at nearby nonacupoints without manipulation. Of 220 randomized men per arm, 206 acupuncture recipients and 208 sham recipients completed treatment; mean attendance was 18.9 (SD 3.9) and 19.1 (SD 3.5) sessions, respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Investigators recorded 20 adverse events in the acupuncture arm and 14 in the sham arm (9.1% and 6.4% of 220 randomized men per arm); events were counted by type, not as distinct affected patients. Treatment-related subcutaneous hematomas numbered 9/220 (4.1%) and 5/220 (2.3%), localized infection 1/220 (0.5%) and 0/220, and nausea 1/220 (0.5%) and 0/220, respectively. Treatment-related events were mild and transient; no serious adverse events were reported in either arm (0/220 each).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The sham procedure may have had physiologic effects. Participants were relatively young, and the 20-session schedule may be burdensome in other countries.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU's 2026 chronic pelvic pain guideline recommends offering acupuncture for primary prostate pain syndrome <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>; its evidence summary states that acupuncture improved symptoms and quality of life over sham treatment.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The AUA's 2025 male chronic pelvic pain guideline states that clinicians may offer acupuncture to patients with CP/CPPS <Rec>(Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
