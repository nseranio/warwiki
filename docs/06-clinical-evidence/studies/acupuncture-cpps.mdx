# Sun acupuncture for CP/CPPS: Acupuncture versus sham for chronic prostatitis/chronic pelvic pain syndrome

<TrialBottomLine id="acupuncture-cpps" />

This multicenter, randomized, sham-controlled trial compared acupuncture with sham acupuncture in 440 men with moderate to severe chronic prostatitis/chronic pelvic pain syndrome (CP/CPPS); after 20 sessions over 8 weeks, the acupuncture group had more NIH-CPSI responders at both week 8 and week 32.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="acupuncture-cpps" />

## Design

Ten tertiary hospitals in China enrolled men irrespective of prior acupuncture exposure. The primary outcome was a reduction of at least 6 points from baseline on the National Institutes of Health Chronic Prostatitis Symptom Index (NIH-CPSI). The prespecified test for sustained efficacy required a significant between-group difference at both week 8, when treatment ended, and week 32, after a further 24 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At week 8, NIH-CPSI response was 60.6% with acupuncture and 36.8% with sham (adjusted risk difference 21.6 percentage points, 95% CI 12.8–30.4; P &lt; 0.001). At week 32, response was 61.5% and 38.3% (adjusted risk difference 21.1 percentage points, 95% CI 12.2–30.1; P &lt; 0.001). The prespecified requirement for significance at both time points was met.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Each group was assigned 20 acupuncture or sham-acupuncture sessions over 8 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
Sessions lasted 30 minutes and were delivered by certified, experienced acupuncturists.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

The investigators reported 20 adverse events in the acupuncture arm (9.1% relative to 220 assigned men) and 14 in the sham arm (6.4% relative to 220 assigned men). No serious adverse events were reported.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The sham procedure may have had physiologic effects. Treatment was delivered at Chinese tertiary centers, where acupuncture is an established therapy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU's 2026 chronic pelvic pain guideline recommends offering acupuncture for primary prostate pain syndrome <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>; its evidence summary states that acupuncture improved symptoms and quality of life over sham treatment.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The AUA's 2025 male chronic pelvic pain guideline states that clinicians may offer acupuncture to patients with CP/CPPS <Rec>(Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
