# Silodosin US pivotal trials: Pooled Phase 3 Studies in Symptomatic BPH

<TrialBottomLine id="silodosin-pivotal" />

**The silodosin US pivotal trials** were two pooled, double-blind phase 3 randomized studies of silodosin 8 mg daily versus placebo in 923 men with symptomatic BPH; week-12 mean IPSS improvement was −6.4 versus −3.5 points (adjusted difference −2.8, 95% CI −3.6 to −2.0; P &lt; 0.0001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="silodosin-pivotal" />

## Design

Eligible men were at least 50 years old, had an IPSS of at least 13, peak urinary flow of 4–15 mL/s and postvoid residual below 250 mL. A 4-week placebo run-in excluded men with at least a 30% IPSS decrease or a flow increase of at least 3 mL/s. Study medication was taken with breakfast. The primary endpoint was change in total IPSS from baseline to week 12; peak urinary flow at week 12 was secondary. The pooled ANCOVA used baseline values as covariates and last observation carried forward for missing week-12 values.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Week-12 total IPSS change favored silodosin: −6.4 ± 6.63 versus −3.5 ± 5.84 points with placebo (adjusted difference −2.8, 95% CI −3.6 to −2.0; P &lt; 0.0001; last observation carried forward; 466 and 457 men in the efficacy sets). At 3–4 days, mean changes were −4.2 ± 5.3 and −2.3 ± 4.4 points (adjusted difference −1.9, 95% CI −2.5 to −1.3; P &lt; 0.0001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Peak urinary flow rose by 2.6 ± 4.43 versus 1.5 ± 4.36 mL/s at week 12 (adjusted difference 1.0 mL/s, 95% CI 0.4–1.5; P = 0.0007; last observation carried forward). At 2–6 hours after the first dose, increases were 2.8 ± 3.4 versus 1.5 ± 3.8 mL/s (adjusted difference 1.3 mL/s, 95% CI 0.9–1.8; P &lt; 0.0001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the subsequent 40-week open-label extension, observed-case mean IPSS change from extension baseline was −4.5 ± 6.7 points among men previously assigned placebo (347 enrolled; P &lt; 0.0001) and −1.6 ± 6.0 among those continuing silodosin (314 enrolled; P &lt; 0.01). All extension participants received silodosin.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Of 661 men entering the extension, 435 (65.8%) completed its 40 weeks of open-label silodosin 8 mg daily with breakfast; 77/661 (11.6%) did not comply with the dosing regimen.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

During blinded treatment, retrograde ejaculation occurred in 131/466 men (28.1%) receiving silodosin and 4/457 (0.9%) receiving placebo; 2.8% of silodosin-treated men discontinued for this event. Orthostatic hypotension occurred in 12/466 (2.6%) and 7/457 (1.5%), respectively. Any adverse event occurred in 257/466 (55.2%) and 168/457 (36.8%); serious events in 6/466 (1.3%) and 7/457 (1.5%); and adverse-event discontinuation in 30/466 (6.4%) and 10/457 (2.2%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

During the open-label extension, 431/661 men (65.2%) reported an adverse event, 29/661 (4.4%) had a serious event, and 93/661 (14.1%) discontinued for an adverse event. Retrograde ejaculation occurred in 138/661 (20.9%): 108/347 (31.1%) starting silodosin after placebo and 30/314 (9.6%) continuing silodosin. Orthostatic hypotension occurred in 17/661 (2.6%) and dizziness in 19/661 (2.9%).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The randomized comparison lasted 12 weeks. The extension enrolled only men who completed a blinded trial and lacked a concurrent placebo group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The FDA approved Rapaflo (silodosin) capsules for signs and symptoms of BPH on October 8, 2008, before the pooled trial report was published.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
