# Silodosin US pivotal trials: Pooled Phase 3 Studies in Symptomatic BPH

<TrialBottomLine id="silodosin-pivotal" />

**The silodosin US pivotal trials** were two pooled, double-blind phase 3 randomized studies of silodosin 8 mg daily versus placebo in 923 men with symptomatic BPH; IPSS improvement favored silodosin within 3–4 days and remained significantly greater through 12 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="silodosin-pivotal" />

## Design

Eligible men were at least 50 years old, had an IPSS of at least 13, and had a peak urinary flow rate of 4–15 mL/s. Study medication was taken with breakfast. The primary endpoint was change in total IPSS from baseline to the last observation; peak urinary flow was secondary. The pooled analysis used ANCOVA to compare the groups.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At 3–4 days, mean total IPSS change was −4.2 ± 5.3 points with silodosin and −2.3 ± 4.4 with placebo, a mean difference of −1.9 points (P &lt; 0.0001). The between-group difference in total IPSS was significant through week 12 (P &lt; 0.0001). Peak urinary flow rose by 2.8 ± 3.4 versus 1.5 ± 3.8 mL/s 2–6 hours after the first dose (mean difference 1.3 mL/s; P &lt; 0.0001); the flow difference remained significant through week 12.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the subsequent 40-week open-label extension, observed-case mean IPSS change from extension baseline was −4.5 ± 6.7 points among men previously assigned placebo and −1.6 ± 6.0 among those continuing silodosin. All extension participants received silodosin.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Of 661 men entering the extension, 435 (65.8%) completed its 40 weeks of open-label silodosin.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

During blinded treatment, treatment-emergent retrograde ejaculation was reported in 28.1% with silodosin and 0.9% with placebo; 2.8% of silodosin-treated men discontinued for this event.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Orthostatic hypotension was reported in 2.6% and 1.5%, respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> During the open-label extension, 431/661 men (65.2%) reported an adverse event, including retrograde ejaculation in 20.9% and orthostatic hypotension in 2.6%.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The randomized comparison lasted 12 weeks. The extension enrolled only men who completed a blinded trial and lacked a concurrent placebo group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The FDA approved Rapaflo (silodosin) capsules for signs and symptoms of BPH on October 8, 2008, before the pooled trial report was published.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
