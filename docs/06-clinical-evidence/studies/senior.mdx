# SENIOR: Solifenacin and Oxybutynin Cognition in Mild Cognitive Impairment

<TrialBottomLine id="senior" />

**SENIOR** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized crossover trial comparing solifenacin, oxybutynin, and placebo in 26 volunteers aged at least 75 years with mild cognitive impairment. In the 23-person efficacy analysis, neither drug differed significantly from placebo on the five prespecified cognitive composites at its peak-time assessment. Oxybutynin reduced continuity of attention at 1 hour and worsened two attention composites in a post hoc pooled 1–2-hour analysis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="senior" />

## Design

Each participant was assigned to three 21-day treatment periods: solifenacin 5 mg once daily, oxybutynin 5 mg twice daily, and placebo, with a 21-day washout between periods. Volunteers had mild cognitive impairment by Stockholm criteria, a Mini-Mental State Examination score above 23, and no current OAB medication; OAB symptoms were not an enrollment requirement. Cognitive Drug Research testing compared change from time-matched baseline with placebo at 6 hours after solifenacin and 2 hours after oxybutynin. The five primary composites were power and continuity of attention, quality of working and episodic memory, and speed of memory. Other time points and tolerability were secondary assessments; pooling adjacent time points was post hoc.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Neither drug differed significantly from placebo on any primary composite at its prespecified peak-time assessment (23 participants analyzed). For power of attention, the adjusted difference was −20.99 ms with solifenacin at 6 hours (95% CI −68.58 to 26.61; p=0.3771) and 17.51 ms with oxybutynin at 2 hours (95% CI −28.85 to 63.87; p=0.4487). At 1 hour, a secondary analysis found lower continuity of attention with oxybutynin versus placebo (difference −2.35, 95% CI −4.06 to −0.64; p=0.0084). In post hoc pooled 1–2-hour testing, oxybutynin worsened power of attention by 26.01 ms (95% CI 1.65 to 50.36; p=0.0365) and continuity of attention by 1.66 (95% CI −2.70 to −0.62; p=0.0019).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Treatment delivery

Twenty of 26 participants completed all three periods; 23 completed cognitive testing in at least two periods and entered the prespecified efficacy analysis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 14/23 (60.9%) participants during solifenacin treatment, 21/25 (84.0%) during oxybutynin treatment, and 11/22 (50.0%) during placebo. Dry mouth occurred in 4/23 (17.4%), 13/25 (52.0%), and 2/22 (9.1%), respectively. Infections and infestations were reported in 2/23 (8.7%), 5/25 (20.0%), and 3/22 (13.6%), respectively. Two serious events were judged unrelated to treatment; two of 26 participants discontinued because of adverse events.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The trial assessed each drug over 21 days in 26 relatively high-functioning volunteers with mild cognitive impairment, of whom 23 entered the efficacy analysis. No formal power calculation was performed.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU's 2026 female lower urinary tract symptoms guideline cites SENIOR in its discussion of anticholinergic use in older women. It advises caution with long-term anticholinergic treatment in older women, especially those with or at risk of cognitive dysfunction <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
