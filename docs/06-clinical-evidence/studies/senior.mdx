# SENIOR: Solifenacin and Oxybutynin Cognition in Mild Cognitive Impairment

<TrialBottomLine id="senior" />

**SENIOR** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized crossover trial comparing solifenacin, oxybutynin, and placebo in 26 volunteers aged at least 75 years with mild cognitive impairment. Neither active drug produced a significant change from baseline in the five primary composite cognitive outcomes; secondary testing found worse attention with oxybutynin versus placebo at 1–2 hours postdose.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="senior" />

## Design

Each participant was assigned to three 21-day treatment periods: solifenacin 5 mg once daily, oxybutynin 5 mg twice daily, and placebo, with a 21-day washout between periods. Cognitive function was measured with computerized Cognitive Drug Research testing. The primary analysis examined change from baseline at 6 hours after solifenacin and 2 hours after oxybutynin, near each drug's predicted peak concentration. The five composites were power and continuity of attention, quality of working and episodic memory, and speed of memory. Additional postdose testing and tolerability were secondary outcomes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Across the primary testing time points, neither solifenacin nor oxybutynin had a significant change from baseline in any of the five composites. In the secondary time-point analysis, oxybutynin decreased power and continuity of attention versus placebo at 1–2 hours postdose.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Both drugs were described as well tolerated. Mild or moderate dry mouth was the most frequently reported adverse event.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The trial enrolled 26 volunteers and assessed each drug over 21 days. Its prespecified outcome was short-term computerized cognitive performance.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU's 2026 female lower urinary tract symptoms guideline cites SENIOR in its discussion of anticholinergic use in older women. It advises caution with long-term anticholinergic treatment in older women, especially those with or at risk of cognitive dysfunction <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
