# Pisco PAE versus sham: Prostatic Artery Embolisation for Severe LUTS/BPH

<TrialBottomLine id="pae-sham" />

**Pisco PAE versus sham** was a single-centre, patient-blinded superiority trial in 80 men with severe LUTS/BPH despite alpha-blocker treatment that found greater six-month IPSS and quality-of-life improvement after prostatic artery embolisation (PAE) than after prostatic artery catheterisation without embolisation. The adjusted between-group difference in IPSS change was −13.2 points (95% CI −16.2 to −10.2), and the mean quality-of-life score was 2.13 points lower (95% CI 1.57–2.68 for sham minus PAE; P &lt; 0.0001 for each).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="pae-sham" />

## Design

The Lisbon centre enrolled men older than 45 years after at least six months of alpha-blocker treatment. Eligibility required IPSS at least 20, the IPSS quality-of-life item at least 3, Qmax below 12 mL/s, prostate volume at least 40 cm³, and a catheterisable prostatic artery. CT angiography screened access; prior invasive prostate treatment, retention, detrusor failure, recurrent infection, prostate or bladder cancer, and major iliac or prostatic arterial disease were exclusions. Randomisation occurred only after successful catheterisation of a prostatic artery. Patients remained blinded through the six-month efficacy assessment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The study used intention-to-treat analysis with last observation carried forward for missing data. The IPSS change was tested first by analysis of covariance adjusted for baseline IPSS; the quality-of-life score was tested only after that test passed. Both tests used a two-sided 5% threshold. Secondary comparisons used multiplicity-adjusted confidence intervals and P values. The blinded phase ended at six months, when sham recipients were offered PAE; follow-up continued to month 12.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Mean IPSS fell 17.1 points after PAE and 5.03 after sham (adjusted difference −13.2, 95% CI −16.2 to −10.2; P &lt; 0.0001). At the same visit, mean quality-of-life scores were 1.35 and 3.48 (PAE minus sham −2.13, 95% CI −2.68 to −1.57; P &lt; 0.0001). Both tests passed the prespecified gatekeeper sequence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Among secondary outcomes, mean Qmax increased 6.82 versus 2.80 mL/s (adjusted difference 4.22 mL/s, 95% CI 0.86–7.58; adjusted P = 0.005), while mean postvoid residual changed by −59.9 versus +8.63 mL (adjusted difference −60.6 mL, 95% CI −116.7 to −4.6; adjusted P = 0.03). After crossover, the former sham arm improved by a mean 13.6 ± 9.19 IPSS points during its six-month open PAE period; this was a within-group result.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Interventional radiologists used 300–500 μm Bead Block particles for PAE. In the sham arm, the catheter was withdrawn after prostatic artery catheterisation without injecting particles; a brief wait helped preserve patient blinding. Bilateral embolisation was achieved in 37/40 randomized PAE recipients (92.5%). Median procedure time was 75 minutes (IQR 60–90) with PAE and 30 minutes (IQR 30–45) with sham.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

All 38 sham-arm completers underwent PAE after the blinded phase. Study physicians continued tamsulosin until symptoms improved sufficiently to stop it; at six months, 2/39 (5.13%) evaluable PAE recipients and 34/37 (91.9%) sham recipients were still taking it.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

During the blinded period, 14/40 (35.0%) PAE recipients and 13/40 (32.5%) sham recipients had an adverse event; haematuria occurred in 3/40 (7.5%) and 2/40 (5.0%). After crossover PAE, one of 38 former sham recipients (2.6%) expelled prostatic fragments, developed haematuria and acute retention, and underwent TURP for a Clavien–Dindo IIIa event. The event belongs to the open crossover period.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The single centre had experienced PAE operators, randomized only after arterial access was demonstrated, and enrolled men with severe symptoms and prostates of at least 40 cm³. Patient blinding ended at six months; all sham-arm completers then received PAE, leaving no concurrent sham control for the 12-month assessment. Missing outcomes were imputed with last observation carried forward.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="pae-sham" />

This trial measured PAE against a catheter-based sham at six months. PAE-versus-TURP trials in the same BPH procedure decision compare symptom relief and deobstruction against resection, with a different control and operative question.

## Guidelines and approvals

The **AUA 2026 BPH guideline** cites the sham comparison and says clinicians may offer PAE with particle embolics for LUTS/BPH in prostates at least 50 cc <Rec>(Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>. It calls for urologic assessment before PAE <Rec>(Expert Opinion)</Rec>, performance by interventional radiologists trained in PAE <Rec>(Expert Opinion)</Rec>, and counseling about radiation exposure <Rec>(Clinical Principle)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The **EAU 2026 male LUTS guideline** cites this trial and offers PAE to men with moderate-to-severe LUTS/benign prostatic obstruction who wish to consider minimally invasive treatment options and accept less optimal outcomes than TURP <Rec>(Weak)</Rec>. It calls for urologist and trained interventional radiologist collaboration in selection and follow-up <Rec>(Strong)</Rec> and labels PAE under investigation.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
