# Guerrero sling materials: TVT, Pelvicol, and autologous fascial slings

<TrialBottomLine id="guerrero-sling-materials" />

In a four-center randomized trial of women undergoing primary surgery for urodynamically proven stress urinary incontinence, one-year patient-reported improvement was lower with a Pelvicol porcine-dermis sling than with retropubic tension-free vaginal tape (TVT) or autologous fascial sling; TVT and autologous fascia did not differ significantly.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="guerrero-sling-materials" />

## Design

Four UK units randomized 201 women: 72 to TVT, 50 to Pelvicol, and 79 to autologous fascia. The assigned sling techniques were TVT, Pelvicol, and autologous fascial sling.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Investigators and outcome assessors were listed as masked.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The primary outcome was patient-reported improvement; assessments occurred at baseline, six weeks, six months, and one year.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At one year, improvement was reported by 93% after TVT, 61% after Pelvicol, and 90% after autologous fascia (three-arm P &lt; 0.001). At six months the corresponding rates were 92%, 73%, and 95% (P = 0.003). Complete dryness at one year was 55%, 22%, and 48% (P = 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

At median ten-year follow-up, 162/201 women were assessed. Dry-or-improved rates were 73% after TVT, 58% after Pelvicol, and 75.4% after autologous fascia. Complete dryness was 31.7%, 15.7%, and 50.8%, respectively; the autologous-fascia dry rate exceeded Pelvicol (P &lt; 0.001) and TVT (P = 0.036).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The Pelvicol arm was suspended after interim analysis. The primary report gave operative times of 35 minutes for TVT, 36 minutes for Pelvicol, and 54 minutes for autologous fascia. Hospital stay was shortest after TVT, at two days.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Intermittent self-catheterization was reported in 1.5% of TVT patients, none of the Pelvicol patients, and 9.9% of autologous-fascia patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> By one year, one in five Pelvicol patients had undergone further SUI surgery, compared with none in the other arms. At long-term follow-up, reoperation for persistent SUI was reported in two TVT patients (3.2%), five Pelvicol patients (13.1% as reported), and no autologous-fascia patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

The Pelvicol arm stopped early, leaving fewer women assigned to that material. The ten-year analysis assessed 162 of the 201 randomized women and used patient-reported dry-or-improved status as its primary outcome.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA/SUFU female SUI guideline (2023) includes the Guerrero trial among the evidence behind its discussion of inferior results with porcine dermis as a substitute for autologous fascia. For index patients, it recommends counseling on midurethral sling and autologous fascia pubovaginal sling, among other surgical options <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade A)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

The EAU female LUTS guideline (2026) cites Guerrero in its autologous-sling evidence review and recommends offering an autologous sling after discussion of its risks and benefits against other operations <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
