# Guerrero sling materials: TVT, Pelvicol, and autologous fascial slings

<TrialBottomLine id="guerrero-sling-materials" />

In a four-center randomized trial of women undergoing primary surgery for urodynamically proven stress urinary incontinence, patient-reported improvement at the six-week primary assessment was similar after retropubic tension-free vaginal tape (TVT), Pelvicol porcine dermis, and autologous fascial sling; Pelvicol had lower improvement rates at six months and one year.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="guerrero-sling-materials" />

## Design

Four UK units randomized 211 women: 72 to TVT, 55 to Pelvicol, and 84 to autologous fascia; 201 underwent surgery (72, 50, and 79, respectively). All slings were placed at the mid-urethra by experienced surgeons using standardized techniques.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Patients and research nurses collecting outcomes were masked, although the fascial harvest incision could reveal the assignment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The primary outcome was patient-reported dry or improved status at six weeks; assessments also occurred at six months and one year. The report analyzed both respondents and treated women with nonresponse counted as failure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Six-week improvement among respondents was 61/67 (91%) after TVT, 43/47 (91%) after Pelvicol, and 67/71 (94%) after autologous fascia; no pairwise comparison was significant (P = 0.996, 0.817, and 0.734, respectively). At six months the rates were 65/71 (92%), 33/45 (73%), and 69/73 (95%); Pelvicol differed from TVT (P = 0.023) and fascia (P = 0.003). At one year they were 64/69 (93%), 28/46 (61%), and 60/67 (90%); Pelvicol differed from TVT (P &lt; 0.0001) and fascia (P = 0.001), while TVT and fascia did not differ (P = 0.789). Complete dryness at one year was 38/69 (55%), 10/46 (22%), and 32/67 (48%), respectively; Pelvicol differed from TVT (P = 0.001) and fascia (P = 0.014).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

At median ten-year follow-up, 162/201 treated women were assessed (TVT 63/72, Pelvicol 38/50, fascia 61/79). Dry-or-improved rates among respondents were 73%, 58%, and 75.4%, respectively, with no significant pairwise difference (TVT versus fascia P = 0.461). Complete dryness was 31.7%, 15.7%, and 50.8%; the autologous-fascia rate exceeded Pelvicol (P &lt; 0.001) and TVT (P = 0.036).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The Pelvicol arm was suspended after planned interim analysis found lower improvement and dry rates. Pelvicol used a 12 × 2 cm porcine graft mounted on nylon threads and passed retropubically; autologous fascia used an 8–10 × 1.5 cm rectus graft placed by the same route. Cystoscopy followed every sling placement.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Mean operative times were 35 minutes for TVT, 36 minutes for Pelvicol, and 54 minutes for fascia. Median hospital stay was two days after TVT and four days after each of the other procedures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

At six weeks, intermittent self-catheterization was reported by 1/67 TVT patients, 0/47 Pelvicol patients, and 7/71 fascia patients; at one year, none of the 69, 46, and 67 respondents, respectively, reported it.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Bladder injury occurred in 4/72, 1/50, and 2/79 women, respectively; one Pelvicol patient required urethral dilation and one fascia patient underwent urethrolysis by six weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> By one year, 9/46 Pelvicol respondents had undergone further SUI surgery, compared with none in the other arms.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> At long-term follow-up, reoperation for persistent SUI was reported in 2/63 TVT patients, 5/38 Pelvicol patients, and 0/61 fascia patients.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Sling release was reported in 2/63, 1/38, and 2/61, respectively; current self-catheterization in 3/63, 0/38, and 4/61; mesh exposure requiring excision in 1/63 TVT patients; and abdominal-incision scar pain in 2/61 fascia patients.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

The Pelvicol arm stopped early, and recruitment ended before the TVT and fascia arms reached the revised target of 80 women each. The ten-year analysis assessed 162 of the 201 treated women through postal questionnaires or telephone interviews and used patient-reported dry-or-improved status as its primary outcome.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA/SUFU female SUI guideline (2023) includes the Guerrero trial among the evidence behind its discussion of inferior results with porcine dermis as a substitute for autologous fascia. For index patients, it recommends counseling on midurethral sling and autologous fascia pubovaginal sling, among other surgical options <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade A)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

The EAU female LUTS guideline (2026) cites Guerrero in its autologous-sling evidence review and recommends offering an autologous sling after discussion of its risks and benefits against other operations <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
