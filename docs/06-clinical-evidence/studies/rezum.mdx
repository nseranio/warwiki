# Rezūm II: Convective Water Vapor Thermal Therapy Versus Sham for BPH

<TrialBottomLine id="rezum" />

**Rezūm II** was a multicenter, participant-blinded, sham-controlled randomized trial in 197 men aged 50 or older with moderate-to-severe lower urinary tract symptoms, IPSS of 13 or higher, peak flow of 15 mL/s or less and prostate volume of 30–80 cc that compared convective water vapor thermal therapy (Rezūm) with rigid cystoscopy and simulated treatment sounds. At 3 months, mean IPSS fell by 11.2 points with water vapor therapy and by 4.3 points with the sham procedure (p &lt; 0.0001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="rezum" />

## Design

Fifteen US centers enrolled men from September 2013 to August 2014; 384 were screened and 197 were randomized 2:1 by permuted blocks stratified by center and baseline IPSS.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Eligibility required a peak flow of 5–15 mL/s with a voided volume of at least 125 mL and a postvoid residual of 250 mL or less.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The registry exclusions include any prior invasive prostate intervention or other prostate surgery, a penile implant or urethral or prostatic stent, spontaneous urinary retention treated with an indwelling transurethral or suprapubic catheter in the preceding 6 months (a resolved provoked episode was allowed), urethral stricture or bladder neck contracture, neurogenic bladder and known prostate or bladder cancer; a median lobe was not an exclusion.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Treatment-arm men averaged 63.0 years, an IPSS of 22.0, an IPSS quality-of-life score of 4.4, a prostate volume of 45.9 cc, a peak flow of 9.9 mL/s and a postvoid residual of 82.4 mL; 72.5% of the cohort had an IPSS of 19–35.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The control procedure was a 19–21 Fr rigid cystoscopy with simulated active-treatment sounds, and participants were unblinded after the 3-month visit. The Cochrane review judged performance bias high risk because the urologists performing the procedures could not be blinded.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

The primary efficacy endpoint was the change in IPSS from baseline at 3 months. The trial was powered at 80% (one-sided alpha 0.025) to test whether the IPSS reduction with treatment exceeds 125% of the reduction with sham, using a Student t-test in the intention-to-treat population; men who chose another treatment before the 3-month visit were counted as failures with their baseline value carried forward.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The primary safety endpoint was a composite of device-related serious complications at 3 months (new retention lasting more than 21 days, rectourethral fistula, or rectal or gastrointestinal perforation) with a performance goal of 12% or less.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

After unblinding, 53 of the 61 control men requalified and elected treatment within 3 to 6 months of enrollment. Treatment and crossover men were followed at 3, 6 and 12 months and then yearly to 5 years, with adverse events adjudicated by independent data-monitoring and clinical-events committees. A man retreated for BPH, medically or surgically, left follow-up.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

In the intention-to-treat population, IPSS changed by −11.2 ± 7.6 with water vapor therapy and −4.3 ± 6.9 with sham (p &lt; 0.0001); the registry's 95% confidence intervals for the two group means are −12.5 to −9.9 and −6.1 to −2.5.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The Cochrane review calculated a mean difference of −6.9 (95% CI −9.06 to −4.74; 195 men) and graded the certainty low for symptom score, because of performance bias and an interval that crosses the 5-point minimal important difference, and moderate for quality of life.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> A 30% or greater IPSS reduction at 3 months occurred in 106/136 (77.9%) treated men and 21/61 (34.4%) sham men.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The composite serious-complication endpoint occurred in 1/136 treated men, within the 12% performance goal.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> In the treatment arm, IPSS fell from a baseline of 22 to 18.6 at 2 weeks (p = 0.0006) and by 50% or more at 3, 6 and 12 months (p &lt; 0.0001), and peak flow rose by 6.2 mL/s at 3 months and stayed up through 12 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The sham comparison ended at 3 months, so later reports describe treatment-arm and crossover follow-up without a concurrent control. At 24 months the treatment-arm IPSS was 51% below baseline, and 84% and 74% of treated men had a decrease of at least 5 and at least 8 points.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> At 3 years the 50% or greater improvements in IPSS, quality of life, peak flow and BPH Impact Index were maintained and surgical retreatment was 4.4%.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> At 4 years in the original treatment arm, IPSS, quality of life, peak flow and BPH Impact Index had improved by 47%, 43%, 50% and 52% (90 paired men for IPSS, quality of life and BPH Impact Index; 81 for peak flow), with 4.4% surgical retreatment (6/135); the 53 crossover men had 3-year follow-up.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

In the final report, 77 of 135 treated men were evaluated at 5 years. Mean IPSS change was −11.1 ± 7.8 from a baseline of 22.0 (reported as near 48%), the IPSS quality-of-life score fell from 4.4 ± 1.1 to 2.2 ± 1.4 (45%), peak flow rose from 9.9 ± 2.2 to 14.0 ± 5.4 mL/s and the BPH Impact Index was 2.2 ± 1.4. The text gives 49% for peak flow and 45% for the BPH Impact Index, where the abstract gives 44% and 48%. Of the 135 treated men, 82 (61%) were both free from retreatment and had a 30% or greater IPSS reduction at 5 years.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Cumulative surgical retreatment in the treatment arm was 2.2% at 12 months, 3.7% at 24 months and 4.4% (6/135) from 36 months on; 83% of surgical retreatments occurred in the first 2 years and none after year 3. Four of the 6 men had obstructive median tissue that was left untreated at the index procedure. A further 11.1% started BPH medication through 5 years. In the crossover group, 4 men had surgery (3 for previously untreated obstructive median tissue) and 10 started medication.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

No device- or procedure-related de novo erectile dysfunction occurred. In the sexual-function report, IIEF erectile function and MSHQ-EjD function scores in the treatment arm did not differ from control at 3 months or from baseline at 1 year, the ejaculatory bother score improved 31% over baseline (p = 0.0011), and 32% of treated men at 3 months and 27% at 1 year reached the minimal clinically important difference in erectile function.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> At 5 years, IIEF erectile function and MSHQ-EjD function changed by −2.4 ± 9.2 and −2.0 ± 3.9, which the authors attribute to aging of the cohort.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> A post hoc analysis of 125 sexually active men (67 remaining at year 5) found an IIEF erectile-function change of −2.4 ± 8.9 (p = 0.1414) in men without baseline erectile or ejaculatory dysfunction, −3.0 ± 10.1 (p = 0.1259) with a baseline erectile dysfunction history and −4.1 ± 9.2 (p = 0.0127) with a baseline ejaculatory dysfunction history. MSHQ-EjD function fell by −1.6 ± 3.2 (p = 0.0083) in men without baseline dysfunction, and bother scores did not change significantly in any subgroup.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Radiofrequency energy converts sterile water to vapor (103 °C), which a transurethral needle injects into the transition zone of the lateral lobes or into the central zone to treat an obstructive middle lobe. Each injection lasts 9 seconds, and the number of injections depends on prostate size and whether median tissue is treated.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> Treatment-arm men received 4.5 ± 1.8 injections and crossover men 5.1 ± 1.9. The median lobe was treated in 58 of 188 men (30.9%) with 1.6 ± 0.7 additional injections.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Mean procedure time, insertion to removal, was 5.3 ± 3.5 minutes in the treatment arm and 4.4 ± 1.7 minutes in the crossover group. All treatments were done in an office or ambulatory surgery center: oral pain medication in 170 of 188 men (90.4%), a prostate block or epidural in 39 (20.7%) and intravenous sedation in 19 (10.1%).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Of the randomized comparison, 122/135 treated men (90.4%) and 12/61 sham men (19.7%) were catheterized before discharge. Mean catheter time was 3.4 ± 3.2 days (N = 123) with treatment and 0.9 ± 0.8 days (N = 12) with sham, and 83 of the 122 catheterized treated men (68%) were catheterized at physician discretion. Four men with a treated median lobe were recatheterized for retention for an average of 5 days.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Adverse events

Through 3 months, the Cochrane review counted Clavien–Dindo grade III–V events in 7/136 (5.1%) treated men and 0/61 sham men (risk ratio 6.79, 95% CI 0.39–117.00, very low-certainty evidence). Grade I–II events were more frequent with treatment (risk ratio 1.89, 95% CI 1.15–3.11), as was an indwelling catheter (risk ratio 35.58, 95% CI 15.37–82.36).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

In the final report, device- or procedure-related events were dysuria (16.9% of the treatment arm), gross hematuria (12.5%), hematospermia (7.4%), urinary frequency (5.9%), acute urinary retention (4.4%), suspected urinary infection (3.7%) and decreased ejaculatory volume (3.7%); the crossover group had 18.9%, 11.3%, 3.8%, 5.7%, 5.7%, 7.5% and 7.5%. These were mild to moderate and resolved spontaneously or with routine treatment. There were 151 related events in 53 treatment-arm men and 59 in 23 crossover men, no late related events in years 1 to 5 and no withdrawal attributed to a related event.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The manufacturer's table of adjudicated related events through 4 years in treatment and crossover men (data through February 9, 2019) lists dysuria 17%, gross hematuria 12%, urinary retention 5%, decreased ejaculatory volume 5% (33% resolved), anejaculation 3% (none resolved), worsening erectile dysfunction 2% (none resolved), suspected urinary infection 5%, culture-proven infection 3% and urethral stricture 2%. Six serious events occurred in 4 men: prolonged retention from an untreated intravesical lobe, an allergic reaction to a sedative, bladder neck contracture with bladder stones, and urosepsis after cystoscopy. Of treatment and crossover men, 57% had no related event, 80% of events occurred within 30 days and 89% had resolved. Nine of 188 men (5%) sought other treatment within 4 years.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Limitations

The randomized sham comparison ended at 3 months, when 53 of 61 controls crossed over, so the 5-year results are uncontrolled treatment-arm data. The Cochrane review found only short-term data from this one industry-sponsored trial, and the registry names Boston Scientific as the responsible organization.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

Attrition was substantial: 77 of 135 treatment-arm men (57%) were evaluated at 5 years, and follow-up ended at retreatment, so late means describe men who had not been retreated.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The investigators note the absence of urodynamic testing.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The sexual-function analyses were limited to sexually active men, which the Cochrane review rated high risk of bias, and the 5-year analysis was post hoc.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[10]](#ref10)</sup> Eligibility excluded prior prostate intervention, recent retention and prostates over 80 cc.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="rezum" />

The final report compares the 4.4% surgical retreatment with 13.6% in the 5-year L.I.F.T. study and with 5-year surgical retreatment of 14% to 51% for TUNA and 8.9% to 21% for TUMT.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Those are cross-trial figures, and Rezūm II separately counts 11.1% starting BPH medication.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The Cochrane review found no study comparing water vapor therapy with another active treatment; the EAU guideline cites a single-center randomized trial in which bipolar TURP gave greater IPSS, peak flow and postvoid residual improvement at 24 months.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

- **AUA (Management of LUTS Attributed to BPH, 2026), statement 46.** Clinicians should offer water vapor thermal therapy as an option for patients with prostates of 30–80 cc <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>. The supporting text describes IPSS and quality-of-life improvements that remain durable from 3 months through 5 years in appropriately selected patients, and advises that use in prostates up to 150 cc under the expanded FDA indication be reserved for appropriate patients and experienced clinicians.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **EAU (Non-Neurogenic Male LUTS, March 2026).** Water vapor thermal therapy is listed among alternative ablative techniques under investigation without a recommendation; the text summarizes this 197-man trial and a 4.4% surgical retreatment rate over 5 years.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **FDA.** The 510(k) K150786 substantial-equivalence decision (August 27, 2015; letter corrected September 12, 2016) cleared the Rezūm System for men aged 50 or older with a prostate volume of 30–80 cm³, including hyperplasia of the central zone or a median lobe; its summary cites the Rezūm II randomized placebo-controlled trial (136 treatment, 61 control).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> The June 11, 2025 clearance (K250584) indicates the system for men aged 50 or older with a prostate volume of 30–150 cm³.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
