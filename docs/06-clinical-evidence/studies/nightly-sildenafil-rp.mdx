# Padma-Nathan 2008: Nightly Sildenafil After Nerve-Sparing Radical Prostatectomy

<TrialBottomLine id="nightly-sildenafil-rp" />

**Padma-Nathan 2008** was a double-blind randomized trial of nightly sildenafil versus placebo in men with normal preoperative erectile function after bilateral nerve-sparing radical retropubic prostatectomy. Unassisted erectile function after a drug-free period was more frequent among sildenafil recipients who completed assessment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="nightly-sildenafil-rp" />

## Design

The trial randomized 125 men in a 1:1:1 ratio to sildenafil 50 mg, sildenafil 100 mg or placebo. Treatment began four weeks after surgery and continued nightly for 36 weeks, followed by eight weeks without any erectile dysfunction treatment. The primary endpoint required a combined score of at least eight on International Index of Erectile Function questions 3 and 4, which assess penetration and maintenance after penetration, plus a positive answer that erections were adequate for satisfactory sexual activity.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Neither planned dose comparison met the Bonferroni-adjusted primary threshold: spontaneous erectile function at week 48 occurred in 6/23 completers (26%; 95% CI 10–48) with 50 mg and 8/28 (29%; 95% CI 13–49) with 100 mg versus 1/25 (4%; 95% CI 0–20) with placebo; nominal Fisher P values were 0.04 and 0.03, respectively (adjusted alpha 0.025). Pooled across doses, 14/51 sildenafil completers (27%; 95% CI 16–42) responded versus 1/25 placebo completers (4%; 95% CI 0–20; P = 0.0156). The mean end-of-study IIEF erectile-function domain score was 13.1 ± 9.5 with sildenafil and 8.8 ± 7.0 with placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Nocturnal rigidity recovered from the postoperative nadir in the 54-man plethysmography subanalysis: by week 48, the 100-mg group reached 36% of preoperative base-rigidity duration and 65% of tip-rigidity duration, while placebo showed little improvement. Tip rigidity at or above 55% separated sildenafil responders from nonresponders more clearly than base rigidity. Week-48 unassisted responders numbered 4/17 (24%) with 50 mg, 6/18 (33%) with 100 mg, and 1/19 (5%) with placebo.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The operation was bilateral nerve-sparing radical retropubic prostatectomy. Experienced surgeons used the standard Walsh open technique; operative and pathology reports were checked to confirm bilateral nerve sparing, and no laparoscopic procedures were included.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Adverse events occurred in 32/40 men (80%) receiving 50 mg, 35/41 (85%) receiving 100 mg, and 26/42 (62%) receiving placebo. Headache occurred in 6/40 and 5/41 sildenafil recipients versus 3/42 placebo recipients; dyspepsia occurred in 7/40 and 7/41 versus 0/42. Serious adverse events affected 2/40, 1/41 and 3/42 men, respectively; none was considered treatment related. Adverse events led to discontinuation in 5/40, 1/41 and 1/42 men, respectively, including two 50-mg recipients with treatment-related symptoms.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Enrollment stopped after a blinded interim review of 35 men showed fewer responders than expected. Of 125 randomized men, 123 received treatment, 82 completed 36 weeks of treatment, and 76 completed the drug-free assessment used for the primary efficacy analysis. The nocturnal-rigidity analysis included 54 of these completers.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA Erectile Dysfunction guideline (2018) advises informing men seeking erectile preservation after radical prostatectomy or radiotherapy that early PDE5 inhibitor use may not improve spontaneous, unassisted erectile function <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
