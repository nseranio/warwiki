# Botox versus solifenacin: OnabotulinumtoxinA or Solifenacin Compared with Placebo in Refractory Overactive Bladder

<TrialBottomLine id="botox-vs-solifenacin" />

**Botox versus solifenacin** was a multicenter, double-blind phase 3b randomized trial in 356 solifenacin-naïve adults with urinary incontinence after an inadequate response to or intolerance of an anticholinergic; onabotulinumtoxinA 100 U and solifenacin 5–10 mg each reduced incontinence more than placebo at 12 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="botox-vs-solifenacin" />

## Design

Participants were assigned to onabotulinumtoxinA (145), solifenacin (151), or placebo (60). Active and sham bladder injections were paired with active or placebo capsules to maintain blinding. The coprimary endpoints were change in daily urinary incontinence episodes and complete continence at week 12. The onabotulinumtoxinA–solifenacin comparison was post hoc.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

Mean daily incontinence episodes changed by −3.19 with onabotulinumtoxinA, −2.56 with solifenacin, and −1.33 with placebo (both active comparisons P &lt; 0.001). Complete continence occurred in 33.8% (49/145), 24.5% (37/151), and 11.7% (7/60), respectively (P &lt; 0.001 and P = 0.028 versus placebo).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The post hoc direct comparison of episode reduction favored onabotulinumtoxinA (P = 0.022).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

OnabotulinumtoxinA 100 U or saline placebo was injected intradetrusor on day 1.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Solifenacin began at 5 mg daily and could increase to 10 mg at week 6.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Patients meeting retreatment criteria could request open-label onabotulinumtoxinA after at least 12 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Urinary tract infection occurred in 25.5% (37/145) with onabotulinumtoxinA, 10.2% (15/147) with solifenacin, and 10.0% (6/60) with placebo.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Urinary retention occurred in 6.9% (10/145), 0.7% (1/147), and 0% (0/60), respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The direct active-treatment comparison was post hoc. The primary blinded comparison ended at week 12, and treatment 2 was open-label onabotulinumtoxinA in eligible patients from every initial arm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

AUA/SUFU 2024 recommends offering sacral neuromodulation, percutaneous tibial nerve stimulation, and/or intradetrusor botulinum toxin injection when OAB responds inadequately to pharmacotherapy or behavioral therapy, or when those therapies cause intolerable effects <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade A)</Rec>. It also recommends measuring post-void residual before injection <Rec>(Clinical Principle)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
