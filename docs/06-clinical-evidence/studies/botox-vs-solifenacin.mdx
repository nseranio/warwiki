# Botox versus solifenacin: OnabotulinumtoxinA or Solifenacin Compared with Placebo in Refractory Overactive Bladder

<TrialBottomLine id="botox-vs-solifenacin" />

**Botox versus solifenacin** was a multicenter, double-blind phase 3b randomized trial in 356 solifenacin-naïve adults with urinary incontinence after an inadequate response to or intolerance of an anticholinergic; onabotulinumtoxinA 100 U and solifenacin 5–10 mg each reduced incontinence more than placebo at 12 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="botox-vs-solifenacin" />

## Design

Participants were assigned to onabotulinumtoxinA (145), solifenacin (151), or placebo (60). Active and sham bladder injections were paired with active or placebo capsules to maintain blinding. The coprimary endpoints were change in daily urinary incontinence episodes and complete continence at week 12. Efficacy analyses used the intent-to-treat population; statistical comparisons were not adjusted for multiplicity. The onabotulinumtoxinA–solifenacin comparison was post hoc.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

At treatment-1 week 12, least squares mean daily incontinence episodes changed by −3.19 with onabotulinumtoxinA (n=145), −2.56 with solifenacin (n=151), and −1.33 with placebo (n=60; both active comparisons P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Complete continence occurred in 33.8% (49/145), 24.5% (37/151), and 11.7% (7/60), respectively (P &lt; 0.001 and P = 0.028 versus placebo).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The post hoc direct comparison of episode reduction favored onabotulinumtoxinA (P = 0.022).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Daily micturitions also fell at treatment-1 week 12: least squares mean changes were −2.3 (95% CI −2.7 to −1.9) with onabotulinumtoxinA, −2.1 (95% CI −2.5 to −1.7) with solifenacin, and −1.1 (95% CI −1.7 to −0.4) with placebo (P = 0.002 and P = 0.007 for active arms versus placebo).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Among patients evaluable 12 weeks after open-label treatment 2 with onabotulinumtoxinA, complete continence occurred in 43.2% (32/74), 37.6% (32/85), and 41.9% (18/43) from the original onabotulinumtoxinA, solifenacin, and placebo arms, respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

OnabotulinumtoxinA 100 U in 10 mL or saline placebo was injected into the detrusor on day 1, avoiding the trigone.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Solifenacin began at 5 mg daily; 48/151 patients (31.8%) escalated to 10 mg at week 6.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> At weeks 12, 18 or 24, patients could request open-label onabotulinumtoxinA 100 U if at least 12 weeks had elapsed since injection, they had at least two urgency incontinence episodes per day, at most one incontinence-free day in a 3-day diary, and post-void residual below 200 mL; 213/356 (59.8%) received treatment 2 (78/145, 90/151 and 45/60 by original arm).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

During the 24-week first treatment cycle, urinary tract infection occurred in 25.5% (37/145) with onabotulinumtoxinA, 10.2% (15/147) with solifenacin, and 10.0% (6/60) with placebo; the trial defined infection by urine culture and leukocyturia regardless of symptoms.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Symptomatic infection occurred in 8.3% (12/145), 1.4% (2/147), and 3.3% (2/60), respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Urinary retention occurred in 6.9% (10/145), 0.7% (1/147), and 0% (0/60), respectively; retention required post-void residual at least 350 mL, or 200–349 mL with symptoms requiring catheterization.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Clean intermittent catheterization was used by 6.2% (9/145), 0.7% (1/147), and 0% (0/60), respectively; dry mouth occurred in 2.8% (4/145), 8.2% (12/147), and 0% (0/60).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The direct active-treatment comparison was post hoc and not powered to detect a difference. The primary blinded comparison ended at week 12, and treatment 2 was open-label onabotulinumtoxinA in eligible patients from every initial arm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

AUA/SUFU 2024 recommends offering sacral neuromodulation, percutaneous tibial nerve stimulation, and/or intradetrusor botulinum toxin injection when OAB responds inadequately to pharmacotherapy or behavioral therapy, or when those therapies cause intolerable effects <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade A)</Rec>. It also recommends measuring post-void residual before injection <Rec>(Clinical Principle)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
