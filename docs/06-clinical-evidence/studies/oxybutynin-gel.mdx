# Oxybutynin topical gel (OG05009): Once-daily 10% gel for overactive bladder

<TrialBottomLine id="oxybutynin-gel" />

**OG05009** was a multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled randomized trial of once-daily oxybutynin chloride 10% topical gel versus placebo gel in adults with urgency-predominant urinary incontinence. Oxybutynin gel produced a greater reduction in daily urinary incontinence episodes over 12 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="oxybutynin-gel" />

## Design

Investigators at 76 US clinics randomized 789 adults, including 704 women (89.2%); the mean age was 59 years. Eligibility required at least 8 voids and 4 urge incontinence episodes daily, mean voided volume of 350 mL or less, and postvoid residual of 250 mL or less. Participants received 1 g of the ethanol-based gel or matching placebo once daily. Three-day urinary diaries supplied the primary endpoint, change from baseline in daily urinary incontinence episodes, including episodes attributable to urgency and/or stress. The modified intent-to-treat analysis included all 789 randomized participants and used ANCOVA with last observation carried forward at study end.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Mean daily urinary incontinence episodes changed from 5.4 to 2.4 among 389 oxybutynin gel recipients and from 5.4 to 2.9 among 400 placebo recipients (changes −3.0 versus −2.5 episodes/day; P &lt; 0.0001), meeting the trial's primary efficacy comparison. Mean daily urinary frequency changed by −2.7 versus −2.0 voids (P = 0.0017), and mean voided volume increased by 21.0 versus 3.8 mL (P = 0.0018). Nocturia episodes changed by −0.75 versus −0.65 per day (P = 0.1372). Complete urinary continence on the study-end diary occurred in 108/389 (27.8%) versus 69/400 (17.3%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Gel was applied daily to rotating sites on the abdomen, upper arm or shoulder, and thigh. Study completion was 346/389 (88.9%) with oxybutynin gel and 355/400 (88.8%) with placebo; sachet-count compliance was 90.7% and 91.2%, respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Any adverse event occurred in 221/389 oxybutynin recipients (56.8%) and 193/400 placebo recipients (48.3%); treatment-related events occurred in 73/389 (18.8%) and 45/400 (11.3%). Treatment-related dry mouth occurred in 27/389 (6.9%) and 11/400 (2.8%), respectively (P = 0.0060). Application-site reactions occurred in 21/389 (5.4%) and 4/400 (1.0%), leading to withdrawal in 3/389 (0.8%) and 1/400 (0.3%). Serious adverse events occurred in 7/389 (1.8%) and 10/400 (2.5%); none were treatment related. Treatment-related urinary retention occurred in 0/389 and 1/400 (0.3%); one participant in each arm had postvoid residual of at least 250 mL at study end.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The randomized treatment period was 12 weeks, and the report's efficacy analysis carried forward the last observed diary measurements for missing data.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The FDA's January 2009 Gelnique 10% label indicates treatment of overactive bladder with urge urinary incontinence, urgency, and frequency. It directs application of one 1-g sachet daily to intact skin on the abdomen, upper arms or shoulders, or thighs, rotating sites; urinary retention is a contraindication. The label also advises covering the dried application site when skin contact is expected and washing hands after application to reduce transfer to another person.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
