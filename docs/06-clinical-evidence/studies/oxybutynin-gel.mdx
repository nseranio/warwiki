# Oxybutynin topical gel (OG05009): Once-daily 10% gel for overactive bladder

<TrialBottomLine id="oxybutynin-gel" />

**OG05009** was a multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled randomized trial of once-daily oxybutynin chloride 10% topical gel versus placebo gel in adults with urgency-predominant urinary incontinence. Oxybutynin gel produced a greater reduction in daily urge incontinence episodes over 12 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="oxybutynin-gel" />

## Design

Investigators at 76 US clinics randomized 789 adults, including 704 women (89.2%); the mean age was 59 years. Participants received 1 g of the ethanol-based gel or matching placebo once daily. Three-day urinary diaries supplied the primary endpoint, change from baseline in daily urge incontinence episodes. The analysis used ANCOVA with last observation carried forward.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Mean daily urge incontinence episodes changed by −3.0 with oxybutynin gel and −2.5 with placebo (P &lt; 0.0001), meeting the trial's primary efficacy comparison. Mean daily urinary frequency changed by −2.7 versus −2.0 voids (P = 0.0017), and mean voided volume increased by 21.0 versus 3.8 mL (P = 0.0018).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Treatment-related dry mouth occurred in 27/389 oxybutynin recipients (6.9%) and 11/400 placebo recipients (2.8%). Application-site reactions occurred in 21/389 (5.4%) and 4/400 (1.0%), respectively. The investigators reported no serious treatment-related adverse events.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The randomized treatment period was 12 weeks, and the report's efficacy analysis carried forward the last observed diary measurements for missing data.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The FDA's January 2009 Gelnique 10% label indicates treatment of overactive bladder with urge urinary incontinence, urgency, and frequency. It directs application of one 1-g sachet daily to intact skin on the abdomen, upper arms or shoulders, or thighs, rotating sites; urinary retention is a contraindication. The label also advises covering the dried application site when skin contact is expected and washing hands after application to reduce transfer to another person.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
