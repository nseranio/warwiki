# Vagifem 10 µg: Ultra-Low-Dose Estradiol Vaginal Tablets for Vaginal Atrophy

<TrialBottomLine id="vagifem-10" />

In a multicenter, double-blind randomized trial of postmenopausal women with vaginal atrophy, 10-µg vaginal estradiol tablets improved vaginal cytology, vaginal pH grade, and the most bothersome urogenital symptom score more than placebo tablets at week 12.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="vagifem-10" />

## Design

The trial randomized 309 women aged at least 45 years with an intact uterus, at least three urogenital symptoms (one moderate to severe), vaginal pH above 5, and endometrial thickness below 4 mm: 205 received estradiol and 104 received matching placebo. Its week-12 primary efficacy domains were vaginal cytology, vaginal pH, and change in each participant’s most bothersome urogenital symptom score. Vaginal health grading was a secondary efficacy assessment. Week-12 and week-52 efficacy analyses used intention to treat with last observation carried forward. Endometrial biopsies, examinations, and adverse-event recording were safety assessments.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At week 12, vaginal cytology favored estradiol: changes in parabasal, superficial, and intermediate cells were −37% versus −9%, +13% versus +4%, and +24% versus +5% with placebo. Adjusted between-group differences were −29 percentage points (95% CI −34.6 to −22.8), +10 (6.2 to 13.8), and +19 (12.3 to 25.0), respectively (P &lt; 0.001 each). Vaginal Maturation Value changed by +25.0 versus +6.5, an adjusted difference of 19.3 (95% CI 15.5 to 23.2; P &lt; 0.001). Vaginal pH **grade** changed by −1.3 versus −0.4 (P &lt; 0.001), and pH was below 5.5 in 145/202 (71.8%) versus 37/102 (36.3%). The most bothersome symptom score changed by −1.23 versus −0.87, an adjusted difference of −0.33 (95% CI −0.55 to −0.12; P = 0.003). Vaginal health grade, a secondary assessment, changed by −0.91 versus −0.51 (P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The cytology and pH comparisons differed from placebo after 2 weeks. For the most bothersome symptom, the treatment effect appeared after 4 weeks and reached statistical significance at week 8. The treatment effects remained statistically significant at week 52.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Estradiol 10 µg or placebo was inserted vaginally once daily for 14 days, then twice weekly through week 52. Completion was 164/205 (80.0%) with estradiol and 70/104 (67.3%) with placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Adverse events occurred in 158/205 (77.1%) estradiol recipients and 77/103 exposed placebo recipients (74.8%); serious events occurred in 5/205 (2.4%) and 2/103 (1.9%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Adverse events led to withdrawal in 11/205 (5.4%) estradiol recipients and 5/104 (4.8%) randomized placebo recipients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Pooled endometrial hyperplasia or carcinoma incidence was 2/386 (0.52%; two-sided 95% CI 0.06–1.86%) among 541 estradiol recipients: 205 from this randomized trial and 336 from a separate open-label safety trial. The one-sided 95% CI upper bound of 1.62% was below the paper’s stated FDA threshold of 4%, and the two-sided 95% CI upper bound of 1.86% was below its stated EMA threshold of 2%. Of the 541 treated women, 456 (84.3%) completed their studies and 443 underwent biopsy at week 52; the incidence analysis counted events during treatment regardless of duration. The original trial contributed 0/50 evaluable placebo biopsies. One event was complex hyperplasia without atypia after 9 days of exposure; the other was grade-2, stage-1B endometrioid adenocarcinoma after 324 days of exposure, with no evaluable screening biopsy. Mean endometrial thickness at week 52 was 2.13 mm (n=456) with estradiol and 2.21 mm (n=68) with placebo (P = 0.921 for change from baseline).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The primary efficacy analysis used cytologic and pH measures alongside a symptom score. The 52-week endometrial analysis combined randomized and open-label active-treatment cohorts, and 386 biopsies were evaluable for its hyperplasia or carcinoma endpoint.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA/SUFU/AUGS 2025 GSM guideline recommends offering local low-dose vaginal estrogen for vulvovaginal discomfort or irritation, dryness, and/or dyspareunia <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The FDA approved the 10-µg Vagifem vaginal tablet on November 25, 2009, for atrophic vaginitis due to menopause.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
