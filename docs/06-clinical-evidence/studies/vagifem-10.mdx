# Vagifem 10 µg: Ultra-Low-Dose Estradiol Vaginal Tablets for Vaginal Atrophy

<TrialBottomLine id="vagifem-10" />

In a multicenter, double-blind randomized trial of postmenopausal women with vaginal atrophy, 10-µg vaginal estradiol tablets improved vaginal cytology, vaginal pH grade, and the most bothersome urogenital symptom score more than placebo tablets at week 12.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="vagifem-10" />

## Design

The trial randomized 309 women to estradiol or matching placebo vaginal tablets for 52 weeks. Its week-12 primary efficacy domains were vaginal cytology, vaginal pH, and change in each participant’s most bothersome urogenital symptom score. Vaginal health grading was a secondary efficacy assessment. Endometrial biopsies, examinations, and adverse-event recording were safety assessments.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At week 12, the estradiol and placebo groups had changes of −37% and −9% in parabasal cells, +13% and +4% in superficial cells, and +24% and +5% in intermediate cells (P &lt; 0.001 for each comparison). Vaginal Maturation Value changed by +25.0 and +6.5 (P &lt; 0.001). Vaginal pH **grade** changed by −1.3 and −0.4 (P &lt; 0.001); the most bothersome symptom score changed by −1.23 and −0.87 (P = 0.003). These primary efficacy comparisons favored estradiol. Vaginal health grade, a secondary assessment, changed by −0.91 and −0.51 (P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The cytology and pH comparisons differed from placebo after 2 weeks. For the most bothersome symptom, the treatment effect appeared after 4 weeks and reached statistical significance at week 8. The abstract reports that treatment effects remained statistically significant at week 52.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

A later endometrial analysis pooled 205 estradiol recipients from this randomized trial with 336 women from a separate open-label safety trial. Of 541 treated women, 456 completed their studies, 443 underwent biopsy at week 52, and 386 had samples evaluable for hyperplasia or carcinoma. Two such events occurred (2/386, 0.52%): one complex hyperplasia without atypia after 9 days of exposure and one grade-2 endometrioid adenocarcinoma for which a baseline biopsy was not evaluable.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The primary efficacy analysis used cytologic and pH measures alongside a symptom score. The 52-week endometrial analysis combined randomized and open-label active-treatment cohorts, and 386 biopsies were evaluable for its hyperplasia or carcinoma endpoint.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA/SUFU/AUGS 2025 GSM guideline recommends offering local low-dose vaginal estrogen for vulvovaginal discomfort or irritation, dryness, and/or dyspareunia <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The FDA approved the 10-µg Vagifem vaginal tablet on November 25, 2009, for atrophic vaginitis due to menopause.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
