# LIFE (Eclipse): Vaginal Bowel-Control System for Fecal Incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="eclipse-pivotal" />

**LIFE** was a multicenter, open-label, single-arm study of women with at least four fecal incontinence episodes in two weeks that compared baseline leakage with one month of Eclipse vaginal bowel-control treatment; 48/61 successfully fitted women (78.7%) had at least 50% fewer episodes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="eclipse-pivotal" />

## Design

Women underwent device fitting at six clinical sites. The primary response definition was at least a 50% reduction in incontinence episodes at one month. The intention-to-treat analysis started with the 61 women who were fitted and entered treatment; the per-protocol analysis required a valid one-month diary. An optional two-month extension followed. The abstract reports a significance test but does not specify its prespecified performance threshold.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The per-protocol response was 48/56 (85.7%). Among 44 women assessed after the optional extension, 38 (86.4%; 95% CI 73–95%) met the same episode-response definition at three months. At one month, 48/56 (85.7%) rated bowel symptoms “much better” or “very much better.”<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In a secondary analysis of 56 evaluable women, the proportion of bowel movements accompanied by urgency fell from 54% at baseline to 26% at one month, and the proportion with liquid stool (Bristol 6 or 7) fell from 36% to 21%.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> A fitting analysis associated prior prolapse surgery and shorter vaginal length with unsuccessful fit; women without prior prolapse surgery had 4.7 times the odds of successful fitting (95% CI 1.53–14.53), and the odds increased 1.49-fold per additional centimeter of vaginal length (95% CI 1.02–2.17).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Eclipse consists of a vaginal insert and pressure-regulated pump. Of 110 women who underwent attempted fitting, 61 (55.5%) entered treatment; 44 had a three-month assessment after the optional extension.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

No serious adverse events were reported. Device-related pelvic cramping or discomfort occurred in 25/110 women (22.7%); 16/25 such events (64%) arose during fitting.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The study had no control group. Its treated-population response excludes the 49 women who could not be fitted, and the three-month assessment included 44 women after an optional extension.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

ASCRS 2023 states that vaginal mechanical inserts are not routinely recommended for fecal incontinence <Rec>(Conditional; Very low)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> On February 12, 2015, the FDA granted De Novo classification of the Eclipse System as a class II prescription rectal-control device indicated for fecal incontinence in adult women.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
