# LIFE (Eclipse): Vaginal Bowel-Control System for Fecal Incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="eclipse-pivotal" />

**LIFE** was a multicenter, open-label, single-arm study of women with at least four minor or major fecal incontinence episodes in two weeks that compared baseline leakage with one month of Eclipse vaginal bowel-control treatment; 48/61 women entering treatment after fitting (78.7%; 95% CI 66–88%) had at least 50% fewer episodes (P&lt;.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="eclipse-pivotal" />

## Design

Women aged 19–75 years with fecal incontinence for at least six months underwent device fitting at six clinical sites. The primary response definition was at least a 50% reduction in incontinence episodes at one month, tested against a prespecified success rate of 40% using a one-sided α of 0.025. The intention-to-treat analysis started with the 61 women who were fitted and entered treatment; the per-protocol supporting analysis required at least seven days of one-month diary data. An optional two-month extension followed.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The primary one-month response exceeded the prespecified 40% threshold; the per-protocol response was 48/56 (85.7%; 95% CI 74–94%; P&lt;.001). Among per-protocol women, 23/56 (41.1%; 95% CI 28–55%) had complete continence, and mean episodes per two weeks fell from 11.6 ± 9.5 to 2.1 ± 2.9 (P&lt;.001). At one month, 48/56 (85.7%) rated bowel symptoms “much better” or “very much better.” Among 44 women with analyzable diaries after the optional extension, 38 (86.4%; 95% CI 73–95%) met the episode-response definition at three months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In a secondary analysis of 56 evaluable women, the mean within-patient proportion of bowel movements accompanied by urgency fell from 54% at baseline to 26% at one month (P&lt;.0001), and the proportion with incomplete evacuation fell from 39% to 26% (P=.0034).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Liquid stool (Bristol 6 or 7) fell from 36% to 21% among 55 women with one-month Bristol data (P=.0001); 8/12 women with more than two bowel movements daily at baseline shifted to two or fewer.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> A fitting analysis associated prior prolapse surgery and shorter vaginal length with unsuccessful fit; prior prolapse surgery was present in 5/61 successfully fitted versus 16/49 unsuccessfully fitted women, and mean vaginal length was 8.6 ± 1.2 versus 8.0 ± 1.1 cm.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Women without prior prolapse surgery had 4.7 times the adjusted odds of successful fitting (95% CI 1.53–14.53; P=.007), and the odds increased 1.49-fold per additional centimeter of vaginal length (95% CI 1.02–2.17; P=.041).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Eclipse consists of a vaginal insert and pressure-regulated pump; users deflated the insert for defecation and were encouraged to remove it weekly for cleaning. Of 110 women who entered fitting, 61 (55.5%) entered treatment and 49 did not: 28 had an unsatisfactory fit, four could not self-manage the insert, one was lost to follow-up, two had logistical or timing constraints, and 14 withdrew.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Among 56 per-protocol women, 54 (96.4%) reported that the insert was comfortable or could not be felt.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Forty-eight entered the optional extension and 44 had analyzable three-month diaries.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The fitting analysis describes 13 round or elliptical base sizes and assessment of comfort, pubic-bone position, retention on Valsalva and rectal occlusion with the balloon inflated. Up to four fitting visits were allowed; 68/110 (61.8%) needed one. Among 49 women who did not complete fitting, 16 failed initial attempts, 29 later reported displacement or discomfort, and four withdrew.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Adverse events

No serious device-related adverse events were reported. During fitting, 47/110 women (42.7%) had 64 mild or moderate device-related events, including urinary incontinence, urgency, or frequency in 11/110 (10.0%) and vaginal erythema, petechiae, abrasion, irritation, or bruising in 10/110 (9.1%). Device-related pelvic cramping or discomfort occurred in 25/110 women (22.7%); 16/25 (64%) arose during fitting.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The study had no control group. Its treated-population response excludes the 49 women who did not enter treatment after fitting assessment, and the three-month assessment included 44 women after an optional extension.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

ASCRS 2023 states that vaginal mechanical inserts are not routinely recommended for fecal incontinence <Rec>(Conditional; Very low)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> On February 12, 2015, the FDA granted De Novo classification of the Eclipse System as a class II prescription rectal-control device indicated for fecal incontinence in adult women.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
