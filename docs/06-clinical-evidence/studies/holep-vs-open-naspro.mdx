# Naspro 2006: Holmium Laser Enucleation Versus Open Prostatectomy for Prostates &gt;70 g

<TrialBottomLine id="holep-vs-open-naspro" />

Naspro and colleagues randomized 80 men with obstructive symptoms and prostates &gt;70 g to holmium laser enucleation of the prostate (HoLEP) or transvesical open prostatectomy. Symptom scores and peak flow were similar between groups at 24 months; HoLEP shortened catheterization and hospital stay and reduced hemoglobin loss, while open surgery took less operating-room time.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="holep-vs-open-naspro" />

## Design

The Milan group enrolled consecutive patients from March 2003 through December 2004, allocating 41 to HoLEP and 39 to transvesical open prostatectomy by computer-generated randomization. Men had failed drug therapy, had residual urine &lt;150 mL, peak flow &lt;15 mL/s, and urodynamic obstruction (Schäfer grade &gt;2); none had an indwelling catheter at baseline. Baseline testing included symptom, quality-of-life, and erectile-function questionnaires, uroflowmetry, and pressure-flow testing. Follow-up visits were scheduled at 1, 3, 12, and 24 months, with repeat complete urodynamics at 12 months only. The paper did not designate a primary endpoint; its comparisons used a two-sided P &lt;0.05 threshold.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Symptom scores and flow improved after both operations: at 24 months, mean IPSS was 7.9 ± 6.2 after HoLEP versus 8.1 ± 7.1 after open surgery (P = 0.44), and mean peak flow was 19.19 ± 6.3 versus 20.11 ± 8.8 mL/s (P = 0.91). At 12 months, detrusor pressure at maximum flow fell from 80.6 to 30.6 cm H2O with HoLEP and from 83.1 to 34.8 cm H2O with open surgery (between-arm P = 0.66); Schäfer grade fell from 3.8 to 0.7 versus 3.1 to 0.8 (P = 0.18). Thirty-five HoLEP and 30 open-surgery patients were eligible for 24-month follow-up; the tables do not give the number tested for each functional measure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Catheterization and hospital stay were shorter after HoLEP: mean catheterization was 1.5 ± 1.07 versus 4.1 ± 0.5 days, and mean stay was 2.7 ± 1.1 versus 5.43 ± 1.05 days (both P &lt;0.0001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Open surgery took less operating-room time: 58.31 ± 11.95 versus 72.09 ± 21.22 minutes for HoLEP (P &lt;0.0001). HoLEP enucleated the lobes and morcellated the tissue in the bladder; the comparator used a transvesical approach. Resected specimen weight was 59.33 ± 34.77 versus 87.90 ± 41.11 g (P = 0.0046). Two senior urologists performed all procedures after the center had passed its HoLEP learning curve of more than 200 cases.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Hemoglobin loss was lower after HoLEP: the mean drop was 2.12 ± 1.48 versus 3.15 ± 1.33 g/dL (P = 0.0073). Autologous transfusion occurred in 2/41 (4.9%) versus 5/39 (12.8%), and homologous transfusion in 0/41 versus 2/39 (5.1%). Early dysuria occurred in 28/41 (68.3%) versus 16/39 (41.0%; P &lt;0.001), acute retention in 5/41 (12.2%) versus 2/39 (5.1%; P = 0.11), bladder mucosal injury in 3/41 (7.3%) versus 0/39, and stress incontinence in 1/41 (2.4%) versus 1/39 (2.6%). All retention episodes were managed by temporary recatheterization.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Reintervention for early bleeding occurred in 1/41 HoLEP patients and 0/39 open-surgery patients. At 12 months, each arm had two bladder-neck or urethral strictures and two overall reinterventions; at 24 months, one stricture was reported in each arm. The paper did not grade complications by Clavien-Dindo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The trial enrolled 80 men at one center and did not designate a primary endpoint. Only 35/41 HoLEP patients and 30/39 open-surgery patients were eligible for 24-month follow-up; the functional tables do not give assessment-specific denominators or confidence intervals.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU 2026 male LUTS guideline includes this trial among its open-prostatectomy comparisons and recommends offering HoLEP to men with moderate-to-severe LUTS and benign prostatic obstruction as an alternative to TURP or open prostatectomy <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
