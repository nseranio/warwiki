# Maher 2004: Abdominal Sacral Colpopexy or Vaginal Sacrospinous Colpopexy for Vault Prolapse

<TrialBottomLine id="maher-2004" />

**Maher 2004** randomized 95 women with vaginal vault prolapse to abdominal sacral colpopexy or vaginal sacrospinous colpopexy; at approximately two years, reported subjective success was 94% versus 91% and objective success was 76% versus 69%, without a statistically significant between-group difference.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="maher-2004" />

## Design

The prospective study allocated 47 women to the abdominal operation and 48 to vaginal sacrospinous colpopexy. Its stated primary outcomes were subjective, objective, and patient-determined success. Secondary outcomes included bowel, bladder, and sexual function, cost, and quality of life. The abdominal procedure used a sacral route of vault suspension, whereas the comparison used vaginal sacrospinous fixation. Operating time, return to daily activities, and cost were assessed alongside prolapse success, placing perioperative burden and the primary success measures in the same comparison.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Subjective success was 94% after abdominal surgery and 91% after vaginal surgery (P = .19). Objective success was 76% and 69%, respectively (P = .48). Postoperative assessments spanned 6–60 months. Quality of life improved after both operations (P &lt; .05 for the reported within-group changes).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The abdominal sacral colpopexy took longer, was followed by a slower return to daily activities, and cost more than vaginal sacrospinous colpopexy (P &lt; .01 for the reported comparison).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

With 95 randomized women and postoperative assessment times ranging from 6 to 60 months, the study provides imprecise comparative estimates at a nonuniform follow-up interval. Women assessed near 6 months and women assessed near 60 months contributed to the reported postoperative success rates. The nonsignificant between-group comparisons leave comparative success uncertain for this patient group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
