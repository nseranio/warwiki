# Maher 2004: Abdominal Sacral Colpopexy or Vaginal Sacrospinous Colpopexy for Vault Prolapse

<TrialBottomLine id="maher-2004" />

**Maher 2004** randomized 95 women with symptomatic posthysterectomy vaginal vault prolapse to abdominal sacral colpopexy or vaginal sacrospinous colpopexy. At mean follow-up of 24 and 22 months, subjective success was 43/46 (94%) versus 39/43 (91%) and objective success was 35/46 (76%) versus 29/42 (69%); neither difference was statistically significant.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="maher-2004" />

## Design

The prospective study allocated 47 women to abdominal repair and 48 to vaginal repair. Eligible women had prolapse reaching or passing the introitus; previous sacral colpopexy or a markedly foreshortened vagina excluded enrollment. The primary outcomes were subjective success (no prolapse symptoms), objective success (no grade 2 or higher prolapse at any vaginal site on Valsalva), and patient-determined success. Secondary outcomes included bowel, bladder, and sexual function, cost, and quality of life. The investigators used a two-sided 5% significance threshold for between-group comparisons but specified no equivalence margin.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The primary success comparisons showed no significant between-group difference: subjective success was 43/46 (94%) after abdominal repair versus 39/43 (91%) after vaginal repair (P = .19); objective success was 35/46 (76%) versus 29/42 (69%) (P = .46); and patient-determined satisfaction was reported as 39/46 (85%) versus 35/43 (81%) (P = .78). Follow-up averaged 24 months (range 6–60) and 22 months (range 6–58), respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Anterior wall or vault prolapse occurred in 6/46 (13%) after abdominal repair versus 19/42 (45%) after vaginal repair (P = .01). Urinary symptom and incontinence impact scores improved within both groups (P &lt; .05); adjusted between-group changes in measured SF-36 domains were nonsignificant.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Abdominal repair used Prolene mesh from the vault to the sacral promontory, extended anteriorly to the bladder trigone and posteriorly 7–8 cm along the vagina; vaginal repair used unilateral sacrospinous fixation. Concomitant Burch colposuspension was performed in 14/47 and 15/48 women, respectively. Posterior colporrhaphy was performed in 11/47 and 44/48, paravaginal repair in 19/47 abdominal cases, and enterocele repair in 28/48 vaginal cases.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Abdominal repair had a longer mean operating time (106 versus 76 minutes), a longer mean return to daily activities (34.0 versus 25.7 days), and a higher mean cost (A$6450 versus A$4575); each comparison had P &lt; .01.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

De novo stress urinary incontinence occurred in 2/22 (9%) abdominal versus 8/24 (33%) vaginal patients without preoperative incontinence (P = .09); new voiding dysfunction occurred in one woman in each group. The abdominal group had one transfusion, one cystotomy, one mesh rejection, one infected-mesh removal, one wound infection, and two incisional hernia repairs. The vaginal group had one transfusion, one cystotomy, one rectovaginal hematoma, and one case of vaginal pain. Subsequent surgery was reported for six abdominal and seven vaginal patients, including two Fenton repairs in the vaginal group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Six randomized women did not complete at least six months of follow-up, and the postoperative assessment interval varied from 6 to 60 months. The trial was powered for a larger difference in success than the observed between-group differences.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
