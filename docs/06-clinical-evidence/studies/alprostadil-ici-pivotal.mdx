# Intracavernosal alprostadil: Dose response and self-injection for erectile dysfunction

<TrialBottomLine id="alprostadil-ici-pivotal" />

Intracavernosal alprostadil at 2.5–20 µg produced more clinical and RigiScan erectile responses than placebo in a randomized, double-blind study of 296 men with erectile dysfunction; none of 59 placebo recipients responded. In a separate, open-label six-month study of 683 men, sexual activity was reported possible after 94% of injections.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="alprostadil-ici-pivotal" />

## Design

The report combines a double-blind, placebo-controlled dose-response study, a single-blind 201-man dose-finding study, and an open-label 683-man self-injection study. Erectile dysfunction had vasculogenic, neurogenic, psychogenic, or mixed causes; 12 dose-finding and 40 home-use participants had also joined the dose-response study. Clinical and RigiScan erectile responses and patients' and partners' assessments of sexual activity were the primary efficacy measures. RigiScan response required at least 70% rigidity for at least 10 minutes; the dose-response analysis included men who received at least one injection. Home-use outcomes were recorded per injection.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Erectile response increased with dose in the 296-man placebo-controlled study: alprostadil was given at 2.5 µg (57 men), 5 µg (60), 10 µg (62), or 20 µg (58), versus placebo (59). None of the placebo recipients had a clinical or RigiScan response; every dose exceeded placebo on both assessments (pairwise P &lt; 0.01 or P &lt; 0.001), and the clinical dose-response trend had P &lt; 0.001.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Among 135 completers of the 201-man dose-finding study, a minimal effective dose of ≤2 µg was found in 7/31 (23%) with neurogenic, 10/50 (20%) with vasculogenic, 9/24 (38%) with psychogenic, and 7/30 (23%) with mixed erectile dysfunction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Satisfactory sexual activity was reported after 11,924/13,762 (87%) home injections with men's ratings, including 11,223 followed by intercourse and 701 by masturbation; partners rated 8,496/9,892 (86%) injections satisfactory. Among 551 men who reported sexual-activity information, 507 (92%) reported satisfactory activity at least once.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Sexual functioning improved during open-label self-injection: 579 men entered home use, and the weighted paired six-month analysis (or nearest available visit; 397–513 respondents by domain) found mean score changes of +2.3 for frequency, +33.9 for erection quality, +12.3 for orgasm quality, and +25.3 for satisfaction (joint P &lt; 0.001). All 10 questionnaire items improved at post-initiation assessments.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Psychological symptoms and mental health also improved in the paired analysis: interpersonal sensitivity, anxiety, depression, and global severity scores fell by 0.053, 0.046, 0.052, and 0.034, respectively (449–457 respondents across domains; joint nine-domain P = 0.006), while Duke mental health rose 2.09 points (484–495 respondents across domains; P = 0.002). Duke pain increased 3.48 points (P = 0.018). Most sexual-function and Duke score changes persisted through 18 months, although erection quality fell 2.5 points between months 12 and 18 (P = 0.02).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Office titration established an adequate dose for 606/683 (89%) men in the home-use study: 577 began self-injection, and 471/683 (69%) completed six months. Of 577 home users, 247 (43%) kept their starting dose, 231 (40%) increased it, and 99 (17%) reduced it; the mean dose changed from 17.7 to 20.7 µg. The dose-finding study started at 0.5 µg and escalated to 30 µg. Injections used a 27–30-gauge half-inch needle perpendicular to the taut penis at either side of the base, avoiding visible veins, with five minutes of pressure afterward. Men were trained before home use, kept injection and intercourse diaries, and attended monthly visits.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Office titration established the initial home dose for the 579 men who entered open-label, flexible-dose self-injection, with use recommended one to three times weekly. Of these men, 471 completed six months; 416 entered the one-year extension and 321 completed it. Of 203 men who discontinued, 115 returned questionnaires; failure to achieve a firm erection contributed at least as a minor factor for 52.3% of respondents and was the major reason for 26.6%.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Penile pain, usually mild, occurred in 343/683 (50%) men and after 1,873/16,575 (11%) injections; 43/683 (6%) withdrew because of pain. Prolonged erection lasting four to six hours occurred in 35/683 (5%) men, with three discontinuations; priapism lasting more than six hours occurred in 5/683 (1%), one requiring drug therapy. Penile fibrotic complications occurred in 15/683 (2%) men, including nine with Peyronie's disease, four with curvature or deviation, and two with nodules; hematoma or ecchymosis occurred in 57/683 (8%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Possibly related systemic medical events occurred in 39/683 (6%) men, none serious; 9/683 (1%) had symptoms potentially related to hypotension.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Pain contributed to stopping self-injection among the 115 men who completed the discontinuation questionnaire: pain during erection was a major reason for 19.3%, and injection pain for 17.3%.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The home-use outcomes came from a separate, noncomparative self-injection cohort and were recorded by patients and partners after injections.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The quality of life analyses were uncontrolled, used paired responses with variable missingness, and did not adjust for multiple comparisons. Treatment was regularly monitored and provided without charge; partner questionnaire results were omitted because of low response.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA 2018 erectile dysfunction guideline advises informing men about intracavernosal injection as a treatment option, including benefits and risks <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C)</Rec>, and performing an in-office injection test before treatment <Rec>(Clinical Principle)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
