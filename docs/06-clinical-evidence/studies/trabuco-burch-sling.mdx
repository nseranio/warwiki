# Trabuco Burch–sling trial: Retropubic Midurethral Sling Versus Burch Urethropexy With Concurrent Sacrocolpopexy

<TrialBottomLine id="trabuco-burch-sling" />

This two-center, single-blind randomized superiority trial assigned women with stage II or greater prolapse and symptomatic or occult stress incontinence to a retropubic midurethral sling or Burch urethropexy during open abdominal sacrocolpopexy; neither six-month coprimary continence endpoint met the prespecified significance threshold.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="trabuco-burch-sling" />

## Design

Participants had chosen abdominal prolapse repair and had stress, stress-predominant mixed, or occult stress incontinence. The investigators dynamically balanced allocation by age, body mass index, prior incontinence surgery, intrinsic sphincter deficiency, incontinence diagnosis, and prolapse stage. Patients and stress-test assessors were masked; suprapubic sling incisions could disclose the assignment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Overall continence required a negative standardized cough stress test, no interim stress-incontinence retreatment, and an International Consultation on Incontinence Questionnaire Short Form score of zero. Stress-specific continence replaced the last criterion with no reported stress-related leakage on six questionnaire items. The planned sample provided 80% power to detect a 25-percentage-point difference in continence, assuming a 63% Burch rate; analyses used a two-sided P &lt; 0.05 threshold. All randomized participants entered the intention-to-treat denominators.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At six months, overall continence occurred in 29/57 sling recipients (50.9%) and 23/56 Burch recipients (41.1%; odds ratio 1.49, 95% CI 0.71–3.13; P = 0.30). Stress-specific continence occurred in 42/57 (73.7%) and 32/56 (57.1%; odds ratio 2.10, 95% CI 0.95–4.64; P = 0.06). Satisfaction among respondents was 38/49 (77.6%) versus 27/47 (57.4%; P = 0.04); baseline urgency incontinence resolved in 18/25 (72.0%) versus 9/22 (40.9%; P = 0.03).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The planned follow-up found one-year overall continence in 28/57 sling and 16/56 Burch participants (49.1% versus 28.6%; absolute risk difference 20.6 percentage points, 95% CI 3.0–38.1; P = 0.03). One-year stress-specific continence was 40/57 versus 26/56 (70.2% versus 46.4%; difference 23.8 points, 95% CI 6.1–41.4; P = 0.01). At two years, overall continence was 27/57 versus 18/56 (47.4% versus 32.1%; difference 15.2 points, 95% CI −2.6 to 33.1; P = 0.10), while stress-specific continence was 40/57 versus 25/56 (70.2% versus 44.6%; published difference 25.5 points, 95% CI 7.9–43.2; P = 0.006). Missing assessments were counted as failures in these intention-to-treat analyses.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

All participants received their assigned procedure. The sacrocolpopexy suspended the vaginal apex to the sacrum with polypropylene mesh. Burch urethropexy used a modified Tanagho technique, suspending periurethral vaginal tissue to Cooper ligaments bilaterally; the other arm received an Align retropubic polypropylene sling at the midurethra. Fellowship-trained surgeons had each performed more than 20 operations of both types; they could add a posterior repair. Median operating time for the combined procedure was 251 minutes (IQR 218–281) with sling and 253 minutes (IQR 219–284) with Burch.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

At six months, serious adverse events occurred in 11% of sling and 9% of Burch participants (P = 0.77). Urinary tract infection occurred in 18/57 (31.6%) and 17/56 (30.4%). Sacrocolpopexy mesh exposure required surgical removal in 2/57 (3.5%) and 1/56 (1.8%); no bladder perforation occurred during sling trocar passage.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

By two years, among women with a postoperative visit, sacrocolpopexy mesh exposure requiring revision was recorded in 7/56 sling recipients (12.5%) and 1/55 Burch recipients (1.8%; P = 0.06). One sling exposure resolved with topical estrogen without revision. Two Burch recipients and no sling recipients required reoperation for incomplete bladder emptying.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The original power calculation targeted a 25-percentage-point effect, and the six-month primary comparisons had wide confidence intervals. At two years, assessments were available for 48/57 sling and 45/56 Burch recipients; the intention-to-treat analysis treated missing assessments as failures.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The trial enrolled predominantly white women undergoing open surgery at two centers, with surgeons trained at the same institution.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
