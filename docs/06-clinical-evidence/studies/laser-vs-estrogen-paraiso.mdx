# VeLVET: Vaginal Laser Versus Vaginal Estrogen for Genitourinary Syndrome of Menopause

<TrialBottomLine id="laser-vs-estrogen-paraiso" />

**VeLVET** was a multicenter, assessor-blinded noninferiority randomized trial comparing fractional CO₂ vaginal laser with vaginal conjugated estrogen cream in menopausal women with bothersome vaginal dryness. Both groups improved at six months, but early closure prevented a conclusion of laser noninferiority.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="laser-vs-estrogen-paraiso" />

## Design

Women at six US centers were eligible after at least a year without menstruation and a vaginal dryness score of at least 7 cm on a 10-cm visual analog scale (VAS). Exclusions included active genital infection, estrogen contraindication, relevant gynecologic malignancy or radiation, recent pelvic surgery, and prior transvaginal mesh reconstruction. A prior midurethral sling or synthetic-mesh sacrocolpopexy was allowed. Participants knew their assignment; personnel performing follow-up examinations and questionnaires were blinded.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The primary endpoint was change in vaginal dryness VAS at six months. The prespecified noninferiority margin was 1.0 cm on that scale, assessed primarily in treated participants under a per-protocol analysis. The investigators planned 196 participants; enrollment ended at 69 after the FDA required the sponsor to obtain and maintain an investigational device exemption. Sixty-two completed the six-month protocol.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The primary VAS comparison showed no reported between-group difference. The investigators stated that the truncated sample could not establish their prespecified noninferiority claim. The intention-to-treat sensitivity analysis did not change their per-protocol findings.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Vaginal maturation index change favored estrogen: mean change 25 ± 22.6 among 18 estrogen participants versus 3.9 ± 30.6 among 16 laser participants (P = 0.04; adjusted P = 0.02). Questionnaire measures of sexual function, urinary symptoms, and the day-to-day impact of vaginal aging showed no reported between-group difference after the authors' analyses.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The laser arm received three intravaginal SmartXide2 V2LR/MonaLisa Touch fractional CO₂ treatments at least six weeks apart. The protocol used 30 W dot power, 1,000 μs dwell time, 1,000 μm dot spacing, and smart stack 1 for the first treatment and 3 thereafter. A 90° probe was advanced to the vaginal apex, withdrawn in centimeter steps, and rotated for circumferential treatment. Introital and vestibular treatment was optional, using a flat probe at 26 W; 16 participants received both vaginal and vestibular treatment, while 17 received vaginal treatment alone. Participants avoided intercourse for at least three days after treatment, with topical lidocaine 5% ointment available for vulvar pain.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The comparator was Premarin conjugated estrogen cream, 0.5 g intravaginally daily for 14 days, then 0.5 g twice weekly for 24 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Ten mild or moderate adverse events were reported across the treatment groups, including vaginal bleeding, pain or discharge, breast tenderness, urinary tract infection, migraine, and abdominal cramping. The report found no between-group adverse-event difference and reported no serious adverse events.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Enrollment stopped far short of the planned sample. There was no sham procedure, participants knew their treatment, and vestibular laser treatment varied by clinician.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA/SUFU/AUGS 2025 GSM guideline cites VeLVET in its evidence review and advises counseling that evidence does not support CO₂ laser, Er:YAG laser, or radiofrequency for the specified GSM symptoms and outcomes <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation, Evidence Level: Grade C)</Rec>. It permits consideration of CO₂ laser for selected patients who cannot use or prefer alternatives to FDA-approved treatments, with disclosure that use outside a clinical trial is experimental <Rec>(Expert Opinion)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The NAMS 2020 GSM position statement cites VeLVET among active-comparator trials and calls for long-term sham-controlled safety and efficacy studies before routine use of vaginal energy therapies <Rec>(Level C)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
