# Gao PAE versus TURP: Prostatic Arterial Embolization versus Transurethral Resection for BPH

<TrialBottomLine id="pae-vs-turp-gao" />

**Gao PAE versus TURP** randomized 114 men with BPH to prostatic arterial embolization (PAE) or transurethral resection of the prostate (TURP). Both procedures improved measured outcomes through 24 months; TURP produced greater symptom and flow improvement at 1 and 3 months, while PAE had more overall adverse events and shorter hospital stays.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="pae-vs-turp-gao" />

## Design

The trial enrolled men with IPSS greater than 7 after failed medical therapy, prostate volume 20–100 mL, and peak flow below 15 mL/s; 57 were assigned to each procedure. IPSS, quality of life, peak flow, postvoid residual, prostate-specific antigen, and prostate volume were assessed at 1, 3, 6, 12, and 24 months. The sample size targeted a 3-point IPSS difference. No primary endpoint was expressly designated. Outcomes were analyzed using a modified intention-to-treat population; observers were unblinded.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Both groups improved on all six functional measures at each visit compared with baseline (P = .001). TURP produced greater improvements in IPSS, quality of life, peak flow, and postvoid residual at 1 and 3 months (P &lt; .05); those improvements were comparable between groups at 12 and 24 months. TURP produced greater reductions in prostate-specific antigen and prostate volume at every visit (P &lt; .05). Clinical failure occurred in 5/54 treated PAE and 2/53 treated TURP patients (P = .262); failure meant IPSS decrease ≤25%, IPSS ≥18, quality-of-life decrease ≤1, and quality-of-life score ≥4 and/or flow increase &lt;2.5 mL/s with peak flow ≤7 mL/s, followed by repeat TURP at six months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

PAE achieved technical success in 54/57 assigned men (94.7%); three procedures failed because iliac arterial tortuosity and atherosclerosis prevented embolization. All 53 TURP procedures were technically successful. PAE used local anesthesia, right femoral access, and 355–500 µm polyvinyl alcohol particles injected to arterial stasis; 48 men had bilateral and six unilateral embolization. TURP used epidural anesthesia, a bipolar 26-F resectoscope, and a 22-F three-way Foley with saline irrigation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

PAE and TURP procedure times were 89.7 ± 17.1 and 83.5 ± 17.5 minutes, respectively (P = .066). Postoperative catheterization was required in 19/54 (35.2%) and 53/53 (100%), and hospital admission in 26/54 (48.1%) and 53/53 (100%); among admitted patients, mean hospital stays were 2.9 ± 1.6 and 4.8 ± 1.8 days, respectively (P &lt; .001 for catheterization, admission, and stay).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Overall adverse events or complications occurred in 30/57 PAE patients (52.6%) and 17/57 TURP patients (29.8%; P = .029). Clavien I–II events occurred in 22/57 (38.6%) and 13/57 (22.8%); grades III–IV occurred in 8/57 (14.0%) and 4/57 (7.0%). The eight major PAE events comprised three technical and five clinical failures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Acute urinary retention required bedside recatheterization in 14/54 PAE patients (25.9%) and 3/53 TURP patients (5.7%; P = .004). Postembolization syndrome occurred in 6/54 PAE patients (11.1%) and no TURP patients. Urinary tract infection occurred in 1/54 (1.9%) and 2/53 (3.8%); two TURP patients required transfusion, and one developed transurethral resection syndrome.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The small study used a modified intention-to-treat analysis with unblinded observers, and five patients were lost to follow-up by 12 months. Functional outcomes were plotted without tabulated arm estimates or confidence intervals. The interventional radiologists had different levels of experience.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA 2026 BPH guideline cites Gao and colleagues among its PAE-versus-TURP studies. It says clinicians may offer particle-based PAE for LUTS/BPH in patients with prostates at least 50 cc <Rec>(Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>. It calls for urologic assessment before PAE <Rec>(Expert Opinion)</Rec> and performance by an interventional radiologist trained in PAE <Rec>(Expert Opinion)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
