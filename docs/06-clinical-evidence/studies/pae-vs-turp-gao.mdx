# Gao PAE versus TURP: Prostatic Arterial Embolization versus Transurethral Resection for BPH

<TrialBottomLine id="pae-vs-turp-gao" />

**Gao PAE versus TURP** was a prospective randomized controlled trial in 114 men with BPH that compared prostatic arterial embolization (PAE) with transurethral resection of the prostate (TURP); both improved measured outcomes through 24 months, while TURP produced greater early symptom and flow improvement and PAE had more adverse events and complications.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="pae-vs-turp-gao" />

## Design

The investigators assigned 57 men to each procedure. They assessed International Prostate Symptom Score (IPSS), quality of life, peak urinary flow, postvoid residual, prostate-specific antigen, and prostate volume at 1, 3, 6, 12, and 24 months. The analysis used between-group comparisons and repeated measures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

All six measured outcomes improved from baseline in both groups at each visit (P = 0.001). At 1 and 3 months, TURP produced greater improvement in IPSS, quality of life, peak flow, and postvoid residual than PAE (P &lt; 0.05). Reductions in prostate-specific antigen and prostate volume favored TURP at every reported visit (P &lt; 0.05). The reported clinical failure rates were 9.4% after PAE and 3.9% after TURP.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Technical success was reported in 94.7% of PAE procedures and 100% of TURP procedures; the PAE technical failure rate was 5.3%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

The PAE arm had more overall adverse events and complications (P = 0.029). Acute urinary retention was reported in 25.9% and postembolization syndrome in 11.1% of PAE recipients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Six functional measures were compared at five visits, without an identified endpoint hierarchy. The published results do not quantify the between-group symptom or flow changes or define clinical failure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA 2026 BPH guideline cites Gao and colleagues among its PAE-versus-TURP studies. It says clinicians may offer particle-based PAE for LUTS/BPH in patients with prostates at least 50 cc <Rec>(Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>. It calls for urologic assessment before PAE <Rec>(Expert Opinion)</Rec> and performance by an interventional radiologist trained in PAE <Rec>(Expert Opinion)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
