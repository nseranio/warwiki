# NAPRUTI lactobacilli: Oral Probiotics Versus Antibiotic Prophylaxis After Menopause

<TrialBottomLine id="napruti-lactobacilli" />

**NAPRUTI lactobacilli** was a multicenter, double-blind, double-dummy noninferiority trial in community-dwelling postmenopausal women with recurrent symptomatic UTI, comparing oral *Lactobacillus rhamnosus* GR-1 plus *Lactobacillus reuteri* RC-14 with daily trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX). After 12 months, lactobacilli did not meet the prespecified noninferiority margin for symptomatic recurrence; *E. coli* resistance increased with TMP-SMX and not with lactobacilli.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="napruti-lactobacilli" />

## Design

Women needed at least three self-reported symptomatic UTIs in the preceding year. Recruitment used advertisements and referrals from Dutch family physicians and specialists at 10 centers. Active UTI, recent antibiotics, renal failure, renal transplant, TMP-SMX contraindication or interacting medication excluded participation. Probiotic, cranberry and estrogen prophylaxis stopped before enrollment and remained off during follow-up. Women with structural or functional urinary tract abnormalities, prior urologic surgery, and catheters were included; randomization was stratified by center and complicating host factors.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The experimental regimen was an oral capsule containing at least 10^9 colony-forming units of the two-strain preparation twice daily plus a nightly placebo tablet; the comparator was TMP-SMX 480 mg nightly plus twice-daily placebo capsules. Symptomatic recurrence meant a woman's report of typical UTI symptoms, usually dysuria, frequency or urgency. Microbiologic recurrence additionally required at least 10^3 CFU/mL in midstream urine. The noninferiority analysis tested whether the upper 95% confidence limit for the difference in mean symptomatic recurrences remained below a prespecified 10% margin relative to TMP-SMX. The analysis called intention-to-treat included women who took at least one dose.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Mean symptomatic recurrences over 12 months were 3.3 with lactobacilli and 2.9 with TMP-SMX (difference 0.4 UTIs per woman-year, 95% CI −0.4 to 1.5; P = 0.42). The upper confidence limit exceeded the 10% noninferiority margin. Kaplan-Meier estimates for at least one symptomatic UTI were 79.1% and 69.3%; median time to first symptomatic UTI was 3 and 6 months, respectively (log-rank P = 0.02). These are symptomatic endpoints; culture confirmation was not required for the primary recurrence count.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The secondary mean count of microbiologically confirmed symptomatic UTIs was 1.8 versus 1.2 (difference 0.6, 95% CI 0.0 to 1.4; P = 0.02). In fecal and urinary *E. coli* isolates from asymptomatic women, resistance to TMP-SMX, trimethoprim and amoxicillin rose from approximately 20%–40% at baseline to 80%–95% after one month of TMP-SMX prophylaxis. Resistance did not increase during lactobacilli prophylaxis and declined during the three months after TMP-SMX stopped. The resistance ranges describe sampled isolates, rather than the proportion of women carrying a resistant organism.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Women collected urine, fecal and vaginal specimens monthly during prophylaxis and for three months afterward; symptomatic episodes prompted urine culture. The analysis included 123 of 125 women randomized to lactobacilli and 115 of 127 randomized to TMP-SMX. *L. reuteri* was not detected in vaginal swabs in either arm at baseline or after treatment; month-12 mean Nugent scores were 6.0 with lactobacilli and 6.1 with TMP-SMX.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Any adverse event occurred in 72/123 (58.5%) with lactobacilli and 66/115 (57.4%) with TMP-SMX; serious adverse events occurred in 14/123 (11.4%) and 17/115 (14.8%). Events led to withdrawal in 15/123 (12.2%) and 6/115 (5.2%), respectively. The masked safety board judged adverse events leading to withdrawal likely treatment-related in 11/123 (8.9%) lactobacilli recipients and 3/115 (2.6%) TMP-SMX recipients. One systemic allergic reaction occurred with TMP-SMX. UTI requiring hospitalization occurred in 3/123 (2.4%) versus 0/115 (0%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The trial did not reach its target enrollment. Pre-enrollment UTI counts were self-reported, and not all symptomatic recurrences during treatment were culture-confirmed. The trial permitted catheters and complicated host factors, with an imbalance in catheter use between groups. Baseline TMP-SMX resistance was high.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="napruti-lactobacilli" />

The interlinked NAPRUTI cranberry trial tested a different nonantibiotic strategy in premenopausal women.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Other probiotic trials used vaginal dosing or different strains; this trial tested oral GR-1/RC-14 against daily TMP-SMX after menopause.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU Urological Infections guideline, limited update March 2026, advises patients on local or oral probiotics containing strains with evidence for vaginal flora regeneration to prevent cystitis <Rec>(Weak)</Rec>. Its evidence summary names GR-1 and RC-14 among the studied strains.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
