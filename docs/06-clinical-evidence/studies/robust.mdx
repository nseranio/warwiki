# ROBUST III: Optilume Drug-Coated Balloon for Recurrent Anterior Urethral Stricture

<TrialBottomLine id="robust" />

**ROBUST III** was a multicenter, single-blind, randomized superiority trial of Optilume paclitaxel-coated balloon dilation versus standard endoscopic management (uncoated balloon dilation, urethral sounds or direct vision internal urethrotomy) in 127 men with recurrent anterior urethral strictures of 3 cm or less after at least two endoscopic treatments, with anatomical success at 6 months as the primary endpoint. Success was 74.6% (50/67) with the drug-coated balloon (DCB) and 26.8% (11/41) with standard management, a difference of 44.4 percentage points (95% CI 27.6–61.1).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="robust" />

## Design

Men were adults with an anterior stricture of 3 cm or less and 12 Fr or narrower on urethrography, at least two prior endoscopic treatments, an International Prostate Symptom Score (IPSS) of 11 or higher and a peak flow rate (Qmax) below 15 mL/s. Prior urethroplasty, hypospadias repair, lichen sclerosus and unresolved confounders (bladder neck contracture, neurogenic bladder, benign prostatic hyperplasia) excluded patients. Enrollment ran from October 2018 to December 2020 at 21 US sites and 1 Canadian site. Randomization was 2:1 before the index procedure, stratified by prior pelvic radiotherapy and by number of prior endoscopic treatments (fewer than 5 versus 5 or more), and patients were blinded through the 6-month primary endpoint.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

At baseline 92.1% of strictures were bulbar and the mean length was 1.7 cm. Participants had a mean of 3.6 prior treatments, and 23/127 (18.1%) had five or more. Penile strictures numbered 8/79 in the DCB group and 2/47 (with recorded location) in the control group, and prior pelvic radiation was recorded in 9/79 and 6/48.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The primary endpoint was anatomical success: atraumatic passage of a 16 Fr flexible cystoscope or a 14 Fr catheter through the treated segment at 6 months, with repeat intervention before 6 months counted as failure. Missing data were handled by multiple imputation, and a sample of 126 gave 90% power for a 32% between-group difference. The primary safety endpoint was freedom from urethral fistula, unresolved de novo stress incontinence or urethral rupture through 3 months. Freedom from repeat dilation, DVIU or urethroplasty at 1 year was a key secondary endpoint.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At 6 months, 50/67 DCB patients (74.6%) and 11/41 control patients (26.8%) with an evaluable result passed the lumen test; the model-based difference was 44.4 percentage points (95% CI 27.6–61.1; P &lt; 0.0001), which met the primary endpoint. Cystoscopy was missing for 12 DCB and 7 control patients, and 2 DCB and 18 control patients had repeat intervention before 6 months. Assuming all 7 missing controls succeeded and all 12 missing DCB patients failed, success was still 63% versus 38%. The effect was consistent in prespecified subgroups with 5 or more versus fewer than 5 prior treatments and with stricture length of 2 cm or more versus under 2 cm; etiology, penile location and prior radiation subgroups were too small to assess. Among the 70 DCB patients and 11 control patients dilated with a 30 Fr balloon, success was 75% versus 22% (P &lt; 0.01).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Kaplan–Meier freedom from repeat intervention at 1 year was 83.2% with the DCB and 21.7% with standard management (P &lt; 0.0001). Mean Qmax at 1 year was 15.5 mL/s versus 7.6 mL/s (baseline 7.6 and 7.4), and mean IPSS was 9.0 versus 19.9 (baseline 22.0 and 22.8); the report gives these as group means without a between-group test. In controls, Qmax rose through 30 days and then declined from 3 months, while IPSS fell through 30 days and then rose as symptoms worsened.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The control arm was followed for 1 year and the DCB arm is followed annually to 5 years. In the DCB arm, Kaplan–Meier freedom from repeat intervention was 77.8% at 2 years (control arm at 1 year, 23.6%; difference 54.2 points, 95% CI 38.7–69.7) and 71.9% at 3 years.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Mean IPSS rose from 9.0 at 1 year to 10.1 at 2 years and 11.6 at 3 years, and Qmax fell from 15.5 to 12.6 and 10.6 mL/s. An IPSS improvement of at least 30% without repeat intervention was present in 61% (38/62) at 2 years and 48% (27/56) at 3 years, and treatment failures numbered 15 by 2 years and 20 by 3 years.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> In an exploratory 2-year subgroup comparison, freedom from reintervention was 46.9% (95% CI 10.3–83.4) for penile strictures (n = 8) and 82.0% (95% CI 72.7–91.2) for bulbar strictures (n = 71).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Of the 48 control patients, 32 had recurrence and crossed over to the DCB; more than 75% of them were free from repeat intervention 1 year after crossover.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

ROBUST I, a separate single-arm cohort of 53 men with recurrent bulbar strictures of 2.0 cm or less treated at 4 Latin American sites, reached 5-year closeout with Kaplan–Meier freedom from repeat intervention of 71.7% and functional success (IPSS improvement of at least 50% without re-treatment) in 58% (25/43); 31 of 53 completed all follow-up. Functional success was 38% (8/21) with the 24 Fr balloon and 77% (17/22) with the 30 Fr balloon, a nonrandomized comparison.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

DCB-arm strictures were pretreated to at least 20 Fr with an uncoated balloon (92.4%), DVIU (5.1%) or both (2.5%); direct dilation with the DCB was avoided so that a stricture that dilated poorly would not receive a second paclitaxel dose. Balloon diameter and length were chosen from the lumen and the urethrographic stricture length to overlap normal urethra by 0.5–1 cm at each end. The most used diameter was 30 Fr, in 30 mm (28.2%) or 50 mm (60.8%) lengths. The balloon was inflated to its rated burst pressure for at least 5 minutes (mean 8 minutes 42 seconds from insertion to removal), then a 12–14 Fr Foley catheter was placed. Estimated post-treatment lumen on urethrography averaged 24 Fr in both groups.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Control patients received the endoscopic method standard at their site: uncoated balloon dilation in 58.3% (24 Fr in 16, 28 Fr in 1, 30 Fr in 11), DVIU in 25.0% and urethral sounds in 16.7%, with no prespecified lumen goal.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Control patients with confirmed recurrence (recurrent symptoms, reduced flow and a lumen under 12 Fr on urethrography) could cross to the DCB before 12 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In the 2-year report the DCB cohort used 30 Fr in 70, 24 Fr in 6 and 36 Fr in 3 of 79 men.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

No participant had a primary safety event through 3 months. Adverse event types and rates were similar between groups except for post-procedure hematuria and dysuria, each 11.4% with the DCB and 2.1% with standard management; the events were mild and resolved within 30 days in 10 of 11 men. Serious adverse events occurred in 10.1% of DCB and 16.7% of control patients, and one serious urinary tract infection in each group was judged possibly related to the device or procedure. IIEF did not change in either group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In a nonrandomized cohort of 15 men, paclitaxel was detected in semen in 9/15 (60%) at 30 days, 5/13 (39%) at 3 months and 1/12 (8.3%) at 6 months; the authors recommend contraception through 6 months when the partner can conceive.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In the DCB arm, treatment-related hematuria was 13.9%, dysuria 6.3% and urinary tract infection 6.3% at 2 years, with no additional treatment-related events after 12 months.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> At 3 years the figures were 11.4%, 7.6% and 6.3%, with no late treatment-related events.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Through 5 years ROBUST I recorded no serious treatment-related adverse events in 53 men, and 15 device- or procedure-related events, all grade 1–2.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Limitations

Surgeons were not blinded, and patients were unblinded after 6 months; Qmax and postvoid residual favored the DCB both before and after unblinding. The DCB arm received two dilations (pretreatment and coated balloon) against one in the control arm, and most controls were dilated with a 24 Fr balloon against 30 Fr in most DCB patients; post-treatment urethrograms showed similar lumen estimates, and the 30 Fr subset analysis agreed with the overall result.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The primary outcome was missing for 12 DCB and 7 control patients. Penile strictures, prior pelvic radiation and lichen sclerosus were too few or excluded for any conclusion, and the effect of prior DCB treatment on later urethroplasty is unknown.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The control arm was not followed beyond 1 year; IPSS data were available for 62/79 at 2 years and 56/79 at 3 years.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Urotronic was the sole funder of the 1- and 3-year reports, and several authors disclose consulting for the manufacturer.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="robust" />

The AUA panel read ROBUST III together with the OPEN trial. OPEN used patient-reported voiding symptoms as its primary outcome in a comparison of urethrotomy with urethroplasty; ROBUST III used urethral patency at 6 months and freedom from retreatment at 1 year. The panel considered both trials at greater risk of bias as unreplicated single studies and described ROBUST III as an efficacy-style design in a highly selected population. ROBUST III did not compare the DCB with urethroplasty.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

- **AUA, Urethral Stricture Disease guideline (published 2016, amended 2023).** Statement 11b: surgeons may offer urethral dilation or direct visual internal urethrotomy, combined with drug-coated balloons, for recurrent bulbar urethral strictures &lt;3 cm in length <Rec>(Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>. The panel restricted the recommendation to recurrent bulbar strictures because penile strictures were about 10% of ROBUST III and the trial was not powered for them, and it states that the efficacy of repeated DCB use has not been ascertained and is not recommended.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **EAU, Urethral Strictures limited update (2026).** Offer drug (paclitaxel)-coated balloon dilatation for a short (&lt;3 cm) bulbar stricture recurring after at least two prior endoscopic treatments, in patients not fit or unwilling to undergo urethroplasty <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>. The summary of evidence is level 1b; the guideline reports ROBUST III as the randomized source and advises collecting data in a trial or registry for strictures outside the ROBUST indications.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **FDA (December 3, 2021).** Premarket approval P210020 for the Optilume Urethral Drug Coated Balloon to treat obstructive urinary symptoms associated with anterior urethral stricture in adult males with strictures of 3 cm or less. The summary of safety and effectiveness lists hypersensitivity to paclitaxel or structurally related compounds and urologic implants such as penile implants or artificial urinary sphincters as contraindications.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
