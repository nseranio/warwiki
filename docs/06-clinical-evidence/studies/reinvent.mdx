# REINVENT: Nightly versus On-Demand Vardenafil After Nerve-Sparing Radical Prostatectomy

<TrialBottomLine id="reinvent" />

**REINVENT** randomized 628 men after bilateral nerve-sparing radical prostatectomy to nightly vardenafil, on-demand vardenafil, or placebo in a multicenter, double-blind, double-dummy trial. The prespecified overall comparison of IIEF-EF scores of at least 22 after a two-month washout was not significant.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="reinvent" />

## Design

Eligible men were 18–64 years old, had a preoperative International Index of Erectile Function erectile-function domain (IIEF-EF) score of at least 26 without erectile treatment, and had an operative report confirming bilateral nerve sparing and no positive tumor margins. Randomization occurred within 14 days of surgery across 87 centers in Europe, Canada, South Africa, and the United States. The primary endpoint was the proportion with IIEF-EF at least 22 after washout, using last observation carried forward in the modified intention-to-treat (mITT) population with a washout IIEF-EF measurement. The prespecified overall three-group test used p &lt; 0.05; pairwise tests followed only if it was significant. Sexual Encounter Profile question 3 (SEP3) measured successful intercourse as a secondary outcome.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Unassisted erectile recovery after washout did not differ significantly among groups: IIEF-EF scores of at least 22 occurred in 24.1% with nightly vardenafil, 29.1% with on-demand vardenafil, and 28.9% with placebo among the 445 men in the mITT analysis. SEP3 success after washout likewise showed no significant between-group difference.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

During the nine-month double-blind treatment period, IIEF-EF scores of at least 22 at last observation carried forward occurred in 32.0% with nightly vardenafil, 48.2% with on-demand vardenafil, and 24.8% with placebo (on demand versus placebo, p = 0.0001; on demand versus nightly, p = 0.0065). Mean per-patient SEP3 success across treatment was 34.5%, 45.9%, and 25.0%, respectively (nightly versus placebo, p = 0.0344; on demand versus placebo, p &lt; 0.0001). These secondary comparisons were nominal.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Treatment began within 14 days of bilateral nerve-sparing prostatectomy: nightly vardenafil started at 10 mg (reducible to 5 mg), and on-demand vardenafil started at 10 mg (adjustable to 5 or 20 mg), each with matching placebo dosing; the control arm received both placebos. Nine months of double-blind treatment were followed by two months of single-blind placebo washout without devices and an optional two-month open-label on-demand period. Of 997 men screened, 628 were randomized, 617 entered the safety population, 599 entered ITT, 445 entered mITT, and 423 completed the study.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Adverse events during double-blind treatment occurred in 141/207 men (68%) taking nightly vardenafil, 136/204 (67%) taking on-demand vardenafil, and 115/206 (56%) taking placebo. Headache occurred in 21/207 (10%), 33/204 (16%), and 11/206 (5%), respectively; flushing occurred in 13/207 (6%), 15/204 (7%), and 3/206 (1%). Adverse events led to study discontinuation in 8%, 6%, and 5%, respectively; 39 men discontinued overall.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The primary assessment ended two months after treatment withdrawal; the later treatment period was open-label.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="reinvent" />

REACTT later tested daily and on-demand tadalafil after bilateral nerve-sparing prostatectomy, also assessing unassisted erectile function after a drug-free period. Its washout was shorter than REINVENT's.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA erectile-dysfunction guideline (2018) advises informing men who want to preserve erectile function after prostate-cancer treatment that early PDE5-inhibitor use may not improve spontaneous, unassisted erections <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The EAU sexual and reproductive health guideline (2026) states that data are inadequate to support a specific penile-rehabilitation regimen <Rec>(Weak)</Rec> and recommends starting pro-erectile treatment at the earliest opportunity after radical prostatectomy <Rec>(Weak)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
