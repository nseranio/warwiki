# Ospemifene pivotal studies: Oral therapy for postmenopausal vulvovaginal atrophy

<TrialBottomLine id="ospemifene-pivotal" />

The Bachmann phase 3 double-blind trial randomized 826 postmenopausal women with vulvovaginal atrophy to oral ospemifene 30 mg, 60 mg, or placebo; at 12 weeks, 60 mg met all four coprimary endpoints.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="ospemifene-pivotal" />

## Design

Entry required at most 5% superficial cells on a vaginal smear, vaginal pH &gt;5.0, and at least one moderate or severe symptom. The coprimary measures were changes in superficial cells, parabasal cells, pH, and severity of the most bothersome symptom, vaginal dryness or dyspareunia. Randomization was 1:1:1, and all participants received a nonhormonal vaginal lubricant for use as needed.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

In the Bachmann trial, the 30-mg group met the cytology, pH, and most-bothersome vaginal dryness endpoints; its dyspareunia comparison missed significance.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Portman and colleagues separately randomized 605 women with dyspareunia as their most bothersome symptom to ospemifene 60 mg (303) or placebo (302). At 12 weeks, all four coprimary endpoints—superficial cells, parabasal cells, vaginal pH, and dyspareunia severity—favored ospemifene (p &lt;0.0001 for the first three; p = 0.0001 for dyspareunia).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The Bachmann treatment course lasted 12 weeks. In its blinded extension, 180 women with a uterus continued their assigned ospemifene dose or placebo for 40 more weeks, yielding 52 weeks of total treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Adverse events

In Portman's trial, at least one treatment-emergent adverse event occurred in 186/303 (61.4%) ospemifene recipients and 154/302 (51.0%) placebo recipients.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Treatment-related hot flushes occurred in 6.6% versus 3.6%; one participant per group discontinued.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In the 180-person Bachmann extension, three ospemifene recipients reported self-limited vaginal bleeding or spotting; no endometrial hyperplasia, carcinoma, or venous thromboembolism was reported. More than 95% of week-52 endometrial biopsy samples were atrophic, inactive, or insufficient for diagnosis.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

The pivotal efficacy comparison lasted 12 weeks with lubricant available in every arm. The extension enrolled a subset of the parent cohort, and its endometrial results were reported without treatment-specific denominators.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="ospemifene-pivotal" />

The Portman dyspareunia trial was a separate randomized cohort, while the Simon safety extension continued women from the Bachmann trial.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

AUA/SUFU/AUGS 2025 says clinicians may offer ospemifene for GSM-related vulvovaginal dryness and/or dyspareunia <Rec>(Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The North American Menopause Society's 2020 position statement includes ospemifene among options for moderate to severe GSM <Rec>(Level A)</Rec>.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> FDA approved Osphena on February 26, 2013, for moderate to severe dyspareunia due to menopausal vulvar and vaginal atrophy.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
