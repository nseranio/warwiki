# Ospemifene pivotal studies: Oral therapy for postmenopausal vulvovaginal atrophy

<TrialBottomLine id="ospemifene-pivotal" />

The Bachmann phase 3 double-blind trial randomized 826 postmenopausal women with vulvovaginal atrophy to oral ospemifene 30 mg (282), 60 mg (276), or placebo (268); at 12 weeks, 60 mg met the coprimary cytology, pH, and most-bothersome-symptom comparisons.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="ospemifene-pivotal" />

## Design

Entry required at most 5% superficial cells on a vaginal smear, vaginal pH &gt;5.0, and at least one moderate or severe symptom. The four coprimary measures were changes in superficial cells, parabasal cells, pH, and severity of the most bothersome symptom, assessed separately for vaginal dryness and dyspareunia. The intent-to-treat analysis used last observation carried forward; testing stepped down from 60 mg to 30 mg and from dryness to dyspareunia at two-sided p ≤0.05.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At 12 weeks in Bachmann’s intent-to-treat analysis, superficial cells increased by 10.8 percentage points with 60 mg, 7.8 with 30 mg, and 2.2 with placebo; parabasal cells decreased by 30.1 and 21.9 points versus a 3.98-point increase with placebo. Vaginal pH decreased by 1.01 and 0.67 versus 0.10 (p &lt;0.001 for each dose on all three measures). Among women naming dryness as most bothersome, severity fell by 1.26 and 1.22 versus 0.84 (p = 0.021 and 0.04). Among those naming dyspareunia, severity fell by 1.19 and 1.02 versus 0.89; 60 mg met the comparison (p = 0.023), while 30 mg did not.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Portman and colleagues separately randomized 605 women with dyspareunia as their most bothersome symptom to ospemifene 60 mg (303) or placebo (302). In the 12-week intent-to-treat analysis, superficial cells increased by 12.3 versus 1.7 percentage points, parabasal cells decreased by 40.2 points versus no reduction, and pH fell by 0.94 versus 0.07 (all p &lt;0.0001). Dyspareunia severity fell by 1.5 versus 1.2 points (p = 0.0001); all four coprimary comparisons met significance.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

In Bachmann’s trial, 689/826 (83.4%) women completed 12 weeks; all arms received lubricant, used by 22% of the 60-mg group, 31% of the 30-mg group, and 29% of the placebo group in the last week.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In the extension, 180 women with a uterus continued their assigned ospemifene dose or placebo each morning with food for 40 more weeks, yielding 52 weeks of total treatment; 140/180 completed.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Portman’s trial also supplied lubricant and dosed 60 mg once daily with food; 278/303 (91.7%) treated women and 266/302 (88.1%) placebo recipients completed 12 weeks.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Hot flashes and urinary infections were more frequent with ospemifene in Bachmann’s 12-week trial: hot flashes occurred in 23/276 (8.3%) at 60 mg, 27/282 (9.6%) at 30 mg, and 9/268 (3.4%) with placebo; reported urinary infections occurred in 20/276 (7.2%), 13/282 (4.6%), and 6/268 (2.2%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In Portman’s trial, at least one treatment-emergent adverse event occurred in 186/303 (61.4%) ospemifene recipients versus 154/302 (51.0%) placebo recipients; hot flushes occurred in 20/303 (6.6%) versus 13/302 (4.3%), urinary infection in 17/303 (5.6%) versus 11/302 (3.6%), and vaginal candidiasis in 14/303 (4.6%) versus 1/302 (0.3%).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In the 180-woman extension, two ospemifene recipients reported three self-limited bleeding or spotting events. At week 52, 53/55 (96.4%) samples at 60 mg, 44/46 (95.7%) at 30 mg, and 32/32 (100%) with placebo were atrophic, inactive, or insufficient; no hyperplasia or carcinoma was found in those 133 biopsies.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Treated arms had one atypical and one disordered proliferative finding; no venous thromboembolism occurred among 180 enrollees. Mean endometrial thickness changed by 1.14 mm at 60 mg, 0.68 mm at 30 mg, and −0.04 mm with placebo among women assessed at week 52.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

The extension enrolled 180 women with a uterus from the parent cohort; 133 had week-52 biopsy results.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="ospemifene-pivotal" />

The Portman dyspareunia trial was a separate randomized cohort, while the Simon safety extension continued women from the Bachmann trial.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

AUA/SUFU/AUGS 2025 says clinicians may offer ospemifene for GSM-related vulvovaginal dryness and/or dyspareunia <Rec>(Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The North American Menopause Society's 2020 position statement includes ospemifene among options for moderate to severe GSM <Rec>(Level A)</Rec>.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> FDA approved Osphena on February 26, 2013, for moderate to severe dyspareunia due to menopausal vulvar and vaginal atrophy.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
