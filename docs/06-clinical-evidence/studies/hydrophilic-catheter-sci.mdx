# Cardenas hydrophilic-catheter SCI trial: Intermittent catheterization after acute spinal cord injury

<TrialBottomLine id="hydrophilic-catheter-sci" />

This prospective, multicenter randomized trial compared single-use hydrophilic-coated SpeediCath with uncoated Conveen in 224 intermittent-catheter users with acute traumatic spinal cord injury; first antibiotic-treated symptomatic urinary tract infection occurred later with the coated catheter, with a 33% lower daily risk.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="hydrophilic-catheter-sci" />

## Design

Participants had sustained traumatic spinal cord injury less than three months before enrollment and were recruited at 15 North American spinal cord injury centers. Randomization occurred within ten days of starting intermittent catheterization. The primary outcome was time from first study catheterization to first antibiotic-treated symptomatic UTI. Follow-up covered the hospital or rehabilitation stay and up to three months after discharge, with a maximum study duration of six months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The primary time-to-event comparison favored hydrophilic coating; the abstract reports a significant delay to first treated symptomatic UTI and a 33% lower daily first-infection risk. During the institutional period, antibiotic-treated symptomatic UTI incidence was 21% lower with the coated catheter (P &lt; 0.05). Group-specific infection counts, absolute incidence rates and a confidence interval for the primary comparison are not given in the abstract.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The coated arm used single-use SpeediCath; the comparator used an uncoated polyvinyl chloride Conveen catheter. Catheter assignment began during inpatient care and continued through the study follow-up period.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Antibiotic-treated symptomatic UTI was the reported clinical complication outcome. Comparative urethral trauma and bleeding counts are unavailable in the abstract.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The maximum follow-up was six months, including at most three months after institutional discharge. The published abstract gives relative infection reductions without the arm-specific event counts needed to calculate absolute benefit.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA/SUFU 2021 adult neurogenic lower urinary tract dysfunction guideline cites this trial in the evidence discussion for intermittent catheterization and says hydrophilic catheters may be associated with lower UTI and urethral trauma rates, specifically in patients with spinal cord injury. Its statement 35 recommends intermittent rather than indwelling catheterization for bladder emptying <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C)</Rec>; that statement compares drainage approaches, not catheter coatings.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The EAU 2026 urological infections guideline recommends hydrophilic-coated catheters to reduce catheter-associated UTI <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>, drawing on a meta-analysis of randomized catheter comparisons.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
