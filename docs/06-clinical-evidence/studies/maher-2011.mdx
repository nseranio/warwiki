# Maher 2011: Laparoscopic Sacral Colpopexy Versus Total Vaginal Mesh for Vault Prolapse

<TrialBottomLine id="maher-2011" />

**Maher 2011** was a single-center randomized trial in 108 women with symptomatic stage 2 or higher vaginal vault prolapse that compared laparoscopic sacral colpopexy with total transvaginal Gynecare Prolift mesh repair; objective success at all vaginal sites favored laparoscopy at a mean two years.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="maher-2011" />

## Design

Participants were allocated by a computer-generated sequence stratified for overt or occult stress urinary incontinence, with allocation obtained by telephone after consent. Prior sacral colpopexy or vaginal mesh prolapse surgery, inability to undergo general anesthesia, marked obesity, extensive prior laparotomy, and short vaginal length were exclusion criteria. The consultant urogynecologist and fellow performed the operations; both had completed at least 30 procedures of each type before the study. Participants and surgeons knew the assignment, while coauthors masked to allocation assessed outcomes after the surgeon's initial postoperative visit.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The prespecified primary measure was anatomic success at each POP-Q site and collectively: sites Aa, Ba, C, Bp and Ap all had to measure less than −1 cm, corresponding to stage 0 or 1 at every site. The analysis followed randomized assignment and used a two-sided significance threshold of 0.05.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At mean two-year review, 41/53 women assigned to laparoscopy and 23/55 assigned to total vaginal mesh met the collective anatomic endpoint (OR 4.75, 95% CI 2.06–10.98; P &lt; 0.001), meeting the prespecified threshold. Symptomatic prolapse occurred in 1/53 and 4/55 (P = 0.18). Mean patient-rated satisfaction scores were 87 ± 21 and 79 ± 20 on a 0–100 scale (mean difference 8.09, 95% CI 0.20–15.98; P = 0.002). Both groups improved on pelvic-floor symptom and quality-of-life questionnaires, without a detected between-group difference in change.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

For laparoscopy, the surgeons dissected the bladder to the trigone and the rectovaginal space along the posterior vagina. They fashioned a large-pore polypropylene Y mesh, attached it to the anterior and posterior vaginal walls with polydioxanone sutures, fixed it to the sacral promontory with a ProTack device, and closed the peritoneum over it. Concomitant operations included 27 low posterior repairs, 21 colposuspensions and 27 paravaginal repairs.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The vaginal operation used a total Gynecare Prolift kit, with absorbable sutures securing its distal tails to vaginal fascia. Overt or occult stress incontinence was treated with an inside-out transobturator sling; 23 participants received one. Median operating time was 97 versus 50 minutes, blood loss 100 versus 150 mL, and inpatient stay 2 versus 3 days. Median return to normal activity was 21 days in both groups (53 laparoscopic and 54 vaginal-mesh participants assessed); the reported mean difference was −5.34 days (95% CI −8.36 to −2.32).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Surgery-related reoperation affected 3/53 women after assigned laparoscopy and 12/55 after assigned vaginal mesh (P = 0.006). These women underwent 3 and 15 operations, respectively; vaginal-arm indications included mesh contraction or erosion, continence surgery and recurrent prolapse. Vaginal mesh erosion was observed in 1/53 and 7/55 (P = 0.07).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The laparoscopic arm had one cystotomy and one small-bowel enterotomy, both repaired during surgery. The enterotomy led to conversion to vaginal mesh repair; the participant remained in the laparoscopic intention-to-treat group. Urinary tract infections numbered 2 and 3, and one patient in each arm received a transfusion. One vaginal mesh patient was readmitted with an infected pelvic hematoma treated with intravenous antibiotics.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The trial was conducted at one tertiary unit by a consultant and fellow, and the surgical approaches could not be masked to participants. Concomitant continence and compartment repairs differed by assigned operation and clinical findings. Annual follow-up attendance declined after the initial postoperative assessment, while analysis retained randomized arm denominators.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
