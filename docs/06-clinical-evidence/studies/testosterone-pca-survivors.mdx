# SPIRIT: Testosterone Treatment in Prostate Cancer Survivors With Hypogonadism

<TrialBottomLine id="testosterone-pca-survivors" />

**SPIRIT** was a two-center, double-blind phase 2 randomized trial comparing testosterone cypionate with placebo in symptomatic hypogonadal men after radical prostatectomy for low-grade prostate cancer; testosterone increased sexual activity over 12 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="testosterone-pca-survivors" />

## Design

Eligible men were at least 40 years old, had organ-confined Gleason 3+3 or 3+4 cancer, undetectable PSA for at least 2 years after prostatectomy, a mean of two testosterone measurements below 275 ng/dL, and low libido, erectile dysfunction, or fatigue. Allocation was concealed and stratified by age and phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitor use. Sexual activity was the primary efficacy outcome; biochemical recurrence was defined as PSA at least 0.2 ng/mL.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Among 136 randomized men, the age- and phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitor-adjusted between-group difference in sexual activity was 0.91 daily events in favor of testosterone (95% CI 0.56–1.26; P &lt; 0.001). Sexual desire and the prostate-cancer quality-of-life sexual domain improved more with testosterone; erectile function did not change. Testosterone also reduced negative affect and improved body composition, loaded stair-climbing power, and peak aerobic performance compared with placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Each arm contained 68 men. Testosterone cypionate 100 mg or placebo was injected intramuscularly each week for 12 weeks; 125 of 136 randomized men (91.9%) completed the study.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

No biochemical recurrence was observed in either randomized arm during the study.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The study lasted 12 weeks and enrolled 136 men, so it could not estimate clinical recurrence or long-term safety. Eligibility was restricted to men with low-grade disease, undetectable PSA after prostatectomy, and no radiotherapy or androgen deprivation therapy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
