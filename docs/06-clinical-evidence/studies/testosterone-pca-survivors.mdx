# SPIRIT: Testosterone Treatment in Prostate Cancer Survivors With Hypogonadism

<TrialBottomLine id="testosterone-pca-survivors" />

**SPIRIT** was a two-center, double-blind phase 2 randomized trial comparing testosterone cypionate with placebo in symptomatic hypogonadal men after radical prostatectomy for low-grade prostate cancer; testosterone increased sexual activity over 12 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="testosterone-pca-survivors" />

## Design

Eligible men were at least 40 years old, had organ-confined Gleason 3+3 or 3+4 cancer, stable PSA at most 0.1 ng/mL (later at most 0.04 ng/mL) for at least 2 years after prostatectomy, a mean of two fasting morning total testosterone measurements below 275 ng/dL and/or free testosterone at most 70 pg/mL, and low libido, erectile dysfunction, or fatigue. Allocation was concealed and stratified by site, age, and phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitor use. Change in sexual activity was the primary efficacy outcome; biochemical recurrence, the primary safety outcome, required at least two consecutive PSA readings of at least 0.2 ng/mL.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Sexual activity increased more with testosterone among 136 randomized men: the age-, phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitor-, and baseline-adjusted difference in change across weeks 6 and 12 was 0.91 daily events (95% CI 0.56–1.26; P &lt; .001). From baseline means of 1.64 and 1.51 (66 measured per arm), modeled week-12 changes were +0.99 and +0.06 events with testosterone and placebo, respectively. Sexual desire also improved more with testosterone (between-group difference 2.96 points, 95% CI 1.46–4.45; P &lt; .001), as did the EPIC-26 sexual-function domain (5.35 points, 95% CI 1.63–9.07; P = .005). Erectile-function scores rose within both arms, without a significant between-group difference (0.93 points, 95% CI −0.43 to 2.29; P = .18). Testosterone also reduced negative affect and improved body composition, loaded stair-climbing power, and peak aerobic performance compared with placebo; stair-climbing power improved by 33.48 W (95% CI 5.65–61.31; P = .02) in the Boston-site assessed subset (31 testosterone, 32 placebo).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Each arm contained 68 men. Testosterone cypionate 100 mg or placebo was injected intramuscularly each week for 12 weeks; adherence was 95% and 94% of scheduled injections, respectively. Of 136 randomized men, 125 (91.9%) completed the study; week-12 outcomes were assessed in 64 testosterone and 61 placebo participants.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

No biochemical recurrence occurred during treatment or the 12-week post-treatment follow-up among 66 testosterone and 64 placebo recipients in the primary safety analysis. During treatment, PSA became detectable below 0.2 ng/mL in 2/64 testosterone and 1/62 placebo participants in the Table 2 safety set. At least one adverse event occurred in 49/64 versus 47/62, a serious adverse event in 3/64 versus 1/62, and erythrocytosis (hemoglobin &gt;18 g/dL) in 3/64 versus 0/62.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The study randomized 136 men to 12 weeks of treatment and followed PSA for another 12 weeks, so it could not estimate clinical recurrence or long-term safety. Eligibility was restricted to men with low-grade disease, stable low PSA after prostatectomy, and no radiotherapy or androgen deprivation therapy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
