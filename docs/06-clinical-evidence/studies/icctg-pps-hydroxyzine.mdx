# ICCTG PPS–hydroxyzine: Oral Pentosan Polysulfate and Hydroxyzine for Interstitial Cystitis

<TrialBottomLine id="icctg-pps-hydroxyzine" />

The ICCTG multicenter randomized factorial pilot compared oral pentosan polysulfate sodium (PPS), hydroxyzine, both agents, and placebo in 121 participants with interstitial cystitis; neither pooled drug comparison showed a statistically significant difference in patient-reported global response.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="icctg-pps-hydroxyzine" />

## Design

Participants met NIH–NIDDK interstitial cystitis criteria and had at least moderate pain and urinary frequency for six months or longer. The 2×2 factorial allocation allowed a PPS comparison across participants assigned PPS or no PPS and a separate hydroxyzine comparison across those assigned hydroxyzine or no hydroxyzine. The primary endpoint was patient-reported global response assessment; symptom indexes and reports of pain, urgency, and frequency were secondary endpoints.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Global response occurred in 34% of participants assigned PPS and 18% assigned no PPS (P = 0.064). In the separate factorial comparison, response occurred in 31% assigned hydroxyzine and 20% assigned no hydroxyzine (P = 0.26). The report found no treatment differences in the secondary symptom measures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Recruitment reached 121 participants, 89% of the target of 136, over 18 months rather than the planned 10 months. Complete follow-up data were available from 79% of participants.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

The report described adverse events as mostly minor.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The pilot recruited below its target and had incomplete follow-up. Its factorial main-effect estimates pooled participants across the other drug's assignment; they do not quantify the combination's separate effect.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA IC/BPS guideline, amended in 2022, cites this trial and lists amitriptyline, cimetidine, hydroxyzine, and PPS as oral medications that may be administered <Rec>(Option (Evidence Strength: Grades B, B, C, and B))</Rec>. The same guideline directs counseling about possible macular damage and vision-related injury before PPS treatment <Rec>(Clinical Principle)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
