# ICCTG PPS–hydroxyzine: Oral Pentosan Polysulfate and Hydroxyzine for Interstitial Cystitis

<TrialBottomLine id="icctg-pps-hydroxyzine" />

The ICCTG multicenter, double-masked randomized factorial pilot compared oral pentosan polysulfate sodium (PPS), hydroxyzine, both agents, and placebo in 121 participants with interstitial cystitis; neither 24-week pooled drug comparison showed a statistically significant difference in patient-reported global response.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="icctg-pps-hydroxyzine" />

## Design

Participants had cystoscopy- and hydrodistention-confirmed NIH–NIDDK interstitial cystitis, at least 11 voids daily, and pain/discomfort of at least 4 on a 0–9 scale for at least 24 weeks. The 2×2 factorial allocation allowed a PPS comparison across participants assigned PPS or no PPS and a separate hydroxyzine comparison across those assigned hydroxyzine or no hydroxyzine. The primary endpoint was patient-reported global response at 24 weeks: moderate or marked improvement on a seven-point scale. Early withdrawals counted as nonresponders in the intent-to-treat analysis; symptom indexes and reports of pain, urgency, and frequency were secondary endpoints.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At 24 weeks, global response occurred in 20/59 (34%) assigned PPS and 11/62 (18%) assigned no PPS (P = 0.064). In the separate factorial comparison, response occurred in 19/61 (31%) assigned hydroxyzine and 12/60 (20%) assigned no hydroxyzine (P = 0.26). Individual-arm responses were 12/30 (40%) with both agents, 8/29 (28%) with PPS alone, 7/31 (23%) with hydroxyzine alone, and 4/31 (13%) with placebo. Among 96 participants with complete secondary data, symptom measures showed no statistically significant treatment differences.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Recruitment reached 121 participants, 89% of the target of 136, over 18 months rather than the planned 10 months. PPS was given at 100 mg three times daily; hydroxyzine was titrated from 10 to 25 to 50 mg nightly over three weeks, then continued at the highest tolerated dose for 21 weeks. Of hydroxyzine recipients, 66% reached 50 mg daily (71% with hydroxyzine alone and 60% with both agents), versus 90% receiving equivalent placebo (P = 0.0019). Complete 24-week follow-up data were available from 96/121 (79%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Adverse events were mostly minor: at least one occurred in 50/61 (82%) receiving hydroxyzine versus 51/60 (85%) without it, and in 50/59 (85%) receiving PPS versus 51/62 (82%) without it. Worst Common Toxicity Criteria grade 3 events occurred in 9/61 versus 13/60 in the hydroxyzine comparison and 12/59 versus 10/62 in the PPS comparison. Nine serious adverse events were reported, none judged treatment-related; 10/121 (8%) participants withdrew because of adverse events.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The pilot recruited below its target and had incomplete follow-up. Its factorial main-effect estimates pooled participants across the other drug's assignment and had limited power for individual-arm comparisons; secondary outcome analyses included completers rather than all randomized participants.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA IC/BPS guideline, amended in 2022, cites this trial and lists amitriptyline, cimetidine, hydroxyzine, and PPS as oral medications that may be administered <Rec>(Option (Evidence Strength: Grades B, B, C, and B))</Rec>. The same guideline directs counseling about possible macular damage and vision-related injury before PPS treatment <Rec>(Clinical Principle)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
