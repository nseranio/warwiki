# NEPTUNE: Fixed-dose Solifenacin and Tamsulosin for Male Storage and Voiding Symptoms

<TrialBottomLine id="neptune" />

**NEPTUNE** was a double-blind phase 3 randomized trial in 1,334 men with storage and voiding lower urinary tract symptoms that compared fixed-dose solifenacin 6 or 9 mg plus tamsulosin OCAS 0.4 mg with tamsulosin OCAS alone and placebo. At 12 weeks, the 6 mg combination met the prespecified symptom-score criteria; the 9 mg combination did not meet all criteria against tamsulosin.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="neptune" />

## Design

Eligibility required International Prostate Symptom Score (IPSS) at least 13, maximum flow 4–12 mL/s, at least two severe urgency episodes and eight micturitions daily, estimated prostate weight &lt;75 g, and postvoid residual (PVR) at most 150 mL.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> A two-week placebo run-in preceded randomized treatment. Coprimary endpoints were total IPSS and Total Urgency and Frequency Score (TUFS). Success required IPSS superiority to placebo in the full analysis set and noninferiority to tamsulosin in the full analysis and per-protocol sets, using a 0.5-point margin, plus TUFS superiority to tamsulosin in the full analysis set.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The 6 mg combination met both coprimary endpoints at 12 weeks in the full analysis set: adjusted mean IPSS change was −7.0 points (n=311), versus −6.2 (n=297) with tamsulosin and −5.4 (n=318) with placebo (P&lt;.001 for superiority to placebo; P=.001 for noninferiority to tamsulosin). TUFS changed by −8.1 points (n=314), versus −6.7 (n=299) and −4.4 (n=318), respectively (P=.025 versus tamsulosin). The 9 mg combination improved IPSS by −6.5 points (n=299; P=.006 versus placebo), but noninferiority to tamsulosin was not established after multiplicity adjustment (nominal P=.028); TUFS improved by −7.6 points (n=302), without superiority to tamsulosin (P=.162).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the open-label extension, pooled flexible-dose recipients had mean reductions from the original baseline of 9.0 IPSS points (n=1,004) and 10.1 TUFS points (n=1,005) at the end of treatment, with exposure extending to 52 weeks.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> A secondary analysis found that the 6 mg combination favored responder outcomes in 8/8 analyses versus placebo and 6/8 versus tamsulosin; these counts refer to analyses, not patients.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Randomized treatment followed a two-week placebo run-in and was given as matching once-daily tablets for 12 weeks, with visits at weeks 4, 8, and 12. Among treated men, 298/337 (88.4%) completed the 6 mg combination, 292/324 (90.1%) the 9 mg combination, 294/326 (90.2%) tamsulosin, and 315/341 (92.4%) placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

NEPTUNE II enrolled 1,067 of 1,199 NEPTUNE completers; 1,066 received treatment, and 960/1,066 (90.1%) completed it. All started the 6 mg combination for four weeks, then could select either dose at later visits.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

During randomized treatment, any treatment-emergent adverse event occurred in 99/337 (29.4%) with the 6 mg combination, 100/324 (30.9%) with the 9 mg combination, 74/326 (22.7%) with tamsulosin, and 87/341 (25.5%) with placebo; serious events occurred in 5/337 (1.5%), 9/324 (2.8%), 10/326 (3.1%), and 3/341 (0.9%), respectively. Drug-related dry mouth occurred in 27/337 (8.0%), 34/324 (10.5%), 1/326 (0.3%), and 4/341 (1.2%); constipation occurred in 9/337 (2.7%), 16/324 (4.9%), 1/326 (0.3%), and 1/341 (0.3%), respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the extension safety population, treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 499/1,066 (46.8%); dry mouth occurred in 132/1,066 (12.4%), constipation in 55/1,066 (5.2%), and 42/1,066 (3.9%) discontinued because of adverse events.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> During randomized treatment, catheter-requiring acute urinary retention occurred in 1/337 (0.3%) with the 6 mg combination, 3/324 (0.9%) with the 9 mg combination, and 1/326 (0.3%) with tamsulosin.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Across both studies, urinary retention occurred in 13/1,208 (1.1%) exposed to either combination; 8/1,208 (0.7%) required catheterization.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Limitations

The flexible-dose extension enrolled NEPTUNE completers without a concurrent control.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The **EAU 2026 male LUTS guideline** cites NEPTUNE and recommends an α1-blocker plus a muscarinic receptor antagonist in men with moderate-to-severe LUTS whose storage symptoms remain insufficiently relieved by either monotherapy <Rec>(Weak)</Rec>. It advises against the combination at PVR &gt;150 mL <Rec>(Weak)</Rec>.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
