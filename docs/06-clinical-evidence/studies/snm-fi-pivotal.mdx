# InterStim FI pivotal study: Sacral Nerve Stimulation for Fecal Incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="snm-fi-pivotal" />

The **InterStim FI pivotal study** was a prospective 16-center single-arm study of 120 patients implanted after successful test stimulation. At 12 months, 88/106 with evaluable diaries (83%) had at least 50% fewer weekly incontinent episodes than at baseline (95% CI 74%–90%; P &lt; 0.0001), meeting the prespecified objective that at least half of implanted patients respond.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="snm-fi-pivotal" />

## Design

Candidates had more than two frank stool-loss episodes per week for over six months (or over 12 months after vaginal delivery) despite conservative treatment or when unsuitable for it. Patients with an external anal sphincter defect over 60 degrees or uncontrolled watery diarrhea were excluded. The primary efficacy objective required at least half of implanted patients to have at least 50% fewer weekly incontinent episodes at 12 months. The study had no randomized comparison group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The 12-month complete-case analysis found complete continence in 43/106 (41%), with mean weekly incontinent episodes falling from 9.4 at baseline (n = 120) to 1.9 (n = 106). At least 50% fewer weekly incontinent days occurred in 88/106 (83%; P &lt; 0.0001), meeting a prespecified secondary objective. At 24 months, 57/67 (85%) met the episode-response definition. A modified worst-case analysis of missing 12-month diaries estimated response in 88/120 implanted patients (73%); including the 13 tested but not implanted yielded 88/133 (66%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The three-year complete-case analysis found therapeutic success in 66/77 (86%) and complete continence in 31/77 (40%); 83 patients completed all or part of the assessment.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Among all 120 implanted patients, response was 95/120 (79%; P &lt; 0.0001) with last observation carried forward and 71/120 (59%; P = 0.055) with modified worst-case imputation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> At five years, 76/120 patients remained in follow-up.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Among 72 with evaluable episode diaries, 64/72 (89%) had at least 50% fewer episodes and 26/72 (36%) reported complete continence; mean weekly episodes fell from 9.1 at baseline to 1.7.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Imputation for 48 missing five-year diaries yielded a response rate of 83/120 (69%; P &lt; 0.0001).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Of 285 screened patients, 133 underwent acute test stimulation, 129 entered 10–14-day subchronic testing, and 120/133 (90.2%) received permanent implantation after a response. A quadripolar lead was placed at S3 in 112/120, S4 in 5/120, and S2 in 3/120; 112/120 received a tined lead. The pulse generator was placed in a contralateral gluteal pocket, with recommended settings of 210 microseconds and 15 Hz.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

The primary report recorded 307 device- or therapy-related events in 96/120 implanted patients, including 26/307 serious events; no unanticipated adverse device effects were reported.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Implant-site infection occurred in 13/120 (10.8%); 7/120 (5.8%) required surgery, including five explants and two replacements.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> At the three-year report cutoff, implant-site pain affected 28% of 120 implanted patients, paresthesia 15%, altered stimulation sensation 12%, and postimplant infection 12/120 (10%); one infection from the earlier report had been reclassified as starting during testing.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Six infections required surgery, including five explants and one replacement.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Across all 120 implanted patients in the five-year report, 47/120 (39.2%) had a revision, replacement, or explant, including 22/120 (18.3%) with system explant.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Among the 76 followed at least five years, 27/76 (35.5%) underwent an intervention.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

Only test-phase responders received an implant, and there was no concurrent control arm. The five-year complete-case success estimate used episode diaries from 72/120 originally implanted patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The American Society of Colon and Rectal Surgeons' 2023 guideline says sacral neuromodulation may be considered a first-line surgical option for incontinent patients with or without sphincter defects <Rec>(Conditional; Low)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The FDA approved the Medtronic InterStim system on March 14, 2011, for chronic fecal incontinence after failure or intolerance of more conservative treatments.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
