# InterStim FI pivotal study: Sacral Nerve Stimulation for Fecal Incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="snm-fi-pivotal" />

The **InterStim FI pivotal study** was a prospective multicenter single-arm study of 120 patients with fecal incontinence implanted after successful test stimulation; 83% of those assessed at 12 months had at least 50% fewer weekly incontinent episodes than at baseline (95% CI 74%-90%; P &lt; 0.0001), exceeding the prespecified 50% responder threshold.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="snm-fi-pivotal" />

## Design

Candidates underwent test stimulation and received chronic InterStim therapy only after at least 50% improvement. The primary efficacy objective required at least half of implanted patients to achieve a reduction of at least 50% in weekly incontinent episodes at 12 months. There was no randomized comparison group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Complete continence at 12 months was reported in 41%; the abstract reports mean weekly incontinent episodes of 9.4 at baseline and 1.9 at 12 months. Therapeutic success was 85% at 24 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the three-year report, 83 patients completed all or part of that assessment. Therapeutic success was 86%, and complete continence was 40%.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> At five years, 76 of the original 120 implanted patients remained in follow-up. Among 72 with evaluable episode diaries, 64 (89%) had at least 50% fewer episodes and 26 (36%) reported complete continence; mean weekly episodes were 9.1 at baseline and 1.7 at five years in the long-term analysis.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Of 133 patients who underwent test stimulation, 120 received chronic InterStim implantation, a reported 90% test success rate.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The five-year report describes a 14-day test stimulation period before permanent implantation in responders.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Adverse events

The primary report stated that no unanticipated adverse device effects were reported.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Through mean 36-month follow-up, device- or therapy-related events included implant-site pain in 28%, paresthesia in 15%, altered sensation of stimulation in 12%, and infection in 10%.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Among 76 patients followed for at least five years, 27 (35.5%) required device revision, replacement, or explantation.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

Only patients who responded to test stimulation received an implant. The study had no concurrent control arm. The five-year success estimate used episode diaries from 72 of the 120 originally implanted patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The American Society of Colon and Rectal Surgeons' 2023 guideline says sacral neuromodulation may be considered a first-line surgical option for incontinent patients with or without sphincter defects <Rec>(Conditional; Low)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The FDA approved the Medtronic InterStim system on March 14, 2011, for chronic fecal incontinence after failure or intolerance of more conservative treatments.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
