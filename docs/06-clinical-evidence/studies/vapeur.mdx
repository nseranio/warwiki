# VAPEUR-RCT: Rezūm Water Vapor Therapy Versus Combination Pharmacotherapy for Benign Prostatic Obstruction

<TrialBottomLine id="vapeur" />

**VAPEUR-RCT** found greater one-year urinary symptom improvement with Rezūm water vapor thermal therapy than with an alpha-blocker plus a 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor in 151 sexually active men with benign prostatic obstruction refractory to alpha-blocker monotherapy. IPSS noninferiority and superiority met their prespecified tests; MSHQ superiority did not meet its threshold under multiple imputation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="vapeur" />

## Design

The trial randomized 75 men to Rezūm and 76 to combination pharmacotherapy. Eligible men were at least 45 years old, with IPSS at least 13, peak flow at most 15 ml/s, post-void residual at most 250 ml, and prostate volume at least 30 ml. The three primary tests were 12-month IPSS noninferiority (3-point margin), then IPSS superiority and MSHQ superiority; the two superiority tests each used a one-sided 1.25% threshold. The [registered protocol](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04838769) specified open-label assignment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

IPSS improvement met noninferiority and superiority at 12 months: available-case mean improvement was 10.8 ± 6.8 points with Rezūm and 6.2 ± 7.7 with medication (difference in change scores −4.6 points, 97.5% CI −7.6 to −1.6; p &lt; 0.001). With multiple imputation, the difference was −6.5 points (97.5% CI −9.8 to −3.2; p &lt; 0.0001). IPSS reached the mild range (0–7) in 33/63 versus 8/57 available cases.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

MSHQ changed by +1.1 ± 16.9 versus −5.2 ± 16.4 points. The multiple-imputation difference was 7.0 points (97.5% CI −0.8 to 14.7; p = 0.022), which missed the prespecified 1.25% superiority threshold; last-observation-carried-forward analysis showed a 6.5-point difference (97.5% CI 0.7–12.3; p = 0.006).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Quality of life improved more after Rezūm: the IPSS quality-of-life item changed by −2.7 ± 1.8 versus −1.8 ± 2.0 points (p = 0.01). Surgical retreatment occurred in 1/75 (1.3%) versus 7/76 (9.2%), while new BPO medication was started in 9/75 (12%) versus 1/76 (1.3%). IPSS worsened by at least four points in 2/75 (2.7%) versus 10/76 (13%); retention requiring catheterization after 90 days occurred in 0/75 versus 1/76 (1.3%). The prespecified composite of these four outcomes had a hazard ratio of 0.52 (CI 0.25–1.1; p = 0.08), missing its 1.25% threshold.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Rezūm was performed under ambulatory general anesthesia, with a mean 5.1 ± 2.1 vapor treatments per prostate and 13.8 ± 16.6 minutes of procedure time (n = 72); the median lobe was treated in 45/72 (63%). All patients who underwent Rezūm were discharged the same day with a catheter (mean dwell 5.8 ± 2.20 days). The medication arm continued an alpha-blocker and added a 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor within seven days; at one year, 75% reported taking at least 85% of doses.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Treatment-related adverse events affected 30/75 (40%) after Rezūm and 21/76 (28%) with medication; serious adverse events affected 9/75 (12%) and 1/76 (1.3%), respectively. Six serious Rezūm events were procedure-related: two urinary retention events and one each of bacterial prostatitis, prostatic obstruction, persistent LUTS, and anejaculation. Infections affected 14/75 (19%) Rezūm patients and were graded Clavien-Dindo II; catheterization to manage an adverse event occurred in 9/75 (12%) versus 1/76 (1.3%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Open-label assignment could influence patient-reported symptom and sexual-function scores. The protocol was redesigned for IPSS noninferiority after an enrollment shortfall, before any outcomes review. The one-year results leave later comparative retreatment and adverse-event outcomes unresolved.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
