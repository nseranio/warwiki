# PILLAR: Mirabegron for Overactive Bladder Wet in Older Adults

<TrialBottomLine id="pillar" />

**PILLAR** was a multicenter, double-blind phase 4 randomized trial of mirabegron versus placebo in community-dwelling adults aged at least 65 years with overactive bladder wet; mirabegron significantly improved both coprimary 12-week diary outcomes, daily micturitions and incontinence episodes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="pillar" />

## Design

After a 2-week placebo run-in, eligibility required overactive bladder for at least 3 months, at least one incontinence episode and three urgency episodes during a 3-day diary, and an average of at least eight micturitions per 24 hours. The trial randomized 888 patients: 445 to mirabegron and 443 to matched placebo. Treatment began at 25 mg daily, with optional escalation to 50 mg after week 4 or 8 according to efficacy, tolerability, and patient and investigator discretion.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The two coprimary endpoints were change from baseline to end of treatment in mean micturitions and incontinence episodes per 24 hours. The trial used analysis of covariance for micturitions and stratified rank analysis of covariance for incontinence episodes. Secondary diary measures included voided volume per micturition, urgency episodes, and urgency-incontinence episodes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Both coprimary changes favored mirabegron at 12 weeks and were reported as statistically significant. Mean voided volume per micturition, daily urgency episodes, and daily urgency-incontinence episodes also improved significantly versus placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the prespecified cognitive safety analysis, adjusted mean Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) change at week 12 was −0.2 points (SE 0.1) with mirabegron and −0.1 points (SE 0.1) with placebo (p=0.471). A post hoc analysis found a decline of at least four MoCA points in 24/425 (5.6%) mirabegron patients and 24/411 (5.8%) placebo patients with available scores.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Of the 445 patients assigned mirabegron, 219 (49.2%) increased to 50 mg. Adverse events were collected through week 16, after the 12-week treatment period.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Adverse events

In the treated safety set, at least one treatment-emergent adverse event occurred in 209/445 (47.0%) mirabegron patients and 174/442 (39.4%) placebo patients. Serious events occurred in 15/445 (3.4%) and 12/442 (2.7%), respectively; investigators attributed no serious event to mirabegron. The safety analysis did not perform inferential comparisons between groups.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Urinary tract infection adverse events occurred in 25/445 (5.6%) versus 31/442 (7.0%). Urinary retention was reported in two patients per group, and none required catheterization. Increased blood pressure was recorded as an adverse event in five mirabegron patients and two placebo patients. Office and home vital-sign monitoring found no clinically meaningful between-group difference in mean changes through end of treatment.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

Treatment and cognitive follow-up ended at 12 weeks. Participants lived in the community and had low baseline Charlson comorbidity scores; the cognitive analysis was not powered to detect a difference in MoCA scores. Repeating the same MoCA version could produce a practice effect.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA/SUFU 2024 idiopathic overactive bladder guideline cites PILLAR in its discussion of efficacy by age. Statement 16 recommends offering an antimuscarinic or β3 agonist to improve urgency, frequency, and/or urgency urinary incontinence <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade A)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
