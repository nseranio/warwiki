# Fesoterodine pivotal trials: Fixed-dose treatment for overactive bladder

<TrialBottomLine id="fesoterodine-pivotal" />

**SP583** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized trial of fesoterodine 4 mg or 8 mg daily versus placebo, with tolterodine ER 4 mg as an active control, in adults with overactive bladder; both fesoterodine doses improved micturition frequency, urgency urinary incontinence, and patient-rated treatment response at 12 weeks versus placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="fesoterodine-pivotal" />

## Design

The international SP583 trial randomized 1,135 participants. Its primary endpoint was change in micturitions per 24 hours; coprimary endpoints were urgency urinary incontinence episodes and a yes-or-no treatment response based on a four-point patient benefit scale. SP583 required at least eight daily voids and either at least six urgency or at least three UUI episodes in a three-day diary.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The separate US trial, SP584, randomized 836 participants to the same two fesoterodine doses or placebo, without a tolterodine arm. Both used fixed daily doses for 12 weeks. SP584 required at least six months of overactive bladder symptoms and excluded neurogenic overactive bladder, clinically relevant obstruction, and baseline post-void residual volume greater than 100 mL.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Results

In SP583’s full analysis set, least-squares mean daily micturitions fell by 1.76 with fesoterodine 4 mg (n=265), 1.88 with 8 mg (n=276), 1.73 with tolterodine ER (n=283), and 0.95 with placebo (n=279; P &lt; 0.001 for each fesoterodine dose versus placebo).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Among participants incontinent at baseline, daily urgency incontinence episodes fell by 1.95 (n=199), 2.22 (n=223), 1.74 (n=223), and 1.14 (n=211), respectively (P=0.001 for 4 mg; P &lt; 0.001 for 8 mg).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Patient-rated treatment response was 75% (n=265), 79% (n=276), 72% (n=283), and 53% (n=279), respectively (P &lt; 0.001 for each fesoterodine dose versus placebo).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

Mean voided volume increased by 27.72 mL with fesoterodine 4 mg (n=265), 33.62 mL with 8 mg (n=275), 23.64 mL with tolterodine (n=282), and 9.37 mL with placebo (n=278; nominal P &lt; 0.001 for each fesoterodine dose). Nocturnal micturition changes did not differ from placebo (P=0.394 and 0.418 for 4 and 8 mg).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the independent SP584 trial, micturitions fell by least-squares means of 1.61 with fesoterodine 4 mg (n=267), 2.09 with 8 mg (n=267), and 1.08 with placebo (n=266; P=0.032 and P &lt; 0.001 versus placebo). Among participants incontinent at baseline, daily urgency incontinence episodes fell by 1.65 (n=228), 2.28 (n=218), and 0.96 (n=205), respectively (P=0.003 and P &lt; 0.001). Patient-rated treatment response was 64% (n=267), 74% (n=267), and 45% (n=266), respectively (P &lt; 0.001 for each dose versus placebo). For the secondary endpoint of mean voided volume per micturition, changes were +16.5 mL (n=266), +33.6 mL (n=265), and +8.38 mL (n=261), respectively (nominal P=0.150 and P &lt; 0.001 versus placebo).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

The pooled open-label extensions retained 83/185 men (45%) and 356/705 women (50%) on fesoterodine for at least 24 months. At months 4, 12, and 24, at least 92% of men and 91% of women assessed rated tolerance good or excellent. At month 24, micturition diaries from 74 men and 325 women showed improvement from double-blind baseline (P &lt; 0.05 for men; P ≤ 0.0001 for women); no diary variable changed significantly from open-label baseline in men.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Treatment delivery

SP583 used a two-week placebo run-in and double-dummy treatment at 150 sites in 19 countries. 1,132/1,135 participants received medication.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In SP584, at least 96% of participants were compliant with study medication during the double-blind phase, and 681/836 randomized participants completed 12 weeks.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

In the extensions, all 890 participants began fesoterodine 8 mg daily; reduction to 4 mg was permitted after month 1, with reduction and re-escalation each allowed once annually. The initial 8 mg dose was maintained throughout treatment by 156/185 men and 525/705 women. Within 24 months, insufficient response led 30/185 men and 90/705 women to discontinue.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Adverse events

In SP583’s safety population, dry mouth occurred in 59/272 on fesoterodine 4 mg, 97/287 on 8 mg, 49/290 on tolterodine ER, and 20/283 on placebo; constipation occurred in 9/272, 13/287, 8/290, and 4/283, respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Adverse events led to discontinuation in 7/272, 14/287, 9/290, and 6/283.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Urinary retention occurred in 1/272 on 4 mg and 2/287 on 8 mg; the 4 mg event led to discontinuation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> One man on 8 mg required catheterization for acute retention.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

In SP584, dry mouth occurred in 45/282 on fesoterodine 4 mg, 99/279 on 8 mg, and 19/271 on placebo; constipation occurred in 14/282, 21/279, and 7/271, respectively.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Urinary tract infection occurred in 10/282, 15/279, and 11/271.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Urinary retention was reported as an adverse event in 4/282, 6/279, and 1/271; eight participants had post-void residual volume greater than 200 mL, and none required catheterization.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Adverse events led to withdrawal in 17/282, 25/279, and 11/271, respectively.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> In the pooled open-label extensions, dry mouth occurred in 44/185 men and 227/705 women, urinary tract infection in 6/185 and 128/705, and any treatment-emergent adverse event in 140/185 and 539/705.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Adverse events led to discontinuation in 29/185 men and 83/705 women over the full extension period.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Limitations

The controlled treatment period was 12 weeks in each trial. SP583 used tolterodine ER as an active control, while its primary efficacy comparisons tested fesoterodine against placebo. The extensions had no placebo group, enrolled double-blind trial completers after an administrative delay that precluded some eligible patients, and were analyzed by sex post hoc.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The FDA approved fesoterodine for overactive bladder symptoms of urgency urinary incontinence, urgency, and frequency on October 31, 2008.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
