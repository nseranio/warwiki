# Fesoterodine pivotal trials: Fixed-dose treatment for overactive bladder

<TrialBottomLine id="fesoterodine-pivotal" />

**SP583** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized trial of fesoterodine 4 mg or 8 mg daily versus placebo, with tolterodine ER 4 mg as an active control, in adults with overactive bladder; both fesoterodine doses improved micturition frequency, urgency urinary incontinence, and patient-rated treatment response at 12 weeks versus placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="fesoterodine-pivotal" />

## Design

The international SP583 trial randomized 1,135 participants. Its primary endpoint was change in micturitions per 24 hours; coprimary endpoints were urgency urinary incontinence episodes and a yes-or-no treatment response based on a four-point patient benefit scale. The separate US trial, SP584, randomized 836 participants to the same two fesoterodine doses or placebo, without a tolterodine arm. Both used fixed daily doses for 12 weeks. SP584 required at least six months of overactive bladder symptoms and excluded neurogenic overactive bladder, clinically relevant obstruction, and baseline post-void residual volume greater than 100 mL.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Results

In SP583, mean micturitions per day fell by 1.7 with fesoterodine 4 mg, 1.9 with 8 mg, and 1.0 with placebo (P &lt; 0.001 for each dose versus placebo).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Among participants incontinent at baseline, daily urgency incontinence episodes fell by 2.1 (n=199), 2.3 (n=223), and 1.2 (n=211), respectively (P=0.001 for 4 mg; P &lt; 0.001 for 8 mg).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Both fesoterodine groups also reported greater treatment response than placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

In the independent SP584 trial, micturitions fell by least-squares means of 1.61 with fesoterodine 4 mg (n=267), 2.09 with 8 mg (n=267), and 1.08 with placebo (n=266; P=0.032 and P &lt; 0.001 versus placebo). Among participants incontinent at baseline, daily urgency incontinence episodes fell by 1.65 (n=228), 2.28 (n=218), and 0.96 (n=205), respectively (P=0.003 and P &lt; 0.001). Patient-rated treatment response was 64% (n=267), 74% (n=267), and 45% (n=266), respectively (P &lt; 0.001 for each dose versus placebo). For the secondary endpoint of mean voided volume per micturition, changes were +16.5 mL (n=266), +33.6 mL (n=265), and +8.38 mL (n=261), respectively (nominal P=0.150 and P &lt; 0.001 versus placebo).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

In pooled open-label extensions of the two trials, 83/185 men (45%) and 356/705 women (50%) continued fesoterodine for at least 24 months. Bladder-diary improvements were sustained or increased among those followed through month 24.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Treatment delivery

In SP584, at least 96% of participants were compliant with study medication during the double-blind phase, and 681/836 randomized participants completed 12 weeks.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Adverse events

In SP584, dry mouth occurred in 45/282 on fesoterodine 4 mg, 99/279 on 8 mg, and 19/271 on placebo; constipation occurred in 14/282, 21/279, and 7/271, respectively.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Urinary tract infection occurred in 10/282, 15/279, and 11/271.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Urinary retention was reported as an adverse event in 4/282, 6/279, and 1/271; eight participants had post-void residual volume greater than 200 mL, and none required catheterization.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Adverse events led to withdrawal in 17/282, 25/279, and 11/271, respectively.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> In the pooled flexible-dose extensions, dry mouth occurred in 24% of men and 32% of women.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Limitations

The controlled treatment period was 12 weeks in each trial. SP583 used tolterodine ER as an active control, while its primary efficacy comparisons tested fesoterodine against placebo. The extensions had no placebo group and enrolled patients who completed the double-blind trials.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The FDA approved fesoterodine for overactive bladder symptoms of urgency urinary incontinence, urgency, and frequency on October 31, 2008.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
