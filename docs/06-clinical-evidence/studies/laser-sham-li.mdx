# Li fractional CO₂ laser–sham trial: Fractional Carbon Dioxide Laser for Postmenopausal Vaginal Symptoms

<TrialBottomLine id="laser-sham-li" />

**The Li trial** was a single-center, double-blind randomized comparison of fractional microablative CO₂ vaginal laser and sham laser in postmenopausal women seeking treatment for vaginal symptoms; neither prespecified symptom instrument showed a significant laser advantage at 12 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="laser-sham-li" />

## Design

Women had amenorrhea for at least 12 months and dyspareunia, burning, itching, or dryness severe enough to seek care. Prior vaginal energy treatment, active genital or urinary infection, prior vaginal mesh surgery, and prolapse protruding beyond the hymen were exclusions. Vaginal estrogen had to be stopped before enrollment. About half had iatrogenic menopause. All treatment took place at a Sydney tertiary referral hospital.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The co-primary instruments were the 0–100 visual analog scale (VAS) for overall symptom severity and the 0–20 Vulvovaginal Symptom Questionnaire (VSQ). Both had to show statistical significance for the trial to be declared positive. The investigators specified a 50% reduction in both severity scores as clinically important. Assessors and participants were masked; the treating physician knew the allocation and had no other participant interaction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Mean overall VAS change from baseline to 12 months was −17.2 points with laser and −26.6 with sham, a laser-minus-sham mean difference of 9.4 points (95% CI −28.6 to 47.5). VSQ changed by −3.1 and −1.6 points, respectively (mean difference −1.5, 95% CI −5.9 to 3.0). Neither co-primary comparison met the trial's threshold. The VAS change for each participant's most severe symptom was −24.5 versus −20.4 points (difference −4.1, 95% CI −32.5 to 24.3).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Quality-of-life change was +6.3 versus +1.4 points (reported mean difference 4.8, 95% CI −3.9 to 13.5), and Vaginal Health Index Score change was +0.9 versus +1.3 (difference −0.4, 95% CI −4.3 to 3.6). Among paired biopsies, postmenopausal-to-premenopausal histology shifts occurred in 2/22 (9%) and 3/24 (12.5%); the between-group histologic comparison was not significant.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Laser-certified physicians used the SmartXide² V2LR/MonaLisa Touch system for three sessions spaced 4–8 weeks apart. Active settings were 40 W, 1000 μs dwell time, 1000 μm DOT spacing, SmartStack 2 in DP mode, and 5.37 J/cm² fluence. Sham settings were 0.5 W, 100 μs dwell, 2000 μm DOT spacing, and SmartStack 1 in SmartPulse mode, delivering 0 J/cm². The device's visual and audible effects and the procedural maneuvers were matched.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Both arms received 4% topical lignocaine at the introitus for 20 minutes and plume evacuation. All randomized women received their assigned treatment without discontinuation. At 12 months, 38/43 (88.4%) laser recipients and 40/42 (95.2%) sham recipients completed follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

At least one adverse event was reported by 16/43 laser recipients and 17/42 sham recipients. Events included self-resolving vaginal pain or discomfort, spotting, discharge, and lower urinary tract symptoms or urinary infection. One laser recipient had an upper urinary tract infection treated with oral antibiotics. No severe adverse events were reported in either arm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The sample-size calculation assumed a large, 50% clinically important symptom reduction. The study was not powered to assess uncommon complications or differences between natural and iatrogenic menopause. Vaginal cytology was abandoned after poor early specimens and replaced with biopsies, leaving fewer paired histology samples. The 12-month follow-up missed five laser recipients and two sham recipients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA/SUFU/AUGS 2025 GSM guideline cites this trial among sham comparisons and advises counseling that evidence does not support CO₂ laser, Er:YAG laser, or radiofrequency for GSM symptoms and related quality-of-life outcomes <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation, Evidence Level: Grade C)</Rec>. It allows consideration of CO₂ laser through shared decision-making for patients who are not candidates for, or prefer alternatives to, FDA-approved GSM treatments, with disclosure that use outside clinical trials is experimental <Rec>(Expert Opinion)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
