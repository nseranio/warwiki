# TITAN 2: Implantable Tibial Neuromodulation for Urgency Urinary Incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="titan-2" />

**TITAN 2** was a prospective, multicenter, open-label, single-arm pivotal study of a leadless rechargeable tibial neurostimulator in 126 implanted adults with urgency urinary incontinence after failure or intolerance of at least two overactive bladder medications; its six-month responder rate exceeded the prespecified performance goal.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="titan-2" />

## Design

The study enrolled 188 people at 26 US sites and implanted 126. Implantation required neither a preliminary stimulation test nor prior percutaneous tibial neuromodulation. Three-day diaries measured daily urgency urinary incontinence (UUI) episodes. A responder had at least 50% fewer episodes at six months. The primary hypothesis tested whether the responder rate exceeded a 40% performance goal, with a one-sided alpha of 0.025. The goal was based on lower confidence limits from sacral neuromodulation studies and had also been used in the eCoin pivotal study.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The six-month efficacy analysis used participants with baseline and follow-up data because missingness stayed below the prespecified 5% imputation threshold. Missingness exceeded that threshold at 12 months, so the later analysis imputed outcomes for the implanted cohort. Safety was assessed in all implanted participants, with related events adjudicated by an external clinical events committee.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At six months, 59% had at least 50% fewer daily UUI episodes (95% CI 49%–67%; P &lt; 0.0001 versus the 40% goal). At 12 months, the imputed responder rate was 61%. Complete dryness on the diary was reported by 19% at six months and 22% at 12 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Among participants with baseline urinary frequency of at least 10 voids per day, mean frequency fell from 12.9 to 10.4 daily voids at six months, a mean reduction of 2.5 (P &lt; 0.0001). The mean Urgency Perception Scale increase was 0.4 points (95% CI 0.3–0.6; P &lt; 0.0001). Mean OAB-q health-related quality-of-life improvement was 27.1 points (95% CI 22.6–31.7) at six months and 25.5 points (95% CI 20.7–30.3) at 12 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Investigators placed the device under local anesthetic in a subcutaneous pocket superficial to the deep fascia over the tibial nerve. Their landmarks were the medial malleolus, posterior tibial border and anterior Achilles border. The article describes a mark 4 cm proximal to the malleolar midpoint, a second mark three-quarters of the way from tibia to Achilles, and an incision and insertion point 5 cm proximal to that mark. A suture loop at the proximal device end permitted retrieval for repositioning; shallow fixation to fascia was optional. The authors state that the commercial implant procedure differs in some respects from this investigational procedure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

All 126 implants elicited a sensory or motor response. Procedures occurred in hospital operating rooms (36/126), ambulatory surgical centers (44/126), and outpatient clinic procedure rooms (46/126). Therapy was to begin within 24 hours, initially programmed for 30 minutes every other day at 20 Hz and a 200-microsecond pulse width, with amplitude set to a comfortable sensory or motor response. Weekly charging was recommended for the investigational device; the article says the commercial device typically requires annual or twice-yearly charging. Follow-up was completed by 125 participants at six months and 118 at 12 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Through 12 months, 25/126 implanted participants (19.8%) had 30 related adverse device effects.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The article reported procedure-related implant-site infection in 7%; one wound infection led to device explantation, while the other infections resolved with antibiotics.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> It identified one serious related event, resolved *Clostridioides difficile* colitis possibly associated with perioperative antibiotics. The FDA's approval review additionally classified the wound infection requiring explantation as serious.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

Participants and investigators were unblinded, and the study had no sham or active comparator. The 40% performance goal came from other neuromodulation studies, so its clinical meaning depends on that choice. The published report covers outcomes through 12 months; longer follow-up was planned. Sponsor employees were among the authors, and the trial was Medtronic sponsored.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="titan-2" />

TITAN 2 used an implanted, automatically scheduled rechargeable stimulator. The Revi system uses an external wearable during stimulation, and eCoin is a nonrechargeable implant; their pivotal studies used different eligibility rules and stimulation schedules.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The FDA approved the Medtronic Altaviva system on September 18, 2025, for UUI in patients who failed or could not tolerate more conservative treatments. Its original approval rested on TITAN 2 data through 12 months.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
