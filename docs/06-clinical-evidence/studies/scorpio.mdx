# SCORPIO: Mirabegron Versus Placebo and Active Control in Overactive Bladder

<TrialBottomLine id="scorpio" />

**SCORPIO** was a multicenter, double-blind phase 3 trial in adults with overactive bladder that compared mirabegron 50 mg and 100 mg with placebo over 12 weeks, with tolterodine ER 4 mg as an active control. Both mirabegron doses met the co-primary placebo comparisons for daily incontinence episodes and micturitions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="scorpio" />

## Design

The trial randomized 1,987 adults at 189 sites across 27 countries in Europe and Australia; 1,978 received study drug. Entry required symptoms for at least 3 months, at least 8 micturitions per day, and at least 3 urgency episodes during a 3-day baseline diary. Stress-predominant mixed incontinence was excluded. A 2-week single-blind placebo run-in preceded once-daily oral treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The co-primary endpoints were adjusted mean change from baseline to the final visit in incontinence episodes and micturitions per 24 hours. The incontinence analysis included 1,165 participants with at least one baseline episode (FAS-I); the micturition analysis included 1,906 (FAS). Final-visit analyses used last observation carried forward. A prespecified stepwise gatekeeping procedure controlled multiplicity across the co-primary and three key secondary endpoints. Tolterodine was included as an active control; the planned primary comparisons were mirabegron against placebo, with a secondary tolterodine-placebo comparison.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

Both mirabegron doses met both co-primary endpoints at the final visit. In the FAS-I, adjusted daily incontinence episodes fell by 1.57 with 50 mg (n=293), 1.46 with 100 mg (n=281), 1.17 with placebo (n=291), and 1.27 with tolterodine (n=300). The reported placebo-adjusted differences were −0.41 episodes/day (95% CI −0.72 to −0.09; rank-based P=0.003) for 50 mg and −0.29 (95% CI −0.61 to 0.03; rank-based P=0.010) for 100 mg; the CIs came from a separate ANCOVA model. In the FAS, adjusted daily micturitions fell by 1.93 with 50 mg (n=473), 1.77 with 100 mg (n=478), 1.34 with placebo (n=480), and 1.59 with tolterodine (n=475). The reported differences were −0.60/day (95% CI −0.90 to −0.29; P &lt; 0.001) and −0.44/day (95% CI −0.74 to −0.13; P=0.005), respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The prespecified key secondary endpoints also favored both mirabegron doses over placebo. At the final visit, adjusted volume voided per micturition increased by 24.2 mL with 50 mg (n=472) and 25.6 mL with 100 mg (n=478), versus 12.3 mL with placebo (n=480): placebo-adjusted differences were 11.9 mL (95% CI 6.3–17.4; P &lt; 0.001) and 13.2 mL (95% CI 7.7–18.7; P &lt; 0.001). At week 4, incontinence episodes changed by −1.04/day with 50 mg (n=293) and −1.03/day with 100 mg (n=281), versus −0.65/day with placebo (n=291); micturitions changed by −1.16/day (n=471) and −1.29/day (n=477), versus −0.77/day (n=479). Placebo-adjusted changes were −0.39 and −0.38 incontinence episodes/day (95% CIs −0.71 to −0.06 and −0.71 to −0.05; P=0.002 for each), and −0.40 and −0.52 micturitions/day (95% CIs −0.66 to −0.13 and −0.79 to −0.26; P=0.004 and P &lt; 0.001), for 50 mg and 100 mg, respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In a post-hoc analysis of participants who had stopped an antimuscarinic for insufficient efficacy, mirabegron 50 mg had a placebo-adjusted mean difference of −0.76 incontinence episodes per day (95% CI −1.32 to −0.19) and −0.59 micturitions per day (95% CI −1.15 to −0.04).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Once-daily oral placebo (n=494), mirabegron 50 mg (n=493), mirabegron 100 mg (n=496), or tolterodine ER 4 mg (n=495) was given for 12 weeks after the placebo run-in. Adverse events and concomitant medication were assessed 30 days after treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 211/493 (42.8%) with mirabegron 50 mg, 199/496 (40.1%) with 100 mg, 214/494 (43.3%) with placebo, and 231/495 (46.7%) with tolterodine. Dry mouth occurred in 14/493 (2.8%), 14/496 (2.8%), 13/494 (2.6%), and 50/495 (10.1%), respectively; hypertension in 29/493 (5.9%), 27/496 (5.4%), 38/494 (7.7%), and 40/495 (8.1%). Urinary tract infection occurred in 7/493 (1.4%), 9/496 (1.8%), 7/494 (1.4%), and 10/495 (2.0%), respectively. At the final visit, postvoid residual exceeded 300 mL in 1/493 with 50 mg, 2/496 with 100 mg, and 1/495 with tolterodine. Study drug was discontinued due to an adverse event in 4.9%, 3.2%, 2.6%, and 4.4% of the respective safety sets.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Treatment lasted 12 weeks. The trial did not make a direct statistical comparison between mirabegron and tolterodine. The prior-treatment analysis was post hoc and was not powered for treatment differences within its subgroups; participants could give multiple reasons for stopping prior medication.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

- **AUA/SUFU (2024).** Offer antimuscarinic medications or beta-3 agonists to patients with overactive bladder to improve urgency, frequency, and/or urgency urinary incontinence <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade A)</Rec>. Its evidence review cites both the primary report and the post-hoc analysis.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **EAU (2026).** Offer beta-3 agonists to women with overactive bladder who fail conservative management <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **FDA (June 28, 2012).** Myrbetriq (mirabegron extended-release tablets) was approved for adult overactive bladder.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
