# Lactin-V: Intravaginal Lactobacillus crispatus for Recurrent UTI Prevention

<TrialBottomLine id="lactin-v" />

**Lactin-V** was a single-center, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 2 randomized trial of intravaginal *Lactobacillus crispatus* CTV-05 versus placebo in 100 premenopausal women after treatment of acute uncomplicated cystitis; fewer women receiving Lactin-V had a recurrent UTI during 10 weeks, but the clinical difference did not reach statistical significance.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="lactin-v" />

## Design

The University of Washington student health center enrolled women aged 18–40 years who had current symptomatic cystitis and at least one previously treated symptomatic UTI in the preceding 12 months. Participants with pyelonephritis, urologic abnormalities or renal stones, diabetes, immunocompromise, pregnancy, or persistent symptoms or pyuria after treatment were excluded. Random assignment used computer-generated blocks, and identical packets concealed allocation from clinicians and laboratory staff.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Recurrence required dysuria, frequency, or urgency together with pyuria and a positive midstream urine culture. The report named both reduction in cystitis incidence and high-level vaginal *L. crispatus* colonization at the final visit as primary objectives. Colonization was measured by strain-specific quantitative polymerase chain reaction; high level meant at least 10^6 16S rRNA gene copies per vaginal swab. The authors reported relative risks and confidence intervals but did not specify an alpha threshold in the published statistical plan.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Recurrent UTI occurred in 7/48 (15%) Lactin-V recipients and 13/48 (27%) placebo recipients by 10 weeks (RR 0.5, 95% CI 0.2–1.2). At the final visit, high-level *L. crispatus* colonization was present in 39/42 (93%) and 30/44 (68%), respectively (P = 0.004). The colonization comparison was significant in the authors' analysis, although a formal alpha threshold was not given.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Within the Lactin-V arm, sustained high-level colonization was associated with fewer recurrences; the corresponding colonization pattern in the placebo arm was not. Colonization was measured after treatment, making this subgroup analysis observational.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

After standard antimicrobial treatment for the index cystitis, participants self-administered gelatin vaginal capsules without an applicator. Lactin-V contained *L. crispatus* CTV-05 at 10^8 CFU/mL. Capsules were used daily for 5 days and then weekly for 10 weeks. Scheduled assessments were at 1 and 10 weeks after starting treatment; symptomatic episodes prompted additional assessment and culture. Participants who developed cystitis were treated and remained in the study. Recurrence outcomes were evaluable in 48/50 randomized women in each arm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Adverse effects were reported by 56% of Lactin-V recipients and 50% of placebo recipients. Vaginal discharge or itching and moderate abdominal discomfort were the most commonly reported effects. Bacterial vaginosis or candidal vaginitis occurred in 0–5% across the arms. One placebo recipient and no Lactin-V recipient discontinued treatment for adverse effects. No pyelonephritis episodes were reported in either group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

This single-center phase 2 study enrolled predominantly young, sexually active women after an acute cystitis episode and assessed recurrence for 10 weeks. The small recurrence analysis produced an imprecise effect estimate. Lactin-V was compared with placebo after acute antimicrobial treatment; the study did not directly compare it with antimicrobial prophylaxis. Colonization after treatment was a measured response, so the within-arm colonization association cannot establish which patients would benefit before treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="lactin-v" />

Other recurrent-UTI probiotic trials have tested different strains, delivery routes, and comparators. The regimen and measured vaginal colonization here are specific to *L. crispatus* CTV-05.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU 2026 Urological Infections guideline advises local or oral probiotics containing strains of proven efficacy for vaginal flora regeneration to prevent cystitis <Rec>(Weak)</Rec>. Its evidence summary names *L. crispatus* CTV-05 among strains with a trend toward preventing recurrence.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
