# Chapple OAB Botox Trial: OnabotulinumtoxinA 100 U for Idiopathic Overactive Bladder with Urinary Incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="chapple-oab-botox" />

The **Chapple OAB Botox Trial** was a multicenter, double-blind phase 3 randomized comparison of intradetrusor onabotulinumtoxinA 100 U and placebo in 548 adults with idiopathic overactive bladder and urinary incontinence inadequately managed by anticholinergics; both coprimary outcomes favored onabotulinumtoxinA at week 12.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="chapple-oab-botox" />

## Design

Eligibility required at least 3 urgency incontinence episodes over 3 days, at least 8 daily micturitions and baseline PVR at most 100 mL. Randomization assigned 277 patients to onabotulinumtoxinA and 271 to placebo. The intention-to-treat analysis used all randomized patients and last observation carried forward for missing coprimary data. Coprimary endpoints were daily incontinence episodes and positive Treatment Benefit Scale response at week 12.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Both coprimary outcomes favored onabotulinumtoxinA at week 12: mean daily incontinence episodes changed by −2.95 (95% CI −3.37 to −2.53; ITT n = 277) versus −1.03 (95% CI −1.39 to −0.67; ITT n = 271; P &lt; 0.001), and positive Treatment Benefit Scale responses occurred in 62.8% versus 26.8% (P &lt; 0.001). Urgency incontinence episodes also fell by −2.80 versus −0.82 per day, and I-QOL total scores rose by 23.1 versus 6.3 points at week 12. Improvement in urgency, micturition, nocturia and other quality-of-life domains was greater with onabotulinumtoxinA.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

A prespecified analysis pooled this cohort with its companion trial: among 1,105 patients, incontinence reduction favored onabotulinumtoxinA across groups with 1, 2 or at least 3 prior anticholinergics (P &lt; 0.001 each). The open-label pooled extension found week-12 mean reductions of 3.1 to 3.8 incontinence episodes per day after treatments 1 through 6, among 812 to 136 patients with available data; positive Treatment Benefit Scale responses ranged from 74.0% to 83.2% across those cycles.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Its 7.6-month median was calculated from patient-level median effect durations of 438 patients who received only 100 U and had complete retreatment cycles; 430/839 extension entrants (51.3%) completed 3.5 years.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Treatment used 20 intradetrusor injections of 0.5 mL, totaling 100 U in the active arm and sparing the trigone.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Patients could receive local bladder anesthesia and/or sedation. Retreatment with 100 U could begin at week 12 if requested and at least 2 incontinence episodes were recorded over 3 days.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The extension analyzed up to 6 treatments of 100 U; its retreatment criteria also required PVR below 200 mL.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Adverse events

Urinary tract events were more frequent with onabotulinumtoxinA in the 2013 safety population (274 active, 270 placebo): within 12 weeks, UTI occurred in 56/274 (20.4%) versus 14/270 (5.2%), and urinary retention in 16/274 (5.8%) versus 1/270 (0.4%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Across the entire treatment cycle, CIC was initiated in 19/274 (6.9%) versus 2/270 (0.7%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Mean PVR at week 2 was 46.9 mL versus 10.1 mL (P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In the two-trial pooled safety population, urinary tract infection occurred in 141/552 (25.5%) versus 52/542 (9.6%), and urinary retention in 32/552 (5.8%) versus 2/542 (0.4%).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In the long-term pooled cohort, de novo CIC followed the first 100 U treatment in 33/828 patients (4.0%); urinary retention occurred in 32/829 (3.9%), and subsequent-cycle de novo CIC ranged from 0.6% to 1.7%.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

The 2013 placebo comparison ended at week 12 because retreatment could follow; its UTI definition included laboratory-positive cases without symptoms. The extension enrolled completers of both pivotal trials and had no placebo arm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="chapple-oab-botox" />

The companion Nitti trial tested the same dose against placebo; subsequent reports combined both cohorts.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

- **AUA/SUFU (Idiopathic Overactive Bladder, 2024), statement 25.** Offer sacral neuromodulation, percutaneous tibial nerve stimulation and/or intradetrusor botulinum toxin injection after inadequate response to, or intolerable adverse effects from, pharmacotherapy or behavioral therapy <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade A)</Rec>. The guideline cites Chapple among its placebo-controlled toxin trials.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **AUA/SUFU (2024), statement 26.** Measure PVR before intradetrusor botulinum toxin injection <Rec>(Clinical Principle)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **FDA (January 18, 2013).** Approved BOTOX for adults with overactive bladder symptoms of incontinence, urgency and frequency after inadequate response to, or intolerance of, an anticholinergic medication.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
