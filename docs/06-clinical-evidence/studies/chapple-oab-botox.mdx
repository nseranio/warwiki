# Chapple OAB Botox Trial: OnabotulinumtoxinA 100 U for Idiopathic Overactive Bladder with Urinary Incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="chapple-oab-botox" />

The **Chapple OAB Botox Trial** was a multicenter, double-blind phase 3 randomized comparison of intradetrusor onabotulinumtoxinA 100 U and placebo in 548 adults with idiopathic overactive bladder and urinary incontinence inadequately managed by anticholinergics; both coprimary outcomes favored onabotulinumtoxinA at week 12.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="chapple-oab-botox" />

## Design

Eligibility required at least 3 urgency incontinence episodes over 3 days and at least 8 daily micturitions. Randomization assigned 277 patients to onabotulinumtoxinA and 271 to placebo. Coprimary endpoints were daily incontinence episodes and positive Treatment Benefit Scale response at week 12.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Mean daily incontinence episodes changed by −2.95 with onabotulinumtoxinA versus −1.03 with placebo (P &lt; 0.001). Positive Treatment Benefit Scale responses occurred in 62.8% versus 26.8% (P &lt; 0.001). Both coprimary endpoints met their superiority tests. The primary abstract also reports greater improvement in urgency, micturition, nocturia and quality-of-life domains.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

A prespecified analysis pooled this cohort with its companion trial: among 1,105 patients, incontinence reduction favored onabotulinumtoxinA across groups with 1, 2 or at least 3 prior anticholinergics (P &lt; 0.001 each). The pooled extension calculated a 7.6-month median from the patient-level median effect durations of 438 patients who received only 100 U and had complete retreatment cycles; 430/839 extension entrants (51.3%) completed 3.5 years.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Treatment used 20 intradetrusor injections of 0.5 mL, totaling 100 U in the active arm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The pivotal injections spared the trigone. The extension analyzed up to 6 treatments of 100 U.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Adverse events

CIC was initiated in 6.9% of onabotulinumtoxinA recipients versus 0.7% of placebo recipients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Mean PVR at week 2 was 46.9 mL versus 10.1 mL (P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In the two-trial pooled safety population, urinary tract infection occurred in 141/552 (25.5%) versus 52/542 (9.6%), and urinary retention in 32/552 (5.8%) versus 2/542 (0.4%).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In the long-term pooled cohort, de novo CIC followed the first 100 U treatment in 33/828 patients (4.0%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

The comparator was placebo. The extension enrolled completers of both pivotal trials and had no placebo arm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="chapple-oab-botox" />

The companion Nitti trial tested the same dose against placebo; subsequent reports combined both cohorts.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

- **AUA/SUFU (Idiopathic Overactive Bladder, 2024), statement 25.** Offer sacral neuromodulation, percutaneous tibial nerve stimulation and/or intradetrusor botulinum toxin injection after inadequate response to, or intolerable adverse effects from, pharmacotherapy or behavioral therapy <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade A)</Rec>. The guideline cites Chapple among its placebo-controlled toxin trials.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **AUA/SUFU (2024), statement 26.** Measure PVR before intradetrusor botulinum toxin injection <Rec>(Clinical Principle)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **FDA (January 18, 2013).** Approved BOTOX for adults with overactive bladder symptoms of incontinence, urgency and frequency after inadequate response to, or intolerance of, an anticholinergic medication.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
