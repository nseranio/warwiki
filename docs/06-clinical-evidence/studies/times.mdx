# TIMES: Tolterodine and Tamsulosin in Men With LUTS and Overactive Bladder

<TrialBottomLine id="times" />

**TIMES** was a double-blind, four-arm randomized trial in 879 men with bothersome LUTS and diary-documented overactive bladder (OAB) that compared tolterodine ER plus tamsulosin, either drug alone, and placebo; after 12 weeks, 172/215 (80.0%) combination recipients versus 132/214 (61.7%) placebo recipients reported treatment benefit (risk difference 18.3 percentage points, 95% CI 9–28; P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="times" />

## Design

Ninety-five US urology clinics enrolled men aged at least 40 years with total IPSS at least 12, IPSS quality-of-life item at least 3, at least moderate bladder bother, at least eight micturitions and three urgency episodes per day, and symptoms for at least three months. Urgency urinary incontinence was optional. Men with postvoid residual (PVR) &gt;200 mL **and** maximum flow &lt;5 mL/s, prior catheterized retention, prior prostate surgery, neurologic conditions, or contraindications to antimuscarinics were excluded. Recent α-blocker and antimuscarinic use also precluded enrollment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The prespecified primary endpoint was a yes/no response to whether treatment had provided any benefit at week 12. The trial was powered for the combination-versus-placebo comparison at a two-sided alpha of 0.05. The protocol-specified efficacy population required at least one dose and a postbaseline assessment; missing responses were imputed from the last observation. Bladder diaries, IPSS, ultrasound PVR, and uroflowmetry supplied secondary and safety measures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The protocol-specified primary analysis included 215 combination, 209 tolterodine ER, 207 tamsulosin, and 214 placebo recipients. Perceived benefit was reported by 136/209 (65.1%) with tolterodine ER and 146/207 (70.5%) with tamsulosin.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Neither monotherapy differed significantly from placebo on this endpoint; the corrected tamsulosin-versus-placebo P value is 0.064.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

At week 12, adjusted mean changes with combination therapy versus placebo were −0.88 versus −0.31 urgency incontinence episodes per day (P = 0.005; among men incontinent at baseline), −3.33 versus −2.54 urgency episodes without incontinence (P = 0.03), −2.54 versus −1.41 daily micturitions (P &lt; 0.001), and −0.59 versus −0.39 nocturnal micturitions (P = 0.02). Total IPSS changed by −8.02 versus −6.19 points (P = 0.003).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Combination therapy improved storage measures versus placebo in both post hoc baseline PSA strata at week 12: at PSA ≥1.3 ng/mL (the median), 24-hour frequency changed by −2.88 versus −1.65 micturitions (108 versus 106 men; P &lt; 0.001), frequency-urgency sum by −11.78 versus −8.25 (P = 0.006), and IPSS storage score by −4.30 versus −2.92 (P = 0.001). At PSA &lt;1.3 ng/mL, tolterodine ER and combination therapy reduced 24-hour frequency by −2.08 and −2.11 versus −1.20 with placebo (111, 109, and 109 men; each P = 0.010) and improved IPSS storage scores (P = 0.033 and 0.018). In the higher-PSA stratum, tamsulosin improved total IPSS and IPSS voiding scores versus placebo (P = 0.007 and 0.005); neither comparison was significant in the lower-PSA stratum.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Participants took tolterodine ER 4 mg, tamsulosin 0.4 mg, both agents, or matched placebo once daily, about 30 minutes after dinner. More than 95% of the efficacy population in each arm took at least 80% of assigned medication.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

In the treated safety population, dry mouth occurred in 47/225 (20.9%) combination recipients, 16/216 (7.4%) tolterodine ER recipients, 15/215 (7.0%) tamsulosin recipients, and 5/220 (2.3%) placebo recipients. Five men stopped combination treatment because of dry mouth. Catheterization for acute urinary retention occurred in 1/225 (0.4%) on combination therapy, 1/216 (0.5%) on tolterodine ER, and none on tamsulosin or placebo. Mean PVR changed by +6.42 mL on combination therapy and −3.61 mL on placebo; between-group PVR changes were not significant.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The PSA subgroup report recorded urinary retention in four men with PSA ≥1.3 ng/mL and none with PSA &lt;1.3 ng/mL; one recipient each of tolterodine ER and combination therapy discontinued because of acute retention.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

Treatment lasted 12 weeks. The trial was powered for combination therapy versus placebo, while the monotherapy comparisons and PSA-stratified analysis have less secure inference. Prior catheterized retention and the specified high-PVR, low-flow combination were excluded. Pfizer funded the study, maintained the database, and performed its statistical analyses.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="times" />

TIMES started both agents together after medication washout. The EAU recommendation for adding an antimuscarinic after insufficient monotherapy draws on a broader set of combination trials.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The **EAU 2026 male LUTS guideline** recommends an α1-blocker plus a muscarinic receptor antagonist when moderate-to-severe LUTS include storage symptoms insufficiently relieved by either drug alone <Rec>(Weak)</Rec>. It also recommends against the combination when PVR exceeds 150 mL <Rec>(Weak)</Rec> and recommends measuring PVR during treatment.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
