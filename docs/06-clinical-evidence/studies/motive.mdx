# MOTIVE: Behavioral Versus Drug Treatment for Overactive Bladder in Men

<TrialBottomLine id="motive" />

**MOTIVE** was a two-site randomized equivalence trial in men with persistent urgency and frequency after alpha-blocker treatment that compared behavioral therapy with added oxybutynin extended release and found equivalent mean daily voiding frequency after eight weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="motive" />

## Design

The Veterans Affairs outpatient trial randomized 143 volunteers aged 42–88 years (73 to behavioral treatment, 70 to drug therapy) who still had urgency and more than eight voids per day after a four-week alpha-blocker run-in; men with low uroflow or elevated postvoid residual were excluded. The prespecified equivalence margin for posttreatment daily voiding frequency was ±15% of the drug-group mean. A research assistant blinded to treatment group scored the seven-day bladder diaries, which measured voiding frequency, nocturia and incontinence; a validated scale measured urgency.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Daily voiding frequency was equivalent between groups at eight weeks: among 64 behavioral and 60 drug-treatment completers, mean daily voids fell from 11.3 to 9.1 and from 11.5 to 9.5, respectively (baseline-adjusted equivalence P = .001). The last-observation-carried-forward analysis of all 143 randomized men also met equivalence (P = .003). Nocturia decreased by a mean 0.70 versus 0.32 episodes per night among 64 versus 60 completers (P = .05), favoring behavioral treatment; maximum urgency scores decreased by 0.12 versus 0.44 among 61 versus 60 assessed completers (P = .02), favoring oxybutynin.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Behavioral treatment reduced nocturia more than oxybutynin in the planned secondary analysis of the same trial: among 127 men with at least one baseline episode per night (64 behavioral, 63 drug), mean reductions were 0.97 versus 0.56 episodes (P = .01). Among 76 men with at least two baseline episodes (38 per arm), reductions were 1.26 versus 0.61 episodes (P = .008). Missing posttreatment values were carried forward.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Both strategies were added after alpha-blocker treatment and delivered over four clinic visits in eight weeks. Behavioral treatment taught pelvic floor contractions with anal-palpation feedback (usually 15 exercises three times daily), delayed voiding, urge suppression and fluid restriction after 6 p.m. Oxybutynin extended release started at 10 mg/day and was titrated to 5–30 mg/day; a postvoid residual &gt;150 mL prompted dose reduction to 5 mg. Treatment and posttreatment assessment were completed by 64/73 behavioral and 60/70 drug participants.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the nocturia analysis, self-rated pelvic floor exercise adherence was 63% of prescribed exercises, and pill-count oxybutynin adherence averaged 96%.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

At eight weeks, somewhat or extremely bothersome side effects were reported by 12.6% of behavioral-treatment respondents versus 28.8% of drug-treatment respondents (P = .01 for the ordinal side-effect ratings).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The two-site volunteer sample and eight-week randomized period limit the duration and setting represented by the equivalence result. The comparison used one antimuscarinic drug, and the later nocturia analysis included only men with baseline nocturia.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA 2026 LUTS/BPH guideline cites MOTIVE and advises adding behavioral or lifestyle interventions for persistent storage symptoms despite pharmacologic treatment <Rec>(Expert Opinion)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
