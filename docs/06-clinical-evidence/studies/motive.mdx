# MOTIVE: Behavioral Versus Drug Treatment for Overactive Bladder in Men

<TrialBottomLine id="motive" />

**MOTIVE** was a two-site randomized equivalence trial in men with persistent urgency and frequency after alpha-blocker treatment that compared behavioral therapy with added oxybutynin extended release and found equivalent mean daily voiding frequency after eight weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="motive" />

## Design

The Veterans Affairs outpatient trial randomized 143 volunteers aged 42–88 years who still had urgency and more than eight voids per day after a four-week alpha-blocker run-in; men with bladder outlet obstruction were excluded. The behavioral program combined pelvic floor muscle exercises, urge suppression and delayed voiding. The drug arm received individually titrated oxybutynin extended release, 5–30 mg/day. Seven-day bladder diaries measured voiding frequency, nocturia and incontinence; a validated scale measured urgency.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Mean daily voids fell from 11.3 to 9.1 with behavioral treatment and from 11.5 to 9.5 with oxybutynin. The posttreatment means met the trial's equivalence test (P &lt; .01). Nocturia decreased by a mean 0.70 versus 0.32 episodes per night (P = .05), favoring behavioral treatment; maximum urgency scores decreased by 0.12 versus 0.44 (P = .02), favoring oxybutynin.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the later analysis of 127 participants with at least one baseline nocturia episode per night, behavioral treatment reduced nocturia by a mean 0.97 episodes versus 0.56 with oxybutynin (P = .01). Among 76 men with at least two baseline episodes, the reductions were 1.26 versus 0.61 (P = .008). These were analyses of subsets from the same randomized trial.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Both strategies were added after alpha-blocker treatment. The behavioral intervention used pelvic floor training and bladder-control techniques rather than a procedure; the comparator was titrated oxybutynin extended release.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The two-site volunteer sample and eight-week randomized period limit the duration and setting represented by the equivalence result. The comparison used one antimuscarinic drug, and the later nocturia analysis included only men with baseline nocturia.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA 2026 LUTS/BPH guideline cites MOTIVE and advises adding behavioral or lifestyle interventions for persistent storage symptoms despite pharmacologic treatment <Rec>(Expert Opinion)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
