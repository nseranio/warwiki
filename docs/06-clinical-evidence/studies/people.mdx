# PEOPLE: Pessary Versus Surgery for Symptomatic Pelvic Organ Prolapse

<TrialBottomLine id="people" />

**PEOPLE** was a multicenter, open-label noninferiority randomized trial in women with symptomatic stage 2 or greater prolapse that compared initial pessary treatment with prolapse surgery; pessary treatment did not meet the prespecified criterion for noninferior patient-reported improvement at 24 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="people" />

## Design

Participants had moderate to severe prolapse symptoms, a successful 30-minute pessary fitting trial, and no prior prolapse or incontinence surgery or pessary use. Participating gynecologists had each fitted at least 100 pessaries and performed at least 100 vaginal prolapse operations. Treatment allocation could not be concealed from patients or clinicians.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The primary endpoint was a response of “much better” or “very much better” on the Patient Global Impression of Improvement (PGI-I) scale at 24 months. The prespecified noninferiority margin was −10 percentage points for the pessary-minus-surgery risk difference; both the as-randomized and per-protocol analyses informed the conclusion. The per-protocol group retained pessary recipients still using a pessary and surgery recipients who had received no additional prolapse treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Among primary-outcome respondents, 132/173 assigned to pessary treatment (76.3%) and 132/162 assigned to surgery (81.5%) reported improvement. The reported risk difference was −6.1 percentage points; its one-sided 95% confidence lower bound was −12.7 points (P=.16 for noninferiority), crossing the −10-point margin. In the per-protocol analysis, improvement occurred in 52/74 (70.3%) versus 125/150 (83.3%); risk difference −13.1 points, lower bound −23.0 points (P=.69 for noninferiority).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

An exploratory secondary PGI-S comparison found improved symptom severity in 121/162 pessary respondents (74.7%) versus 134/157 surgery respondents (85.4%); risk difference −10.7 points (95% CI −19.3 to −2.0). The as-randomized PFDI-20 and PFIQ-7 comparisons did not show between-group differences.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

A subsequent economic evaluation of the randomized cohort reported mean 24-month healthcare costs of €3,932 with initial pessary treatment and €5,782 with surgery (unadjusted mean difference −€1,850, 95% CI −€2,228 to −€1,476). Mean quality-adjusted life-years were 1.80 and 1.82, respectively (difference −0.02, 95% CI −0.06 to 0.02). These estimates used Dutch care costs.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Supportive and occlusive pessaries were allowed; expulsion could prompt refitting with another size or shape. Women cleaned their own pessaries or attended clinician visits at least every 4 months. Surgical procedures were selected by the treating gynecologist with the patient: anterior or posterior colporrhaphy for the corresponding compartment, hysterectomy or uterine preservation for uterine descent, and abdominal sacrocolpopexy when indicated. No concomitant stress-incontinence operations were performed.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

All 218 assigned to pessary started treatment; 199/221 eligible women assigned surgery underwent an operation (90.0%). During follow-up, 118/218 pessary recipients (54.1%) crossed over to surgery, and 123/204 with treatment-status data discontinued pessary use. After initial surgery, 12/221 women (5.4%) received additional treatment and 8/221 (3.6%) underwent repeat surgery.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Among pessary recipients, discomfort occurred in 93/218 (42.7%), expulsion in 45/218 (20.6%), and vaginal bleeding in 25/218 (11.5%). Urinary tract infection was reported in 5/218 pessary recipients (2.3%) and 20/221 surgery recipients (9.0%). Postoperative bladder retention occurred in 11/221 (5.0%); one rectal lesion was recognized and repaired during surgery.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Only women who passed the initial fitting trial entered randomization, and many women approached for enrollment preferred to select their own treatment. Loss to follow-up reduced the primary-outcome sample, while crossover to surgery affected more than half of the pessary arm. The per-protocol estimate therefore describes a selected group of continuing pessary users. Surgical technique was individualized, and no concomitant stress-incontinence operation was performed.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
