# COMPARE: Single-Use Versus Reusable Catheters for Intermittent Catheterization in Urinary Retention

<TrialBottomLine id="compare-catheters" />

**COMPARE** was an assessor-masked, multicenter noninferiority randomized trial comparing reusable uncoated with single-use intermittent catheters in 386 people with urinary retention who catheterized at least twice daily; reusable catheters met the prespecified noninferiority criterion for symptomatic UTI incidence over one year.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="compare-catheters" />

## Design

Dutch outpatient urology clinics enrolled patients aged 16 years or older who had performed clean intermittent catheterization for at least two weeks. Neurogenic and nonneurogenic retention were eligible. Exclusions included nonurethral or temporary catheterization, urethral stricture, urinary tract stones, bladder augmentation, active bladder cancer follow-up, immunosuppression, and impaired comprehension. Participants and research staff knew the allocation; outcome assessors were masked.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The reusable arm used uncoated Cliny catheters for males and PureCath catheters for females. Users rinsed them with tap water before and after catheterization, stored them in 2% sodium hypochlorite renewed daily, and replaced them every two weeks. Single-use catheters were allowed for up to 20% of weekly catheterizations in this arm. The comparison arm continued its usual single-use catheters.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The primary endpoint required acute UTI symptoms plus a positive urine test; culture, sediment, dipslide, or nitrite testing qualified. The prespecified noninferiority margin was +0.07 UTIs per patient-month for the reusable-minus-single-use difference, assessed by the upper limit of a two-sided 95% confidence interval. The primary modified intention-to-treat analysis required completion of a six-week lead-in visit.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

In the modified intention-to-treat analysis, symptomatic UTI incidence was 0.050 per patient-month with reusable catheters and 0.054 with single-use catheters. The absolute incidence-rate difference was −0.004 (95% CI −0.025 to 0.019); its upper bound was below the +0.07 margin. At least one UTI occurred in 40/134 (29.9%) versus 59/191 (30.9%), respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Prespecified sensitivity analyses produced differences of −0.002 (95% CI −0.028 to 0.026) in the intention-to-treat population and −0.01 (95% CI −0.032 to 0.011) in the per-protocol population; both upper bounds remained below the prespecified margin. The adherence-adjusted complier-average causal effect estimate was 0.016 (95% CI −0.126 to 0.158) UTIs per patient-month.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

At week 52, catheter-related quality of life favored reusable catheters (mean score difference 3.6, 95% CI 0.3 to 7.0), while catheterization satisfaction favored single-use catheters (mean score difference −0.12 for reusable minus single-use, 95% CI −0.22 to −0.01).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Of 193 people randomized to reusable catheters, 185 started treatment and 113 completed one year. Among those who started, 72/185 (39%) discontinued; ease-of-use problems were reported as a discontinuation reason by 39/185 (21.1%), and urethral irritation by 13/185 (7.0%). Reusable catheters accounted for a mean 88.6% of catheterizations in the intention-to-treat population. Of the 113 who completed one year, 105/113 (92.9%) preferred to continue using the reusable device.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Low-grade urethral irritation occurred in 37/185 (20.0%) reusable users versus 9/192 (4.7%) single-use users. UTI-related hospitalization occurred in 3/185 (1.6%) and 3/192 (1.6%), respectively. Macroscopic hematuria was reported in 23/185 and 24/192, and urethral stricture in one participant in each group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The six-week lead-in excluded many early discontinuers from the primary analysis, particularly in the reusable arm. The noninferiority margin of +0.07 UTIs per patient-month exceeded the observed single-use incidence of 0.054; the adherence-adjusted estimate had a wide interval spanning that margin. Participants and research staff were unblinded, and the UTI definition permitted positive urine tests other than culture. The findings reflect the tested uncoated devices and their daily hypochlorite cleaning regimen.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
