# Soave harvest-site closure: Buccal Graft Donor-Site Closure Versus Nonclosure

<TrialBottomLine id="soave-harvest-closure" />

**Soave harvest-site closure** was a randomized noninferiority trial in 135 analyzed men undergoing single-stage buccal mucosa graft substitution urethroplasty that compared leaving the inner-cheek harvest site open with closing it. Nonclosure was noninferior for oral pain intensity and affective quality at every postoperative assessment through 6 months; sensory and total pain met noninferiority only at day 21 and month 3.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="soave-harvest-closure" />

## Design

Computer-based randomization assigned 72 men to nonclosure and 63 to closure after inner-cheek buccal graft harvest. Of 163 men who consented between October 2014 and December 2015, 28 were excluded after intraoperative changes: 25 underwent two-stage urethroplasty and 3 required bilateral cheek harvest. Allocation was concealed until graft harvest; six reconstructive surgeons performed the operations. Patients completed standardized questionnaires before surgery, on postoperative days 1, 5, and 21, and at months 3 and 6. Oral pain intensity and quality were coprimary outcomes; oral morbidity and perineogenital pain intensity were secondary outcomes. The prespecified noninferiority margin was 1 point, assessed against the upper bound of the 95% confidence interval for the nonclosure-minus-closure difference. Generalized linear mixed models examined time and other covariates.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The coprimary pain comparisons showed noninferiority of nonclosure for oral pain intensity and affective pain quality at all five postoperative assessments. Sensory and total pain quality met noninferiority at day 21 and month 3, but not at days 1 or 5 or month 6.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In mixed-model covariate analyses, oral pain intensity and sensory, affective, and total pain quality declined with time from urethroplasty (P &lt; 0.001 for each). Greater graft length was associated with higher oral pain intensity (1.014-fold NRS score per additional millimeter; P = 0.001), sensory pain quality (P = 0.020), and total pain quality (P = 0.042).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The randomized intervention concerned management of an ovoid, 15-mm-wide inner-cheek graft harvest wound. The cheek was infiltrated with 2% lidocaine with adrenaline and the donor site treated with bipolar hemostasis. Closure used interrupted 4-0 monofilament sutures; the nonclosure site was left open. Median graft lengths were 45 mm (quartiles 40–60) with nonclosure and 50 mm (50–80) with closure. Both groups received a temporary cottonoid gauze dressing, daily chamomile rinses, and cheek cooling on postoperative days 1–5.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Oral morbidity persisted in questionnaire respondents at month 6: numbness affected 30/42 in the nonclosure arm and 24/42 in the closure arm; impaired mouth opening affected 14/41 and 20/42, respectively. Other reported domains included bleeding, swelling, altered salivation and taste, and impaired smiling, whistling, eating, and speech. The group-by-time morbidity comparisons were not significant.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Only Clavien-Dindo grade I oral complications were reported. At day 21, they occurred in 25/71 (35%) nonclosure and 23/62 (37%) closure respondents; at month 3, in 2/70 (2.9%) and 4/62 (6.5%); and at month 6, in 0/67 and 2/62 (3.2%). Events comprised pain or swelling requiring medication and bleeding treated with compression.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The analysis excluded 28 consented patients after intraoperative changes and used available questionnaire responses; up to 52/135 (39%) lacked a month-6 response for some morbidity domains. Oral morbidity questions were nonvalidated, and every graft was ovoid and 15 mm wide.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
