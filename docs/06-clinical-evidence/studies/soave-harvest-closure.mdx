# Soave harvest-site closure: Buccal Graft Donor-Site Closure Versus Nonclosure

<TrialBottomLine id="soave-harvest-closure" />

**Soave harvest-site closure** was a randomized noninferiority trial in 135 patients undergoing buccal mucosa graft substitution urethroplasty that compared leaving the inner-cheek harvest site open with closing it; nonclosure was noninferior for oral pain intensity and affective pain quality at each postoperative assessment through 6 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="soave-harvest-closure" />

## Design

Computer-based randomization assigned 72 patients to nonclosure and 63 to closure after inner-cheek buccal graft harvest. Enrollment ran from October 2014 through December 2015. Patients completed standardized questionnaires before surgery, on postoperative days 1, 5, and 21, and at months 3 and 6. Oral pain intensity and quality were coprimary outcomes. Oral morbidity and perineogenital pain intensity were secondary outcomes. Generalized linear mixed models examined the effects of time and other covariates.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The coprimary comparisons covered pain intensity and quality at postoperative days 1, 5, and 21 and months 3 and 6. In covariate analyses, time from urethroplasty affected pain intensity and sensory, affective, and total pain quality (P &lt; 0.001 for each); graft length affected pain intensity (P = 0.001), sensory pain quality (P = 0.020), and total pain quality (P = 0.042).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The randomized intervention concerned management of the inner-cheek donor wound during buccal graft substitution urethroplasty. The comparison was closure of the harvest wound versus leaving it open; urethral reconstruction itself was common to both groups.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Reported oral morbidity included bleeding, swelling, numbness, changes in salivation and taste, and difficulty opening the mouth, smiling, whistling, eating, or speaking.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Oral pain and morbidity were assessed through 6 months, so later donor-site symptoms fall outside the stated follow-up schedule.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
