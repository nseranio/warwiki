# St Mark's biofeedback trial: Advice, sphincter exercises, and biofeedback for fecal incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="st-marks-biofeedback" />

**The St Mark's biofeedback trial** randomized 171 patients with fecal incontinence to standard advice, advice plus sphincter exercise instruction, hospital-based pressure biofeedback, or hospital biofeedback plus a home electromyogram device; patient-rated improvement was 53% with hospital pressure biofeedback and 54% with advice, with no added benefit found for exercise or biofeedback.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="st-marks-biofeedback" />

## Design

The four groups separated the effects of advice, explicit sphincter exercise instruction, instrumented hospital training, and additional home device use. Outcomes included a bowel diary, symptom and continence scores, patient-rated change, quality-of-life and psychological scales, and anal manometry.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

In pooled before-and-after analyses, median diary incontinence episodes fell from 2 to 0 per week (P&lt;0.001; 39% of post-treatment diaries were missing), and the median continence score fell from 15 to 13 on a scale whose worst score is 20 (P&lt;0.001). Disease-specific quality of life, selected SF-36 domains, psychological scores, and resting and squeeze pressures also improved. The abstract reports no between-group difference for these outcomes and says the improvements were largely maintained 1 year after treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The hospital biofeedback arm used computer-assisted sphincter pressure feedback. A separate arm added a home electromyogram feedback device to hospital biofeedback. Standard care consisted of advice; another arm added sphincter exercise instruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The comparison used an active advice control and had no sham-feedback arm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The American Society of Colon and Rectal Surgeons' 2023 fecal incontinence guideline cites this trial among the randomized comparisons underlying its statement that biofeedback may be considered for patients with fecal incontinence <Rec>(Conditional; Low)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
