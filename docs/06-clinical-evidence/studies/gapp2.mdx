# GaPP2: Gabapentin for Chronic Pelvic Pain in Women

<TrialBottomLine id="gapp2" />

**GaPP2** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized trial of oral gabapentin versus matching placebo in women with chronic pelvic pain and no obvious pathology at laparoscopy; neither worst nor average pain improved significantly with gabapentin at weeks 13–16.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="gapp2" />

## Design

Women were recruited at 39 UK hospital centers. Eligibility required pelvic pain for at least 3 months, age 18–50 years, contraception, and laparoscopy within the preceding 36 months showing no obvious pathology. Dysmenorrhea and dyspareunia could coexist with pelvic pain. Women with pain confined to dysmenorrhea, suspected gastrointestinal pain, previous gabapentin or pregabalin use, or planned surgery were excluded. The laparoscopy criterion excluded visible endometriosis, substantial ovarian cysts or fibroids, and dense adhesions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Allocation was balanced by dysmenorrhea, psychological distress, hormonal contraception, and hospital. Participants, clinicians, and research staff were masked. Worst and average pain were separate coprimary outcomes on a 0–10 numerical rating scale, recorded weekly during weeks 13–16. The analysis used adjusted mean differences, 97.5% confidence intervals, and a two-sided significance threshold of 0.025 for each outcome; the sample-size calculation targeted a 1-point difference.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Among 306 randomized women, worst-pain scores were available for 124/153 assigned gabapentin and 122/153 assigned placebo. Mean change from baseline was −1.4 versus −1.2 points; adjusted mean difference −0.20 (97.5% CI −0.81 to 0.42; P = 0.47). Average-pain scores were available for 123/153 and 121/153; mean change was −1.1 versus −0.9 points, with an adjusted mean difference of −0.18 (97.5% CI −0.71 to 0.35; P = 0.45). Neither coprimary outcome met its prespecified threshold.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For the secondary outcomes, at least 30% improvement in worst pain occurred in 30/124 (24%) versus 21/122 (17%); the risk ratio was 1.38 (99% CI 0.72–2.64). Very marked or marked global improvement was reported by 34/112 (30%) versus 22/108 (20%); the risk ratio was 1.48 (99% CI 0.80–2.73). The report did not assign formal P values to secondary outcomes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Gabapentin or matched placebo was taken daily for 16 weeks. The protocol started at 300 mg/day and added 300 mg every 3 days during a 4-week titration, up to pain relief, dose-limiting effects, or 2700 mg/day. Participants continued their highest tolerated dose. Other analgesics, including opioids and antidepressants, were permitted. Adherence was self-reported as taking more than half of assigned doses and was not checked by pill count.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Serious adverse events occurred in 10/153 (7%) with gabapentin and 3/153 (2%) with placebo (P = 0.04). A participant assigned gabapentin died after pneumonia complications; investigators judged the death unrelated to study participation. Dizziness occurred in 66/122 (54%) versus 32/114 (28%), drowsiness in 64/124 (52%) versus 34/116 (29%), and visual disturbances in 25/113 (22%) versus 12/110 (11%). Side-effect denominators reflect respondents to each item.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Coprimary outcomes were available for 246/306 (80%) and 244/306 (80%) participants, respectively. Multiple-imputation and other prespecified sensitivity analyses gave similar estimates. Adherence relied on participant reports. The selected population had recent laparoscopy without obvious pelvic pathology; the study did not enroll women with visible endometriosis or other defined pelvic disease. Follow-up ended shortly after treatment, so longer-term pain and safety outcomes were not measured.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

SOGC Guideline No. 445 (2024) advises considering alternatives to gabapentin for chronic pelvic pain without gynecologic pathology, while allowing it as an option for neuropathic pain <Rec>(strong, moderate)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
