# MOSAIC: Retrosigmoid Versus Conventional Ileal Conduit After Radical Cystectomy

<TrialBottomLine id="mosaic" />

**MOSAIC** was a multicenter, open-label randomized trial in 303 patients with bladder cancer scheduled for robot-assisted radical cystectomy and intracorporeal ileal conduit; transposing the conduit behind the sigmoid instead of routing the left ureter behind it did not significantly reduce left ureteroenteric strictures within two years.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="mosaic" />

## Design

All five Danish cystectomy centers enrolled patients. Randomization was stratified by center and preoperative hydronephrosis with hydroureter. Prior abdominal or pelvic radiotherapy, major bowel surgery or enteric stoma, planned left-sided urostomy, a solitary or single-functioning kidney, and complete ureteral duplication excluded patients. Experienced cystectomy surgeons performed the operations, although each had performed few retrosigmoid conduits before the trial.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The primary endpoint was a left ureteroenteric stricture diagnosed or persisting after the early postoperative period, demonstrated by imaging with functional consequences and either treated with improvement or accompanied by substantial untreated loss of relative renal function. The investigators planned to detect a reduction from 15% to 5%, using a two-sided 0.05 significance threshold. Analysis retained randomized conduit assignment but excluded patients who did not undergo cystectomy, because they were not at risk of an anastomotic stricture. CT urography and laboratory surveillance were scheduled after surgery, with renography before surgery and at the early postoperative visit.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Among 297 patients assessed for stricture, the primary endpoint occurred in 6/148 (4.1%) assigned to the retrosigmoid conduit and 10/149 (6.7%) assigned to the conventional conduit (P = 0.31). Any left- or right-sided ureteroenteric stricture occurred in 9/148 (6.1%) and 15/149 (10.1%), respectively. The full-text Results section reports median follow-up of 755 days.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The lower-than-planned control stricture incidence left fewer primary events than anticipated. The prespecified endpoint did not meet the superiority threshold; these event counts do not give a precise estimate of the possible difference between techniques.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The planned operation in both groups used intracorporeal diversion after cystectomy and extended lymphadenectomy. The conventional Bricker conduit used approximately 15 cm of ileum; the left ureter was gently mobilized and carried behind the sigmoid to the right side. The retrosigmoid conduit used approximately 25 cm of ileum; its oral end crossed behind the sigmoid to the left ureter, permitting a more proximal left ureteral division. Both ureters were spatulated, stented and joined separately end-to-side with running 4-0 absorbable monofilament suture. Stents were removed 5–10 days after surgery.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The early report found median left ureteral resection of 5.50 cm (IQR 4.00–7.00) with the retrosigmoid route versus 3.90 cm (IQR 2.50–5.00) with the conventional route (P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Median operative time was 298 versus 288 minutes (P = 0.73). Of 149 patients allocated to the retrosigmoid arm, 137 received the allocated conduit, including two during open cystectomy; 11 of 148 operated patients instead received a conventional conduit. Reasons included sigmoid anatomy, adhesions, difficulty identifying the cecum, and protocol error.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

In the 90-day analysis of 298 operated patients, Clavien-Dindo grade III or higher complications affected 52/148 (35%) retrosigmoid and 41/150 (27%) conventional-conduit patients. Ureteroenteric leaks occurred in 13/148 (8.8%) and 11/150 (7.3%); urosepsis in 17/148 (11%) and 14/150 (9.3%); and mechanical ileus in 9/148 (6.1%) and 6/150 (4.0%).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

By two years, metabolic acidosis affected 35/148 (24%) and 31/149 (21%), vitamin B12 deficiency 22/148 (15%) and 23/149 (15%), and persistent diarrhoea treated with cholestyramine 2/148 (1.4%) and 2/149 (1.3%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The trial excluded patients with prior pelvic radiation or major bowel surgery. Surgeons had limited prior experience with the modified route, and 11 operated patients assigned to it received a conventional conduit. The primary analysis excluded patients without cystectomy, while follow-up of one operated control patient stopped after the early visit. Conduit length was intended by technique but was not systematically measured. The open-label design also extended to local data collection and early complication grading.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
