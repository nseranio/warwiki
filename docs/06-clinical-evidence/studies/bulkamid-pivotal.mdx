# Bulkamid pivotal trial: Polyacrylamide Hydrogel versus Collagen for Female Stress Incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="bulkamid-pivotal" />

The **Bulkamid pivotal trial** was a single-blind, multicenter noninferiority randomized comparison of Bulkamid polyacrylamide hydrogel with Contigen collagen gel in women with stress or stress-predominant mixed urinary incontinence; Bulkamid met the trial's one-year composite response criterion for noninferiority.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="bulkamid-pivotal" />

## Design

Investigators at 33 North American centers randomized 345 women to hydrogel or collagen. The primary endpoint combined a reduction of at least 50% in leakage and incontinence episodes at 12 months after the last injection. Assessments included bladder diaries, quality-of-life questionnaires, pad weight and, at the final visit, Valsalva leak point pressure. Women could receive up to three injections spaced one month apart.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The journal report found the composite response in 53.2% of hydrogel recipients and 55.4% of collagen recipients. Zero stress incontinence episodes were reported by 47.2% and 50.0%, respectively; 77.1% and 70.0% considered themselves cured or improved. The report concluded that Bulkamid was noninferior to Contigen.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

A post hoc analysis pooled 291 women from both treatment groups. At one year, 74 (25%) were cured, 164 (56%) improved and 53 (18%) had no effect. Age at least 60 years and fewer than 2.5 daily stress episodes were associated with cure; in that subgroup, 90% had a treatment effect and 38% were cured, compared with 13% cured among younger women with at least 2.5 episodes.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Bulkamid was placed in the urethral submucosa with its dedicated injection device. Repeat injection was allowed for persistent leakage. The FDA physician labeling describes endoscopic injection into the proximal half of the urethra under direct vision.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Adverse events

The trial abstract reports major adverse events in both groups without event counts. FDA physician labeling identifies acute retention, implant-site pain, urinary infection and hematuria among treatment-related events observed in the Bulkamid group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

The journal abstract gives no noninferiority margin, interval estimate or analysis denominators for its reported composite percentages. The comparator, Contigen, was subsequently withdrawn from the market. The post hoc patient-characteristics analysis combined both agents and included 291 of the 345 randomized women.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="bulkamid-pivotal" />

The later [Bulkamid versus TVT trial](/docs/clinical-evidence/studies/bulking-vs-tvt) compared hydrogel injection with a retropubic sling; this pivotal study used another bulking agent as its control.

The FDA approved the Bulkamid Urethral Bulking System on January 28, 2020, for urethral injection in adult women with stress incontinence due to intrinsic sphincter deficiency, including stress-predominant mixed incontinence.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
