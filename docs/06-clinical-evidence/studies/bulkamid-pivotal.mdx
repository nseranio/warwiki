# Bulkamid pivotal trial: Polyacrylamide Hydrogel versus Collagen for Female Stress Incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="bulkamid-pivotal" />

The **Bulkamid pivotal trial** was a single-blind, multicenter noninferiority randomized comparison of Bulkamid polyacrylamide hydrogel with Contigen collagen gel in women with stress or stress-predominant mixed urinary incontinence; Bulkamid met the trial's one-year composite response criterion for noninferiority.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="bulkamid-pivotal" />

## Design

Investigators at 33 North American centers randomized 345 women to hydrogel or collagen. Eligible women had stress incontinence due to intrinsic sphincter deficiency or stress-predominant mixed incontinence; urethral hypermobility, prior bulking and stage 3–4 prolapse were exclusions. The primary endpoint combined at least a 50% reduction in 24-hour pad-test leakage and daily incontinence episodes at 12 months after the last injection. The prespecified noninferiority margin was 15 percentage points, with a one-sided significance threshold of 0.05. Assessments included bladder diaries, quality-of-life questionnaires, pad weight and, at the final visit, Valsalva leak point pressure. Women could receive up to three injections spaced one month apart.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

Bulkamid met the prespecified noninferiority criterion for the 12-month composite response: in the per-protocol analysis, 75/141 (53.2%) hydrogel recipients and 36/65 (55.4%) collagen recipients responded (difference −2.2 percentage points; one-sided *P* = 0.0430 for noninferiority; *P* = 0.6156 for superiority). In the intention-to-treat analysis, response occurred in 105/229 (45.9%) and 48/116 (41.4%), respectively (*P* = 0.0003 for noninferiority). Zero stress incontinence episodes were reported by 91/193 (47.2%) and 50/99 (50.5%); 145/188 (77.1%) and 70/100 (70.0%) considered themselves cured or improved. By the 24-hour pad test, 44/183 (24.0%) and 22/90 (24.4%) were dry.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

A post hoc analysis pooled 291 women from both treatment groups who completed the 12-month diary and symptom questionnaire. Cure, defined as no diary stress episodes and no stress symptoms on the ICIQ-UI-SF, occurred in 74/291 (25.4%); 164/291 (56.4%) improved and 53/291 (18.2%) had no effect. Among 124 women in the adjusted cure-versus-no-effect model, age at least 60 years was associated with cure (OR 3.00, 95% CI 1.35–6.66; *P* = 0.007), as were fewer than 2.5 daily stress episodes (OR 2.39, 95% CI 1.08–5.26; *P* = 0.031). In the older, lower-frequency subgroup, 90% had a treatment effect and 38% were cured, compared with 13% cured among younger women with at least 2.5 episodes.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Bulkamid was placed under endoscopic guidance into the urethral submucosa at three sites, using 1–2 mL per session and no more than 0.6 mL per site under local anesthesia.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Repeat injection was allowed for persistent leakage: 177/229 hydrogel recipients received a second injection and 82/229 a third; mean total volume was 3.3 mL versus 8.6 mL with collagen (*P* &lt; 0.0001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The FDA physician labeling describes endoscopic injection into the proximal half of the urethra under direct vision.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Adverse events

Procedure- or device-related adverse events affected 58/229 (25.3%) hydrogel recipients and 32/116 (27.6%) collagen recipients. Acute retention occurred in 13/229 (5.7%) versus 11/116 (9.5%), urinary infection in 8/229 (3.5%) versus 7/116 (6.0%), and implant-site pain in 28/229 (12.2%) versus 9/116 (7.8%). Across both arms, 24 serious adverse events occurred in 22 women; one transient hematuria event in the hydrogel arm was considered probably procedure-related. FDA physician labeling identifies acute retention, implant-site pain, urinary infection and hematuria among treatment-related events observed in the Bulkamid group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

The per-protocol primary analysis included 206 of 345 randomized women, while the intention-to-treat analysis included all 345. The comparator, Contigen, was subsequently withdrawn from the market. The post hoc patient-characteristics analysis combined both agents and included 291 of the 345 randomized women.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="bulkamid-pivotal" />

The later [Bulkamid versus TVT trial](/docs/clinical-evidence/studies/bulking-vs-tvt) compared hydrogel injection with a retropubic sling; this pivotal study used another bulking agent as its control.

The FDA approved the Bulkamid Urethral Bulking System on January 28, 2020, for urethral injection in adult women with stress incontinence due to intrinsic sphincter deficiency, including stress-predominant mixed incontinence.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
