# Ferrante vaginal estrogen: Vaginal Estrogen for Prevention of Recurrent UTI

<TrialBottomLine id="ferrante-vaginal-estrogen" />

**Ferrante vaginal estrogen (VESPR)** was a multicenter, single-blind randomized trial comparing vaginal estrogen ring or cream with placebo cream in postmenopausal women with recurrent UTI. The pooled estrogen arms had fewer six-month UTI endpoint failures when withdrawals counted as failures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="ferrante-vaginal-estrogen" />

## Design

Investigators initially allocated women equally among an estrogen ring, estrogen cream, and placebo cream. Slow recruitment prompted a revised sample-size and block-randomization plan that pooled the two active treatments for the placebo comparison. The primary endpoint was occurrence of UTI at six months. Women could receive open-label estrogen ring or cream for another six months afterward.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

In the intention-to-treat analysis, UTI endpoint failure affected 11/18 (61.1%) women assigned vaginal estrogen and 16/17 (94.1%) assigned placebo cream (P = 0.041). Among completers, UTI occurred in 8/15 (53.3%) and 10/11 (90.9%), respectively (P = 0.036).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Nine women withdrew before the six-month assessment: three in the pooled estrogen group (one ring, two cream) and six in the placebo group. The resulting completer groups numbered 15 and 11.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The trial randomized only 35 women, and nine withdrew before the primary assessment. Combining ring and cream after slow recruitment leaves their separate effects unresolved. The comparison was single-blind.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="ferrante-vaginal-estrogen" />

Raz–Stamm compared estriol cream with placebo cream; Eriksen compared an estradiol ring with no estrogen treatment. Ferrante tested two contemporary vaginal formulations in one trial, then pooled them for the primary comparison.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA/CUA/SUFU recurrent UTI guideline, amended in 2025, recommends vaginal estrogen for peri- and postmenopausal women with recurrent UTI when it is not contraindicated <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>. Its evidence review includes Ferrante and rates that study at high risk of bias.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The AUA/SUFU/AUGS 2025 GSM guideline recommends local low-dose vaginal estrogen for patients with GSM and recurrent UTI to reduce future UTI risk <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>; its discussion cites Ferrante.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
