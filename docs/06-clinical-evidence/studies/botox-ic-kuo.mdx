# Kuo 2016 IC/BPS: Suburothelial OnabotulinumtoxinA Plus Hydrodistension for Refractory Bladder Pain

<TrialBottomLine id="botox-ic-kuo" />

**Kuo 2016 IC/BPS** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized trial in patients with IC/BPS refractory to conventional treatment that compared suburothelial onabotulinumtoxinA with saline injection, both with hydrodistension; pain reduction at week 8 favored onabotulinumtoxinA.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="botox-ic-kuo" />

## Design

Sixty patients, including eight men and 52 women, were allocated 2:1 to onabotulinumtoxinA or saline, yielding groups of 40 and 20. Mean age was 52.9 ± 14.3 years in the toxin arm and 50.2 ± 13.2 years in the saline arm. Patients had persistent or recurrent symptoms after at least two conventional treatments for at least 6 months; those with Hunner lesions were excluded. Both groups received hydrodistension, so the randomized comparison measures the added effect of suburothelial toxin injection within that procedure. The primary endpoint was patient-reported change in pain on a visual analog scale (VAS) at week 8. Voiding diary and urodynamic measures were secondary endpoints. The investigators used Wilcoxon signed-rank and rank-sum tests.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Mean VAS change at week 8 was −2.6 ± 2.8 with onabotulinumtoxinA (n = 40) and −0.9 ± 2.2 with saline (n = 20), a calculated between-arm mean difference of −1.7 points (P = 0.021). Global response assessment improvement of at least one point at 3 months occurred in 25/40 and 3/20 patients, respectively (P = 0.028). Cystometric bladder capacity changed by +67.8 ± 164.3 mL and −45.4 ± 138.5 mL, respectively, at week 8 (P = 0.020). Postvoid residual changed by +63.4 ± 119.3 mL and −3.8 ± 126.6 mL, respectively (P = 0.095). Other measured variables did not differ significantly between groups.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The active treatment was 100 U of onabotulinumtoxinA in 10 mL saline, delivered as twenty 0.5 mL suburothelial injections (5 U each) into the posterior and lateral bladder walls, sparing the trigone; the control received twenty 0.5 mL saline injections. The saline arm served as the procedural placebo. Hydrodistension was performed in both arms at 80 cm H₂O for 15 minutes, followed by an overnight 14 Fr Foley catheter and 7 days of cephalexin.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

At week 8, 20/40 onabotulinumtoxinA recipients and 19/20 saline recipients had no adverse event (overall adverse-event comparison P = 0.033). Dysuria occurred in 16/40 versus 1/20, urinary tract infection in 2/40 versus 0/20, and hematuria in 1/40 versus 0/20. One toxin recipient (1/40) developed urinary retention together with a urinary tract infection; no saline recipient had retention.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The primary endpoint was assessed at 8 weeks. Baseline pain VAS was 5.1 versus 3.7 in the randomized groups (P = 0.056).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
