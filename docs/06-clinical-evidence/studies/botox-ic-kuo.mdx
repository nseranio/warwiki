# Kuo 2016 IC/BPS: Suburothelial OnabotulinumtoxinA Plus Hydrodistension for Refractory Bladder Pain

<TrialBottomLine id="botox-ic-kuo" />

**Kuo 2016 IC/BPS** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized trial in patients with IC/BPS refractory to conventional treatment that compared suburothelial onabotulinumtoxinA with saline injection, both with hydrodistension; pain reduction at week 8 favored onabotulinumtoxinA.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="botox-ic-kuo" />

## Design

Sixty patients, including eight men and 52 women, were allocated 2:1 to onabotulinumtoxinA or saline, yielding groups of 40 and 20. Mean age was 50.8 ± 13.9 years. Both groups received hydrodistension, so the randomized comparison measures the added effect of suburothelial toxin injection within that procedure. The primary endpoint was patient-reported change in pain on a visual analog scale (VAS) at week 8. Voiding diary and urodynamic measures were secondary endpoints. The investigators used Wilcoxon signed-rank and rank-sum tests.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Mean VAS change at week 8 was −2.6 ± 2.8 with onabotulinumtoxinA and −0.9 ± 2.2 with saline, a calculated between-arm mean difference of −1.7 points (P = 0.021). Overall success occurred in 26/40 and 3/20 patients, respectively (P = 0.028). Cystometric bladder capacity increased more with onabotulinumtoxinA. Other measured variables did not differ significantly between groups.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The active treatment was 100 U of onabotulinumtoxinA delivered by suburothelial bladder injections; the control received an equivalent amount of saline. The saline arm served as the procedural placebo. Hydrodistension was performed in both arms.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

The investigators reported no difference in adverse events between groups.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The primary endpoint was assessed at 8 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
