# GROUP: Group-Based Versus Individual Pelvic Floor Muscle Training in Older Women

<TrialBottomLine id="group-pfmt" />

**GROUP** was a two-center, assessor-blinded noninferiority trial in 362 older women with stress or mixed urinary incontinence that compared supervised group-based with individual pelvic floor muscle training (PFMT); group training was noninferior for diary-recorded leakage reduction one year after randomization.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="group-pfmt" />

## Design

Community-dwelling women aged at least 60 years had at least three involuntary leakage episodes weekly and stress or mixed incontinence confirmed by questionnaire. Women who required a mobility aid, had substantial prolapse, or had recently undergone incontinence physiotherapy or surgery were excluded. Randomization was stratified by center and incontinence type. Outcome assessors were masked; participants and treating physiotherapists were aware of the assignment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The primary endpoint was median percentage reduction in leakage episodes from baseline to one year, measured with a seven-day bladder diary. The prespecified noninferiority margin was 10 percentage points for individual minus group training; the upper bound of a two-sided 95% confidence interval had to fall below that margin. The main analysis used women completing follow-up. Because the observed reductions were skewed, investigators changed the planned mean-based test to a nonparametric median comparison, with an intention-to-treat sensitivity analysis carrying the last observation forward for missing follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At one year, 319 of 362 randomized women completed follow-up: 154 of 178 assigned group training and 165 of 184 assigned individual training. Median leakage-episode reductions were 74% and 70%, respectively. The reported individual-minus-group difference was −4 percentage points (95% CI −10 to 7); its upper bound was below the 10-point margin. The intention-to-treat sensitivity analysis gave reductions of 69% and 67% and a difference of −2 points (95% CI −12 to 5).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Exploratory patient-reported outcomes were similar. At one year, 132/153 women in the group arm (86%) and 138/163 in the individual arm (85%) felt much or very much better; 139/153 (91%) and 148/164 (90%) reported satisfaction defined as no need for another treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

An economic evaluation of the same randomized cohort found group-minus-individual cost differences of −CA$914 per woman from the participant perspective (95% CI −CA$970 to −CA$863) and −CA$509 from the provider perspective (95% CI −CA$523 to −CA$496), using 2019 Canadian dollars over one year. Clinical effect differences between arms were small in that analysis.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Both arms first received individual instruction to establish a pelvic floor contraction. Experienced pelvic floor physiotherapists then led 12 weekly one-hour sessions, each with 15 minutes of education and 45 minutes of progressive exercises addressing strength, power, endurance, coordination, and contraction during activities such as coughing. Group sessions enrolled eight women. Individual sessions included 10–15 minutes of intravaginal electromyographic biofeedback; 12/166 group participants completing treatment (7%) requested an additional 20-minute private contraction check.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Home exercises were prescribed five days weekly during treatment and three days weekly for the next nine months. Mean attendance was 11.4 of 12 group sessions and 11.7 of 12 individual sessions. By one year, 7/154 (5%) and 5/165 (3%), respectively, reported other incontinence care despite a request to avoid it during follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Five women in the group arm reported vaginal discomfort. In the individual arm, 21 reported discomfort and six reported spotting during intravaginal biofeedback, for 27 women with minor events. Events occurred mainly in the first two sessions and resolved without treatment. Neither arm reported a serious adverse event.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The intensive program was tested in community-dwelling older women able to participate in repeated sessions; women requiring a mobility aid and those with substantial prolapse were excluded. Biofeedback was used in the individual arm, while short private contraction checks were available in the group arm. The primary analysis included completers, and the statistical test changed from the planned mean comparison to a median comparison after investigators found a skewed outcome distribution.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The **EAU 2026 guideline on non-neurogenic female lower urinary tract symptoms** cites GROUP when discussing group versus individual training and recommends at least three months of supervised, intensive PFMT as first-line treatment for women with stress or mixed urinary incontinence, including older women <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
