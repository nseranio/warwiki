# Liedberg prophylactic conduit mesh: Prevention of Parastomal Hernia After Ileal Conduit

<TrialBottomLine id="conduit-mesh-liedberg" />

**Liedberg prophylactic conduit mesh** was a three-center, open-label randomized trial in 242 adults scheduled for open radical cystectomy and ileal conduit that compared sublay Vypro mesh with conventional stoma construction; mesh lowered the hazard of clinical parastomal hernia (HR 0.45, 95% CI 0.24–0.86; p=0.02).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="conduit-mesh-liedberg" />

## Design

The trial enrolled patients without a previous stoma at two university hospitals and one county hospital in Sweden. Randomization used closed envelopes. One patient assigned to conventional construction could not receive an ileal conduit because of severe adhesions and instead received ureteroureteric anastomosis and a unilateral nephrostomy; the operative and safety analyses therefore included 123 controls and 118 mesh recipients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Clinical parastomal hernia was assessed at scheduled postoperative visits, with symptomatic and asymptomatic findings both counted. The investigators did not set an a priori clinical definition. Radiological hernia was assessed on oncological CT with the patient prone, the stoma centered in an inflatable ring, and the patient straining. Clinicians and radiologists knew the assigned treatment. The sample-size calculation used a binary clinical-hernia comparison at 24 months; during the trial, the primary analysis changed to time to clinical hernia, censoring at last clinical follow-up or death.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Clinical parastomal hernia occurred in 12/118 evaluable mesh recipients and 26/123 controls over follow-up; the primary time-to-event analysis gave HR 0.45 (95% CI 0.24–0.86; p=0.02). At 24 months, hernia had been documented in 10/92 mesh recipients and 20/89 controls alive at that time point (binary p=0.06). Median follow-up among patients without hernia was 3 years in each arm. The adjusted Cox analysis, accounting for hospital, body mass index and preoperative chemotherapy, gave HR 0.49 (95% CI 0.26–0.95; p=0.04).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Among patients alive at 24 months, radiological hernia occurred in 17/92 mesh recipients and 22/89 controls; bulging without hernia occurred in 22/92 and 13/89. Neither secondary comparison was significant. During follow-up, 2 mesh recipients and 5 controls underwent parastomal-hernia surgery.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The stoma was marked in the right rectus by a stoma nurse, except at one hospital where the operating urologist marked it. The intervention used a 10 × 10 cm lightweight Vypro mesh in the sublay plane; both arms had anterior fixation of the conduit to the rectus sheath. Median operating time was 415 minutes (IQR 340–480) with mesh and 365 minutes (IQR 305–450) without mesh, a 50-minute difference (p=0.007).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Within 90 days, the highest Clavien-Dindo grade was 3–5 in 32/118 mesh recipients (27%) and 30/123 controls (24%); grade 1–2 complications occurred in 21/118 (18%) and 19/123 (15%). Grade 3–5 postoperative infection was listed for 3/118 mesh recipients and 5/123 controls.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Conduit ischemia occurred in one mesh recipient (partial) and one control (complete, requiring reoperation). No other potential mesh-related complication was observed within 90 days in the prophylactic-mesh arm, and none was noted during long-term follow-up. A control patient later received sublay mesh during hernia repair; that mesh eroded into the conduit and required revision.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

During follow-up, one patient in each arm had another stoma reoperation (flat bud with mesh, elongated stoma without mesh); a mesh recipient had a minor prolapse treated conservatively. Midline incisional hernia occurred in 3/118 mesh recipients and 4/123 controls.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Assessors were unblinded, and the clinical endpoint had no prespecified examination definition. The number of clinical follow-up visits per patient was not recorded, and additional CT scans did not consistently use the prone, ring-centered protocol. The primary analysis changed from the binary endpoint used for sample-size planning to time-to-event analysis during the study. Fewer patients remained evaluable at later time points, and the trial included open cystectomy only.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
