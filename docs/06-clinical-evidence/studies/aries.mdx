# ARIES: Mirabegron for Overactive Bladder in North America

<TrialBottomLine id="aries" />

**ARIES** was a double-blind phase 3 trial in 1,329 adults with overactive bladder that compared mirabegron 50 mg and 100 mg once daily with placebo for 12 weeks; both doses met the co-primary endpoints of reduced incontinence episodes and micturitions per day.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="aries" />

## Design

Investigators in the United States and Canada enrolled adults with at least three months of overactive bladder symptoms. A two-week placebo run-in preceded 1:1:1 randomization. Participants recorded symptoms in diaries. The co-primary endpoints were change from baseline to the final visit in mean daily incontinence episodes and micturitions. Safety assessments included adverse events, blood pressure and other vital signs, electrocardiography, and post-void residual volume.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Mean changes from baseline to the final visit were as follows; parentheses show 95% confidence intervals for the within-arm changes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Co-primary endpoint, episodes per 24 hours | Mirabegron 50 mg | Mirabegron 100 mg | Placebo |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Incontinence | −1.47 (−1.69 to −1.25) | −1.63 (−1.86 to −1.40) | −1.13 (−1.35 to −0.91) |
| Micturitions | −1.66 (−1.92 to −1.40) | −1.75 (−2.01 to −1.48) | −1.05 (−1.31 to −0.79) |

Both mirabegron doses had statistically greater reductions than placebo on both endpoints (P &lt; 0.05). The report also describes significant improvement in its key secondary micturition and incontinence endpoints for both doses.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Dry mouth occurred in 0.5% with mirabegron 50 mg, 2.1% with 100 mg, and 1.5% with placebo. The reported incidence of hypertension, urinary tract infection, headache, and nasopharyngitis was similar across groups.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Randomized treatment lasted 12 weeks, with placebo as the only comparator; the trial did not assess longer-term symptom control or direct comparative efficacy against an antimuscarinic.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA/SUFU 2024 idiopathic overactive bladder guideline recommends offering antimuscarinic medications or β3 agonists to improve urgency, frequency, or urgency urinary incontinence <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade A)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The FDA approved mirabegron 25 mg and 50 mg extended-release tablets for overactive bladder on June 28, 2012.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
