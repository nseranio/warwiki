# ARIES: Mirabegron for Overactive Bladder in North America

<TrialBottomLine id="aries" />

**ARIES** was a double-blind phase 3 trial in 1,329 adults with overactive bladder that compared mirabegron 50 mg and 100 mg once daily with placebo for 12 weeks; both doses met the co-primary endpoints of reduced incontinence episodes and micturitions per day.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="aries" />

## Design

Investigators in the United States and Canada enrolled adults with at least three months of overactive bladder symptoms, at least eight micturitions and three severe urgency or urgency-incontinence episodes per 24 hours at baseline. A two-week single-blind placebo run-in preceded 1:1:1 randomization. Participants recorded symptoms in diaries. The co-primary endpoints were change from baseline to the final visit in mean daily incontinence episodes and micturitions. Incontinence was analyzed in the full analysis set with baseline incontinence (FAS-I; n=933); micturitions were analyzed in the full analysis set (FAS; n=1,270). A stepwise parallel gatekeeping procedure controlled multiplicity across the two co-primary and three key secondary endpoints. Safety assessments included adverse events, blood pressure and other vital signs, electrocardiography, and post-void residual volume.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Both mirabegron doses reduced incontinence episodes in FAS-I (50 mg n=312; 100 mg n=296; placebo n=325) and micturitions in FAS (50 mg n=425; 100 mg n=412; placebo n=433) more than placebo at the final visit. The table gives adjusted mean changes from baseline; parentheses show 95% confidence intervals for within-arm changes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

| Co-primary endpoint, episodes per 24 hours | Mirabegron 50 mg | Mirabegron 100 mg | Placebo |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Incontinence | −1.47 (−1.69 to −1.25) | −1.63 (−1.86 to −1.40) | −1.13 (−1.35 to −0.91) |
| Micturitions | −1.66 (−1.92 to −1.40) | −1.75 (−2.01 to −1.48) | −1.05 (−1.31 to −0.79) |

Both co-primary comparisons met the multiplicity-adjusted threshold for each mirabegron dose (P &lt; 0.05). All three key secondary endpoints also met that threshold for both doses: voided volume per micturition increased by 18.2 mL (95% CI 13.4 to 22.9) with 50 mg, 18.0 mL (13.1 to 22.8) with 100 mg, and 7.0 mL (2.3 to 11.7) with placebo; at week 4, incontinence episodes changed by −1.20 (−1.43 to −0.97), −1.18 (−1.42 to −0.94), and −0.72 (−0.95 to −0.50), and micturitions by −1.19 (−1.44 to −0.93), −1.37 (−1.62 to −1.11), and −0.77 (−1.02 to −0.52), respectively (P &lt; 0.05 for each mirabegron-placebo comparison).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Treatment delivery

Overall study discontinuation was 13.3% with mirabegron 50 mg, 12.2% with 100 mg, and 15.2% with placebo; discontinuation for adverse events was 4.1%, 4.4%, and 3.7%, respectively. Visits occurred at weeks 4, 8, and 12, with telephone follow-up 30 days after the final visit.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Dry mouth occurred in 2/442 (0.5%) with mirabegron 50 mg, 9/433 (2.1%) with 100 mg, and 7/453 (1.5%) with placebo. Hypertension treatment-emergent adverse events under the paper's prespecified blood-pressure definition occurred in 33/442 (7.5%), 27/433 (6.2%), and 32/453 (7.1%), respectively. Urinary tract infection was reported in 12/442 (2.7%), 16/433 (3.7%), and 8/453 (1.8%), respectively; these reports were not necessarily culture-confirmed. Acute urinary retention was reported in neither mirabegron group (0/442 and 0/433). Headache and nasopharyngitis were reported at similar frequencies across groups.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Randomized treatment lasted 12 weeks, with placebo as the only comparator; the trial did not assess longer-term symptom control or direct comparative efficacy against an antimuscarinic.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA/SUFU 2024 idiopathic overactive bladder guideline recommends offering antimuscarinic medications or β3 agonists to improve urgency, frequency, or urgency urinary incontinence <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade A)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The FDA approved mirabegron 25 mg and 50 mg extended-release tablets for overactive bladder on June 28, 2012.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
