# WATER III: Aquablation Versus Transurethral Laser Enucleation for Large Prostates

<TrialBottomLine id="water-iii" />

**WATER III** was an international, multicenter, nonblinded, partially randomized noninferiority trial comparing Aquablation with transurethral laser enucleation in men with symptomatic 80–180 mL prostates; Aquablation met the Bayesian noninferiority criterion for three-month IPSS improvement.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="water-iii" />

## Design

The trial enrolled 202 men with moderate to severe lower urinary tract symptoms. Of 186 men who underwent surgery, 98 received Aquablation and 88 received laser enucleation. The coprimary endpoints were IPSS change from baseline to three months and Clavien–Dindo grade 2 or higher complications, or grade 1 complications persisting at three months. The analysis combined randomized and nonrandomized participants using Bayesian noninferiority methods.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Three-month mean IPSS change was −12.9 ± 6.9 points after Aquablation and −13.1 ± 7.5 after laser enucleation. The Bayesian estimated difference was 0.93 points (95% credible interval −1.48 to 3.53), with a noninferiority probability &gt;0.999. The procedural safety composite occurred in 40.8% and 56.8%, respectively; its model-estimated difference was −9.4 percentage points (95% credible interval −31.8 to 12.9), with a noninferiority probability of 0.952.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In a six-month meeting report, mean IPSS reduction was 15.1 ± 6.6 points after Aquablation and 14.8 ± 6.3 after laser enucleation (P = 0.768). Mean Qmax increased by 11 ± 10.3 and 17.9 ± 13 mL/s, respectively (P = 0.009).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The registered protocol specified AquaBeam waterjet ablation with transurethral electroresection for residual bladder-neck tissue and hemostasis. The comparison was holmium or thulium laser enucleation performed according to each center's standard technique.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Three-month data were available for 170 men: 66 randomized and 104 nonrandomized.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

At six months, 84/98 Aquablation recipients and 80/88 laser-enucleation recipients remained in follow-up. Additional tissue resection for BPH had occurred in three Aquablation patients and none after laser enucleation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Retrograde ejaculation at three months was reported in 14.8% after Aquablation and 77.1% after laser enucleation (P &lt;0.001). Persistent stress urinary incontinence was reported in 0% and 9.3%, respectively (P &lt;0.05).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The six-month abstract defined de novo incontinence by new pad use in men continent at baseline. Persistent mixed incontinence occurred after Aquablation in one patient (1.4%). After laser enucleation, one patient had urgency incontinence (1.9%), one had mixed incontinence (1.9%), and three had stress incontinence (5.6%).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

Only 66 of the 170 men with three-month data were randomized. Patients and investigators were not blinded. The six-month report was a meeting abstract and did not present a randomized-only analysis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
