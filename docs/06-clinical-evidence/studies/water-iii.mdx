# WATER III: Aquablation Versus Transurethral Laser Enucleation for Large Prostates

<TrialBottomLine id="water-iii" />

**WATER III** was an international, multicenter, nonblinded, partially randomized noninferiority trial comparing Aquablation with transurethral laser enucleation in men with symptomatic 80–180 mL prostates; Aquablation met the Bayesian noninferiority criterion for three-month IPSS improvement but missed the coprimary safety criterion.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="water-iii" />

## Design

The trial enrolled 202 men aged 45–80 years with IPSS ≥8 and prostate volume 80–180 mL. Of 186 men who underwent surgery, 98 received Aquablation and 88 received laser enucleation. The coprimary endpoints were IPSS change from baseline to three months and Clavien–Dindo grade 2 or higher complications, or grade 1 complications persisting at three months. The Bayesian primary analysis drew its inference from randomized patients while borrowing information from nonrandomized cohorts. Noninferiority required a posterior probability &gt;0.975, with margins of 4.7 IPSS points and 10 percentage points for safety.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Three-month mean IPSS change was −12.9 ± 6.9 points after Aquablation and −13.1 ± 7.5 after laser enucleation. The Bayesian estimated difference was 0.93 points (95% credible interval −1.48 to 3.53), with a noninferiority probability &gt;0.999. The procedural safety composite occurred in 40/98 (40.8%) and 50/88 (56.8%), respectively; its model-estimated difference was −9.4 percentage points (95% credible interval −31.8 to 12.9). Its noninferiority probability of 0.952 missed the prespecified &gt;0.975 threshold.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Secondary functional outcomes favored laser enucleation at three months: mean Qmax was 19.8 ± 10.6 versus 27.5 ± 11.3 mL/s, and mean prostate volume was 51 versus 26 mL after Aquablation and laser enucleation, respectively (P &lt;0.0001 for each comparison).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In a six-month meeting report, mean IPSS reduction was 15.1 ± 6.6 points after Aquablation and 14.8 ± 6.3 after laser enucleation (P = 0.768). Mean Qmax increased by 11 ± 10.3 and 17.9 ± 13 mL/s, respectively (P = 0.009).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The registered protocol specified AquaBeam waterjet ablation with transurethral electroresection for residual bladder-neck tissue and hemostasis. The comparison was holmium or thulium laser enucleation performed according to each center's standard technique.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Mean procedure time was 58.1 ± 19.6 minutes for Aquablation (2.4 ± 0.6 ablation runs) and 61.8 ± 25.8 minutes for laser enucleation. Of 88 laser patients, 79 were treated by surgeons with &gt;200 prior procedures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Three-month data were available for 170 men: 66 randomized and 104 nonrandomized.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

At six months, 84/98 Aquablation recipients and 80/88 laser-enucleation recipients remained in follow-up. Additional tissue resection for BPH had occurred in three Aquablation patients and none after laser enucleation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Persistent de novo ejaculatory dysfunction at three months was reported in 14.8% after Aquablation and 77.1% after laser enucleation (P &lt;0.0001) among men sexually active at baseline and follow-up who met baseline MSHQ-EjD-SF ejaculatory-function criteria. Persistent stress urinary incontinence occurred in 0/69 and 5/54 (9.3%) men continent at baseline, respectively (P &lt;0.05).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Grade ≥3 adverse events occurred in 8/98 (8.2%) after Aquablation and 5/88 (5.7%) after laser enucleation; one laser patient had a grade 4a hypervolemia event requiring one night in intensive care. Antibiotic-treated urinary tract infection occurred in 28/98 (28.6%) and 21/88 (23.9%). Fulguration was needed in 5/98 (5.1%) and 3/88 (3.4%), while transfusion and bladder tamponade each occurred in 0/98 and 2/88 (2.3%), respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The six-month abstract defined de novo incontinence by new pad use in men continent at baseline. Persistent mixed incontinence occurred after Aquablation in one patient (1.4%). After laser enucleation, one patient had urgency incontinence (1.9%), one had mixed incontinence (1.9%), and three had stress incontinence (5.6%).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

Only 66 of the 170 men with three-month data were randomized. Patients and investigators were not blinded. The six-month report was a meeting abstract and did not present a randomized-only analysis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
