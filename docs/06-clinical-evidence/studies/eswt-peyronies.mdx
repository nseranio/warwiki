# Palmieri ESWT: Focused Shock Wave Therapy for Peyronie's Disease

<TrialBottomLine id="eswt-peyronies" />

Focused shock wave therapy improved pain and erectile-function scores at 12 weeks after treatment in Palmieri and colleagues' randomized trial of 100 men with Peyronie's disease of no more than 12 months' duration and no prior Peyronie's-related treatment; plaque area and curvature did not differ significantly between arms at that assessment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="eswt-peyronies" />

## Design

The prospective, double-blind study assigned 50 men to each arm. Investigators measured erectile function with IIEF-5, erection pain on a 0–10 visual analog scale, plaque area in cm², curvature in degrees, and quality of life with an unvalidated questionnaire. Assessments occurred at baseline and 12 and 24 weeks after the final session.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Pain resolved by 12 weeks in 23/43 men with baseline pain after active treatment and 3/42 after sham treatment. Mean pain, IIEF-5 and quality-of-life scores also favored active treatment at 12 weeks (between-arm p&lt;0.001 for each); 25/50 treated men versus 0/50 sham-treated men had no erectile dysfunction by IIEF-5 category. At 24 weeks, mean pain had fallen from baseline in both arms, while mean IIEF-5 and quality-of-life scores remained higher with active treatment. The corrected questionnaire scoring assigns higher quality-of-life scores to less symptom burden.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Plaque area and curvature decreased nonsignificantly with active treatment and increased nonsignificantly with sham at 12 weeks. By 24 weeks, both measures were higher in the sham arm than at baseline and than in the active arm (p&lt;0.05); the active arm did not differ significantly from baseline. For curvature, these comparisons involved the 44 men per arm with recurvatum. At 24 weeks, 35/50 treated men and 9/50 sham-treated men said they would recommend the treatment to a friend.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

All 50 men in each arm completed four weekly sessions and both follow-up assessments. Active sessions used a Storz Duolith device to deliver 2,000 focused shock waves at 0.25 mJ/mm² and 4 Hz, with a static focus and manually operated probe; sham sessions used a nonfunctioning transducer. Treatment required no anesthesia. The study did not involve penile surgery or intralesional treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Treatment-site bruising occurred in 4/50 men with active treatment and 2/50 with sham treatment. No major complications were observed in the active arm, and no participant needed analgesics during treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The quality-of-life questionnaire was unvalidated, erectile dysfunction was not characterized by cause, and enrollment was restricted to men without prior Peyronie's treatment and with recent disease.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

This study tested shock waves against a sham transducer. Traction and intralesional injection trials in the same Peyronie's decision lane tested different treatments and endpoints.

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU Sexual and Reproductive Health guideline (2026) recommends ESWT for penile pain in the acute phase <Rec>(Weak)</Rec> and recommends against ESWT to improve penile curvature <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
