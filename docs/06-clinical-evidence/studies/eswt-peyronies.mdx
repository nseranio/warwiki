# Palmieri ESWT: Focused Shock Wave Therapy for Peyronie's Disease

<TrialBottomLine id="eswt-peyronies" />

Palmieri and colleagues randomized 100 men with Peyronie's disease of no more than 12 months' duration and no prior Peyronie's-related treatment to focused extracorporeal shock wave therapy or a nonfunctioning sham transducer; mean pain, IIEF-5 and study-specific quality-of-life scores improved in the treated arm at 12-week follow-up, while mean plaque size and curvature were unchanged in that arm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="eswt-peyronies" />

## Design

The prospective, double-blind study assigned 50 men to each arm. Investigators measured erectile function with IIEF-5, erection pain on a 0–10 visual analog scale, plaque area in cm², curvature in degrees, and quality of life with an unvalidated questionnaire. Assessments occurred at baseline and at 12- and 24-week follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At 12-week follow-up, mean VAS pain, IIEF-5 and quality-of-life scores improved significantly in the active arm. Plaque area and curvature did not change in that arm; both increased slightly in the sham arm, without significant change from baseline. At 24 weeks, pain scores were significantly below baseline in both arms. IIEF-5 and quality-of-life scores remained stable in the active arm, while sham-arm plaque area and curvature were significantly higher than both baseline and the active-arm values.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Each man underwent four weekly sessions. Active sessions delivered 2,000 focused shock waves; sham sessions used a nonfunctioning transducer. The study did not involve penile surgery or intralesional treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The quality-of-life questionnaire was unvalidated, erectile dysfunction was not characterized by cause, and enrollment was restricted to men without prior Peyronie's treatment and with recent disease.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="eswt-peyronies" />

This study tested shock waves against a sham transducer. Traction and intralesional injection trials in the same Peyronie's decision lane tested different treatments and endpoints.

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU Sexual and Reproductive Health guideline (2026) recommends ESWT for penile pain in the acute phase <Rec>(Weak)</Rec> and recommends against ESWT to improve penile curvature <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
