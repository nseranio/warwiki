# MsFLASH Vaginal Health Trial: Vaginal Estradiol or Moisturizer for Postmenopausal Vulvovaginal Symptoms

<TrialBottomLine id="msflash-vaginal" />

**MsFLASH Vaginal Health Trial** was a two-center, double-blind randomized trial in 302 postmenopausal women with moderate or severe vulvovaginal symptoms that compared a vaginal 10-μg estradiol tablet plus placebo gel, Replens moisturizer plus placebo tablet, and dual placebo; neither active regimen met the prespecified superiority threshold for reducing most-bothersome-symptom severity at 12 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="msflash-vaginal" />

## Design

Women aged 45–70 years were eligible after at least two years without menses if they reported weekly moderate or severe vulvovaginal itching, pain, irritation, or dryness, or monthly pain with vaginal penetration. Current vaginal infection, recent hormonal therapy, recent antibiotics or moisturizer use, and chronic premenopausal vulvovaginal symptoms were exclusions. Recruitment took place at Kaiser Permanente Washington in Seattle and the University of Minnesota in Minneapolis. Participants, study staff, and clinicians were masked with matching tablets and gels.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Each woman selected her most bothersome symptom at enrollment and rated its severity from zero (none) to three (severe). The primary analysis compared change from baseline at weeks 4 and 12, with a two-sided α of 0.025 for each active-versus-placebo comparison. The modified intention-to-treat analysis included randomized women with baseline and at least one follow-up symptom score; 294/302 (97%) contributed data. This was a comparison of each active regimen against dual placebo, without a planned direct estradiol-versus-moisturizer test.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At week 12, mean most-bothersome-symptom severity fell by 1.4 points with estradiol (n=96) and 1.3 with dual placebo (n=95); the adjusted mean difference in change was −0.1 points (95% CI −0.4 to 0.2; model P=0.25). The moisturizer arm fell by 1.2 points (n=99), with an adjusted difference from dual placebo of 0.2 points (95% CI −0.1 to 0.4; model P=0.31). A decrease of at least two points occurred in 47/96 (49%), 35/99 (35%), and 43/95 (45%) women, respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Among women with paired measurements and the specified baseline pH or maturation threshold, estradiol changed vaginal pH from &gt;5 to ≤5 in 36 women (46%), versus 10 (12%) with dual placebo; 45 (57%) versus 8 (11%) moved from ≤5% to &gt;5% superficial cells on the vaginal maturation index. Among moisturizer users with paired measurements and the specified baseline thresholds, 8 (9%) had the pH change and 8 (11%) had the cytology change. Neither active regimen improved Female Sexual Function Index total score more than dual placebo: mean changes were +5.4, +3.1, and +4.5 points, respectively. Self-reported meaningful benefit was more frequent with estradiol than dual placebo (79 [80%] versus 62 [65%]; P=0.02), but not with moisturizer (57 [58%] versus 62 [65%]; P=0.39).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In a prespecified secondary quality-of-life analysis, 12-week total MENQOL scores improved by 1.0 point with estradiol (n=89), 0.5 with moisturizer (n=94), and 0.7 with dual placebo (n=86); lower scores indicate less bother. The adjusted mean difference was −0.3 points for estradiol versus placebo (95% CI −0.5 to 0.0; model P=0.01) and 0.2 for moisturizer versus placebo (95% CI −0.1 to 0.4; model P=0.38). The estradiol signal concerned menopause-related quality of life, while the primary symptom and Female Sexual Function Index comparisons remained negative.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

In a post hoc analysis of available serum samples, week-12 geometric mean estradiol was 4.3 pg/mL in 88 estradiol users and 3.5 pg/mL in 86 dual-placebo users (adjusted percentage difference 23.8%, 95% CI 6.9%–43.3%). Treatment assignment was not associated with week-12 estrone or sex hormone–binding globulin concentrations.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The active tablet was Vagifem; the active moisturizer was polycarbophil-containing Replens. The placebo gel was hydroxyethylcellulose, a vaginal vehicle with viscosity similar to Replens. Tablets were inserted daily for two weeks, then twice weekly for ten weeks; gel was used every three days. Among returned products, 94% met tablet adherence and 90% met gel adherence, defined as use of more than 80% of doses.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Microscopy diagnosed vaginal candidiasis in 5/102 (5%) estradiol users, 2/100 (2%) moisturizer users, and 2/100 (2%) dual-placebo users.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The primary report found no between-group difference in adverse events. The serum analysis measured hormone concentrations in a subset and did not assess clinical safety outcomes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

The hydroxyethylcellulose placebo gel supplied a regularly applied vaginal vehicle and produced substantial symptom improvement, limiting separation of specific estradiol or moisturizer effects from the gel regimen. Follow-up lasted 12 weeks, and the enrolled population was predominantly white. The post hoc serum analysis included only women with available paired samples and measured concentrations rather than adverse clinical events.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="msflash-vaginal" />

The trial's dual-placebo arm received both a tablet and a vaginal gel. Its symptom response and the estradiol arm's cytologic response diverged, making the patient-reported endpoint and vaginal maturation index distinct measures in this comparison.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The **AUA/SUFU/AUGS 2025 GSM guideline** cites this trial and recommends offering local low-dose vaginal estrogen for vulvovaginal discomfort, dryness, or dyspareunia <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C)</Rec>. It recommends vaginal moisturizers and/or lubricants, alone or with other treatment, for dryness and/or dyspareunia <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
