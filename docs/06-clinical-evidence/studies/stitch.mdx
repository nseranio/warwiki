# STITCH: Small Bites versus Large Bites for Closure of Abdominal Midline Incisions

<TrialBottomLine id="stitch" />

**STITCH** was a double-blind, multicenter randomized trial in 560 adults having elective midline laparotomy at ten Dutch hospitals that compared continuous small-bites fascial closure (5 mm bites and spacing, 2-0 polydioxanone on a 31 mm needle) with continuous large-bites closure (1 cm bites and spacing, looped size 1 polydioxanone on a 48 mm needle). Incisional hernia at the 1-year visit occurred in 35 of 268 patients (13%) after small bites and 57 of 277 (21%) after large bites (adjusted OR 0.52, 95% CI 0.31–0.87), with similar complication rates and 4 minutes longer closure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Design

Patients 18 or older scheduled for elective abdominal surgery through a midline incision were enrolled from October 20, 2009 to March 12, 2012 in surgical and gynecological departments. Exclusions were a history of incisional hernia or fascial dehiscence after midline laparotomy, midline laparotomy within the previous 3 months, pregnancy and participation in another intervention trial. Most operations were gastrointestinal: lower gastrointestinal in 47% and 51% of the large-bites and small-bites groups, upper gastrointestinal in 31% and 27%, gynecological in 14% and 15%, and vascular in 7% and 8%. Median age was 63 and 62 years.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Randomization took place in the operating room about 15 minutes before closure, 1:1 by a computer-generated sequence, stratified by centre and by surgeon versus resident with minimization. Patients and study investigators were masked; the examiners who performed the follow-up physical examination and ultrasonography were also masked.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The primary outcome was incisional hernia during follow-up, defined by the European Hernia Society as any abdominal wall gap with or without bulge in the area of a postoperative scar that is perceptible or palpable by clinical examination or imaging. Patients were seen at 1 month and 1 year (a visit up to month 15), with physical examination and abdominal ultrasonography; any postoperative abdominal CT was also used, and imaging was decisive when findings conflicted. The trial was sized for 259 patients per group to detect a fall from 15% to 7.5% with 80% power at a two-sided alpha of 0.05, and planned 576 patients (288 per group) to allow for 10% loss to follow-up; 560 were randomized. The protocol's Cox regression was replaced by a binary endpoint at up to 15 months, analyzed by chi-squared test and covariate-adjusted logistic regression.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Of 560 randomized patients (284 large bites, 276 small bites), 545 (97%) completed follow-up and were analyzed. At the 1-year visit, incisional hernia was present in 35 of 268 patients (13%) in the small-bites group and 57 of 277 (21%) in the large-bites group (unadjusted p = 0.0220; covariate-adjusted OR 0.52, 95% CI 0.31–0.87; p = 0.0131), which met the primary hypothesis of a lower incidence with small bites. Tests of interaction showed no subgroup effects (all p &gt; 0.20). The mean fascial defect among hernias was 3.4 cm (SD 4.4) and did not differ between groups.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Follow-up was by clinical and radiological examination in 338 patients (62%), radiological examination alone in 76 (14%) and physical examination alone in 131 (24%). Among 87 patients with incisional hernia assessed by both methods, 43 hernias (49%) were found by both, 41 (47%) by imaging alone and 3 (3%) by physical examination alone.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

At 13 years postoperatively, in an analysis of 275 patients in the small-bites group and 284 in the control group with a median time to censoring of 8 years (IQR 2–13), the cumulative incidence of incisional hernia was 34% versus 49% (HR 0.61, 95% CI 0.43–0.86), accounting for competing risks. Hernias wider than 20 mm occurred in 17% versus 34% (HR 0.36, 95% CI 0.21–0.60), and mean hernia width at final follow-up was 25 mm versus 43 mm (p = 0.02). Hernia repair rates were reported as similar. Of 170 patients alive without hernia at final follow-up, 122 (72%) had additional ultrasound, and imaging done during routine care was available for 238 patients.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

In the small-bites group the surgeon placed at least twice as many stitches as the incision length in centimetres, with USP 2-0 PDS Plus II on a 31 mm needle, bites of 5 mm and spacing of 5 mm; every stitch took aponeurosis only, avoiding fat and muscle. The large-bites group had mass closure with bites of at least 1 cm and spacing of 1 cm, using USP 1 double-loop PDS Plus II on a 48 mm needle. In both groups suturing started at the two ends of the incision and ran toward the centre, where the cranial and caudal sutures overlapped by at least 2 cm and were knotted separately; an extra knot was allowed. Both techniques aimed for a suture length to wound length ratio of 4:1 or higher.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Small-bites closure used a mean of 45 stitches (SD 12) versus 25 (SD 10), a suture length to wound length ratio of 5.0 (SD 1.5) versus 4.3 (SD 1.4), and 110 cm (SD 39) versus 95 cm (SD 34) of suture for a mean wound length of 22 cm in both groups. Mean closure time was 14 minutes (SD 6) versus 10 minutes (SD 4) (p &lt; 0.0001). Two small-bites patients did not receive the allocated closure because of fragile fascia.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Postoperative complications occurred in 125 of 276 patients (45%) after small bites and 129 of 284 (45%) after large bites (p = 1.000). Surgical site infection occurred in 58 of 276 (21%) versus 68 of 284 (24%) (p = 0.419): superficial incisional 8% versus 12%, deep incisional 3% versus 4%, and organ or space 10% versus 8%. Burst abdomen occurred in 4 of 276 (1%) versus 2 of 284 (1%) (p = 0.444). Ileus (10% versus 12%), pneumonia (13% versus 14%) and cardiac events (9% versus 11%) did not differ significantly. Mean hospital stay was 15 days (SD 35) versus 14 days (SD 24). First-week pain scores and 12-month SF-36 and EQ-5D results did not differ between groups, with the data not shown; patients who developed incisional hernia had lower SF-36 general health scores at 12 months (mean 60.16 versus 64.84; p = 0.0326).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The two arms differed in bite size, spacing, suture caliber (2-0 versus size 1 looped) and needle length (31 versus 48 mm). The primary analysis was at 1 year, imaging was done in roughly three-quarters of patients, and the remaining 131 analyzed patients (24%) were assessed by physical examination alone. Only elective operations were included. Ethicon, the manufacturer of the suture, co-funded the trial; the report states that the funders had no role in design, data collection, analysis or reporting.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The European and American Hernia Societies' updated guideline (2022) suggests a continuous small-bites suturing technique with a slowly absorbable suture for closure of elective midline incisions <Rec>(Quality of evidence: XX00 (low); Strength of recommendation: Weak)</Rec>. The guideline cites STITCH among the trials of the small-bites technique and defines the technique as tissue bites of 5–9 mm from the wound edges that incorporate aponeurosis only, with stitches placed 5 mm apart in a continuous suture; it states that the technique implies a ratio of suture length to wound length of at least 4:1.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
