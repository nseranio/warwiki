# REJOICE: Vaginal Estradiol Soft-Gel Inserts for Symptomatic Vulvar and Vaginal Atrophy

<TrialBottomLine id="rejoice" />

**REJOICE** was a multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 trial in postmenopausal women whose most bothersome symptom was moderate-to-severe dyspareunia; vaginal estradiol soft-gel inserts at 4, 10, or 25 µg improved all four coprimary cytology, pH, and dyspareunia outcomes versus placebo at 12 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="rejoice" />

## Design

Investigators at 89 sites in the United States and Canada randomized 764 women equally among three estradiol doses and a matching soft-gel placebo. Eligibility required age 40–75 years, vaginal pH &gt; 5.0, at most 5% superficial cells, and sexual activity with vaginal penetration. Women with an intact uterus needed an evaluable screening endometrial biopsy. Exclusions included unexplained vaginal bleeding, endometrial hyperplasia or cancer, thromboembolic disease, major cardiovascular disease, and recent hormone therapy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The coprimary outcomes were changes from baseline in vaginal superficial cells, parabasal cells, pH, and patient-rated dyspareunia on a 0–3 severity scale. The modified intention-to-treat efficacy population comprised 747 women with baseline endpoint data and at least one follow-up measure; 764 who received treatment were included in safety analyses. A stepwise multiple-testing procedure addressed doses and endpoints, with significance at P &lt; 0.05.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At week 12, the least-squares mean increases in superficial cells were 17.5, 16.7, and 23.2 percentage points with 4, 10, and 25 µg, versus 5.6 with placebo. Parabasal cells declined 40.6, 44.1, and 45.6 percentage points versus 6.7; vaginal pH declined 1.32, 1.42, and 1.34 units versus 0.28. Each dose comparison for these three endpoints had P &lt; 0.0001.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Dyspareunia severity declined 1.52, 1.69, and 1.69 points with 4, 10, and 25 µg, versus 1.28 with placebo. The 4 µg comparison had P = 0.0149; both higher doses had P &lt; 0.0001. The four coprimary endpoints met the trial's testing threshold for each dose. Vaginal dryness also improved versus placebo at week 12 for all three doses.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

A secondary Female Sexual Function Index analysis found greater improvement in total score versus placebo with 10 µg (P &lt; 0.05) and 25 µg (P = 0.0019), but not 4 µg. Pain and lubrication improved with 10 and 25 µg; arousal and satisfaction improved with 25 µg. Desire and orgasm did not differ from placebo.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Participants inserted one capsule digitally without an applicator at approximately the same time daily for 2 weeks, then twice weekly for 10 weeks. The soft-gel placebo matched the active insert. In all, 704/764 randomized participants (92.1%) completed the study.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Any treatment-emergent adverse event occurred in 97/191 (50.8%), 94/191 (49.2%), and 93/190 (48.9%) in the 4, 10, and 25 µg groups, compared with 111/192 (57.8%) on placebo. Vaginal discharge occurred in 5/191 (2.6%), 6/191 (3.1%), and 4/190 (2.1%) versus 13/192 (6.8%); urinary tract infection occurred in 5/191 (2.6%), 5/191 (2.6%), and 8/190 (4.2%) versus 4/192 (2.1%). No endometrial hyperplasia or malignancy was diagnosed on week-12 biopsies, and no serious adverse event was attributed to treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Follow-up ended at 12 weeks, limiting assessment of uncommon adverse events and long-term endometrial outcomes. Enrollment required dyspareunia as the most bothersome symptom, sexual activity with vaginal penetration, and objective vaginal atrophy; most participants were white. The soft-gel placebo may have had a lubricating effect on symptom ratings.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA/SUFU/AUGS 2025 guideline cites REJOICE in its evidence review and recommends offering local low-dose vaginal estrogen for GSM-related discomfort or irritation, dryness, and/or dyspareunia <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C)</Rec>. It also advises counseling that local low-dose vaginal estrogen does not increase the risk of atypical endometrial hyperplasia or endometrial cancer <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The FDA's 2018 Imvexxy label covers 4 and 10 µg vaginal inserts for moderate-to-severe dyspareunia due to menopause, with daily insertion for 2 weeks followed by twice-weekly maintenance. The trial's 25 µg dose is absent from that label.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
