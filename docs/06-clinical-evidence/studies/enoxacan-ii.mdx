# ENOXACAN II: Extended Enoxaparin after Open Cancer Surgery

<TrialBottomLine id="enoxacan-ii" />

**ENOXACAN II** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized trial in patients having planned curative open abdominal or pelvic cancer surgery that compared approximately four weeks of enoxaparin with one week followed by placebo and found fewer venographic or confirmed symptomatic venous thromboembolic events with the extended course.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="enoxacan-ii" />

## Design

The trial enrolled patients scheduled for general-anesthetic surgery for gastrointestinal, genitourinary, or female reproductive cancer. They received an initial course of enoxaparin and were randomized after surgery only if they had received that course without objectively confirmed VTE or major bleeding. Renal or hepatic insufficiency, recent VTE, active bleeding disorders, and recent anticoagulant treatment were among the exclusions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The efficacy endpoint combined centrally read bilateral venographic DVT at postoperative days 25–31 with objectively confirmed symptomatic VTE before screening. Bleeding during the double-blind treatment period was the primary safety endpoint. The efficacy analysis required an interpretable venogram or a confirmed VTE event; safety included everyone randomized and treated. The prespecified superiority test was two-sided at the 0.05 level.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The primary VTE endpoint occurred in 8/165 patients (4.8%) assigned to extended enoxaparin and 20/167 (12.0%) assigned to placebo after the initial enoxaparin course (RR 0.40, 95% CI 0.18–0.90; P=0.02). The endpoint met its prespecified threshold. Of the 28 detected VTE events, 27 were found on scheduled venography; the one confirmed symptomatic event before screening was a pulmonary embolism in the placebo group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

At three months, cumulative VTE occurred in 9/165 (5.5%) and 23/167 (13.8%), respectively (RR 0.40, 95% CI 0.19–0.83; P=0.01). These counts include venographic events from the initial endpoint assessment and subsequent confirmed events. The placebo count includes one upper-extremity DVT.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Enoxaparin was given subcutaneously at 40 mg daily, starting 10–14 hours before surgery. After 6–10 days, patients received either the same dose or placebo for another 19–21 days. Stockings were permitted, while intermittent pneumatic compression was excluded. Injections after discharge could be given by the patient, a caregiver, or a district nurse.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Of 501 randomized and treated patients, 332 entered the efficacy analysis: 165/253 in the extended arm and 167/248 in the placebo arm. Venography was missing or uninterpretable for 88 and 81 patients, respectively. Among efficacy-evaluable patients, gastrointestinal operations accounted for 141/165 (85.5%) and 137/167 (82.0%); genitourinary operations accounted for 11/165 (6.7%) and 17/167 (10.2%). Although operations were planned as curative, surgeons classified 16/165 (9.7%) and 6/167 (3.6%) as palliative intraoperatively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

During double-blind treatment, major hemorrhage occurred in 1/253 patients (0.4%) receiving extended enoxaparin and 0/248 receiving placebo (P &gt;0.99); any hemorrhage occurred in 13/253 (5.1%) and 9/248 (3.6%), respectively (P=0.51). Cumulative major hemorrhage through three months occurred in 3/253 (1.2%) and 1/248 (0.4%) (P=0.62). The trial defined major hemorrhage to include critical-site bleeding, substantial hemoglobin decline or transfusion, a serious clinical event, or intervention to control bleeding.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The primary efficacy result was driven largely by screening venography: only one VTE before the scheduled assessment was confirmed after symptoms. Venography was unavailable or uninterpretable for 169/501 randomized patients, leaving 332 for efficacy analysis. Most efficacy-evaluable operations were gastrointestinal; the genitourinary subgroup comprised 28 patients across both arms. Intraoperative reclassification to palliative surgery was more frequent in the extended arm (16/165 versus 6/167). The safety comparison had few major bleeds.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The American College of Chest Physicians' 2012 nonorthopedic surgery guideline recommends four weeks of low-molecular-weight heparin over a limited course for high-VTE-risk patients undergoing abdominal or pelvic cancer surgery who are not at high risk for major bleeding <Rec>(Grade 1B)</Rec>. The guideline cites ENOXACAN II in its evidence review.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
