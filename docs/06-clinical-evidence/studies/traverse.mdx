# TRAVERSE: Cardiovascular Safety of Testosterone-Replacement Therapy

<TrialBottomLine id="traverse" />

**TRAVERSE** was a multicenter, double-blind, phase 4 noninferiority trial of daily 1.62% testosterone gel versus placebo gel in symptomatic hypogonadal men aged 45–80 years with established or elevated cardiovascular risk; over mean 33.0-month follow-up, major adverse cardiac events occurred in 182/2,596 (7.0%) versus 190/2,602 (7.3%) treated men (HR 0.96, 95% CI 0.78–1.17), meeting the prespecified noninferiority criterion.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="traverse" />

## Design

Participants had two fasting testosterone concentrations below 300 ng/dL and at least one symptom of hypogonadism. Enrollment across 316 US sites produced 5,246 randomization IDs; after duplicate enrollments were removed, the full analysis contained 5,204 unique men, and 5,198 received study drug. Men with prostate cancer, elevated screening PSA, severe lower urinary tract symptoms, thrombophilia, uncontrolled heart failure, or a recent acute cardiovascular event were excluded.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The primary endpoint was the first cardiovascular death, nonfatal myocardial infarction, or nonfatal stroke in the treated safety population. Noninferiority required the upper bound of the HR's 95% CI to be below 1.5. The secondary composite added coronary revascularization; secondary comparisons had no multiplicity adjustment. Cardiovascular events were independently adjudicated.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The primary HR's upper confidence bound was 1.17, below the 1.5 margin (P &lt; 0.001 for noninferiority). The prespecified sensitivity analysis censoring events more than 365 days after the last dose also met noninferiority: 154/2,596 (5.9%) versus 152/2,602 (5.8%; HR 1.02, 95% CI 0.81–1.27; P &lt; 0.001 for noninferiority). The secondary composite occurred in 269/2,596 (10.4%) testosterone recipients and 264/2,602 (10.1%) placebo recipients (HR 1.02, 95% CI 0.86–1.21).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Testosterone increased sexual activity in the nested Sexual Function Study of 1,161 men with low libido (587 testosterone, 574 placebo). The available-questionnaire analysis found between-group differences of 0.49 acts per day (95% CI 0.19–0.79) at 6 months and 0.47 (95% CI 0.11–0.83) at 12 months (omnibus P = 0.011). At 24 months, the difference was 0.48 (95% CI −0.01–0.96; 106 and 95 questionnaires); the 12- and 24-month effects did not differ (P = 0.978). At 12 months, hypogonadal symptoms (HIS-Q difference −2.5, 95% CI −3.9 to −1.1) and desire (−3.3, 95% CI −5.5 to −1.1) improved, but erectile function did not (IIEF-5 difference 0.6, 95% CI −0.4 to 1.6).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

In the prostate-safety substudy, adjudicated high-grade prostate cancer occurred in 5/2,596 (0.19%) testosterone recipients and 3/2,602 (0.12%) placebo recipients (HR 1.62, 95% CI 0.39–6.77; P = 0.51). Any prostate cancer occurred in 12/2,596 (0.46%) versus 11/2,602 (0.42%). Adjudicated acute urinary retention occurred in 20/2,596 (0.77%) versus 16/2,602 (0.61%), and invasive prostate surgery for BPH in 23/2,596 (0.89%) versus 12/2,602 (0.46%); neither difference reached the substudy's significance threshold.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Clinical fractures were more frequent with testosterone in the fracture substudy: 91/2,601 (3.50%) versus 64/2,603 (2.46%) over median 3.19-year follow-up (HR 1.43, 95% CI 1.04–1.97); its fracture-reduction hypothesis was not met. Prespecified non–high-impact fractures occurred in 75/2,601 (2.88%) versus 57/2,603 (2.19%; HR 1.32, 95% CI 0.94–1.86). Both analyses used the 5,204 unique randomized men.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Testosterone was dispensed in a metered-dose pump; a central algorithm titrated it to 350–750 ng/dL and adjusted for hematocrit above 54%, with sham adjustments in the placebo group. The mean daily testosterone dose was 65±22 mg. Study drug was discontinued by 61.4% of testosterone recipients and 61.7% of placebo recipients; mean treatment duration was 21.7 months. The assigned regimen covered 67.5% and 67.3% of potential treatment time, respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the fracture subtrial, returned pump weights indicated approximately 90% adherence in both groups.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

The prostate substudy excluded men with PSA above 3.0 ng/mL (above 1.5 ng/mL when taking a 5-alpha reductase inhibitor), IPSS above 19, or a prostate nodule or induration. PSA was measured at baseline, 3 months, 12 months, and annually; IPSS and digital examination followed a scheduled protocol. A blinded committee adjudicated prostate cancers, acute retention, and BPH procedures.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Adjudicated venous thromboembolic events occurred in 44/2,596 (1.7%) testosterone recipients and 30/2,602 (1.2%) placebo recipients (HR 1.46, 95% CI 0.92–2.32); pulmonary embolism occurred in 24/2,596 (0.9%) versus 12/2,602 (0.5%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Investigator-reported nonfatal arrhythmia warranting intervention occurred in 134/2,596 (5.2%) versus 87/2,602 (3.3%; P = 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Atrial fibrillation occurred in 91/2,596 (3.5%) versus 63/2,602 (2.4%), and acute kidney injury in 60/2,596 (2.3%) versus 40/2,602 (1.5%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> These safety comparisons were unadjusted for multiplicity. Investigator-reported urinary retention in the primary report was 50/2,596 (1.9%) versus 34/2,602 (1.3%); the prostate substudy applied a narrower, adjudicated acute-retention definition.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

High study-drug discontinuation and incomplete retention limit interpretation of longer exposure. The 95% CI for high-grade prostate cancer was wide because only eight events occurred.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Prostate safety was evaluated after excluding men with prior cancer, elevated PSA, and severe LUTS, and the trial tested transdermal gel rather than other formulations.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="traverse" />

TRAVERSE enrolled men with cardiovascular disease or elevated risk and used adjudicated cardiovascular events. The Sexual Function Study enrolled only participants with low libido; its sexual outcomes answer a different question from the parent trial's cardiovascular endpoint.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The **EAU Sexual and Reproductive Health Guidelines (limited update, March 2026)** cite TRAVERSE in their cardiovascular safety discussion. They recommend assessing cardiovascular risk factors before testosterone therapy <Rec>(Strong)</Rec> and assessing men with known cardiovascular disease for symptoms before treatment and monitoring them closely during treatment <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>. They advise caution with careful monitoring of testosterone and hematocrit in men with pre-existing cardiovascular disease, venous thromboembolism, or chronic cardiac failure who require therapy <Rec>(Weak)</Rec>.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
