# PPS pivotal trials: Oral Pentosan Polysulfate Versus Placebo in Interstitial Cystitis

<TrialBottomLine id="pps-pivotal" />

The five-center, double-blind Mulholland trial assigned 54 patients with interstitial cystitis to oral pentosan polysulfate sodium (PPS) and 56 to placebo; after three months, patient-reported global improvement greater than 25% occurred in 28% versus 13% (15-percentage-point difference from rounded rates; P = 0.04).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="pps-pivotal" />

## Design

The Mulholland study enrolled patients with at least one year of symptoms, frequency of at least ten voids per day, nocturia of at least two voids, cystoscopic findings, and prior unsuccessful conventional treatment. Patient-rated overall improvement was the primary efficacy measure; the response threshold was greater than 25% improvement at three months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The separate seven-center Parsons trial randomized 74 patients per arm for three months; its prespecified success threshold was at least 50% patient-rated overall symptom improvement.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Patient and investigator global ratings and the pain and urgency scales were designated primary efficacy variables.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

In the Mulholland trial, investigator-rated global improvement was 26% with PPS and 11% with placebo (P = 0.03). Patient-rated pain improvement was 27% versus 14% (P = 0.08), and a pain-scale reduction of at least one point occurred in 46% versus 29% (P = 0.07). Improvement in pressure to urinate was 22% versus 11% (P = 0.08), while urgency-scale response was 39% versus 46% (not significant).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the independent Parsons trial, 24/74 (32%) PPS and 12/74 (16%) placebo recipients reported at least 50% overall improvement at three months (P = 0.01); investigator-rated improvement of at least 50% was 36% versus 15% (P = 0.002). At least one-point reductions on the pain and urgency scales occurred in 66% versus 51% (P = 0.04) and 61% versus 43% (P = 0.01).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Patient-rated pain and pressure-to-urinate improvement of at least 50% were 38% versus 18% (P = 0.005) and 30% versus 18% (P = 0.04); 40% versus 24% increased average volume per void by at least 20 mL (P = 0.02).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The Mulholland trial administered oral PPS 100 mg three times daily, one hour before meals or two hours after meals, or matching placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Among participants who completed the double-blind period, 44% were continuing PPS at 1.5 years after study start.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The Parsons trial administered oral PPS 100 mg three times daily, one hour before meals or two hours after meals, or identical placebo capsules; 65/74 participants in each arm completed three months.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

In the Mulholland trial, adverse reactions occurred in 3/54 (6%) PPS recipients and 7/56 (13%) placebo recipients; the authors reported no serious reactions, and 1/54 versus 2/56 patients discontinued treatment because of adverse reactions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Table III lists one PPS recipient with severe mood swings and one with suicidal ideation; it does not specify whether these were the same person.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In the Parsons trial, 7/74 (9%) PPS and 10/74 (14%) placebo recipients reported adverse experiences; 3/74 versus 5/74 discontinued treatment because of them.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The authors considered none serious; Table 4 lists one bowel obstruction with PPS and one suicide attempt with placebo.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The primary study measured symptom response over three months; 3/54 PPS and 9/56 placebo participants did not complete treatment, and adverse reactions were volunteered by patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Its placebo comparison does not establish relative benefit against another oral treatment, and the independent Parsons trial lasted three months, with nine participants per arm not completing treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA IC/BPS guideline, amended in 2022, cites both trials and lists amitriptyline, cimetidine, hydroxyzine, and PPS as oral medications that may be administered <Rec>(Option (Evidence Strength: Grades B, B, C, and B))</Rec>. It directs counseling about possible macular damage and vision-related injury before PPS use <Rec>(Clinical Principle)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The FDA approved Elmiron on September 26, 1996, for relief of bladder pain or discomfort associated with interstitial cystitis.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
