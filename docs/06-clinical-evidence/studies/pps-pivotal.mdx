# PPS pivotal trials: Oral Pentosan Polysulfate Versus Placebo in Interstitial Cystitis

<TrialBottomLine id="pps-pivotal" />

The multicenter, double-blind Mulholland trial compared oral pentosan polysulfate sodium (PPS) with placebo in 110 patients with interstitial cystitis; after three months, patient-reported global improvement greater than 25% occurred in 28% versus 13% (P = 0.03).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="pps-pivotal" />

## Design

The primary study measured overall symptom improvement through patient and investigator assessments after three months of treatment. Its patient-reported threshold was greater than 25% improvement.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The Parsons study was a separate randomized, double-blind comparison at seven centers enrolling 148 patients, with global symptoms rated as worse or improved by 0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, or 100%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

In the Mulholland trial, investigator-rated global improvement was 26% with PPS and 11% with placebo (P = 0.04). Pain and pressure to urinate also favored PPS, with P = 0.07 and 0.08, respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the independent Parsons trial, significant overall symptom improvement occurred in 32% with PPS versus 16% with placebo (P = 0.01). Pain and urgency on analogue scales improved more with PPS (P = 0.04 and 0.01); more PPS recipients also had a mean voided-volume increase greater than 20 mL (P = 0.02).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The Parsons trial administered oral PPS 100 mg three times daily or placebo.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

In the Mulholland trial, adverse reactions occurred in 6% of PPS recipients and 13% of placebo recipients; all were minor, and treatment stopped in one versus two patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The Parsons report likewise described minor adverse effects, with seven reported in the PPS group and ten in the placebo group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The primary study measured symptom response over three months. Its placebo comparison does not establish relative benefit against another oral treatment, and the independent Parsons cohort provides no longitudinal extension of the Mulholland results.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA IC/BPS guideline, amended in 2022, cites both trials and lists amitriptyline, cimetidine, hydroxyzine, and PPS as oral medications that may be administered <Rec>(Option (Evidence Strength: Grades B, B, C, and B))</Rec>. It directs counseling about possible macular damage and vision-related injury before PPS use <Rec>(Clinical Principle)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The FDA approved Elmiron on September 26, 1996, for relief of bladder pain or discomfort associated with interstitial cystitis.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
