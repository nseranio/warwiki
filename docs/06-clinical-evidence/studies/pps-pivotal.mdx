# PPS pivotal trials: Oral Pentosan Polysulfate Versus Placebo in Interstitial Cystitis

<TrialBottomLine id="pps-pivotal" />

The five-center, double-blind Mulholland trial assigned 54 patients with interstitial cystitis to oral pentosan polysulfate sodium (PPS) and 56 to placebo; after three months, patient-reported global improvement greater than 25% occurred in 28% versus 13% (15-percentage-point difference from rounded rates; P = 0.04).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="pps-pivotal" />

## Design

The Mulholland study enrolled patients with at least one year of symptoms, frequency of at least ten voids per day, nocturia of at least two voids, cystoscopic findings, and prior unsuccessful conventional treatment. Patient-rated overall improvement was the primary efficacy measure; the response threshold was greater than 25% improvement at three months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The Parsons study was a separate randomized, double-blind comparison at seven centers enrolling 148 patients, with global symptoms rated as worse or improved by 0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, or 100%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

In the Mulholland trial, investigator-rated global improvement was 26% with PPS and 11% with placebo (P = 0.03). Patient-rated pain improvement was 27% versus 14% (P = 0.08), and a pain-scale reduction of at least one point occurred in 46% versus 29% (P = 0.07). Improvement in pressure to urinate was 22% versus 11% (P = 0.08), while urgency-scale response was 39% versus 46% (not significant).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the independent Parsons trial, significant overall symptom improvement occurred in 32% with PPS versus 16% with placebo (P = 0.01). Pain and urgency on analogue scales improved more with PPS (P = 0.04 and 0.01); more PPS recipients also had a mean voided-volume increase greater than 20 mL (P = 0.02).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The Mulholland trial administered oral PPS 100 mg three times daily, one hour before meals or two hours after meals, or matching placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Among participants who completed the double-blind period, 44% were continuing PPS at 1.5 years after study start.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The Parsons trial administered oral PPS 100 mg three times daily or placebo.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

In the Mulholland trial, adverse reactions occurred in 3/54 (6%) PPS recipients and 7/56 (13%) placebo recipients; the authors reported no serious reactions, and 1/54 versus 2/56 patients discontinued treatment because of adverse reactions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Table III lists one PPS recipient with severe mood swings and one with suicidal ideation; it does not specify whether these were the same person.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The Parsons report likewise described minor adverse effects, with seven reported in the PPS group and ten in the placebo group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The primary study measured symptom response over three months; 3/54 PPS and 9/56 placebo participants did not complete treatment, and adverse reactions were volunteered by patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Its placebo comparison does not establish relative benefit against another oral treatment, and the independent Parsons cohort provides no longitudinal extension of the Mulholland results.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA IC/BPS guideline, amended in 2022, cites both trials and lists amitriptyline, cimetidine, hydroxyzine, and PPS as oral medications that may be administered <Rec>(Option (Evidence Strength: Grades B, B, C, and B))</Rec>. It directs counseling about possible macular damage and vision-related injury before PPS use <Rec>(Clinical Principle)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The FDA approved Elmiron on September 26, 1996, for relief of bladder pain or discomfort associated with interstitial cystitis.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
