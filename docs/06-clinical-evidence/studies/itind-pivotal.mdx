# iTind pivotal (MT-03): Temporary Nitinol Implantation Versus Sham for BPH Symptoms

<TrialBottomLine id="itind-pivotal" />

**MT-03** was a multicenter, single-blind randomized trial of temporary iTind implantation versus a sham catheter procedure in men with symptomatic BPH: the published three-month IPSS responder outcome favored iTind, while the FDA's prespecified three-month mean IPSS comparison did not reach statistical significance.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="itind-pivotal" />

## Design

The trial randomized 185 men, 128 to iTind and 57 to sham. Eligibility required age at least 50 years, IPSS at least 10, peak flow at most 12 mL/s, and prostate volume 25–75 mL. An obstructing median lobe, postvoid residual &gt;250 mL, prior prostate surgery, neurogenic bladder, active urinary infection, and current antithrombotic treatment were among the exclusions. Alpha blockers and 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors required washout before treatment. Investigators used center-stratified allocation, and participants remained blinded until three months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The publication designates an IPSS reduction of at least three points at three months as its primary responder endpoint. The FDA decision summary identifies two co-primary endpoints in the prespecified statistical analysis: between-group mean IPSS change at three months and within-iTind mean change at 12 months.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The sham group was unblinded at three months and could resume active treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

At three months, 78.6% of iTind recipients and 60.0% of sham recipients had an IPSS reduction of at least three points, a risk difference of 18.6 percentage points (P = 0.029).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In the FDA prespecified mixed model, mean IPSS reductions were 10.6 and 8.3 points among 84 and 40 assessed men, with an adjusted difference of 2.5 points (95% CI −0.5 to 5.6; P = 0.104).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The publication reports a separate intent-to-treat analysis of mean changes of 9.0 and 6.6 points (P = 0.063).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The FDA's within-iTind 12-month co-primary analysis found a mean IPSS change of −8.7 points among 81 assessed men (95% CI −10.6 to −6.9; P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In the publication's per-protocol analysis, 78 men with paired IPSS measurements improved by 9.25 points and 55 with paired flow measurements had a 3.52 mL/s increase in Qmax; these were within-group findings after sham unblinding.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The later sexual-function analysis found no significant difference between iTind and sham at three months in SHIM (13.13 versus 13.00; P = 0.502) or total IIEF (41.47 versus 39.31; P = 0.645). In the iTind subgroup without baseline erectile dysfunction, total IIEF rose 6.07 points at 12 months (P = 0.034). Ejaculatory function was measured with IIEF question 9.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The second-generation device has nitinol struts at 12, 5, and 7 o'clock, an anchoring leaflet at 6 o'clock, and a retrieval suture. It was placed under rigid-cystoscopic visualization, remained for 5–7 days, and was removed with a rigid cystoscope or open-ended 22F catheter. The sham procedure inserted and removed an 18F Foley catheter at both visits while the treating surgeon described device placement. Implantation and removal could use local, intravenous, or general anesthesia; routine postoperative catheterization was not used.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Of the 128 assigned to iTind, 118 underwent implantation; all 57 sham-assigned men underwent the procedure. Through 12 months, six iTind-assigned men had another BPH operation and six started BPH medication, each 4.7% of those randomized to iTind.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Within 30 days, adverse events occurred in 45/118 iTind recipients (38.1%) and 10/57 sham recipients (17.5%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Dysuria affected 27/118 (22.9%) versus 5/57 (8.8%), and hematuria 16/118 (13.6%) versus 0/57.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Urinary retention occurred in 7/118 iTind recipients (5.9%) versus 0/57 sham recipients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Five procedure- or device-related serious events occurred in three iTind recipients: two retention episodes, two urinary tract infections, and one sepsis episode. Most events were mild and transient.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The sham involved catheter insertion rather than cystoscopic device deployment, and participant blinding ended at three months. IPSS and Qmax measurements at 12 months came from smaller active-arm subsets. The sexual-function analysis included visit attendees who had not started BPH medication, and its ejaculatory assessment relied on one IIEF question.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA's 2026 BPH guideline says clinicians may offer a temporary implantable prostate device for LUTS/BPH in patients with prostates 25–75 cc and no obstructing median lobe <Rec>(Expert Opinion)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The FDA granted iTind De Novo authorization on February 25, 2020, for symptoms of urinary outflow obstruction secondary to BPH in men aged at least 50 years.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
