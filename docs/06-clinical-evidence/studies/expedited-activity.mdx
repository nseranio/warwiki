# O'Shea 2023: Standard Restrictions vs Expedited Activity After Pelvic Organ Prolapse Surgery

<TrialBottomLine id="expedited-activity" />

**O'Shea 2023** was a single-center, assessor-masked randomized noninferiority trial in physically active women undergoing vaginal or robotic laparoscopic apical prolapse reconstruction; expedited activity without lifting or work restrictions was noninferior to standard restrictions for anatomic support and prolapse symptoms at three months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="expedited-activity" />

## Design

Eligible women had bothersome stage II–IV prolapse and could walk several blocks and climb stairs without difficulty. The operation was a vaginal apical repair or robotic laparoscopic mesh sacrocolpopexy. Randomization was stratified by surgical route and concomitant hysterectomy. Surgeons and the clinicians performing the follow-up POP-Q examinations were masked to assignment; participants knew their instructions. The primary analysis included 107 of 123 randomized participants with complete three-month outcome data.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The coprimary endpoints were maximum support loss (SLmax), the most distal POP-Q point, and the Pelvic Organ Prolapse Distress Inventory (POPDI) score. Higher SLmax indicates more distal support loss, and higher POPDI indicates greater symptom distress. Prespecified noninferiority required the upper bound of the 95% confidence interval for the baseline-adjusted difference (expedited minus standard) to remain below +1.0 cm for SLmax and +34.3 points for POPDI. The primary analysis used participants who followed the protocol.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At three months, mean SLmax was −1.7 cm (SD 1.4) with expedited instructions and −1.5 cm (SD 1.4) with standard restrictions. The baseline-adjusted mean difference was −0.18 cm (95% CI −0.68 to 0.33), meeting the +1.0-cm noninferiority margin. Mean POPDI scores were 23.7 (SD 41.8) and 25.7 (SD 39.3) points; the baseline-adjusted mean difference was −5.79 points (95% CI −20.41 to 8.84), meeting the +34.3-point margin.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In an exploratory analysis of employed participants, median return to work was 28 days (IQR 11–45) with expedited instructions and 34 days (IQR 7–44) with standard restrictions (P = 0.58). The groups did not differ in accelerometer-measured recovery to baseline activity.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The expedited group was instructed to resume lifting, exercise, and work as able. Standard instructions prohibited lifting more than 10 lb for six weeks and delayed return to sedentary work for two weeks and manual work for six weeks. Both groups avoided sexual activity until the six-week visit. The instruction package was delivered before surgery, and wrist accelerometers recorded activity before surgery and through the postoperative recovery period.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Robotic laparoscopic sacrocolpopexy used synthetic polypropylene mesh and was performed in 24/53 (45.3%) expedited-group participants and 24/54 (44.4%) standard-group participants. Vaginal apical procedures included uterosacral and sacrospinous ligament suspension, sacrospinous hysteropexy, and ileococcygeus suspension. Concomitant midurethral slings were placed in 22/53 (41.5%) and 21/54 (38.9%), respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Postoperative complications did not differ between groups, and the investigators reported no major complications.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Follow-up ended at three months, before many prolapse recurrences would be apparent. The single-center population was screened for preoperative mobility, and relatively few employed participants reported heavy manual work. Participants were unmasked, and baseline accelerometer activity differed between groups. Complete three-month outcome data were available for 107 of 123 randomized participants.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## In conversation

The earlier ReCOUP trial tested liberal against restricted postoperative activity as a superiority comparison. O'Shea and colleagues tested prespecified noninferiority margins for anatomic support and prolapse symptoms after apical reconstruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
