# Weber anterior colporrhaphy trial: Three Surgical Techniques for Anterior Vaginal Prolapse

<TrialBottomLine id="weber-anterior" />

The Weber trial randomized 114 women undergoing surgery for anterior vaginal prolapse to standard anterior colporrhaphy, standard repair with polyglactin 910 mesh, or ultralateral repair; the original report found similar strict anatomic cure across techniques at a median 23.3 months, with no improvement from mesh augmentation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="weber-anterior" />

## Design

At Cleveland Clinic, women scheduled for anterior prolapse surgery could undergo concurrent repairs of other prolapsed compartments. Planned Burch colposuspension, sling, or needle suspension excluded participation; suburethral plication was allowed. Computer-generated randomization assigned 38 women to each technique. A blinded examiner performed postoperative POP-Q examinations. The original definition of cure required both Aa and Ba to reach stage 0 or I, with stage I also improved from baseline.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

Of 83 women who returned for follow-up, strict anatomic cure occurred in 10/33 (30%) after standard repair, 11/26 (42%) after mesh augmentation, and 11/24 (46%) after ultralateral repair. The original report described similar anatomic and symptom outcomes among techniques.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The 2011 reanalysis's anatomic analysis, restricted to women with baseline prolapse beyond the hymen, found hymen-level success in 67/75 (89%) evaluable women at 1 year: 25/28 (89%) after standard repair, 22/23 (96%) after mesh augmentation, and 20/24 (83%) after ultralateral repair. Its composite of no prolapse beyond the hymen, no symptoms, and no retreatment occurred in 66/75 (88%): 25/28 (89%), 21/23 (91%), and 20/24 (83%), respectively. Absence of prolapse symptoms occurred in 80/84 (95%): 32/32 (100%), 21/23 (91%), and 27/29 (93%), respectively. All arm comparisons had P &gt; .05.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Kaplan–Meier estimates at 30 months were 81.5% for the composite, 82.3% for anatomic success, 93.9% for symptom success, and 97.1% for no retreatment, with loss to follow-up censored.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The experimental repair added absorbable polyglactin 910 mesh to standard anterior colporrhaphy; the third arm used an ultralateral colporrhaphy. Of 114 randomized women, 109 underwent surgery and 83/109 (76%) returned for follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The later reanalysis recorded frequent concurrent surgery: 57/110 (51.8%) had vaginal hysterectomy, 100/110 (90.9%) posterior colporrhaphy, and 50/110 (45.5%) vaginal vault suspension. Four of 114 randomized women did not undergo surgery, three received a different assigned repair, and 97 returned for at least one follow-up visit (median 26 months).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

One of 113 women (1%) had surgery for recurrent bulge at 29 months after mesh-augmented anterior repair; the repeat operation comprised vaginal vault suspension and enterocele repair.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

Only 83 of the 109 women operated on returned for the original follow-up assessment. The original endpoint required stage 0 or I support at both anterior POP-Q points; the reanalysis assessed all compartments at the hymen and had 75 women evaluable for the 1-year composite, using later observations carried backward when 1-year data were missing. Counting every participant lost to follow-up as a success gave 96/107 (90%); counting every loss as a failure gave 66/107 (62%).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Concurrent hysterectomy, posterior repair, and apical suspension were common. The small trial was powered for a 30% between-group difference.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
