# Weber anterior colporrhaphy trial: Three Surgical Techniques for Anterior Vaginal Prolapse

<TrialBottomLine id="weber-anterior" />

The Weber trial randomized 114 women undergoing surgery for anterior vaginal prolapse to standard anterior colporrhaphy, standard repair with polyglactin 910 mesh, or ultralateral repair; the primary comparison of strict anatomic cure found no significant difference (log-rank P = .578) at a median 23.3 months among women returning for follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="weber-anterior" />

## Design

At Cleveland Clinic, women scheduled for anterior prolapse surgery could undergo concurrent repairs of other prolapsed compartments. Planned Burch colposuspension, sling, or needle suspension excluded participation; suburethral plication was allowed. Computer-generated randomization assigned 38 women to each technique. A blinded examiner performed postoperative POP-Q examinations. The original definition of cure required both Aa and Ba to reach stage 0 or I, with stage I also improved from baseline.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Strict anatomic cure did not differ significantly by randomized assignment (Kaplan–Meier/log-rank P = .578). Among 83 women who returned for follow-up, cure occurred in 10/33 (30%) in the standard repair group, 11/26 (42%) in the mesh augmentation group, and 11/24 (46%) in the ultralateral repair group. Prolapse symptom severity fell from 6.9 ± 2.7 to 1.1 ± 0.8 on a 10-point scale; manual pressure to void resolved in 23/24 (96%) women who had needed it before surgery. Symptom outcomes did not differ between groups.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The 2011 reanalysis's anatomic analysis, restricted to women with baseline prolapse beyond the hymen, found hymen-level success in 67/75 (89%) evaluable women at 1 year: 25/28 (89%) after standard repair, 22/23 (96%) after mesh augmentation, and 20/24 (83%) after ultralateral repair. Its composite of no prolapse beyond the hymen, no symptoms, and no retreatment occurred in 66/75 (88%): 25/28 (89%), 21/23 (91%), and 20/24 (83%), respectively. Absence of prolapse symptoms occurred in 80/84 (95%): 32/32 (100%), 21/23 (91%), and 27/29 (93%), respectively. All arm comparisons had P &gt; .05.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Kaplan–Meier estimates at 30 months were 81.5% for the composite, 82.3% for anatomic success, 93.9% for symptom success, and 97.1% for no retreatment, with loss to follow-up censored.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Standard repair plicated vaginal muscularis without tension using interrupted No. 0 polydioxanone. The mesh arm fixed a cut-to-fit absorbable polyglactin 910 sheet over this plication; ultralateral repair dissected to both pubic rami and plicated paravaginal tissue under tension. All arms received suburethral plication and 2-0 polyglactin epithelial closure. Of 114 randomized women, 109 underwent anterior repair and 83/109 (76%) returned for follow-up. Five received a different technique from the one assigned.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Among 109 operated women, the original report counted 57 concurrent vaginal hysterectomies, 101 posterior colporrhaphies, 28 enterocele repairs, and 48 vaginal vault suspensions. Suprapubic catheters were used in 100 women for a mean 20.4 ± 18.5 days; mean hospital stay was 2.1 ± 0.5 days.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The later reanalysis recorded frequent concurrent surgery: 57/110 (51.8%) had vaginal hysterectomy, 100/110 (90.9%) posterior colporrhaphy, and 50/110 (45.5%) vaginal vault suspension. Four of 114 randomized women did not undergo surgery, three received a different assigned repair, and 97 returned for at least one follow-up visit (median 26 months).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Hemorrhage requiring transfusion occurred in 1/39 standard repairs and pulmonary embolism in 1/35 ultralateral repairs. Mesh erosion occurred in 1/35 mesh-group repairs and was treated by office excision of the exposed mesh, followed by vaginal healing.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

One of 113 women (1%) had surgery for recurrent bulge at 29 months after mesh-augmented anterior repair; the repeat operation comprised vaginal vault suspension and enterocele repair.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

Only 83 of the 109 women operated on returned for the original follow-up assessment; 15 women assigned to ultralateral repair did not return, compared with 9 assigned to mesh repair and 2 assigned to standard repair (P = .0007).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The original endpoint required stage 0 or I support at both anterior POP-Q points; the reanalysis assessed all compartments at the hymen and had 75 women evaluable for the 1-year composite, using later observations carried backward when 1-year data were missing. Counting every participant lost to follow-up as a success gave 96/107 (90%); counting every loss as a failure gave 66/107 (62%).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Concurrent hysterectomy, posterior repair, and apical suspension were common. The small trial was powered for a 30% between-group difference.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
