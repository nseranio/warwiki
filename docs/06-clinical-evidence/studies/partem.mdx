# PARTEM: Prostatic Artery Embolisation Versus Medical Treatment for Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia

<TrialBottomLine id="partem" />

**PARTEM** was a multicentre, open-label, phase 3 superiority randomized trial of prostatic artery embolisation versus dutasteride–tamsulosin in men with bothersome BPH symptoms despite alpha-blocker monotherapy; embolisation produced a greater reduction in the nine-month International Prostate Symptom Score (IPSS).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="partem" />

## Design

Eligible men were aged 50–85 years, had received an alpha-blocker for more than one month, and had IPSS &gt;11, quality-of-life score &gt;3, and prostate volume at least 50 mL by ultrasound or MRI. Severe renal failure, severe iodine-contrast allergy, and recent 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor use were exclusions. Urologists and interventional radiologists counselled participants; central allocation balanced treatment centre, symptom severity, and prostate volume. Participants and investigators were unblinded.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The prespecified primary endpoint was change in IPSS from randomization to nine months. The sample size targeted a four-point between-group difference at a two-sided significance level of 0.05. The principal analysis included randomized patients with an evaluable primary outcome: 44 after embolisation and 43 after combination therapy. After nine months, further treatment followed patient and referring-urologist choice.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At nine months, mean IPSS changed by −10.0 points after embolisation and −5.7 points with combination therapy; the baseline-adjusted mean difference was −4.4 points (95% CI −6.9 to −1.9; P = 0.0008), meeting the prespecified superiority test and exceeding the four-point design target. The quality-of-life score changed by −2.8 versus −1.1 points (adjusted difference −1.7, 95% CI −2.4 to −1.1).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Among 38 and 34 evaluable men, respectively, mean IIEF-15 changed by +8.2 versus −2.8 points (adjusted difference 11.0, 95% CI 3.3–18.7). Maximum urinary flow improved by +2.4 versus +2.1 mL/s among 36 and 34 evaluable men (adjusted difference 0.3 mL/s, 95% CI −1.7 to 2.3). These were secondary comparisons without multiplicity adjustment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Interventional radiologists used femoral arterial access under local anaesthesia, superselectively catheterised both prostatic arteries, and embolised proximally to stasis and then distally with 300–500 μm Embosphere microspheres. The first case at each site was proctored. Four of 45 men assigned to embolisation underwent unilateral treatment because the second artery could not be selectively catheterised; two had a second attempt. Mean procedure duration was 134 minutes (range 36–260).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The embolisation arm received oral tamsulosin 0.4 mg daily from randomisation until 15 days after the procedure. The comparison arm received dutasteride 0.5 mg plus tamsulosin 0.4 mg daily for at least nine months; 10/43 evaluable men (23.3%) were nonadherent at nine months. Between months nine and 24, invasive BPH treatment occurred in 5/42 embolisation follow-up completers (11.9%; all prostatic surgery) and 18/38 combination-therapy completers (47.4%; 14 embolisations and four prostatic surgeries).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

By nine months, 31/45 patients assigned to embolisation (68.9%) and 21/45 assigned to combination therapy (46.7%) had at least one adverse event. The event table lists 17 post-embolisation-syndrome events and three arterial dissections or perforations in the embolisation group, versus none of either event with combination therapy. No treatment-related adverse event required hospitalization or prolonged hospitalization in either group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The unblinded primary endpoint was patient-reported. The nine-month analysis excluded randomized men without an evaluable IPSS, and adherence to combination therapy was incomplete. Subsequent procedures were chosen after the randomized treatment period, so the 24-month retreatment counts describe treatment pathways after crossover became available. Secondary outcomes were tested without adjustment for multiple comparisons.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA's 2026 BPH guideline cites PARTEM among trials comparing embolisation with medication. It says clinicians may offer particle-based prostatic artery embolisation for LUTS/BPH in patients with prostates at least 50 cc <Rec>(Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
