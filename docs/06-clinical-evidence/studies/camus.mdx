# CAMUS: Escalating-Dose Saw Palmetto for Male Lower Urinary Tract Symptoms

<TrialBottomLine id="camus" />

**CAMUS** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized superiority trial of escalating-dose ethanolic saw palmetto extract versus matching placebo in men with moderate lower urinary tract symptoms; saw palmetto did not improve the prespecified 72-week symptom endpoint more than placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="camus" />

## Design

The trial randomized 369 men aged at least 45 years with American Urological Association Symptom Index (AUASI) scores of 8–24 at two screening visits and peak urinary flow of at least 4 mL/s. Men with prior invasive BPH treatment, recent use of LUTS medication or phytotherapy, relevant neurologic disease, recurrent infection, or prostate or bladder cancer were excluded. The extract was a specified proprietary product, PROSTA-URGENIN UNO, made by lipidic ethanolic extraction of *Serenoa repens* fruit.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The primary endpoint was change in AUASI from baseline to week 72. The prespecified superiority test used one-sided alpha 0.05; 176 saw palmetto recipients and 181 placebo recipients contributed to the modified intention-to-treat analysis. Participants who discontinued before the endpoint had their week-72 scores multiply imputed. Secondary symptom, urinary-flow, residual-volume, and PSA comparisons were not adjusted for multiple testing.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Mean AUASI fell by 2.20 points with saw palmetto and 2.99 points with placebo at week 72. The between-group mean difference in change was 0.79 points favoring placebo (one-sided P = 0.91); the upper one-sided 95% confidence bound most favorable to saw palmetto was 1.77 points. The primary superiority threshold was missed. A decrease of at least 3 AUASI points occurred in 42.6% and 44.2% (one-sided P = 0.66). Peak flow changed by −0.18 and −0.79 mL/s, respectively (one-sided P = 0.84).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the subsequent PSA analysis, mean serum PSA changed by +0.23 ng/mL with saw palmetto and +0.16 ng/mL with placebo through week 72 (P = 0.50). This was a secondary analysis of the same randomized population.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Saw palmetto recipients took 320 mg daily for 24 weeks, then 640 mg for 24 weeks, then 960 mg for 24 weeks; the placebo arm increased capsule count on the same schedule. Among 306 participants who finished 72 weeks on treatment, all reached the triple dose. Excluding visits after dropouts, attendance was 97.0% of scheduled visits, and median pill count across attended visits was 98.2%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

The dedicated safety analysis found serious adverse events in 17/176 saw palmetto recipients (9.7%) and 17/181 placebo recipients (9.4%; P = 1.00). At least one nonserious adverse event occurred in 136/176 (77.3%) and 137/181 (75.7%; P = 0.80). The analysis found no significant between-group difference in vital signs, examination findings, overall laboratory abnormalities, or withdrawal rates through 18 months.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

The trial tested one ethanolic extract; its composition and extraction method define the product studied. The broad symptom-based entry criteria did not require demonstrated prostatic obstruction, and the modified primary analysis excluded randomized participants without a post-baseline symptom assessment. The sequential dose schedule assessed escalation within one randomized treatment strategy, with secondary comparisons unadjusted for multiplicity.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
