# CUPIDO 2: Vaginal Prolapse Repair With or Without a Midurethral Sling for Occult Stress Incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="cupido-2" />

**CUPIDO 2** was a multicenter, unblinded randomized trial in stress-continent women with symptomatic stage II or greater prolapse and occult stress urinary incontinence (SUI), comparing vaginal prolapse repair with versus without a midurethral sling. At 12 months, the sling arm had more reported absence of SUI and more postoperative complications.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="cupido-2" />

## Design

Women entered the randomized comparison when stress leakage appeared during cough testing with the prolapse reduced, despite a negative cough test without reduction and no more than weekly reported leakage. The prolapse had to be at least stage II. Reduction used a swab to lift the middle compartment without obstructing the urethra. Office testing was initially followed by mandatory urodynamics; an amendment made urodynamics optional. Women without demonstrable occult SUI had prolapse repair alone and entered a parallel, nonrandomized cohort.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Central web-based allocation used blocks stratified by center and the leading prolapse compartment. Patients and outcome assessors knew treatment assignment. The prespecified outcomes covered absence of stress or urgency leakage on the Urogenital Distress Inventory (UDI) at 12 months and treatment for SUI or overactive bladder during the first postoperative year. The SUI composite combined bothersome symptoms, a positive cough test, or SUI treatment, so treatment before the assessment counted as an event. Recruitment ended early because enrollment was slow.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

For absence of any urinary incontinence, the UDI responses were 31/41 (75.6%) with sling and 18/46 (39.1%) without it (RR 1.93, 95% CI 1.30–2.88). Absence of SUI was reported by 36/42 (86%) and 22/46 (48%) (RR 1.79, 95% CI 1.29–2.48).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Additional SUI treatment was received by 0/42 (0%) women assigned to sling and 10/47 (21.3%) controls. Six of the treated controls underwent a sling. The secondary composite SUI endpoint occurred in 0/42 (0%) versus 18/47 (38.3%). Additional treatment for overactive bladder occurred in 4/42 (9.5%) versus 3/47 (6.4%) (RR 1.49, 95% CI 0.35–6.28). Global improvement was reported by 31/41 (75.6%) versus 34/44 (77.3%) (RR 0.98, 95% CI 0.77–1.24).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

A subsequent observational analysis pooled women from CUPIDO 1 and CUPIDO 2 who underwent prolapse repair without incontinence surgery. Postoperative bothersome SUI or SUI treatment occurred in 9/32 (28.1%) women with leakage on office testing and 7/140 (5.0%) without it (diagnostic OR 7.4, 95% CI 2.5–22). Among office-negative women who also underwent urodynamics, the outcome occurred in 1/15 (6.7%) with urodynamic leakage and 3/51 (5.9%) without it; adding urodynamics did not improve prediction in this subset.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Surgeons selected the vaginal prolapse repair and placed any sling after correcting the prolapse. Anterior repair was performed in 37/43 (86%) versus 41/47 (87%), apical repair in 32/43 (74%) versus 31/47 (66%), and posterior repair in 28/43 (65%) versus 22/47 (47%). Of those assigned a sling, 41/43 (95%) received one: 38 transobturator and 3 retropubic. One woman declined the combined procedure and one sling was omitted. Kelly plication was prohibited.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Any postoperative complication occurred in 20/43 (46.5%) women assigned a sling versus 8/47 (17.0%) controls (RR 2.73, 95% CI 1.35–5.55). Severe complications occurred in 3/43 (7.0%) versus 2/47 (4.3%). Three sling-group patients had a vaginal tape perforation or exposure: one during insertion, one small asymptomatic exposure, and one symptomatic exposure treated with partial excision. Postoperative urinary tract infection occurred in 5/43 (11.6%) versus 1/47 (2.1%); catheterization beyond a week for incomplete emptying occurred in 3/42 (7.1%) versus 1/44 (2.3%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The study closed before reaching its planned sample size, leaving limited precision for less frequent complications. The choice of prolapse repair and sling route was pragmatic and varied by surgeon. Subjective continence outcomes and assessments were unblinded, and the diagnostic analysis selected only women who underwent prolapse repair without a sling.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="cupido-2" />

CUPIDO 1 enrolled women with overt SUI, whereas CUPIDO 2 randomized women whose leakage appeared only after prolapse reduction. OPUS studied stress-continent women having vaginal prolapse repair without requiring a positive reduction test and used a retropubic sling with sham incisions. CUPIDO 2 allowed transobturator or retropubic slings and measured symptom-defined SUI and subsequent treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA/SUFU guideline, amended in 2023, says clinicians may perform an incontinence procedure during concomitant prolapse repair and SUI surgery <Rec>(Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C)</Rec>; its discussion cites the CUPIDO 2 comparison.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The EAU female LUTS guideline, limited update March 2026, recommends prolapse-reduction testing in continent women and counseling about additional anti-incontinence surgery <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> For women with symptomatic or occult SUI requiring prolapse surgery, it recommends offering simultaneous surgery only after discussing its risks and benefits <Rec>(Strong)</Rec> and informing women of the increased adverse-event risk <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
