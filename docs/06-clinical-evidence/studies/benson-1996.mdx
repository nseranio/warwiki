# Benson 1996: Vaginal Versus Abdominal Reconstructive Surgery for Pelvic Support Defects

<TrialBottomLine id="benson-1996" />

**Benson 1996** randomized 88 women with significant cervical or vault prolapse and anterior wall descent to abdominal colposacral suspension with paravaginal repair or vaginal bilateral sacrospinous vault suspension with paravaginal repair; optimal effectiveness at mean 2.5-year follow-up was 58% versus 29% among 80 evaluated women.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="benson-1996" />

## Design

Eligibility required cervical descent to or beyond the hymen, or inversion of more than half the vaginal vault length with anterior wall descent to or beyond the hymen. The abdominal and vaginal groups contained 40 and 48 women, respectively. Ancillary operations were performed as indicated. A nonsurgeon coauthor examined participants yearly while they stood and strained maximally.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

“Optimal” effectiveness required no symptoms, an apex above the levator plate, and no vaginal tissue beyond the hymen. An unsatisfactory outcome meant symptoms, apical descent exceeding half the vaginal length, or vaginal wall protrusion beyond the hymen. The abstract does not identify a prespecified primary endpoint or testing threshold.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Eighty women were evaluated 1–5.5 years after surgery: 38 after abdominal and 42 after vaginal repair. The reported relative risk of optimal effectiveness with the abdominal route was 2.03 (95% CI 1.22–9.83). Unsatisfactory outcome leading to reoperation occurred in 16% and 33%, respectively; the reported relative risk with the vaginal route was 2.11 (95% CI 0.90–4.94).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Both operations included paravaginal repair. Vaginal reconstruction used bilateral sacrospinous vault suspension; abdominal reconstruction used colposacral suspension. The vaginal group had shorter operative time and lower hospital charge but longer catheter use. The abstract gives no operative-time, charge, or catheter-duration values.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Urinary tract infection and incontinence were reported more often after vaginal repair. Reoperations for recurrent incontinence were reported in 12% of the vaginal group and 2% of the abdominal group. Overall morbidity, complications, hemoglobin change, dyspareunia, pain, and hospital stay did not differ significantly between groups.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Eight randomized women were unavailable for outcome evaluation. Ancillary procedures were performed as indicated, and follow-up duration ranged from 1 to 5.5 years. The abstract gives no event counts for the reoperation percentages or absolute complication rates.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="benson-1996" />

Maher 2004 also compared abdominal sacral colpopexy with vaginal sacrospinous repair, but enrolled women with vault prolapse and reported subjective and objective success rather than Benson's optimal-effectiveness definition.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
