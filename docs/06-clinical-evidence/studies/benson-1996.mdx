# Benson 1996: Vaginal Versus Abdominal Reconstructive Surgery for Pelvic Support Defects

<TrialBottomLine id="benson-1996" />

**Benson 1996** assigned 101 women with cervical prolapse to or beyond the hymen, or vaginal vault inversion exceeding half its length with anterior wall descent to or beyond the hymen and a paravaginal defect, to an abdominal or vaginal approach; 88 underwent surgery and 80 were evaluated at a mean 2.5 years. Optimal effectiveness followed abdominal colposacral suspension with paravaginal repair in 22/38 (58%) and vaginal bilateral sacrospinous vault suspension with paravaginal repair in 12/42 (29%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="benson-1996" />

## Design

Eligibility required cervical descent to or beyond the hymen, or inversion of more than half the vaginal vault length with anterior wall descent to or beyond the hymen and a lateral paravaginal defect. Exclusions included more than two prior abdominal pelvic operations, morbid obesity, and prior inflammatory bowel or pelvic disease. The abdominal and vaginal groups contained 40 and 48 operated women, respectively. Ten women declined surgery after assignment and three assigned to abdominal surgery requested vaginal surgery and were withdrawn. A nonsurgeon coauthor examined participants at six months and yearly thereafter while they stood and strained maximally.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

“Optimal” effectiveness required no symptoms, an apex above the levator plate, and no vaginal tissue beyond the hymen. A satisfactory outcome was asymptomatic and improved but did not meet the optimal criteria; an unsatisfactory outcome meant symptomatic apical descent exceeding half the vaginal length or vaginal wall protrusion beyond the hymen. The paper did not designate a primary endpoint. Its sample-size calculation specified 124 women to detect a 20% difference with 80% power and α = .05; enrollment stopped at 88 operations after an interim analysis found an outcome disparity.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Among 80 women evaluated 1–5.5 years after surgery, optimal effectiveness occurred in 22/38 (58%) after abdominal and 12/42 (29%) after vaginal repair (reported RR 2.03, 95% CI 1.22–9.83). Satisfactory outcomes occurred in 10/38 (26%) and 16/42 (38%); unsatisfactory outcomes leading to reoperation occurred in 6/38 (16%) and 14/42 (33%), respectively (reported RR for vaginal versus abdominal repair 2.11, 95% CI 0.90–4.94).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Both operations included paravaginal repair with permanent monofilament suture. Vaginal reconstruction used bilateral sacrospinous vault suspension; abdominal reconstruction used colposacral suspension. Hysterectomy accompanied 24/48 vaginal and 20/40 abdominal operations; autologous sling urethropexy accompanied 10/48 and 2/40, respectively. Mean operating time was 196 ± 38 versus 215 ± 47 minutes and hospital charge $6,537 ± $851 versus $8,048 ± $2,623 (vaginal versus abdominal; both P &lt; .05). Catheter use beyond five days was reported in 75% versus 48% (P &lt; .05).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Urinary tract infection occurred in 9/42 (21%) after vaginal repair and 0/38 after abdominal repair; postoperative incontinence was reported in 44% versus 23% (P &lt; .05). Among reoperated women, recurrent cystocele affected 12/42 (29%) versus 4/38 (11%), vault inversion 5/42 (12%) versus 1/38 (3%), and incontinence 5/42 (12%) versus 1/38 (3%). These diagnoses overlapped. Wound infections occurred in 0/42 versus 3/38 (8%); transfusion in 0/42 versus 2/38 (5%); and cystotomy in 1/42 (2%) versus 1/38 (3%). Overall morbidity, complications, hemoglobin change, dyspareunia, pain, and hospital stay did not differ significantly between groups.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Eight operated women were unavailable for outcome evaluation. Ancillary procedures varied between arms, including more autologous sling urethropexies with vaginal surgery. The trial stopped before its planned sample size was reached, and follow-up ranged from 1 to 5.5 years.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
