# SOFIA: Flexible-Dose Fesoterodine in Older Adults with Overactive Bladder

<TrialBottomLine id="sofia" />

**SOFIA** was a double-blind randomized trial of flexible-dose fesoterodine versus placebo in 794 adults aged at least 65 years with overactive bladder; the adjusted mean reduction in the prespecified primary endpoint of daily urgency episodes was 3.47 versus 1.92 at 12 weeks (P &lt; .001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="sofia" />

## Design

Participants had overactive bladder symptoms for at least 3 months, at least eight micturitions and three urgency episodes per day, moderate bladder bother, and a Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) score of at least 20. Randomization assigned 398 to fesoterodine and 396 to placebo and was stratified by age and morning or evening dosing. The efficacy analysis included 374 and 382, respectively, with missing observations carried forward; the safety analysis included 392 and 393. Participants completed bladder diaries and questionnaires covering symptom burden, treatment benefit, and satisfaction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At week 12, adjusted mean daily urgency episodes fell by 3.47 with fesoterodine and 1.92 with placebo (P &lt; .001). Adjusted mean daily micturitions fell by 1.95 versus 1.11 (P &lt; .001); nocturnal micturitions, pad use, Overactive Bladder Questionnaire scores, and several patient-reported response measures also improved more with fesoterodine. Among the 46% with urgency urinary incontinence (UUI) at baseline, median daily UUI episodes fell by 1.0 versus 0.7 (P = .73), and diary-dry rates at weeks 8 and 12 were 53% versus 45% (odds ratio 1.52, 95% CI 0.91–2.53; P = .11). Mean MMSE changes were +0.24 versus +0.23 points.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the subsequent 12-week open-label extension, patients continuing fesoterodine maintained their symptom response, while those switching from placebo improved after starting fesoterodine. Of 655 randomized-phase completers, 654 entered and 581 completed the extension. At week 24, mean daily urgency episodes had fallen from the randomized baseline by 4.1 and 4.0 in prior-fesoterodine patients aged 65–75 and older than 75, respectively, versus 4.6 and 3.6 in prior-placebo patients; all were receiving fesoterodine, and the extension had no formal between-group hypothesis test.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Fesoterodine began at 4 mg daily, with an option to increase to 8 mg at week 4 or 8 and to reduce back to 4 mg at week 8.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Placebo recipients made sham dose adjustments. By week 8, 64% of the fesoterodine group and 71% of the placebo group had chosen escalation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> At extension week 8, 52.7% of prior-fesoterodine patients and 50.8% of prior-placebo patients were receiving 8 mg daily.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Dry mouth occurred in 133/392 (33.9%) fesoterodine recipients versus 21/393 (5.3%) with placebo; constipation occurred in 35/392 (8.9%) versus 10/393 (2.5%). Urinary retention occurred in 5/392 (1.3%) versus 1/393 (0.3%), with catheterization in 3/392 (0.8%) versus 1/393 (0.3%). Urinary tract infection occurred in 10/392 (2.6%) versus 7/393 (1.8%); adverse events led to discontinuation in 46/392 (11.7%) versus 22/393 (5.6%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

During the open-label extension, adverse events were reported by 96/313 (30.7%) prior-fesoterodine patients and 164/341 (48.1%) prior-placebo patients; treatment-emergent adverse events led to discontinuation in 6/313 (1.9%) and 32/341 (9.4%), respectively. Dry mouth occurred in 21/313 (6.7%) versus 95/341 (27.9%), urinary tract infection in 14/313 (4.5%) versus 4/341 (1.2%), and urinary retention in 1/313 (0.3%) versus 4/341 (1.2%). Both groups received fesoterodine in that phase.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The randomized treatment period lasted 12 weeks. MMSE was the reported cognitive measure, and participants entered with scores of at least 20; mean baseline scores were about 28 in both groups.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Only 46% had UUI at baseline.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The extension enrolled randomized-phase completers and treated everyone with fesoterodine, so its later symptom and safety observations have no concurrent placebo comparison.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU 2026 guideline cites the SOFIA extension while discussing cognitive assessment during anticholinergic treatment. For older women considered for overactive bladder anticholinergics, it recommends assessing anticholinergic burden and associated comorbidity <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
