# SOFIA: Flexible-Dose Fesoterodine in Older Adults with Overactive Bladder

<TrialBottomLine id="sofia" />

**SOFIA** was a double-blind randomized trial of flexible-dose fesoterodine versus placebo in 794 adults aged at least 65 years with overactive bladder; fesoterodine produced a greater reduction in the prespecified primary endpoint of daily urgency episodes after 12 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="sofia" />

## Design

Participants had overactive bladder symptoms for at least 3 months, at least eight micturitions and three urgency episodes per day, moderate bladder bother, and a Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) score of at least 20. Randomization was stratified by age and morning or evening dosing. Participants completed bladder diaries and questionnaires covering symptom burden, treatment benefit, and satisfaction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At week 12, micturitions, nocturnal micturitions, pad use, Overactive Bladder Questionnaire scores, and several patient-reported response measures improved more with fesoterodine than placebo. Urgency urinary incontinence episodes did not differ significantly between groups. The report also found no change in MMSE score during treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the subsequent open-label extension, patients continuing fesoterodine maintained their symptom response, while those switching from placebo improved after starting fesoterodine. The extension enrolled 654 completers of the randomized phase and continued treatment for another 12 weeks.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Fesoterodine began at 4 mg daily, with an option to increase to 8 mg at week 4 or 8 and to reduce back to 4 mg at week 8. Placebo recipients made sham dose adjustments. By week 8, 64% of the fesoterodine group and 71% of the placebo group had chosen escalation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Dry mouth occurred in 34% of fesoterodine recipients versus 5% with placebo; constipation occurred in 9% versus 3%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> During the open-label extension, adverse events were reported by 30.7% of prior-fesoterodine patients and 48.1% of prior-placebo patients, and led to discontinuation in 1.9% and 9.4%, respectively.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Both groups received fesoterodine in that phase.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The randomized treatment period lasted 12 weeks. MMSE was the reported cognitive measure, and participants entered with scores of at least 20. The extension enrolled randomized-phase completers and treated everyone with fesoterodine, so its later symptom and safety observations have no concurrent placebo comparison.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU 2026 guideline cites the SOFIA extension while discussing cognitive assessment during anticholinergic treatment. For older women considered for overactive bladder anticholinergics, it recommends assessing anticholinergic burden and associated comorbidity <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
