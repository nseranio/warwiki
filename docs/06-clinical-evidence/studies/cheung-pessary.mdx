# Cheung pessary trial: Vaginal Pessary in Women With Symptomatic Pelvic Organ Prolapse

<TrialBottomLine id="cheung-pessary" />

In a single-blind randomized trial of previously untreated women with symptomatic stage I–III pelvic organ prolapse, adding a ring pessary to pelvic floor exercise training improved prolapse symptom distress at 6 and 12 months and quality-of-life impact at 12 months more than exercise training alone.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="cheung-pessary" />

## Design

The tertiary-center trial assigned 139 women to pessary insertion plus pelvic floor exercise training and 137 to exercise training alone. The primary outcomes were changes in prolapse symptoms and quality of life measured with the Pelvic Floor Distress Inventory and Pelvic Floor Impact Questionnaire at 6 and 12 months. Both groups received exercise training, so the randomized comparison measured the added effect of pessary treatment. Primary outcomes used intention-to-treat analysis with missing questionnaire scores imputed; the prespecified significance level was 5%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Pessary addition improved the prolapse subscales at 12 months: the intention-to-treat mean change in Pelvic Organ Prolapse Distress Inventory score was −29.7 points (n=139) versus −4.7 (n=137; calculated between-group difference, −25.0; P &lt; 0.01), and the Pelvic Organ Prolapse Impact Questionnaire changes were −29.0 versus +3.5 (calculated difference, −32.5; P &lt; 0.01). Lower scores indicate less symptom distress or impact. The minimal important difference was reached by 86/139 (62%) versus 48/137 (35%) for distress (P &lt; 0.01) and 49/139 (35%) versus 23/137 (16.8%) for impact (P = 0.01).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

At 6 months, median prolapse distress scores were 40.7 versus 54.8 (P = 0.02), while median prolapse impact scores were 5.6 versus 8.3 (P = 0.22); the latter comparison did not meet the prespecified 5% threshold. At 12 months, the corresponding median scores were 32.1 versus 49.4 (P = 0.04) and 0.3 versus 8.9 (P = 0.02). Symptom improvement at 12 months was reported by 80/132 (60.6%) pessary-arm questionnaire completers versus 36/128 (28.1%) controls (adjusted odds ratio 5.3, 95% CI 2.88–9.91; P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Pessary insertion accompanied pelvic floor exercise training in the intervention group. The largest comfortable ring was fitted, with up to three refitting attempts after expulsion; 92/139 (66%) women were successfully fitted and comfortable at 2 weeks, 87/139 (63%) retained it at 6 months, and 83/139 (60%) at 12 months. Both groups received a teaching session and individual exercise sessions at weeks 4, 8, and 16, with advice to practice at least two sets of 8–12 repetitions daily; regular exercise at 12 months was reported by 72/135 (53.3%) versus 56/130 (43.1%; P = 0.10). At 12 months, 132/139 (95.0%) in the pessary group and 128/137 (93.4%) in the exercise-only group completed questionnaires; 61 women across arms had received treatment different from their allocation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Vaginal bleeding occurred in 9/132 (6.8%) pessary recipients versus 4/128 (3.1%) controls (P = 0.17), and bothersome discharge in 6/132 (4.5%) versus 2/128 (1.6%; P = 0.16); one pessary recipient had bacterial vaginosis requiring antibiotics. De novo stress incontinence occurred in 24/50 (48.0%) versus 13/58 (22.4%; P = 0.01). De novo urge incontinence occurred in 17/73 (23.3%) versus 19/84 (22.6%; P = 0.85), and voiding difficulty in 10/92 (10.9%) versus 8/97 (8.2%; P = 0.54); these denominators exclude women with the respective baseline symptom.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Participants could not be blinded to pessary insertion. The exercise-only comparator leaves the effect of pessary treatment without exercise training unmeasured, and the reported follow-up ends at 12 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

NICE's 2019 guideline advises considering a vaginal pessary for symptomatic prolapse, either alone or alongside supervised pelvic floor muscle training <Rec>(Consider)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
