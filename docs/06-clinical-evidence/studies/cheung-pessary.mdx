# Cheung pessary trial: Vaginal Pessary in Women With Symptomatic Pelvic Organ Prolapse

<TrialBottomLine id="cheung-pessary" />

In a single-blind randomized trial of women with symptomatic stage I–III pelvic organ prolapse, adding a vaginal pessary to pelvic floor exercise training improved prolapse symptom distress and quality-of-life impact scores more than exercise training alone at 12 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="cheung-pessary" />

## Design

The trial assigned 139 women to pessary insertion plus pelvic floor exercise training and 137 to exercise training alone. The primary outcomes were changes in prolapse symptoms and quality of life measured with the Pelvic Floor Distress Inventory and Pelvic Floor Impact Questionnaire. Both groups received exercise training, so the randomized comparison measured the added effect of pessary treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At 12 months, the mean change in the Pelvic Organ Prolapse Distress Inventory score was −29.7 points with pessary addition and −4.7 points with exercise alone (calculated between-group difference, −25.0 points; P &lt; 0.01). The corresponding Pelvic Organ Prolapse Impact Questionnaire changes were −29.0 and +3.5 points (calculated difference, −32.5 points; P &lt; 0.01). Lower scores indicate less symptom distress or impact.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Pessary insertion accompanied pelvic floor exercise training in the intervention group. At 12 months, 132/139 (95.0%) in that group and 128/137 (93.4%) in the exercise-only group completed follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

The report described complication rates as low and similar between groups.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Participants could not be blinded to pessary insertion. The exercise-only comparator leaves the effect of pessary treatment without exercise training unmeasured, and the reported follow-up ends at 12 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

NICE's 2019 guideline advises considering a vaginal pessary for symptomatic prolapse, either alone or alongside supervised pelvic floor muscle training <Rec>(Consider)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
