# Perez-Marrero DMSO: A Controlled Study of Dimethyl Sulfoxide in Interstitial Cystitis

<TrialBottomLine id="dmso-ic" />

**Perez-Marrero DMSO** was a randomized, saline-controlled crossover trial of intravesical dimethyl sulfoxide in 33 patients with biopsy findings suggestive of interstitial cystitis; first-period subjective and objective improvement rates favored DMSO.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="dmso-ic" />

## Design

The study enrolled 30 women and three men. Mean age was 48 years, and mean symptom duration was 5.5 years; none had Hunner ulcers or a contracted bladder. Treatment order was randomly assigned, with 15 patients allocated DMSO first and 18 saline first; one saline-first patient was removed after pregnancy, leaving 32 for first-period analysis. Independent assessors were blinded to treatment when judging urodynamic, voiding-chart and symptom-scale responses.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

First-period marked subjective improvement occurred in 6/15 (40%) DMSO-first patients versus 3/17 (18%) saline-first patients; moderate or marked improvement occurred in 13/15 (87%) versus 10/17 (59%). Blinded assessors judged objective improvement in 14/15 (93%) versus 6/17 (35%), requiring a 50% increase in cystometric urge or capacity and/or disappearance of filling pain, supported by voiding charts or symptom scales.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

After crossover, marked subjective improvement was reported in 8/15 (53%) of the initial DMSO group while receiving saline and 7/15 (47%) of the initial saline group while receiving DMSO. Objective improvement persisted in 13/15 of the DMSO-first group and was present in 10/15 of the saline-first group after DMSO; one initial DMSO responder relapsed.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Patients received 50 cc of intravesical 50% DMSO or normal saline every two weeks for four instillations, followed by a four-week rest and crossover to four instillations of the other solution. They were asked to retain each instillation for at least 15 minutes; an outpatient nurse administered every treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Complications occurred in 5/33 patients (15%): 2/33 had uncomplicated lower urinary tract infections and 1/33 had biliary colic; these events did not interrupt treatment. Two other patients (2/33) had hemiparesis and severe migraine, respectively; both events occurred during saline treatment, resolved within 48 hours, and prompted discontinuation. The authors reported no significant side effects attributable to DMSO.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The small crossover trial enrolled biopsy-selected patients without Hunner ulcers or contracted bladders. DMSO's recognizable garlic odor complicated patient blinding. Persistent treatment or placebo effects could influence the later period; the AUA guideline considered first-period data when reported for DMSO crossover trials.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="dmso-ic" />

The trial tested DMSO alone against saline. Later intravesical regimens often combine DMSO with heparin, steroid, or local anesthetic; the guideline considers those mixtures separately.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The 2022 American Urological Association IC/BPS guideline cites this trial and states that DMSO, heparin, and/or lidocaine may be administered as intravesical treatments; DMSO carries Grade C evidence <Rec>(Option (Evidence Strength: Grades C, C, and B))</Rec>. The agents are listed without a hierarchy. For DMSO, the panel suggests a 15–20-minute dwell because longer retention is associated with pain. It cautions that DMSO can increase absorption of drugs in instillation mixtures, including lidocaine, and reports no clinical comparisons of cocktail safety or efficacy against DMSO alone.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
