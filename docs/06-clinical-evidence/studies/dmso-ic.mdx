# Perez-Marrero DMSO: A Controlled Study of Dimethyl Sulfoxide in Interstitial Cystitis

<TrialBottomLine id="dmso-ic" />

**Perez-Marrero DMSO** was a randomized, saline-controlled crossover trial of intravesical dimethyl sulfoxide in 33 patients with biopsy findings suggestive of interstitial cystitis; both subjective and objective improvement rates favored DMSO.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="dmso-ic" />

## Design

The study enrolled 30 women and three men. Mean age was 48 years, and mean symptom duration was 5.5 years. Treatment order was randomly assigned. Response was assessed symptomatically and urodynamically.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Marked subjective improvement was reported for 53% of DMSO-treated patients versus 18% of saline-treated patients. Objective improvement was reported for 93% versus 35%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Patients received intravesical 50% DMSO or saline every two weeks in two sequences of four instillations, crossing to the other treatment between sequences.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

The authors reported no significant side effects from DMSO.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The small crossover trial enrolled biopsy-selected patients. Persistent treatment or placebo effects could influence the later period; the AUA guideline considered first-period data when reported for DMSO crossover trials.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="dmso-ic" />

The trial tested DMSO alone against saline. Later intravesical regimens often combine DMSO with heparin, steroid, or local anesthetic; the guideline considers those mixtures separately.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The 2022 American Urological Association IC/BPS guideline cites this trial and states that DMSO, heparin, and/or lidocaine may be administered as intravesical treatments; DMSO carries Grade C evidence <Rec>(Option (Evidence Strength: Grades C, C, and B))</Rec>. The agents are listed without a hierarchy. For DMSO, the panel suggests a 15–20-minute dwell because longer retention is associated with pain. It cautions that DMSO can increase absorption of drugs in instillation mixtures, including lidocaine, and reports no clinical comparisons of cocktail safety or efficacy against DMSO alone.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
