# eCoin pivotal study: Leadless tibial nerve stimulation for urgency urinary incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="ecoin-pivotal" />

The eCoin pivotal study was a prospective, open-label, single-arm study of an implanted leadless tibial nerve stimulator in adults with refractory urgency urinary incontinence; 68% of the published intention-to-treat cohort had at least 50% fewer urgency incontinence episodes at 48 weeks after activation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="ecoin-pivotal" />

## Design

Fifteen US centers enrolled 137 adults; 133 received an implant and 132 entered the published intention-to-treat analysis. The primary efficacy endpoint was at least a 50% reduction in urgency urinary incontinence episodes at 48 weeks after activation. Device-related adverse events through 52 weeks after implantation were the primary safety measure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The primary responder estimate had a 95% confidence interval of 60%–76%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In the two-year extension, 72 participants completed the 96-week evaluation. Among participants completing the 96-week evaluation, the reported responder rate was 78% (95% CI 67%–87%); 48% had at least 75% fewer urgency incontinence episodes, 22% were dry on a three-day diary, and the extension reported that 91.3% did not require additional OAB medication.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The leadless device was placed in the lower leg above the fascia over the tibial nerve. FDA physician labeling describes implantation primarily in office settings under local anesthesia, activation after a 4-week healing period, and automatic 30-minute sessions every 3 days for 18 weeks, then every 4 days.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Adverse events

Device-related events occurred in 16% of implanted participants through 52 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> FDA labeling for the cohort reports device- or procedure-related events in 27/133 (20%) and serious related events in 4/133 (3%): three infections and one contact dermatitis, all resulting in explantation.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> No serious or unanticipated adverse events were reported during the extension phase.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The study had no sham or active comparator. The published primary analysis included one fewer participant than the FDA labeling analysis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> At 96 weeks, 72 of the 133 implanted participants completed evaluation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

AUA/SUFU 2024 cites the eCoin study and allows minimally invasive OAB therapy without preceding behavioral, noninvasive, or pharmacologic trials through shared decision-making <Rec>(Expert Opinion)</Rec>. For patients with OAB who have an inadequate response to, or intolerable side effects from, pharmacotherapy or behavioral therapy, its named treatment offer covers percutaneous tibial nerve stimulation, sacral neuromodulation, and intradetrusor botulinum toxin injection <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade A)</Rec>; the panel gave no recommendation specific to implanted tibial stimulators.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

The FDA approved eCoin for urgency urinary incontinence after inadequate response or intolerance to conservative treatment, or after a successful percutaneous tibial nerve stimulation trial, on March 1, 2022 (P200036).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
