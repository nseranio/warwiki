# eCoin pivotal study: Leadless tibial nerve stimulation for urgency urinary incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="ecoin-pivotal" />

The eCoin pivotal study was a prospective, open-label, single-arm study of an implanted leadless tibial nerve stimulator in adults with refractory urgency urinary incontinence; 68% of the 132-person published intention-to-treat cohort had at least 50% fewer urgency incontinence episodes at 48 weeks after activation, meeting the prespecified 40% performance goal.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="ecoin-pivotal" />

## Design

Fifteen US centers enrolled 137 adults; 133 received an implant and 132 entered the published intention-to-treat analysis after one participant with zero baseline UUI episodes was excluded. The primary efficacy endpoint was at least a 50% reduction in urgency urinary incontinence episodes at 48 weeks after activation. The study met its 40% performance goal if the lower bound of the two-sided 95% confidence interval exceeded 40%. Device-related adverse events through 52 weeks after implantation were the primary safety measure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The 48-week primary responder estimate was 68% (95% CI 60%–76%), exceeding the prespecified 40% performance goal.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Among 120 participants with available diaries, mean daily UUI episodes decreased by 2.61 (SD 2.97; P &lt; 0.001); among 116 questionnaire respondents, symptom bother changed by −34.2 (SD 27.5) and health-related quality of life by +34.5 (SD 25.9), both P &lt; 0.001.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In the two-year extension, 72 participants attended the 96-week visit. Among 69 with available diary data, 54/69 (78%; 95% CI 67%–87%) had at least 50% fewer urgency incontinence episodes, 33/69 (48%; 95% CI 36%–60%) had at least 75% fewer, and 15/69 (22%; 95% CI 13%–33%) were dry on a three-day diary.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Mean daily urgency incontinence episodes decreased by 2.97 (SD 2.64) in the 69 with available data; 67 questionnaire respondents had mean changes of −33.9 (SD 27.4) in symptom bother and +34.6 (SD 26.3) in health-related quality of life.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> During the extension, when concurrent OAB medication was permitted, 91.3% chose not to take it.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The leadless device was placed subcutaneously in the medial lower leg above the fascia over the tibial nerve; mean incision-to-final-suture time was 19.8 minutes (SD 14.3) under local anesthesia.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Stimulation used 20 pulses per second, a 200-microsecond pulse width and 0.5–15 mA amplitude adjusted to an acceptable sensory level.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> FDA physician labeling describes implantation primarily in office settings under local anesthesia, activation after a 4-week healing period, and automatic 30-minute sessions every 3 days for 18 weeks, then every 4 days.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Adverse events

Device-related events occurred in 16% of implanted participants through 52 weeks. The journal's exploratory table counts device- or procedure-related events in 26/133 (20%): 15/133 mild, 8/133 moderate and 3/133 severe; infections affected 9/133 (7%), including postoperative wound infection in 6/133 (5%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Device stimulation issues affected 6/133 (5%), and device dislocation affected 2/133 (2%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> It reports one serious procedure-related infection requiring explantation, which resolved without sequelae.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> FDA labeling for the cohort reports device- or procedure-related events in 27/133 (20%) and serious related events in 4/133 (3%): three infections and one contact dermatitis, all resulting in explantation.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The extension report counts cumulative device- or procedure-related events in 29/133 (22%) through 96 weeks after implantation, including infections in 9/133 (7%).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> It counts serious device-related events in 3/133 (2%): two implant-site infections and one contact dermatitis, all in the first year.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Two additional nonserious related events occurred between 48 and 96 weeks; no device- or procedure-related serious events or unanticipated adverse device effects occurred during the extension.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The study had no sham or active comparator. The published primary analysis included one fewer participant than the FDA labeling analysis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> At 96 weeks, 72 of the 133 implanted participants attended evaluation and 69 had available UUI diary data; missing data were not imputed for the extension analyses.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

AUA/SUFU 2024 cites the eCoin study and allows minimally invasive OAB therapy without preceding behavioral, noninvasive, or pharmacologic trials through shared decision-making <Rec>(Expert Opinion)</Rec>. For patients with OAB who have an inadequate response to, or intolerable side effects from, pharmacotherapy or behavioral therapy, its named treatment offer covers percutaneous tibial nerve stimulation, sacral neuromodulation, and intradetrusor botulinum toxin injection <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade A)</Rec>; the panel gave no recommendation specific to implanted tibial stimulators.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

The FDA approved eCoin for urgency urinary incontinence after inadequate response or intolerance to conservative treatment, or after a successful percutaneous tibial nerve stimulation trial, on March 1, 2022 (P200036).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
