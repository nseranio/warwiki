# PROSPERE: Laparoscopic Mesh Sacropexy Versus Transvaginal Mesh Repair for Primary Cystocele

<TrialBottomLine id="prospere" />

**PROSPERE** was a multicenter randomized trial of laparoscopic mesh sacropexy versus transvaginal mesh repair in women aged 45–75 years with primary POP-Q stage 2 or higher cystocele. At one year, grade II or higher complications occurred in 17% versus 26% (difference, transvaginal minus laparoscopic, 8.6 percentage points; 95% CI −1.5 to 18; P = 0.088), so the primary safety endpoint did not meet its superiority test.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="prospere" />

## Design

French public hospitals enrolled 262 women without previous prolapse surgery: 130 were assigned to laparoscopic sacropexy and 132 to transvaginal mesh. Five withdrew before intervention, leaving 129 and 128, respectively. The primary endpoint was the one-year rate of modified Clavien-Dindo grade II or higher surgical complications. Reintervention, symptoms and anatomical outcomes were secondary measures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Both procedures placed synthetic nonabsorbable mesh in the vesicovaginal space. Laparoscopic sacropexy sutured the mesh to the promontory; transvaginal repair suspended it with arms through pelvic ligaments. The extension assessed Pelvic Floor Distress Inventory-20 (PFDI-20) as its primary four-year outcome.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

At four years, PFDI-20 improved in both groups; the between-group mean difference was −7.2 points for sacropexy minus transvaginal mesh (95% CI −14.0 to −0.05; P = 0.029), below the minimal clinically important difference. Composite success was 70% versus 71% (HR 0.92, 95% CI 0.55–1.54; P = 0.75). POP-Q point C was −57 mm versus −48 mm (P = 0.0093); other POP-Q measurements did not differ.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Conversion from laparoscopic sacropexy to transvaginal mesh repair occurred in 6.3% of the laparoscopic group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

At one year, grade III or higher complications occurred in 0.8% after sacropexy and 9.4% after transvaginal mesh repair (difference 8.6 percentage points, 95% CI 3.4–15; P = 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Total reoperation occurred in 4.7% versus 10.9% (difference 6.3 percentage points, 95% CI −0.4 to 13.3; P = 0.060).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> At four years, grade III or higher complications were 2% versus 8.7% (HR 4.6, 95% CI 1.007–21.0; P = 0.049).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The four-year assessment followed 220 of 262 randomized women.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
