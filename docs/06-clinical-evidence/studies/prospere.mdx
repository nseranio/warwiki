# PROSPERE: Laparoscopic Mesh Sacropexy Versus Transvaginal Mesh Repair for Primary Cystocele

<TrialBottomLine id="prospere" />

**PROSPERE** found a lower reported one-year rate of grade II or higher complications after laparoscopic mesh sacropexy than after transvaginal mesh repair for primary cystocele, but the primary superiority test was not met: 17% versus 26% (difference, transvaginal minus laparoscopic, 8.6 percentage points; 95% CI −1.5 to 18; P = 0.088). The multicenter randomized trial enrolled women aged 45–75 years with POP-Q stage 2 or higher cystocele.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="prospere" />

## Design

French public hospitals enrolled 262 women without previous prolapse surgery: 130 were assigned to laparoscopic sacropexy and 132 to transvaginal mesh. Five withdrew before intervention, leaving 129 and 128, respectively, for the operated randomized analysis; missing follow-up data were imputed. The primary endpoint was the one-year rate of modified Clavien-Dindo grade II or higher surgical complications. Reintervention, symptoms and anatomical outcomes were secondary measures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Both procedures placed synthetic nonabsorbable mesh in the vesicovaginal space above the bladder neck. Laparoscopic sacropexy sutured the mesh to the promontory; transvaginal repair suspended it with four arms through pelvic ligaments. Apical support was mandatory in both groups. The extension assessed Pelvic Floor Distress Inventory-20 (PFDI-20) as its primary four-year outcome.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

The one-year operated-group counts underlying the primary complication comparison were 21/129 (16.3%) after sacropexy and 33/128 (25.8%) after transvaginal mesh; the report gives 17% and 26% for its analysis. Composite success at one year was 109/129 (84.5%) versus 112/128 (87.5%; difference, transvaginal minus laparoscopic, 3.0 percentage points; 95% CI −5.6 to 11.6; P = 0.823). Among women still sexually active at one year, dyspareunia or pain-related apareunia occurred in 10/71 (14%) versus 18/61 (30%; difference 15 percentage points, 95% CI 1.4–29; P = 0.031).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

At four years, PFDI-20 improved in both groups and favored sacropexy in the extension cohort (104 sacropexy, 116 transvaginal mesh): mean scores were 19.2 ± 24.6 versus 26.1 ± 29.4 points (adjusted difference, sacropexy minus transvaginal mesh, −7.2 points; 95% CI −14.0 to −0.05; P = 0.048), below the 45-point minimal clinically important difference. Composite success was 70% versus 71% (HR 0.92, 95% CI 0.55–1.54; P = 0.75); surgical revision for recurrence was 4.3% versus 5.8% (OR 0.74, 95% CI 0.17–3.0; P = 0.76). POP-Q point C was −59.7 ± 21.7 mm versus −51.7 ± 27.1 mm (adjusted difference, transvaginal minus sacropexy, 8.5 mm; 95% CI 2.2–14.9; P = 0.0093); other POP-Q measurements did not differ.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Surgery lasted a mean 119 ± 46 minutes with sacropexy versus 59 ± 34 minutes with transvaginal mesh.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Posterior mesh was placed in 113/129 (87.6%) versus 29/128 (22.7%), and concomitant stress-incontinence surgery in 48/129 (37.2%) versus 34/128 (26.6%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Seven of 129 laparoscopic assignments (5.4%) converted to vaginal surgery and one (0.8%) to laparotomy; all were strategic conversions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Of 262 randomized women, 220 entered the extension (104 sacropexy, 116 transvaginal mesh), and 209 completed the 48-month assessment (96 and 113, respectively).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

At one year, grade III or higher complications occurred in 1/129 (0.8%) after sacropexy and 12/128 (9.4%) after transvaginal mesh repair (difference 8.6 percentage points, 95% CI 3.4–15; P = 0.001). The transvaginal group had 11 grade III events and one grade IV event; the sacropexy group had one grade III event. Reoperation for complications occurred in 1/129 (0.8%) versus 11/128 (8.6%; P = 0.003), including one sling section for bladder outlet obstruction and one umbilical abscess drainage in the transvaginal group. Vaginal or bladder mesh exposure occurred in 0/129 versus 3/128 (2.3%). Total reoperation occurred in 6/129 (4.7%) versus 14/128 (10.9%; difference 6.3 percentage points, 95% CI −0.4 to 13.3; P = 0.060).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Among extension participants, four-year cumulative grade III or higher complications were 2% versus 8.7% (HR 4.6, 95% CI 1.007–21.0; P = 0.049).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In the sensitivity analysis of all randomized women, with nonparticipants censored at one year, rates were 2.8% versus 8.8% (HR 3.5, 95% CI 0.99–12.7; P = 0.052).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Three later grade III-or-higher complications required surgery: vaginal synechiae and urinary obstruction after transvaginal repair, and a rectovaginal fistula involving sacropexy mesh after recurrence treatment. Three vaginal mesh exposures occurred after transvaginal repair and none after sacropexy. Pelvic or perineal pain was reported at least once between six months and four years by 34/93 (36.6%) versus 39/105 (37.1%); 13/205 (6.3%) met chronic-pain criteria overall.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

Women who entered the four-year extension had better baseline sexual function and urinary scores than those who declined.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
