# STAR: Solifenacin Versus Tolterodine ER for Overactive Bladder

<TrialBottomLine id="star-solifenacin" />

**STAR** was a double-blind, double-dummy randomized trial in adults with overactive bladder comparing flexible-dose solifenacin with fixed-dose tolterodine ER; over 12 weeks, solifenacin produced larger reductions in urgency and incontinence symptoms.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="star-solifenacin" />

## Design

Participants received solifenacin 5 mg or tolterodine ER 4 mg once daily.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> After 4 weeks, participants in either masked group could request a dose increase, but only the solifenacin regimen could rise to 10 mg under the approved dosing schedule.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The primary variable was change in micturitions per 24 hours.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

Micturitions per 24 hours fell by 2.45 with solifenacin and 2.24 with tolterodine ER in the full analysis set; the per-protocol analysis (525 and 524 recipients) met the prespecified noninferiority criterion (P = 0.004).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The primary report describes greater reductions with solifenacin in urgency, overall incontinence, urgency incontinence, and pad use, together with greater volume voided per micturition. More solifenacin recipients became continent and reported improvement in perceived bladder condition.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

At 4 weeks on randomized starting doses, the primary frequency variable fell by 1.71 micturitions per 24 hours with solifenacin 5 mg and 1.47 with tolterodine ER 4 mg in the full analysis set (578 and 599 patients); the difference was not statistically significant. Incontinence episodes fell by 1.30 and 0.90 per 24 hours (difference in change calculated from rounded means, −0.40; ANCOVA P = 0.0181), and pad use fell by 1.21 and 0.80 pads per 24 hours (calculated difference, −0.41; ANCOVA P = 0.0089). In the patient-selected no-dose-increase subgroups, incontinence fell by 1.56 and 1.23 episodes per 24 hours at the 12-week endpoint or last visit (290 and 283 full-analysis-set patients); this comparison was descriptive.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Dose escalation was available only in the solifenacin arm despite identical masked requests in both groups. The comparison therefore tested a flexible solifenacin regimen against a fixed tolterodine ER regimen.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Most reported adverse events were mild or moderate. Adverse-event discontinuations were described as low and comparable between groups.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

At 4 weeks on the randomized starting doses, adverse-event withdrawals occurred in 18/593 (3.0%) solifenacin and 17/607 (2.8%) tolterodine ER recipients. Dry mouth occurred in 108/593 (18.2%) and 91/607 (15.0%), constipation in 18/593 (3.0%) and 7/607 (1.2%), and blurred vision in 1/593 (0.2%) and 9/607 (1.5%), respectively.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

Follow-up ended at 12 weeks. The flexible-versus-fixed dosing contrast combines drug choice with dose escalation. The later endpoint analysis (12 weeks or last clinic visit) selected patients who chose not to escalate, whereas the 4-week comparison retained randomized starting doses.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
