# MV140: Sublingual Bacterial Immunoprophylaxis for Recurrent Urinary Tract Infection

<TrialBottomLine id="mv140" />

**MV140** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized trial in women with recurrent uncomplicated cystitis that compared three or six months of sublingual MV140 with placebo; both active schedules reduced the prespecified count of culture-confirmed UTIs during the nine-month efficacy period.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="mv140" />

## Design

Investigators in Spain and the United Kingdom enrolled women aged 18–75 years who had experienced at least five uncomplicated cystitis episodes in the preceding year. Complicated infection, genitourinary comorbidities, and immunologic disease were exclusions. Participants were allocated equally to six months of placebo, three months of MV140 followed by three months of placebo, or six months of MV140. Participants and trial personnel were masked during recruitment and follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

MV140 contained heat-inactivated whole cells of *Escherichia coli*, *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, *Enterococcus faecalis*, and *Proteus vulgaris* in equal proportions. The matching placebo contained the same excipients without bacteria. The primary endpoint counted UTIs during months 4–12 after treatment began. An episode required symptoms and a positive urine culture of at least 10³ colony-forming units/mL. The protocol-defined primary analysis included women who completed week 12; the study did not plan a formal comparison of the two active durations.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Of 240 randomized women, 215 entered the protocol-defined primary analysis: 70 after three months of MV140, 69 after six months, and 76 after placebo. Median culture-confirmed UTIs over the nine-month efficacy period were 0.0 (interquartile range 0.0–1.0) in each MV140 group and 3.0 (0.5–6.0) with placebo (P &lt; 0.001 for each active comparison). The primary endpoint met its prespecified test. The secondary UTI-free outcome was 39/70 (56%), 40/69 (58%), and 19/76 (25%), respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Across the full 12-month study, median UTI counts were 1.0 (0.0–2.0) in each MV140 group and 5.0 (1.0–7.0) with placebo. Median time to the first UTI after month 3 was 275 days in both active arms and 48 days with placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

A later secondary analysis compared the three-month MV140 arm with placebo. Over months 0–12, mean days on antibiotics were 12.4 (SD 17.7) versus 28.7 (25.2); mean total symptom scores during UTIs were 102.2 (222.9) versus 194.2 (178.8). Over months 0–12, the between-arm difference in total SF-36 change from baseline favored three-month MV140 by 15.7 points (95% CI 8.80–22.64). This analysis included 74 active-arm and 76 placebo participants.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Participants self-administered two sprays under the tongue daily. The three-month arm changed to placebo for the remaining three treatment months, preserving masking. Clinics saw participants every three months; symptomatic women supplied a urine culture, and episode antibiotics were permitted. Of the randomized women, 229 initiated treatment, 87% completed the six-month treatment period, and 193 completed the 12-month study.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Investigators recorded 205 adverse events in 101 participants: 76 events in the three-month MV140 arm, 48 in the six-month arm, and 81 with placebo. Seven serious events occurred in five participants, with six events in the three-month arm, one in the six-month arm, and none with placebo. Investigators judged none related to MV140; no deaths occurred.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The primary efficacy analysis required completion of week 12 and excluded 25 randomized women. Longer-term durability and retreatment were unmeasured. The later personal-burden analysis compared only the three-month arm with placebo. The sponsors funded the trial and participated in data analysis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU *Guidelines on Urological Infections* (2026) recommends immunomodulatory prophylaxis for women with recurrent cystitis only in a well-regulated clinical trial <Rec>(Weak)</Rec>. Its evidence summary identifies MV140 as a distinct preparation and cites one randomized placebo comparison for its agent-specific estimate.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
