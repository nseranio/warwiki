# Transdermal oxybutynin dose-ranging trial: Oxybutynin patches for urge and mixed urinary incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="oxybutynin-transdermal" />

In a randomized, double-blind dose-ranging trial of 520 adults with overactive bladder and urge or urge-predominant mixed urinary incontinence, transdermal oxybutynin 3.9 mg/day reduced weekly incontinence episodes more than a placebo patch over 12 weeks (median change −19.0 versus −14.5 episodes/week; P = 0.0165). The reported P value exceeded the paper's stated per-dose threshold of 0.016.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="oxybutynin-transdermal" />

## Design

After symptom stabilization or withdrawal of previous treatment, 520 adults with at least 10 urge incontinence episodes and 56 voids in a baseline 7-day diary were assigned to patches delivering oxybutynin 1.3 (n = 130), 2.6 (n = 133), or 3.9 mg/day (n = 125), or to placebo (n = 132). Approximately 92% were women. Patches were applied twice weekly. Patient diaries recorded incontinence and voiding measures; the Incontinence Impact Questionnaire measured incontinence-specific quality of life. The modified intention-to-treat analysis included 512 patients with at least one application and one efficacy assessment, with last observation carried forward. A subsequent 12-week open-label period allowed dose titration.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The high-dose primary comparison had P = 0.0165 against a stated per-dose threshold of 0.016, despite the authors' description of it as significant. Modified intention-to-treat median weekly changes were −19.0 episodes with 3.9 mg/day (n = 123), −14.0 with 2.6 mg/day (n = 131), −15.0 with 1.3 mg/day (n = 128), and −14.5 with placebo (n = 130); the two lower doses did not differ significantly from placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The FDA statistical review of this study reported that the 3.9 mg/day difference in weekly incontinence episodes was significant by rank analysis (P = 0.0165) but not by the protocol-specified parametric ANCOVA (P = 0.2831); a corrected reanalysis excluding one site was significant (P = 0.0265).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

At 3.9 mg/day, mean daily voiding frequency fell by 2.3 versus 1.7 voids with placebo (P = 0.0457). Median average voided volume increased by 24 versus 6 mL (P = 0.0063), and the mean Incontinence Impact Questionnaire total score improved by 39% versus 28% with placebo (P = 0.0327). The 2.6 mg/day dose increased median average voided volume by 19 mL versus 6 mL with placebo (P = 0.0157); these voided-volume differences were significant only for patients with baseline volumes in the first two quartiles, including the median, and not above the baseline median of 167 mL. The lower doses had no other reported significant differences from placebo. Complete continence at the 12-week endpoint was recorded in 16/123, 7/131, 12/128, and 10/130 patients in the 3.9, 2.6, 1.3 mg/day, and placebo groups, respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The active intervention used two abdominal skin patches combining active and placebo 13-cm² and 26-cm² systems to preserve blinding, with the stated daily dose delivered through twice-weekly changes. The extension began at 1.3 mg/day and allowed titration to 2.6 or 3.9 mg/day after weeks 2 and 4 according to symptoms and adverse effects. Blinded treatment was completed by 447/520 (86.0%) patients; 411 entered and 358/411 (87.1%) completed the extension. Final extension doses were 3.9 mg/day in 211/411 (51.3%), 2.6 mg/day in 139/411 (33.8%), and 1.3 mg/day in 61/411 (14.8%). Dosing compliance was approximately 90%, and patch adherence exceeded 90% at more than 90% of application-site assessments.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Application-site pruritus occurred in 16.8%, 13.5%, and 10.8% of the 3.9, 2.6, and 1.3 mg/day groups, versus 6.1% with placebo; treatment-related erythema occurred in 5.6%, 4.5%, 3.1%, and 2.3%, respectively. Dry mouth occurred in 12/125 (9.6%), 9/133 (6.8%), and 6/130 (4.6%) of active-dose patients, versus 11/132 (8.3%) with placebo; the pooled active-dose rate was 27/388 (7.0%; P = 0.98 versus placebo). Adverse events led to discontinuation in 53/520 (10.2%) during blinded treatment, including 25 application-site-reaction withdrawals; during the extension, 30/411 (7.3%) discontinued because of adverse events, including 15 application-site-reaction withdrawals.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The headline results combine patients with urge and urge-predominant mixed incontinence. The dose-titration extension was open label and had no placebo group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## In conversation

A separate randomized study in previously treated patients compared the oxybutynin patch, oral extended-release tolterodine, and placebo. Both active treatments reduced daily incontinence episodes versus placebo in that cohort.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
