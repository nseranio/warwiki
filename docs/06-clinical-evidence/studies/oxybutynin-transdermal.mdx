# Transdermal oxybutynin dose-ranging trial: Oxybutynin patches for urge and mixed urinary incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="oxybutynin-transdermal" />

In a randomized, double-blind dose-ranging trial of adults with overactive bladder and urge or mixed urinary incontinence, transdermal oxybutynin 3.9 mg/day reduced weekly incontinence episodes more than a placebo patch over 12 weeks (median change −19.0 versus −14.5 episodes/week; P = 0.0165).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="oxybutynin-transdermal" />

## Design

After symptom stabilization or withdrawal of previous treatment, 520 adults were assigned to patches delivering oxybutynin 1.3, 2.6, or 3.9 mg/day or to placebo. Patches were applied twice weekly. Patient diaries recorded incontinence and voiding measures; the Incontinence Impact Questionnaire measured incontinence-specific quality of life. A subsequent 12-week open-label period allowed dose titration.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The FDA statistical review of this study reported that the 3.9 mg/day difference in weekly incontinence episodes was significant by rank analysis (P = 0.0165) but not by the protocol-specified parametric ANCOVA (P = 0.2831); a corrected reanalysis excluding one site was significant (P = 0.0265).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

At 3.9 mg/day, mean daily voiding frequency fell by 2.3 versus 1.7 voids with placebo (P = 0.0457). Median average voided volume increased by 24 versus 6 mL (P = 0.0063), and the Incontinence Impact Questionnaire total score improved (P = 0.0327). The 2.6 mg/day dose increased median average voided volume by 19 mL versus 6 mL with placebo (P = 0.0157); the lower doses had no other reported significant differences from placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The active intervention was a skin patch, with its stated daily dose delivered through twice-weekly changes. The extension used open-label dose titration.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Application-site pruritus occurred in 10.8%–16.8% across oxybutynin doses and 6.1% with placebo. Dry mouth was reported in 7.0% with transdermal oxybutynin and 8.3% with placebo; the reported difference was not significant.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The headline results combine patients with urge and mixed incontinence. The dose-titration extension was open label and had no placebo group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="oxybutynin-transdermal" />

A separate randomized study in previously treated patients compared the oxybutynin patch, oral extended-release tolterodine, and placebo. Both active treatments reduced daily incontinence episodes versus placebo in that cohort.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
