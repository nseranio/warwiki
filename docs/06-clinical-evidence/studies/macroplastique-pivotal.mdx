# Macroplastique pivotal trial: Polydimethylsiloxane versus collagen injection for female stress incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="macroplastique-pivotal" />

The Macroplastique pivotal trial was a multicenter, single-blind randomized comparison of transurethral Macroplastique and Contigen in women with stress incontinence primarily due to intrinsic sphincter deficiency; at 12 months after the last injection, Stamey-grade improvement was 61.5% versus 48.0%, and dryness was 36.9% versus 24.8%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="macroplastique-pivotal" />

## Design

The trial randomized 260 women. Eligibility required failed conservative treatment and urodynamic stress incontinence primarily due to intrinsic sphincter deficiency. The FDA protocol defined success as improvement by at least one Stamey grade and used a 15-percentage-point noninferiority margin.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The 12-month Stamey analysis included 122 Macroplastique and 125 Contigen recipients treated as randomized, with discontinuations counted as failures; pad weight and I-QOL used the 102 and 94 follow-up attendees.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The paper-defined intent-to-treat Stamey-grade improvement was 75/122 (61.5%) versus 60/125 (48.0%), a 13.5-percentage-point Macroplastique advantage that met the 15-point noninferiority margin (*p* &lt; 0.001). The reported 95% upper bound for the Contigen-minus-Macroplastique difference was below zero. Dryness was 45/122 (36.9%) versus 31/125 (24.8%), a 12.1-point difference (*p* &lt; 0.05). Among 12-month attendees, mean one-hour pad-weight reductions were 25.4 versus 22.8 mL (*p* = 0.64), and mean I-QOL improvements were 28.7 versus 26.4 points (*p* = 0.49).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Stamey improvement persisted to 24 months in 56/67 (84%) assessed Macroplastique responders, and 45/67 (67%) were dry; 75 had qualified at 12 months and eight withdrew before follow-up. Of 38 women dry at 12 months, 33 remained dry; 12 of 29 who had improved without becoming dry were dry at 24 months. Counting withdrawals as failures gave 56/75 (74.7%) with sustained improvement; last observation carried forward gave 64/75 (85.3%).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

One-hour pad weight fell by at least half from baseline in 53/67 (79.1%) followed responders at 24 months; the mean was 24 g at baseline and 4 g at both 12 and 24 months. Among 63 with paired I-QOL data, the mean score rose 28.2 ± 24.0 points from baseline (*p* &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Both agents were injected transurethrally through a rigid 12-degree lens cystoscope using a 5 Fr or 7 Fr needle, under local or general anesthesia, occasionally with mild sedation. The first treatment used a mean 4.6 mL in both arms; mean total volume was 6.8 mL for Macroplastique and 7.2 mL for Contigen. A second injection after the 3-month visit was used in 64/122 (52.5%) and 73/125 (58.4%), respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Repeat injection was used in 33/67 (49.3%) responders assessed at 24 months. Mean injected volume was 4.5 mL at the first treatment and 4.3 mL at the second among repeat recipients; mean total volume was 6.6 mL per patient; local anesthesia was used for 73% of treatments.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Adverse events

Adverse events affected 72/122 (59.0%) Macroplastique and 68/125 (54.4%) Contigen recipients. Urinary retention occurred in 8/122 (6.6%) versus 4/125 (3.2%), urinary tract infection in 29/122 (23.8%) versus 31/125 (24.8%), and urethral erosion in 2/122 (1.6%) versus 1/125 (0.8%). No serious treatment-related event occurred with Macroplastique; one control recipient had a serious kidney infection.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

During follow-up of 67 selected responders from 12 to 24 months, four women had six urinary tract infection events. Investigators reported no treatment- or device-related adverse events.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

The journal's intent-to-treat analysis excludes 13 randomized women who were never implanted as allocated.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The extension selected initial Macroplastique responders and did not follow a randomized Contigen comparison group.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The FDA approved Macroplastique on October 30, 2006, for transurethral injection in adult women with stress urinary incontinence primarily due to intrinsic sphincter deficiency.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
