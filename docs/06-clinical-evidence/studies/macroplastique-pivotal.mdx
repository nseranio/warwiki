# Macroplastique pivotal trial: Polydimethylsiloxane versus collagen injection for female stress incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="macroplastique-pivotal" />

The Macroplastique pivotal trial was a multicenter, single-blind randomized comparison of transurethral Macroplastique and Contigen in women with stress incontinence primarily due to intrinsic sphincter deficiency; at 12 months after the last injection, Stamey-grade improvement was 61.5% versus 48.0%, and dryness was 36.9% versus 24.8%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="macroplastique-pivotal" />

## Design

The trial randomized 260 women. Eligibility required failed conservative treatment and urodynamic stress incontinence primarily due to intrinsic sphincter deficiency. The FDA protocol defined success as improvement by at least one Stamey grade and used a 15-percentage-point noninferiority margin.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Missing 12-month outcomes counted as failures in its all-randomized analysis. The journal report presents outcomes for 122 Macroplastique and 125 Contigen recipients analyzed according to allocation.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The treated-per-protocol Stamey-grade improvement difference was 13.5 percentage points; the prespecified FDA noninferiority test was met. Dryness differed by 12.1 percentage points (*p* &lt; 0.05). Mean one-hour pad-weight reductions were 25.4 versus 22.8 mL (*p* = 0.64), and mean I-QOL improvements were 28.7 versus 26.4 points (*p* = 0.49).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Stamey improvement persisted to 24 months in 56/67 (84%) assessed Macroplastique responders, and 45/67 (67%) were dry; 75 had qualified at 12 months and eight withdrew before follow-up. Of 38 women dry at 12 months, 33 remained dry; 12 of 29 who had improved without becoming dry were dry at 24 months. Counting withdrawals as failures gave 56/75 (74.7%) with sustained improvement; last observation carried forward gave 64/75 (85.3%).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

One-hour pad weight fell by at least half from baseline in 53/67 (79.1%) followed responders at 24 months; the mean was 24 g at baseline and 4 g at both 12 and 24 months. Among 63 with paired I-QOL data, the mean score rose 28.2 ± 24.0 points from baseline (*p* &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Both agents were injected transurethrally under cystoscopic visualization, usually at three urethral sites. Macroplastique was placed submucosally 1.5–2 cm distal to the bladder neck until coaptation. A repeat injection was permitted after the 3-month visit; 64/122 (52.5%) Macroplastique recipients and 74/126 (58.7%) control-assigned recipients received one. The control treatment count includes a patient implanted with Macroplastique.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Repeat injection was used in 33/67 (49.3%) responders assessed at 24 months. Mean injected volume was 4.5 mL at the first treatment and 4.3 mL at the second among repeat recipients; mean total volume was 6.6 mL per patient; local anesthesia was used for 73% of treatments.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Adverse events

The FDA review recorded postprocedure catheterization in 53/122 (43.4%) versus 30/125 (24.0%), urinary retention in 26/122 (21.3%) versus 18/125 (14.4%), and urinary tract infection in 31/122 (25.4%) versus 31/125 (24.8%). Most catheterizations were immediate intermittent drainage in clinic.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

During follow-up of 67 selected responders from 12 to 24 months, four women had six urinary tract infection events. Investigators reported no treatment- or device-related adverse events.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

The journal's treated analysis excludes women who were randomized but never implanted.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The extension selected initial Macroplastique responders and did not follow a randomized Contigen comparison group.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The FDA approved Macroplastique on October 30, 2006, for transurethral injection in adult women with stress urinary incontinence primarily due to intrinsic sphincter deficiency.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
