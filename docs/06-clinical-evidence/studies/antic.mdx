# AnTIC: Continuous Low-Dose Antibiotic Prophylaxis in Intermittent Catheter Users

<TrialBottomLine id="antic" />

**AnTIC** was a multicenter, open-label superiority randomized trial in community-dwelling adults using clean intermittent self-catheterisation (CISC) with recurrent UTI; once-daily low-dose nitrofurantoin, trimethoprim, or cefalexin reduced symptomatic antibiotic-treated UTI incidence over 12 months compared with no prophylaxis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="antic" />

## Design

Participants had at least two symptomatic catheter-related UTIs in the preceding year or one UTI requiring admission. Those already taking prophylaxis stopped it for three months before randomization. The regimen was selected for individual suitability. Central trial and laboratory staff assessing outcomes were masked; participants and treating clinicians were not.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The primary endpoint required a symptom from a prespecified list and a treatment antibiotic course. Symptoms included difficulty catheterising, altered urine appearance, increased limb spasticity, abdominal pain, and systemic features. The modified intention-to-treat analysis required at least six months of follow-up; the trial prespecified a 20% reduction in average UTI frequency as clinically important and tested superiority at the 5% level.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The primary analysis included 181/203 (89%) assigned prophylaxis and 180/201 (90%) assigned no prophylaxis. Symptomatic antibiotic-treated UTI incidence was 1.3 (95% CI 1.1–1.6) versus 2.6 (2.3–2.9) episodes per person-year, respectively: incidence rate ratio (IRR) 0.52 (95% CI 0.44–0.61; p&lt;0.0001), meeting the superiority hypothesis and exceeding the prespecified clinically important reduction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Microbiologically confirmed symptomatic UTI incidence was 0.74 versus 1.5 episodes per person-year (IRR 0.49, 95% CI 0.39–0.60; p&lt;0.0001). Febrile UTI incidence was 0.11 versus 0.16 per person-year (IRR 0.71, 95% CI 0.40–1.26; p=0.24); 15 and 22 participants, respectively, had at least one febrile episode.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Prophylaxis was nitrofurantoin 50 mg, trimethoprim 100 mg, or cefalexin 250 mg once daily. Clinicians could change the selected agent for intolerance. During observation, 34/203 (17%) assigned prophylaxis stopped it, while 26/201 (13%) assigned no prophylaxis started it; eligible participants remained analyzed by original assignment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

At months 9–12, nitrofurantoin resistance in surveillance urinary isolates was found in 12/51 (24%) versus 6/64 (9%) participants with at least one isolate; trimethoprim resistance in 34/51 (67%) versus 21/64 (33%); and co-trimoxazole resistance in 26/49 (53%) versus 15/62 (24%). These were urine specimens collected during asymptomatic periods. Nineteen of 203 prophylaxis-assigned participants (9%) reported 22 non-serious events attributed to prophylaxis, chiefly gastrointestinal disturbance, rash, and candidal infection.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The open-label design and participant-reported primary outcome allowed differential symptom reporting, although masked central assessors applied prespecified episode criteria. The primary analysis excluded participants with less than six months of follow-up, and strategy crossover occurred in both arms. Symptomatic antibiotic-treated UTI incidence was assessed over 12 months; optional urine and perianal samples were collected at 18 months, when resistance in asymptomatic urinary isolates appeared to decrease.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

- **AUA/SUFU neurogenic lower urinary tract dysfunction guideline (2021):** Clinicians may offer oral antimicrobial prophylaxis to patients with neurogenic lower urinary tract dysfunction who perform clean intermittent catheterization and have recurrent UTI, following shared decision-making and discussion of the increased risk of antibiotic resistance <Rec>(Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **EAU urological infections guideline (2026):** Do not routinely use antibiotic prophylaxis for patients performing intermittent self-catheterisation <Rec>(Weak)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **EAU non-neurogenic female LUTS guideline (2026):** Offer prophylactic antibiotics to patients with recurrent UTI only after discussing the risk of increasing antimicrobial resistance <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
