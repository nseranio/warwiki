# T4DM: Testosterone Treatment to Prevent or Revert Type 2 Diabetes

<TrialBottomLine id="t4dm" />

**T4DM** was a double-blind, phase 3b randomized trial in 1,007 men with central obesity and impaired glucose tolerance or newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes; intramuscular testosterone undecanoate plus a lifestyle programme reduced diabetes-range two-hour glucose at two years compared with placebo plus the same programme.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="t4dm" />

## Design

Six Australian tertiary centers enrolled men aged 50–74 years with waist circumference ≥95 cm, screening serum testosterone ≤14.0 nmol/L, and no pathological hypogonadism. Eligible two-hour oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) glucose was 7.8 to &lt;11.1 mmol/L for impaired glucose tolerance or 11.1–15.0 mmol/L for newly diagnosed diabetes. The two primary outcomes were diabetes-range two-hour OGTT glucose (≥11.1 mmol/L) and mean change in two-hour glucose at two years; the allocated significance thresholds were 3.5% and 2.5%, respectively, and either could make the trial positive. Intention-to-treat analyses included participants with a 21–27-month OGTT, including those who stopped treatment or began diabetes medication.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Among men with two-year OGTT data, diabetes-range glucose occurred in 55/443 (12%) assigned testosterone and 87/413 (21%) assigned placebo (RR 0.59, 95% CI 0.43–0.80; P = 0.0007). Mean two-hour glucose changed by −1.70 and −0.95 mmol/L, respectively (between-group mean difference −0.75 mmol/L, 95% CI −1.10 to −0.40; P &lt; 0.0001). Both primary comparisons were statistically significant.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Glucose normalized to a two-hour OGTT value &lt;7.8 mmol/L in 230/443 (52%) testosterone recipients versus 179/413 (43%) placebo recipients (RR 1.20, 95% CI 1.04–1.38; P = 0.012). HbA1c change differed by −0.02 percentage points (95% CI −0.07 to 0.03; P = 0.42), with mean changes of −0.05 and −0.03, respectively. These were unadjusted secondary comparisons.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

A postrandomization survey completed by 599/1,007 men at a median 5.1 years after the last injection found no between-group difference in self-reported new diabetes diagnoses; it did not repeat OGTT testing.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In a prespecified secondary sexual-function analysis, 932 men contributed at least one IIEF-15 domain measure. At two years, adjusted erectile-function domain means were 17.9 versus 15.3 points (adjusted mean difference 2.5, 95% CI 1.7–3.4), and sexual-desire means were 6.9 versus 6.2 points (adjusted mean difference 0.68, 95% CI 0.49–0.86); both P &lt; 0.001. A correction changed the analysis count from 931 to 932.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Clinic staff slowly administered testosterone undecanoate 1,000 mg (4 mL) or matching placebo by deep gluteal intramuscular injection at baseline, six weeks, then every three months for two years, without dose or interval adjustment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Both groups had access to a lifestyle programme with an interactive website, diet and activity guidance, self-monitoring tools, and weekly group meetings.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Two years of treatment were completed by 386/504 testosterone and 370/503 placebo recipients; 116/504 and 131/503, respectively, discontinued early.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> At two years, 144/480 (30%) versus 132/471 (28%) reported using the lifestyle website or attending meetings since the previous visit.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

A hematocrit safety trigger at ≥54% occurred in 106/491 (22%) testosterone recipients versus 6/484 (1%) placebo recipients; confirmed elevation stopped treatment in 25 and one, respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> A PSA rise ≥0.75 μg/mL triggered review in 109/480 (23%) versus 87/468 (19%). Prespecified serious adverse events occurred in 55/504 (10.9%) versus 37/503 (7.4%); two men died in each group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> BPH-related hospital admissions numbered 8/504 versus 3/503, prostate cancers 4/504 versus 5/503, and venous thromboses 2/504 versus 0/503, respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Total lower urinary tract symptom scores changed by 1.35 versus 0.89 points (mean difference 0.46, 95% CI −0.15 to 1.07; P = 0.14).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In the later survey, a new sleep apnea diagnosis during treatment was reported by 3.0% versus 0.4% (P = 0.03).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The two-year OGTT endpoint was available for 856/1,007 randomized men. Men at high cardiovascular risk or with baseline hematocrit &gt;50% were excluded.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The postrandomization survey had 599/1,007 completers and used self-reported diagnoses. The sexual-function analysis included 932/1,007 men and did not adjust its secondary comparisons for multiplicity.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU Sexual and Reproductive Health guideline (limited update, 2026) cites T4DM in its discussion of metabolic effects and recommends against using testosterone therapy to reduce weight and enhance cardiometabolic status <Rec>(Weak)</Rec>.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
