# Finnish TVT versus TVT-O trial: Retropubic versus Transobturator Midurethral Tape for Stress Incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="finnish-tvt-tvto" />

The **Finnish TVT versus TVT-O trial** randomized women undergoing primary surgery for stress urinary incontinence to retropubic tension-free vaginal tape (TVT) or transobturator tension-free vaginal tape (TVT-O) at seven hospitals; objective and subjective cure did not differ significantly at two months, while early complications and postoperative opioid use were higher with TVT-O.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="finnish-tvt-tvto" />

## Design

Seven Finnish hospitals enrolled 273 women. Preoperative and two-month assessments included a cough stress test, the Urinary Incontinence Severity Score, the Detrusor Instability Score, the short-form Incontinence Impact Questionnaire and Urogenital Distress Inventory, and a visual analog scale. The investigators also recorded operation and theater time, length of stay, and intraoperative and immediate postoperative complications.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The five-year report lists previous incontinence surgery, postvoid residual &gt;100 mL, prolapse beyond second degree, and body mass index &gt;35 kg/m² among exclusions.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

The two-month report found improvement within both groups on every condition-specific quality-of-life questionnaire used, without a between-group difference. Five-year objective success required a negative cough stress test, a negative 24-hour pad test (&lt;8 g), and no SUI retreatment: 111/131 (84.7%) assessed TVT recipients and 106/123 (86.2%) assessed TVT-O recipients met all three criteria. Counting women lost to follow-up as failures gave 111/136 (81.6%) and 106/132 (80.3%), respectively. Expectations were completely or partly met in 128/136 (94.1%) after TVT and 121/132 (91.7%) after TVT-O; 115/136 (84.6%) and 113/132 (85.6%) reported complete satisfaction. Neither primary outcome differed significantly between groups.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> At five years, 254/268 (94.8%) operated women were assessed according to protocol.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The comparison used the retropubic TVT and transobturator TVT-O procedures. Of 273 randomized women, 267 underwent the allocated operation: 136 received TVT and 131 received TVT-O. One woman assigned TVT-O received TVT because of technical problems, so 268 underwent surgery.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Both slings used macroporous monofilament type 1 polypropylene, placed from the vaginal incision under local analgesia with light sedation; surgeons adjusted tape during a cough test with a 300-mL filled bladder, performed cystoscopy in every case, and did no concomitant surgery.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Hospital stay was significantly longer after TVT-O.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

The primary report found a statistically higher overall complication rate and greater postoperative opioid analgesia use after TVT-O. The investigators judged the complication difference not clinically significant. Five-year complication rates were described as low and similar between groups, with no late-onset tape-material adverse effects reported. No assessed woman had tissue reaction, erosion, or tape protrusion at the five-year visit. During follow-up, two TVT-O recipients had tape problems: one extrusion required partial excision and later TVT for recurrent incontinence; one retention case required two midline tape incisions. De novo urgency incontinence occurred in 4/131 (3.1%) TVT and 3/123 (2.4%) TVT-O recipients. Postvoid residual exceeded 100 mL in 6/131 (4.6%) and 7/123 (5.7%); 54/254 (21.3%) women reported an antibiotic-treated UTI in the preceding two years.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The initial assessment was at two months. The five-year objective endpoint combined stress testing, 24-hour pad testing, and freedom from retreatment, whereas the primary report assessed early cure and perioperative outcomes. The five-year report used questionnaires and postvoid residual to assess urgency and voiding symptoms; it did not perform urodynamics.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

**AUA/SUFU (2023).** For index patients who select midurethral sling surgery, clinicians may offer a retropubic, transobturator, or single-incision sling <Rec>(Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade A [retropubic/transobturator midurethral sling]/Grade B [single-incision sling])</Rec>. The unabridged guideline cites the Finnish five-year report in its evidence review.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
