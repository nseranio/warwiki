# STEP: Saw Palmetto for Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia

<TrialBottomLine id="step-saw-palmetto" />

**STEP** was a single-center, double-blind randomized trial of saw palmetto extract versus placebo in 225 men with moderate-to-severe BPH symptoms; after one year, neither symptom score nor peak urinary flow improved more with the extract.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="step-saw-palmetto" />

## Design

Eligible men were older than 49 years, had an American Urological Association Symptom Index (AUASI) score of at least 8, and had peak urinary flow below 15 mL/s. Men at high risk of retention, with previous BPH surgery or urethral stricture, or using medicines that affected urination were excluded. A placebo run-in screened adherence before randomization. The groups were balanced by symptom severity, and participants, outcome assessors, and analysts were blinded. Intention-to-treat mixed-effects analyses included all available data from randomized men.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The prespecified primary measures were change in AUASI score and change in peak urinary flow over 12 months. The planned superiority test used a two-sided alpha of 0.04. Prostate volume by transrectal ultrasound, postvoid residual by bladder scan, BPH bother, sexual function, quality of life, and laboratory measures were secondary outcomes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Neither primary measure favored saw palmetto: mean AUASI change was -0.68 points with saw palmetto and -0.72 with placebo, a between-group difference of 0.04 points (95% CI -0.93 to 1.01). Mean peak-flow change was +0.42 versus -0.01 mL/s, a difference of 0.43 mL/s (95% CI -0.52 to 1.38). Both missed the prespecified superiority threshold; likelihood-ratio tests of the full response curves gave P = 0.73 for AUASI and P = 0.65 for peak flow. At month 12, AUASI observations were available for 107/112 versus 109/113 men and peak-flow observations for 101/112 versus 105/113, with all available data included in the intention-to-treat models.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Secondary measures were similarly close. Postvoid residual rose by 14.10 versus 18.62 mL, a mean difference in change of -4.51 mL (95% CI -24.44 to 15.42). Prostate volume rose by 3.76 versus 4.98 mL, a mean difference of -1.22 mL (95% CI -3.90 to 1.47). BPH Impact Index scores changed by -0.33 versus -0.09 points, a difference of -0.24 points (95% CI -0.60 to 0.13). The authors reported no significant between-group difference in BPH bother, sexual function, quality of life, or PSA.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The active preparation was a single-batch, carbon-dioxide extract made by Indena and given as a 160 mg soft gelatin capsule twice daily. It contained 92.1% total fatty acids before treatment and 90.7% total fatty acids and 0.33% total sterols at the midpoint assay. The matched placebo contained polyethylene glycol-400. At one year, 107/112 men (95.5%) assigned saw palmetto and 109/113 (96.5%) assigned placebo completed follow-up, 216/225 (96.0%) overall. Capsule counts indicated that 92% of study medication was consumed, with no significant difference in adherence between groups. The studied carbon-dioxide preparation was distinct from hexane-extracted *Serenoa repens* products.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

At least one serious adverse event occurred in 6/112 men (5.4%) assigned saw palmetto and 11/113 (9.7%) assigned placebo (P = 0.31).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The later detailed safety analysis found at least one nonserious symptomatic adverse event in 39/112 (34.8%) and 34/113 (30.1%), respectively (P = 0.48).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> No serious event was judged probably related to study medication, and no deaths occurred.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Most sexual-function and laboratory comparisons were similar between groups; the few statistically different laboratory measures were small.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The study tested one standardized carbon-dioxide extract for one year, with no active-drug comparator. The exclusions removed men at high risk of retention or with prior outlet surgery. Its sample size and duration limited assessment of uncommon adverse events and longer-term BPH progression.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
