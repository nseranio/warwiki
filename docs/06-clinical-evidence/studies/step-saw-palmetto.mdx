# STEP: Saw Palmetto for Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia

<TrialBottomLine id="step-saw-palmetto" />

**STEP** was a single-center, double-blind randomized trial of saw palmetto extract versus placebo in 225 men with moderate-to-severe BPH symptoms; after one year, neither symptom score nor peak urinary flow improved more with the extract.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="step-saw-palmetto" />

## Design

Eligible men were older than 49 years, had an American Urological Association Symptom Index (AUASI) score of at least 8, and had peak urinary flow below 15 mL/s. Men at high risk of retention, with previous BPH surgery or urethral stricture, or using medicines that affected urination were excluded. A placebo run-in screened adherence before randomization. The groups were balanced by symptom severity, and participants, outcome assessors, and analysts were blinded. The analysis used all available data from randomized men.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The prespecified primary measures were change in AUASI score and change in peak urinary flow over 12 months. The planned superiority test used a two-sided alpha of 0.04. Prostate volume by transrectal ultrasound, postvoid residual by bladder scan, BPH bother, sexual function, quality of life, and laboratory measures were secondary outcomes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Mean AUASI change was -0.68 points with saw palmetto and -0.72 with placebo. The mean between-group difference in change was 0.04 points (95% CI -0.93 to 1.01), so the primary symptom endpoint did not meet the superiority threshold. Mean peak-flow change was +0.42 versus -0.01 mL/s; the difference was 0.43 mL/s (95% CI -0.52 to 1.38), also below the prespecified threshold for superiority.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Secondary measures were similarly close. Postvoid residual rose by 14.10 versus 18.62 mL, a mean difference in change of -4.51 mL (95% CI -24.44 to 15.42). Prostate volume rose by 3.76 versus 4.98 mL, a mean difference of -1.22 mL (95% CI -3.90 to 1.47). The authors reported no significant between-group difference in BPH bother, sexual function, quality of life, or PSA.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The active preparation was a single-batch, carbon-dioxide extract made by Indena and given as a 160 mg soft gelatin capsule twice daily. The matched placebo contained polyethylene glycol-400. At one year, 216/225 men (96.0%) completed follow-up, and capsule counts indicated that 92% of study medication was consumed. The studied carbon-dioxide preparation was distinct from hexane-extracted *Serenoa repens* products.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

At least one serious adverse event occurred in 6/112 men (5.4%) assigned saw palmetto and 11/113 (9.7%) assigned placebo (P = 0.31).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The later detailed safety analysis found at least one nonserious symptomatic adverse event in 39/112 (34.8%) and 34/113 (30.1%), respectively (P = 0.48).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> No serious event was judged probably related to study medication, and no deaths occurred. Most sexual-function and laboratory comparisons were similar between groups; the few statistically different laboratory measures were small.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The study tested one standardized carbon-dioxide extract for one year, with no active-drug comparator. The exclusions removed men at high risk of retention or with prior outlet surgery. Its sample size and duration limited assessment of uncommon adverse events and longer-term BPH progression.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
