# Intibia Pivotal Study: Sham-Controlled Implantable Tibial Nerve Stimulation for Urgency Urinary Incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="intibia-sham" />

The **Intibia Pivotal Study** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized trial of active Intibia implantable tibial nerve stimulation versus sham stimulation in 208 adults with urgency urinary incontinence after conservative treatment. Its first published report, a planned secondary quality-of-life analysis, found greater patient-reported improvement with active stimulation at three months; the prespecified UUI responder results were deferred to a separate report.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="intibia-sham" />

## Design

Investigators at US and Dutch sites randomized participants after implantation, assigning 139 to active stimulation and 69 to sham. Entry required diary-confirmed urgency incontinence despite conservative or behavioral treatment, including pharmacotherapy. Prior tibial or sacral stimulation, recent intradetrusor botulinum toxin, stress or mixed incontinence, and neurological disease affecting bladder or tibial nerve function were exclusion criteria. OAB medication was withheld throughout follow-up. Investigators did not perform urodynamics; diagnosis relied on questionnaire responses and a negative cough stress test.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Participants, investigators, and staff remained blinded until completion of the three-month diaries and questionnaires. The reported quality-of-life outcomes included Patient Global Impression of Improvement (PGI-I), OAB Questionnaire Short Form (OABq-SF) symptom bother and health-related quality of life, and the ICIQ-UI SF. The published analyses used available complete cases, and the study's sample size was set for its pivotal efficacy outcomes rather than these quality-of-life measures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At three months, 40/137 active participants (29.2%) versus 9/67 sham participants (13.4%) rated their bladder condition much or very much better on PGI-I: a 15.8-percentage-point difference (95% CI 4.6–26.9; P = 0.0133). The OABq-SF symptom-bother clinically important improvement threshold was exceeded by 86/137 (62.8%) versus 28/67 (41.8%; P = 0.0046), and the health-related quality-of-life threshold by 78/136 (57.4%) versus 28/66 (42.4%; P = 0.0463).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Mean ICIQ-UI SF scores improved by 2.5 points in the active group and 1.9 points in the sham group at three months; the between-group mean difference was −0.55 points (95% CI −1.75 to 0.65; P = 0.3687). After sham crossover, 93/130 active-arm respondents (71.5%) exceeded the OABq-SF symptom-bother threshold at 12 months; this was a within-arm comparison with baseline.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Through one incision, the pulse generator was placed subcutaneously above the fascia and its integrated electrode lead was deployed beneath the fascia adjacent to the tibial nerve. Intraoperative stimulation confirmed lead location. All devices remained off for two weeks after implantation. Programming used sensory feedback or great-toe flexion and participant comfort; active treatment delivered 20-Hz, 200-microsecond pulses for 30 minutes daily for two weeks, then 30 minutes weekly. Sham devices stayed inactive through the blinded assessment, after which those participants crossed over to adjustable active stimulation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Three-month visits were completed by 137/139 active and 67/69 sham participants. At 12 months, 129/139 active and 62/69 sham participants completed follow-up, although the sham group had crossed over after the blinded phase.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Seven of the 208 randomized participants withdrew because of adverse events. This quality-of-life report did not give adverse-event types, device attribution, or arm-specific withdrawal counts.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The sham comparison ended at three months, so the later observations followed crossover and individual stimulation adjustments. Complete-case questionnaires exclude missing responses, and enrollment was predominantly female. The trial excluded prior nerve stimulation and concomitant OAB medication, limiting direct comparison with treated populations seen in practice. The quality-of-life report does not present the study's prespecified UUI responder analysis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
