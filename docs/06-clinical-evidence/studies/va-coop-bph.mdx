# VA Cooperative BPH: Terazosin, Finasteride, or Both for Symptomatic BPH

<TrialBottomLine id="va-coop-bph" />

**VA Cooperative BPH** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized trial of terazosin, finasteride, both drugs, or placebo in 1,229 men with symptomatic BPH. At one year, terazosin-containing treatment improved AUA symptom scores and peak urinary flow over placebo; finasteride alone did not, and adding it to terazosin gave no further benefit.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="va-coop-bph" />

## Design

Eligibility required AUA symptom score ≥8, peak flow 4–15 mL/s, voided volume ≥125 mL, and residual &lt;300 mL; prostate size was unrestricted.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The assigned daily doses were terazosin 10 mg and finasteride 5 mg.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> AUA symptom score and peak urinary flow were measured at baseline and periodically thereafter.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Terazosin or matching placebo was taken at bedtime and titrated from 1 mg on days 1–3 to 2 mg on days 4–7, 5 mg on days 8–14, and 10 mg from day 15; investigators could reduce terazosin to 5 mg for an attributed adverse effect.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Finasteride or matching placebo was taken as 5 mg nightly.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Treatment completion was 254/305 placebo, 256/305 terazosin, 243/310 finasteride, and 254/309 combination recipients (78%–84%); pill-count adherence was 94%–98%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At 52 weeks, mean AUA symptom scores fell by 2.6 points with placebo (n = 265), 6.1 with terazosin (n = 275), 3.2 with finasteride (n = 260), and 6.2 with both drugs (n = 278). Mean peak flow rose by 1.4 (n = 264), 2.7 (n = 275), 1.6 (n = 252), and 3.2 mL/s (n = 277), respectively. In the repeated-measures analysis of men with any follow-up data, both terazosin-containing arms differed from placebo and finasteride on both measures (P &lt; 0.001); finasteride did not differ from placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Prostate volume rose 0.5 cm³ with placebo and terazosin and fell 6.1 with finasteride, 7.0 with both drugs at 52 weeks (P &lt; 0.001 for either finasteride arm versus either other arm).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Terazosin-containing treatment reduced symptom bother and BPH impact more than placebo or finasteride at 52 weeks. Mean symptom problem scores fell by 1.4 points (n = 265) with placebo, 1.7 (n = 259) with finasteride, 3.9 (n = 276) with terazosin, and 4.2 (n = 279) with combination treatment; BPH impact scores fell by 0.5, 0.5, 1.2, and 1.7 points in the same arms and denominators. Repeated-measures comparisons through 52 weeks favored each terazosin-containing arm over placebo for both scores (P &lt; 0.0001); finasteride versus placebo and combination versus terazosin did not differ significantly.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Patient-rated marked or moderate improvement at 52 weeks occurred in 103/264 (39%) placebo recipients, 114/257 (44%) finasteride recipients, 167/273 (61%) terazosin recipients, and 180/278 (65%) combination recipients. The tested comparison counted any improvement: 190/264 (72.0%), 178/257 (69.3%), 219/273 (80.2%), and 232/278 (83.5%), respectively. Terazosin versus placebo yielded P = 0.025 and combination versus placebo P = 0.001; finasteride versus placebo and combination versus terazosin did not differ significantly.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Finasteride increased peak flow in the baseline prostate-volume subgroup &gt;50 cm³ by 2.7 mL/s in 43 men versus 0.6 mL/s in 56 placebo recipients (difference 2.1 mL/s, 95% CI 0.72–3.48; P = 0.0027). AUA symptom, symptom problem, and BPH impact scores did not differ significantly between these two arms in that subgroup.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Paired PSA measurements showed mean PSA rising from 2.34 to 2.60 ng/mL with placebo and 2.18 to 2.49 with terazosin; it fell from 2.26 to 1.22 with finasteride and 2.38 to 1.36 with both drugs at 52 weeks. The declines were significant (P &lt; 0.001); terazosin and placebo did not differ. A 40%–60% decrease occurred in 34.0% with finasteride and 30.3% with both drugs.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Adverse events

Adverse-event discontinuation occurred in 5/305 (1.6%) placebo, 18/305 (5.9%) terazosin, 15/310 (4.8%) finasteride, and 24/309 (7.8%) combination recipients. Dizziness affected 22/305, 79/305, 26/310, and 66/309; postural hypotension affected 3/305, 23/305, 7/310, and 27/309.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Sexual adverse effects included impotence in 14/305 placebo, 18/305 terazosin, 29/310 finasteride, and 29/309 combination recipients; ejaculatory abnormalities occurred in 4/305, 1/305, 6/310, and 21/309, and decreased libido in 4/305, 8/305, 14/310, and 15/309, respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The reported efficacy endpoints were one-year symptom score and peak flow, with no comparison of later urinary retention or BPH surgery in the primary report.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## In conversation

EAU 2026 distinguishes the one-year symptom findings of this trial from later four-year MTOPS and CombAT findings on progression, retention, and BPH surgery.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

**EAU (March 2026).** Offer an alpha-1 blocker plus a 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor to men with moderate-to-severe LUTS and increased progression risk, such as prostate volume &gt; 40 mL <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>. Its evidence discussion cites the VA trial for the short-term combination comparison.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
