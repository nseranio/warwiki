# Paraiso rectocele repair: Three Surgical Techniques Including Graft Augmentation

<TrialBottomLine id="paraiso-rectocele" />

The Paraiso randomized trial assigned women with stage II or greater posterior vaginal wall prolapse to posterior colporrhaphy, site-specific rectocele repair, or site-specific repair augmented with porcine small-intestinal-submucosa graft; the graft arm had the highest one-year anatomic failure rate.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="paraiso-rectocele" />

## Design

The three arms enrolled 37, 37, and 32 women, respectively. Examination and validated pelvic-floor questionnaires were scheduled before surgery and at six months, one year, and two years. The anatomic endpoint was posterior POP-Q point Bp ≥−2 at one year. The graft was Fortagen, a porcine small-intestinal-submucosa product used to augment the site-specific repair.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

One-year anatomic failure occurred in 4/28 (14%) after colporrhaphy, 6/27 (22%) after native-tissue site-specific repair, and 12/26 (46%) after graft-augmented repair (overall P = .02). Pelvic Floor Distress Inventory and Pelvic Floor Impact Questionnaire prolapse, colorectal, and summary scores improved in all arms (within-group P &lt; .001), without a between-group difference. Functional failure was 15% overall and did not differ significantly among arms. Sexual function scores improved in all arms (P &lt; .001), without a between-group difference.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Surgery was performed in 105/106 randomized women. The study compared posterior colporrhaphy with site-specific repair, with or without the porcine graft. Among those operated on, 98 returned for follow-up; their mean follow-up was 17.5 ± 7 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Dyspareunia rates did not change significantly at one year and did not differ significantly among arms.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The anatomic endpoint used 28, 27, and 26 assessed patients from randomized groups of 37, 37, and 32. Follow-up beyond the one-year anatomic endpoint was incomplete.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

NICE NG123 (2019) recommends posterior vaginal repair without mesh for women with posterior vaginal wall prolapse <Rec>(Offer)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Its surgical evidence review includes the Paraiso trial in the posterior-repair comparison.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
