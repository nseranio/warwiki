# Paraiso rectocele repair: Three Surgical Techniques Including Graft Augmentation

<TrialBottomLine id="paraiso-rectocele" />

The Paraiso randomized trial assigned 106 women with stage II or greater posterior vaginal wall prolapse to posterior colporrhaphy, site-specific rectocele repair, or site-specific repair augmented with a Fortagen porcine small-intestinal-submucosa graft; the graft arm had the lowest one-year anatomic cure rate.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="paraiso-rectocele" />

## Design

The three arms enrolled 37, 37, and 32 women, respectively. Women at least 21 years old were eligible; concomitant prolapse or incontinence surgery was permitted. Examination and validated pelvic-floor questionnaires were scheduled before surgery and at six months, one year, and two years. The primary endpoint was anatomic cure, posterior POP-Q point Bp ≤ −2 at 12 months. Patients were blinded immediately after surgery but could learn their assignment at six weeks; a blinded nurse performed postoperative assessments.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

One-year anatomic cure was 24/28 (86%) after colporrhaphy, 21/27 (78%) after native-tissue site-specific repair, and 14/26 (54%) after graft-augmented repair (overall P = .02). These were the 81 women with anatomic assessments, analyzed according to randomized assignment. Prolapse to or beyond the hymen occurred in 2/28 (7%), 2/27 (7%), and 5/25 (20%), respectively (P = .27).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

One-year functional failure, defined as worsening prolapse or colorectal symptom scores, occurred in 5/31 (16%), 4/29 (14%), and 6/28 (21%) women (P = .61). Mean one-year PFDI-20 totals were 39, 46, and 34 (between-group P = .28); mean PFIQ-7 totals were 10, 22, and 10 (P = .65). Prolapse, colorectal, urinary, and total scores improved within all three groups (P &lt; .001), with no between-group differences. Mean PISQ-12 sexual-function scores at one year were 36, 36, and 37 (between-group P = .24); each arm improved from baseline (P &lt; .001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Perineorrhaphy was associated with less bothersome splinting at one year after adjustment for treatment group and baseline symptoms (OR 0.08, 95% CI 0.004–0.71; P = .038).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Surgery was performed in 105/106 randomized women, and 98/105 returned for follow-up (mean 17.5 ± 7 months). Three protocol violations were analyzed in their assigned groups. Posterior colporrhaphy plicated rectovaginal fibromuscularis without levator plication; site-specific repair reapproximated discrete defects. The graft arm added a 4 × 8 cm Fortagen graft, perforated in rows of 3-mm incisions and secured to posterior fibromuscularis, levator ani fascia, and perineal body. Mean operating times were 150 ± 68, 151 ± 69, and 169 ± 62 minutes (P = .61). All but four women had concomitant procedures; 82/105 underwent perineorrhaphy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Dyspareunia reported as "usually" or "always" at one year was 4/20, 3/22, and 1/19 by arm; dyspareunia did not change significantly within groups or differ between them (distribution P = .45). Urinary tract infections within six weeks occurred in 4/37, 7/37, and 6/31; infection requiring antibiotics occurred in 4/37, 0/37, and 3/31. Reoperation for prolapse in any compartment during the study occurred in 1/33, 2/37, and 3/29 (P = .47). One graft-arm patient returned to the operating room on the day of surgery for bleeding. No graft exposures or graft complications were reported among the 31 operated women assigned to the graft arm; one received SurgiSIS instead of Fortagen.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The anatomic endpoint used 28, 27, and 26 assessed patients from randomized groups of 37, 37, and 32. Follow-up beyond the one-year anatomic endpoint was incomplete, and all but four women underwent other pelvic procedures with the rectocele repair. Patients could learn their assignment at six weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

NICE NG123 (2019) recommends posterior vaginal repair without mesh for women with posterior vaginal wall prolapse <Rec>(Offer)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Its surgical evidence review includes the Paraiso trial in the posterior-repair comparison.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
