# RAZOR: Robot-Assisted Versus Open Cystectomy With Urinary Diversion

<TrialBottomLine id="razor" />

**RAZOR** was a multicenter, open-label, phase 3 noninferiority randomized trial of robot-assisted versus open radical cystectomy, both with extracorporeal urinary diversion, in adults with bladder cancer or refractory carcinoma in situ. Robot-assisted surgery met the prespecified noninferiority criterion for two-year progression-free survival.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="razor" />

## Design

Fifteen US centers randomized 350 patients (176 robotic; 174 open). Eligible patients had biopsy-proven clinical T1–T4, N0–N1, M0 disease or refractory carcinoma in situ. Previous open abdominal or pelvic surgery and conditions precluding pneumoperitoneum excluded patients. Pathologists were masked; patients and surgeons were not. Surgeons had performed at least ten radical cystectomies in the preceding year and were fellowship trained or focused on bladder cancer.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The primary analysis included only patients who received their assigned procedure: 150 robotic and 152 open. Progression-free survival included radiographic or pathological progression, recurrence, and death from any cause. Noninferiority required the lower confidence bound for the difference in two-year progression-free survival (robotic minus open) to exceed −15 percentage points. The trial also analyzed patients who were randomized and underwent either operation as a sensitivity set.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

In the per-protocol analysis, two-year progression-free survival was 72.3% (95% CI 64.3–78.8; n = 150) after robotic surgery and 71.6% (95% CI 63.6–78.2; n = 152) after open surgery, a risk difference of 0.7 percentage points (95% CI −9.6 to 10.9; one-sided noninferiority P = 0.001). The modified intention-to-treat sensitivity analysis yielded 72.3% (95% CI 64.5–78.6; n = 159) and 71.8% (95% CI 63.8–78.3; n = 153), respectively (difference 0.5 percentage points, 95% CI −9.7 to 10.6; noninferiority P = 0.001). Positive surgical margins occurred in 9/150 (6%) and 7/152 (5%; difference 1.4 percentage points, 95% CI −3.7 to 6.5; P = 0.59). Local recurrence occurred in 6/150 (4%) and 4/152 (3%; P = 0.54); no port-site recurrences were reported.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

At three years, estimated progression-free survival was 68.4% versus 65.4% (P = 0.600), and overall survival was 73.9% versus 68.5% (P = 0.334), in the robotic and open per-protocol groups, respectively.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> A separate quality-of-life analysis of subsets of this cohort found no between-approach difference in FACT–Vanderbilt Cystectomy Index scores at baseline, three months, or six months.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Diversion was extracorporeal in both groups. The per-protocol robotic group received 113 ileal conduits, 36 neobladders, and one continent cutaneous reservoir; the open group received 122 ileal conduits and 30 neobladders. Diversion choice followed patient and surgeon preference. Centers selected standard or extended pelvic lymphadenectomy and perioperative chemotherapy according to institutional practice. Standard lymphadenectomy extended proximally to the ureter crossing the common iliac vessels; extended lymphadenectomy reached the aortic bifurcation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Among assigned patients, 17/176 in the robotic group and 21/174 in the open group had no surgery. Nine robotic-assigned patients received a different operation, including five conversions to open surgery; one open-assigned patient received robotic surgery. Median estimated blood loss was 300 versus 700 mL, and perioperative transfusion occurred in 35/143 (24%) versus 65/143 (45%). Median operative time was 428 versus 361 minutes, with median hospital stay of six versus seven days. Extended lymphadenectomy was performed in 76/149 (51%) versus 84/152 (55%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Within 90 days, any Clavien–Dindo grade I–V complication occurred in 101/150 (67%) robotic versus 105/152 (69%) open recipients; grade III–V complications occurred in 33/150 (22%) versus 34/152 (22%), including grade V complications in 4/150 (3%) versus 4/152 (3%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Urinary tract infection occurred in 53/150 (35%) versus 39/152 (26%); postoperative ileus in 33/150 (22%) versus 31/152 (20%); ureteral stricture in 13/150 (9%) versus 10/152 (7%); anastomotic bowel leak in 3/150 (2%) versus 0/152; and deep wound infection in 3/150 (2%) versus 10/152 (7%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> A later RAZOR analysis reported 90-day readmission of 24.1% versus 23.1% and one-year readmission of 29.5% versus 28.5%.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Limitations

The primary analysis omitted patients who did not undergo their assigned operation. All robotic diversions were extracorporeal. The surgical teams were experienced, and diversion type, lymphadenectomy extent, chemotherapy, and perioperative care varied by patient or institution. The prespecified noninferiority margin allowed a difference as large as 15 percentage points in two-year progression-free survival. Cost data could not be analyzed across centers because of missing and heterogeneous submissions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU 2026 muscle-invasive bladder cancer guideline recommends informing patients of the advantages and disadvantages of open and robot-assisted radical cystectomy <Rec>(Strong)</Rec> and selecting centers for either approach by experience <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>. Its evidence review includes trials with extracorporeal and intracorporeal diversion.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
