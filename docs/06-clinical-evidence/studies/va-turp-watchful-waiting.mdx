# VA TURP versus watchful waiting: Transurethral resection for moderate BPH symptoms

<TrialBottomLine id="va-turp-watchful-waiting" />

**VA TURP versus watchful waiting** was a multicenter randomized trial of immediate transurethral resection of the prostate (TURP) versus watchful waiting in 556 men older than 54 with moderate BPH symptoms; TURP reduced the three-year composite treatment-failure rate and improved urinary symptoms and bother.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="va-turp-watchful-waiting" />

## Design

Men with symptom scores of 10–20 on the trial’s 0–27 scale were eligible; prior prostate surgery, postvoid residual &gt;350 mL, and creatinine &gt;3.0 mg/dL were among the exclusions. The primary outcome was treatment failure: death, repeated or intractable urinary retention, postvoid residual &gt;350 mL, bladder calculus, new persistent incontinence requiring a device, symptom score ≥24 once or ≥21 twice, or doubled serum creatinine. Symptoms, urinary bother, and interference with daily activities were assessed by standardized questionnaires and medical evaluation. The analysis retained men in their randomized groups after treatment changes; two-sided P ≤ 0.05 defined statistical significance.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Immediate TURP reduced three-year treatment failure: over a mean 2.8 years, failure occurred in 23/280 (8.2%) assigned to TURP and 47/276 (17.0%) assigned to watchful waiting (relative risk 0.48, 95% CI 0.30–0.77; P = 0.002 for event rates). The excess failures during watchful waiting included urinary retention (8/276 [2.9%] versus 1/280 [0.4%]), high residual volume (16/276 [5.8%] versus 3/280 [1.1%]), and high symptom scores (12/276 [4.3%] versus 1/280 [0.4%]). At three years, symptom scores were 4.9 versus 9.1, urinary-bother scores 75.7 versus 57.6, and daily-activity scores 86.4 versus 75.6 after assignment to TURP versus watchful waiting (P &lt; 0.001 for between-group change in each). Peak flow was 17.8 versus 12.7 mL/s (P &lt; 0.001 for change), and postvoid residual was 51 versus 72 mL (P = 0.015 for change). Among men assigned TURP, urinary bother improved in 134/148 (91%) who were more bothered at baseline versus 45/73 (62%) who were less bothered.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the intention-to-treat follow-up extending to five years (mean 3.4 years for assigned TURP and 3.6 years for assigned watchful waiting), treatment failure occurred in 28/280 (10.0%) assigned to TURP and 58/276 (21.0%) assigned to watchful waiting (relative risk 0.476, 95% CI 0.313–0.724; P = 0.0004), meeting the paper's two-sided 0.05 criterion. Urinary retention occurred in 1/280 (0.4%) versus 9/276 (3.3%) (relative risk 0.110, 95% CI 0.014–0.859; P = 0.011), and high residual urine volume in 5/280 (1.8%) versus 19/276 (6.9%) (relative risk 0.259, 95% CI 0.098–0.685; P = 0.003). Outcomes also favored initial TURP for symptom score, peak urinary flow, postvoid residual, and urinary bother. In the as-treated model for men with baseline peak flow of 7 mL/s, the estimated 60-month gain was 8.682 mL/s after initial TURP, 4.676 mL/s after later crossover, and 2.225 mL/s with continued watchful waiting (initial TURP versus later crossover, P = 0.0002); subsequent urinary bother was similar after early and later TURP.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

TURP was delivered within two weeks of randomization to 249/280 (88.9%) assigned to surgery.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Among 242 procedures with complete operative information, 97% were resected to the capsule, median tissue weight was 14 g (IQR 8–21 g), and 78% had a hospital stay of four days or less (mean 4 ± 4 days).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Both groups were advised to avoid coffee, alcohol, and other liquids after dinner and symptom-worsening medications; physicians were asked to avoid alpha-adrenergic antagonists. Visits occurred at six to eight weeks and then twice yearly for three years.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Of 276 assigned to watchful waiting, 65 (23.6%) underwent surgery within three years, 20 because of treatment failure; 76/276 (27.5%) underwent TURP during observed follow-up, while the five-year Kaplan–Meier cumulative crossover estimate was 36%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

At three years, persistent incontinence occurred in 4/280 (1.4%) assigned to TURP and 4/276 (1.4%) assigned to watchful waiting; sexual-performance scores changed by −3.0 versus −3.2 points on a 0–100 scale (P = 0.920). Among 242 TURP procedures with complete operative information, 30-day complications included repeat catheter placement in 9/242 (3.7%), capsule perforation in 5/242 (2.1%), transfusion in 3/242 (1.2%), urinary infection in 2/242 (0.8%), and thrombophlebitis in 1/242 (0.4%); no deaths were associated with surgery. Within three years after surgery, nine men needed endoscopic surgery for bladder-neck contracture, nine underwent urethral dilation for stricture, and eight underwent a second TURP, four for adenocarcinoma.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Through the extended follow-up, persistent incontinence occurred in 4/280 (1.4%) assigned to TURP and 8/276 (2.9%) assigned to watchful waiting (P = 0.259); 11/280 (3.9%) assigned to TURP underwent a repeat procedure.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

Treatment crossover reduced the separation between randomized strategies over follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU 2026 guideline cites both VA reports in its watchful-waiting evidence review and recommends offering watchful waiting to men with mild or moderate LUTS who are minimally bothered by their symptoms <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
