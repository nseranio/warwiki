# VA TURP versus watchful waiting: Transurethral resection for moderate BPH symptoms

<TrialBottomLine id="va-turp-watchful-waiting" />

**VA TURP versus watchful waiting** was a multicenter randomized trial of immediate transurethral resection of the prostate (TURP) versus watchful waiting in 556 men older than 54 with moderate BPH symptoms; TURP reduced the three-year composite treatment-failure rate and improved urinary symptoms and bother.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="va-turp-watchful-waiting" />

## Design

The primary outcome was treatment failure: death, repeated or intractable urinary retention, postvoid residual &gt;350 mL, bladder calculus, new persistent incontinence, a high symptom score, or doubled serum creatinine. Symptoms, urinary bother, and interference with daily activities were assessed by standardized questionnaires and medical evaluation. The analysis retained men in their randomized groups after treatment changes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Over a mean 2.8 years, treatment failure occurred in 23/280 (8.2%) assigned to TURP and 47/276 (17.0%) assigned to watchful waiting (relative risk 0.48, 95% CI 0.30–0.77). Symptom, urinary-difficulty, and daily-activity interference scores improved more after TURP (P &lt; 0.001 for each comparison); improvement was greatest among men with greater baseline urinary bother.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the five-year follow-up, treatment failure was 10% after assignment to TURP versus 21% after assignment to watchful waiting (P = 0.0004). Outcomes favored initial TURP for symptom score, peak urinary flow, postvoid residual, and urinary bother. Among men who eventually crossed over to surgery, those with low baseline peak flow had less improvement in peak flow than comparable men assigned to immediate TURP; subsequent urinary bother was similar.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Of 280 assigned to TURP, 249 (89%) underwent resection within two weeks of randomization.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Of 276 assigned to watchful waiting, 65 (24%) underwent surgery within three years; crossover reached 36% by five years.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Surgery was not associated with impotence or urinary incontinence in the three-year comparison.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Treatment crossover reduced the separation between randomized strategies over follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU 2026 guideline cites both VA reports in its watchful-waiting evidence review and recommends offering watchful waiting to men with mild or moderate LUTS who are minimally bothered by their symptoms <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
