# Stamm 1980: Antimicrobial Prophylaxis of Recurrent Urinary Tract Infections

<TrialBottomLine id="stamm-1980" />

**Stamm 1980** was a double-blind randomized trial in 60 women with recurrent urinary tract infections that compared daily trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, trimethoprim, or nitrofurantoin macrocrystals with placebo; each antibiotic reduced infections during six months of prophylaxis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="stamm-1980" />

## Design

Women received one of four once-daily regimens: trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole 40 mg/200 mg, trimethoprim 100 mg, nitrofurantoin macrocrystals 100 mg, or placebo. The outcome reported was urinary tract infections per patient-year during the treatment period. The study also examined infections after prophylaxis stopped, including recurrence in women with at least three infections in the preceding year.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

During prophylaxis, infection rates were 0.15 per patient-year with trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, 0.0 with trimethoprim, and 0.14 with nitrofurantoin, versus 2.8 with placebo (P &lt; 0.001 for placebo versus each drug regimen). The rates among the three active treatments were described as comparable.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Protection was limited to the period of antimicrobial use. After treatment stopped, infections were more likely in women who had at least three infections in the year before prophylaxis (P &lt; 0.005).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The intervention was a once-daily preventive regimen continued for six months; the trial involved no procedure or device.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

The investigators described the regimens as well tolerated and emergence of trimethoprim-resistant *Escherichia coli* as rare. Non-*E. coli* infections occurred more often after prophylaxis ended (P &lt; 0.05).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The study enrolled 60 women and tested six months of prophylaxis. The small cohort limits precision for uncommon adverse outcomes, and the treatment period addresses prevention only during active dosing.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA/CUA/SUFU recurrent UTI guideline, amended 2025, permits antibiotic prophylaxis after discussion of risks, benefits, and alternatives to decrease future UTIs in women previously diagnosed with UTIs <Rec>(Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>. It cites this trial among the antibiotic comparisons.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
