# Stamm 1980: Antimicrobial Prophylaxis of Recurrent Urinary Tract Infections

<TrialBottomLine id="stamm-1980" />

**Stamm 1980** enrolled 60 women with recurrent urinary tract infections: 45 were randomized to daily trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, nitrofurantoin macrocrystals, or placebo, and 15 with drug allergies received trimethoprim nonrandomly. Both randomized antibiotics reduced infections during six months of prophylaxis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="stamm-1980" />

## Design

Eligible nonpregnant women had at least two culture-documented urinary infections in the preceding year. Women received one of four bedtime regimens: trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole 40 mg/200 mg, trimethoprim 100 mg, nitrofurantoin macrocrystals 100 mg, or placebo. The report gives urinary infections per patient-year and the proportion infected during treatment, then follows recurrence for six months after treatment. It names no prespecified primary endpoint or significance threshold.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

During prophylaxis, infection occurred in 1/13 women on trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (0.15 infections per patient-year), 1/13 on nitrofurantoin (0.14), and 10/13 on placebo (2.8; P &lt; 0.001 for each randomized active arm versus placebo). The nonrandom trimethoprim group had 0/14 infections (0 per patient-year; P &lt; 0.001 versus placebo).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

By one month after prophylaxis stopped, the four groups no longer differed in cumulative infections. Over six months, infection occurred in 7/13 former trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole recipients (1.3 per patient-year), 8/10 trimethoprim recipients (1.7), 6/14 nitrofurantoin recipients (1.2), and 10/13 placebo recipients (3.0). Among women with at least three prior-year infections, 18/24 recurred after treatment, versus 3/13 with two prior infections (P = 0.004).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Study medication was taken once daily at bedtime for six months or until a urinary infection developed.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

After active-drug prophylaxis, *Escherichia coli* caused 12/21 infections, versus 38/45 before treatment (P = 0.02). During the six-month postprophylactic observation, no recurrent infection with trimethoprim-, trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole-, or nitrofurantoin-resistant *E. coli* was seen; trimethoprim resistance appeared in 1/316 rectal, urethral, or vaginal *E. coli* isolates during or after prophylaxis. A yeast infection occurred in 1/15 trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole recipients, 1/15 trimethoprim recipients stopped treatment for skin burning without rash, and 2/15 nitrofurantoin recipients reported minor gastrointestinal effects.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The study enrolled 60 women, including 15 nonrandom trimethoprim recipients, and tested six months of prophylaxis. On-treatment infection counts were reported for 13 or 14 women per arm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA/CUA/SUFU recurrent UTI guideline, amended 2025, permits antibiotic prophylaxis after discussion of risks, benefits, and alternatives to decrease future UTIs in women previously diagnosed with UTIs <Rec>(Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>. It cites this trial among the antibiotic comparisons.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
