# ALFAUR: Alfuzosin Before Trial Without Catheter After Acute Urinary Retention

<TrialBottomLine id="alfaur" />

**ALFAUR** was a double-blind randomized trial comparing alfuzosin 10 mg once daily with placebo before catheter removal in men with a first episode of spontaneous BPH-related acute urinary retention; alfuzosin improved successful trial without catheter within 24 hours of removal.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="alfaur" />

## Design

Emergency catheterization preceded 2:1 allocation to three days of alfuzosin or placebo. The initial primary endpoint was successful voiding within 24 hours after catheter removal. Men who passed the trial without catheter were blindly rerandomized to alfuzosin or placebo for six months, with BPH surgery assessed at one, three, and six months as the second phase's primary endpoint.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

Successful voiding occurred in 146/236 (61.9%) assigned alfuzosin and 58/121 (47.9%) assigned placebo, a 14.0 percentage-point difference (P = 0.012).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

After the second randomization, 14/82 (17.1%) assigned alfuzosin and 20/83 (24.1%) assigned placebo underwent BPH surgery within six months, a −7.0 percentage-point raw difference; the six-month Kaplan–Meier surgery-free survival difference was 8.3 percentage points (P = 0.20). The second phase therefore did not meet its six-month surgery endpoint. Most operations followed recurrent retention: 11/14 (78.6%) and 16/20 (80.0%) of patients who required surgery in the respective arms.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The intervention was medical treatment during catheter drainage before a planned voiding trial. Patients who voided successfully entered a separate blinded treatment allocation for continued medical management.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

The investigators described alfuzosin 10 mg once daily as well tolerated.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The reported voiding-trial denominators total 357, whereas 360 men were randomized. The six-month analysis included only the 165 men rerandomized after a successful voiding trial; its surgery result applies to that selected group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA 2026 LUTS/BPH guideline calls for a uroselective alpha blocker for at least three days before a voiding trial in patients with BPH-related acute urinary retention <Rec>(Clinical Principle)</Rec>. It also calls for counseling men who pass the trial about recurrent retention risk <Rec>(Clinical Principle)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
