# ALFAUR: Alfuzosin Before Trial Without Catheter After Acute Urinary Retention

<TrialBottomLine id="alfaur" />

**ALFAUR** was a double-blind randomized trial comparing alfuzosin 10 mg once daily with placebo before catheter removal in men with a first episode of spontaneous BPH-related acute urinary retention; alfuzosin improved successful trial without catheter within 24 hours of removal.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="alfaur" />

## Design

Emergency catheterization preceded 2:1 allocation of 363 men older than 50 years with 500–1500 mL drained to alfuzosin or placebo; 360 received treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The initial primary endpoint was satisfactory voiding within 24 hours after catheter removal without recatheterization; no postvoid-residual cutoff was specified.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Of 204 men who passed the trial without catheter, 169 were blindly rerandomized and 165 received alfuzosin (82) or placebo (83) for six months, with BPH surgery assessed at one, three, and six months as the second phase's primary endpoint.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

Successful voiding occurred in 146/236 (61.9%) assigned alfuzosin and 58/121 (47.9%) assigned placebo, a 14.0 percentage-point difference (P = 0.012). After adjustment for age and drained volume, the alfuzosin odds ratio was 1.979 (95% CI 1.226–3.217; P = 0.0055). Across arms, success was 49.0% in men aged 65 years or older versus 74.1% in younger men, and 47.8% with at least 1,000 mL drained versus 66.7% below 1,000 mL (both P &lt;0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

BPH surgery within six months occurred in 14/82 (17.1%) alfuzosin-treated and 20/83 (24.1%) placebo-treated men; the Kaplan–Meier surgery-free survival difference was 8.3 percentage points (95% CI −4.6 to 21.3; P = 0.20). At one month, surgery had occurred in 5/82 (6.1%) versus 13/83 (15.7%), with a surgery-free survival difference of 9.6 points (95% CI 0.2 to 19.0; P = 0.04); at three months, the counts were 8/82 (9.8%) versus 17/83 (20.5%) and the difference was 11.4 points (95% CI 0.3 to 22.4; P = 0.04). Emergency surgery after recurrent retention occurred in 11/82 (13.4%) versus 16/83 (19.3%), accounting for 11/14 (78.6%) and 16/20 (80.0%) of each arm's surgeries.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Lower urinary tract symptoms and bother at treatment end were lower with alfuzosin: last-assessed IPSS was 8.75 versus 11.45 (P = 0.012), and bother score was 1.66 versus 2.27 (P = 0.004).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Alfuzosin 10 mg once daily or placebo was given during urethral catheter drainage before the planned voiding trial; the catheter was removed after at least two doses, and one additional tablet was given the next day.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Mean catheterization lasted 53 ± 10 hours with alfuzosin and 52 ± 12 hours with placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Patients who voided successfully entered a separate blinded treatment allocation for continued medical management. Scheduled surgery criteria included peak flow &lt;5 mL/s, postvoid residual &gt;350 mL, IPSS &gt;25, recurrent infection, progressive uropathy, macroscopic hematuria, and patient request.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

During the initial phase, at least one adverse event occurred in 20/238 (8.4%) alfuzosin recipients and 16/122 (13.1%) placebo recipients. Orthostatic hypotension was reported in 6/238 (2.5%) alfuzosin recipients. Adverse-event withdrawals occurred in 4/238 (1.7%) and 1/122 (0.8%), respectively: two alfuzosin recipients withdrew for postural hypotension, one for catheter-related infection, and one for unrelated hemorrhoids; the placebo withdrawal was for catheter-related infection.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the six-month phase, serious adverse events were reported in 4.8% of the alfuzosin group and 4.9% of the placebo group; adverse-event withdrawals occurred in 1/82 (1.2%) and 4/83 (4.8%). No potentially vasodilatory adverse events were reported in either arm during this phase.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

Of 363 men randomized initially, 360 received treatment and 357 had voiding-trial data. Of 204 successful voiding trials, 169 men were rerandomized and 165 received second-phase treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA 2026 LUTS/BPH guideline calls for a uroselective alpha blocker for at least three days before a voiding trial in patients with BPH-related acute urinary retention <Rec>(Clinical Principle)</Rec>. It also calls for counseling men who pass the trial about recurrent retention risk <Rec>(Clinical Principle)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
