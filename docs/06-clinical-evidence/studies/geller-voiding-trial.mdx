# Geller Voiding Trial: Diagnostic Accuracy of Retrograde and Spontaneous Postoperative Voiding Trials

<TrialBottomLine id="geller-voiding-trial" />

Retrograde-fill testing had higher specificity for postoperative retention lasting more than 7 days in a randomized-order paired diagnostic study of 50 women after incontinence or prolapse surgery: 58.1% versus 25.8% with spontaneous testing. Sensitivities were 94.4% and 100%, respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="geller-voiding-trial" />

## Design

Women undergoing surgery for urinary incontinence, pelvic organ prolapse, or both between November 2009 and March 2010 received both postoperative voiding tests on postoperative day 1. Randomization determined test order, with 25 women assigned to each sequence. Diagnostic performance was assessed against retention lasting more than 7 days; duration follow-up was available for 49 of 50 women. Patients and staff performing the tests were not blinded.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The two test failure rates describe the same cohort after its two tests. A postvoid residual greater than 100 mL failed either method; spontaneous testing also counted a voided volume below 200 mL as a failure. Patient preference was assessed separately with a nonvalidated questionnaire.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

For retention lasting more than 7 days, retrograde testing had 94.4% sensitivity (17/18; 95% CI 70.6–99.7%) and 58.1% specificity (18/31; 95% CI 39.3–74.9%); spontaneous testing had 100% sensitivity (18/18; 95% CI 78.1–100%) and 25.8% specificity (8/31; 95% CI 12.5–44.9%). Test failure was associated with prolonged retention for each method (retrograde P&lt;.001; spontaneous P=.02).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Retrograde testing failed in 31/50 women (62%), versus 42/50 (84%) with spontaneous testing (P=.001). Its positive and negative predictive values were 56.7% (17/30; 95% CI 37.7–74.0%) and 94.7% (18/19; 95% CI 71.9–99.7%), versus 43.9% (18/41; 95% CI 28.8–60.1%) and 100% (8/8; 95% CI 59.8–100%) for spontaneous testing. Spontaneous failure resulted from high postvoid residual in 34/50 women (68%) and inadequate voided volume in 8/50 (16%); all retrograde failures resulted from high postvoid residual.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The reported preference was 51.1% for retrograde testing versus 44.4% for spontaneous testing, irrespective of assigned order (P=.40).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Every participant underwent both methods. Retrograde testing instilled 300 mL saline through a Foley catheter and required a void within 30 minutes; spontaneous testing allowed up to 4 hours of filling and required two consecutive passes. Women failing either method were discharged on intermittent self-catheterization until two consecutive postvoid residuals were below 100 mL, without prophylactic antibiotics.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Postoperative retention lasted 12.6 ± 14.4 days among women failing both tests, compared with 2.5 ± 2.1 days among those failing only one (P=.004). Urinary tract infection occurred in 16.0% of the retrograde-first group and 12.5% of the spontaneous-first group (P=.79). One postoperative transfusion occurred in the spontaneous-first group, and one postoperative fever occurred in each sequence group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The preference questionnaire was not validated, and its reported between-method difference was not statistically significant. Retrograde postvoid residual was estimated by subtracting voided volume from the 300 mL instilled rather than by catheterization.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
