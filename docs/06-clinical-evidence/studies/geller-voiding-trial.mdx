# Geller Voiding Trial: Diagnostic Accuracy of Retrograde and Spontaneous Postoperative Voiding Trials

<TrialBottomLine id="geller-voiding-trial" />

In a randomized-order paired diagnostic study of 50 women after incontinence or prolapse surgery, retrograde-fill and spontaneous voiding trials detected voiding dysfunction lasting at least 7 days with sensitivities of 94.4% and 100% and specificities of 58.1% and 25.8%, respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="geller-voiding-trial" />

## Design

Women undergoing surgery for urinary incontinence, pelvic organ prolapse, or both between November 2009 and March 2010 received both postoperative voiding tests. Randomization determined test order, with 25 women assigned to each sequence. Diagnostic performance was assessed against voiding dysfunction lasting at least 7 days.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The two reported test failure rates describe the same cohort after its two tests. Sensitivity, specificity, and predictive values describe whether each immediate test result identified the duration-based postoperative outcome. Patient preference was assessed separately from diagnostic performance and reported irrespective of the randomized sequence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Retrograde testing failed 62% of participants, versus 84% with spontaneous testing. Its positive and negative predictive values for prolonged dysfunction were 56.7% and 94.7%, versus 43.9% and 100% for spontaneous testing. The reported preference for retrograde testing was 51.1%, versus 44.4% for spontaneous testing, irrespective of assigned order.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Every participant underwent both test methods. The randomized comparison concerned which test came first; both methods were evaluated in the same postoperative cohort.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Women who failed both tests had a mean 12.6 ± 14.4 days of postoperative retention, compared with 2.5 ± 2.1 days for women who failed only one test (P = .004).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The paired study enrolled 50 women, with randomization to test order and diagnostic performance assessed against prolonged postoperative voiding dysfunction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
