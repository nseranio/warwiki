# OPEN: Open Urethroplasty versus Endoscopic Urethrotomy for Recurrent Bulbar Urethral Stricture

<TrialBottomLine id="open" />

**OPEN** was an open-label, patient-randomized superiority trial of open urethroplasty versus endoscopic urethrotomy in 222 men with recurrent bulbar urethral stricture at 38 UK NHS hospitals, with the 24-month voiding symptom score as its primary endpoint. Symptom scores did not differ (adjusted mean difference −0.36, 95% CI −1.74 to 1.02), while 15 men allocated to urethroplasty and 29 allocated to urethrotomy needed a reintervention (hazard ratio 0.52, 95% CI 0.31–0.89).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="open" />

## Design

Eligible men were adults with bulbar urethral stricture who had already had at least one surgical intervention for it. Current perineal sepsis and a urethrocutaneous fistula were exclusions. Men were identified at 53 NHS providers (38 randomized at least one man); 1,262 were identified and 222 were randomized between February 27, 2013 and December 23, 2015. Two men found ineligible after randomization were excluded, leaving 108 allocated to urethroplasty and 112 to urethrotomy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

At baseline, mean stricture length was 2.0 cm (SD 1.4) in the urethroplasty group and 1.7 cm (SD 1.1) in the urethrotomy group, but length was recorded in only 67 and 63 men. Men had had a mean of 1.9 and 1.8 previous interventions of any type, mostly urethrotomy (1.6 and 1.4); prior urethroplasty averaged 0.1 in each group. The last procedure was 12 months or more earlier in 67% and 68%, the cause was unknown in 70% and 72%, and 21% and 13% were currently using intermittent self-dilatation. Mean baseline maximum flow rate was 10.0 and 9.7 mL/s and mean baseline voiding score 13.5 and 13.2.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Allocation was 1:1 through a central automated system with minimization on recruiting site and time since the last procedure (under or over 12 months). Participants and surgeons knew the allocation; central trial staff were masked. The primary outcome was the area under the curve of the patient-reported voiding symptom score, which sums six items (delay before starting, poor stream strength, straining, intermittent stream, incomplete emptying, post-micturition dribbling) each scored 0 to 4, for a total of 0 to 24. Questionnaires were sent at baseline, before the operation, at 3, 6, 9, 12 and 24 months after it, and at 18 and 24 months after randomization. The primary analysis required a baseline score, an early score (up to 12 months after the operation) and a later score (18 or 24 months after randomization), and analyzed men as randomized.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The investigators set the minimum clinically important difference at more than 10% of the symptom profile, with 90% power. After recruitment proved slow, the required sample was revised to 170 men, and the target of 210 allowed for 19% attrition. The trial was also powered (104 men, 90% power) to detect a 30% relative reduction in reintervention. A reintervention was any procedure after the allocated one, excluding self-dilatation. Recurrence was a reintervention performed or scheduled, or deterioration of the maximum flow rate or voiding score to its pre-intervention value. Improvement in maximum flow rate was an increase of at least 10 mL/s.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The primary analysis included 69 of 108 men allocated to urethroplasty and 90 of 112 allocated to urethrotomy. The mean voiding score profile over 24 months was 7.4 (SD 3.8) after urethroplasty and 7.8 (SD 4.2) after urethrotomy; p = 0.6.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Multiple-imputation intention-to-treat analysis gave a difference of −0.33 (95% CI −1.74 to 1.09) and a modified intention-to-treat analysis −0.52 (95% CI −2.0 to 0.96).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Subgroups by time since last procedure, age, stricture length and number of previous interventions showed no heterogeneity. The full monograph reports mean scores falling from 13.4 to 6 with urethroplasty and from 13.2 to 6.4 with urethrotomy between baseline and 24 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Forty-four men had at least one reintervention, and there were 52 reinterventions overall. Among men who had one, the median interval from surgery was 474 days (IQR 399–577) after urethroplasty and 308 days (IQR 211–448) after urethrotomy. Counting multiple events per man gave a hazard ratio of 0.49 (95% CI 0.30–0.82; p = 0.006), and a per-protocol analysis of men who received the allocated procedure gave 0.28 (95% CI 0.15–0.55; p &lt; 0.001). Among men who had surgery (93 in the urethroplasty arm and 104 in the urethrotomy arm), recurrence occurred in 19 and 39, respectively (hazard ratio 0.46, 95% CI 0.29–0.72; p = 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

An improvement in maximum flow rate of at least 10 mL/s at 12 or 24 months was reported in 18 of 93 men (19%) in the urethroplasty arm and 13 of 104 (13%) in the urethrotomy arm; among the 44 and 63 men with flow-rate data, respectively, the adjusted odds ratio was 2.64 (95% CI 1.14–6.15; p = 0.024). At 3 months the odds ratio was 2.1 (95% CI 1.05–4.12).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Impact of symptoms on daily activity (difference 0.06, 95% CI −0.19 to 0.30) and satisfaction with sexual function (difference 0.35, 95% CI −0.06 to 0.75; p = 0.090) did not differ.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the base-case cost-effectiveness analysis of 46 men per group, urethroplasty cost £2,148 more per patient over 24 months (95% CI £689–£3,606) with a QALY difference of −0.01 (95% CI −0.17 to 0.14). In a 10-year Markov model the base case cost £8,026 and 7.61 QALYs for urethroplasty versus £6,553 and 7.60 QALYs for urethrotomy, an incremental cost of £301,073 per QALY.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Treatment received departed from allocation. In the urethroplasty group, 71 of 108 men (66%) had a urethroplasty, 22 (20%) had a urethrotomy or dilatation instead and 15 (14%) had no procedure. In the urethrotomy group, 93 of 112 (83%) had urethrotomy or dilatation, 11 (9.8%) had a urethroplasty and 8 (7.1%) had no procedure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Of the 71 urethroplasties in the urethroplasty group, 45 were dorsal graft, 9 anastomotic without transection, 9 anastomotic with transection, 7 ventral graft and 1 was a perineal urethrostomy. The 11 crossovers to urethroplasty comprised 4 dorsal graft, 4 anastomotic without transection, 2 ventral graft and 1 anastomotic with transection. Endoscopic treatment was optical cold-knife urethrotomy in 105 of the 115 men who received it (15 in the urethroplasty group, 90 in the urethrotomy group), hot-knife urethrotomy in 1 and dilatation in 9 (7 and 2).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Intermittent self-dilatation was started after allocation in 29 of 112 men (26%) in the urethrotomy group and 9 of 108 (8.3%) in the urethroplasty group; all 9 had crossed over to urethrotomy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Adverse events are reported by procedure received (82 men had urethroplasty and 115 urethrotomy). At least one adverse event occurred in 50 of 82 men (61.0%) after urethroplasty and 30 of 115 (26.1%) after urethrotomy. Perioperative events after urethroplasty were mouth pain in 12 (14.6%, graft harvest site), wound infection in 4 (4.9%) and urinary infection in 3 (3.7%); after urethrotomy mouth pain occurred in 2 (1.7%), with no wound or urinary infection. During follow-up, erectile dysfunction was recorded in 4 of 82 (4.9%) and 3 of 115 (2.6%), wound pain in 5 (6.1%) and 1 (0.9%), and urinary tract infection in 5 (6.1%) and 6 (5.2%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

At least one serious adverse event occurred in 7 of 82 men (8.5%) after urethroplasty and 10 of 115 (8.7%) after urethrotomy; two of the 22 serious events were related to the intervention.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Two urethrotomy patients were readmitted (four readmissions in total), and one urethrotomy patient died of deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism, reported as unrelated to the intervention. The monograph reports two postoperative complications requiring reintervention in the urethroplasty group and five in the urethrotomy group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The symptom analysis excluded 39 of 108 men allocated to urethroplasty and 22 of 112 allocated to urethrotomy, and the reasons for the unequal loss are unknown; the investigators suggest that more men received their allocated treatment in the urethrotomy group, or the shorter wait for urethrotomy. Of those excluded, 15 and 8 had no surgery at all. Sensitivity analyses with multiple imputation and pattern-mixture models were robust except under extreme assumptions. The 159 men analyzed were fewer than the 170 required after the sample-size revision.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Participants and surgeons were unblinded, 37 of 108 men allocated to urethroplasty (34%) and 19 of 112 allocated to urethrotomy (17%) received a different procedure or none, and stricture length was missing in 90 of 220 men.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Symptoms and costs were assessed over 24 months, whereas reinterventions were tracked to database closure, at least 24 and up to 48 months. The cost-effectiveness analysis did not include the loss of quality of life during recovery from later procedures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="open" />

ROBUST III, which the AUA guideline discusses together with OPEN, tested a paclitaxel-coated balloon with dilation or urethrotomy against dilation or urethrotomy alone, and used urethral patency and freedom from retreatment as endpoints. OPEN's experimental arm was urethroplasty and its primary endpoint was patient-reported symptoms.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

- **AUA, Urethral Stricture Disease guideline (published 2016, amended 2023).** Surgeons should offer urethroplasty instead of repeated endoscopic management for recurrent anterior urethral strictures after failed dilation or direct visual internal urethrotomy <Rec>(Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C)</Rec>. The supporting discussion cites OPEN's reintervention and flow-rate results and describes the trial as a pragmatic comparison whose symptom endpoints did not differ.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **EAU, Urethral Strictures limited update (2026).** The section on direct vision internal urethrotomy for recurrent strictures reviews OPEN. The guideline recommends not performing repetitive (more than two) direct vision internal urethrotomies or dilatations if urethroplasty is a viable option <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
