# BESIDE: Mirabegron Add-on Therapy After Inadequate Solifenacin Response

<TrialBottomLine id="beside" />

**BESIDE** was a multicenter, double-blind phase 3b randomized trial in 2,174 adults still incontinent after four weeks of solifenacin 5 mg that compared add-on mirabegron with continued solifenacin 5 mg or escalation to 10 mg; combination improved the primary daily incontinence endpoint over continued 5 mg after 12 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="beside" />

## Design

Adults had overactive bladder symptoms for at least three months and averaged at least two incontinence episodes per 24 hours at screening. After a two-week screening and washout period, all received single-blind solifenacin 5 mg daily for four weeks. Randomization required at least one incontinence episode in a three-day diary at the end of that run-in. The full analysis set included 707 combination, 705 solifenacin 5 mg, and 698 solifenacin 10 mg participants. Patients with clinically significant bladder outlet obstruction or stress-predominant mixed incontinence were excluded.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The primary endpoint was change from randomization to end of treatment in mean incontinence episodes per 24 hours, tested for superiority of combination over solifenacin 5 mg. Key secondary endpoints were change in daily micturitions and the number of incontinence episodes in a three-day diary. For the comparison with solifenacin 10 mg, these endpoints were tested first for noninferiority in the per-protocol population, then for superiority; the noninferiority margins were 0.20 micturitions per 24 hours and a rate ratio of 1.11 for three-day incontinence episodes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The primary adjusted change in daily incontinence episodes was −1.80 with combination versus −1.53 with solifenacin 5 mg (mean difference −0.26 episodes per 24 hours; P = 0.001). At the 12-week endpoint, combination reduced daily micturitions by an adjusted 0.45 more than solifenacin 5 mg (P &lt; 0.001). It was noninferior to solifenacin 10 mg on both key secondary endpoints and superior to 10 mg on daily micturitions; superiority over 10 mg was not demonstrated for three-day incontinence episodes. Combination also reduced urgency incontinence by an adjusted −1.82 episodes per 24 hours versus −1.54 with 5 mg and −1.63 with 10 mg; the mean difference against 5 mg was −0.27 (95% CI −0.47 to −0.07; P = 0.003).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Complete continence at end of treatment occurred in 325/706 (46.0%) with combination, 267/704 (37.9%) with solifenacin 5 mg, and 280/697 (40.2%) with solifenacin 10 mg. The odds ratio for combination versus 5 mg was 1.47 (95% CI 1.17–1.84; P = 0.001). In an exploratory composite responder analysis, at least 50% fewer daily incontinence episodes plus at least a 10-point improvement in OAB-q symptom bother occurred in 432/694 (62.2%), 342/683 (50.1%), and 382/676 (56.5%), respectively; the odds ratio versus 5 mg was 1.66 (95% CI 1.33–2.07; P &lt; 0.001). The adjusted improvement in total OAB-q health-related quality of life exceeded 5 mg by 3.15 points (95% CI 1.35–4.95; P = 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

A prespecified age analysis reported symptom improvement in each age cohort, with the largest numerical reductions under combination treatment.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

All randomized treatment was oral and once daily. Combination treatment paired solifenacin 5 mg with mirabegron 25 mg for the first four weeks, then mirabegron 50 mg for the remaining eight weeks; the comparison groups continued solifenacin 5 mg or received solifenacin 10 mg. The randomized counts were 727, 728, and 719, and the corresponding safety populations were 725, 728, and 719.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Any treatment-emergent adverse event occurred in 260/725 (35.9%) with combination, 241/728 (33.1%) with solifenacin 5 mg, and 283/719 (39.4%) with solifenacin 10 mg. Dry mouth occurred in 43/725 (5.9%), 41/728 (5.6%), and 68/719 (9.5%); constipation in 33/725 (4.6%), 22/728 (3.0%), and 34/719 (4.7%). Serious treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 13/725 (1.8%), 10/728 (1.4%), and 15/719 (2.1%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Urinary retention was reported in 2/725 (0.3%), 1/728 (0.1%), and 5/719 (0.7%); none required catheterization. The cardiovascular safety analysis reported hypertension adverse events in 8/725 (1.1%), 5/728 (0.7%), and 6/719 (0.8%), and ECG QT prolongation in one patient (0.1%) per group. Investigators reported no treatment-related serious cardiovascular event.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

The four-week solifenacin run-in selected patients who remained incontinent yet tolerated the drug. Randomized treatment lasted 12 weeks, and there was no mirabegron-only or placebo arm. Astellas funded the trial and participated in its design, analysis, and manuscript preparation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="beside" />

BESIDE enrolled patients with persistent incontinence after a solifenacin run-in and compared add-on mirabegron with continued or escalated solifenacin.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The **AUA/SUFU 2024** idiopathic OAB guideline says clinicians may offer a drug from a different class in combination when a single OAB medication gives inadequate improvement <Rec>(Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>. Its discussion cites BESIDE among the combination trials.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

The **EAU 2026** guideline on non-neurogenic female lower urinary tract symptoms says to offer mirabegron as additional therapy when solifenacin 5 mg is inadequate <Rec>(Weak)</Rec>.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
