# Duloxetine North American SUI: Duloxetine Versus Placebo for Stress Urinary Incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="duloxetine-sui" />

**Duloxetine North American SUI** was a double-blind phase 3 randomized trial in women with predominant stress urinary incontinence that compared duloxetine 40 mg twice daily with placebo; duloxetine reduced weekly leakage episodes and improved incontinence-related quality of life during 12 weeks of treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="duloxetine-sui" />

## Design

The 683 participants were 22–84 years old and had at least seven incontinence episodes weekly, a positive cough stress test and stress pad test, and bladder capacity of at least 400 mL. Predominant urgency incontinence, abnormal diurnal or nocturnal frequency, and smaller bladder capacity were excluded. A two-week placebo lead-in preceded randomization to duloxetine (344 women) or placebo (339 women). The stated primary outcomes were percent change in weekly incontinence episode frequency (IEF) and the Incontinence Quality of Life questionnaire score. IEF analysis was stratified by baseline weekly frequency below 14 versus 14 or more episodes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Weekly IEF decreased by 50% with duloxetine and 27% with placebo (P &lt; 0.001). Incontinence-related quality-of-life scores improved by 11.0 and 6.8 points, respectively (P &lt; 0.001). Both stated primary comparisons favored duloxetine. A 50%–100% IEF reduction occurred in 51% versus 34% of women (P &lt; 0.001); the voiding interval increased by 20 versus 2 minutes (P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Adverse events led to discontinuation in 24% of the duloxetine arm and 4% of the placebo arm (P &lt; 0.001). Nausea was the most common adverse effect and accounted for duloxetine discontinuation in 6.4%. Of 78 duloxetine recipients with treatment-emergent nausea, 58 (74%) completed the trial.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The placebo comparison lasted 12 weeks and measured symptom frequency and quality of life during treatment. It did not compare duloxetine with an active conservative treatment, and the enrolled population excluded predominant urgency incontinence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU 2026 guideline offers duloxetine where licensed to selected women with stress urinary incontinence unresponsive to other conservative management who want to avoid invasive treatment, with careful counseling about adverse events <Rec>(Weak)</Rec>. It recommends dose titration at initiation and withdrawal because of adverse-event risk <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
