# Duloxetine North American SUI: Duloxetine Versus Placebo for Stress Urinary Incontinence

<TrialBottomLine id="duloxetine-sui" />

**Duloxetine North American SUI** was a double-blind phase 3 randomized trial in women with predominant stress urinary incontinence that compared duloxetine 40 mg twice daily with placebo; duloxetine reduced weekly leakage episodes and improved incontinence-related quality of life during 12 weeks of treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="duloxetine-sui" />

## Design

The 683 participants were 22–84 years old and had at least seven stress incontinence episodes weekly, a positive cough stress test and stress pad test, and tolerance of retrograde bladder filling to 400 mL. Predominant urgency incontinence, daytime voiding frequency of eight or more, and nocturnal frequency of three or more were excluded. A two-week no-drug lead-in and two-week placebo lead-in preceded randomization to duloxetine (344 women) or placebo (339 women). The prespecified primary outcomes were percent change in weekly incontinence episode frequency (IEF) and the Incontinence Quality of Life (I-QOL) total score. IEF analysis was stratified by baseline weekly frequency below 14 versus 14 or more episodes. Intent-to-treat analyses required at least one post-randomization measure and carried the last observation forward after early discontinuation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Both primary outcomes favored duloxetine in the intent-to-treat analyses: median weekly IEF decreased by 50.0% versus 27.5% with placebo, and mean I-QOL total score improved by 11.1 versus 6.8 points (P &lt; 0.001 for each). At least one post-randomization diary was available for 83% of 344 duloxetine recipients and 95% of 339 placebo recipients; the corresponding I-QOL proportions were 97% and 98%. The median absolute IEF reduction was seven versus three episodes weekly (P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Other symptom measures also favored duloxetine: a 50%–100% IEF reduction occurred in 51.4% versus 33.5% of women (P &lt; 0.001), and 10.5% versus 5.9% had no episodes at the last diary (P &lt; 0.05). The mean voiding interval increased by 20.0 versus 1.7 minutes, and 62.0% versus 39.6% rated their condition improved on PGI-I (P &lt; 0.001 for each).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Treatment delivery

Women received duloxetine 40 mg twice daily or matching placebo for 12 weeks, with visits every four weeks. Study completion was 69% of 344 randomized women with duloxetine and 87% of 339 with placebo; mean doses ingested were 80% versus 93% (P &lt; 0.001 for both comparisons).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Adverse events led to discontinuation in 24.1% of 344 randomized women receiving duloxetine and 4.1% of 339 receiving placebo (P &lt; 0.001); 89% of adverse-event discontinuations occurred within four weeks. Any treatment-emergent adverse event occurred in 74% versus 50% (P &lt; 0.001). Nausea occurred in 78 duloxetine recipients (22.7% of 344) versus 2.1% of 339 placebo recipients and caused discontinuation in 6.4% versus none. Of the 78 duloxetine recipients with nausea, 58 (74%) completed the trial. Fatigue occurred in 14.8% versus 3.8%, insomnia in 14.2% versus 2.4%, and dry mouth in 12.2% versus 0.9% (P &lt; 0.001 for each).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The placebo comparison lasted 12 weeks and measured symptom frequency and quality of life during treatment. It did not compare duloxetine with an active conservative treatment, and the enrolled population excluded predominant urgency incontinence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU 2026 guideline offers duloxetine where licensed to selected women with stress urinary incontinence unresponsive to other conservative management who want to avoid invasive treatment, with careful counseling about adverse events <Rec>(Weak)</Rec>. It recommends dose titration at initiation and withdrawal because of adverse-event risk <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
