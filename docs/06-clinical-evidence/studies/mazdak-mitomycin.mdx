# Mazdak mitomycin C trial: Submucosal Injection before Internal Urethrotomy

<TrialBottomLine id="mazdak-mitomycin" />

In 40 men with previously untreated trauma-related bulbar strictures, this prospective randomized trial found fewer recurrences after submucosal mitomycin C injection before urethrotomy than after urethrotomy alone during a mean 15 months of follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="mazdak-mitomycin" />

## Design

Twenty men were assigned to each group. All had a history of straddle injury or other blunt perineal trauma; men with strictures &gt;1.5 cm, posterior strictures or previous treatment were excluded. Recurrence was assessed by retrograde urethrography every six months or when obstructive symptoms developed. The report compares recurrence by chi-square test without naming a primary endpoint or prespecified significance threshold. Blinding is not reported.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

During follow-up averaging 15 months (range 6–24), recurrence occurred in 2/20 men (10%) after mitomycin C and 10/20 (50%) without mitomycin C (p = 0.006); all 40 men had follow-up data. The authors describe the difference as statistically significant.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Before urethrotomy, the intervention group received 0.1 mg mitomycin C in 2 mL distilled water by submucosal injection at 1, 5, 7 and 11 o'clock with a 22-gauge cystoscopic needle; the control group received no injection. One surgeon then performed a 12 o'clock cold-knife incision under general anesthesia with a 20F Wolf urethrotome. An 18F or 20F catheter remained for five days in both groups.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

The report gives no adverse-event or injection-site complication counts.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The comparison included 40 men and follow-up ranged from 6 to 24 months. Recurrence surveillance used retrograde urethrography rather than urethroscopy, and blinding was not reported.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA urethral stricture guideline (amended 2023) allows DVIU as an initial treatment for a short bulbar stricture &lt;2 cm <Rec>(Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C)</Rec>. Its discussion cites Mazdak for the recurrence finding and notes limited long-term follow-up for mitomycin C injection.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The EAU 2026 urethral stricture guideline advises that intralesional injections be used only within a clinical trial <Rec>(Weak)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
