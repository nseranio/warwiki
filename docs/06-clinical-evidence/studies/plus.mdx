# PLUS: Mirabegron Add-on to Tamsulosin in Men With Overactive Bladder Symptoms

<TrialBottomLine id="plus" />

**PLUS** was a multicenter, double-blind phase 4 randomized trial in men with persistent overactive bladder symptoms and LUTS attributed to BPH that found mirabegron added to tamsulosin reduced daily micturitions more than placebo added to tamsulosin at 12 weeks (adjusted difference −0.39 per day, 95% CI −0.76 to −0.02).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="plus" />

## Design

Men entered a 4-week run-in with tamsulosin 0.4 mg before double-blind add-on treatment. Mirabegron started at 25 mg and increased to 50 mg after 4 weeks; the control group received matched placebo. The primary endpoint was change from baseline in mean micturitions per day at the end of 12 weeks; the efficacy analysis included 676 men.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Mean daily micturitions changed by −2.00/day with mirabegron and −1.62/day with placebo; the adjusted between-arm mean difference met the superiority endpoint. Mean voided volume, urgency episodes per day, and total urgency and frequency score also favored mirabegron. Total International Prostate Symptom Score did not differ significantly.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

The later PLUS safety analysis included 352 mirabegron-treated and 354 placebo-treated men. Overall treatment-emergent adverse events were more frequent with placebo, while drug-related events were more frequent with mirabegron. Drug-related serious events occurred in 2/352 (0.6%) and 1/354 (0.3%), respectively. Two mirabegron-treated men required catheterization for urinary retention; neither discontinued treatment. Mean postvoid residual and maximum flow changes were not considered clinically meaningful by the investigators.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

Treatment and safety were assessed over 12 weeks; longer-term symptom persistence and retention were not measured.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The **AUA BPH guideline (2026)** says clinicians may offer a β3 agonist alone or with an α-blocker for moderate-to-severe predominant storage LUTS <Rec>(Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> The **EAU male LUTS guideline (2026)** recommends adding a β3 agonist for persistent storage LUTS after α1-blocker monotherapy <Rec>(Weak)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
