# PLESS: Finasteride for Symptomatic Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia

<TrialBottomLine id="pless" />

**PLESS** randomized 3,040 symptomatic men with enlarged prostates to finasteride or placebo; finasteride improved symptoms and reduced retention and surgery over four years.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="pless" />

## Design

Of 3,016 evaluable men, 2,760 had complete event data. Symptom score was primary; surgery and retention were adjudicated secondary endpoints. MRI covered 312 men.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Results

Prespecified symptom-score analysis including discontinuations showed decreases of 2.6 versus 1.0 points (difference 1.6, 95% CI 0.7–2.5; P &lt; 0.001); completers: 3.3 versus 1.3. Four-year surgery or retention: 100/1,513 (7%) versus 199/1,503 (13%; reduction 51%, 95% CI 38–61%); surgery: 69/1,513 (5%) versus 152/1,503 (10%; reduction 55%, 95% CI 41–65%); retention: 42/1,513 (3%) versus 99/1,503 (7%; reduction 57%, 95% CI 40–69%). Completer flow gain was 1.9 versus 0.2 mL/s (difference 1.7, 95% CI 1.3–2.1); MRI-subset volume changed −18% versus +14% (difference 32 percentage points, 95% CI 28–36%; both P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Baseline PSA and prostate volume predicted spontaneous retention in the placebo arm of a secondary PLESS analysis: areas under the curve were 0.70 and 0.81, respectively, versus 0.53 and 0.67 in the finasteride arm (between-arm P = 0.012 for PSA; P = 0.665 for volume). Prostate volume was measured in a 312-man MRI subset.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Across the lowest (0.2–1.3 ng/mL) and highest (3.3–12.0 ng/mL) baseline PSA tertiles, four-year retention-or-surgery incidence was 7.8% versus 19.9% with placebo and 4.4% versus 8.3% with finasteride; the finasteride-to-placebo relative risks were 0.57 (95% CI 0.35–0.95; P = 0.030) and 0.40 (95% CI 0.29–0.56; P &lt; 0.001), respectively.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In the prostate-volume subset, placebo incidence rose from 8.9% to 22.0% across the lowest and highest tertiles, while finasteride incidence ranged from 5.1% to 5.6%.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Another analysis separated spontaneous from precipitated retention; about half of the retention episodes were precipitated by another factor. Subsequent BPH surgery followed spontaneous retention in 8/20 finasteride recipients (40%) and 39/52 placebo recipients (75%; P = 0.01), and precipitated retention in 14% and 26%, respectively.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

During the two-year open-label finasteride extension, annual acute-retention-or-BPH-surgery incidence was 0.49% (6/1,231 at risk) in year five and 1.23% (13/1,056) in year six with continuous finasteride, versus 0.97% (11/1,137) and 1.16% (11/952) after switching from placebo. Any acute retention occurred in 1/1,275 (0.08%) and 6/1,090 (0.55%) continuous finasteride users versus 6/1,217 (0.49%) and 5/1,021 (0.49%) switchers in years five and six. BPH surgery occurred in 5/1,285 (reported annual incidence 0.40%) and 11/1,079 (1.02%) versus 8/1,179 (0.68%) and 8/987 (0.81%), respectively. No placebo control remained after year four.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Finasteride was 5 mg daily; 524/1,524 (34%) discontinued versus 633/1,516 (42%) on placebo, and complete four-year surgery or acute-retention outcome data, including after treatment discontinuation, were available for 92% of men in each group (2,760/3,016 evaluable men overall).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Of 1,006 original finasteride and 891 placebo recipients completing four years, 908 and 785 entered the two-year open-label finasteride 5 mg daily extension; complete six-year outcomes were available for 2,463/3,016 originally evaluable men, including 770 who discontinued treatment during years one to six.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Extension event rates used life-table analysis, and safety assessments were restricted to serious drug-related adverse events.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Adverse events

Sexual adverse events were more frequent with finasteride during year one of PLESS: 15% of recipients versus 7% with placebo spontaneously reported an investigator-attributed event (P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> The primary report found more decreased libido (6.4% versus 3.4%) and impotence (8.1% versus 3.7%) in year one.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Over four years, at least one such event occurred in 22% versus 14%; severe events occurred in 3.6% versus 2.4%.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Sexual adverse events led to discontinuation in 57/1,524 (4%) and 32/1,516 (2%), respectively.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

New investigator-attributed sexual adverse events during years two to four occurred in 7% of each arm among men followed for at least one year without a first-year event. Among men reporting a sexual adverse event, it resolved during continued therapy in 12% with finasteride and 19% with placebo; among those who stopped treatment with an event present, it had resolved at six-month follow-up in 50% and 41%, respectively. Sexual dysfunction history was reported at screening by 695/1,524 (46%) and 700/1,516 (46%); treatment-emergent events were collected through spontaneous reports at four-month visits.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

Drug-related sexual adverse events during the two-year extension occurred in 27/908 (3%) continuous finasteride users and 61/785 (8%) men who switched from placebo. Impotence was reported in 14/908 (1.5%) and 33/785 (4.2%), and decreased libido in 6/908 (0.7%) and 24/785 (3.1%), respectively. Serious adverse experiences did not differ significantly between cohorts.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Limitations

Prostate-volume analyses used a subset. Sexual adverse events were collected through spontaneous reports. The extension was open label, and complete six-year outcome data were available for 2,463/3,016 originally evaluable participants.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The **EAU 2026 male LUTS guideline** cites PLESS in its 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor evidence review and recommends 5-ARIs for men with moderate-to-severe LUTS and increased progression risk, such as prostate volume &gt;40 mL <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> The **AUA 2026 BPH guideline** says clinicians may offer a 5-ARI to patients with LUTS/BPH to reduce clinical progression when estimated prostate volume is &gt;30 cc and/or PSA &gt;1.5 ng/mL <Rec>(Clinical Principle)</Rec>.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
