# PLESS: Finasteride for Symptomatic Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia

<TrialBottomLine id="pless" />

**PLESS** was a double-blind randomized trial of finasteride 5 mg daily versus placebo in 3,040 men with moderate-to-severe urinary symptoms and enlarged prostates; finasteride improved symptom scores and reduced acute urinary retention and BPH surgery over four years.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="pless" />

## Design

Symptoms, urinary flow, and clinical events were assessed every four months in 3,016 evaluable men; complete event data were available for 2,760. The symptom-score change was reported for men who completed treatment, whereas event counts used the evaluable groups. Prostate volume was measured in a subset. A later analysis found that an endpoint committee blinded to allocation classified retention and surgery events.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Results

Among completers, mean symptom-score decrease was 3.3 points with finasteride versus 1.3 with placebo on a 1–34 scale (P &lt; 0.001). BPH surgery occurred in 69/1,513 (5%) versus 152/1,503 (10%), a reported relative risk reduction of 55% (95% CI 41–65%). Acute urinary retention occurred in 42/1,513 (3%) versus 99/1,503 (7%), a reported relative risk reduction of 57% (95% CI 40–69%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Baseline PSA and prostate volume predicted spontaneous retention in the placebo arm of a secondary PLESS analysis: areas under the curve were 0.70 and 0.81, respectively, versus 0.53 and 0.67 in the finasteride arm (between-arm P = 0.012 for PSA; P = 0.665 for volume). Prostate volume was measured in a 312-man MRI subset.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Across the lowest (0.2–1.3 ng/mL) and highest (3.3–12.0 ng/mL) baseline PSA tertiles, four-year retention-or-surgery incidence was 7.8% versus 19.9% with placebo and 4.4% versus 8.3% with finasteride; the finasteride-to-placebo relative risks were 0.57 (95% CI 0.35–0.95; P = 0.030) and 0.40 (95% CI 0.29–0.56; P &lt; 0.001), respectively.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In the prostate-volume subset, placebo incidence rose from 8.9% to 22.0% across the lowest and highest tertiles, while finasteride incidence ranged from 5.1% to 5.6%.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Another analysis separated spontaneous from precipitated retention: subsequent BPH surgery followed spontaneous retention in 8/20 finasteride recipients (40%) and 39/52 placebo recipients (75%).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The six-year report added a two-year open-label finasteride extension. Lower retention-or-surgery incidence persisted among continuous finasteride users; men switched from placebo to finasteride had a similar extension incidence. No placebo control remained after year four.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Finasteride was assigned at 5 mg orally each day.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In the extension, 908 original finasteride recipients and 785 original placebo recipients took open-label finasteride; complete six-year outcomes were available for 2,463/3,016 originally evaluable men.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Adverse events

Sexual adverse events were more frequent with finasteride during year one of PLESS: 15% of recipients versus 7% with placebo spontaneously reported an investigator-attributed event (P &lt; 0.001). Over four years, at least one such event occurred in 22% versus 14%; severe events occurred in 3.6% versus 2.4%. Sexual adverse events led to discontinuation in 57/1,524 (4%) and 32/1,516 (2%), respectively.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

New investigator-attributed sexual adverse events during years two to four occurred in 7% of each arm among men followed for at least one year without a first-year event. Among men reporting a sexual adverse event, it resolved during continued therapy in 12% with finasteride and 19% with placebo; among those who stopped treatment with an event present, it had resolved at six-month follow-up in 50% and 41%, respectively. Sexual dysfunction history was reported at screening by 695/1,524 (46%) and 700/1,516 (46%); treatment-emergent events were collected through spontaneous reports at four-month visits.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Limitations

Symptom-score results were reported among completers. Prostate-volume analyses used a subset. Sexual adverse events were collected through spontaneous reports. The extension was open label, and complete six-year outcome data were available for 2,463/3,016 originally evaluable participants.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The **EAU 2026 male LUTS guideline** cites PLESS in its 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor evidence review and recommends 5-ARIs for men with moderate-to-severe LUTS and increased progression risk, such as prostate volume &gt;40 mL <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> The **AUA 2026 BPH guideline** says clinicians may offer a 5-ARI to patients with LUTS/BPH to reduce clinical progression when estimated prostate volume is &gt;30 cc and/or PSA &gt;1.5 ng/mL <Rec>(Clinical Principle)</Rec>.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
