# Withagen recurrent prolapse trial: Trocar-Guided Mesh Versus Conventional Vaginal Repair

<TrialBottomLine id="withagen-recurrent" />

The **Withagen recurrent prolapse trial** was a multicenter, open-label randomized comparison of trocar-guided polypropylene mesh with conventional vaginal native-tissue repair in women undergoing surgery for recurrent stage II or higher prolapse; mesh reduced treated-compartment anatomic failure at 12 months, while patient-reported improvement was similar.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="withagen-recurrent" />

## Design

Surgeons at 13 Dutch centers enrolled women with recurrent anterior or posterior prolapse. Randomization was by center. The primary endpoint was POP-Q stage II or higher, or repeat surgery, in a compartment treated at the index operation. An unblinded surgeon or colleague performed the anatomic examination. The prespecified superiority threshold was two-sided P &lt; 0.05. Patients and surgeons were also unblinded.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Twenty-two gynecologists experienced in pelvic-floor reconstruction performed the operations after Prolift training. Conventional repair was left to surgeon choice within technique guidance; allowable apical operations included hysterectomy with suspension, sacrospinous fixation, and modified Manchester-Fothergill. Adjunct mesh was prohibited in that arm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The 2012 secondary analysis included 121 women with complete 12-month POP-Q data after anterior or posterior repair, excluding combined repairs. De novo prolapse meant stage II or higher in an untreated compartment below stage II at baseline; a power calculation specified two-sided P &lt; 0.05.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

At 12 months, treated-compartment failure occurred after mesh in 8/83 women (9.6%) and after conventional repair in 38/84 (45.2%); the reported odds ratio for conventional repair versus mesh was 7.7 (95% CI 3.3–18; P &lt; 0.001), meeting the trial's superiority threshold. Patient-reported global improvement occurred in 63/78 (81%) and 64/80 (80%), respectively. Under an alternative definition encompassing any compartment or repeat prolapse surgery, failure occurred in 41/83 (49%) and 56/84 (66%; P = 0.03).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

De novo stage II or higher prolapse in an untreated compartment occurred in 29/62 mesh patients (47%) versus 10/59 controls (17%; OR 4.3, 95% CI 1.9–10.0; P &lt; 0.001), meeting the 2012 analysis's stated threshold. At or beyond the hymen, rates were 21/62 (34%) versus 3/59 (5%; OR 9.5, 95% CI 2.5–43.5; P &lt; 0.001); beyond the hymen, 13/62 (21%) versus 1/59 (2%; OR 15.4, 95% CI 1.9–122; P &lt; 0.001). An exploratory anterior-Prolift subgroup had de novo prolapse in 14/26 (54%) after isolated repair versus 0/6 (0%) with apical repair (P = 0.02).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

At seven years, composite success—absence of prolapse beyond the hymen, bulge symptoms, and POP retreatment—was 31/58 (53%) after mesh and 37/69 (54%) after native-tissue repair (risk difference −1 percentage point, 95% CI −18 to 17). Treated-compartment failure was 3/58 (5%) versus 18/69 (26%); untreated-compartment failure was 17/58 (29%) versus 5/69 (7%). Repeat POP surgery occurred in 14/56 (25%) versus 11/69 (16%; risk difference 9 points, 95% CI −5 to 23).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

At twelve years, mean Patient Global Impression of Improvement score was 2.5 ± 1.5 after mesh and 2.6 ± 1.2 after native-tissue repair (P = 0.19). Much or very much improvement was reported by 30/42 (71%) and 23/39 (59%; P = 0.24).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The mesh arm used anterior, posterior, or total Prolift, placed through a full-thickness midline vaginal incision with trocar-guided arms. The vaginal incision was closed without tissue excision; simultaneous hysterectomy and T incisions were prohibited. Native-tissue anterior and posterior repairs used midline fibromuscular plication with delayed absorbable suture. Additional apical native-tissue procedures were permitted in either arm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Of 194 randomized women, 93/95 assigned to mesh and 97/99 assigned to conventional repair underwent surgery. Four conventional-repair patients and no mesh patients underwent another operation for symptomatic recurrence in a treated compartment by 12 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the secondary-analysis cohort, mesh repair was anterior in 32/62 (52%) and posterior in 30/62 (48%); conventional colporrhaphy was anterior in 26/59 (44%) and posterior in 33/59 (56%). Median operating time was 50 versus 40 minutes, respectively (ranges 29–125 versus 16–90; P = 0.002).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Mesh exposure was found in 14/83 examined women (16.9%) by 12 months; nine were asymptomatic. Five underwent excision and mucosal closure after local estrogen. Temporary urinary retention was more frequent after mesh, with normal micturition restored within 14 days in affected patients. Reported lower abdominal or genital pain was 8/79 (10%) after mesh and 10/85 (11.7%) after conventional repair.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the secondary-analysis cohort, temporary retention occurred in 6/62 mesh patients (10%) versus 3/59 controls (5%; P = 0.49); 12-month mesh exposure was 8/62 (13%). Untreated-compartment reoperation within one year occurred in 6/62 (10%) versus 1/59 (2%; P = 0.06).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

At seven years, implanted-material exposure was recorded in 22/53 mesh patients (42%) and 4/67 native-tissue patients (6%); the latter exposures involved other material, including subsequent mesh or fixation suture. Exposure reoperation occurred in 7/53 (13%) and 0/68 (0%). Questionnaire pain was reported by 26/66 (39%) and 36/72 (50%).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

At twelve years, 18/45 mesh respondents (40%) and 8/43 native-tissue respondents (19%) reported an additional operation for POP, stress urinary incontinence, or exposure (P = 0.03). Among the 45 mesh-group respondents at 12 years, 18 (40%) had a mesh exposure since index surgery; one suture exposure was reported among 43 native-tissue respondents (2%).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Limitations

The primary anatomic examination was unblinded, and conventional operations varied by surgeon. Its treated-compartment endpoint excluded new prolapse elsewhere. The 2012 secondary analysis also used unblinded examinations; 13 eligible women lacked 12-month POP-Q data.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Seven-year composite success was calculable in 127 operated women; twelve-year questionnaires were completed by 45 mesh and 43 native-tissue participants. Later estimates therefore reflect smaller follow-up groups.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="withagen-recurrent" />

The FDA ordered manufacturers to stop selling transvaginal mesh devices for pelvic organ prolapse repair in the United States in 2019; the devices were withdrawn from that market.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
