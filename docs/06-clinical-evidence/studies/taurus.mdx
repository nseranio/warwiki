# TAURUS: Twelve-Month Safety and Efficacy of Mirabegron in Overactive Bladder

<TrialBottomLine id="taurus" />

**TAURUS** was a multicenter, double-blind, active-controlled randomized safety trial in adults with overactive bladder that compared mirabegron 50 mg and 100 mg with tolterodine ER 4 mg for 12 months; treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 59.7%, 61.3%, and 62.6% of treated patients, respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="taurus" />

## Design

Eligibility required at least 3 months of overactive bladder symptoms, at least eight micturitions per day, and at least three urgency episodes in a 3-day diary. A 2-week single-blind placebo run-in preceded assignment. The registry records 2,452 randomized participants; the published safety analysis included 812, 820, and 812 patients who received the three study drugs.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The primary variable was the incidence and severity of treatment-emergent adverse events. Secondary variables were changes in key overactive bladder symptoms during treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Most treatment-emergent adverse events were mild or moderate. Morning systolic blood pressure changed by an adjusted mean of +0.2, +0.4, and −0.5 mm Hg from baseline to final visit, respectively. Each group showed symptom improvement at the first measured visit at 4 weeks, maintained through 12 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Serious treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 5.2%, 6.2%, and 5.4% of treated patients. Dry mouth occurred in 2.8%, 2.3%, and 8.6%, respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The trial had no placebo arm. Its primary variable was safety, while symptom changes were secondary.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA/SUFU overactive bladder guideline (2024) recommends offering antimuscarinic medication or a β3 agonist to improve urgency, frequency, and/or urgency urinary incontinence <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade A)</Rec>. It also recommends counseling patients on oral medication adverse effects when choosing treatment <Rec>(Clinical Principle)</Rec>; its discussion cites this trial among studies of quality-of-life outcomes.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
