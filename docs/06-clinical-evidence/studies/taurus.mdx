# TAURUS: Twelve-Month Safety and Efficacy of Mirabegron in Overactive Bladder

<TrialBottomLine id="taurus" />

**TAURUS** was a multicenter, double-blind, active-controlled randomized safety trial in adults with overactive bladder that compared mirabegron 50 mg and 100 mg with tolterodine ER 4 mg for 12 months; treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 485/812 (59.7%), 503/820 (61.3%), and 508/812 (62.6%) treated patients, respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="taurus" />

## Design

Eligibility required at least 3 months of overactive bladder symptoms, at least eight micturitions per day, and at least three urgency episodes in a 3-day diary. A 2-week single-blind placebo run-in preceded assignment. The registry records 2,452 randomized participants; the published safety analysis included 812, 820, and 812 patients who received the three study drugs.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Drug compliance was checked by pill counts at each visit, with 80–120% of prescribed doses defining compliance.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The primary variable was the incidence and severity of treatment-emergent adverse events in patients who received at least one dose; the paper reports no comparative safety hypothesis test. Secondary symptom analyses used 789, 802, and 791 patients with baseline and postbaseline diary measurements, and the incontinence analysis included 479, 483, and 488 of these patients with at least one baseline incontinence episode. Final-visit analyses carried the last observation forward for patients who withdrew early.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Secondary symptom measures improved numerically from month 1 through month 12 in each group. At the final visit, adjusted mean daily micturitions changed by −1.27, −1.41, and −1.39; daily incontinence episodes changed by −1.01, −1.24, and −1.26 in the baseline-incontinent subset. The study made no formal between-group efficacy comparisons.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Morning systolic blood pressure changed by an adjusted mean of +0.2 mm Hg (95% CI −0.4 to +0.9), +0.4 mm Hg (−0.2 to +1.1), and −0.5 mm Hg (−1.1 to +0.2) from baseline to final visit, respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Most treatment-emergent adverse events were mild or moderate; serious events occurred in 5.2% of 812, 6.2% of 820, and 5.4% of 812 treated patients. Dry mouth occurred in 23/812 (2.8%), 19/820 (2.3%), and 70/812 (8.6%); hypertension occurred in 75/812 (9.2%), 80/820 (9.8%), and 78/812 (9.6%); urinary tract infection occurred in 48/812 (5.9%), 45/820 (5.5%), and 52/812 (6.4%). Urinary retention occurred in 1/812 (0.1%), 1/820 (0.1%), and 3/812 (0.4%); acute retention requiring catheterization affected one patient each in the mirabegron 100 mg and tolterodine groups. Adverse events led to discontinuation in 52/815 (6.4%), 49/824 (5.9%), and 49/813 (6.0%) randomized patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The trial had no placebo arm. Its primary variable was safety, while symptom changes were secondary; 81.3% of patients had participated in earlier phase 3 studies.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA/SUFU overactive bladder guideline (2024) recommends offering antimuscarinic medication or a β3 agonist to improve urgency, frequency, and/or urgency urinary incontinence <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade A)</Rec>. It also recommends counseling patients on oral medication adverse effects when choosing treatment <Rec>(Clinical Principle)</Rec>; its discussion cites this trial among studies of quality-of-life outcomes.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
