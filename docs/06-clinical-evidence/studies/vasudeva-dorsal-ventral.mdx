# Vasudeva 2015: Dorsal Versus Ventral Onlay Buccal Mucosal Graft Urethroplasty

<TrialBottomLine id="vasudeva-dorsal-ventral" />

**Vasudeva 2015** randomized 80 patients with long-segment incomplete bulbar stricture to dorsal or ventral onlay buccal mucosal graft urethroplasty. Its primary IPSS and Qmax outcomes did not differ significantly between groups at 12 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="vasudeva-dorsal-ventral" />

## Design

The trial enrolled patients with an incomplete bulbar stricture longer than 2 cm at V. M. Medical College and Safdarjang Hospital, New Delhi. Eighty eligible patients were randomized equally between the two graft positions. IPSS and Qmax were the designated primary outcomes; intraoperative parameters and complications were secondary. Failure meant further surgery or Qmax below 14 mL/s. The between-group significance threshold was P &lt; 0.05. Baseline symptom scores, flow rates, and intraoperative measures did not differ significantly between the groups.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Primary symptom and flow outcomes improved within each arm but did not differ between arms: at 12 months, mean IPSS was 5.57 versus 5.15 (P = 0.69), and mean Qmax was 17.32 versus 17.31 mL/s (P = 0.99), dorsal versus ventral. These means included 38 patients per arm after two failures per arm at 3 months; between-group comparisons at 3 months were also nonsignificant. By 12 months, 37/40 (92.5%) dorsal and 36/40 (90%) ventral patients remained free of failure (P = 0.51).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Two consultant urologists performed modified Barbagli dorsal onlay or Wessells ventral onlay repair, with separate graft-harvest and perineal-dissection teams. Mean operative time was 116.75 versus 113.25 minutes (P = 0.43); mean graft length was 6.27 versus 6.20 cm (P = 0.81). A pericatheter urethrogram at 4 weeks guided catheter removal, with one additional week of catheterization for extravasation.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Reported complications were wound hematoma in 2/40 (5%) versus 1/40 (2.5%), combined wound infection and graft necrosis in 1/40 (2.5%) versus 3/40 (7.5%), and donor-site complications in 0/40 versus 2/40 (5%), dorsal versus ventral. All were managed conservatively. Redo urethroplasty was planned for the three dorsal and four ventral patients classified as failures by 12 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Enrollment was confined to one center, with 40 randomized patients per arm. The 12-month IPSS and Qmax means excluded two early failures per arm, while success used all 40 per arm. The authors identified flow-based failure assessment and 12-month follow-up as limitations.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU 2026 Urethral Strictures guideline cites this trial in its review of graft location and recommends choosing a dorsal, dorsolateral, or ventral approach according to surgical practice, expertise, and intraoperative findings <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
