# Low-dose onabotulinumtoxinA in noncatheterizing MS: Intradetrusor 100 U for neurogenic detrusor overactivity

<TrialBottomLine id="botox-ms-100u" />

In this double-blind phase 3 randomized trial of noncatheterizing adults with multiple sclerosis and neurogenic detrusor overactivity despite anticholinergic treatment, intradetrusor onabotulinumtoxinA 100 U reduced daily urinary incontinence more than placebo at week 6.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="botox-ms-100u" />

## Design

Eligible patients had stable multiple sclerosis, urodynamically demonstrated involuntary detrusor contractions, and urinary incontinence inadequately managed by an anticholinergic. Patients already using intermittent catheterization, with a high screening postvoid residual, or previously treated with botulinum toxin for a urologic condition were excluded. They had to be willing to start catheterization if needed. Baseline incontinence, anticholinergic use, multiple sclerosis duration, and age determined randomization strata; the study drug and placebo were packaged identically.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The primary endpoint was mean change in daily urinary incontinence episodes from baseline at week 6, measured with a bladder diary. The prespecified superiority test used an analysis of covariance adjusted for baseline and randomization stratum. The placebo-controlled analysis covered the first 12 weeks; later retreatment was open-label. Urodynamics were centrally reviewed, and incontinence-specific quality of life was measured with I-QOL.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At week 6, daily incontinence episodes changed by −3.3 with onabotulinumtoxinA and −1.1 with placebo, an approximate between-arm mean difference of −2.2 episodes/day calculated from the rounded arm changes (P &lt; 0.001). The primary superiority endpoint was met. Complete incontinence reduction occurred in 53.0% versus 10.3% (P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The mean I-QOL total-score change was +40.4 versus +9.9 points at week 6 (P &lt; 0.001); the instrument's minimally important difference was +11 points. Median time to a retreatment request was 51.7 versus 12.6 weeks (P &lt; 0.001). Urodynamic testing found increased cystometric capacity and reduced detrusor pressure with active treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Study treatment was delivered cystoscopically as 30 one-mL detrusor injections distributed around the bladder while sparing the trigone. Local anesthesia was usual, with general anesthesia or no anesthesia allowed at the physician's discretion. A second, open-label onabotulinumtoxinA injection could be given after at least 12 weeks if requested and if diary and residual-volume criteria were met. It was given to 30/66 (45.5%) initially assigned to active treatment and 67/78 (85.9%) assigned to placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

During the first 12 weeks, protocol-defined urinary tract infection occurred in 17/66 (25.8%) versus 5/78 (6.4%). The protocol counted culture-positive infection with leukocyturia whether or not symptoms were present; symptomatic infection occurred in 9/66 (13.6%) versus 1/78 (1.3%). Three onabotulinumtoxinA recipients were hospitalized for UTI during treatment cycle 1.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Clean intermittent catheterization for retention began in 10/66 (15.2%) versus 2/78 (2.6%) at some point during treatment cycle 1. Median catheterization duration was 64.0 versus 2.0 days. The study defined retention using residual volume and symptoms, with catheterization at the investigator's discretion for symptomatic residuals.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The study enrolled spontaneously voiding patients with stable multiple sclerosis and excluded patients already catheterizing or with a high screening residual. The randomized comparison used placebo; the adult neurogenic 200 U dose was examined in separate trials. Retreatment after the initial placebo-controlled period was open-label, so the later retreatment-request result has a different comparison structure from the week 6 endpoint.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="botox-ms-100u" />

The earlier Cruz and Ginsberg trials tested higher onabotulinumtoxinA doses in mixed multiple sclerosis and spinal cord injury populations. Their results informed the dose question and sample-size calculation here; this trial tested 100 U specifically in patients with multiple sclerosis who were not catheterizing.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

- **AUA/SUFU (2021).** For spinal cord injury or multiple sclerosis refractory to oral medication, recommend onabotulinumtoxinA to improve storage parameters, incontinence, and quality of life <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade A)</Rec>. For spontaneously voiding patients, discuss retention and possible intermittent catheterization before treatment <Rec>(Clinical Principle)</Rec>. The guideline names Tullman 2018 among the supporting trials.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **EAU Neuro-urology (2026).** Use detrusor botulinum toxin A injections for neurogenic detrusor overactivity when antimuscarinic therapy is ineffective <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>. Its discussion cites Tullman 2018 for the lower 100 U dose in patients with multiple sclerosis.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **FDA label (August 2023).** The BOTOX label specifies 200 U for adult detrusor overactivity associated with a neurologic condition and 100 U for adult idiopathic overactive bladder.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
