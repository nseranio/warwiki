# LAVA: Laparoscopic sacrohysteropexy versus vaginal sacrospinous hysteropexy for uterine prolapse

<TrialBottomLine id="lava" />

**LAVA** was a multicenter, nonblinded randomized noninferiority trial in women with stage 2 or higher uterine prolapse and no previous pelvic floor surgery that compared laparoscopic sacrohysteropexy with vaginal sacrospinous hysteropexy; laparoscopic repair met the prespecified noninferiority criterion for symptomatic apical failure or retreatment at 12 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="lava" />

## Design

The trial randomized participants equally, stratified by center and uterine-prolapse stage. Surgeons followed a common operative protocol discussed at a study masterclass; a urogynecology-trained physician uninvolved in treatment assessed the 12-month outcome. Previous pelvic floor surgery, significant uterine or cervical pathology, contraindications to laparoscopy, and a wish to preserve fertility excluded enrollment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The primary endpoint combined recurrent apical prolapse at POP-Q stage 2 or higher with bothersome bulge or protrusion, repeat surgery, or pessary use. The noninferiority margin allowed at most a 10-percentage-point increase in failure with laparoscopy. The main analysis kept patients in their assigned groups and carried forward the last observation when follow-up was incomplete.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At 12 months, apical surgical failure occurred in 1/64 women (1.6%) assigned laparoscopy and 2/61 (3.3%) assigned vaginal repair. The risk difference was −1.7 percentage points (95% CI −7.1 to 3.7); its upper bound was below the prespecified margin. The vaginal analysis excluded a randomized participant with no follow-up data.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Overall surgical failure, which also counted prolapse in other compartments, was 42/64 (65.6%) versus 38/61 (62.3%). All-compartment composite success—no prolapse beyond the hymen, bothersome bulge, or retreatment—was achieved by 53/64 (82.8%) versus 51/61 (83.6%). Overall anatomical failure at examination was 35/55 (63.6%) versus 36/58 (62.1%); most such failures were anterior. Repeat apical surgery occurred in 0/64 versus 2/61 women. Quality-of-life outcomes did not differ between groups.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Laparoscopic sacrohysteropexy used bifurcated polypropylene mesh passed through broad-ligament windows, fixed to the cervix with permanent sutures and to the sacral promontory with titanium staples, then covered by peritoneum. Vaginal sacrospinous hysteropexy used a posterior vaginal incision and pararectal dissection to the right sacrospinous ligament, with permanent sutures passed through the ligament and posterior cervix. Associated compartment repairs and incontinence surgery were allowed.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Anterior wall repair accompanied 55/64 (85.9%) laparoscopic assignments and 61/62 (98.4%) vaginal assignments. Six of 64 women assigned laparoscopy received a different operation because of technical difficulty: five had vaginal sacrospinous hysteropexy and one a Manchester-Fothergill procedure. A polyp removed during laparoscopic surgery contained endometrial carcinoma; the woman underwent hysterectomy later.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Among women with preoperative overactive bladder, symptoms persisted in 14/50 (28%) after laparoscopic repair versus 7/52 (13.5%) after vaginal repair. Persistent fecal incontinence was reported by 6/59 (10.2%) versus 2/58 (3.4%). Among sexually active women, dyspareunia was reported by 5/37 (13.5%) versus 13/39 (33.3%). These were secondary symptom comparisons.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Patient-reported bulge and symptom components came from unblinded participants. Technical difficulty led to six departures from assigned laparoscopy, although the main analysis retained the original assignment. The published report used symptomatic apical recurrence or retreatment, whereas the earlier protocol also specified an anatomical midvaginal point-C threshold for success. The reported comparison covers 12 months of follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
