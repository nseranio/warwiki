# PESSRI: Ring-with-Support versus Gellhorn Pessaries for Pelvic Organ Prolapse

<TrialBottomLine id="pessri" />

**PESSRI** was a multicenter randomized crossover trial in 134 women with symptomatic pelvic organ prolapse comparing a ring with support versus a Gellhorn pessary for three months each; both improved symptom distress and life impact, with no clinically significant between-device difference in the reported scales.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="pessri" />

## Design

Eligible women had POP-Q stage II or greater prolapse; the median stage was III. The initial randomized sequence assigned 71 women to the ring and 63 to the Gellhorn. Participants then crossed to the other device. The investigators assessed satisfaction on a visual analog scale and symptom distress and life impact with the Pelvic Floor Distress Inventory (PFDI) and Pelvic Floor Impact Questionnaire (PFIQ).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

After each device period, most PFDI scales and many PFIQ scales showed statistically and clinically significant improvement from baseline with both pessaries. The authors reported no clinically significant between-device difference in these measures. Their conclusion specifically names relief of protrusion and voiding dysfunction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Three-month outcome data were available for 94 ring periods and 99 Gellhorn periods. These are device-period observations in a crossover study.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

The reported symptom and life-impact outcomes do not quantify device-specific complications.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The comparison measured short-term patient-reported symptoms and life impact.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Outcome capture was incomplete relative to enrollment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

**ACOG/AUGS (Practice Bulletin 214, 2019).** Women considering treatment for pelvic organ prolapse should be offered a vaginal pessary as an alternative to surgery <Rec>(Level B)</Rec>. The bulletin cites PESSRI when discussing pessary effectiveness.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
