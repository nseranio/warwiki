# ALTESS: Alfuzosin for Long-Term BPH Progression Prevention

<TrialBottomLine id="altess" />

**ALTESS** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized trial comparing alfuzosin 10 mg once daily with placebo for two years in men with LUTS/BPH at risk of progression; first acute urinary retention, its primary endpoint, occurred in 16/749 (2.1%) versus 14/757 (1.8%) of the efficacy population (P = 0.82).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="altess" />

## Design

The trial randomized 1,522 men, 759 to alfuzosin and 763 to placebo; 1,506 (749 and 757) had at least one postbaseline efficacy criterion and entered the intent-to-treat analysis. The [trial registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00029822) excluded men with previous acute urinary retention, prostate surgery or prostate cancer. First acute urinary retention was the prespecified primary endpoint; BPH-related surgery, total IPSS, bother score, peak urinary flow and PSA were secondary endpoints. The analyses of an IPSS increase of at least 4 points and of a composite of retention, surgery or symptom worsening were post hoc.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

BPH-related surgery occurred in 38/749 (5.1%) of the alfuzosin group and 49/757 (6.5%) of the placebo group (P = 0.18). The reported relative risk reduction was 22% (95% CI −18% to 48%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the post hoc analyses, IPSS deterioration occurred in 88/749 (11.7%) versus 127/757 (16.8%) (P = 0.0013), and overall clinical progression occurred in 122/749 (16.3%) versus 167/757 (22.1%) (P &lt; 0.001). The reported relative risk reductions were 30% (95% CI 10% to 46%) and 26% (95% CI 9% to 40%), respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The prespecified symptom and flow measures favored alfuzosin: mean IPSS change was −5.9 versus −4.7 (P = 0.0017), bother-score change was −1.3 versus −0.9 (P &lt; 0.001), and peak-flow change at 12 months was +2.0 versus +1.3 mL/s (P = 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Alfuzosin 10 mg was taken once daily without initial dose titration; both arms took study treatment after the same meal. Study follow-up was completed by 529/759 (69.7%) assigned alfuzosin and 480/763 (62.9%) assigned placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

In the exposed population, at least one treatment-emergent adverse event occurred in 400/754 (53.1%) on alfuzosin and 390/761 (51.2%) on placebo; serious events occurred in 83/754 (11.0%) and 81/761 (10.6%), and adverse events led to discontinuation in 69/754 (9.2%) and 58/761 (7.6%). Dizziness or postural dizziness occurred in 45/754 (6.0%) versus 35/761 (4.6%), hypotension or postural hypotension in 9/754 (1.2%) versus 4/761 (0.5%), and ejaculation failure or retrograde ejaculation in 3/754 (0.4%) versus 0/761 (0%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Acute urinary retention was uncommon during the two-year follow-up. The post hoc composite combined symptom deterioration with retention and surgery.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
