# ALTESS: Alfuzosin for Long-Term BPH Progression Prevention

<TrialBottomLine id="altess" />

**ALTESS** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized trial comparing alfuzosin 10 mg once daily with placebo for two years in men with LUTS/BPH at risk of progression; first acute urinary retention, its primary endpoint, occurred in 2.1% versus 1.8% (P = 0.82).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="altess" />

## Design

The trial randomized 1,522 men, 759 to alfuzosin and 763 to placebo. The [trial registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00029822) excluded men with previous acute urinary retention, prostate surgery or prostate cancer. First acute urinary retention was the prespecified primary endpoint, and BPH-related surgery was also assessed. The analyses of an International Prostate Symptom Score (IPSS) increase of at least 4 points and of a composite of retention, surgery or symptom worsening were post hoc.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

BPH-related surgery occurred in 5.1% of the alfuzosin group and 6.5% of the placebo group (P = 0.18). The reported relative risk reduction was 22% (95% CI −18% to 48%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the post hoc analyses, IPSS deterioration occurred in 11.7% versus 16.8% (P = 0.0013), and overall clinical progression occurred in 16.3% versus 22.1% (P &lt; 0.001). The reported relative risk reductions were 30% (95% CI 10% to 46%) and 26% (95% CI 9% to 40%), respectively. IPSS, quality of life and peak urinary flow also favored alfuzosin.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

The investigators reported comparable overall adverse-event incidence between alfuzosin and placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Acute urinary retention was uncommon during the two-year follow-up. The post hoc composite combined symptom deterioration with retention and surgery.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
