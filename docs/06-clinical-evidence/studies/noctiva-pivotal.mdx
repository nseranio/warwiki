# SER120 DB3/DB4: Desmopressin Nasal Spray for Nocturia

<TrialBottomLine id="noctiva-pivotal" />

**SER120 DB3/DB4** pooled two multicenter, double-blind randomized trials in adults aged at least 50 years with nocturia: both desmopressin nasal spray doses reduced nocturnal voids and increased responder rates versus placebo during 12 weeks of treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="noctiva-pivotal" />

## Design

The pooled intent-to-treat population comprised 1,333 patients with at least 2.16 nocturnal voids per night during a two-week screening period. Patients were assigned to nightly intranasal desmopressin acetate 1.66 mcg, 0.83 mcg, or placebo. Coprimary endpoints were mean change in nightly nocturnal voids and the proportion with at least a 50% reduction. DB4 also measured the Impact of Nighttime Urination questionnaire; other secondary endpoints included time to first nocturnal void and nights with at most one void.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Mean nocturnal voids changed by −1.5 with 1.66 mcg, −1.4 with 0.83 mcg, and −1.2 with placebo (both active comparisons P &lt; 0.0001). Responder rates in the pooled intent-to-treat groups (n = 439, 448, and 446) were 48.7%, 37.9%, and 30.3%, respectively (P &lt; 0.0001 and P = 0.0159 versus placebo). Both coprimary endpoints met significance in the pooled analysis; the 0.83-mcg responder endpoint reached significance only after pooling. In DB4, the Impact of Nighttime Urination overall impact score changed by −14.1 with 1.66 mcg (n = 243), −12.4 with 0.83 mcg (n = 247), and −11.5 with placebo (n = 249); the higher dose improved the score versus placebo (P = 0.0255). In the pooled analysis, time to first nocturnal void increased by 108, 96, and 72 minutes, respectively (both active comparisons P &lt; 0.0001), and the percentage of nights with at most one nocturnal void increased by 46.58, 39.94, and 33.75 percentage points (P &lt; 0.0001 and P = 0.0121 versus placebo).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Treatment was one intranasal spray in either nostril nightly before bedtime for 12 weeks, with study assessments every two weeks and no imposed fluid restriction or behavioral therapy. The FDA label specifies one spray in either nostril about 30 minutes before bedtime; its two strengths are distinct products, so two 0.83-mcg sprays are not interchangeable with one 1.66-mcg spray.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Protocol-defined hyponatremia occurred in 5/448 (1.1%) with 1.66 mcg, 0/454 (0%) with 0.83 mcg, and 1/454 (0.2%) with placebo in the safety population; all five high-dose cases were older than 65 years.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The definition was sodium ≤125 mmol/L regardless of symptoms or &lt;130 mmol/L with symptoms.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Nasal discomfort occurred in 25/448 (5.6%), 16/454 (3.5%), and 25/454 (5.5%), respectively; urinary tract infection occurred in 7/448 (1.6%), 16/454 (3.5%), and 6/454 (1.3%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> Adverse events led to discontinuation in 80/1,356 (5.9%) across the pooled safety population.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The FDA label carries a boxed hyponatremia warning and requires normal sodium before treatment, early monitoring after starting or increasing the dose, and periodic monitoring thereafter.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The pooled report gives a short treatment horizon and combines two trials. Although both doses met the pooled coprimary endpoints, the FDA label states that 0.83 mcg did not meet all prespecified efficacy endpoints in the individual trials.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The pooled analysis enrolled patients with nocturia broadly; the FDA indication is restricted to nocturia due to documented nocturnal polyuria.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The Canadian Urological Association's 2022 nocturia best practice report cites this trial and offers pharmacological therapy, including desmopressin, for refractory nocturnal polyuria affecting quality of life <Rec>(strong recommendation, low level of evidence)</Rec>. It excludes the intranasal formulation from its dosing recommendations because it judged the nocturnal-polyuria evidence inadequate.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The FDA approved NOCTIVA on March 3, 2017 for adults who awaken at least twice nightly to void because of nocturnal polyuria.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
