# Zhou 2025: Intravaginal Conjugated Oestrogen and Pessary Continuation

<TrialBottomLine id="pessary-estrogen-zhou" />

**Zhou 2025** was a multicentre, double-blind randomized trial in postmenopausal women with symptomatic pelvic organ prolapse successfully fitted with a ring pessary with support; vaginal conjugated oestrogen cream and matching placebo produced similar 12-month rates of continued pessary use with patient-reported improvement (181/208 [87.0%] versus 176/203 [86.7%]; risk difference 0.3 percentage points, 95% CI −6.2 to 6.9).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="pessary-estrogen-zhou" />

## Design

Women with symptomatic stage 2 or greater prolapse first completed a two-week trial of a ring pessary with support. A successful fit held the prolapse above the hymen, caused no obvious erosion or infection, and was acceptable to the participant. The trial enrolled women across 12 Chinese hospitals. Exclusions included acute infection, genital fistula, suspected or untreated lower genital tract tumour, elevated postvoid residual, recent hormone therapy, and contraindications to oestrogen. Allocation was central and stratified by centre; participants, care providers, investigators, and outcome assessors were masked. The creams had identical appearance and scent.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The primary endpoint required both pessary use at least five days each week and a Patient Global Impression of Improvement response of “much better” or “very much better.” The primary analysis included randomized participants who remained eligible and attended at least one follow-up visit. The study was powered to detect a 14-percentage-point absolute difference, with per-protocol sensitivity analyses based on cream adherence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Pessary continuation alone was 186/208 (89.4%) with oestrogen and 182/203 (89.7%) with placebo (risk difference −0.2 percentage points, 95% CI −6.1 to 5.7). Among participants at least 50% adherent to cream throughout 12 months, continuation with satisfaction was 143/148 (96.6%) versus 144/150 (96.0%); this per-protocol result agreed with the primary analysis. Model-estimated Pelvic Floor Distress Inventory-20 scores improved in both groups at 12 months, with changes of −47.1 and −49.9 points, respectively (between-group mean difference 2.9 points, 95% CI −5.7 to 11.4).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Participants or caregivers were taught to remove the ring pessary at bedtime and reinsert it the next morning at least weekly. They applied 1 g of conjugated oestrogen cream (0.625 mg/g) or matching placebo vaginally every night for two weeks, then twice weekly for 12 months. Follow-up occurred every three months; telephone visits substituted when in-person assessment was infeasible. Of 420 randomized women, 208/210 in the oestrogen arm and 203/210 in the placebo arm entered the modified intention-to-treat analysis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Prespecified secondary safety outcomes favoured oestrogen for any pessary-related adverse event (54/208 [26.0%] versus 75/203 [36.9%]), excessive discharge (34/208 [16.3%] versus 52/203 [25.6%]), vaginal erosion or ulcer (4/208 [1.9%] versus 14/203 [6.9%]), and vaginal bleeding (3/208 [1.4%] versus 13/203 [6.4%]). Endometrial thickening on ultrasound occurred in 8/208 (3.8%) versus 4/203 (2.0%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Enrollment required a successful ring-with-support fitting, and the protocol included regular removal and reinsertion. Follow-up ended at 12 months. Some visits occurred by telephone during the covid-19 pandemic, when vaginal erosion or ulcer could have been missed without examination. The modified intention-to-treat analysis omitted four women lost before any visit and five found ineligible after randomization.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
