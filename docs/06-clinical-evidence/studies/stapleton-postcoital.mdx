# Stapleton postcoital prophylaxis: Trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole after intercourse for recurrent urinary tract infection

<TrialBottomLine id="stapleton-postcoital" />

**Stapleton postcoital prophylaxis** was a double-blind randomized trial in healthy young women prone to recurrent urinary tract infections that found fewer recurrences with postcoital trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole than with postcoital placebo over more than six months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="stapleton-postcoital" />

## Design

The investigators randomized 16 women to trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole after intercourse and 11 to postcoital placebo. The groups were similar in age, parity, diaphragm use, prior UTI history, intercourse frequency and lifetime number of sexual partners. Follow-up exceeded six months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Recurrent UTI developed in 2/16 women (12.5%) assigned trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole and 9/11 (81.8%) assigned placebo. The corresponding infection rates were 0.3 and 3.6 per patient-year. The authors reported a benefit at both lower and higher intercourse frequencies.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

The authors described few side effects and excellent adherence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The trial enrolled 27 women, limiting the precision of the recurrence estimate and its ability to characterize uncommon adverse effects. Its observation period was more than six months, and its comparator was placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA/CUA/SUFU recurrent UTI guideline (2025 amendment) permits antibiotic prophylaxis in women previously diagnosed with UTIs after discussion of risks, benefits and alternatives <Rec>(Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>. Its discussion cites this randomized trial for postcoital prophylaxis and lists single-dose treatment immediately before or after intercourse for women with intercourse-associated recurrences.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
