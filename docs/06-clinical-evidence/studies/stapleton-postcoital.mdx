# Stapleton postcoital prophylaxis: Trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole after intercourse for recurrent urinary tract infection

<TrialBottomLine id="stapleton-postcoital" />

**Stapleton postcoital prophylaxis** was a double-blind randomized trial in young women with recurrent urinary tract infections. During 180 days, symptomatic UTI occurred in 2/16 women analyzed in the postcoital trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole arm and 9/11 analyzed in the postcoital placebo arm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="stapleton-postcoital" />

## Design

The investigators randomized 35 women: 17 to postcoital trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole, 12 to postcoital placebo and six to daily trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole with postcoital placebo. Entry infections excluded one woman from each postcoital arm and two from the daily arm; the analysis compared the remaining 16 and 11 women in the postcoital arms. Eligible women were nonpregnant university students with at least two culture-documented UTIs in the preceding year and estimated intercourse fewer than 12 times per month. The postcoital groups were similar in age, parity, diaphragm use, intercourse frequency and lifetime number of sexual partners; median culture-documented UTIs in the preceding year were three versus four, respectively (P=.02).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Postcoital trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole reduced symptomatic recurrence during the 180-day prophylaxis period: 2/16 analyzed women (12.5%) developed UTI versus 9/11 (81.8%) given postcoital placebo (P=.0001). The corresponding infection rates were 0.3 and 3.6 per patient-year. The authors reported a benefit at both lower and higher intercourse frequencies.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Treatment delivery

Women took trimethoprim 40 mg/sulfamethoxazole 200 mg or placebo within two hours after intercourse, with no additional postcoital tablet after further intercourse within 24 hours. Postcoital tablet adherence was 92% with active treatment and 86% with placebo, based on daily diaries. Two women in the active postcoital arm withdrew at three and 4.5 months because of suspected or confirmed pregnancy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Among the 16 women analyzed in the active postcoital arm, one reported nausea and completed treatment, two developed confirmed vaginal candidiasis, and one self-treated vaginal symptoms without a confirmed diagnosis; none of the 11 placebo recipients reported side effects. Both breakthrough infections in the active arm involved multidrug-resistant organisms, including resistance to trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole, versus one of nine infections in the placebo arm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The postcoital comparison analyzed 27 women, limiting the precision of the recurrence estimate and its ability to characterize uncommon adverse effects. The prophylaxis period was 180 days, and the comparator was placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA/CUA/SUFU recurrent UTI guideline (2025 amendment) permits antibiotic prophylaxis in women previously diagnosed with UTIs after discussion of risks, benefits and alternatives <Rec>(Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>. Its discussion cites this randomized trial for postcoital prophylaxis and lists single-dose treatment immediately before or after intercourse for women with intercourse-associated recurrences.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
