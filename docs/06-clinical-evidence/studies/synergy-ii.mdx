# SYNERGY II: Long-term Mirabegron and Solifenacin Combination Therapy for Wet Overactive Bladder

<TrialBottomLine id="synergy-ii" />

**SYNERGY II** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized trial in 1,829 adults with wet overactive bladder that compared mirabegron 50 mg plus solifenacin 5 mg with either drug alone for 12 months; the combination improved daily incontinence and micturition counts more than either monotherapy in nominal efficacy analyses. Treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 49% with combination therapy, 41% with mirabegron, and 44% with solifenacin.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="synergy-ii" />

## Design

Eligible adults had at least three months of urinary frequency and urgency with incontinence. The trial used a two-week single-blind placebo run-in to wash out previous overactive bladder medication, followed by once-daily double-blind tablets and a two-week treatment-free follow-up. Randomization was stratified by sex, age group, and geographic region. The full analysis set included 1,794 patients; 1,622 (90%) had previously participated in SYNERGY and 136 (7.6%) in BESIDE.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The primary objective was safety, assessed through treatment-emergent adverse events without a formal between-group test. The designated primary *efficacy* variables were change from baseline to end of treatment in incontinence episodes and micturitions per 24 hours, recorded in a seven-day electronic diary. Efficacy tests were exploratory and were not adjusted for multiple comparisons. For incontinence, P values came from a stratified rank analysis, while adjusted mean differences and confidence intervals came from a separate analysis of covariance.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At the end of treatment, adjusted mean incontinence episodes fell by 2.0 per day with the combination, 1.6 with mirabegron, and 1.9 with solifenacin. The combination-minus-monotherapy adjusted mean differences were −0.5 episodes/day versus mirabegron (95% CI −0.7 to −0.2; nominal P &lt; 0.001) and −0.1 versus solifenacin (95% CI −0.4 to 0.1; nominal P = 0.002). The latter interval crosses zero because it came from a different analysis than the P value.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Adjusted mean micturitions fell by 2.6 per day with the combination, 2.1 with mirabegron, and 2.2 with solifenacin. The adjusted mean differences were −0.5 micturitions/day versus mirabegron (95% CI −0.8 to −0.2; nominal P &lt; 0.001) and −0.4 versus solifenacin (95% CI −0.7 to −0.1; nominal P = 0.004). All four efficacy comparisons met the trial's nominal superiority threshold.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the prespecified age analysis, patients aged 65 years or older had adjusted mean reductions in incontinence episodes of 2.24/day with combination therapy, 1.52/day with mirabegron, and 1.73/day with solifenacin. Combination-minus-monotherapy differences were −0.72/day (95% CI −1.13 to −0.31) and −0.51/day (95% CI −0.92 to −0.10), respectively. Among those aged 75 years or older, the differences were −0.89/day versus mirabegron (95% CI −1.68 to −0.09) and −0.49/day versus solifenacin (95% CI −1.23 to 0.24). The subgroup report calculated no between-treatment P values.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Patients received two tablets daily, with matching placebo tablets keeping the three regimens indistinguishable. Of 1,829 randomized patients, 1,814 entered the safety set and 1,794 entered the efficacy analysis set. The combination arm was assigned four times as often as each monotherapy arm, yielding 1,218, 306, and 305 randomized patients, respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

In the safety set, serious treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 51/1,206 (4.2%) combination recipients and 8/305 (2.6%) mirabegron and 8/303 (2.6%) solifenacin recipients. Investigators considered one serious event, atrial fibrillation in the mirabegron arm, possibly treatment related. Dry mouth occurred in 74/1,206 (6.1%), 12/305 (3.9%), and 18/303 (5.9%); constipation in 40/1,206 (3.3%), 3/305 (1.0%), and 7/303 (2.3%), respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Grouped urinary tract infection events occurred in 101/1,206 (8.4%) with combination therapy, 19/305 (6.2%) with mirabegron, and 18/303 (5.9%) with solifenacin. Urinary retention was recorded in 9/1,206 (0.7%), 1/305 (0.3%), and 1/303 (0.3%), respectively. The age analysis describes one combination-treated man who required catheterization after 279 treatment days; retention resolved after eight days.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Among patients aged 65 years or older, any treatment-emergent adverse event occurred in 237/414 (57.2%) with the combination, 43/104 (41.3%) with mirabegron, and 54/104 (51.9%) with solifenacin. Corresponding rates at age 75 years or older were 57/106 (53.8%), 15/29 (51.7%), and 13/35 (37.1%).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The safety objective had no prespecified comparative hypothesis, and the efficacy comparisons were exploratory without multiplicity control. Most participants had already entered a shorter combination-drug trial, and the age-75-or-older monotherapy groups were small. The end-of-treatment efficacy analysis used last observation carried forward after early withdrawal.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="synergy-ii" />

SYNERGY II provided 12-month exposure after the shorter SYNERGY and BESIDE programs. BESIDE enrolled patients still incontinent after a solifenacin run-in; SYNERGY II drew mostly from prior trial participants and compared the combination with both constituent monotherapies.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA/SUFU 2024 idiopathic overactive bladder guideline says clinicians may offer combination therapy with a medication from a different class when one overactive bladder medication gives inadequate improvement <Rec>(Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
