# Solifenacin pivotal trial (Cardozo 2004): Once-Daily Solifenacin for Overactive Bladder

<TrialBottomLine id="solifenacin-pivotal" />

In a multinational, double-blind, randomized trial of patients with overactive bladder, solifenacin 5 mg and 10 mg once daily reduced mean micturitions per 24 hours more than placebo over 12 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="solifenacin-pivotal" />

## Design

Cardozo and colleagues randomized 911 adults with overactive bladder symptoms for at least 3 months across three arms after a 2-week placebo run-in. Eligibility required at least 8 micturitions per 24 hours and at least 3 urgency and/or incontinence episodes in a 3-day diary. The primary efficacy variable was change from baseline in mean micturitions per 24 hours at the end of treatment. Secondary variables included urgency, nocturia, and incontinence episodes per 24 hours and mean volume voided per micturition. The prespecified analysis used ANOVA with two-sided α = 0.05 and the last available on-treatment observation carried forward.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Both solifenacin doses met the primary micturition endpoint in the 857-patient efficacy set: mean micturitions per 24 hours fell by 2.37 with 5 mg (n = 286), 2.81 with 10 mg (n = 290), and 1.59 with placebo (n = 281). The differences versus placebo were −0.78 (95% CI −1.27 to −0.29; P = 0.0018) and −1.22 (95% CI −1.71 to −0.72; P = 0.0001), respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Urgency and nocturia also decreased: mean urgency episodes per 24 hours fell by 2.84 (51%) and 2.90 (52%) with 5 mg and 10 mg versus 1.98 with placebo (P = 0.003 and P = 0.002). The 10 mg dose reduced mean nocturia episodes by 0.71 (38.5%) versus 0.52 (16.4%) with placebo (between-arm difference about −0.19, 95% CI −0.38 to −0.01; P = 0.036); the 5 mg reduction of 0.58 did not reach significance.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Incontinence episodes decreased with both doses: baseline-adjusted mean changes were −1.73 and −1.58 episodes per 24 hours for 5 mg and 10 mg (P = 0.002 and P = 0.016 versus placebo). Urge-incontinence changes were −1.33 and −1.24 (P = 0.014 and P = 0.042). Among participants incontinent at baseline, 50.3% on 5 mg and 49.7% on 10 mg reported no incontinence at the endpoint.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Mean volume voided per micturition rose by 30.8 mL and 36.0 mL with solifenacin versus 10.67 mL with placebo (P = 0.0001 for each comparison).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Participants took oral solifenacin 5 mg or 10 mg, or placebo, once daily for 12 weeks after the run-in, with 3-day micturition diaries before visits every 4 weeks. Of 911 randomized, 907 received treatment, 857 entered the efficacy analysis, and 830/907 (91.5%) completed treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Anticholinergic adverse events increased with dose in the 907-patient safety set: dry mouth occurred in 23/299 (7.7%) on 5 mg, 71/307 (23.1%) on 10 mg, and 7/301 (2.3%) on placebo; most cases were mild and neither solifenacin arm had severe dry mouth. Constipation occurred in 11/299 (3.7%), 28/307 (9.1%), and 6/301 (2.0%), respectively; blurred vision occurred in 12/299 (4.0%), 18/307 (5.9%), and 7/301 (2.3%). Adverse events led to discontinuation in 7/299 (2.3%), 12/307 (3.9%), and 10/301 (3.3%), respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The comparison lasted 12 weeks. Each fixed dose was compared with placebo; no direct statistical comparison between solifenacin doses was reported.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
