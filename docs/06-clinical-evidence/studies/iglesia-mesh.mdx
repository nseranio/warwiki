# Iglesia vaginal mesh trial: Vaginal Mesh for Prolapse, a Randomized Controlled Trial

<TrialBottomLine id="iglesia-mesh" />

**The Iglesia vaginal mesh trial** was a double-blind, multicenter randomized comparison of polypropylene mesh-augmented vaginal colpopexy and traditional vaginal repair in women with POP-Q stage 2–4 prolapse. The early report found no objective or subjective cure advantage, and enrollment stopped after mesh exposure reached its prespecified threshold.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="iglesia-mesh" />

## Design

The primary outcome was POP-Q stage 1 or lower at three months. The mesh arm received anterior or total vaginal mesh with colpopexy; the native-tissue arm underwent vaginal colpopexy, primarily uterosacral ligament suspension, with concurrent colporrhaphy. The median follow-up when enrollment stopped was 9.7 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The one-year report defined objective success as overall POP-Q stage 1 or lower; repeat surgery or pessary use for recurrent prolapse also counted as failure. Five mesh patients had no evaluable 12-month examination, including three who underwent repeat prolapse surgery before that visit.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

Overall recurrence was reported in 19 mesh and 24 native-tissue participants (P = 0.28); patient-reported bulge cure was 93.3% and 100%, respectively. Quality-of-life scores did not differ at three months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

At 12 months, objective success was 12/32 (37.5%) with mesh and 10/33 (30.3%) without mesh (P = .45); overall stage 2 or higher recurrence was 20/32 (62.5%) and 23/33 (69.7%), respectively (P = .45). Anterior-compartment recurrence was 15/32 (46.9%) and 20/33 (60.6%), respectively (P = .40). Bulge cure was reported by 25/26 (96.2%) mesh participants and 30/33 (90.9%) native-tissue participants (P = .62).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The planned final analysis found no group difference in anatomic, symptomatic, or combined cure under its reported definitions at three years. POP-Q stage improved from baseline in 90% and 86%, respectively. Follow-up questionnaires were available for 51/65 participants and blinded POP-Q examinations for 41/65.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Among 32 mesh operations in the initial report, 24 used anterior mesh and eight used total mesh. Native-tissue operations included concomitant colporrhaphy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Six fellowship-trained surgeons used anterior or total Prolift mesh in the mesh arm and uterosacral or sacrospinous suspension without mesh. Each had performed more than 30 vaginal colpopexies and at least 10 Prolift procedures before enrollment. Mean operative time was 3.0 ± 0.8 hours with mesh and 3.1 ± 1.0 hours without mesh (P = .53); concomitant posterior repair was more common without mesh (82% versus 56%, P = .026).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Five mesh recipients had vaginal exposure (5/32, 15.6%). The mesh arm also had two cystotomies and one transfusion, with none reported in the control arm. By the final analysis, three participants had undergone reoperation for recurrent prolapse; all were assigned mesh.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

In the one-year report, three mesh exposures required operating-room excision, and two persisted without symptoms. Reoperation for prolapse or mesh exposure affected 5/32 (15.6%) mesh recipients and 0/33 controls (P = .017); three mesh recipients underwent repeat prolapse surgery (3/32 versus 0/33, P = .11), with one patient counted in both indication groups. One mesh recipient underwent sling revision for persistent voiding dysfunction. Apical Gore-Tex suture exposure occurred in 5/33 controls, two of whom underwent office suture removal.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

Early closure left a small randomized cohort. At three years, examination data were available for 41/65 participants; participants reoperated for recurrent prolapse were omitted from anatomic and subjective analyses but counted as failures for combined cure.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
