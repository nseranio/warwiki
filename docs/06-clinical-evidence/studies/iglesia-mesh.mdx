# Iglesia vaginal mesh trial: Vaginal Mesh for Prolapse, a Randomized Controlled Trial

<TrialBottomLine id="iglesia-mesh" />

**The Iglesia vaginal mesh trial** was a double-blind, multicenter randomized comparison of polypropylene mesh-augmented vaginal colpopexy and traditional vaginal repair in women with POP-Q stage 2–4 prolapse. The early report found no objective or subjective cure advantage, and enrollment stopped after five mesh exposures among 32 recipients exceeded its prespecified 15% threshold.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="iglesia-mesh" />

## Design

The primary outcome was overall POP-Q stage 1 or lower at three months, with repeat prolapse surgery or pessary use counted as failure. Of 65 randomized participants, 33 were assigned mesh and 32 no mesh; one mesh-assigned patient received native-tissue repair because vaginal caliber was inadequate and was analyzed with the 33 no-mesh operations in the early reports. The median follow-up when enrollment stopped was 9.7 months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The one-year report defined objective success as overall POP-Q stage 1 or lower; repeat surgery or pessary use for recurrent prolapse also counted as failure. Five mesh patients had no evaluable 12-month examination, including three who underwent repeat prolapse surgery before that visit.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Results

Overall POP-Q stage 1 or lower was reported in 13/32 mesh recipients (40.6%) and 9/33 no-mesh recipients (27.3%; P = .28) in the early report's anatomic table at median 9.7-month follow-up. Stage 2 or higher recurrence occurred in 19/32 (59.4%) and 24/33 (72.7%), respectively (P = .28); 33 of the 43 recurrences remained at or above the hymen. Patient-reported bulge cure was 93.3% and 100%, respectively; quality-of-life scores did not differ at three months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

At 12 months, objective success was 12/32 (37.5%) with mesh and 10/33 (30.3%) without mesh (P = .45); overall stage 2 or higher recurrence was 20/32 (62.5%) and 23/33 (69.7%), respectively (P = .45). Anterior-compartment recurrence was 15/32 (46.9%) and 20/33 (60.6%), respectively (P = .40). Bulge cure was reported by 25/26 (96.2%) mesh participants and 30/33 (90.9%) native-tissue participants (P = .62).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The planned final analysis found composite cure (POP-Q stage 1 or lower, no bulge, improvement, and no repeat prolapse treatment) in 8/23 mesh participants (35%) and 8/21 no-mesh participants (38%; RR 0.91, 95% CI 0.42–1.99; P &gt; .99). Among those examined, anatomic cure by POP-Q stage 1 or lower was 9/20 (45%) and 9/21 (43%; RR 1.05, 95% CI 0.56–1.96; P &gt; .99); using no descent beyond the hymen, it was 17/20 (85%) and 15/21 (71%; RR 1.19, 95% CI 0.86–1.65; P = .45). Bulge symptoms were absent in 23/25 (92%) and 21/26 (81%; RR 1.14, 95% CI 0.91–1.42; P = .42). POP-Q stage improved from baseline in 90% and 86%, respectively. Follow-up questionnaires were available for 51/65 participants and blinded POP-Q examinations for 41/65.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Among 32 mesh operations in the initial report, 24 used anterior mesh and eight used total mesh. Prolift mesh was placed through transobturator and ischiorectal trocar paths, attached to the apex with delayed-absorbable suture, and trimmed to vaginal caliber; the no-mesh operations included 29 uterosacral, three sacrospinous and one iliococcygeus suspensions, with anterior colporrhaphy in all 33.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Six fellowship-trained surgeons used anterior or total Prolift mesh in the mesh arm and uterosacral or sacrospinous suspension without mesh. Each had performed more than 30 vaginal colpopexies and at least 10 Prolift procedures before enrollment. Mean operative time was 3.0 ± 0.8 hours with mesh and 3.1 ± 1.0 hours without mesh (P = .53); concomitant posterior repair was more common without mesh (82% versus 56%, P = .026).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Five mesh recipients had vaginal exposure (5/32, 15.6%); three required operating-room excision, one resolved after office trimming and estrogen, and one persisted without requiring removal.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The mesh arm also had two cystotomies (2/32 versus 0/33) and one transfusion (1/32 versus 0/33); neither arm had a major infection requiring postoperative antibiotics.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In the early report, four mesh recipients underwent five reoperations for prolapse, exposure or another indication (4/32 versus 0/33; P = .048).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> By the final analysis, three mesh recipients had undergone reoperation for recurrent prolapse (3/23 versus 0/21 in the composite analysis); one had urinary retention and underwent a second prolapse operation at 17.5 months.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> De novo dyspareunia occurred in two mesh and one no-mesh participant, and de novo stress urinary incontinence in three and two, respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

In the one-year report, three mesh exposures required operating-room excision, and two persisted without symptoms. Reoperation for prolapse or mesh exposure affected 5/32 (15.6%) mesh recipients and 0/33 controls (P = .017); three mesh recipients underwent repeat prolapse surgery (3/32 versus 0/33, P = .11), with one patient counted in both indication groups. One mesh recipient underwent sling revision for persistent voiding dysfunction. Apical Gore-Tex suture exposure occurred in 5/33 controls, two of whom underwent office suture removal.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

Early closure left a small randomized cohort. At three years, examination data were available for 41/65 participants; participants reoperated for recurrent prolapse were omitted from anatomic and subjective analyses but counted as failures for combined cure.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
