# Trospium pivotal trials: Immediate-Release Trospium Chloride for Overactive Bladder

<TrialBottomLine id="trospium-pivotal" />

**Trospium pivotal trials** comprised two multicenter, double-blind phase III randomized studies of oral trospium chloride 20 mg twice daily versus placebo for 12 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In Zinner's 523 randomized patients with overactive bladder and urge incontinence, both coprimary diary endpoints improved with trospium in the intention-to-treat analysis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="trospium-pivotal" />

## Design

Zinner and colleagues randomized 262 patients to trospium and 261 to placebo at 51 sites and specified two coprimary diary endpoints: change in average toilet voids and urge incontinence episodes per 24 hours. Secondary measures included voided volume, urgency severity, day and night frequency, time to onset, and the Incontinence Impact Questionnaire. Efficacy analyses used 256 patients per arm with last observation carried forward.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Rudy and colleagues randomized 329 patients per arm at 52 sites. Their sole primary endpoint was change in average toilet voids per 24 hours; urge urinary incontinence episodes were secondary. Efficacy analyses used 323 trospium and 325 placebo recipients with last observation carried forward.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Rudy's cohort was independent of Zinner's.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The FDA medical review identifies the two US studies as pivotal trials IP631-003 and IP631-005.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Results

Zinner's coprimary endpoints improved at week 12: daily toilet voids fell by 2.37 with trospium versus 1.29 with placebo (between-arm change difference 1.08 fewer voids), and urge incontinence episodes fell by 59.0% versus 44.2% (14.8 percentage points greater reduction; both P ≤0.0001). Both effects were present by week 1. At week 12, voided volume rose by 32.1 versus 7.7 mL, nocturnal voids fell by 0.47 versus 0.29 per day, and 21% versus 11% were completely dry. Urgency severity and daytime frequency also decreased; the nocturnal difference first reached significance at week 4. Total Incontinence Impact Questionnaire score improved by 54.0 versus 36.0 points at week 12 (P ≤0.05), with a significant difference in women but not men.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Rudy's primary endpoint improved at week 12: daily toilet voids fell by 2.67 with trospium versus 1.76 with placebo (between-arm change difference 0.91 fewer voids; P &lt;0.0001). Daily urge urinary incontinence episodes fell by 1.86 versus 1.29 (P &lt;0.0001), nocturnal voids by 0.57 versus 0.29 (P = 0.0026), and volume per void rose by 35.59 versus 9.44 mL (P &lt;0.0001). Urgency severity and urge frequency also decreased; most symptom effects appeared by week 1.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Dry mouth occurred in 57/262 (21.8%) trospium versus 17/261 (6.5%) placebo recipients in Zinner's study; constipation occurred in 25/262 (9.5%) versus 10/261 (3.8%). Adverse events led to discontinuation in 8.8% versus 5.7%.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Rudy reported dry mouth in 65/329 (19.8%) versus 17/329 (5.2%), constipation in 36/329 (10.9%) versus 19/329 (5.8%), and urinary tract infection in 16/329 (4.9%) versus 8/329 (2.4%). Adverse events led to discontinuation in 7.3% versus 4.6%; urinary retention was described as comparable between groups without counts.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The 12-week placebo comparisons left active-comparator efficacy and longer-term outcomes unmeasured.

## In conversation

<InConversation id="trospium-pivotal" />

The two studies used the same trospium dose and duration, but Zinner specified two coprimary symptom counts whereas Rudy specified daily toilet voids as the single primary endpoint.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The FDA approved Sanctura (trospium chloride) 20 mg tablets for overactive bladder with urge urinary incontinence, urgency, and urinary frequency in 2004.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
