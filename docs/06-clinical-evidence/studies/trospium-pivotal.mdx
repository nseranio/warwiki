# Trospium pivotal trials: Immediate-Release Trospium Chloride for Overactive Bladder

<TrialBottomLine id="trospium-pivotal" />

**Trospium pivotal trials** comprised two multicenter, double-blind phase III randomized studies of oral trospium chloride 20 mg twice daily versus placebo for 12 weeks.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In Zinner's 523 patients with overactive bladder and urge incontinence, both daily toilet voids and urge incontinence episodes decreased more with trospium.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="trospium-pivotal" />

## Design

Zinner and colleagues enrolled patients at 51 sites and specified two coprimary diary endpoints: change in average toilet voids and urge incontinence episodes per 24 hours. Secondary measures included voided volume, urgency severity, day and night frequency, time to onset, and the Incontinence Impact Questionnaire. Patients were randomized 1:1 to trospium or placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Rudy and colleagues randomized 658 patients at 52 sites. Their sole primary endpoint was change in average toilet voids per 24 hours; urge urinary incontinence episodes were secondary.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Rudy's cohort was independent of Zinner's.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The FDA medical review identifies the two US studies as pivotal trials IP631-003 and IP631-005.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Results

In Zinner's study, symptom effects were present by week 1 and persisted through week 12. Voided volume increased and urgency severity and daytime frequency decreased; nocturnal frequency decreased by week 4, and Incontinence Impact Questionnaire scores improved at week 12.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In Rudy's study, daily toilet voids, urgency severity, urge frequency, and urge urinary incontinence episodes decreased, while volume per void increased at weeks 1, 4, and 12.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

The Rudy report names dry mouth and constipation among adverse events.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The 12-week placebo comparisons left active-comparator efficacy and longer-term outcomes unmeasured.

## In conversation

<InConversation id="trospium-pivotal" />

The two studies used the same trospium dose and duration, but Zinner specified two coprimary symptom counts whereas Rudy specified daily toilet voids as the single primary endpoint.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The FDA approved Sanctura (trospium chloride) 20 mg tablets for overactive bladder with urge urinary incontinence, urgency, and urinary frequency in 2004.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
