# SYNERGY: Solifenacin Plus Mirabegron for Wet Overactive Bladder

<TrialBottomLine id="synergy" />

**SYNERGY** was a multinational, double-blind, six-arm superiority trial that randomized 3,527 adults with wet overactive bladder to solifenacin 5 mg plus mirabegron 25 or 50 mg, either monotherapy, or placebo for 12 weeks. The 5 mg plus 50 mg combination improved daily incontinence versus solifenacin alone, but the comparison with mirabegron 50 mg missed the prespecified threshold and stopped confirmatory testing of the remaining endpoints.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="synergy" />

## Design

Enrollment at 435 sites in 42 countries followed a four-week single-blind placebo run-in. Participants had at least three months of urgency, frequency, and urinary incontinence, with at least eight micturitions and one grade 3 or 4 urgency episode daily, plus at least three incontinence episodes over a seven-day electronic diary. Mixed stress and urgency incontinence was eligible when urgency incontinence predominated. At baseline, 2,223/3,398 (65.4%) safety-set participants had urgency incontinence alone; 1,577/3,398 (46.4%) had used OAB medication previously.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The co-primary outcomes were change from baseline to end of treatment in daily incontinence episodes and micturitions. The first multiplicity-controlled step tested solifenacin 5 mg plus mirabegron 50 mg against both constituent monotherapies for daily incontinence. Only after that step could daily micturitions and mean voided volume be formally tested. Incontinence P values came from stratified rank ANCOVA, while the reported mean-difference confidence intervals came from a parametric ANCOVA; these methods can yield a P value below 0.05 with an interval crossing zero.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At 12 weeks, the adjusted mean difference in daily incontinence change for solifenacin 5 mg plus mirabegron 50 mg was −0.20 episodes versus solifenacin 5 mg (95% CI −0.44 to 0.04; rank-based P = 0.033) and −0.23 versus mirabegron 50 mg (95% CI −0.47 to 0.01; P = 0.052). The first comparison met its test; the second did not. The confirmatory sequence then stopped. Frequency comparisons were descriptive: placebo-adjusted changes were −0.95 micturitions/day for the 5 mg plus 50 mg combination, −0.85 for the 5 mg plus 25 mg combination, −0.56 for solifenacin 5 mg, −0.39 for mirabegron 50 mg, and −0.36 for mirabegron 25 mg.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the secondary responder analysis, zero incontinence episodes during the last three diary days occurred in 426/816 (52.2%) with solifenacin 5 mg plus mirabegron 50 mg, 417/823 (50.7%) with solifenacin 5 mg plus mirabegron 25 mg, 177/413 (42.9%) with solifenacin 5 mg, 188/406 (46.3%) with mirabegron 50 mg, 166/409 (40.6%) with mirabegron 25 mg, and 155/412 (37.6%) with placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The separate SYNERGY patient-reported-outcomes analysis found greater improvement in OAB-q symptom bother for both combinations than for the monotherapies (nominal P &lt; 0.001) and better health-related quality-of-life total scores (P ≤ 0.002) at the end of treatment.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Participants took their assigned oral regimen once daily for 12 double-blind weeks, followed by a two-week placebo run-out. Of 3,527 randomized participants, 3,494 received double-blind medication; 3,398 entered the safety set and 3,308 the full analysis set. Ninety-six treated participants from one site were excluded from both analysis sets for protocol noncompliance.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

In the safety set, treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 314/848 (37.0%) on solifenacin 5 mg plus mirabegron 50 mg, 345/853 (40.4%) on the lower-dose combination, 149/423 (35.2%) on solifenacin 5 mg, 147/422 (34.8%) on mirabegron 50 mg, 135/423 (31.9%) on mirabegron 25 mg, and 145/429 (33.8%) on placebo. Urinary retention under the sponsor-defined term set occurred in 10/848 (1.2%) and 8/853 (0.9%) in the combination groups versus 3/423 (0.7%) with solifenacin and none with mirabegron or placebo. Four retention events required catheterization, two in each combination group.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Urinary tract infection occurred in 44/848 (5.2%) with the 5 mg plus 50 mg combination and 60/853 (7.0%) with the 5 mg plus 25 mg combination, versus 21/423 (5.0%) with solifenacin 5 mg, 16/422 (3.8%) with mirabegron 50 mg, 18/423 (4.3%) with mirabegron 25 mg, and 21/429 (4.9%) with placebo. Dry mouth was reported in 61/848 (7.2%) and 74/853 (8.7%) in the combination groups; constipation in 31/848 (3.7%) and 38/853 (4.5%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the cardiovascular analysis, a nonfatal myocardial infarction occurred in 1/853 (0.1%) participants on the lower-dose combination and nonfatal strokes in 2/848 (0.2%) on the higher-dose combination; no major cardiovascular events were reported in the other arms. Mean heart rate rose by about one beat/minute in mirabegron groups, with no clinically meaningful between-group QTcF difference.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Limitations

Treatment lasted 12 weeks, and the trial enrolled patients with wet OAB after a placebo run-in. Fewer than half had received prior OAB medication. The stopped testing sequence leaves the frequency, voided-volume, and lower-dose-combination comparisons descriptive despite favorable nominal results. The protocol-noncompliant site's exclusion reduced the treated safety set by 96 participants.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="synergy" />

BESIDE tested add-on mirabegron after persistent incontinence on solifenacin; SYNERGY enrolled a broader wet-OAB population. SYNERGY II was a separate 12-month randomized study of solifenacin 5 mg plus mirabegron 50 mg versus either monotherapy, with fewer daily incontinence episodes and micturitions in the combination group at its end-of-treatment assessment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA/SUFU 2024 idiopathic OAB guideline permits a medication from a different class to be added when a single OAB drug gives inadequate improvement <Rec>(Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>. The panel cites SYNERGY and BESIDE in its discussion of this statement.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
