# Cyclosporine A versus pentosan polysulfate: Oral treatment for interstitial cystitis

<TrialBottomLine id="cyclosporine-ic" />

**Sairanen 2005** found more patients halved their daily micturition frequency after six months of cyclosporine A than pentosan polysulfate sodium in a randomized comparison of patients meeting National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases criteria for interstitial cystitis.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="cyclosporine-ic" />

## Design

The open-label comparison enrolled 64 patients, including 53 women and 11 men, with 32 assigned to each arm; 5 cyclosporine recipients and 10 pentosan polysulfate recipients had Hunner's ulcers. Cyclosporine A was given at 1.5 mg/kg twice daily and pentosan polysulfate sodium at 100 mg three times daily. The primary endpoint was the proportion whose daily micturition frequency fell by at least half, tested at a two-sided P &lt; 0.05 threshold. The report also measured mean frequency change; specified secondary endpoints included voided volume, nocturia, O'Leary-Sant symptom and problem indexes, pain, and subjective global response. Global response counted categories 4–6 (moderately better through completely cured) on a six-point scale.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Cyclosporine met the primary endpoint: 11/32 patients (34%) halved daily micturition frequency versus 0/32 with pentosan polysulfate (P &lt; 0.001). Mean change in micturitions per 24 hours was −6.7 ± 4.7 versus −2.0 ± 5.1, a calculated difference of −4.7 (P &lt; 0.001). Global response was 24/32 (75%) versus 6/32 (19%; P &lt; 0.001). Nocturia episodes fell by 2.2 ± 1.6 versus 0.2 ± 2.1 per night, and pain VAS scores fell by 4.7 ± 3.5 versus 1.6 ± 3.3 cm (both P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In a later quality-of-life analysis of this oral-treatment cohort, cyclosporine had a greater effect on emotional well-being, social functioning, days of activity limitation, pain, and physical capacity than pentosan polysulfate (P &lt; 0.05). That report also analyzed a separate randomized intravesical-treatment cohort.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Both arms had 29 of 32 patients complete six-month follow-up. Cyclosporine's starting dose was halved in 4/32 patients for blood-pressure, creatinine, or other adverse effects; 3/32 discontinued within one month. In the pentosan polysulfate arm, 3/32 discontinued treatment. Blood pressure and serum creatinine were checked monthly during cyclosporine treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Adverse events occurred in 30/32 cyclosporine recipients versus 18/32 pentosan polysulfate recipients. The study's worst-event severity scale recorded mild events in 23/32 versus 14/32, moderate in 4/32 versus 3/32, and significant in 3/32 versus 1/32; these were not Clavien-Dindo grades. Two cyclosporine recipients had clinically significant diastolic blood-pressure increases, and one had a creatinine rise that persisted despite dose reduction and normalized after stopping treatment. One pentosan polysulfate recipient withdrew after gross hematuria.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The open-label active-comparator design provides no placebo estimate, and follow-up ended at six months. The small cohort limits precision for adverse-event comparisons.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA's 2022 IC/BPS amendment cites this trial and says oral cyclosporine A may be offered particularly for patients with Hunner lesions refractory to fulguration and/or triamcinolone <Rec>(Option; Evidence Strength: Grade C)</Rec>. It calls for close renal-function and blood-pressure monitoring during treatment.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
