# Cyclosporine A versus pentosan polysulfate: Oral treatment for interstitial cystitis

<TrialBottomLine id="cyclosporine-ic" />

**Sairanen 2005** randomized patients meeting National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases criteria for interstitial cystitis to oral cyclosporine A or pentosan polysulfate sodium; cyclosporine produced a greater reduction in daily micturition frequency after six months.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="cyclosporine-ic" />

## Design

The comparison enrolled 64 patients, including 53 women and 11 men, with 32 assigned to each arm. Cyclosporine A was given at 1.5 mg/kg twice daily and pentosan polysulfate sodium at 100 mg three times daily. Daily micturition frequency was the primary endpoint. Secondary measures included voided volume, nocturia, O'Leary-Sant symptom and problem indexes, pain, and subjective global response.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Mean change in micturitions per 24 hours was −6.7 ± 4.7 with cyclosporine and −2.0 ± 5.1 with pentosan polysulfate, a calculated difference of −4.7 per 24 hours. The primary report described this comparison as statistically significant. Global response was 75% versus 19% (P &lt; 0.001); the published abstract did not give responder counts.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In a later quality-of-life analysis of this oral-treatment cohort, cyclosporine had a greater effect on emotional well-being, social functioning, days of activity limitation, pain, and physical capacity than pentosan polysulfate (P &lt; 0.05). That report also analyzed a separate randomized intravesical-treatment cohort.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Both arms had 29 of 32 patients complete six-month follow-up.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

The cyclosporine arm had more adverse events than the pentosan polysulfate arm. The primary abstract does not provide event counts or categories.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The active-comparator design provides no placebo estimate, and follow-up ended at six months. The small cohort limits precision for adverse-event comparisons.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA's 2022 IC/BPS amendment cites this trial and says oral cyclosporine A may be offered particularly for patients with Hunner lesions refractory to fulguration and/or triamcinolone <Rec>(Option; Evidence Strength: Grade C)</Rec>. It calls for close renal-function and blood-pressure monitoring during treatment.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
