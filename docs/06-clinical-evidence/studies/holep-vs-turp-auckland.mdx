# HoLEP versus TURP (Tauranga): Holmium Enucleation and Transurethral Resection for Larger Obstructing Prostates

<TrialBottomLine id="holep-vs-turp-auckland" />

**HoLEP versus TURP (Tauranga)** was a single-center randomized trial comparing holmium laser enucleation with transurethral resection in men with urodynamically confirmed obstruction and 40–200 g prostates; AUA symptom score, quality of life, and maximum urinary flow did not differ significantly at 24 months, while six-month pressure-flow measures favored HoLEP.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="holep-vs-turp-auckland" />

## Design

Tauranga Hospital enrolled 61 men; one assigned to HoLEP died before treatment, leaving 30 treated in each arm. Eligibility required a maximum flow of 15 mL/s or less, AUA symptom score of at least 8, postvoid residual below 400 mL, and Schaffer obstruction grade of at least 2. Men with a catheter or previous urethral or prostatic surgery were excluded. Allocation used sequential envelopes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The prespecified primary measures were AUA symptom score, a single-question quality-of-life score, and maximum flow at serial visits through 24 months. The authors compared groups using the Mann–Whitney U test; they did not specify a noninferiority margin. Six-month pressure-flow measurements, residual volume, continence, potency, recatheterization, and reoperation were secondary measures.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At 24 months, 48 of the 60 treated men were assessed: 22 after HoLEP and 26 after TURP. Mean AUA scores were 6.1 and 5.2, quality-of-life scores were 1.25 and 1.25, and maximum flows were 21.0 and 19.3 mL/s, respectively; none of these primary comparisons was significant.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

At six months, mean detrusor pressure at maximum flow was 20.8 versus 40.7 cm H2O, and mean residual prostate volume by ultrasound was 28.4 versus 46.6 mL (P &lt; 0.001 for each comparison), both favoring HoLEP.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

At a mean 7.6 years, 31 of the original 61 men were available: 14 after HoLEP and 17 after TURP. Mean AUA scores were 8.0 ± 5.2 and 10.3 ± 7.42; maximum flows were 22.09 ± 15.47 and 17.83 ± 8.61 mL/s. The follow-up report found no significant difference in these measures beyond the first year.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Two surgeons performed HoLEP with a holmium laser set to a maximum of 100 W and a Versacut morcellator. Three surgeons performed TURP with a tungsten loop at 160 W cutting and 80 W coagulation; each had performed more than 500 TURPs. The HoLEP group had shorter catheterization and hospital stay, more tissue retrieved, and longer operations. Most HoLEP patients had a standard Foley catheter normally removed the next day; TURP patients had an irrigating catheter removed after hematuria settled.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Through 24 months, recatheterization occurred in 5/30 HoLEP and 4/30 TURP patients; transfusion in 0/30 and 1/30; urethral stricture in 1/30 and 3/30; documented urinary infection in 0/30 and 2/30; and reoperation in 0/30 and 2/30. The HoLEP stricture was submeatal; the TURP strictures were two submeatal and one bulbar. Two patients in each arm reported new erectile dysfunction at 24 months. Retrograde ejaculation was reported by 12/16 assessed HoLEP patients and 8/13 assessed TURP patients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In the seven-year assessable subset, reoperation for recurrent BPH had occurred in 0/14 HoLEP patients and 3/17 TURP patients.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

Only 48 of 60 treated men completed the 24-month assessment. At long-term follow-up, 12 of the original 61 had died and 18 were lost; the retreatment comparison uses the remaining 14 and 17 patients. The trial used a small, single-center surgical cohort and no prespecified equivalence or noninferiority margin. Continence and potency questions preceded standardized questionnaires.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

- **AUA, 2026.** Educate men considering BPH procedures about possible changes in orgasmic, erectile, and ejaculatory function <Rec>(Clinical Principle)</Rec>; the supporting discussion cites the two-year report. The guideline also recommends offering HoLEP or ThuLEP as an option for LUTS/BPH <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade A)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **EAU, 2026.** Offer HoLEP to men with moderate-to-severe LUTS and benign prostatic obstruction as an alternative to TURP or open prostatectomy <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>. Its evidence discussion cites the seven-year report for comparable long-term functional results.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
