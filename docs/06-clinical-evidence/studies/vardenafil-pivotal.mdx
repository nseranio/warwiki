# Vardenafil North American Pivotal Trial: As-Needed Vardenafil for Erectile Dysfunction

<TrialBottomLine id="vardenafil-pivotal" />

**Vardenafil North American Pivotal Trial** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized trial of as-needed vardenafil 5, 10, or 20 mg versus placebo in 805 men with erectile dysfunction; all three doses improved the prespecified week-12 erectile-function, penetration, and intercourse-completion measures at the multiplicity-adjusted threshold.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="vardenafil-pivotal" />

## Design

At 54 US and Canadian centers, eligible men had erectile dysfunction for more than six months and failed to maintain an erection through intercourse on at least half of four attempts during a treatment-free baseline period. The trial excluded men with penile anatomic abnormalities impairing erections, radical prostatectomy, spinal cord injury–related erectile dysfunction, prior nonresponse to sildenafil, nitrate use, or unstable cardiovascular disease. Men with previously effective sildenafil treatment could enroll.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The three prespecified primary measures at week 12 were the International Index of Erectile Function (IIEF) erectile-function domain score and diary-based mean per-patient success rates for penetration and for erection maintenance through intercourse. Each dose was compared with placebo using a Bonferroni-adjusted significance threshold of P &lt; 0.0167. The IIEF analysis used last observation carried forward for missing later measurements.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At week 12, mean IIEF erectile-function scores were 18.4, 20.6, and 21.4 with vardenafil 5, 10, and 20 mg, respectively, versus 15.0 with placebo (P &lt; 0.0001 for each dose). Mean per-patient penetration success rates were 65.5%, 75.5%, and 80.5% versus 51.7%; erection-maintenance success rates were 50.6%, 64.5%, and 64.5% versus 32.2% (each dose P &lt; 0.0001 versus placebo). These diary percentages average each man's success rate, rather than counting all attempts together.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

At week 12, modeled yes-response proportions for the secondary question about improved erections in the intention-to-treat population were 64.5%, 72.9%, and 80.9% with vardenafil 5, 10, and 20 mg, respectively, versus 38.6% with placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The 26-week overall analysis favored all three doses over placebo for erectile-function scores, penetration, and intercourse completion (P &lt; 0.001 for each dose). Least-squares erectile-function scores with last observation carried forward were 17.79, 21.2, and 21.83 versus 14.84; mean per-patient penetration rates were 66%, 76%, and 81% versus 52%; and intercourse-completion rates were 52%, 65%, and 67% versus 33%.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Penetration improved significantly at weeks 4 and 8 with 5 mg and at every visit with 10 or 20 mg. The separate week-26 erectile-function comparison for 5 mg had a reported nominal P &lt; 0.0385.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

A secondary analysis of the same cohort reported satisfaction with erection hardness of 38%, 52%, and 58% for 5, 10, and 20 mg versus 18% with placebo; satisfaction with the overall sexual experience was 45%, 58%, and 62% versus 23%.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> In a later post hoc diary analysis, the cumulative probability of at least one successful penetration by the third valid medicated attempt was 82%, 88%, and 85% versus 68% with placebo; corresponding probabilities for maintaining an erection through intercourse were 66%, 81%, and 77% versus 53%.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Participants took one assigned dose approximately one hour before intended intercourse, with no more than one dose per day, during up to 26 weeks of blinded treatment. Of 805 randomized men, 762 entered the safety analysis and 749 the efficacy analysis. By week 26, discontinuation was 38%, 27%, and 30% in the 5-, 10-, and 20-mg groups, versus 54% with placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

Insufficient therapeutic effect prompted discontinuation in 26/205 (13%) at 5 mg and 20% of 197 assigned placebo.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

In the safety population, headache occurred in 19/193 (10%), 44/199 (22%), and 40/188 (21%) vardenafil recipients versus 8/182 (4%) placebo recipients. Rhinitis occurred in 17/193 (9%), 27/199 (14%), and 32/188 (17%) versus 9/182 (5%); flushing in 9/193 (5%), 20/199 (10%), and 24/188 (13%) versus 0/182 (0%); and dyspepsia in 2/193 (1%), 8/199 (4%), and 12/188 (6%) versus 1/182 (&lt;1%). Serious treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 5%, 3%, and 4% of the active-dose groups versus 5% with placebo. One myocardial infarction occurred in the placebo arm; no deaths were reported.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Adverse events led to discontinuation in 15/197 (8%) men assigned 20 mg; headache, flushing, and dyspepsia were reported more often in the first four weeks and generally decreased over treatment.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The trial excluded prior sildenafil nonresponders, men after radical prostatectomy, men with spinal cord injury–related erectile dysfunction, and several cardiovascular risk groups. Placebo discontinuation was higher than active-treatment discontinuation, and the erectile-function analysis used last observation carried forward. The later attempt-by-attempt analysis was post hoc and used valid diary entries, which might omit an earlier actual attempt.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA erectile dysfunction guideline (2018) says men with erectile dysfunction should be informed about FDA-approved oral PDE5 inhibitors, including benefits and risks/burdens, unless contraindicated <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>. It recommends instructions to maximize efficacy <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C)</Rec> and dose titration for optimal efficacy <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

The FDA approved Levitra (vardenafil hydrochloride film-coated tablets) for erectile dysfunction on August 19, 2003, under NDA 21-400.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
