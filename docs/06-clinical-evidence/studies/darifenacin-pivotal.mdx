# Darifenacin pivotal trial: Once-Daily Darifenacin for Overactive Bladder

<TrialBottomLine id="darifenacin-pivotal" />

**The darifenacin pivotal trial** was a multicenter, double-blind randomized comparison of once-daily darifenacin and placebo in 561 patients with overactive bladder; at 12 weeks, the 7.5 mg and 15 mg doses produced greater reductions in weekly incontinence episodes than placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="darifenacin-pivotal" />

## Design

Participants had symptoms for more than 6 months; some had previously used antimuscarinics. After washout and a 2-week placebo run-in, they received controlled-release darifenacin 3.75 mg, 7.5 mg or 15 mg, or matching placebo. The 3.75 mg group served as a null-dose arm without formal sample sizing or statistical analysis. Electronic diaries recorded incontinence episodes, voiding frequency and volume, urgency, clothing or pad changes, and nocturnal awakenings before visits at weeks 2, 6 and 12.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At week 12, weekly incontinence episodes had fallen from baseline by 67.7% with 7.5 mg and 72.8% with 15 mg, versus 55.9% with placebo (P = 0.010 and P = 0.017, respectively). Both tested doses also improved micturition frequency, mean volume voided, urgency frequency and severity, and incontinence requiring a clothing or pad change. Nocturnal awakenings due to overactive bladder did not decline significantly.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Dry mouth and constipation were generally mild to moderate. No patient withdrew because of dry mouth; constipation-related discontinuation was 0.9% across darifenacin groups and 0.6% with placebo. No blurred vision was reported. The authors described CNS and cardiac safety profiles as comparable to placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Follow-up ended at 12 weeks. The 3.75 mg group was not formally sized or analyzed.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
