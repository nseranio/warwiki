# Hooton Water Intake: Additional Daily Water for Recurrent Cystitis Prevention

<TrialBottomLine id="hooton-water" />

**Hooton water intake** was a single-center, open-label randomized trial in 140 healthy premenopausal women with recurrent cystitis and low baseline fluid intake: adding 1.5 L of water daily to usual intake, versus continuing usual intake alone, reduced mean culture-confirmed episodes over 12 months from 3.2 to 1.7 per woman (control-minus-water difference 1.5, 95% CI 1.2–1.8; P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="hooton-water" />

## Design

At a clinical research center in Sofia, Bulgaria, investigators enrolled women with at least three clinician-attended cystitis episodes in the preceding year, including one culture-confirmed episode. Entry required self-reported fluid intake below 1.5 L/day and a 24-hour urine volume below 1.2 L; a later protocol amendment added urine osmolality of at least 500 mOsm/kg. Women with recent pyelonephritis, interstitial cystitis, symptomatic vulvovaginitis, or pregnancy were excluded. A central system concealed the 1:1 allocation until assignment. Participants and staff knew the assigned regimen.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The primary endpoint counted episodes with at least one cystitis symptom and at least 10³ CFU/mL of a uropathogen in a midstream culture. The primary intention-to-treat comparison used an unadjusted Poisson model and a two-sided significance threshold of 0.05. Secondary-outcome P values were nominal. Study staff called participants monthly to ask about symptoms, medication use, adverse events, and adherence; fluid diaries and 24-hour urine collections assessed intake and hydration.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The water group had 111 total cystitis episodes and the control group 216. Mean antimicrobial regimens used for cystitis were 1.9 versus 3.6 per woman (control-minus-water difference 1.7, 95% CI 1.3–2.1; nominal P &lt; 0.001). The mean interval between episodes was 142.8 versus 84.4 days (water-minus-control difference 58.4 days, 95% CI 39.4–77.4; nominal P &lt; 0.001). In a post-hoc analysis, median time to first cystitis episode was 148.0 versus 93.5 days (age-adjusted HR 0.51, 95% CI 0.36–0.74; nominal P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The intervention supplied three 500-mL bottles of water daily, in addition to usual beverages. Bottles were delivered to participants' homes every two weeks. Self-reported mean daily water intake in the intervention arm rose by 1.15 L at month 12; the control arm reported no change. Twelve-month follow-up was completed by 64/70 women (91%) assigned water and 66/70 (94%) controls.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Headache was reported by 12/70 women in each arm, and gastrointestinal symptoms by 8/70 in each arm. No serious adverse events occurred.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The trial was conducted at one center and could not blind participants to water intake. The intervention combined supplied bottled water with monthly adherence calls. The investigators did not test different water increments or follow participants beyond the intervention year. Danone Research, which sells the water supplied in the trial, funded the study and participated in its design and manuscript preparation; independent contract research organizations oversaw trial conduct and analyzed the primary and secondary outcomes.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

- **AUA/CUA/SUFU (2025 amendment), statement 16.** When women with recurrent UTI have water intake below 1.5 L/day, clinicians may offer increased water intake for prophylaxis <Rec>(Conditional Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C)</Rec>. The guideline cites this trial in its rationale.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **EAU Urological Infections (March 2026), recurrent cystitis section.** Advise premenopausal women regarding increased fluid intake because it might reduce recurrent cystitis <Rec>(Weak)</Rec>. The guideline cites this trial.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
