# VUSIS-II: Value of Urodynamics Before Stress Urinary Incontinence Surgery

<TrialBottomLine id="vusis-ii" />

**VUSIS-II** embedded a noninferiority randomized trial in a Dutch diagnostic cohort of women with predominant stress urinary incontinence whose urodynamic findings disagreed with their clinical assessment. Immediate midurethral sling surgery was noninferior to urodynamics-guided tailored therapy for improvement in the Urogenital Distress Inventory at 12 months: 44 versus 39 points, with a prespecified five-point margin.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="vusis-ii" />

## Design

Women were eligible for continence surgery on clinical assessment, then all underwent urodynamics. Discordance included absent demonstrable stress leakage, detrusor overactivity, weak bladder contractility or flow, elevated residual urine, outflow obstruction, or abnormal bladder capacity, sensation, or compliance. Among 578 women studied, 268 had discordant findings; 126 consented to randomization, with 64 assigned immediate surgery and 62 assigned individualized treatment based on the test findings.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The prespecified primary analysis compared mean 12-month Urogenital Distress Inventory improvement by intention to treat. A difference in mean improvement of five points or less was the noninferiority threshold. This endpoint measured overall urinary symptom distress, rather than an objective stress test.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Mean improvement was 44 points (SD 24) after immediate surgery and 39 points (SD 25) after tailored therapy. The mean difference was five points in favor of immediate surgery; its reported one-sided 95% confidence interval ran from negative infinity to five points. The prespecified noninferiority criterion was met. Cure did not differ between groups.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

In a later retrospective review of 301 operated participants' records, urinary incontinence symptoms were documented in 71/301 (23.6%). Stress incontinence was documented in 38/301 (12.6%), comprising 18/301 (6.0%) persistent and 20/301 (6.6%) recurrent cases. Urgency incontinence appeared in 51/301 (16.9%), including 18 women with mixed symptoms. These figures pool women from the original cohort and do not compare randomized assignments.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The immediate-surgery strategy used a midurethral sling. The original protocol permitted retropubic or transobturator placement at the surgeon's discretion and excluded single-incision slings; the later reviewed cohort included 88 retropubic and 213 transobturator procedures. Tailored treatment could also include sling surgery. Among women with recorded postoperative incontinence, 42/71 (59.2%) received additional treatment, and 5/301 (1.7%) of the reviewed cohort underwent another sling placement.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Complication rates did not differ between randomized strategies. In the retrospective operated cohort, tape exposure was documented in 7/301 (2.3%), and obstructive voiding symptoms, including retention, in 22/301 (7.3%).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Five tape-exposure excisions and nine tape transections were recorded; the investigators reported 17 sling-related repeat surgeries overall.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

Randomization applied only to women with discordant tests who consented. The retrospective review analyzed records from 12 of the original 30 hospitals. Median time from surgery to chart review was 7.8 years, but the median last recorded clinical visit was two months after surgery; incontinence and complications depended on what appeared in those records.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

VALUE randomized women before testing to office assessment alone or office assessment plus urodynamics. VUSIS-II tested the treatment decision after all women had urodynamics, randomizing only those with discordant findings.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU 2026 female lower urinary tract symptoms guideline cites VUSIS-II and advises against routine urodynamics when offering treatment for uncomplicated stress urinary incontinence <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>. It recommends urodynamics when findings may change the choice of invasive treatment or when the diagnosis is uncertain <Rec>(Weak)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
