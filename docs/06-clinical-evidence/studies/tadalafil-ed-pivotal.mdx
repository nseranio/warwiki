# Tadalafil ED integrated trials: Placebo-Controlled Studies of As-Needed Tadalafil

<TrialBottomLine id="tadalafil-ed-pivotal" />

The Brock integrated analysis pooled five double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trials in 1,112 men with erectile dysfunction; as-needed tadalafil improved the erectile-function measures over 12 weeks, and 81% of men given 20 mg reported improved erections versus 35% given placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="tadalafil-ed-pivotal" />

## Design

The constituent trials enrolled men with mild to severe erectile dysfunction of varied etiologies. The three coprimary measures were change in the International Index of Erectile Function erectile-function domain and affirmative responses to Sexual Encounter Profile questions 2 (penetration) and 3 (successful intercourse). The Global Assessment Question asked whether erections had improved. Treatment was assigned at a fixed dose, without food or alcohol restrictions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

With 20-mg tadalafil, mean erectile-function domain score rose 7.9 points from baseline (p &lt; 0.001 versus placebo). The report describes significant improvement in both Sexual Encounter Profile measures; at 20 mg, 75% of intercourse attempts were completed successfully (p &lt; 0.001 versus placebo). The Global Assessment Question favored tadalafil, 81% versus 35% (p &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

An expanded integrated analysis reported 2,102 men across eleven placebo-controlled trials. Mean erectile-function domain improvement was 6.5 points with 10 mg and 8.6 points with 20 mg (both p &lt; 0.001 versus placebo). Successful intercourse attempts occurred in 58% and 68% of attempts, respectively, versus 31% with placebo. Improved erections were reported by 71% and 84% versus 33%.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The initial program used fixed, as-needed tadalafil doses of 2.5, 5, 10, or 20 mg, no more than once daily.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The expanded analysis evaluated the 10-mg and 20-mg as-needed doses.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Headache and dyspepsia were the most frequent adverse events in the initial analysis. The expanded analysis also listed back pain and myalgia among the most frequent events.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The constituent trials used placebo controls and fixed doses, limiting direct assessment of dose titration and comparison with another oral PDE5 inhibitor.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA erectile dysfunction guideline (2018) cites the Brock analysis and recommends informing men about FDA-approved oral PDE5 inhibitors, including benefits and risks, unless contraindicated <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>. It recommends titrating the prescribed PDE5-inhibitor dose for optimal efficacy <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
