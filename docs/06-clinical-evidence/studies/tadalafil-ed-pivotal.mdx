# Tadalafil ED integrated trials: Placebo-Controlled Studies of As-Needed Tadalafil

<TrialBottomLine id="tadalafil-ed-pivotal" />

The Brock integrated analysis pooled five double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trials in 1,112 men with erectile dysfunction; as-needed tadalafil improved all three co-primary erectile-function measures over 12 weeks. In the four trials with Global Assessment Question data, 81% of men given 20 mg (n=165) reported improved erections versus 35% given placebo (n=261).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="tadalafil-ed-pivotal" />

## Design

The constituent trials enrolled men with mild to severe erectile dysfunction of varied etiologies who made at least four intercourse attempts during a four-week treatment-free run-in. Men who failed to achieve an erection after radical prostatectomy or pelvic surgery were excluded. The three co-primary measures were mean change in the International Index of Erectile Function erectile-function domain and mean changes in affirmative responses to Sexual Encounter Profile questions 2 (penetration) and 3 (successful intercourse). The Global Assessment Question asked whether erections had improved. Efficacy analyses used men with baseline and at least one post-baseline measure; safety analyses included all randomized men. Treatment was assigned at a fixed dose, without food or alcohol restrictions.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At 20 mg, tadalafil improved all three co-primary outcomes versus placebo at 12 weeks (each pairwise p &lt; 0.001): mean erectile-function domain score changed by +7.9 versus +0.6 points (endpoints 23.9 versus 15.1); mean affirmative SEP-Q2 responses changed by +27 versus +2 percentage points (endpoints 80% versus 48%); and mean affirmative SEP-Q3 responses changed by +39 versus +6 percentage points (endpoints 70% versus 31%). In the secondary analysis of all intercourse attempts, 75% were completed successfully with 20 mg versus 32% with placebo. Normal erectile-function scores at endpoint occurred in 59% versus 11% (p &lt; 0.001). The four-study Global Assessment Question favored tadalafil, 81% (n=165) versus 35% (n=261; p &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

An expanded integrated analysis reported 2,102 men across eleven placebo-controlled trials. Mean erectile-function domain improvement was 6.5 points with 10 mg and 8.6 points with 20 mg (both p &lt; 0.001 versus placebo). Successful intercourse attempts occurred in 58% and 68% of attempts, respectively, versus 31% with placebo. Improved erections were reported by 71% and 84% versus 33%.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The initial program used fixed, as-needed tadalafil doses of 2.5, 5, 10, or 20 mg, no more than once daily; 995/1,112 men (89%) completed treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> At 20 mg, 73%–80% of intercourse attempts at selected intervals from 30 minutes to 36 hours after dosing were successful.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The expanded analysis evaluated the 10-mg and 20-mg as-needed doses.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Adverse events

Headache and dyspepsia were the most frequent adverse events in the initial analysis: with 20 mg, headache occurred in 55/258 men (21%) versus 19/308 (6%) with placebo, and dyspepsia in 45/258 (17%) versus 7/308 (2%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> More than one treatment-emergent adverse event was reported in 188/258 (73%) versus 159/308 (52%); adverse events led to discontinuation in 8/258 (3.1%) versus 4/308 (1.3%).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The expanded analysis also listed back pain and myalgia among the most frequent events.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Limitations

The constituent trials used placebo controls and fixed doses, limiting direct assessment of dose titration and comparison with another oral PDE5 inhibitor.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA erectile dysfunction guideline (2018) cites the Brock analysis and recommends informing men about FDA-approved oral PDE5 inhibitors, including benefits and risks, unless contraindicated <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>. It recommends titrating the prescribed PDE5-inhibitor dose for optimal efficacy <Rec>(Strong Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade B)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
