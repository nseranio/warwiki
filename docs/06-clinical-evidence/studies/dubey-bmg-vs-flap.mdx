# Dubey 2007: Dorsal Onlay Buccal Mucosa Versus Penile Skin Flap Urethroplasty

<TrialBottomLine id="dubey-bmg-vs-flap" />

**Dubey 2007** assigned 55 patients with inflammatory or idiopathic anterior urethral strictures by alternation to dorsal onlay buccal mucosa graft or penile skin flap urethroplasty. Recurrence requiring internal urethrotomy occurred in 3 of 27 and 4 of 28 patients, respectively, with longer operations and more penile morbidity after flap repair.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="dubey-bmg-vs-flap" />

## Design

Stricture sites included pendulous, bulbar, and bulbopendulous urethra; their distributions were comparable, and mean lengths were 5.6 cm (range 4–15) in the buccal group and 6.0 cm (4–12) in the flap group. Patients with balanitis xerotica obliterans, unhealthy penile skin, oral mucosal pathology, prior urethroplasty, or more than one prior dilation or internal urethrotomy were excluded. The study assigned alternating patients to buccal mucosa (27) or flap (28), defined failure as a subsequent urethral procedure, and did not designate a primary endpoint or prespecified significance threshold. The report assessed operative time, hospital stay, complications, recurrence, and patient satisfaction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

Recurrence requiring internal urethrotomy occurred in 3/27 buccal graft patients (11.1%) and 4/28 flap patients (14.3%) at median follow-up of 22.6 and 24.2 months, respectively; every patient completed at least 12 months. Table 4 reports success of 89.9% versus 85.8% (P &gt; 0.5), although the recurrence counts imply 24/27 (88.9%) versus 24/28 (85.7%) without retreatment. One buccal graft patient subsequently required penile flap urethroplasty. At six months, 24/27 buccal patients (88.9%; reported as 89%) and 18/28 flap patients (64.3%) would recommend the procedure (P = 0.001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Buccal repair used a two-team harvest and urethral approach, whereas flap repair used one team; both mobilized and rotated the urethra 180 degrees for a dorsal onlay. Buccal grafts came from one cheek in 10/27 patients, both cheeks in 11/27, and both cheeks plus lower lip in 6/27; flaps were longitudinal in 5/28 and circumpenile in 23/28. Mean operative time was 162 minutes (range 120–210) with buccal mucosa and 224 minutes (180–410) with a flap (P = 0.001). A voiding cystogram was obtained at three weeks; uroflowmetry and urethral calibration or cystoscopy followed at one month, every three months in year one and every six months thereafter.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Troublesome postvoid dribbling requiring manual compression occurred in 4/27 buccal graft patients (14.8%) and 9/28 flap patients (32.1%; P = 0.001). In the flap arm, 6/28 (21.4%) had superficial penile skin necrosis that healed within six weeks, 1/28 (3.6%) had extensive skin loss requiring grafting, 2/28 (7.1%) had mild penile torsion, and 3/28 (10.7%) had penile hypoesthesia without interference with intercourse. The report does not specify whether the extensive skin-loss case overlapped with superficial necrosis. Buccal repair was followed by minor oral morbidity in six patients in the prose, while Table 3 lists seven oral complications; symptoms settled within four weeks. Hematoma occurred in 2/27 buccal and 2/28 flap patients, wound infection in 1/27 and 0/28, and three-week cystogram extravasation requiring another week of catheterization in 1/27 and 2/28.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Allocation used alternation; blinding and a prespecified primary endpoint or significance threshold were not reported. The printed success percentages do not reconcile with the recurrence counts, and the prose and Table 3 differ on the oral morbidity count.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The EAU 2026 urethral stricture guideline cites this trial in its graft-versus-flap comparison and recommends a graft when both are equally indicated <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
