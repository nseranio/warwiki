# Nickel PPS dose trial: Oral Pentosan Polysulfate Versus Placebo in IC/BPS

<TrialBottomLine id="pps-dose-nickel" />

This multicenter, double-blind randomized trial compared pentosan polysulfate sodium (PPS) 100 mg once daily, 100 mg three times daily, and placebo in 369 adults with IC/BPS symptoms (368 treated and analyzed); neither active dose showed a superior study-end ICSI responder rate over placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="pps-dose-nickel" />

## Design

Eligibility required IC/BPS symptoms for at least six months, an Interstitial Cystitis Symptom Index (ICSI) total score of at least 8, a score above zero on each of its four items, and at least 10 daily voids including nocturia. Cystoscopic findings did not determine eligibility. A responder had at least a 30% reduction in total ICSI score from baseline at study end. ICSI assessments were scheduled at baseline and weeks 4, 8, 12, 18, and 24; the ITT analysis carried the last observed score forward for participants who withdrew before week 24.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The prespecified ICSI responder threshold was reached by 51/128 (39.8%) patients treated with once-daily PPS, 52/122 (42.6%) with three-times-daily PPS, and 48/118 (40.7%) with placebo. Neither active-to-placebo comparison nor the comparison between active doses was statistically significant. The first sequential comparison, three-times-daily PPS versus placebo, crossed the interim futility boundary (P ≥ 0.08).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Patient-rated moderate or marked global improvement, a secondary endpoint, occurred in 43/128 (33.6%) once-daily PPS recipients, 44/122 (36.1%) three-times-daily PPS recipients, and 37/118 (31.4%) placebo recipients, without a statistically significant between-group difference.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Participants received oral PPS 100 mg once daily or three times daily, or matching placebo, for 24 weeks. An unblinded interim analysis after six years, when 369/645 (57.2%) of the planned patients had been randomized, led to early trial termination. Treatment was completed by 74/128 (57.8%) once-daily PPS recipients, 69/122 (56.6%) three-times-daily PPS recipients, and 63/118 (53.4%) placebo recipients.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Adverse events led to withdrawal in 17/128 (13.3%) once-daily PPS recipients, 14/122 (11.5%) three-times-daily PPS recipients, and 12/118 (10.2%) placebo recipients. Serious adverse events occurred in 4/128 (3.1%), 0/122 (0%), and 4/118 (3.4%), respectively; one placebo recipient had urinary retention, and urinary tract infection was among the serious events in the once-daily group. Treatment-emergent urinary tract infection occurred in 10/128 (7.8%), 8/122 (6.6%), and 4/118 (3.4%), respectively.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Early termination reduced the planned sample size, and 162/368 (44.0%) treated participants withdrew. Enrollment based on symptoms without cystoscopic criteria leaves the treatment effect in lesion-defined subgroups unresolved.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="pps-dose-nickel" />

The earlier PPS pivotal trial reported greater patient-rated global improvement with PPS than placebo; Nickel used an ICSI reduction threshold and assessed treatment through 24 weeks.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA IC/BPS guideline, amended in 2022, includes this trial in its PPS evidence review and lists PPS among oral medications that may be administered <Rec>(Option (Evidence Strength: Grades B, B, C, and B))</Rec>. It directs counseling about potential macular damage and vision-related injuries before PPS use <Rec>(Clinical Principle)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The EAU chronic pelvic pain guideline (2026) cites this report and recommends offering oral pentosane polysulphate for primary bladder pain syndrome <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
