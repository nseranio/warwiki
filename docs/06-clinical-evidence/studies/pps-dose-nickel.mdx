# Nickel PPS dose trial: Oral Pentosan Polysulfate Versus Placebo in IC/BPS

<TrialBottomLine id="pps-dose-nickel" />

This multicenter, double-blind randomized trial compared pentosan polysulfate sodium (PPS) 100 mg once daily, 100 mg three times daily, and placebo in 368 adults with IC/BPS symptoms; neither active dose showed a superior 24-week symptom responder rate over placebo.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="pps-dose-nickel" />

## Design

Eligibility required an Interstitial Cystitis Symptom Index (ICSI) total score of at least 8 and a score above zero on each of its four items. Cystoscopic findings did not determine eligibility. A responder had at least a 30% reduction in total ICSI score from baseline at study end. ICSI assessments were scheduled at baseline and weeks 4, 8, 12, 18, and 24.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

The prespecified responder threshold was reached by 51/128 (39.8%) patients assigned once-daily PPS, 52/122 (42.6%) assigned three-times-daily PPS, and 48/118 (40.7%) assigned placebo. Neither active-to-placebo comparison nor the comparison between active doses was statistically significant.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Participants received oral PPS 100 mg once daily or three times daily, or matching placebo, for 24 weeks. An unblinded interim analysis after six years, when 54% of the planned 645 patients had enrolled, led to early trial termination.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

Discontinuation because of an adverse event ranged from 10.2% to 13.3% across the three groups.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Early termination reduced the planned sample size. Enrollment based on symptoms without cystoscopic criteria leaves the treatment effect in lesion-defined subgroups unresolved.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="pps-dose-nickel" />

The earlier PPS pivotal trial reported greater patient-rated global improvement with PPS than placebo; Nickel used an ICSI reduction threshold and assessed treatment through 24 weeks.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA IC/BPS guideline, amended in 2022, includes this trial in its PPS evidence review and lists PPS among oral medications that may be administered <Rec>(Option (Evidence Strength: Grades B, B, C, and B))</Rec>. It directs counseling about potential macular damage and vision-related injuries before PPS use <Rec>(Clinical Principle)</Rec>.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

The EAU chronic pelvic pain guideline (2026) cites this report and recommends offering oral pentosane polysulphate for primary bladder pain syndrome <Rec>(Strong)</Rec>.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
