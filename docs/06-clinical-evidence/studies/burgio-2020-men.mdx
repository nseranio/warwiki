# Burgio 2020: Combined Behavioral and Drug Therapy for Overactive Bladder Symptoms in Men

<TrialBottomLine id="burgio-2020-men" />

**Burgio 2020** was a three-site, two-stage randomized trial of behavioral training plus tolterodine–tamsulosin versus either treatment alone in 204 men with urgency and at least nine daily voids; after six weeks, combined therapy reduced voiding frequency more than drugs alone but not more than behavioral training alone.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="burgio-2020-men" />

## Design

Community-dwelling men aged at least 40 years entered after a seven-day diary confirmed at least nine voids per 24 hours. Screening included examination, urinalysis, uroflowmetry, postvoid residual measurement and cognitive screening. Indicators of outlet obstruction, a positive dementia screen, urinary infection, cancer, neurologic disease and other medical contributors to symptoms were exclusions. Randomization was stratified by urge incontinence and baseline voiding frequency.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The primary outcome was change in diary-recorded voids per 24 hours after the first six-week stage. Two planned comparisons tested combination treatment against each monotherapy, adjusting for baseline frequency and age. The corrected two-sided primary threshold was P &lt; .025. Missing outcomes were multiply imputed for the intention-to-treat analysis. Participants and interventionists knew treatment assignment; outcomes were scored blinded.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

At six weeks, mean daily voids were 8.2 (SD 2.3) with combined therapy, 8.8 (SD 2.1) with behavioral training and 10.3 (SD 2.7) with drugs. The unadjusted mean differences were −2.1 voids per day for combination versus drugs (adjusted P &lt; .001, threshold met) and −0.6 for combination versus behavioral training (P = .19, threshold missed). Behavioral training alone also yielded fewer daily voids than drugs alone (P &lt; .001).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Secondary six-week measures showed mean nocturia episodes of 1.3, 1.3 and 1.8 per night, respectively, and mean Overactive Bladder Questionnaire symptom-bother scores of 23.8, 43.0 and 39.5; the overall adjusted between-group tests were P &lt; .001 for both. After the monotherapy groups stepped up to combination treatment, mean daily voids at 12 weeks were 8.0, 8.0 and 8.6 in the same group order (overall P = .33).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

Nurses and advanced practice clinicians delivered behavioral training at three clinic visits over six weeks. Pelvic floor contraction and relaxation were taught with verbal feedback from anal palpation, followed by urge suppression, progressive voiding delay and diary-guided home practice. The prescribed home exercise schedule was 45 contractions daily in three sets of 15. Nocturia counseling included limiting fluids before bedtime and using urge suppression when awakened.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Drug treatment was sustained-release tolterodine 4 mg daily plus tamsulosin 0.4 mg nightly; tolterodine could be reduced to 2 mg for adverse effects. The combination arm received both protocols. In the second six-week stage, both monotherapy arms added the other treatment. The first-stage assessment was completed by 59/65 assigned combination treatment, 63/71 assigned behavioral training and 61/68 assigned drugs (183/204 overall).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

At six weeks, 21/61 (34%) combination recipients, 55/64 (86%) behavioral recipients and 21/65 (32%) drug recipients reported no adverse effects or effects that were not bothersome (overall P &lt; .001). Extremely bothersome effects were reported by 1/61 (2%), 0/64 (0%) and 6/65 (9%), respectively. These were patient ratings; the report does not tabulate urinary retention or catheterization by arm.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

The behavioral protocol required more in-person contact and self-monitoring than the drug protocol, and treatment assignment was visible to participants. Each randomized monotherapy comparison lasted six weeks. The drug arm combined an antimuscarinic with an α-blocker, and men with indicators of outlet obstruction were excluded. After crossover to combination treatment, the 12-week comparison evaluated treatment sequence rather than ongoing monotherapy.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="burgio-2020-men" />

MOTIVE enrolled men with persistent OAB symptoms while taking an α-blocker and compared adding behavioral training with adding an antimuscarinic. Burgio 2020 started both tolterodine and tamsulosin together in its drug arm and compared that regimen with behavioral training and combined treatment.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Guidelines and approvals

The AUA/SUFU 2024 idiopathic OAB guideline allows combining behavioral therapy, noninvasive therapy, pharmacotherapy and/or minimally invasive therapy when monotherapy is inadequate <Rec>(Expert Opinion)</Rec>; its discussion cites this trial.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> The AUA 2026 LUTS/BPH guideline advises adding behavioral and lifestyle interventions for persistent storage symptoms despite medication <Rec>(Expert Opinion)</Rec>; its discussion also cites this trial.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
