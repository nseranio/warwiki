# Posterior Urethral Stenosis

**Posterior urethral stenosis** is narrowing of the urethra proximal to the corpus spongiosum: the bladder neck, prostatic urethra and membranous urethra, including the bulbomembranous junction. The SIU/ICUD consultation uses **stricture** for the anterior urethra, where scar involves the epithelium and corpus spongiosum (spongiofibrosis). It uses **stenosis** for the posterior urethra, which has no spongiosum.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

The distinction has practical consequences. Posterior scar lies against the bladder neck and external urethral sphincter, so every treatment weighs patency against continence. Evidence from anterior strictures, such as the case against repeated urethrotomy, does not transfer directly. After radical prostatectomy, for example, repeated endoscopic treatment of anastomotic stenosis is often justified.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

This page defines the entities and the shared decision framework. Management details are on the entity pages, which are linked in the table below. For anterior disease, see [Male Urethral Stricture](/docs/clinical-conditions/03b-voiding-outlet/urethral-stricture).

---

## At a Glance

- **Name the entity first.** Bladder neck contracture after benign outlet surgery, anastomotic stenosis after prostatectomy, radiation-induced stenosis, pelvic fracture injury and membranous stenosis after endoscopic prostate surgery differ in biology, sphincter risk and treatment.
- **Characterize before treating.** Assess obliteration, length, proximal extent, bladder-neck competence, baseline continence, bladder function, prior radiation and fistula.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Non-obliterative stenosis:** endoscopic treatment first. Unlike anterior stricture, a repeat attempt is reasonable.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Obliterative, radiated or recalcitrant stenosis:** reconstruct or divert. Do not attempt blind endoscopy.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Patency first, continence second.** Place an artificial urinary sphincter (AUS) only after the outlet is stable. Non-transecting repairs leave a better AUS bed.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- **Look early with a cystoscope.** A lumen narrower than 17 Fr at the first postoperative urethroscopy strongly predicts later reintervention.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## The Entities

| Entity | Usual cause | Level | Key issue | Detailed page |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bladder neck contracture (BNC)** | TURP, enucleation, vaporization, bladder-neck incision | Native bladder neck | External sphincter usually spared; endoscopic treatment often durable | [Bladder Neck Contracture](/docs/clinical-conditions/03b-voiding-outlet/bladder-neck-stenosis) |
| **Vesicourethral anastomotic stenosis (VUAS)** | Radical prostatectomy, with or without radiation | Vesicourethral anastomosis | Rhabdosphincter immediately distal to the scar | [Vesicourethral Anastomotic Stenosis](/docs/clinical-conditions/03b-voiding-outlet/vesicourethral-anastomotic-stenosis) |
| **Radiation-induced stenosis** | EBRT, brachytherapy, combined treatment or radiation after prostatectomy | Usually bulbomembranous; also bladder neck, prostatic urethra or anastomosis | Ischemic tissue, higher incontinence and AUS risk, possible fistula or necrosis | [Radiation-Induced Posterior Urethral Stenosis](/docs/clinical-conditions/03b-voiding-outlet/radiation-posterior-urethral-stenosis) |
| **Pelvic fracture urethral injury (PFUI)** | Pelvic fracture with urethral distraction | Membranous urethra | Obliterative gap with a displaced prostate; delayed anastomotic repair | [PFUI](/docs/special-populations/05a-trauma-emergencies/pfui) |
| **Membranous stenosis after endoscopic prostate surgery** | TURP, laser or enucleation, without radiation | Membranous or bulbomembranous urethra | After outlet surgery the bladder neck is often incompetent, so continence depends on the sphincter segment that is scarred | [Reconstruction below](#reconstruction-by-entity) |

Older series often grouped BNC and VUAS together, and many posterior reconstruction series mix several entities. Check the population before applying a result.

## Incidence

| Setting | Estimate | Source and limits |
|---|---|---|
| BNC after transurethral prostate surgery | 1.3% after TURP, 0.66% after enucleation, 1.2% after ablation | Meta-analysis of randomized trials; differences were not significant<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| VUAS after radical prostatectomy | 851 of 17,904 men (4.8%), at median 3.4 months | Single-institution registry 1987–2013, excluding prior radiation. Robotic approach (OR 0.39) and complete nerve sparing (OR 0.63) were protective<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| Stricture after prostate radiotherapy | Pooled 2.2% at median 4 years (EBRT 1.5%, brachytherapy 1.9%, combined 4.9%) | Meta-analysis; risk rose with longer follow-up<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| Long-term urethral complications after prostate cancer treatment | At 15 years: EBRT plus cryotherapy 42%, EBRT plus brachytherapy 26%, prostatectomy plus EBRT 22%, prostatectomy alone 9%, observation 8% | Population registry; composite of stenosis, fistula or urethral procedure<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |

For PFUI incidence and classification, see [PFUI](/docs/special-populations/05a-trauma-emergencies/pfui).

## Evaluation

Common to all entities:<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- **Symptoms and function.** Voiding and storage symptoms, retention or catheter dependence, infection, and patient-reported outcome measures.
- **Urethral rest.** Avoid evaluating through an indwelling urethral catheter. One robotic reconstruction group places a suprapubic tube and allows about 4 weeks of rest before assessment.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Uroflowmetry and post-void residual.**
- **[Cystoscopy](/docs/evaluation/ancillary-tests/endoscopy).** Many series define stenosis as failure to pass a 16–17 Fr cystoscope.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> In a patient with a suprapubic tube, antegrade flexible cystoscopy through the tract shows the proximal side.
- **[Retrograde urethrography with voiding cystourethrography](/docs/evaluation/imaging/rug-vcug).** Defines location and length. If the proximal urethra does not fill, perform a combined antegrade and retrograde study.
- **[MRI](/docs/evaluation/imaging/mri).** For PFUI planning, and for suspected fistula, osteomyelitis or prostatic cavitation.
- **Bladder-neck competence and baseline continence.** Assess on cystogram or antegrade cystoscopy. These predict continence after the outlet is opened.
- **Urodynamics** when storage dysfunction or incontinence would change the plan. Renal function and PSA in cancer survivors.

## Management Framework

| Scenario | Usual approach | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Non-obliterative, first presentation** | Dilation, incision or resection | No head-to-head trials. Warn that relieving obstruction can reveal stress incontinence<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Recurrence after endoscopy** | A further endoscopic attempt is reasonable; escalate after repeated failure | For VUAS, one treatment succeeded in 44.2%, but 91% were patent after a mean of 2.1 treatments. Retreatment rates were 34% at 1 year and 42% at 5 years. Some reconstructive centers instead offer reconstruction after a single failed attempt<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[8]](#ref8)[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Obliterative stenosis** | Reconstruction or diversion | EAU strongly discourages endoluminal treatment<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Radiation-induced stenosis** | Early referral for reconstruction planning | See [Radiation-Induced Posterior Urethral Stenosis](/docs/clinical-conditions/03b-voiding-outlet/radiation-posterior-urethral-stenosis) |
| **PFUI** | Delayed perineal anastomotic urethroplasty | See [PFUI](/docs/special-populations/05a-trauma-emergencies/pfui) |
| **Devastated outlet** | Diversion with or without cystectomy, or long-term suprapubic drainage | EAU weak recommendation<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

## Endoscopic Treatment

**Outcomes.** A meta-analysis of 40 studies (1,452 patients) of endoscopic VUAS treatment found 72.8% overall success, falling to 62.9% after correction for publication bias.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> Holmium laser incision outperformed the other modalities in one 142-patient series.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Techniques are described on [Transurethral Incision of BNC](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/transurethral-incision-bnc), [Transurethral Bladder Neck Resection](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/transurethral-bladder-neck-resection) and [DVIU and Urethral Dilation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/dviu).

**Patency versus continence.** A deeper incision opens the ring more reliably but threatens continence, especially at the anastomosis:

- In a 103-patient multi-institutional VUAS series, new incontinence occurred after 31% of incisions versus 12% of resections.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- After deep lateral incision through the bladder-neck fibers into perivesical fat, 97.7% of 43 men were patent after up to three incisions. However, 88.1% used at least one pad daily and 16.7% later received an AUS.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

Counsel before the first incision.

**Adjuncts and newer endoscopic options:**

| Option | Evidence | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Mitomycin C** | 55-patient TURNS series: 58% success after one treatment, 75% overall; 7% serious adverse events<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> | EAU restricts use to clinical trials.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> See [Antimitotics & Antifibrotics](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/dermatologic-topical-urethral/antimitotics-antifibrotics) |
| **Intralesional corticosteroid** | Recalcitrant VUAS: incision with triamcinolone succeeded in 15 of 18 (83%; 78% radiated; 28% complications). Plasma-button incision with triamcinolone gave 85% patency in 20 patients, but every completely obliterated stenosis recurred<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[19]](#ref19)</sup> | Small retrospective series. See [Intralesional Corticosteroids](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/dermatologic-topical-urethral/intralesional-corticosteroids) |
| **Transurethral incision with transverse mucosal realignment (TUITMR)** | First series: 19 BNC/VUAS patients, 89% success after one procedure. Later registry: 92 men with BNC after benign prostate surgery, 93.5% anatomic success, 83.7% free of reintervention after one procedure, no new incontinence at median 15.9 months<sup>[[20]](#ref20)[[21]](#ref21)</sup> | Single-group data from the originating team. See [TUITMR](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/transurethral-incision-transverse-mucosal-realignment) |
| **Drug-coated balloon** | Retrospective 141-patient VUAS/BNC comparison: better recurrence-free survival than standard endoscopy (HR 0.40)<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> | No guideline recommendation for posterior disease. See [Drug-Coated Balloon Therapy](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/dermatologic-topical-urethral/drug-coated-balloon-therapy) |
| **Permanent urethral stent** | Low patency and high incontinence | EAU discourages; SIU allows<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |

## Reconstruction by Entity

- **BNC.** Y-V or T-plasty and robotic reconstruction. See the [BNC treatment atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc).
- **VUAS.** In the TURNS robotic series, 24 of 32 men (75%) were patent at median 12 months, and 11 of 13 initially continent men stayed continent.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> A 28-patient comparison found similar patency after open perineal and robotic repair (77.8% versus 80%). New incontinence was far more common after perineal repair (100% versus 16.6%), though the numbers were small.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> Open series report durable patency, but most men are incontinent afterward, often because they were incontinent beforehand:
  - Transperineal reanastomosis (15 men): patency 14 of 15. Fourteen were incontinent beforehand, incontinence worsened in 9, and 10 received an AUS.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>
  - Abdominal, perineal or combined repair (12 men, 43% obliterated): 92% patent at median 75.5 months, but only 25% continent.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>
  - In a single-surgeon robotic series, reintervention was needed in 10% of men after prostatectomy alone versus 80% after radiation.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>
  - See the [VUAS treatment atlas](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/vuas) and [Robotic Posterior Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/robotic-posterior-urethroplasty).
- **Membranous stenosis after endoscopic prostate surgery.** A non-transecting dorsal onlay buccal graft avoids circumferential sphincter dissection. In a 107-patient multi-institutional series, 10 men (9.35%) had recurrence at mean 59.3 months, with one case of new stress incontinence. Prior radiation predicted recurrence (OR 8.3).<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup> Barbagli's sphincter-preserving ventral onlay reported 11 recurrences and 3 cases of incontinence among 69 men.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup> See [Dorsal Onlay BMG for BNC / VUAS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/dorsal-bmg-bnc).
- **PFUI.** Delayed perineal anastomotic urethroplasty with progressive maneuvers; abdominoperineal repair when needed. See [PFUI](/docs/special-populations/05a-trauma-emergencies/pfui) and [Abdominoperineal (Transpubic) Urethroplasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04a-urethral-reconstruction/abdominoperineal-urethroplasty).
- **Radiation-induced stenosis.** Excision and primary anastomosis gives the most durable patency for short segments. A non-transecting buccal graft is used for longer disease or when the urethra must be preserved for an AUS. See [Radiation-Induced Posterior Urethral Stenosis](/docs/clinical-conditions/03b-voiding-outlet/radiation-posterior-urethral-stenosis#choosing-a-reconstruction).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Devastated outlet.** See [Urinary Diversion Principles](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion/urinary-diversion-principles) and [Cancer Survivorship](/docs/special-populations/05d-cancer-survivorship).

### One center's VUAS algorithm

Naser-Tavakolian and Lee choose a robotic VUAS repair by stenosis caliber and whether it involves the external urinary sphincter (EUS):<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

| Finding | Repair |
|---|---|
| Stenosis 10 Fr or wider, EUS not involved | Non-transecting bladder flap: [Y-V plasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/yv-plasty) or [T-plasty](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/t-plasty-bnc) |
| Stenosis narrower than 10 Fr, EUS not involved | Excision with [primary reanastomosis](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/primary-reanastomosis) |
| Anastomosis not achievable because of fibrosis or length | [Tanagho-type](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/tanagho-flap) closure of the bladder neck with a tubularized anterior bladder flap |
| Stenosis involving or extending beyond the EUS | [Combined](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/bnc-vuas/combined-abdominoperineal) robotic transabdominal and open transperineal repair, expecting incontinence and a later AUS |

This is a single institution's approach. The review's senior author consults for a robotic manufacturer, and its conclusion that robotic repair causes less new incontinence than open repair rests on early, noncomparative series.

## Continence, AUS and Follow-Up

**Continence and AUS.** Treat stress incontinence only after the stenosis is resolved and stable on cystoscopy. EAU advises waiting at least 6 months after a redo vesicourethral anastomosis.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> In observational data, prior urethroplasty is associated with more AUS explantation, and transecting repairs carry more risk than non-transecting ones. In a 15-institution cohort, explantation followed 56.2% of AUS placements after transecting urethroplasty versus 23.5% after non-transecting repair.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[30]](#ref30)</sup> The [radiation page](/docs/clinical-conditions/03b-voiding-outlet/radiation-posterior-urethral-stenosis#continence-and-the-artificial-urinary-sphincter) covers AUS risk after radiation. See also [Artificial Urinary Sphincter (Procedure)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter).

**Follow-up.** Flow rate alone misses recurrence. In a prospective endoscopic follow-up series, 26 of 27 recurrences appeared in the first year, and 7 of 11 recurrences with flow data had a peak flow above 15 mL/s.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup> A lumen narrower than 17 Fr at the first urethroscopy (median 3.7 months) predicted secondary intervention in 64%, versus 15% with a normal lumen.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Follow patients with radiation, long stenosis or prior failed repair for longer.

---

## Evidence Limitations

- The literature is almost entirely retrospective, with mixed entities and different definitions of success.
- No randomized trial compares dilation, incision and resection for posterior stenosis. The best number of endoscopic attempts before escalation is undefined.
- Data on mitomycin C, drug-coated balloons, TUITMR and robotic reconstruction are early or single-group.
- New-incontinence rates depend on whether men who were already incontinent are counted in the denominator.
