# Cutaneous & Complex Fistulas

Cutaneous genitourinary fistulas involve an abnormal communication between any part of the urinary tract and the skin surface. Complex fistulas involve multiple tracts, multiple organ systems, hostile tissue environments (radiation, infection, malignancy), or have failed prior repairs. Both categories demand a systematic, staged reconstructive approach.

## Cutaneous Fistulas

### Types

| Fistula Type | Common Cause |
|---|---|
| Urethrocutaneous | Hypospadias repair breakdown; urethroplasty dehiscence; trauma |
| Vesicoutaneous | Post-operative; radiation; chronic catheterization |
| Ureterocutaneous | Ureteral injury; post-operative; percutaneous nephrostomy site |
| Nephrocutaneous | Infected obstructed kidney; staghorn calculus; post-operative |
| Colovesical / Enterovesical | Diverticulitis, Crohn's, malignancy — primarily bowel etiology |

### Urethrocutaneous Fistula (UCF)

The most common cutaneous GU fistula encountered in reconstructive urology.

**Etiology:**
- Complication of hypospadias repair (most common in pediatric and adult redo cases)
- Urethroplasty wound breakdown (buccal mucosa graft, skin graft)
- Perineal trauma or Fournier's gangrene sequela
- Catheter erosion

**Presentation:**
- Urinary spraying or split stream
- Visible pinpoint opening on penile shaft, perineum, or scrotum
- Leakage during voiding; may be intermittent

**Evaluation:**
- Clinical examination — tract may be probed gently
- RUG / voiding cystourethrogram: exclude concurrent urethral stricture (critical — distal obstruction drives fistula persistence)
- Cystoscopy: assess urethral lumen integrity

**Principles of Repair:**
1. Exclude and treat concurrent urethral obstruction first — a fistula distal to a stricture will not heal
2. Allow minimum 6 months from prior surgery for tissue maturation
3. Core excision of the fistula tract; layered, tension-free closure
4. Dartos flap interposition to separate suture lines
5. Avoid overlapping suture lines
6. Urethral catheter diversion for 10–14 days

## Complex Fistulas

### Definition

A fistula is considered complex when one or more of the following apply:
- **Multiplicity**: two or more fistulous tracts
- **Prior failed repair(s)**: ≥1 previous attempt
- **Hostile tissue**: radiation, ischemia, infection, or inflammatory bowel disease
- **Large defect**: significant tissue loss precluding primary closure
- **Multi-organ involvement**: urinary + enteric + vaginal/cutaneous
- **Associated malignancy or recurrent cancer**

### Approach to Complex Fistula

#### Staging
Complex fistula repair is almost always staged:

1. **Damage control**: urinary and/or fecal diversion; drainage of abscess or urinoma; nutritional optimization
2. **Tissue preparation**: allow 3–6 months (longer in radiation cases); optimize tissue perfusion; hyperbaric oxygen in select radiation cases
3. **Definitive reconstruction**: single-stage if possible; may require multiple procedures

#### Tissue Interposition — The Non-Negotiable Step

In any complex fistula, vascularized tissue interposition is mandatory:

| Tissue | Best Use |
|---|---|
| **Martius flap** (labial fat pad) | Vaginal/perineal fistulas; women; reliable, straightforward |
| **Gracilis muscle flap** | Perineal/rectourethral; irradiated pelvis; excellent bulk |
| **Omental pedicle** | Abdominal approach; radiation; fills dead space |
| **Peritoneal flap** | Laparoscopic/robotic VVF repair; limited bulk |
| **Fasciocutaneous flap** | Large cutaneous defects; perineal reconstruction |

#### Multi-Compartment Fistulas
- Simultaneous involvement of bladder, vagina, rectum (cloacal-type) — requires coordinated urologic, gynecologic, and colorectal reconstruction
- Often requires fecal + urinary diversion, staged repair, and gracilis or omental flap
- Urinary diversion (continent or incontinent) may be the most reliable long-term solution when bladder is unsalvageable

### Radiation Fistulas — Special Considerations
- Tissue quality is the limiting factor in all radiation fistulas
- Hyperbaric oxygen (HBO) may improve tissue oxygenation pre-operatively (20–40 sessions)
- Never repair without vascularized tissue interposition
- Recurrence rates are higher; patient counseling about realistic expectations is essential
- Permanent urinary and/or fecal diversion should be presented as a valid and dignified option

## Key Points

- **Treat obstruction before fistula** — no repair will hold against back-pressure
- **Vascularized tissue is the common denominator** in all complex repair
- **Diversion is not failure** — for radiation or multiply recurrent fistulas, it may be the best quality-of-life decision
- Multidisciplinary planning (urology, colorectal, plastics, gynecology) is essential for multi-compartment cases
- Document each prior repair attempt, technique, and tissue used — this drives the next-step strategy
