# Urinary Fistula After Kidney Transplant

A **post-transplant urinary fistula** is urinary leakage from any point in the transplanted collecting system — most commonly the **ureteroneocystostomy** anastomosis, the **distal transplant ureter** (which depends entirely on the donor-origin lower-pole artery for its blood supply and is highly susceptible to ischemic necrosis), or the **renal pelvis / calyceal system**. It is the principal early urologic complication of renal transplantation, occurring in approximately **2–9%** of cases, and is most often managed by a combination of **retrograde or antegrade ureteral stenting**, **percutaneous nephrostomy**, and — for failed drainage or extensive ureteral necrosis — **surgical revision** (re-implantation, ureteral reconstruction with native ureter, or ileal interposition).

See also: [The Ureters](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/urinary-tract/ureter-anatomy-physiology), [Fistulas landing page](/docs/clinical-conditions/03f-fistulas).
