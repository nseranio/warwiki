# Vesicouterine Fistula

A **vesicouterine fistula (VUF)** is an abnormal communication between the bladder and the uterine cavity. It is less common than vesicovaginal fistula but is increasing in frequency worldwide with the global rise in cesarean delivery rates — **cesarean section is the dominant etiology** in high-CD-rate populations. The classic presentation is the **Youssef triad**, first described by Youssef in 1957: **cyclic hematuria during menstruation (menouria), amenorrhea or reduced vaginal menstrual flow, and preserved urinary continence** (because the trigone and bladder neck sit below the fistula). Repair is typically transabdominal and transvesical (O'Conor) with omental interposition; the fistula sits too high for standard transvaginal VVF approaches.

For the source operation, see [Cesarean Section](/docs/special-populations/05a-trauma-emergencies/intraoperative-consultation/procedures-causing-gu-injury/cesarean-section).
