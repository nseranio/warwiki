# Obstetric Fistula

**Obstetric fistula** is the genitourinary (and sometimes rectovaginal) fistula that results from **prolonged obstructed labor** without timely cesarean access — the global paradigm of catastrophic obstetric GU injury. Tissue necrosis occurs when the fetal presenting part is impacted against the maternal pelvis for hours to days, producing **ischemic necrosis of the anterior vaginal wall, bladder base, urethra, and sometimes the rectum**. The fistula declares itself 3–10 days postpartum as continuous urinary and/or fecal leakage, most often in an adolescent or young woman who has also lost the pregnancy. It remains prevalent in sub-Saharan Africa, South Asia, and conflict-affected regions where access to emergency obstetric care is limited.

The clinical character, anatomic geography, and reconstructive approach differ meaningfully from iatrogenic VVF — the tissue loss is larger, the urethra is more often involved, associated injuries (pubic osteomyelitis, foot-drop from sacral plexus compression, secondary infertility, social displacement) are the rule rather than the exception, and reconstruction is almost always staged. The **Hamlin, Addis Ababa, and Danja fistula hospitals** and the **FIGO / UNFPA Campaign to End Fistula** have codified global approaches to classification (Goh, Waaldijk), surgical technique, and reintegration.
