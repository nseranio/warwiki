# Urethrocutaneous Fistula

A **urethrocutaneous fistula (UCF)** is an abnormal communication between the urethral lumen and the skin of the penis, scrotum, or perineum. It is the most common cutaneous genitourinary fistula encountered in reconstructive urology and occurs almost exclusively in men — most often as a complication of **hypospadias repair**, **urethroplasty wound breakdown**, **perineal trauma**, or **Fournier's gangrene sequela**.

See also: [Male Urethra](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/urinary-tract/male-urethra), [Flaps in GU Reconstruction](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/flaps-gu-reconstruction), and the [Fistulas](/docs/clinical-conditions/03f-fistulas) landing page for the complex-fistula framework that applies to all recurrent / hostile-tissue cases.

---

## Etiology

- **Hypospadias repair** — the single most common setting (both primary pediatric breakdown and adult redo cases).
- **Urethroplasty wound breakdown** — after buccal mucosa graft, skin graft, or anastomotic repair; most commonly at the dorsal or ventral suture line.
- **Perineal trauma** — straddle injury, penetrating injury, or post-Fournier's reconstruction.
- **Catheter erosion** — particularly at the penoscrotal junction in chronically catheterized patients.
- **Radiation** — after external-beam or brachytherapy for prostate or penile cancer; fistulas in this setting are complex (see below).
- **Prior urethral diverticulectomy** in men.

## Presentation

- Urinary spraying, split stream, or forked stream during voiding
- Visible pinpoint or larger opening on the penile shaft, scrotum, or perineum
- Leakage during voiding — may be intermittent and position-dependent
- In chronic fistulas: skin maceration, recurrent UTI, or local abscess

## Evaluation

- **Clinical examination** — the fistula tract can often be gently probed; note the relationship to any prior urethroplasty or hypospadias repair
- **Retrograde urethrogram (RUG) and voiding cystourethrogram (VCUG)** — *essential* to exclude concurrent urethral stricture distal to the fistula. **A fistula distal to a stricture will not heal** — treat the obstruction first.
- **Cystoscopy** — assess urethral lumen integrity, identify all fistulous openings, characterize tissue quality
- **MRI** (select cases) — delineate complex or multi-tract fistulas, particularly post-trauma or post-Fournier's

## Principles of Repair

1. **Exclude and treat concurrent urethral obstruction first.** A fistula distal to an unrelieved stricture will recur.
2. **Allow a minimum of 6 months** from the prior surgery for tissue maturation before attempting repair.
3. **Core excision of the fistula tract** with layered, tension-free closure.
4. **Dartos flap interposition** to separate the urethral and skin suture lines — this is the single most important step.
5. **Avoid overlapping suture lines** between the urethral and dartos/skin layers.
6. **Urethral catheter diversion** for 10–14 days; suprapubic diversion considered for complex or recurrent cases.

## Complex / Recurrent UCF

Recurrent UCF, radiation-associated UCF, or UCF in a field of prior multiply-failed urethroplasty is managed by the complex-fistula framework — see the [Fistulas landing page](/docs/clinical-conditions/03f-fistulas). Vascularized tissue interposition (additional dartos, tunica vaginalis, gracilis, or Singapore flap) is mandatory in any recurrent case.
