# Urethroperineal Fistula

A **urethroperineal fistula** is an abnormal communication between the urethra and the perineal skin. It most often arises as a **Fournier's gangrene sequela** (with extensive perineal tissue loss and a resulting fistula after debridement and reconstruction), a **urethroplasty failure** at a perineal anastomotic line, or after **perineal trauma** (straddle injury, penetrating wound). The repair must account for the frequently hostile perineal tissue bed — previously debrided, previously grafted, or fibrotic — and almost always requires **vascularized tissue interposition** (gracilis flap is the workhorse) plus **urinary diversion** (suprapubic tube) during healing.

See also: [Urethrocutaneous Fistula](/docs/clinical-conditions/03f-fistulas/in-males/urethrocutaneous), [The Perineum](/docs/foundations/anatomy-physiology/pelvis-support/perineum), [Fournier's Gangrene](/docs/special-populations/05a-trauma-emergencies/fourniers-gangrene), [Fistulas landing page](/docs/clinical-conditions/03f-fistulas).
