# Female Genital Mutilation / Cutting (FGM/C)

**Female genital mutilation / cutting (FGM/C)** comprises all procedures involving partial or total removal of the external female genitalia, or other injury to the female genital organs, for non-medical reasons. WHO and UNICEF estimate &gt; 230 million women and girls alive today have undergone FGM/C across &gt; 30 countries.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## WHO Classification

| Type | Description | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **I — Clitoridectomy** | Ia prepuce only; Ib prepuce + partial/total glans clitoridis | Clitoral body and crura typically preserved beneath scar — anatomic basis for the Foldès reconstruction |
| **II — Excision** | IIa: labia minora only; IIb: glans clitoris + labia minora; IIc: glans clitoris + labia minora + labia majora | Type I or II ("flesh removed") is the most commonly reported type among women in most surveyed countries<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **III — Infibulation** | Narrowing of the vaginal orifice by apposition of labia minora and/or majora, ± clitoral excision | **~10%** of cases; concentrated in Somalia, Djibouti, Eritrea, northern Sudan |
| **IV** | All other harmful non-medical procedures: pricking, piercing, incising, scraping, cauterization | Heterogeneous |

Internal clitoral tissue may remain beneath the scar after Type I/II; its individual anatomy and innervation must be assessed when discussing reconstruction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

---

## Epidemiology

- Pooled global prevalence among women 15–49 in studied countries: approximately 36.9%; Somalia 99.2% highest in women, Mali 72.7% highest in girls.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- Pooled sub-Saharan African prevalence across ten high-risk countries: 53.5%.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- FGM/C is predominantly performed from infancy through adolescence, with age varying by setting; in half of countries with available data, most girls are cut before age 5.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- A model using 2012 U.S. demographics and country-of-origin prevalence data estimated that approximately 513,000 women and girls were at risk for FGM/C or its consequences.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
- **Risk factors** (African studies): family history of cutting (pooled AOR 13.71), no formal education (AOR 3.28), rural residence (AOR 2.27), poor household wealth (AOR 1.38).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Ten-country DHS data show the same direction of association (older age, no schooling, rural residence, poverty).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
- Prevalence is declining in most practicing countries but not at a rate compatible with the UN SDG elimination target by 2030.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Health Complications (Reconstructive Focus)

Risk varies with the actual injury and infibulation; classification alone does not predict an individual’s sexual function or all complications.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

### Immediate

- **Hemorrhage** (clitoral dorsal artery or labial branches; AAP cites a reported range of 4–19%), with hypovolemic shock possible after severe blood loss.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Sepsis.** Cellulitis, abscess, gangrene, tetanus
- **Urinary.** Urethral injury, retention, edema
- **Fractures** from physical restraint

### Long-term — reconstructive-urology / urogyn relevance

| Domain | Lesion / sequel | Operative implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Urinary** | Urethral stricture, meatal obstruction, recurrent UTI / pyelonephritis, post-void dribbling, urgency, incontinence, and secondary fistula formation (particularly after Type III FGM/C)<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> | May require meatoplasty, urethral dilation, deinfibulation to expose the meatus, or fistula repair |
| **Vulvovaginal** | Introital stenosis, apareunia, retained menses (hematocolpos), epidermoid inclusion cysts, post-FGM neuromas | Defibulation; cyst excision; neuroma resection or fat grafting |
| **Sexual** | Dyspareunia (OR 2.47),<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> reduced satisfaction, reduced lubrication | Defibulation; clitoral reconstruction; FGM/C fat grafting |
| **Obstetric** | Prolonged labor (OR 2.04), perineal tears (OR 2.63), episiotomy (OR 1.89),<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> plus higher risk of obstructed labor, PPH, fetal distress and stillbirth/neonatal death<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | Discuss antepartum or intrapartum deinfibulation according to circumstances and preference |
| **Mental health** | PTSD symptoms, depression, anxiety and somatization; reported prevalence depends on population and assessment | Multidisciplinary care before reconstructive surgery |

In children, the cephalad apex of the clitoral hood lies close to the urethral orifice, reported as 12 mm at ages 0–3 and 17 mm at ages 4–8, consistent with the urethra's vulnerability during cutting.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

In a cross-sectional sample of 112 women seeking counseling, older age at cutting was associated with more PTSD symptoms.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> In a separate 180-woman Somali hospital sample, sexual dysfunction and trauma history correlated with greater PTSD severity, while resilience correlated with lower severity.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Clinical Management

The AAP 2020 clinical report addresses girls; WHO 2025 provides the current broader clinical framework, updating the earlier WHO guidance on management of health complications.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[18]](#ref18)</sup> Positions:

- Providers must never perform any form of FGM/C (including "ritual nick").<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- Counsel families against the practice, including before travel ("vacation cutting").
- Examine external genitalia at every health-supervision visit and document FGM/C type using ICD-10 N90.810–N90.818.
- **Offer counseling about deinfibulation for Type III**; WHO’s recommendation is conditional and requires informed consent.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- Offer mental health interventions to women and girls with anxiety, depression or PTSD symptoms (WHO strong recommendation, low-certainty evidence) and sexual-health counseling for sexual dysfunction (conditional, very-low-certainty).<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- Evaluate for child abuse if FGM/C is suspected to have occurred within the US or by trafficking abroad.

### Defibulation (deinfibulation)

During [deinfibulation](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/defibulation), a finger beneath the Type III midline scar protects the urethra as the scar is divided to expose the urethral meatus and vaginal introitus.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup> WHO describes suturing the cut edges to keep the introitus open.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

- WHO 2025 conditionally recommends deinfibulation for women and girls with Type III, with counseling and informed consent; reinfibulation should not be performed. For girls without urologic or gynecologic complications, clinicians can wait until assent is possible and genital tissue has estrogenized before suggesting surgery.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **Timing:** WHO 2025 conditionally supports either antepartum or intrapartum deinfibulation and names no preferred timing; preference, facility access and clinician skill guide the plan.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> In very-low-certainty observational comparisons, antepartum deinfibulation was associated with higher odds of postpartum hemorrhage (76/248 versus 72/349; OR 1.51, two studies) and cesarean birth (104/322 versus 131/590; OR 1.66, three studies) than intrapartum deinfibulation.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> RCOG advises offering it before pregnancy or first intercourse where possible, and antenatally (usually the second trimester, about 20 weeks) when adequate vaginal assessment in labor would otherwise be unlikely; intrapartum deinfibulation remains an option if the woman prefers it.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> If cesarean birth prevents planned deinfibulation, RCOG advises discussing opening just after cesarean or arranging gynecology or FGM-clinic follow-up before another pregnancy.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> Reinfibulation is not performed.
- In adults, local anesthesia in a clinic or delivery-suite room is usually sufficient; spinal or general anesthesia may also be used. Offer trauma-informed support.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> For young children, the AAP recommends general anesthesia in all cases. For nonpregnant adolescents, it favors regional or general anesthesia and discourages local anesthesia when those options are available.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- In observational comparisons of pregnant women with Type III FGM/C, deinfibulation was associated with lower odds of emergency cesarean birth in one study (45/230 versus 12/20; OR 0.16) and genital-tract laceration across three studies (174/956 versus 26/111; OR 0.48) than no deinfibulation. Certainty for both outcomes was very low.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- In a 40-patient Type III FGM/C series, 32 women were reached by telephone 6 months to 2 years after deinfibulation; 94% of respondents would highly recommend the procedure, and all reported satisfaction.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

### Clitoral and vulvar reconstruction

Published reconstructive approaches include the following; a 40-study scoping review found that 38 studies evaluated clitoral reconstruction, mostly the Foldès technique or a variation.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

- **[Foldès clitoral reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/foldes-clitoral-reconstruction).** Scar excision, suspensory-ligament division and mobilization of the retained clitoral body. In a prospective, uncontrolled cohort of 2,938 adults with Type II or Type III FGM/C involving clitoral excision, 866 (29%) returned at one year. Of 841 respondents, 430 reported orgasm; among 53 with regular preoperative orgasm, 12 reported reduced orgasm afterward. Immediate complications occurred in 155/2,938 (5.3%).<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- **[O'Dey anatomical reconstruction with aOAP flap](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/aoap-flap).** The aOAP flap reconstructs the labia minora and other vulvovestibular tissue; the omega domed flap reconstructs the clitoral hood. In a retrospective series of 119 women with FGM/C, 36% received aOAP. Reductions in dysmenorrhea, dysuria and dyspareunia at one year were reported for the combined-technique cohort.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **[Mañero vaginal mucosal graft](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/manero-vaginal-graft).** A graft-based reconstructive variant; comparative effectiveness is uncertain.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **[FGM/C fat grafting](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/fgm-fat-grafting).** A 2024 scoping review included a five-woman fat-grafting report for FGM/C-related vulvar scars.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> In a later uncontrolled 13-patient series, scar-severity and sexual-function scores, including the pain domain, improved after autologous fat grafting at a mean 12.2-month follow-up.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

WHO 2025 conditionally suggests clitoral reconstruction for selected women, alongside sexual-health counseling, on very-low-certainty evidence.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> RCOG Green-top 53 (2015) predates most of this literature and advises against clitoral reconstruction.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> A 2026 systematic review of 13 studies (all but one using the Foldès technique) reported lower pain and better body image and sexual function after surgery, largely in uncontrolled before-and-after comparisons; all outcomes had very-low-certainty evidence. Hematoma, infection, suture failure, edema, prolonged postoperative pain and necrosis were reported. The review supported preoperative sexual-health counseling and multidisciplinary assessment.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> See also the [vulvar reconstruction landing and database](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/vulvar) for cross-indication algorithm placement.

## Legal Framework

- **US federal.** Federal Prohibition of FGM Act 1996 criminalized FGM/C on minors. A 2018 district-court ruling (*US v Nagarwala*) found the federal statute unconstitutional on commerce-clause grounds. The STOP FGM Act of 2020 revised the federal prohibition in 18 USC §116.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> State laws and reporting duties vary and require current jurisdiction-specific checking.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Vacation cutting**: Transport for Female Genital Mutilation Act 2013 criminalized taking a child abroad for FGM/C.
- **International.** UN frames FGM/C as torture and a human-rights violation; the 2025 WHO guideline emphasizes integrated health-system, legal, community-education, and survivor-centered approaches.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

## Culturally Sensitive Communication

Discussions require cultural humility without compromising the medical-risk and legal messaging. The AAP recommends standardized provider training in identification, treatment, and culturally appropriate communication.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[16]](#ref16)</sup> Defibulation decisions are often staged across multiple visits to address fear, family pressure, and mental-health needs; survivor- and partner-perspectives qualitative work supports flexible timing options.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
