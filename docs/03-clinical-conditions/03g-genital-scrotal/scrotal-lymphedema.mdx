# Scrotal Lymphedema

*Content in development.*

Massive scrotal lymphedema — the chronic dermal-and-subcutaneous lymphatic obstruction that produces an enlarged, indurated, and frequently macerated scrotum. Etiologies include primary (idiopathic) lymphedema, filariasis (the most common cause worldwide), morbid obesity, prior pelvic / inguinal lymphadenectomy, radiation, recurrent erysipelas / cellulitis, and hidradenitis suppurativa (when chronic perineal HS damages superficial lymphatics). Reconstructive management ranges from compression garments to total scrotectomy with split-thickness skin graft and tunneled / vascularized lymphatic transfer (VLNT) for staged lymphatic restoration.

For the inflammatory dermatologic condition that may co-exist or contribute, see [Hidradenitis Suppurativa (Genitourinary)](/docs/clinical-conditions/03g-genital-scrotal/hidradenitis-suppurativa). For surgical reconstruction technique, see [Scrotal Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/scrotal-reconstruction).
