# Small Penis Syndrome (SPS) &amp; Penile Dysmorphic Disorder (PDD)

**Cosmetic penile augmentation** encompasses a range of investigational and controversial procedures aimed at increasing penile length and/or girth for aesthetic or psychosexual purposes. The Sexual Medicine Society of North America (SMSNA) issued its first formal position statement in 2024, acknowledging the growing interest while emphasizing that most procedures remain investigational with limited high-quality evidence.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> The reconstructive urologist's first job is to distinguish men with objective micropenis from the much larger group with normal anatomy and dysmorphic distress (SPS or PDD), and to lead with multidisciplinary assessment before any procedural intervention.

---

## Part I: Definitions and clinical context

### Normal penile dimensions

The landmark systematic review (Veale et al., 2015; n = 15,521 men) established nomograms for penile size measured by health professionals:<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- **Flaccid pendulous length.** Mean 9.16 cm (SD 1.57).
- **Stretched length.** Mean 13.24 cm (SD 1.89).
- **Erect length.** Mean 13.12 cm (SD 1.66).
- **Flaccid circumference.** Mean 9.31 cm (SD 0.90).
- **Erect circumference.** Mean 11.66 cm (SD 1.10).

### Micropenis

Micropenis is a structurally normal but abnormally small penis, defined by stretched penile length more than 2.5 standard deviations below the mean for age (and population).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[39]](#ref39)[[37]](#ref37)</sup> Centimeter cutoffs depend on the reference and measurement method; the frequently quoted adult figure is a stretched length below about 7.5 cm, and pediatric references are roughly below 2 cm at birth and 4 cm after age 5.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[39]](#ref39)</sup> Measure stretched length from the pubic bone (or penopubic skin junction) to the glans tip, using a population-appropriate nomogram, and separate true micropenis from concealed penis (buried, webbed, trapped or megaprepuce), where the shaft is normal but hidden.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[37]](#ref37)</sup>

#### Micropenis: evaluation and hormonal treatment

- **Evaluation.** Classify as congenital, acquired or dysmorphophobic. Look for associated hypospadias, incomplete scrotal fusion or cryptorchidism, which point toward a difference of sex development (DSD). Work-up includes basal and hCG-stimulated gonadotropins, testosterone, DHT and androstenedione, and karyotype; hypothalamic-pituitary causes (including congenital hypogonadotropic hypogonadism and growth hormone deficiency), partial gonadal dysgenesis, testicular regression and testosterone biosynthesis or action defects are the main causes.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[39]](#ref39)[[40]](#ref40)</sup>
- **Infancy and prepuberty.** Testosterone (intramuscular or topical), topical DHT, or gonadotropin therapy is tried early; response is greatest in early infancy, and endocrine treatment is effective in the prepubertal setting only.<sup>[[39]](#ref39)[[40]](#ref40)[[41]](#ref41)</sup>
- **After puberty.** The EAU recommends against testosterone or other hormonal therapy to increase penile size in post-pubertal males; treat hypogonadism when present, but for its own indications.<sup>[[37]](#ref37)</sup>
- **Surgery for micropenis** has limited evidence and variable satisfaction; radial-forearm phalloplasty is the option for selected adults with severe deficiency and realistic expectations, whereas conservative lengthening or girth procedures give small gains with lower morbidity.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[39]](#ref39)[[41]](#ref41)</sup>

### Small penis syndrome (SPS) and penile dysmorphic disorder (PDD)

The vast majority of men seeking cosmetic penile augmentation have penile dimensions within the normal range but experience significant distress about perceived inadequacy.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

- **Small penis anxiety (SPA).** Worry about penile size without meeting criteria for a psychiatric disorder.
- **Penile dysmorphic disorder (PDD).** A form of body dysmorphic disorder (BDD), a DSM-5 psychiatric condition characterized by debilitating preoccupation with a minor or imagined physical flaw.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- BDD affects approximately 2.5% of the US adult population.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> In one small series of men seeking girth augmentation (n = 37), 11% screened positive on a questionnaire and 14% (4 of 29) met criteria on clinical interview.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Part II: Psychological assessment — a critical first step

Multiple expert reviews and the SMSNA emphasize that a multidisciplinary preoperative assessment including psychiatric or psychological evaluation is needed before any intervention.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> The canonical [Psychosexual Therapy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/psychosexual-therapy) page covers the sensate-focus or CBT or MBI or PLISSIT framework that should precede any cosmetic procedure in men with normal anatomy and dysmorphic distress.

Sharp et al. (2022) characterized men seeking penile girth augmentation (n = 37):<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- The most common motivation was "improve self-confidence" and not sexual function.
- Men perceived their actual penis size as significantly smaller than their ideal, the size they believed it "should be," and their expected post-augmentation size.
- Compared with non-clinical norms, these men had higher PDD symptoms, lower self-esteem, and lower body-image-related quality of life, but comparable psychological distress.
- Men with suspected BDD should be referred for psychiatric or psychological assessment and counseling before any procedure, and surgery should not be offered while BDD is untreated, because it rarely resolves the distress.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[9]](#ref9)[[37]](#ref37)</sup>

After non-surgical girth augmentation, Sharp et al. (2023) found that despite an average girth increase of 3.29 cm, men still perceived their penile dimensions as less than ideal, though the perceived discrepancy was smaller. Broader psychological well-being outcomes were mixed, with no significant changes in distress, self-esteem, or body-image quality of life.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Part III: Non-invasive / conservative approaches

Non-invasive methods should be considered as first-line treatment before surgical intervention.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

### A. Penile traction devices (extenders)

Penile traction therapy (PTT) applies sustained mechanical stretch to induce tissue elongation through cellular proliferation:<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

- Devices include Penimaster PRO, Andropenis, and RestoreX.
- Small studies report modest length gains with months of use; duration and daily wear requirements depend on the device and clinical population.
- Best evidence exists for Peyronie's disease (curvature correction) and post-prostatectomy length preservation.
- For cosmetic lengthening in otherwise healthy men, evidence is limited but encouraging.
- PTT has limited supporting evidence for penile elongation; this does not establish a guaranteed cosmetic benefit.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

An RCT of RestoreX post-prostatectomy (Toussi et al., 2021; n = 82) (82 randomized; 6-month data in 30 traction and 25 control men) demonstrated a +1.6 cm stretched-length gain versus +0.3 cm in controls (p&lt;0.01).<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

### B. Vacuum erection devices (VEDs)

VEDs create negative pressure to engorge the penis. Effective for erectile dysfunction; evidence for permanent penile lengthening is insufficient. VEDs may help maintain penile length post-prostatectomy but are not recommended as a primary cosmetic-augmentation tool.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

## Part IV: Injectable therapies for girth enhancement

### A. Hyaluronic acid (HA) fillers

HA injection is the most commonly studied and best-tolerated injectable for penile girth augmentation.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

- **Technique.** HA is injected into the subcutaneous plane (between dartos fascia and Buck's fascia) using a cannula, typically 20–40 mL per session.
- **Efficacy.** A multicenter RCT (Yang et al., 2019; n = 72) compared HA versus polylactic acid (PLA) fillers and found both achieved significant girth increases maintained to 48 weeks (mean increase 16.95 mm for HA, 13.49 mm for PLA). HA was greater at 4 weeks, but the difference was no longer significant at 48 weeks; 43% of participants dropped out.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- **Satisfaction.** Boiko et al. (2023; n = 132) reported mean girth enlargement of 1.7 cm for midshaft and 1.5 cm for glans, with significant improvements in sexual-relationship satisfaction, confidence, and self-esteem.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- **Limitations.** HA is resorbable (effects diminish over 12–24 months), requiring repeat injections; long-term follow-up data are limited.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[19]](#ref19)</sup>

**SMSNA position.** Temporary fillers have limited data; the society encourages safety and efficacy studies under approved research protocols. Explain temporary results, repeat treatment, and complications.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### B. Polylactic acid (PLA) fillers

PLA stimulates collagen production and may provide longer-lasting results than HA. The Yang et al. RCT showed comparable efficacy and safety to HA at 48 weeks.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

### C. Autologous fat injection

Fat grafting involves liposuction-harvested adipose tissue injected subcutaneously around the penile shaft.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

- **Advantages.** Autologous material; no foreign-body reaction.
- **Disadvantages.** Unpredictable fat resorption (30–70%), irregular contour, nodule formation, calcification.
- **Complications.** Fat necrosis, asymmetry, penile deformity requiring revision.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

### D. Unapproved / illicit substances — DANGEROUS

Injection of silicone, paraffin, mineral oil, petroleum jelly, and other non-medical substances, often performed in non-sterile conditions by non-medical practitioners, represents a major public-health concern:<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

- **Sclerosing lipogranuloma.** Chronic granulomatous foreign-body reaction causing pain, deformity, ulceration, and fibrosis.
- Pang et al. (2024) reported a 10-year single-center experience (n = 35); silicone (45.7%) and liquid paraffin (22.9%) were the most common substances; 91.4% required surgery, with 51% needing multiple procedures.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
- A systematic review of 68 studies; 78.8% of patients with foreign-body injections required surgical treatment, where excision and primary closure with or without skin grafts made up 85.1% of procedures, flaps 12.3%, and penile amputation was needed in 2 patients.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
- Complications include pain, erosion, inflammation, nodules, skin ulcers, necrosis, penile deformity, gangrene, and death.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

**Illicit injectables.** Silicone, paraffin, and mineral-oil injections can cause disabling sclerosing lipogranuloma. The high surgery rate above describes a referred clinical series, not the incidence among everyone ever injected.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

## Part V: Surgical techniques for length enhancement

### A. Suspensory ligament division (ligamentolysis)

The most established surgical technique for penile lengthening involves division of the suspensory ligament that anchors the penile root to the pubic symphysis, allowing the intracorporeal portion to advance anteriorly.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

- **Technique.** Typically performed through a suprapubic incision with V-Y plasty or Z-plasty of the skin; the fundiform and suspensory ligaments are divided down to the pubic symphysis.
- **Length gain.** Highly variable. 1.3 cm average in one series (range −1 to +3 cm); 2–4 cm in another; cadaveric study showed mean gain of 26.4 mm (range 4–60 mm).<sup>[[25]](#ref25)[[22]](#ref22)[[26]](#ref26)</sup>
- **Predictors.** Shorter pre-ligamentolysis length correlates with greater relative gain (r = −0.601; p = 0.014).<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>
- **Satisfaction.** Historically low: only 35% overall satisfaction in one series, and 27% in patients with PDD.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>
- **Limitation.** Gains are primarily in the flaccid state; erect-length gains are more modest. Erection angle may be altered (loss of upward angulation).

**Dead-space management.** Deskoulidi and Caminer (2023) described using distally based fat flaps advanced into the dead space created by ligament division, combined with fat injection for girth, achieving 2–4 cm flaccid-length gain in 75 patients with minimal complications.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

**Novel approach.** Karimian et al. (2026) described a trans-scrotal approach for suspensory ligament release in 21 patients, achieving 2.6 cm flaccid and 2.4 cm stretched-length gains with no major complications; 14 of the 21 patients had BDD, so it should not be read as support for operating on untreated BDD.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

**SMSNA position.** Suspensory-ligament division "may be considered" in select patients but should be performed by experienced surgeons with appropriate counseling regarding realistic expectations.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

### B. Sliding / slicing techniques (penile disassembly)

More aggressive techniques involve partial disassembly of the penis with interposition grafts to increase length. These carry higher complication rates (glans hypoesthesia or ischemia) and are reserved for specific pathologic indications: severe Peyronie's disease with marked shortening and complex deformity, typically combined with concurrent IPP. These operations are not appropriate for cosmetic length augmentation in men with normal anatomy and normal erectile function. For the full per-technique deep-dive (Rolle 2012 sliding, Egydio 2018 MUST, Perovic 1998 / 2003 disassembly antecedent, Egydio 2004 geometrical-principle relaxing incision) see the canonical Peyronie's-section page: [Sliding & Slicing (Penile-Disassembly) Techniques](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease/sliding-slicing-techniques).<sup>[[28]](#ref28)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**SMSNA position.** Sliding or slicing techniques "should be considered investigational" and performed only in the context of clinical research at specialized centers.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Part VI: Surgical techniques for girth enhancement

### A. Dermal / allograft wrapping

Grafts (acellular dermal matrix, bovine pericardium, or autologous dermal grafts) are placed in the subcutaneous plane around the penile shaft:<sup>[[29]](#ref29)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- The [Hardrock Sandwich Technique (HST)](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/hardrock-sandwich-technique) (Sertkaya et al., 2025; n = 456) combines bovine-pericardial grafts with autologous plasma gel as a "sandwich" construct plus SLD plus testicular-prosthesis placement, achieving a 43.1% girth increase (4.51 cm) and 20.8% length increase measured on postoperative day 1, with 83% reporting improved satisfaction at 1 year and no erectile or ejaculatory problems reported in that retrospective single-center cohort; this does not establish zero risk. Complications: seroma 6.1%, wound dehiscence 2.3%, infection 2.7%, hematoma 1.4%.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup>
- Graft implantation may have a negative impact on penile length due to tissue contraction.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

### B. Autologous scrotal dartos flap

Lei et al. (2022; n = 27) described turning a scrotal dartos flap into the plane between superficial penile fascia and Buck's fascia, achieving a 50.8% girth improvement (3.59 cm) with only minor adverse events.<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup>

### C. Silicone sleeve implants — Penuma® / Himplant

The Penuma and Himplant device family has 510(k) clearance for cosmetic augmentation; clearance does not establish long-term comparative safety.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)[[32]](#ref32)[[38]](#ref38)</sup> Himplant is the contemporary rebrand or next-generation iteration of the same device family; single category for indications, technique, complications, and explant counseling. For the full per-device deep-dive (design, FDA status, infrapubic versus concealed-scrotal technique, complete outcome series, removal-and-rehabilitation program, SMSNA 2024 position) see the canonical atlas page: [Penuma & Himplant](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/penuma).

| Parameter | Elist et al. (2018; n = 400) | Siegal et al. (2023; n = 49) |
|---|---|---|
| **Girth increase** | 56.7% (8.5 → 13.4 cm midshaft) | Not separately reported |
| **Length increase** | Not primary endpoint | 52% (8.1 → 12.3 cm flaccid) |
| **Satisfaction** | 81% "high" or "very high" at 2–6 years | Not reported |
| **Self-confidence improvement** | 83% at 6–8 weeks; 72% sustained at 2–6 years | — |
| **Seroma** | 4.8% | — |
| **Infection** | 3.3% | 2% (1 / 49) |
| **Erosion** | — | 4.1% (2 / 49) |
| **Device removal** | 3% | — |

<sup>[[31]](#ref31)[[33]](#ref33)</sup>

Elist et al. is a retrospective single-surgeon (device inventor) series: 526 men were operated on but only the 400 who consented were analyzed, and satisfaction used a nonvalidated instrument. Siegal et al. followed patients for a mean of 6 months.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)[[33]](#ref33)</sup>

**Complications.** Ramazan et al. (2026) noted that silicone implants carry risks of infection, erosion, seroma, and necrosis, with a removal rate of up to 10% in some series, resulting in severe penile deformities.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

**SMSNA position.** Silicone sleeve surgery should be limited to IRB oversight, long-term safety endpoints and no patient cost, with experienced surgeons and explicit counseling about potentially severe complications.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Furr et al. (2018) reported on 11 patients presenting to a referral center with severe complications of various genital-enlargement surgeries, including sexually disabling penile deformity, severe shortening, curvature, edema, non-healing wounds, and sexual dysfunction. Ten required corrective surgery, with 3 needing split-thickness skin grafting.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>

## Part VII: Combined procedures

Many surgeons now combine lengthening and girth enhancement in a single operation:<sup>[[22]](#ref22)[[28]](#ref28)[[29]](#ref29)</sup>

- Suspensory-ligament division and fat injection (Deskoulidi & Caminer, 2023).<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- Suspensory-ligament release and bovine-pericardial graft and plasma gel and testicular prosthesis ([Hardrock Sandwich Technique](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/hardrock-sandwich-technique)).<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup>
- Suprapubic liposuction and ligament release and acellular dermal matrix spacer (Zhang et al., 2019).<sup>[[34]](#ref34)</sup>

The systematic review by Vyas et al. (2020; 1,192 patients) found that combined augmentation had the highest complication rate (14.6% pooled), compared with length-only or girth-only procedures.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>

## Part VIII: Complications — comprehensive overview

| Modality | Common complications | Severe complications |
|---|---|---|
| **HA / PLA fillers** | Injection-site reactions, edema, nodules | Rare; migration, vascular occlusion (theoretical) |
| **Autologous fat** | Fat resorption (30–70%), asymmetry, nodules | Fat necrosis, penile deformity |
| **Illicit injectables** (silicone, paraffin, mineral oil) | Sclerosing lipogranuloma, pain, deformity | Necrosis, gangrene, penile amputation, death |
| **Dermal / allograft wrapping** | Seroma, wound dehiscence, infection | Skin necrosis, graft failure, penile shortening |
| **Silicone sleeve (Penuma / Himplant)** | Seroma (4.8%), scar (4.5%), infection (3.3%) | Erosion, severe deformity, removal (up to 10%) |
| **Suspensory-ligament division** | Altered erection angle, scar contracture | Penile instability, retraction, dissatisfaction |

<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[18]](#ref18)[[23]](#ref23)[[24]](#ref24)[[25]](#ref25)[[28]](#ref28)[[31]](#ref31)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

**Imaging of complications.** Abo-Hedibah et al. (2021) reviewed imaging features of inflammatory complications after filler injection: granuloma, abscess, and cellulitis extending to the scrotum, perineum, or deep pelvic organs. Ultrasound, MRI, and ascending urethrography play important roles in diagnosis.<sup>[[35]](#ref35)</sup>

## Part IX: SMSNA position statements (2024)

The SMSNA issued 6 position statements covering 5 distinct procedure categories:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

1. **Injectable soft-tissue fillers.** Limited temporary benefit; SMSNA encourages approved research protocols.
2. **Suspensory-ligament division.** May be considered for length enhancement in select patients; realistic expectations are necessary; satisfaction rates are historically low.
3. **Graft-and-flap procedures.** Should be considered investigational; limited data on long-term outcomes.
4. **Silicone sleeve implants (Penuma and Himplant).** Investigational; SMSNA recommends IRB oversight, long-term safety tracking and no patient cost.
5. **[Sliding / slicing techniques](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease/sliding-slicing-techniques).** PD-specific, not cosmetic; investigational; performed only in research settings at specialized centers.
6. **General.** A multidisciplinary approach including psychological screening is needed; patients with BDD should be referred for psychiatric treatment rather than surgery.

The EAU Sexual and Reproductive Health guideline (2026 chapter on penile size abnormalities and dysmorphophobia) is consistent: ask about BDD or PDD in men with normal-sized penises who want enlargement and refer suspected BDD for mental health care (strong); do not use silicone, paraffin or petroleum jelly for girth enhancement (strong); do not use HA, other fillers or fat injection for girth in men with PDD; and do not use hormonal therapy to increase penile size after puberty. It also notes that high-level evidence and standardized outcome measures are lacking, that traction therapy has acceptable reported outcomes, and that surgical lengthening or girth procedures should follow extensive counseling.<sup>[[37]](#ref37)[[40]](#ref40)</sup>

## Part X: Management algorithm

Based on the SMSNA position statement and expert consensus:<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

1. **Comprehensive evaluation.** History, physical examination with standardized penile measurements, assessment for micropenis versus SPS or PDD.
2. **Psychological screening.** Use a BDD-focused assessment and refer when suspected; MGSIS and IIEF-5 assess genital self-image and erectile function, not BDD diagnosis.
3. **Patient counseling.** Discuss normal penile-size nomograms; address unrealistic expectations; provide information on all treatment options.
4. **Conservative management first:**
   - Psychological / sexual counseling.
   - Penile traction therapy (4–6 month trial).
   - Weight management when relevant to buried / concealed penis; liposuction is a procedure, not conservative care.
5. **Procedural discussion.** Failure of counseling or traction does not create an indication for escalation to grafts or implants. Apply the procedure-specific research restrictions above; avoid augmentation in untreated PDD. Discuss risks and the option of no procedure.
   - **Buried penis.** Escutcheonectomy, panniculectomy, suprapubic liposuction (these are reconstructive, not purely cosmetic; see [Adult-Acquired Buried Penis](/docs/clinical-conditions/03g-genital-scrotal/buried-penis)).
6. **Follow-up.** Long-term monitoring for complications; psychological reassessment.

## Part XI: Key principles and caveats

1. **Most men seeking augmentation have normal penile dimensions.** Careful psychological screening is mandatory to identify BDD or PDD.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
2. **High-quality evidence is limited.** Randomized filler studies exist, but overall study quality is low, with inconsistent methodology for measuring outcomes and reporting complications.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)[[36]](#ref36)</sup>
3. **Non-invasive approaches should be offered first.** Traction may be discussed with realistic expectations about limited evidence.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
4. **HA fillers are the best-tolerated injectable** for girth but are temporary and require repeat treatments.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
5. **Suspensory-ligament division provides modest, variable length gains** with historically low satisfaction rates (27–35%).<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>
6. **[Penuma / Himplant](/docs/surgical-techniques/04l-cosmetic-genital-surgery/penuma)** carries risks of erosion, infection, removal, shortening and deformity; the cited series do not define a reliable maximum complication rate.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
7. **Illicit injections (silicone, paraffin, mineral oil) are extremely dangerous** and can cause devastating complications including gangrene, amputation, and death.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
8. **Complications are likely under-reported.** Referral-center series reveal severe deformities and functional compromise in men with previously normal anatomy.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
9. **Satisfaction does not always correlate with objective size change.** Psychosocial improvement may be independent of measured augmentation.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
10. **A multidisciplinary approach** involving urology, plastic surgery, psychology or psychiatry, and sexual medicine is necessary for optimal patient selection and outcomes.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
