# Peyronie's Disease

**Peyronie's disease (PD)** is an acquired fibrotic disorder of the tunica albuginea that produces palpable plaque, penile curvature, painful erections, erectile dysfunction, shortening, and substantial psychological morbidity. Prevalence is most often estimated at 3–9% of adult men (reports range 0.1–20.3%, highest with diabetes and ED), with mean onset about 53 years.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[51]](#ref51)[[1]](#ref1)</sup> In an untreated acute-phase subset, penile deformity resolved spontaneously in 3.2% of 63 men after a mean 8.4 months; in a separate untreated cohort of 246 men assessed at a mean disease duration of 18 ± 7 months, 12% of those with curvature improved.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The single FDA-approved non-surgical treatment is intralesional collagenase clostridium histolyticum (CCH, Xiaflex); surgery is an option for stable disease with functionally significant deformity when treatment is desired.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

The AUA defines PD as "an acquired penile abnormality characterized by fibrosis of the tunica albuginea, which may be accompanied by pain, deformity, erectile dysfunction, and/or distress." It belongs to a broader family of superficial fibrosing disorders with Dupuytren's contracture, Ledderhose disease, and tympanosclerosis.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

This page covers the disease and its medical management. For the operative atlas, see the [Peyronie's Disease Surgery section](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease): [Tunica Plication](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease/tunica-plication), [Plaque Incision/Excision and Grafting](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease/plaque-incision-grafting), and [Prosthesis with Straightening](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/peyronies-disease/prosthesis-with-straightening).

## Epidemiology

- **Prevalence** 3.2–8.9% in most population estimates (0.1–20.3% across reports; likely under-reported)<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[51]](#ref51)</sup>
- **Mean age at onset** about 53 years in the Cochrane review; a separate 307-man series reported mean age at presentation of 52.8 years (range 23–76).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Diabetic men with ED.** PD was found in 42/206 (20.3%) diabetic men evaluated for ED in one study; the AUA guideline reports 20.3% as the upper end across specific populations.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[10]](#ref10)[[64]](#ref64)</sup>
- White men over-represented (adjusted OR 8.47, wide CI 1.98–36.2, in a Brazilian case-control study)<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- Strong association with Dupuytren's contracture<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>: 26% (95% CI 17–35%) of men presenting for Dupuytren contracture reported PD-like symptoms in a survey of 85 respondents<sup>[[54]](#ref54)</sup>, and a genetic-association study found the WNT2 locus on chromosome 7, already linked to Dupuytren's disease, was also associated with PD, supporting a shared genetic basis.<sup>[[55]](#ref55)</sup>

## Pathophysiology

PD is a penile wound-healing disorder in genetically predisposed men.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

1. **Inciting event.** Microvascular trauma to the tunica from penile buckling during intercourse (often unrecalled)
2. **Inflammatory phase.** Extravascular protein deposition, fibrin trapping, macrophage recruitment, cytokine overexpression (TGF-β, IL-1, IL-6, PDGF, FGF), elastase release
3. **Fibroproliferative phase.** MMP/TIMP imbalance (decreased MMPs, increased TIMPs), excessive collagen deposition, shift from type I to predominantly type III collagen
4. **Plaque maturation.** Progressive fibrosis, calcification in some, stable scar

**TGF-β** is the central profibrotic mediator; PAI-1, PTX-3, HIF, and IgG4 also contribute. Oxidative stress links PD to systemic metabolic disease.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

## Risk Factors

| Factor | Association |
|---|---|
| **Penile trauma / buckling** | Proposed trigger for abnormal tunical wound healing in susceptible men<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Dupuytren's contracture** | Shared fibrotic diathesis<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Diabetes mellitus** | In a retrospective series of 197 men with both diabetes and PD, early-onset and poorly controlled diabetes correlated with PD complications<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Hypertension, hyperlipidemia, smoking** | Common comorbidities; mostly tied to concurrent ED rather than PD severity<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| **NAFLD / insulin resistance** | Associated with PD in one cross-sectional pilot (49 cases, 50 controls); NAFLD was the only logistic-regression predictor in that dataset, and the pathogenic link remains hypothetical.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| **Prior urologic instrumentation** | TURP, cystoscopy (case-control association)<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Radical prostatectomy** | In a selected cohort of 1,011 men attending a sexual-medicine clinic after RP, 15.9% developed PD within 3 years (mean 13.9 ± 0.7 months); younger age and white race were associated with PD, but RP causality was not established.<sup>[[52]](#ref52)</sup> |
| **Thiazide diuretics** | Adjusted OR 2.29 in a single case-control study<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **White race** | Adjusted OR 8.47<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |

In a retrospective series of 469 men with PD, comorbidity count or severity did not correlate with curvature severity, while hypertension, lipid abnormalities and diabetes were more prevalent among those with concurrent ED.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> A separate retrospective series of 197 men with both diabetes and PD found associations of early diabetes onset and rising HbA1c with earlier PD onset or plaque formation.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> New curvature developing within about a year of radical prostatectomy fits a recognized PD pattern rather than an unrelated finding.<sup>[[52]](#ref52)</sup>

A pilot study of 121 men with PD found testosterone deficiency (total testosterone &lt;300 ng/dL and/or free testosterone &lt;1.5 ng/dL) in 74.4%; men meeting that definition had greater mean curvature than those with normal testosterone (54.3° versus 37.1°, P = .006), and free testosterone inversely correlated with curvature (r = −0.314, P = .016).<sup>[[53]](#ref53)</sup> The pilot study reported an association but did not evaluate routine testosterone screening for PD alone.<sup>[[53]](#ref53)</sup>

## Disease Phases

| Phase | Duration | Features | Implications |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Acute (active)** | Typically 12–18 months from onset | Painful erections, evolving deformity, plaque maturation | In a separate long-term survey, 42% reported penile pain and 64% of those with pain reported complete resolution; in an untreated recent-onset cohort, 89% of men with pain reported resolution. Non-surgical interventions favored<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)[[16]](#ref16)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Chronic (stable)** | After active phase | Fibrosis stabilizes, pain typically gone, fixed deformity | The AUA defines stable disease as symptoms clinically quiescent or unchanged for at least 3 months (pain, if present, is less common and usually with erection); surgical series most often required 12 months of symptoms with 3–6 months of stable curvature. The EAU accepts pain resolution and curvature stability for at least 3 months as criteria of stabilization<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[51]](#ref51)</sup> |

Trost, Mulhall and Hellstrom (2024) proposed the PTNM classification from a retrospective review of 1,098 men with penile deformity, with a mailed survey of 719 men for the relapsing-remitting subtype. It identified four Peyronie's subtypes (classical or nonclassical, calcifying, progressive and relapsing-remitting) and three non-Peyronie's curvature categories (congenital, maturational and trauma-induced), recorded as P (Peyronie's subtype), T (trauma, absent or present), N (non-Peyronie's component) and M (mode, stable or active). Stable disease was defined by subtype: at least 3 months for classical disease, and for progressive, calcifying or trauma-induced disease either 12 months or more with at least 3 months of stability or at least 6 months of stability. Penile pain did not reliably predict progression or stability. PTNM is a proposed system derived from retrospective data, not an established staging standard.<sup>[[62]](#ref62)</sup>

## Natural History

| Outcome | Rate |
|---|---|
| **Curvature worsened** | 48% in an untreated cohort; 20% self-reported among 125 eligible long-term survey responders. Penile deformity deteriorated in 30.2% of a separate 63-man acute-phase subset<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| **Curvature stable** | 40% in an untreated cohort and 37% self-reported among 125 eligible long-term survey responders; penile deformity was unchanged in 66.7% of a separate 63-man acute-phase subset<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| **Curvature spontaneously improved** | Curvature improved in 12% of men with curvature in one untreated cohort; penile deformity resolved completely in 3.2% of 63 men in a separate untreated acute-phase subset<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Pain resolution** | Complete resolution in 64% of survey responders who reported penile pain and 89% of men with pain in an untreated recent-onset cohort<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| **Penile shortening** | 65% self-reported among 125 eligible survey completers at a median 8.4 years since onset; mean stretched flaccid length fell from 12.2 to 11.4 cm by a mean disease duration of 18 ± 7 months in an untreated cohort<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| **Erectile function worsened** | 60% of 125 eligible long-term survey completers self-reported decline (ED already present in 32–54% at presentation in two separate cohorts)<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| **Negative self-esteem impact** | 60% of 88 survey respondents among 514 eligible conservatively managed men reported that PD negatively affected their self-esteem<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> |

Among 125 eligible respondents to a survey of conservatively managed patients, the median interval from symptom onset to survey was 8.4 years; 43% reported curvature resolution, while most reported length loss and worsening sexual function and psychosocial distress.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

## Clinical Presentation

- **Penile curvature.** Dorsal (45–72%), lateral (11–29%), ventral (about 17%)<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Palpable plaque**
- **Painful erection.** Acute phase
- **ED** in 32–54% at presentation across two cohorts; probability rises with age &gt; 60, duration &gt; 12 mo, and ≥ 1 vascular risk factor (86.7%)<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Penile shortening.** Reported by 65% of 125 eligible survey completers who had been conservatively managed (median 8.4 years since symptom onset)<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Hourglass deformity or hinge effect.** Buckling instability that defeats penetration
- **Substantial psychological distress.** 81% emotionally distressed; about 50% depression; 54% relationship difficulties<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

### Psychological burden

A Swedish 3.5-million-man cohort:<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

| Outcome | HR (vs general population) |
|---|---|
| Depression | 1.7 |
| Anxiety disorder | 1.9 |
| Self-injurious behavior | 2.0 |

5-year cumulative depression risk 7.0% versus 4.4%; 10-year 12.5% versus 8.7%.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[20]](#ref20)</sup> An online-forum thematic analysis reported depressed mood in 33% and isolation in 18% of 225 patient posters; among 52 partner posters, 21% reported sexual dissatisfaction and 7.7% dyspareunia. Across all 277 posters, relationship disruption was reported by 5.1% and termination by 3.6%.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> The AUA calls for documenting distress during evaluation and discussing treatment options; psychological referral may be considered when distress is significant. See the canonical [Psychosexual Therapy](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction/psychosexual-therapy) page for the sensate-focus or CBT or MBI or couples-therapy framework.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Diagnostic Evaluation

All four major guidelines (AUA, EAU, CUA, ISSM) converge on the workup.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

| Step | Role |
|---|---|
| **History** (with genital examination) | Onset, duration, deformity, pain, shortening, erectile function, prior treatment, sexual / psychological impact |
| **Photographs of erect penis** (patient-provided) | Document deformity for objective comparison<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> |
| **Physical exam** | Stretch and palpate the flaccid penis for plaque size, location and possible calcification; document stretched or erect penile length and assess curvature during erection.<sup>[[51]](#ref51)</sup> |
| **Intracavernosal injection (ICI) test** | AUA recommends before invasive intervention (with or without duplex); documents curvature, hinge effect and erectile response<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **Penile duplex Doppler ultrasound** (optional) | Calcified vs non-calcified plaque; vascular integrity (PSV, EDV); identifies vasculogenic ED that changes the operation<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[22]](#ref22)[[23]](#ref23)[[24]](#ref24)</sup> |
| **PDQ and IIEF questionnaires** | Symptom bother and erectile function domains |
| **MRI** (select cases) | Thin-section T2-weighted MRI can characterize plaques and demonstrate subtle deformities such as corporal narrowing better than ultrasound, though it shows calcification poorly; cost and availability limit it to select-case use, not routine workup<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> |

Hinge instability can be assessed by applying axial pressure to the glans during erection and observing whether the shaft buckles, with instability recorded as none, mild, moderate or severe; this grading is arbitrary and unvalidated. Each curve is measured separately, with the maximal curve's distance from the coronal sulcus recorded to help localize the site for injection, and angle measurement is best deferred until erection rigidity exceeds 80%, since curvature varies with rigidity — a center-specific threshold rather than a validated cutoff. This approach has been described by Mulhall and Jenkins.<sup>[[61]](#ref61)</sup>

Objective measurement is preferred over patient self-report: before standardized photographs and protractor measurement during full erection, 54% of patients overestimated and 26% underestimated curvature, with only 20% accurate within 5°.<sup>[[56]](#ref56)</sup> Ultrasound can detect sonographic variants without a palpable abnormality: in a series of 722 men evaluated for PD or ED, isolated septal scarring and punctate scarring were found in 217 (30%) and 197 (27%), respectively; physical examination was normal in 72/217 (33%) and 148/197 (75%) of those subgroups. A separate imaging review describes ultrasound detection of gross plaque calcifications, which can inform treatment planning; calcification and CCH candidacy are discussed below.<sup>[[57]](#ref57)[[58]](#ref58)</sup>

## Management

Treatment is dictated by disease phase, deformity, erectile function, and patient goals. A 2026 Guideline of Guidelines comparison found broad consensus across AUA, EAU, CUA, and ISSM.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

### Important caveat on non-surgical evidence

The 2023 Cochrane systematic review (14 RCTs, 1,810 men) found little evidence for most non-surgical therapies. Collagenase reduced curvature by a mean 6.9° at 52 weeks versus placebo (moderate certainty) with more adverse events (RR 2.32); traction (7.4°, one trial) and verapamil or ESWT estimates were very low certainty.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

### A. Oral therapies

The AUA position:<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- **Should not** offer vitamin E, tamoxifen, procarbazine, omega-3 fatty acids, or vitamin E with propionyl-L-carnitine (Moderate Recommendation; Grade B for vitamin E, omega-3 and vitamin E plus carnitine, Grade C for tamoxifen and procarbazine). The panel adds that oral therapy is not appropriate for stable disease
- May offer oral NSAIDs for pain in active disease (Expert Opinion)
- Evidence for pentoxifylline, potassium para-aminobenzoate (Potaba), colchicine and coenzyme Q10 is insufficient even for a conditional AUA recommendation<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

| Agent | Mechanism | Evidence | AUA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vitamin E | Antioxidant | No benefit over placebo<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> | Should not offer (Moderate; Grade B) |
| Tamoxifen | Anti-fibrotic | No benefit over placebo<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> | Should not offer (Moderate; Grade C) |
| Pentoxifylline | PDE inhibitor / anti-inflammatory | Insufficient evidence<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | No recommendation |
| Potaba | Anti-fibrotic | Uncertain benefit<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | No recommendation |
| Colchicine | Anti-inflammatory | Insufficient evidence<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | No recommendation |

### B. Intralesional injection therapies

#### Collagenase clostridium histolyticum (CCH, Xiaflex) — only FDA-approved option<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[26]](#ref26)[[27]](#ref27)[[28]](#ref28)</sup>

- **US label:** Adult men with palpable plaque and curvature ≥ 30° at treatment initiation; XIAFLEX is contraindicated for a plaque involving the penile urethra or in a patient with prior hypersensitivity to XIAFLEX or therapeutic collagenase. AUA selection is narrower: stable disease, curvature >30° and &lt;90°, and intact erectile function with or without medication, given with clinician and patient modeling (Moderate Recommendation; Grade B), with counseling on ecchymosis, swelling, pain and corporal rupture before starting (Clinical Principle).<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Protocol (IMPRESS):** Up to 4 cycles about 6 weeks apart (8 injections total); each cycle has two 0.58-mg injections 1–3 days apart, clinician modeling 1–3 days after the second injection, and daily home modeling for 6 weeks. Do not give subsequent cycles if curvature is &lt;15° after an earlier cycle or further treatment is not clinically indicated.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[26]](#ref26)</sup>
- **IMPRESS I & II (832 randomized; 612 in the label-defined mITT efficacy population):** A post hoc pooled analysis found mean curvature improvement of 17.0° (34%) with CCH versus 9.3° (18.2%) with placebo, a net difference of about 7.7° (Cochrane estimate 6.9° at 52 weeks).<sup>[[26]](#ref26)[[5]](#ref5)[[1]](#ref1)</sup> PDQ bother scores fell by 2.8 versus 1.8 points, and three corporal ruptures were surgically repaired.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)[[26]](#ref26)</sup>
- **Real-world multi-institutional (n = 918):** Mean curvature 48.2° to 32.9° (30.1% improvement); 68.7% achieved ≥ 20% improvement; completing ≥ 4 cycles predicted better outcomes<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>. In a separate single-center registry, 296 men received at least one CCH injection, but 84 had interval-to-final curvature measurements after completing 8 injections. Among those with ≤10° improvement after the first 2 cycles, 70% had further curvature improvement over the final 2 cycles, versus 31% of those with &gt;10° initial improvement — a figure for counseling a man discouraged after 2 cycles against stopping early.<sup>[[60]](#ref60)</sup>
- **Calcification predicts a smaller response:** men with no plaque calcification had significantly greater curvature improvement with CCH than those with moderate or severe calcification (28.1° versus 10.3°, P = .04).<sup>[[59]](#ref59)</sup>
- **Meta-analysis (n = 1,480):** 35% pooled curvature improvement; 41% bother improvement<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>
- **AEs and precautions:** Bruising, swelling and pain are common; corporal rupture and severe hematoma, sometimes followed by skin necrosis, are important risks. The label also warns of anaphylaxis, acute post-injection back pain, and pain-associated syncope or presyncope.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Follow REMS and label counseling: avoid XIAFLEX in coagulation disorders or with anticoagulants other than low-dose aspirin, do not use a vacuum erection device during treatment, and avoid sexual activity between injections and for at least 4 weeks after the second injection until pain and swelling resolve.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[27]](#ref27)[[28]](#ref28)[[29]](#ref29)</sup>
- **No significant penile shortening**<sup>[[29]](#ref29)[[30]](#ref30)</sup>
- **Available in the US; withdrawn from Europe**<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

#### Other intralesional agents

- **Interferon-α2b.** The 2015 AUA guideline states clinicians may administer it (Moderate Recommendation; Grade C), on one placebo-controlled trial with mean curvature reduction 13.5° versus 4.5° with placebo, and asks for counseling about sinusitis, flu-like symptoms and minor penile swelling.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> Historical curvature or pain benefit, but EAU 2026 no longer recommends it given limited evidence and withdrawal from US and European markets.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)[[51]](#ref51)</sup>
- **Verapamil.** Intralesional evidence is weak and inconsistent; AUA 2015 offers a Conditional Recommendation (Grade C) that clinicians may offer it and a Moderate Recommendation (Grade C) against electromotive delivery.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[25]](#ref25)[[31]](#ref31)</sup>
- **Hyaluronic acid.** EAU offers a weak acute-phase option with limited evidence; this is not an FDA-approved PD indication.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[51]](#ref51)</sup>

### C. Mechanical therapies

#### Penile traction therapy (PTT)

Mechano-transduction increases MMP activity and remodels collagen.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

- **RestoreX.** The most studied modern device; only 30–90 min/day required (versus 3–8 h for older devices)<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup>
  - **RCT (n = 110 randomized):** At 3 months, mean composite curvature changed −11.7° in 62 traction users versus +1.3° in 27 controls (p &lt; 0.01)<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup>
  - **6-month open-label, as-treated subgroup (n = 37 using RestoreX ≥15 min/day):** 95% had length gains (mean 2.0–2.2 cm); 61% had curve improvements (16.8–21.4°)<sup>[[33]](#ref33)</sup>
  - **Combined with CCH (nonrandomized single-center registry; 113 of 287 men with traction-use data completed therapy with objective outcomes):** RestoreX plus CCH was associated with 49% mean curvature improvement versus 31% with CCH alone; the RestoreX group was 6.9× more likely than the other groups to achieve ≥20° improvement<sup>[[34]](#ref34)</sup>. Across heterogeneous CCH combination regimens in seven comparative studies (532 men), a 2024 meta-analysis found greater length gain than with CCH alone (mean difference 0.81 cm, 95% CI 0.17–1.45), but no significant additional curvature benefit.<sup>[[65]](#ref65)</sup>
- **Penimaster PRO.** A separate 12-week randomized trial of 93 selected men with stable PD (47 device, 46 observation) used 3–8 h/day and showed greater curvature reduction than observation; it did not compare Penimaster PRO with RestoreX.<sup>[[35]](#ref35)[[66]](#ref66)</sup>

#### Other modalities

- **Extracorporeal shockwave therapy (ESWT).** May relieve acute-phase pain (AUA Conditional Recommendation; Grade B); do not offer to correct curvature or plaque size (AUA Moderate Recommendation; Grade B). AUA also advises against radiotherapy (Moderate; Grade C).<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[51]](#ref51)</sup>
- **Vacuum erection device.** Limited evidence; modest benefits in small series<sup>[[31]](#ref31)[[35]](#ref35)</sup>

### D. Surgical treatment — gold standard for stable PD with functional deformity

Guidelines: surgery for stable disease (EAU: pain resolved and curvature stable ≥ 3 months) by experienced surgeons.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

**EAU 2026 surgical recommendations.** Operate only when disease is stable and intercourse is compromised by the deformity (strong), after assessing length, curvature, erectile function including response to pharmacotherapy, and expectations (strong). Use tunical shortening first for adequate length and rigidity, less severe curvature and no hourglass or hinge deformity (weak); use lengthening for normal erectile function with inadequate length, severe curvature or complex deformity (weak); no procedure or graft has proven superior. Do not use the sliding technique because of the risk of glans necrosis (strong). Degloving with circumcision is the usual approach, and non-absorbable or slowly absorbed sutures such as polydioxanone are advised. In the EAU's pooled data, shortening procedures straighten more than 85% of men, with recurrence and hypoesthesia around 10%, and shortening of 1–1.5 cm in 22–69%; lengthening procedures carry a risk of de novo ED up to 50% in men without preoperative ED, dermal grafts a veno-occlusive ED rate near 20%, and synthetic grafts (Dacron, Gore-Tex) and cadaveric dura should be avoided.<sup>[[51]](#ref51)</sup>

**Levine surgical algorithm** (widely adopted; angular cutoffs guide discussion rather than determine the operation):<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[36]](#ref36)[[37]](#ref37)</sup>

- **Adequate rigidity, mild–moderate curvature (&lt; 60°), no hourglass/hinge** → Tunical plication
- **Adequate rigidity, severe curvature (&gt; 60–70°), hourglass/hinge, significant shortening** → Plaque incision/partial excision and grafting
- **Refractory ED ± curvature** → Inflatable penile prosthesis ± modeling, plication, or grafting

#### 1. Tunical plication (Nesbit / 16-dot / modified Yachia)

- **The most common reconstructive PD operation** (about 50% of surgeries among PD patients who undergo reconstruction)<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Curvature improvement:** The AUA reports rates of at least 90% in most plication studies; in one prospective cohort, 14 plication patients had a mean curvature reduction of 72.0° (92.2%)<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[38]](#ref38)</sup>
- **Penile shortening.** In one prospective comparison, the 14-man plication arm had a mean stretched penile length change of −1.0 cm<sup>[[38]](#ref38)</sup>
- Best candidate: intact erectile function, curvature &lt; 60°<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[37]](#ref37)</sup>
- Lower risk of de novo ED than grafting; no graft required
- **Wound complications across plication and grafting:** In a 189-patient mixed surgical cohort, 12/189 (6.3%) had wound complications within 3 months; ventral and subcoronal incision rates did not differ significantly (9/94 versus 3/95; P = .081)<sup>[[39]](#ref39)</sup>

#### 2. Plaque incision (or partial excision) and grafting

- **Grafting outcomes use different endpoints:** The AUA reports curvature improvement in &gt;80% of patients in most study arms; a 25-man PEG cohort had a mean 94.9% curvature reduction, while a BMG meta-analysis reported 98.6% pooled success<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[38]](#ref38)[[40]](#ref40)</sup>
- Stretched penile length increased by a mean 0.9 cm in the 25-man PEG arm of one comparative cohort; preservation or gain is not guaranteed<sup>[[38]](#ref38)</sup>
- Best candidate: intact erectile function, severe curvature (&gt; 60–70°), hourglass / hinge / significant shortening<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[37]](#ref37)</sup>
- **De novo ED** varies substantially; counsel that risk can reach 50% in some grafting series.<sup>[[40]](#ref40)[[41]](#ref41)[[51]](#ref51)</sup>
- **Compound curvature.** In a retrospective series, 79% of 161 men treated with PEG plus supplemental TAP could engage in penetrative intercourse with or without pharmacotherapy, and 12.4% had recurrent curvature at mean 61-month follow-up<sup>[[42]](#ref42)</sup>

**Graft materials:**

| Material | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Buccal mucosa graft (BMG)** | Autologous | Favorable pooled outcomes in mostly single-arm series, often with lower preoperative curvature; no randomized superiority<sup>[[40]](#ref40)[[41]](#ref41)[[43]](#ref43)</sup> |
| **Tunica vaginalis** | Autologous | 90% technical success (residual ≤ 15°) at 24 mo, similar to BMG in a 40-patient comparison<sup>[[43]](#ref43)</sup> |
| **Bovine pericardium** | Xenograft | Off-the-shelf graft used for plaque incision and grafting without prosthesis; available series do not establish superiority over other grafts<sup>[[41]](#ref41)</sup> |
| **TachoSil (collagen fleece)** | Hemostatic patch | Reported operative-time advantages; no proven best graft<sup>[[38]](#ref38)[[45]](#ref45)</sup> |
| **Dermis** | Autologous | Historical standard<sup>[[41]](#ref41)</sup> |
| **Small intestinal submucosa (SIS)** | Xenograft | Variable<sup>[[38]](#ref38)</sup> |

The 2026 BMG meta-analysis reported 98.6% straightening, 92.1% satisfaction, 1.7% de novo ED and 1.1% shortening in selected studies; comparisons do not establish equivalence or superiority over other grafts.<sup>[[40]](#ref40)</sup>

#### 3. Inflatable penile prosthesis (IPP)

- **Indication:** PD with ED and/or deformity sufficient to prevent coitus despite pharmacotherapy or vacuum therapy (AUA Moderate Recommendation; Grade C); AUA advises an inflatable device (Expert Opinion)<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[46]](#ref46)</sup>
- **Addresses both ED and curvature** in a single operation<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Multicenter cohort (499 PD-IPP patients):**<sup>[[47]](#ref47)</sup>
  - Mean preop curvature 39.4°
  - **17.6% underwent IPP placement without an adjunctive correction procedure**<sup>[[47]](#ref47)</sup>
  - **82.4% received adjunctive correction.** Manual modeling with or without scratch was used in 74.7% (median 26° correction), plication in 4.8% (median 40°), grafting in 2.0% (median 55°), and combined grafting and modeling in 0.8%.<sup>[[47]](#ref47)</sup>
- In a separate selected prosthesis series, satisfaction was assessed at 6 months<sup>[[48]](#ref48)</sup>. In PROPPER, 1-year data were available for 177/250 men and 2-year data for 130/250; among assessed patients, &gt;80% were satisfied or very satisfied and &gt;88% reported device use<sup>[[49]](#ref49)</sup>
- **Self-reported depression in optional PROPPER responses:** 36/187 (19.3%) at baseline versus 6/57 (10.5%) at 1 year (p = 0.02)<sup>[[49]](#ref49)</sup>
- In a selected retrospective series, 29/33 (87.9%) PD patients without ED reported satisfaction 6 months after first-line prosthesis; this does not support routine first-line use with preserved erections.<sup>[[48]](#ref48)[[51]](#ref51)</sup>
- **Scratch technique and postoperative VED.** Combined approach achieving residual curvature of 7.7–9.1° after 24 weeks of vacuum therapy and IIEF-5 rising from 9.8 to 24.1 at 1 yr (145 selected patients)<sup>[[50]](#ref50)</sup>

---

### E. Comparative outcomes

A randomized trial enrolled 40 men (19 per arm with three-month data). At three months, surgery plus RestoreX and sildenafil produced a median 84% reduction in summed two-plane curvature versus 54% with CCH plus the same adjuncts; median flaccid coronal length changed −0.5 versus +1.0 cm, and 50 versus 13 persistent adverse events were recorded. The primary “very satisfied” response was 21% versus 50% (P = .08).<sup>[[63]](#ref63)</sup>

The following table summarizes an earlier prospective nonrandomized multi-institutional comparison (n = 57) of CCH, plication and PEG; selection and baseline deformity differ, limiting causal treatment comparisons:<sup>[[38]](#ref38)</sup>

| Outcome | CCH | Tunical plication | PEG |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mean curvature correction | 23.3° (34.4%) | 72.0° (92.2%) | 71.8° (94.9%) |
| Penile length change | −0.2 cm | −1.0 cm | +0.9 cm |
| IIEF-5 change | +5.7 | +4.9 | +2.2 |

In a retrospective series of 90 men with persistent curvature after a shortened three-injection CCH protocol, subsequent grafting with or without IPP or Nesbit surgery was feasible; 86/90 had no reported surgical complication under the authors’ tabulation, although all patients in the two graft groups reported paresthesia, and 4/90 had persistent curvature &gt;20° at three months. These figures do not establish longer-term outcomes or results after the standard eight-injection course.<sup>[[44]](#ref44)</sup>

## Algorithm Summary

1. **Active phase (evolving symptoms):** Treat pain with NSAIDs when appropriate; discuss traction and its limitations, assess erectile function, consider a PDE5 inhibitor for concomitant ED when appropriate, and offer psychological support as needed. Defer corrective surgery until stable. Do not present unproven oral antifibrotics as standard therapy.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[51]](#ref51)</sup>
2. **Stable phase (deformity unchanged for at least 3 months; pain usually resolved):**<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[51]](#ref51)</sup>
   - **Curvature &lt; 60°, no hourglass/hinge, intact rigidity** → Plication
   - **Severe curvature (proposed &gt;60° threshold), hourglass/hinge, or shortening with intact rigidity** → Consider plaque incision/excision and grafting (BMG, bovine pericardium, TachoSil, or tunica vaginalis); for selected hourglass/hinge indentation, extra-tunical grafting has also been described in retrospective series.<sup>[[51]](#ref51)</sup>
   - **Refractory ED** → IPP ± modeling, plication, or grafting
   - **Patient prefers non-surgical** → CCH (curvature 30–90°, palpable plaque, intact erection) ± RestoreX PTT
3. **Patients considering treatment:** shared decision-making; counsel about expected outcomes (shortening with plication, de novo ED with grafting, modest CCH curvature benefit about 7.7° net versus placebo at 52 weeks); assess psychological distress and consider referral when significant distress is identified.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[18]](#ref18)[[20]](#ref20)[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

## Guideline Principles

- History and genital examination are the minimum evaluation; document erect deformity before treatment.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- Shared decision-making and thorough counseling are universally emphasized
- Plication (shortening) and grafting (lengthening) are reserved for patients with adequate rigidity; EAU notes that the &gt; 60° threshold for &quot;severe&quot; curvature has not been validated<sup>[[51]](#ref51)</sup>
- **Penile prosthesis, with or without straightening procedures, is the recommended surgical option for PD with ED not responding to pharmacotherapy** (EAU strong)<sup>[[51]](#ref51)</sup>
- CCH is an established option for appropriately selected patients; current guidance differs from older recommendations for IFN-α2b
- Vitamin E and tamoxifen should not be offered
- Further pathophysiology and rigorous outcomes research are needed
