# Peyronie's Disease

**Peyronie's disease (PD)** is an acquired fibrotic disorder of the tunica albuginea that produces palpable plaque, penile curvature, painful erections, erectile dysfunction, shortening, and substantial psychological morbidity. Prevalence is **1–20%** of adult men with mean onset ~53 years; the natural history of untreated curvature is unfavorable — only **3–12%** improve spontaneously.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> The single FDA-approved non-surgical treatment is **intralesional collagenase clostridium histolyticum (CCH, Xiaflex)**; **surgery remains the gold standard for stable disease with functionally significant deformity**.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

The AUA defines PD as "an acquired penile abnormality characterized by fibrosis of the tunica albuginea, which may be accompanied by pain, deformity, erectile dysfunction, and/or distress." It belongs to a broader family of **superficial fibrosing disorders** with Dupuytren's contracture, Ledderhose disease, and tympanosclerosis.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

---

## Epidemiology

- **Prevalence** 1–20% (likely under-reported)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Mean age at onset** ~53 years (range 18–79)<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Diabetic men** — prevalence up to **20.3%**<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- White men over-represented (OR 8.47 in case-control)<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- Strong association with Dupuytren's contracture<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

---

## Pathophysiology

PD is a **penile wound-healing disorder** in genetically predisposed men.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)[[8]](#ref8)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

1. **Inciting event** — microvascular trauma to the tunica from penile buckling during intercourse (often unrecalled)
2. **Inflammatory phase** — extravascular protein deposition, fibrin trapping, macrophage recruitment, cytokine overexpression (TGF-β, IL-1, IL-6, PDGF, FGF), elastase release
3. **Fibroproliferative phase** — MMP/TIMP imbalance (decreased MMPs, increased TIMPs), excessive collagen deposition, shift from type I to predominantly type III collagen
4. **Plaque maturation** — progressive fibrosis, calcification in some, stable scar

**TGF-β** is the central profibrotic mediator; PAI-1, PTX-3, HIF, and IgG4 also contribute. Oxidative stress links PD to systemic metabolic disease.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

## Risk Factors

| Factor | Association |
|---|---|
| **Penile trauma / buckling** | Most common inciting event<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Dupuytren's contracture** | Shared fibrotic diathesis<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Diabetes mellitus** | Up to 20.3% in T2DM; early-onset and uncontrolled DM correlate with severity<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Hypertension, hyperlipidemia, smoking** | Common comorbidities; mostly tied to concurrent ED rather than PD severity<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| **NAFLD / insulin resistance** | Independent predictor of PD<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| **Prior urologic instrumentation** | TURP, cystoscopy<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Thiazide diuretics** | OR 2.29 in case-control<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **White race** | OR 8.47<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |

Notably, **comorbidity burden does not correlate with curvature severity** — these comorbidities track with concurrent ED rather than PD pathogenesis itself.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

## Disease Phases

| Phase | Duration | Features | Implications |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Acute (active)** | Typically 12–18 months from onset | Painful erections (~42%), evolving deformity, plaque maturation | Pain resolves spontaneously in 64–89%; non-surgical interventions favored<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| **Chronic (stable)** | After active phase | Fibrosis stabilizes, pain typically gone, fixed deformity | Surgery appropriate after **≥ 3 mo (AUA)** or **≥ 6 mo (EAU)** stability<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |

## Natural History

| Outcome | Rate |
|---|---|
| **Curvature worsened** | 30–48%<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Curvature stable** | 37–67%<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Curvature spontaneously improved** | 3–12%<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Pain resolution** | 64–89%<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| **Penile shortening** | 65–89%<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| **Erectile function worsened** | 54–60%<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Negative psychological impact** | ~50%<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[17]](#ref17)</sup> |

A median 8.4-year follow-up of conservatively managed patients showed that, although 43% reported curvature resolution, the majority lost length and experienced worsening sexual function and psychosocial distress.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

## Clinical Presentation

- **Penile curvature** — dorsal (45–72%), lateral (11–29%), ventral (~17%)<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Palpable plaque**
- **Painful erection** — acute phase
- **ED** in 32–54% at diagnosis; probability rises with age &gt; 60, duration &gt; 12 mo, and ≥ 1 vascular risk factor (86.7%)<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Penile shortening** — 65–89%<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Hourglass deformity / hinge effect** — buckling instability that defeats penetration
- **Substantial psychological distress** — 81% emotionally distressed; ~50% depression; 54% relationship difficulties<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[6]](#ref6)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

### Psychological burden

A Swedish 3.5-million-man cohort:<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

| Outcome | HR (vs general population) |
|---|---|
| Depression | 1.7 |
| Anxiety disorder | 1.9 |
| Self-injurious behavior | 2.0 |

5-year cumulative depression risk **7.0% vs 4.4%**; 10-year **12.5% vs 8.7%**.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[20]](#ref20)</sup> An online-forum thematic analysis reported depressed mood in 33% and feelings of isolation in 18%, with substantial partner impact (sexual dissatisfaction 21%, dyspareunia 7.7%, relationship disruption 8.7%).<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> The AUA recommends explicitly discussing the psychological impact and offering referral for psychological support.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

## Diagnostic Evaluation

All four major guidelines (AUA, EAU, CUA, ISSM) converge on the workup.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

| Step | Role |
|---|---|
| **History** (sufficient for diagnosis) | Onset, duration, deformity, pain, shortening, erectile function, prior treatment, sexual / psychological impact |
| **Photographs of erect penis** (patient-provided) | Document deformity for objective comparison<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> |
| **Physical exam** | Stretching and palpation of flaccid penis — plaque size, location, calcification |
| **Intracavernosal injection (ICI) test** | **Gold standard** before any invasive intervention — objective curvature, hinge effect, erectile response<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup> |
| **Penile duplex Doppler ultrasound** (optional) | Calcified vs non-calcified plaque; vascular integrity (PSV, EDV); identifies vasculogenic ED that changes the operation<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[22]](#ref22)[[23]](#ref23)[[24]](#ref24)</sup> |
| **PDQ and IIEF questionnaires** | Symptom bother and erectile function domains |

## Management

Treatment is dictated by **disease phase, deformity, erectile function, and patient goals**. A 2026 Guideline of Guidelines comparison found broad consensus across AUA, EAU, CUA, and ISSM.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

### Important caveat on non-surgical evidence

The 2023 Cochrane systematic review concluded there is a **paucity of high-quality evidence** for non-surgical therapies — most trials are methodologically limited.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

---

### A. Oral therapies

The AUA position:<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

- **Should not** offer vitamin E or tamoxifen
- **May** offer pentoxifylline or potassium para-aminobenzoate (Potaba) only with appropriate counseling about limited evidence

| Agent | Mechanism | Evidence | AUA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vitamin E | Antioxidant | No benefit over placebo<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> | Should not offer |
| Tamoxifen | Anti-fibrotic | No benefit over placebo<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> | Should not offer |
| Pentoxifylline | PDE inhibitor / anti-inflammatory | Limited; may reduce calcification<sup>[[25]](#ref25)[[26]](#ref26)</sup> | May offer with counseling |
| Potaba | Anti-fibrotic | Likely little to no change in curvature<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> | May offer with counseling |
| Colchicine | Anti-inflammatory | Excluded from Cochrane (data integrity)<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> | Not recommended |

---

### B. Intralesional injection therapies

#### Collagenase clostridium histolyticum (CCH, Xiaflex) — only FDA-approved option<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[26]](#ref26)[[27]](#ref27)[[28]](#ref28)</sup>

- **Indication:** Adult men with palpable plaque and curvature ≥ 30° at the start of therapy
- **Protocol (IMPRESS):** Up to **4 cycles** (8 injections total); each cycle = 2 injections of 0.58 mg separated by 24–72 h, with **clinician modeling after each cycle and patient home modeling between cycles**
- **IMPRESS I & II (n = 832):** Mean curvature improvement **17° (34%)** vs 9.3° (18.2%) placebo — **net benefit ~7.7°**; PDQ bother score significantly improved<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>
- **Real-world multi-institutional (n = 918):** Mean curvature 48.2° → 32.9° (**30.1% improvement**); 68.7% achieved ≥ 20% improvement; completing ≥ 4 cycles predicted better outcomes<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>
- **Meta-analysis (n = 1,480):** 35% pooled curvature improvement; 41% bother improvement<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>
- **AEs** mostly mild (hematoma, pain, swelling); **serious AEs (corporal rupture) &lt; 1%**<sup>[[27]](#ref27)[[28]](#ref28)[[29]](#ref29)</sup>
- **No significant penile shortening**<sup>[[29]](#ref29)[[30]](#ref30)</sup>
- **Available in the US; withdrawn from Europe**<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

#### Other intralesional agents

- **Interferon-α2b** — curvature improvement 12–13.5°; also improves plaque size and pain; recognized by all guidelines as an option<sup>[[25]](#ref25)[[31]](#ref31)</sup>
- **Verapamil** — variable; may help plaque and pain but inconsistent curvature data; electromotive verapamil minimally better than placebo<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[25]](#ref25)[[31]](#ref31)</sup>

---

### C. Mechanical therapies

#### Penile traction therapy (PTT)

Mechano-transduction increases MMP activity and remodels collagen.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

- **RestoreX** — the most studied modern device; only **30–90 min/day** required (vs 3–8 h for older devices)<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup>
  - **RCT (n = 110):** 3-month curvature **−11.7° vs +1.3°** in controls (p &lt; 0.001)<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup>
  - **6-month open-label:** 95% length gains (mean 2.0–2.2 cm); 61% curve improvements (16.8–21.4°)<sup>[[33]](#ref33)</sup>
  - **Combined with CCH:** RestoreX + CCH **49% curvature improvement vs 31% with CCH alone**; 6.9× more likely to achieve ≥ 20° improvement<sup>[[34]](#ref34)</sup>
- **Penimaster PRO** — also well-studied with comparable efficacy<sup>[[35]](#ref35)</sup>

#### Other modalities

- **Extracorporeal shockwave therapy (ESWT)** — reproducibly improves **pain** in stable disease but **does not improve curvature**; AUA recommends against for curvature reduction<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[25]](#ref25)</sup>
- **Vacuum erection device** — limited evidence; modest benefits in small series<sup>[[31]](#ref31)[[35]](#ref35)</sup>

---

### D. Surgical treatment — gold standard for stable PD with functional deformity

All guidelines: surgery for **stable disease (≥ 3–6 months)** by experienced surgeons.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

**Levine surgical algorithm** (widely adopted):<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[36]](#ref36)[[37]](#ref37)</sup>

- **Adequate rigidity, mild–moderate curvature (&lt; 60°), no hourglass/hinge** → **Tunical plication**
- **Adequate rigidity, severe curvature (&gt; 60–70°), hourglass/hinge, significant shortening** → **Plaque incision/partial excision and grafting**
- **Refractory ED ± curvature** → **Inflatable penile prosthesis** ± modeling, plication, or grafting

#### 1. Tunical plication (Nesbit / 16-dot / modified Yachia)

- **The most common PD operation** (~50% of all PD surgery)<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Curvature correction ≥ 90%**<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Penile shortening — mean −1.0 cm** is the trade-off<sup>[[38]](#ref38)</sup>
- Best candidate: intact erectile function, curvature &lt; 60°<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[37]](#ref37)</sup>
- Lower risk of de novo ED than grafting; no graft required
- **Wound complications** ~6.3% — no difference between ventral and subcoronal incisions<sup>[[39]](#ref39)</sup>

#### 2. Plaque incision (or partial excision) and grafting

- **Curvature correction 80–98.6%** depending on graft and technique<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[38]](#ref38)[[40]](#ref40)</sup>
- **May gain length** (mean +0.9 cm) — the key advantage over plication<sup>[[38]](#ref38)</sup>
- Best candidate: intact erectile function, severe curvature (&gt; 60–70°), hourglass / hinge / significant shortening<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[37]](#ref37)</sup>
- **De novo ED risk 1.7–25%** depending on series and graft<sup>[[40]](#ref40)[[41]](#ref41)</sup>
- **Compound curvature** — PEG plus supplemental TAP achieves 79% penetrative intercourse with 12.4% recurrence at 61 months<sup>[[42]](#ref42)</sup>

**Graft materials:**

| Material | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Buccal mucosa graft (BMG)** | Autologous | Highest straightening (98.6%); lowest de novo ED (1.7%); lowest shortening (1.1%)<sup>[[40]](#ref40)[[43]](#ref43)</sup> |
| **Tunica vaginalis** | Autologous | 90% technical success at 24 mo; comparable to BMG<sup>[[43]](#ref43)</sup> |
| **Bovine pericardium** | Allograft | Off-the-shelf; widely used; good results<sup>[[41]](#ref41)[[45]](#ref45)</sup> |
| **TachoSil (collagen fleece)** | Hemostatic patch | Best overall performance when preoperative curvature considered; shorter operative time<sup>[[38]](#ref38)[[45]](#ref45)</sup> |
| **Dermis** | Autologous | Historical standard<sup>[[41]](#ref41)</sup> |
| **Small intestinal submucosa (SIS)** | Xenograft | Variable<sup>[[38]](#ref38)</sup> |

The 2026 BMG meta-analysis: **98.6% straightening, 92.1% satisfaction, 1.7% de novo ED, 1.1% shortening**, with comparable head-to-head outcomes between BMG and other graft methods.<sup>[[40]](#ref40)</sup>

#### 3. Inflatable penile prosthesis (IPP)

- **Indication:** PD with refractory ED (failed PDE5i, VED, ICI) ± deformity<sup>[[6]](#ref6)[[46]](#ref46)</sup>
- **Addresses both ED and curvature** in a single operation<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Multicenter cohort (499 PD-IPP patients):**<sup>[[47]](#ref47)</sup>
  - Mean preop curvature 39.4°
  - **17.6% straightened with IPP alone**
  - **82.4% needed adjuncts** — manual modeling ± scratch (74.7%, median 26° correction); plication (4.8%, 40°); grafting (2%, 55° for the most severe)
- Satisfaction &gt; 80% at 1–2 yr; device use &gt; 88%<sup>[[48]](#ref48)[[49]](#ref49)</sup>
- **Self-reported depression decreased** from 19.3% preop to 10.5% at 1 yr (p = 0.02)<sup>[[49]](#ref49)</sup>
- Emerging data support IPP as first-line surgical option in selected PD patients **even without ED**, with 87.9% satisfaction<sup>[[48]](#ref48)</sup>
- **Scratch technique + postoperative VED** — combined approach achieving residual curvature of 7.7–8.7° at 1 yr with excellent IIEF improvement<sup>[[50]](#ref50)</sup>

---

### E. Comparative outcomes

Prospective multi-institutional comparison of CCH vs plication vs PEG:<sup>[[38]](#ref38)</sup>

| Outcome | CCH | Tunical plication | PEG |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mean curvature correction | 23.3° (34.4%) | 72.0° (92.2%) | 71.8° (94.9%) |
| Penile length change | −0.2 cm | −1.0 cm | +0.9 cm |
| IIEF-5 change | +5.7 | +4.9 | +2.2 |

In patients who fail CCH, surgical correction (grafting ± IPP or Nesbit) is technically feasible and safe — **95.6% no complications**; recurrence only 4.4%.<sup>[[44]](#ref44)</sup>

## Algorithm Summary

1. **Acute phase (evolving symptoms, &lt; 12 mo):** PTT (RestoreX, 30–90 min/day), intralesional CCH or IFN-α2b, pentoxifylline (limited evidence). Avoid surgery until stable.
2. **Stable phase (≥ 3–6 mo):**
   - **Curvature &lt; 60°, no hourglass/hinge, intact rigidity** → **Plication**
   - **Curvature &gt; 60°, hourglass/hinge, or shortening, intact rigidity** → **Plaque incision/excision + graft** (BMG, bovine pericardium, TachoSil, or tunica vaginalis)
   - **Refractory ED** → **IPP** ± modeling, plication, or grafting
   - **Patient prefers non-surgical** → **CCH** (curvature 30–90°, palpable plaque, intact erection) ± **RestoreX PTT**
3. **All patients:** shared decision-making; counsel about expected outcomes (shortening with plication, de novo ED with grafting, modest CCH benefit ~7.7° net); **screen for and address psychological distress**.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[18]](#ref18)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

---

## Guideline Consensus (AUA, EAU, CUA, ISSM)

- History adequate for diagnosis; ICI is the gold standard before invasive intervention<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- Shared decision-making and thorough counseling are universally emphasized
- Plication and grafting reserved for patients with preserved erectile function
- **Penile prosthesis is the preferred surgical option for PD with refractory ED**
- Intralesional CCH and IFN-α2b are recognized as potential options
- Vitamin E and tamoxifen should not be offered
- Further pathophysiology and rigorous outcomes research are needed
