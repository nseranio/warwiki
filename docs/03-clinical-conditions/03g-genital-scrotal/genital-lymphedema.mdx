# Genital Lymphedema

**Genital lymphedema (GL)** is the accumulation of protein-rich lymphatic fluid in the interstitial tissues of the external genitalia — penis, scrotum, vulva, and/or perineum — from impaired lymphatic drainage. It is chronic, progressive, and produces discomfort, cosmetic disfigurement, functional disturbances (urinary, sexual, ambulatory), and profound psychosocial impact.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> No standardized treatment algorithm exists; diagnosis and management remain frequently delayed.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For surgical reconstruction technique see [Scrotal Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/scrotal-reconstruction) and [Scrotal Flap Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/scrotal-flaps). For the inflammatory dermatologic condition that may coexist or contribute, see [Hidradenitis Suppurativa (GU)](/docs/clinical-conditions/03g-genital-scrotal/hidradenitis-suppurativa).

---

## Epidemiology

- **Lymphatic filariasis** (most common cause globally) — *Wuchereria bancrofti* in approximately 90% of infections; older estimates cited ~120 million LF cases; ~15–16 million live with chronic filarial lymphedema; scrotal elephantiasis and hydrocele are hallmarks. Community prevalence 1.3–7.8% in endemic regions.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Prostate cancer treatment** — GL in 0–1% after RP + PLND alone; **2–22%** after pelvic radiation following PLND.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Gynecologic cancer treatment** — GL frequently accompanies lower-limb lymphedema after hysterectomy + PLND + pelvic RT.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Inguinal / ilioinguinal lymphadenectomy** for melanoma, anal, penile cancer.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Pediatric GL** — 0.6% (25/3889) at a vascular-anomalies center; **92% primary / idiopathic**; mean onset 4.5 yr; 60.9% in infancy; penoscrotal in 72%; concurrent lower-extremity lymphedema in 76%.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Obesity-induced lymphedema (OIL)** — risk rises with severe obesity. Published BMI thresholds and MLL associations derive largely from selected limb-lymphedema cohorts and do not define genital disease risk or a universal cutoff.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

---

## Etiology and Classification

**Primary GL** — developmental lymphatic anomalies; congenital / praecox / tarda; syndromic forms (FOXC2 lymphedema-distichiasis, GATA2 Emberger, Meige, GJC2). 84% primary in the largest male GL series.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

**Secondary GL** — filariasis; cancer treatment; obesity (OIL / MLL); recurrent cellulitis / STIs / TB; trauma; non-oncologic surgery; **Crohn's / ano-genital granulomatosis** (40% have intestinal Crohn's; 80% with combined penile + scrotal edema); HS; idiopathic acquired.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)[[11]](#ref11)[[14]](#ref14)[[19]](#ref19)[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

McDougal 2003 — practical division into **self-limited** (conservative) vs **chronic with pathological skin / tissue changes** (surgical).<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

---

## Pathophysiology

Lymphatic obstruction → protein-rich interstitial fluid → chronic lymph stasis → **inflammation + fibrosis + adipose expansion + dysfunctional lymphangiogenesis + local immunosuppression** ("locus minoris resistentiae" predisposing to infection and neoplasia).<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[22]](#ref22)</sup> Recurrent cellulitis further damages lymphatics in a vicious cycle.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> In genital MLL specifically — dartos hyperplasia / hypertrophy with microvascular proliferation at the smooth-muscle–stroma interface.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

## Staging and Severity

| System | Detail |
|---|---|
| **ISL Stage 0–3** | Universal staging; endorsed by NCCN survivorship guidelines.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)[[26]](#ref26)</sup> |
| **Genital Lymphedema Score (GLS, Yamamoto)** | 0–9 symptom-based score (urinary trouble, edema, lymphorrhea); correlates with Genital Dermal Backflow (GDB) stage on ICG lymphography.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)[[28]](#ref28)</sup> |
| **Ehrl 2023 treatment-oriented classification** | Giant penoscrotal lymphedema; guides whether VLNT is added.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup> |
| **MR-lymphography classification (Lu 2016)** | Inguinal-node dysfunction mild / moderate / severe → CDP vs microsurgery vs excision.<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup> |

---

## Clinical Features and Complications

Progressive genital swelling, heaviness, ambulation / hygiene / voiding difficulty (buried penis), sexual dysfunction, **lymphorrhea**, cosmetic disfigurement.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[17]](#ref17)[[31]](#ref31)</sup>

| Complication | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Cellulitis** | Lymphedema OR **6.8** for cellulitis; Stage 3 has ~2× the rate of Stage 2 (61.7% vs 31.8%).<sup>[[23]](#ref23)[[32]](#ref32)</sup> |
| Papillomatosis / warty overgrowth | Verrucous chronic skin changes. |
| Lymph vesicles / lymphocutaneous reflux | Superficial lymphatic cysts that rupture; retrograde lymph flow.<sup>[[33]](#ref33)</sup> |
| Recurrent fungal infection | From impaired local immunity. |
| Malignant transformation | **Stewart-Treves lymphangiosarcoma** — rare but devastating; immunosuppression also predisposes to non-melanoma skin cancer.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)[[34]](#ref34)</sup> |

QoL improvement after surgery — mean **1.3 → 7.7** on a 1–10 scale in the MLL series.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup>

## Diagnosis

Clinical exam (edema, peau d'orange, fibrosis, Stemmer sign).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[35]](#ref35)</sup>

| Modality | Role |
|---|---|
| **Lymphoscintigraphy** | Traditional gold standard; ⁹⁹ᵐTc-colloid; delayed / asymmetric drainage, dermal backflow, collateral channels.<sup>[[36]](#ref36)[[37]](#ref37)</sup> |
| **ICG lymphography** | Sens **0.97**, spec 0.55–0.93; less invasive; characterizes linear → splash → stardust → diffuse progression; differentiates type 1 (leg-to-genital) vs type 2 (non-leg source) genital flow.<sup>[[37]](#ref37)[[38]](#ref38)[[39]](#ref39)[[40]](#ref40)</sup> |
| **MR lymphangiography** | Functional + morphological; grades inguinal-node dysfunction for treatment selection.<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup> |
| **Ultrasound** | Venous duplex evaluates suspected DVT; high-frequency imaging may map superficial lymphatics / perforators for surgery.<sup>[[39]](#ref39)</sup> |
| **Biopsy** | For diagnostic uncertainty / malignancy concern; ano-genital granulomatosis shows non-caseating (50%) and intralymphatic granulomas (14%).<sup>[[19]](#ref19)[[24]](#ref24)</sup> |

## Conservative Management

### Complex Decongestive Therapy (CDT)

The cornerstone — **poorly adapted to genital anatomy** vs extremities.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

| Component | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Manual lymphatic drainage (MLD)** | Genital-anatomy-adapted; partner / self-drainage education.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[41]](#ref41)</sup> |
| **Compression** | Scrotal supports, hip spica bandage, panty girdle, individually fitted compression garments; pressure and fit require specialist assessment.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Exercise** | Muscle / joint pumps; progressive resistance training does not exacerbate.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> |
| **Skin care** | Meticulous hygiene; treat fungi; moisturize.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |

Integrated approach (Torio-Padron n = 51) — perioperative CDP yields a 6% revision rate.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup> Yaman 2024 — six CDT sessions over 14 days achieved significant scrotal reduction in HS-related GL concurrent with ixekizumab.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

### Pneumatic compression devices

A possible adjunct to CDT. Evidence and expert consensus largely concern extremity disease; limb-only pumps can displace fluid into the genitalia, so monitor proximal swelling and select coverage carefully.<sup>[[57]](#ref57)</sup><sup>[[23]](#ref23)[[43]](#ref43)</sup>

### Pharmacotherapy

- **Ketoprofen** — exploratory extremity-lymphedema trials improved skin thickness / histology but not limb volume or bioimpedance. This does not establish benefit for genital lymphedema and is not routine therapy.<sup>[[56]](#ref56)</sup>
- **Low-dose systemic corticosteroids** — Fujimoto 2014: prevented acute progression in acquired idiopathic penile lymphedema with histologic lymphocytic inflammation.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Immunomodulators (Crohn's / ano-genital granulomatosis)** — oral steroids 72% initial response (recurrence common), azathioprine 60%, anti-TNF 3/6 responded.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- **Antifilarials** — treat confirmed active infection with specialist guidance; chronic lymphedema alone is not an indication for DEC. Exposure to onchocerciasis or high-level Loa loa infection changes drug safety.<sup>[[42]](#ref42)[[58]](#ref58)</sup>
- **Recurrent cellulitis** — address edema, skin breaks and other predisposing factors. IDSA considers prophylactic penicillin or erythromycin when 3–4 episodes/year persist despite risk-factor treatment; individualize agent and duration. This is not prophylaxis for every genital-lymphedema patient.<sup>[[59]](#ref59)</sup>

### Weight management — fundamental for obesity-induced GL

Weight management is important, but established lymphatic injury / fibrosis may persist despite weight loss; animal reversibility should not be presented as a guaranteed human result. Continue a structured weight-management plan after excision.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)[[31]](#ref31)</sup>

## Surgical Management

Reported outcomes below are heterogeneous, mainly small retrospective series; comparisons between rows do not establish comparative efficacy or complication risk.

Two complementary categories — **excisional / debulking** and **physiologic / reconstructive** — frequently combined in modern algorithms.<sup>[[44]](#ref44)[[45]](#ref45)</sup>

### Excisional / debulking

| Procedure | Outcomes |
|---|---|
| **Modified Charles procedure** (excision + STSG) | Modolin 2006 n = 17 — symptom regression, 1 recurrence (RT + PLND patient); Salako 2018 n = 11 — hematoma 27.3%, SSI 18.2%, recurrence 9.1%; Singh 2011 n = 48 filarial — all satisfactory cosmesis / function; groin infection 25%.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[46]](#ref46)[[47]](#ref47)</sup> |
| **Excision + primary closure** | Torio-Padron n = 51 — **6% complication rate**; no flaps or grafts needed; Wisenbaugh n = 11 MLL — mean 21 kg resected, QoL 1.3 → 7.7 but most regained weight.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)[[31]](#ref31)</sup> |
| **Excision + flap reconstruction** | Guiotto SR: **54.2% reported complications** in the review’s flap subgroup; severity and selection differences prevent a causal comparison.<sup>[[44]](#ref44)</sup> |
| **MLL excision in obesity** | Machol n = 4 — lateral scrotal flaps ± mid-raphe Z-plasty; **50% recurrence** without weight loss.<sup>[[48]](#ref48)</sup> |
| **Liposuction / SAPL** | Addresses the solid (adipose / fibrotic) component of late ISL stage II–III; most evidence concerns limb disease and ongoing compression; those results cannot be used as a genital-specific volume-reduction prediction.<sup>[[57]](#ref57)</sup> |

### Physiologic / reconstructive

| Procedure | Outcomes |
|---|---|
| **Lymphaticovenous anastomosis (LVA)** | Guiotto SR: **9% reported complications** in the review’s LVA subgroup; not a randomized comparison. Hara & Mihara — combined leg-LVA + genital-LVA per ICG flow type to prevent recurrence; Mukenge spermatic-cord LVA. Genital comparative evidence remains limited.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)[[34]](#ref34)[[36]](#ref36)[[39]](#ref39)[[40]](#ref40)[[44]](#ref44)</sup> |
| **Vascularized lymph node transfer (VLNT)** | Ehrl 2023 n = 9 giant penoscrotal — scrotal VLNT improved lymphatic transport; **0% recurrence at 49 mo**; Results from broader limb-lymphedema series do not establish superiority for genital disease.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)[[43]](#ref43)[[49]](#ref49)[[50]](#ref50)</sup> |
| **SCIP Lymphatic Flap Transfer — "3R" (Yamamoto 2022)** | Radical reduction + reconstruction with pedicled SCIP lymphatic flap + SCIP pure-skin-perforator flap. n = 7 (4 scrotal, 3 penoscrotal); mean resected 1,511 g; **0% complications, 0% recurrence** at mean 22.7 mo; GLS 6.7 → 0.3.<sup>[[51]](#ref51)</sup> |
| **Complete Functional Lymphatic System Pedicled Transfer (Abdelfattah 2023)** | n = 26 advanced scrotal / penoscrotal lymphedema; **100% flap survival**; cellulitis dramatically reduced (p &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[52]](#ref52)</sup> |
| **CHASCIP — Combined Charles + Lymphatic SCIP flap (Ciudad 2025)** | n = 8 ISL Stage III; bilateral pedicled lymphatic SCIP flaps + STSG; mean resected 1,772.7 g; 25% complications; **0% recurrence at 34 mo**; sexual dysfunction 87.5% → 0%.<sup>[[53]](#ref53)</sup> |
| **Lymphatic System Transfer (LYST)** | SCIP flap with both lymph nodes and afferent lymphatic vessels intact; Yoshimatsu 2025 n = 8 — excess volume −11.2% at 39 mo, cellulitis significantly decreased (p = 0.025); Xu 2026 — pedicled SCIP LYST for lymphedema + chronic venous disease, no microsurgical anastomosis needed.<sup>[[54]](#ref54)[[55]](#ref55)</sup> |

## Individualized Treatment Framework

The following are clinical patterns, not validated genital-specific stage-to-procedure rules. GLS symptom scores and ISL tissue stages are not interchangeable treatment thresholds.

| Severity | Approach |
|---|---|
| **Early or predominantly fluid swelling** | CDT, weight management, treat underlying cause (antifilarials, immunomodulators); no routine disease-modifying drug therapy.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Persistent swelling with tissue change** | Specialist CDT; assess pneumatic compression for proximal fluid displacement. Consider prophylactic antibiotics only for recurrent cellulitis despite risk-factor treatment. Imaging, tissue quality and expertise inform selected LVA, VLNT or excisional surgery.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)[[26]](#ref26)[[43]](#ref43)</sup> |
| **Advanced fibrosis / elephantiasis** | Excision with primary closure, graft or flap reconstruction according to tissue quality; selected centers may add **SCIP lymphatic flap transfer or VLNT**. Small uncontrolled series do not establish a universally curative technique or freedom from compression. Perioperative CDP.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)[[41]](#ref41)[[48]](#ref48)[[51]](#ref51)[[53]](#ref53)</sup> |
| **Obesity-induced GL / MLL** | Weight loss is fundamental; comprehensive weight-loss plan must accompany excision (Wisenbaugh).<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[25]](#ref25)[[29]](#ref29)[[48]](#ref48)</sup> |
| **Crohn's / ano-genital granulomatosis** | Coordinate disease-specific treatment with gastroenterology / dermatology; no universal steroid–azathioprine–anti-TNF ladder is established for genital disease. Selected refractory lesions may need reconstruction.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |
