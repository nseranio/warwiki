# Genital Lymphedema

**Genital lymphedema (GL)** is the accumulation of protein-rich lymphatic fluid in the interstitial tissues of the external genitalia — penis, scrotum, vulva, and/or perineum — from impaired lymphatic drainage. It is chronic, progressive, and produces discomfort, cosmetic disfigurement, functional disturbances (urinary, sexual, ambulatory), and profound psychosocial impact.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> No standardized treatment algorithm exists; diagnosis and management remain frequently delayed.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

For surgical reconstruction technique see [Scrotal Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/scrotal-reconstruction) and [Scrotal Flap Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/scrotal-flaps). For the inflammatory dermatologic condition that may coexist or contribute, see [Hidradenitis Suppurativa (GU)](/docs/clinical-conditions/03g-genital-scrotal/hidradenitis-suppurativa).

---

## Epidemiology

- **Lymphatic filariasis** (most common cause globally) — *Wuchereria bancrofti* in 90% of ~120 million LF cases; ~15–16 million live with chronic filarial lymphedema; scrotal elephantiasis and hydrocele are hallmarks. Community prevalence 1.3–7.8% in endemic regions.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Prostate cancer treatment** — GL in 0–1% after RP + PLND alone; **2–22%** after pelvic radiation following PLND.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
- **Gynecologic cancer treatment** — GL frequently accompanies lower-limb lymphedema after hysterectomy + PLND + pelvic RT.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
- **Inguinal / ilioinguinal lymphadenectomy** for melanoma, anal, penile cancer.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
- **Pediatric GL** — 0.6% (25/3889) at a vascular-anomalies center; **92% primary / idiopathic**; mean onset 4.5 yr; 60.9% in infancy; penoscrotal in 72%; concurrent lower-extremity lymphedema in 76%.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Obesity-induced lymphedema (OIL)** — BMI &gt; 56 confers **213-fold** increased MLL risk; nearly universal above BMI 60; OIL may begin once BMI exceeds 40.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

---

## Etiology and Classification

**Primary GL** — developmental lymphatic anomalies; congenital / praecox / tarda; syndromic forms (FOXC2 lymphedema-distichiasis, GATA2 Emberger, Meige, GJC2). 84% primary in the largest male GL series.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)[[18]](#ref18)</sup>

**Secondary GL** — filariasis; cancer treatment; obesity (OIL / MLL); recurrent cellulitis / STIs / TB; trauma; non-oncologic surgery; **Crohn's / ano-genital granulomatosis** (40% have intestinal Crohn's; 80% with combined penile + scrotal edema); HS; idiopathic acquired.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[9]](#ref9)[[12]](#ref12)[[15]](#ref15)[[20]](#ref20)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

McDougal 2003 — practical division into **self-limited** (conservative) vs **chronic with pathological skin / tissue changes** (surgical).<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>

---

## Pathophysiology

Lymphatic obstruction → protein-rich interstitial fluid → chronic lymph stasis → **inflammation + fibrosis + adipose expansion + dysfunctional lymphangiogenesis + local immunosuppression** ("locus minoris resistentiae" predisposing to infection and neoplasia).<sup>[[22]](#ref22)[[23]](#ref23)</sup> Recurrent cellulitis further damages lymphatics in a vicious cycle.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> In genital MLL specifically — dartos hyperplasia / hypertrophy with microvascular proliferation at the smooth-muscle–stroma interface.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>

## Staging and Severity

| System | Detail |
|---|---|
| **ISL Stage 0–3** | Universal staging; endorsed by NCCN survivorship guidelines.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)[[27]](#ref27)</sup> |
| **Genital Lymphedema Score (GLS, Yamamoto)** | 0–9 symptom-based score (urinary trouble, edema, lymphorrhea); correlates with Genital Dermal Backflow (GDB) stage on ICG lymphography.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)[[29]](#ref29)</sup> |
| **Ehrl 2023 treatment-oriented classification** | Giant penoscrotal lymphedema; guides whether VLNT is added.<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup> |
| **MR-lymphography classification (Lu 2016)** | Inguinal-node dysfunction mild / moderate / severe → CDP vs microsurgery vs excision.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup> |

---

## Clinical Features and Complications

Progressive genital swelling, heaviness, ambulation / hygiene / voiding difficulty (buried penis), sexual dysfunction, **lymphorrhea**, cosmetic disfigurement.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[18]](#ref18)[[32]](#ref32)</sup>

| Complication | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Cellulitis** | Lymphedema OR **6.8** for cellulitis; Stage 3 has ~2× the rate of Stage 2 (61.7% vs 31.8%).<sup>[[24]](#ref24)[[33]](#ref33)</sup> |
| Papillomatosis / warty overgrowth | Verrucous chronic skin changes. |
| Lymph vesicles / lymphocutaneous reflux | Superficial lymphatic cysts that rupture; retrograde lymph flow.<sup>[[34]](#ref34)</sup> |
| Recurrent fungal infection | From impaired local immunity. |
| Malignant transformation | **Stewart-Treves lymphangiosarcoma** — rare but devastating; immunosuppression also predisposes to non-melanoma skin cancer.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)[[35]](#ref35)</sup> |

QoL improvement after surgery — mean **1.3 → 7.7** on a 1–10 scale in the MLL series.<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup>

## Diagnosis

Clinical exam (edema, peau d'orange, fibrosis, Stemmer sign).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[36]](#ref36)</sup>

| Modality | Role |
|---|---|
| **Lymphoscintigraphy** | Traditional gold standard; ⁹⁹ᵐTc-colloid; delayed / asymmetric drainage, dermal backflow, collateral channels.<sup>[[37]](#ref37)[[38]](#ref38)</sup> |
| **ICG lymphography** | Sens **0.97**, spec 0.55–0.93; less invasive; characterizes linear → splash → stardust → diffuse progression; differentiates type 1 (leg-to-genital) vs type 2 (non-leg source) genital flow.<sup>[[38]](#ref38)[[39]](#ref39)[[40]](#ref40)[[41]](#ref41)</sup> |
| **MR lymphangiography** | Functional + morphological; grades inguinal-node dysfunction for treatment selection.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup> |
| **UHF ultrasound** | Excludes DVT; identifies lymphatics / perforators for surgical planning.<sup>[[40]](#ref40)</sup> |
| **Biopsy** | For diagnostic uncertainty / malignancy concern; ano-genital granulomatosis shows non-caseating (50%) and intralymphatic granulomas (14%).<sup>[[20]](#ref20)[[25]](#ref25)</sup> |

## Conservative Management

### Complex Decongestive Therapy (CDT)

The cornerstone — **poorly adapted to genital anatomy** vs extremities.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

| Component | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Manual lymphatic drainage (MLD)** | Genital-anatomy-adapted; partner / self-drainage education.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[43]](#ref43)</sup> |
| **Compression** | Scrotal supports, hip spica bandage, panty girdle, custom compression underwear (40–80 mmHg).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Exercise** | Muscle / joint pumps; progressive resistance training does not exacerbate.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup> |
| **Skin care** | Meticulous hygiene; treat fungi; moisturize.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |

Integrated approach (Torio-Padron n = 51) — perioperative CDP yields a 6% revision rate.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> Yaman 2024 — six CDT sessions over 14 days achieved significant scrotal reduction in HS-related GL concurrent with ixekizumab.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

### Pneumatic compression devices

Recommended by 92% of AVF expert panelists; reduces cellulitis, outpatient services, hospitalization.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)[[45]](#ref45)</sup>

### Pharmacotherapy

- **Ketoprofen 75 mg TID × 4 mo** — Rockson pilot + placebo-controlled (n = 21 + 34): reduced skin thickness, improved histopathology, decreased plasma G-CSF; mechanism via LTB₄ inhibition (Tian 2017). **Not FDA-approved**.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>
- **Low-dose systemic corticosteroids** — Fujimoto 2014: prevented acute progression in acquired idiopathic penile lymphedema with histologic lymphocytic inflammation.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Immunomodulators (Crohn's / ano-genital granulomatosis)** — oral steroids 72% initial response (recurrence common), azathioprine 60%, anti-TNF 3/6 responded.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>
- **Selenium (sodium selenite)** — Micke 2003: VAS reduction 4.3 in radiation lymphedema; Cochrane evidence base weak.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>
- **Antifilarials** — DEC, ivermectin, albendazole; mass drug administration programs.<sup>[[44]](#ref44)</sup>
- **Prophylactic antibiotics for recurrent cellulitis** — IDSA 2014 recommends after ≥ 3–4 episodes/yr: oral penicillin 250 mg–1 g BID, erythromycin 250 mg BID, or IM benzathine penicillin 1.2 MU q2–4 wk for 4–52 wk. PATCH trial (Thomas NEJM 2013, n = 274): penicillin 250 mg BID × 12 mo significantly reduced recurrence; benefit waned after discontinuation; failure predictors — BMI ≥ 33, ≥ 3 prior episodes, preexisting edema.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[19]](#ref19)[[33]](#ref33)</sup>

### Weight management — fundamental for obesity-induced GL

OIL is **reversible with weight loss** (Nitti murine model); BMI &gt; 60 → &lt; 60 normalized leg volume in 50% (Greene). Wisenbaugh — most patients **gained 5.2 kg** post-excision without a weight-loss program. The **BLOOM technique** (Sim 2025) combines sleeve gastrectomy with VLNT using repurposed gastroepiploic lymph nodes from the gastrectomy specimen.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[14]](#ref14)[[23]](#ref23)[[24]](#ref24)[[26]](#ref26)[[32]](#ref32)</sup>

## Surgical Management

Two complementary categories — **excisional / debulking** and **physiologic / reconstructive** — frequently combined in modern algorithms.<sup>[[46]](#ref46)[[47]](#ref47)</sup>

### Excisional / debulking

| Procedure | Outcomes |
|---|---|
| **Modified Charles procedure** (excision + STSG) | Modolin 2006 n = 17 — symptom regression, 1 recurrence (RT + PLND patient); Salako 2018 n = 11 — hematoma 27.3%, SSI 18.2%, recurrence 9.1%; Singh 2011 n = 48 filarial — all satisfactory cosmesis / function; groin infection 25%.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)[[48]](#ref48)[[49]](#ref49)</sup> |
| **Excision + primary closure** | Torio-Padron n = 51 — **6% complication rate**; no flaps or grafts needed; Wisenbaugh n = 11 MLL — mean 21 kg resected, QoL 1.3 → 7.7 but most regained weight.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[32]](#ref32)</sup> |
| **Excision + flap reconstruction** | Guiotto SR: **54.2% complication rate** (highest among approaches), reflects disease severity.<sup>[[46]](#ref46)</sup> |
| **MLL excision in obesity** | Machol n = 4 — lateral scrotal flaps ± mid-raphe Z-plasty; **50% recurrence** without weight loss.<sup>[[50]](#ref50)</sup> |
| **Liposuction / SAPL** | Addresses the solid (adipose / fibrotic) component of late ISL stage II–III; SR n = 2,334 — standalone reduced volume 99.7% with continuous compression; combined liposuction + LVA / VLNT reduced compression dependence. AAPS Grade 1C.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[31]](#ref31)[[32]](#ref32)[[33]](#ref33)</sup> |

### Physiologic / reconstructive

| Procedure | Outcomes |
|---|---|
| **Lymphaticovenous anastomosis (LVA)** | Guiotto SR: **9% complication rate** — lowest. Hara & Mihara — combined leg-LVA + genital-LVA per ICG flow type to prevent recurrence; Mukenge spermatic-cord LVA. Thomas 2023 n = 150 — cellulitis 4.22 → 0.10 / yr. AAPS Grade 1C.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)[[35]](#ref35)[[37]](#ref37)[[40]](#ref40)[[41]](#ref41)[[46]](#ref46)</sup> |
| **Vascularized lymph node transfer (VLNT)** | Ehrl 2023 n = 9 giant penoscrotal — scrotal VLNT improved lymphatic transport; **0% recurrence at 49 mo**; Lindenblatt 2025 — VLNT alone reduced volume in 70% vs LVA 25% (p = 0.035). AAPS Grade 1B.<sup>[[30]](#ref30)[[45]](#ref45)[[51]](#ref51)[[52]](#ref52)</sup> |
| **SCIP Lymphatic Flap Transfer — "3R" (Yamamoto 2022)** | Radical reduction + reconstruction with pedicled SCIP lymphatic flap + SCIP pure-skin-perforator flap. n = 7 (4 scrotal, 3 penoscrotal); mean resected 1,511 g; **0% complications, 0% recurrence** at mean 22.7 mo; GLS 6.7 → 0.3.<sup>[[53]](#ref53)</sup> |
| **Complete Functional Lymphatic System Pedicled Transfer (Abdelfattah 2023)** | n = 26 advanced scrotal / penoscrotal lymphedema; **100% flap survival**; cellulitis dramatically reduced (p &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[54]](#ref54)</sup> |
| **CHASCIP — Combined Charles + Lymphatic SCIP flap (Ciudad 2025)** | n = 8 ISL Stage III; bilateral pedicled lymphatic SCIP flaps + STSG; mean resected 1,772.7 g; 25% complications; **0% recurrence at 34 mo**; sexual dysfunction 87.5% → 0%.<sup>[[55]](#ref55)</sup> |
| **Lymphatic System Transfer (LYST)** | SCIP flap with both lymph nodes and afferent lymphatic vessels intact; Yoshimatsu 2025 n = 8 — excess volume −11.2% at 39 mo, cellulitis significantly decreased (p = 0.025); Xu 2026 — pedicled SCIP LYST for lymphedema + chronic venous disease, no microsurgical anastomosis needed.<sup>[[56]](#ref56)[[57]](#ref57)</sup> |

## Synthesized Treatment Algorithm

| Severity | Approach |
|---|---|
| **Stage 0–1 / GLS ≤ 2** | CDT, weight management, treat underlying cause (antifilarials, immunomodulators); investigational ketoprofen.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |
| **Stage 2 / GLS 3–5** | Intensive CDT + PCD; prophylactic penicillin if ≥ 3–4 cellulitis/yr; LVA if functional lymphatics on ICG; VLNT if lymphatics sclerosed; excision + primary closure if conservative fails.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[27]](#ref27)[[45]](#ref45)</sup> |
| **Stage 3 / GLS 6–9 / elephantiasis** | **SCIP lymphatic flap transfer (3R / CHASCIP)** — one-stage curative, no postop compression required; modified Charles when lymphatic-reconstruction expertise unavailable; excision + VLNT for documented transport deficiency. Perioperative CDP.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)[[43]](#ref43)[[50]](#ref50)[[53]](#ref53)[[55]](#ref55)</sup> |
| **Obesity-induced GL / MLL** | Weight loss is fundamental; BLOOM technique combines sleeve gastrectomy + VLNT; comprehensive weight-loss plan must accompany excision (Wisenbaugh).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[26]](#ref26)[[30]](#ref30)[[50]](#ref50)</sup> |
| **Crohn's / ano-genital granulomatosis** | Oral steroids → azathioprine → anti-TNF; circumcision / debulking for refractory cases.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> |
