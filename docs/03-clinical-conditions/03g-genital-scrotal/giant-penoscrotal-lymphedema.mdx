# Giant Penoscrotal Lymphedema (Genital Elephantiasis)

**Giant penoscrotal lymphedema (GPL)** is end-stage (ISL Stage III) lymphedema of the penis and / or scrotum characterized by massive, irreversible tissue enlargement with fibrosis, papillomatosis, and skin changes that can severely impair genital function.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)</sup> No universally accepted size threshold defines "giant," but the term is applied when the mass causes **severe functional impairment** — inability to ambulate, void while standing, perform hygiene, or have intercourse — and resected tissue typically weighs **500 g to &gt; 20 kg** (up to **61 kg / 134 lbs** reported).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Synonyms: **scrotal elephantiasis**, **male genital elephantiasis**, **penoscrotal elephantiasis**.

For the broader clinical-conditions framework see [Genital Lymphedema](/docs/clinical-conditions/03g-genital-scrotal/genital-lymphedema). For surgical reconstruction technique see [Scrotal Reconstruction](/docs/surgical-techniques/04e-genital-reconstruction/scrotal-reconstruction).

---

## Epidemiology

- **Filariasis** is the most common cause worldwide — older estimates cited 120 million infected and 40 million with clinical disease; scrotal elephantiasis / hydrocele are hallmarks in sub-Saharan Africa, South / Southeast Asia, and Pacific Islands. Community prevalence 1.3–7.8% in endemic regions.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Developed world** — morbid obesity (MLL), primary / idiopathic lymphedema, cancer treatment (PLND ± RT).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- In the largest integrated European series (Torio-Padron, n = 51) — **84% primary**, 16% secondary; combined penoscrotal involvement in 53%.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- Male predominance; mean age at presentation 35–48 yr; median symptom duration before surgery 11.5 yr (Salako sub-Saharan series).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Etiology

| Primary (84% in Torio-Padron) | Secondary |
|---|---|
| Developmental lymphatic anomalies — congenital / praecox / tarda; syndromic FOXC2, GATA2, Meige.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | Filariasis (*W. bancrofti* 90%, *B. malayi*); MLL of morbid obesity (mean BMI 48–60 in the series cited below); cancer treatment (PLND ± RT); LGV / donovanosis / TB; Crohn's ano-genital granulomatosis; HS; trauma / non-oncologic surgery; idiopathic acquired.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[5]](#ref5)[[8]](#ref8)[[9]](#ref9)[[12]](#ref12)[[14]](#ref14)[[15]](#ref15)[[16]](#ref16)[[17]](#ref17)</sup> |

---

## Pathophysiology

Lymphatic obstruction → protein-rich interstitial fluid → chronic stasis → **inflammation (Th1/Th17 in filarial disease) + fibrosis + adipose hypertrophy + recurrent ADLA episodes** in a vicious cycle → end-stage elephantiasis with papillomatosis, verrucous skin, lymph vesicles, lymphorrhea.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

- **Filarial** — two-step process: parasite + innate-immune damage → adaptive-immune propagation; the endosymbiont **Wolbachia** drives much of the inflammation.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Obesity / MLL** — dartos hyperplasia / hypertrophy with **microvascular proliferation at the smooth-muscle–stroma interface** — features unique to genital MLL.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

---

## Histopathology

**Common across etiologies** — stromal fibrosis and edema, lymphangiectasia, perivascular chronic inflammation, multinucleated stromal cells.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

**Genital MLL (Lee Johns Hopkins n = 6)** — dartos hyperplasia / hypertrophy in all six; capillary neoangiogenesis at the smooth-muscle–stroma interface in 3; entrapped fat only a minor feature (unlike non-genital MLL); lesions 4–55 cm; all patients obese.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

**Filarial** — adult worms encapsulated within inflammatory granulomas in lymphatic vessels; lymphatic-wall thickening and dilatation.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

**Crohn's / ano-genital granulomatosis** — non-caseating granulomas (50%) and **intralymphatic granulomas** (14%).<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

**Malignant transformation** — Stewart-Treves lymphangiosarcoma in long-standing disease; none reported in the Lee MLL series.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

## Classification Systems

| System | Detail |
|---|---|
| **McDougal 2003** | Congenital vs acquired; self-limited (conservative) vs chronic with pathological tissue changes (surgical). Most chronic forms require excisional surgery with STSG.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Ehrl 2023** | Treatment-oriented for giant penoscrotal lymphedema — determines whether resection alone vs resection + VLNT is indicated. 8/9 patients were end-stage; scrotal VLNT improved lymphatic transport.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> |
| **ISL** | Giant GPL = **Stage III** elephantiasis. |
| **Genital Lymphedema Score (GLS, Yamamoto)** | 0–9; giant GPL typically scores **6–9**. |
| **Lu MRL 2016** | Inguinal lymph-node dysfunction mild → CDP; moderate → microsurgery; severe → excision (n = 36, uncontrolled). At 3–5 yr, patients with hypoplasia and moderate or severe dysfunction who underwent excision had excellent cosmesis and no lymphedema.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |

## Clinical Presentation

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Progressive massive genital swelling | Resected weights up to **61 kg / 134 lbs**.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Buried penis** | Penis engulfed within the lymphedematous mass; loss of standing voiding and sexual function. Risk factors: elevated BMI, chronic scrotal lymphedema, HS, chronic inflammation.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |
| Impaired ambulation | Mass hangs between thighs, causing gait disturbance and skin maceration. |
| Hygiene failure | Skin folds harbor moisture and bacteria. |
| Lymphorrhea | Lymph oozes from skin vesicles. |
| Recurrent cellulitis | Cellulitis was the most common complication (24%) in a pediatric genital lymphedema series.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |
| Sexual dysfunction | Complete inability to perform intercourse. |
| Psychosocial devastation | Profound impact on self-image, relationships, employment, mental health. |

**Exam.** Massive non-pitting firm penoscrotal mass; peau d'orange; papillomatosis / verrucous overgrowth; lymph vesicles; positive Stemmer; buried penis; concurrent LE lymphedema (76% pediatric);<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> associated hydrocele (43% in Torio-Padron).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

## Differential Diagnosis

| Diagnosis | Distinguishing feature |
|---|---|
| Inguinal hernia | Reducible, Valsalva impulse, bowel sounds.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> |
| Giant hydrocele | Transilluminates; ultrasound fluid.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> |
| Scrotal lipoma / liposarcoma | Fat on imaging; lipoma is the most common extratesticular neoplasm.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[22]](#ref22)</sup> |
| Spermatic-cord sarcoma | RMS (pediatric), liposarcoma / leiomyosarcoma (adult); solid, non-transilluminating; MRI for characterization.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)[[23]](#ref23)</sup> |
| Lymphoma | Firm, tender; systemic features.<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup> |
| Fibrous pseudotumor | Paratesticular mass; T2-hypointense on MRI.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> |
| Fournier's gangrene | Acute pain, crepitus, systemic toxicity. |
| Angiokeratoma / lymphangioma | Vascular malformation. |

In adult patients **sarcomas must be considered for all solid scrotal tumors**; biopsy when diagnosis is uncertain.<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> In long-standing lymphedema, new violaceous nodules or non-healing ulceration should prompt biopsy for lymphangiosarcoma; send resected tissue for histology.

## Imaging

| Modality | Role |
|---|---|
| **Ultrasound** | First-line per ACR Appropriateness Criteria; assesses testicular integrity, hydrocele, hernia, Doppler.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)[[26]](#ref26)</sup> |
| **ICG lymphography** | Linear → splash → stardust → diffuse progression. **Klein 2026** (n = 15 AABP) — significant lymphatic congestion in all patients with dermal backflow; **posterior scrotum drainage commonly preserved**, supporting individualized posterior scrotal flap planning.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup> Differentiates type 1 (leg-to-genital) vs type 2 (non-leg source) flow.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> |
| **MR lymphangiography (MRL)** | Lu 2016 (n = 36) — abnormal superficial + deep lymphatics with mild / moderate / severe inguinal-node dysfunction; treatment was assigned by grade (CDP vs lymphovenous microsurgery vs excision) without a comparison group, with no lymphedema at 3–5 yr in the 20 operated patients.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[28]](#ref28)[[29]](#ref29)</sup> |
| **Lymphoscintigraphy** | Traditional reference test; being replaced in many centers by ICG / MRL.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)[[30]](#ref30)</sup> |
| **CT / MRI** | For very large masses to exclude malignancy and plan surgery.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup> |

## Conservative Management

Insufficient as monotherapy for giant GPL but **critical for perioperative optimization**.

- **CDT** poorly adapted to genital anatomy — creative compression / hip-spica / panty-girdle / scrotal supports.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Perioperative CDP** is the cornerstone of Torio-Padron's integrated approach — **6% complication rate** in 51 patients (a retrospective, mostly primary-lymphedema cohort).<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
- **Underlying-cause therapy** — antifilarials (DEC / ivermectin / albendazole); immunomodulators for Crohn's; weight loss / bariatric surgery for obesity-induced MLL (GLP-1 agonist use has one case report of reduced MLL, 40 kg loss at 28 weeks).<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)[[15]](#ref15)[[31]](#ref31)[[40]](#ref40)</sup>
- **Cellulitis prevention** — treat portals of entry (intertrigo, tinea, skin folds) and start early antibiotics for ADLA. Low-dose penicillin reduced recurrence during prophylaxis in leg cellulitis (PATCH I: 22% vs 37%, HR 0.55) but the effect faded after stopping; genital-specific data are lacking, so this is extrapolated.<sup>[[41]](#ref41)</sup>

McDougal 2003 — chronic disease with pathological tissue changes **requires surgery**.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Surgical Management — Excisional / Debulking

### Modified Charles Procedure (excision + STSG)

Workhorse for resource-limited settings.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[31]](#ref31)[[32]](#ref32)[[33]](#ref33)</sup>

**Technique.** Complete excision to tunica vaginalis (scrotum) and Buck's fascia (penis); preserve and reposition testes / cords; midline scrotoplasty (raphe simulation); **zigzag ventral STSG suture line** on penis to prevent circumferential contracture; preserve inner prepucial skin for distal shaft if healthy.

| Series | n | Outcomes |
|---|---|---|
| Modolin 2006<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup> | 17 | Symptom regression; 1 recurrence (post-RT). |
| Singh 2011<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup> | 48 filarial (14 penile-only, 34 penoscrotal) | STSG for penile skin, scrotoplasty for scrotum; groin infection 25%; all reported satisfactory cosmesis at median 48 mo (recurrence not reported). |
| Salako 2018<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | 11 | Hematoma 27.3%, SSI 18.2%, recurrence 9.1%. |
| Morey 1997<sup>[[33]](#ref33)</sup> | 9 | Single-stage; excellent cosmesis. |
| Alwaal 2015<sup>[[34]](#ref34)</sup> | 54 mixed-etiology genital STSG (13 lymphedema) | 52/54 had &gt; 90% graft take; erection and voiding maintained or improved. |

### Excision + primary closure (Torio-Padron integrated)

n = 51 with perioperative CDP — 6% complications requiring revision; no flaps / grafts needed; 43% concurrent hydrocoelectomy; QOL improved on Glasgow Benefit Inventory.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Excision + flap reconstruction

Guiotto SR — 54.2% complication rate (highest), likely reflecting disease severity.<sup>[[35]](#ref35)</sup> Champaneria 2013 (single case) posterior fasciocutaneous scrotal flap + penile graft + panniculectomy; the mass had persisted despite 89 kg weight loss after gastric bypass, so weight loss alone may not reverse established MLL.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> Machol 2014 lateral scrotal flaps ± mid-raphe Z-plasty in 4 MLL patients — **2 of 4 recurred** (mean BMI 53.9; average first resection 3.5 kg).<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

### Buried penis with concurrent scrotal lymphedema

- **Corder 2023** (n = 7, mean BMI 48, mean weight 344 lbs) — concurrent scrotoplasty + infraumbilical panniculectomy; native glans skin salvageable in all but one; shaft STSG or adjacent tissue transfer; **88% wound dehiscence rate**.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Klein 2026** (n = 15 AABP) — ICG showed lymphatic congestion in all; **posterior scrotum drainage preserved**, supporting individualized posterior scrotal flap planning.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

### MLL excision in obesity

Wisenbaugh n = 11 (mean BMI 60, mean resected 21 kg, max 61 kg); QOL 1.3 → 7.7; mean weight change **+5.2 kg** at 26 mo (most patients gained weight) — pair excision with a weight-management plan; wound complications were common.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Surgical Management — Physiologic Lymphatic Reconstruction

A 13-study systematic review found recurrence after excision in four studies and none in lymphovenous anastomosis (LVA) studies; all were non-randomized series of fair quality, and patients receiving excision tended to have later-stage disease, so the groups are not comparable.<sup>[[42]](#ref42)</sup>

### Excision + VLNT (Ehrl 2023) — small retrospective series<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

- 9 patients (8 end-stage giant); penoscrotal resection ± **VLNT from the lateral thoracic region** (5/9) into the groin or scrotum.
- Treatment-oriented classification system developed.
- Median follow-up 49.0 mo; **0% recurrence**.
- Scrotal VLNT significantly improved lymphatic transport vs resection alone.

### Radical Reduction and Reconstruction (3R) — Yamamoto 2022<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

- n = 7 male genital elephantiasis (4 scrotal, 3 penoscrotal); mean resected **1,511 g** (609–2304 g).
- Reconstruction with **pedicled SCIP lymphatic flap transfer** for scrotum + SCIP pure-skin-perforator flap for penis.
- **0% complications, 0% recurrence** at mean 22.7 mo.
- **GLS 6.7 → 0.3** (p &lt; 0.05).

### Complete Functional Lymphatic-System Pedicled Transfer — Abdelfattah 2023<sup>[[36]](#ref36)</sup>

- n = 26 advanced scrotal / penoscrotal lymphedema.
- SCIP lymphatic flap for partial (n = 11) or total (n = 15) scrotal reconstruction + penile skin (n = 11).
- **100% flap survival**; cellulitis rates dramatically reduced (p &lt; 0.001).
- GLS 6.2 → 0.05 at mean 44.9 mo.

### CHASCIP — Combined Charles + Lymphatic SCIP flap — Ciudad 2025<sup>[[37]](#ref37)</sup>

- n = 8 ISL Stage III; bilateral pedicled lymphatic SCIP flaps + STSG.
- Mean resected 1,772.7 g; OR 160 min; EBL 200.6 mL.
- 25% complications (1 seroma / dehiscence, 1 partial graft loss).
- **0% recurrence at 34 mo**; sexual dysfunction **87.5% → 0%**.

## Penile Skin Reconstruction in Giant GPL

| Option | Detail |
|---|---|
| **STSG** | Standard; harvested from thigh 0.012–0.015 inch; applied to Buck's fascia; **zigzag ventral suture line**; 52 of 54 grafts with &gt; 90% take in Alwaal (mixed etiologies); preserve healthy inner prepucial skin for distal shaft.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)[[32]](#ref32)[[34]](#ref34)</sup> |
| **SCIP pure-skin-perforator flap** | For 3R / CHASCIP penile coverage with lymphatic-rich tissue.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[37]](#ref37)</sup> |
| **Integra + delayed STSG** | Two-stage: ADM + NPWT × 3 wk → unmeshed STSG; adequate extensibility during erection.<sup>[[38]](#ref38)</sup> |
| **FTSG** | From the hypogastric region — used in CHASCIP for penile coverage.<sup>[[37]](#ref37)</sup> |

## Individualized Treatment Framework

1. **Preoperative assessment.** ISL / GLS staging; ICG lymphography (note posterior-scrotum drainage for flap planning); MRL (inguinal-node dysfunction grade); exclude malignancy with biopsy if uncertain; optimize comorbidities (weight loss, infection control, antifilarials).
2. **Perioperative CDP.** Inpatient before and after surgery — a 6% revision rate was observed in the 51-patient cohort, without a control group proving that CDP caused the reduction.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
3. **Surgical approach (driven by available expertise):**
   - **If lymphatic-reconstruction expertise available** — consider **3R or CHASCIP SCIP-LFT** (promising small series; not an established gold standard) or excision + VLNT (Ehrl algorithm) when SCIP-LFT not feasible.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[3]](#ref3)[[36]](#ref36)[[37]](#ref37)</sup>
   - **If not available** — modified Charles procedure (radical excision + STSG) or excision + primary closure when perioperative CDP achieves sufficient volume reduction.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[31]](#ref31)[[32]](#ref32)</sup>
4. **Postoperative.** Wound care, individualized compression, prevention and prompt treatment of cellulitis, structured **weight-management plan** (most Wisenbaugh patients gained weight after excision), long-term recurrence surveillance.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

---

## Special Considerations

- **Obesity-related GPL.** Weight loss is fundamental; a four-patient excision series reported 50% recurrence; this is not a general recurrence probability. Consider weight-management / bariatric referral as appropriate.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Filarial GPL.** Evaluate for active infection and coordinate treatment; chronic lymphedema alone does not establish active filariasis or mandate DEC. Exclude relevant onchocerciasis / Loa loa risk before drug selection.<sup>[[39]](#ref39)</sup><sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup>
- **Pediatric genital lymphedema.** In a 25-patient series (not restricted to giant disease; 92% primary), 76% had concurrent lower-extremity lymphedema and 24% had cellulitis.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
