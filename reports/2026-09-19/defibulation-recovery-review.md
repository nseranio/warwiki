# Defibulation evidence recovery — September 19, 2026

## Scope

The prior page made universal timing, anaesthesia, operative, outcome, and counselling claims that exceeded the directly recoverable guidance. It was rebuilt as a bounded evidence overview, not an operative protocol or clinical clearance.

## Sources read

- **WHO guideline on the prevention of FGM and clinical management of complications, 2025**: directly read deinfibulation recommendations, rationale, implementation remarks, timing recommendation, and counselling best-practice statement. WHO conditionally recommends deinfibulation for Type III FGM on low-certainty evidence; either antepartum or intrapartum timing can be considered for vaginal birth on very-low-certainty evidence. Partner/family inclusion is only at the woman’s request.
- **Okusanya et al., 2026** (PMID: 41582683; PMCID: PMC12833633): PubMed record and abstract metadata read; full article is open access but not fully reviewed in this pass. It is retained only as a current synthesis, without using pooled estimates as patient predictions.
- **Jones et al., 2023** (PMID: 36572653): directly identified abstract record of qualitative work on preferences for timing and service provision. It is used only for the existence of preference/access considerations.

## Changes made

- Replaced universal treatment/timing and anaesthesia statements with the current WHO conditional evidence framing.
- Removed step-by-step technique, fixed trimester/labor-stage rules, clinical outcome numbers, complication expectations, and prescriptive counselling claims.
- Preserved trauma-informed, person-centred counselling and the no-reinfibulation boundary, with the woman’s consent controlling partner/family involvement.

## Unresolved

- The full Okusanya review and the full Jones qualitative report were not reviewed in this pass; their tables, supplements, and detailed methods are not used.
- The 2020 AAP text, 2017 systematic review, 2017 qualitative report, 2021 scoping review, and inherited procedure-specific materials were not recovered as complete sources in this pass.

No broad clinical verification is asserted.
