# Barbed-sutures evidence recovery — September 19, 2026

## Scope and disposition

The entire original `docs/01-foundations/surgical-principles/barbed-sutures.mdx` was read, then rebuilt as a bounded evidence reference. It is not a clinical clearance or an operative protocol.

Removed unsupported content included fixed product hierarchies, a universal gauge-upsize rule, product/clip instructions, a universal VUA standard-of-care label, claims of functional superiority, and quantitative upper-tract/bladder risk claims derived from inaccessible primary, animal, bench, or surveillance material. The prior instruction to leave a deliberate gap in VUA closure was removed: the accessible VUA review describes a watertight, optimally apposed, tension-free anastomosis, not intentional gapping.

## Unresolved source record

- Williams 2010 and the prior product-specific pyeloplasty, bench, animal, and product-label claims were not fully accessible in this pass. They are not retained as verified numerical or procedural assertions.
- Clapp 2020 was retained only for the cautious statement that reported cases and MAUDE data have linked exposed barbed tails with bowel obstruction; full text was not accessed and no incidence or comparative-risk claim is made.
- The complete clinical appropriateness of any use in UPJ, ureteral, ureteroenteric, bladder, or diversion reconstruction remains unresolved. The revised article directs readers to procedure-specific methods rather than inferring a rule from VUA or renorrhaphy.

## Editorial result

The revised page preserves the supported narrow conclusions: VUA and renorrhaphy studies show time-related benefits without proving a universal functional advantage. It records the limitations of the available comparisons, preserves ordinary operative discretion, and makes the upper-tract evidence gap visible rather than filling it with confident recommendations.
