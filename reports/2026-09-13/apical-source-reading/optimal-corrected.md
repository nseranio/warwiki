## Findings relevant to WARWIKI

1. This is a superiority trial, not an equivalence/noninferiority trial. Corrected two-year composite success is ULS100/155 (64.5%) versus SSLF94/149 (63.1%): unadjusted difference1.4 percentage points (95%CI−9.4 to12.2); adjusted OR1.1 (0.7–1.7), P=.84. Retain these corrected numbers, remove “equivalent.” Anatomic, symptom and retreatment endpoints are different; 64% is not an isolated apical-support rate.
2. Population: bothersome stageII–IV prolapse with apical descent to at least halfway, symptomatic and objectively demonstrated SUI, planned retropubic TVT. Previous SSLF/ULS, prior synthetic sling, mesh adverse reaction, radiation, and some important comorbidity/pain groups excluded. Uterine preservation was excluded. The results do not establish outcomes for every isolated vault repair or for hysteropexy.
3. Flow:408 initially behavioral-randomized,374 dual-randomized (188ULS/186SSLF); corrected primary surgical endpoint evaluable in304 (155/149). Overall follow-up84.5% is not the primary composite endpoint denominator. Actual surgery differed from assignment:178 in each arm received its assigned suspension;8 crossed each direction;2 assignedULS received no vault suspension. TVT371/374 (186ULS/185SSLF); hysterectomy271/374. Thus say “planned concomitant MUS,” or specify actual99.2%, rather than all received it.
4. Corrected anterior recurrence beyond hymen is20/155ULS(12.9%) versus20/153SSLF(13.1%), OR1.0(0.5–1.8), P=.89. Posterior3/155 versus5/153; apical descent >one-third16/155 versus21/152; apical pointC>0 only1/155 versus3/152. Do not infer inevitable greater anterior recurrence caused by SSLF vaginal-axis change.
5. Retreatment at2years: any POP surgery/pessary8/161ULS(5.0%) versus8/155SSLF(5.2%); surgery5/161 versus4/155. Missing untreated participants excluded from these denominators. These figures must not be called all-randomized reoperation rates.
6. Neurologic pain requiring altered care occurred13/188ULS(6.9%) versus23/186SSLF(12.4%), adjustedOR0.5(0.2–1.0), P=.049. Defined as new buttock/groin/lower-extremity pain within1week requiring additional treatment, not all postoperative pain. Persistent at4–6weeks:1ULS versus8SSLF(4.3%). Three SSLF patients returned for suture removal. Thus “USLS avoids buttock pain” is wrong.
7. Corrected eTable8 confirms six intraoperative ureteral obstructions inULS (6/188=3.2%):five relieved by suture removal,one stented. One additional ureteral injury recognized later. SSLF0observed, not proof of zero risk. Both current publisher and stalePMC prose incorrectly say “5(3.2%)”; prefer actual corrected table. Neither arm had urethral injury. These are ureteral outcomes.
8. Serious AEs31/188ULS16.5% versus31/186SSLF16.7%, OR0.9(0.5–1.6), not evidence of identical safety. Only5/188 and9/186 were considered likely related; all-cause SAE does not equal procedure-specific complication rate. Many bladder injuries were from the concomitant TVT. Mesh exposure reflects TVT, not mesh used for native-tissue prolapse repair.
9. Null perioperative BPMT results apply to this addition around combined prolapse/SUI surgery, not to individualized PFMT for nonsurgical POP or persistent symptoms. Exploratory secondary outcomes and small interactions were not multiplicity-adjusted; do not translate the unexpected apical interaction into a harm recommendation.
