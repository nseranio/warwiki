## Clinically important interpretation

- Review includes 59 RCTs/6705 women; search through 14 March 2022; cannot cover ASPIRe 2024 or later prolapse trials.
- Discussion supports sacral colpopexy advantage principally for post-hysterectomy vault prolapse, with patient suitability caveat; does not establish same comparative advantage for uterine prolapse.
- Native-vaginal comparison pools distinct comparators: two uterosacral versus sacrospinous trials and one versus high levator myorrhaphy. Castro anchoring trial explicitly excluded from this comparison despite opening four-trial count.
- Absorbable versus permanent sutures: five trials/727 allocated across SCP and USLS; awareness RR 0.78 (95% CI 0.39–1.56; four RCTs/493; moderate certainty), any recurrent prolapse RR 0.82 (0.48–1.40; three/417; reported high certainty), exposure RR 0.25 (0.04–1.60; five/688; low certainty, I²70%). Do not convert exposure result into proved benefit.
- SCP route outcomes are generally imprecise; reported laparoscopic versus open hospital-stay difference −1.68 days (−2.45 to −0.92; three/246; I²81%, low certainty); lap versus robotic operating-time difference −43.71 minutes (−67.14 to −20.28; three/245; I²70%). Historical platforms and heterogeneous trials limit current precise estimates.
- SCP graft comparison mixes cadaveric fascia lata, porcine dermis and autologous fascia lata in three trials/264; no generic biological-graft equivalence conclusion.
- Pectopexy category also includes Dubuisson lateral suspension and another lateral/round-ligament procedure; five trials/355 are not five trials of one standardized pectopexy.
- VUE trials excluded as high risk of bias; authors report sensitivity inclusion does not materially change primary vault-effect directions. This is review-author judgment, not evidence that pragmatic trials can generally be discarded.
- Appendix operative descriptions are dated generic descriptions, including required bowel preparation for SCP and anterior repair sometimes for SUI; not suitable for direct contemporary protocol adoption.

Comparison1 data table, read in this segment, reports vaginal versus sacral colpopexy: awareness RR2.31 (95%CI1.27–4.21; four trials/346), prolapse reoperation RR2.33 (1.34–4.04; six/497), any examination recurrence RR1.87 (1.32–2.65; five/422). These are distinct outcomes and denominators; applicability is mainly post-hysterectomy vault prolapse. Dyspareunia RR3.44 (0.61–19.53; three/106) is imprecise. Bladder/bowel injury composite is not proof of equal rare-event safety.

Native-vaginal results: awareness RR0.88 (0.68–1.15; one trial/267; moderate certainty). Intraoperative ureteric injury RR15.91 (2.13–118.51; two/544) reflects rare events and mixed comparator procedures; corrected original OPTIMAL counts are preferable for procedure-specific counseling. Anterior, apical and posterior examination outcomes did not show a clear between-group difference. This does not justify calling all procedures equivalent.

Suture trials show little discernible difference in broad recurrence, but narrow apical-recurrence subgroup RR8.85 (1.16–67.74; two/161) is imprecise and must not be omitted when evaluating the entire evidence. Exposure is a combined mesh/suture outcome across different operations. The reviewed material does not establish a universally best suture.
