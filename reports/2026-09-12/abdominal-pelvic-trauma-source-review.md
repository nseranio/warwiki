# Abdominal vascular exposure and pelvic trauma sources

Reviewed 2026-09-12 by root. This documents source access and interpretation, not clinical verification of the whole site.

## Kobayashi et al. 2016

[Abdominal vascular trauma](https://doi.org/10.1136/tsaco-2016-000015), PMID 29766059, PMC5891707. Entire primary XML text (45,763 characters), one complete table, all 66 references, declarations and all seven actual operative photographs read. Media archive contained image variants, with no supplementary document listed. The old teaching review supplies anatomical exposure context; current recommendations are cross-checked against ESVS 2025 and European trauma bleeding 2023.

Useful distinctions: local Kocher mobilization differs from complete right medial visceral rotation; standard left rotation can include the kidney, whereas the modified anterior renal plane leaves it posteriorly. Supraceliac control uses a gastrohepatic-window/hiatal exposure with esophageal and posterior-branch protection, not a generic emergency mesenteric incision. Total hepatic vascular exclusion needs suprahepatic and infrahepatic caval control plus a Pringle maneuver; Pringle plus infrarenal control alone is incomplete.

Do not import older blanket recommendations for atriocaval shunts, universal fasciotomy after IVC ligation, celiac ligation plus cholecystectomy, internal iliac sacrifice with little consequence, or every penetrating hematoma requiring exploration. Fullen zone-IV wording that says no ischemia cannot establish that segmental SMA branch loss is harmless. Numerical ischemia windows and older observational mortality estimates are not universal thresholds for fresh iatrogenic injury.

## WSES pelvic trauma 2017

[Coccolini et al.](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-017-0117-6), PMID 28115984, PMC5241998. Entire 104,897-character XML-derived text read, including all recommendations, two tables and footnotes, all 156 references/declarations and all three actual graphics (search flow, fracture classification and management pathway). No separate supplementary document was listed; the media archive contains figure variants.

Retained with appropriate age/scope: binder at the greater trochanters for suspected unstable pelvic-ring bleeding, attention to pressure injury and timely replacement; physiology and available resources guide complementary stabilization, preperitoneal packing and arterial embolization. Absence of a CT blush does not exclude bleeding. Packing may use a separate preperitoneal incision while a separate laparotomy treats abdominal injury. The source really does describe pack revision at 48–72 hours; this is not a mandatory delay in persistent hemorrhage or a universal relook rule.

Do not carry forward its negative-FAST reassurance or historic REBOA suggestions of hours of zone-III occlusion/no organ ischemia. Newer bleeding guidance and ESVS 2025 take precedence. Orthopedic definitive-fixation timing is not a GU damage-control timetable. Its older genitourinary imaging paragraphs are not a replacement for current bladder/urethral trauma guidance.

Source quality notes: search describes 1980–December 2015 and no RCT found, but narrative cites later 2016 evidence, including a quasi-randomized study. The actual search flow also has arithmetic inconsistencies (360 full-text papers minus 311 exclusions is 49, not 59 included); do not use it as independently replicated search accounting. References 9 and 36 do not cleanly support the adjacent GRADE/commercial binder claims. An editorial formatting instruction in the XML was ignored as source artifact. The old algorithm is not copied into the website; a new pathway must reflect updated scope and exceptions. None of the 156 antecedent papers is claimed individually full-read from reading this bibliography.
