# Cochrane HBOT for late radiation injury: complete source read

Reviewed September 12, 2026. This is an independent source-access and critical-appraisal record for Lin et al., *Hyperbaric oxygen therapy for late radiation tissue injury*, Cochrane 2023, Issue 8, CD005005.pub5; PMID 37585677; PMCID PMC10426260. It is not clinical clearance of the HBOT page or an independent re-analysis of every included trial.

## Access and actual read extent

- **Primary full text:** [PMC article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10426260/), [publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD005005.pub5). Direct retrieval supplied the complete HTML; a later web-tool open returned a browser-check page, which was not counted as article access.
- **Complete article text read:** all 216,646 extracted characters, including abstract, plain-language summary, two summary-of-findings tables, background, methods, every results and discussion section, conclusions, study characteristics/bias assessments, excluded/ongoing-study records, all six appendices, analysis tables, history, declarations and all 91 reference entries. All 38 HTML tables were included in this sequential read. Counting reference entries means reading the reference list, not reading those 91 publications.
- **All 14 graphics viewed and read:** Figures 1–3 and forest plots 1.1, 2.1–2.4, 3.1, 4.1, 5.1, 6.1 and 7.1–7.2. A readable [81-page review PDF copy](https://diving-rov-specialists.com/index-b-documents_htm_files/scient-c_608-hyperbaric-oxygen-therapy-late-radiation-tissue-injury.pdf) was used for graphics after matching the title, authors, 2023 Issue 8 and pub5 identifier to PMC. PDF pages 13–18, 63 and 65–72 were visually inspected, including continued flow/bias figures. The PDF was not separately reread word for word after the complete primary HTML read.
- The six appendices are five search strategies and the data-extraction form. No separately linked review supplementary file was identified in the primary HTML. Separate original-study protocols, supplements, raw data and most full original papers were **not** newly read in this assignment. Earlier independently read RICH-ART/HOT2 material remains scoped in [the prior peer report](wound-hbot-peer-review.md).
- A new, bounded check of **HOT2's primary Methods, co-primary endpoint text and safety results** was performed from its full-paper cache to resolve the review's extraction discrepancies. This was not a new complete HOT2 read. [HOT2 primary article](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(15)00461-1).

## Findings that matter for WARWIKI

The January 24, 2022 search yielded 18 included studies reporting data on 1,071 participants. The latter total mixes randomized and analyzed counts: the review lists 65 for Forner despite 97 randomized, 74 for HOT2 despite 84 randomized, 79 for RICH-ART despite 87 randomized and 100 for HOPON despite 144 randomized. **“18 trials, 1,071 participants” is defensible; “1,071 patients randomized” is not.** These trials span different organs, severity, treatments and time points, including prevention as well as treatment. The review does not establish one response rate for all radiation injuries or GU reconstruction.

The pooled improvement estimate is low-certainty and heterogeneous. Bladder findings need their own endpoint descriptions: RICH-ART's contribution to the dichotomous improvement forest is **cystoscopic grade improvement**, not the primary patient-reported EPIC symptom endpoint. The latter is a 10.1-point difference in change from baseline, distinct from the review's separate comparison of final EPIC scores. The small hyaluronic-acid comparison does not demonstrate HBOT superiority over that active treatment. No included randomized evidence establishes avoidance of cystectomy, diversion or bowel surgery.

The head/neck wound-dehiscence signal comes from two poorly reported Marx textbook accounts, with unclear methods across all bias domains. It cannot establish improved healing of GU fistulas, urethroplasty grafts or irradiated pelvic reconstruction. Established mandibular ORN and prophylaxis before dental surgery also must remain separate. The review's pain synthesis combines these clinical settings and uses final pain scores under a heading describing change scores.

No demonstrated mortality advantage or neurologic benefit should be inferred. The review supports discussion of selected radiation-injury indications with important uncertainty. Visual refractive effects and pressure-related ear problems remain relevant; rare serious hazards cannot be excluded because few or no events appeared in these small trials. No randomized tumor-recurrence outcome was available to establish oncologic safety.

## Source inconsistencies: do not propagate into patient-facing numbers

These are source-level findings, not newly introduced WARWIKI errors. They do not invalidate all findings, but favor indication-specific original outcomes over copying a summary statistic without checking its inputs.

| Location | Check and editorial implication |
|---|---|
| **Ear-barotrauma forest 7.2** | HOT2 is entered as 15/53 versus **6/37**. The original safety population is 53 versus **28**, with 15 versus six ear-pain/barotrauma events; the review's own study table also gives 6/28. The erroneous extra nine controls explains 515 forest participants versus 506 in the narrative. Do not quote the pooled sham-control RR 1.07 as independently verified. No replacement meta-analysis was calculated. |
| **“No sham compression” subgroup** | Clarke's study table describes brief compression to 1.34 ATA followed by 1.1 ATA, but forest 7.2 places it under no sham compression. This categorization requires qualification; it is not a clean compressed-versus-uncompressed control distinction. |
| **HOT2 extraction table** | Control bowel-change IQR is printed −6 to **90**, leading to SD 71.1; the original is −6 to **9**. Rectal-bleeding SD 11.48 is inconsistent with its own IQR 1–3 (the stated IQR/1.35 method would give approximately 1.48). Rectal LENT-SOMA control change is printed +1.5 where original Table 6 gives **−1.5**. Those converted values should not be reused as valid trial results. |
| **HOT2 eligibility** | The review's table lists history of prostate cancer as an exclusion although its own cohort contains many such patients. Original Methods permit this history subject to specified serial PSA screening. Do not copy the erroneous exclusion. |
| **Overall improvement forest 2.1** | Seven displayed study rows total 501 participants, but the zero-event Pritchard row contributes no estimable RR; six estimable rows total 468. The abstract's “five RCTs” differs from the six-study summary-of-findings entry. The reported effect is based on mixed outcomes and should not be relabeled a common clinical response endpoint. |
| **Marx ORN denominator** | Forests 2.1/4.1 use 52 HBOT and 54 controls, whereas the study description gives 104 participants and dehiscence forest 5.1 uses 52/52. The original textbook reports were not obtained; this denominator conflict remains unresolved. |
| **Head/neck subgroup direction** | Narrative subgroup RRs 2.2 and 8.7 are the inverse direction of the HBOT-versus-control forest values approximately 0.45 and 0.12. Do not mix those directions. The overall dehiscence forest reports RR 0.24 with a wide interval and substantial heterogeneity. |
| **Pain synthesis 3.1** | The plot shows 157 participants and I² 28%; other review passages give 162 participants or I² 40%. Its inputs are final pain means, not necessarily within-person change scores. HOPON contributes a preventive dental-surgery population. Avoid describing this as a precisely quantified treatment effect in established ORN or GU pain. |
| **RICH-ART and Shao narrative** | Cystoscopic response, final EPIC level and baseline-to-follow-up EPIC change are different outcomes. One Shao function paragraph also reverses the 20/16 arm sizes used elsewhere. Preserve primary-trial denominators and endpoints. |

## Effect on the current page

The complete revised `docs/01-foundations/surgical-principles/hyperbaric-oxygen-therapy.mdx`, including all 40 references, was reread after the source work. **No blocking introduced clinical error found in the checked scope.** It uses the original RICH-ART symptom endpoint, distinguishes the uncontrolled five-year extension, keeps HORTIS/HOT2 separate, avoids unsupported GU transfer of head/neck results and describes adverse effects qualitatively. No MDX changes were made by this reviewer.

This read establishes access to the complete 2023 review; it is not a fresh systematic search through September 2026. The review's “ongoing” registry rows are historical and should not be presented as current statuses. Later evidence such as RICH-ART 2025 must be assessed separately, as already done on the page.
