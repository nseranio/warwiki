# Perioperative nutrition correction and source audit — September 12, 2026

The complete original 614-line MDX, all 59 reference entries, and the complete rewritten page were read. This is a substantial correction of recommendations and evidence interpretation. Full page reading does not certify every cited paper or clinical claim. Research PDFs/XML remain outside the repository and deployment.

## Material corrections

- Added **SWOG S1600 (2026)**: the phase 3 cystectomy trial did not show fewer 30-day complications with specialized immunonutrition than matched standard supplements. The primary analysis was 178 of 203 randomized patients; severe malnutrition was excluded. Avoided equivalence, survival-benefit and supplements-versus-no-supplements claims.
- Corrected the **OCULUS (2026)** result: holding one dose produced fewer clinically significant retained gastric contents (3.1%) than continuation (25.0%). The previous article reversed the groups. The publisher also issued visual-abstract and Figure 2 corrections in May 2026; current main text and the corrected abstract were checked. The trial concerns stable users undergoing sedated endoscopy, not all major surgery; no observed aspiration does not establish safety.
- Replaced the stale seven-day ESPEN PN rule with **2025 recommendations 10/11**: consider supplementation when oral/EN remains below 50% within three to four days, and prompt PN when these routes are contraindicated, particularly with severe malnutrition. Preserved the specific Gao trial eligibility rather than applying its outcome to all postoperative patients.
- Corrected malnutrition assessment: GLIM follows screening; albumin/prealbumin are risk/inflammation markers, not body-protein assays, treatment targets or automatic operation-delay rules.
- Removed unsupported inevitability of graft failure, microsurgical extrapolations, one-study screening-performance claims and universal high-dose micronutrient bundles. The 21-patient Kjaer mechanistic study did not demonstrate the claimed significant between-group collagen advantage (49%, P=.10). Bruno's 200-patient study was retrospective observational, not the randomized proof implied by the page. Panayi's 34,571 cohort had albumin data for only 16,250; observational associations do not establish nutritional causation.
- Reframed older immunonutrition evidence: the Cochrane 29-patient confidence interval crosses no effect; Amer's primary complication result was nonsignificant and tested a bundle; INCyst remains a cited protocol, not a result or guaranteed publication date. Removed a universal cystectomy-superiority statement and routine albumin optimization.
- Distinguished access route from completeness of PN. Removed contradictory macronutrient percentages, erroneous nitrogen/protein conversions, central-access 'no limit', fixed potassium/magnesium orders, a universal lipid ceiling and 'never stop PN' instructions.
- Corrected **Omegaven's pediatric PNAC indication**, distinct from routine adult PN or prevention; removed transfer of infant liver-disease estimates to adult reconstruction and a guaranteed liver-disease progression sequence.
- Corrected **vitamin K/warfarin**: monitor INR and adjust anticoagulation rather than automatically omitting vitamin provision.
- Separated **ASPEN and NICE refeeding frameworks**, including two-tier NICE risk logic, oral versus IV thiamine/magnesium, severe-deficit precautions, 12-hour early electrolyte monitoring in high-risk ASPEN patients and prompt emergency glucose despite thiamine risk.
- Removed guaranteed GLP-1 protein depletion and inferred wound mechanisms. Corrected Lee to TriNetX, Aschen to patients with diabetes, and retained conflicting observational findings only as uncertainty. Removed generic bariatric supplement recipes and a body-contouring timetable applied to GU surgery.
- Linked individualized B12/metabolic follow-up to the canonical companion pages; a fixed spared ileal length does not guarantee absorption.

## Primary papers read beyond abstracts

### SWOG S1600

[Hamilton-Reeves et al., JAMA Network Open 2026](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2851786), DOI10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.23622, PMID42475098, PMC13386599.

Read the complete primary article text: abstract, Methods, Results, Discussion/limitations, conclusion, Tables1/2, all49 reference entries, funding/disclosures and article information. Figure captions were read; figure images and protocol/statistical-plan, adverse-event and data-sharing supplements were not inspected. This is a full main-text read, not a claim that all supplements or underlying49 papers were read.

Both drinks were matched for calories/protein; the analysis found 56/90 versus 51/88 complications, OR1.18 (95%CI0.64–2.18). Secondary UTI and exploratory survival signals were not promoted to definitive harm/benefit. The article lists an analysis period extending after its publication date; this internal inconsistency was not copied. Manufacturer provision of the drinks and disclosed interests were read.

### OCULUS

[Ahmad et al., JAMA Internal Medicine 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2026.0027), PMC12993733.

Read the complete main article, abstract, Methods, Results, baseline table, Discussion/limitations, conclusion, all19 references, funding/disclosures and both correction notices as described in article metadata. Figure captions were read, but images and the three supplements were not inspected. Main-text results consistently report hold3.1% versus continue25%; the figure caption still uses inconsistent intervention/control terminology, so the named hold/continue groups and numerical results were used.

Eight exclusions after randomization, early stopping, 90% confidence intervals, single masking, EGD/colonoscopy dietary differences, absent aspiration events and lack of power for rare safety events were considered. The liquid-diet subgroup was not separately randomized; symptom absence did not exclude retained contents.

### Gao supplemental PN trial

[Gao et al., JAMA Surgery 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamasurg.2022.0269), PMC8928091.

Read the complete primary main text and abstract, all Methods/Results, Tables1/2 and footnotes, Discussion/limitations, conclusion, all41 references, funding/disclosures and article information. Figure captions were read but images/supplements were not inspected. Enrollment required NRS2002≥3, ≤30% EN energy delivery on POD2 and expected stay>7days. One patient withdrew after randomization, leaving115 versus114 in the outcome comparison. The infection benefit does not imply a demonstrated length-of-stay or mortality benefit.

### ASPEN visceral-protein position paper

[Evans et al.](https://doi.org/10.1002/ncp.10588): the complete seven-page available prepublication text, references and table text were read, including recovered page6 text. The extracted Table1 columns are imperfect; the table and Figure1 have not been visually inspected. This paper explicitly rejects albumin/prealbumin as proxies for muscle mass or adequacy of feeding.
