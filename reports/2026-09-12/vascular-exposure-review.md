# Vascular control, exposure and pelvic anatomy review

Reviewed September 12, 2026. Three complete original pages and their current revisions were actually read: vascular damage control (original 373 lines/35 references), exposure (333/21) and pelvic vascular anatomy (181/8). All 52 available indexed abstracts from the first two pages and all eight from the anatomy page were read; that does not mean every original full paper was accessible or read. Final source hashes and individual reference access are in [the batch ledger](../2026-09-11/full-site-review/vascular-exposure.json).

## Substantive changes

Vascular management now uses source-specific hemorrhage control and physiological assessment. Removed universal damage-control clocks, an unverified quotation, routine formalin packing, negative-FAST reassurance, harmless-iliac-sacrifice claims and unsupported fixed renal/shunt ischemia rules. Corrected hepatic vascular exclusion, renal and visceral perfusion decisions, REBOA interpretation, and the distinction between traumatic injury evidence and iatrogenic GU application.

The major-trial table includes PATCH-Trauma, PROCOAG, CRYOSTAT-2, FiiRST-2, TOWAR and SWiFT. Primary outcomes, analyzed denominators and uncertainty are preserved; null superiority findings do not establish equivalence or equal safety. UK-REBOA describes randomized strategy assignment rather than comparing actual balloon recipients. WHO 2025 supports a distinct postpartum branch with concurrent first-response treatment, birth-based TXA timing, all balloon prerequisites and parallel preparation for surgical control. Intrauterine gauze is distinguished from vaginal/preperitoneal packing.

[Exposure review](exposure-final-review.md) corrects positioning, instruments, route/operation conflations, ureter safety, Kocher versus right rotation, standard/modified left rotation and hematoma exploration criteria. The 2026 JSES consensus is used for terminology, not as new histological validation or a comparative trial.

[Pelvic vascular anatomy review](pelvic-vascular-anatomy-review.md) corrects pudendal/Martius supply, corona mortis, venous valves and communications, arterial versus venous embolization, presacral hemostasis, cadaveric denominators and lymphatic drainage. Historical papers remain useful for anatomy but do not justify universal safe distances, clinical intervention rules or oncologic dissection templates.
