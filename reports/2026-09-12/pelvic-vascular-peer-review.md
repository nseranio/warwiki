## Independent source checks and boundaries

- **Corona mortis:** read the accessible primary article's complete main narrative and both tables. Results/Table 2 specify 1,455 **hemipelves**, although its abstract incorrectly calls them patients; 13 studies and an approximately 46% pooled prevalence are supported, with substantial heterogeneity (overall I² approximately 95%). The revised page appropriately distinguishes venous and arterial connections and avoids a fixed safe distance. Reference-list output was truncated late in the list; not all original references or actual figures were independently reread in this peer task. Root's separate fuller source read is not represented as this reviewer's own.
- **Presacral distances and iliac tributaries:** complete indexed primary abstracts read for [Wieslander 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajog.2006.07.045) and [Kanjanasilp 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31188181/). The former supports variable vessel relationships; the latter confirms 40 venous systems in 20 cadavers, with relevant tributaries in 20 systems from 15 cadavers. Its 75% figure is a cadaver denominator. Neither full manuscript nor its figures was independently available/read for this peer task.
- **Venous anatomy:** independently read complete physical pages 2–3 and 8–9 plus relevant collateral, pudendal and varicocele passages in Zurcher 2022, DOI [10.1148/rg.220012](https://doi.org/10.1148/rg.220012), cached at `/tmp/vascular11-pelvic-venous2022.pdf` and `.txt`. This checks presacral/vertebral relationships, ovarian–uterine and cross-pelvic connections, normal gonadal outflow and the distinction between reflux/compression anatomy and symptomatic disease. Selected figure captions were read; this peer task did not reinspect the source's actual graphics or reread all 14 pages.
- **Pudendal supply and Martius flap:** read the relevant complete vulvar neurovascular passages in [Yavagal 2011](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0031-1281481) and the flap-harvest technique passages in [Wilson 2017](https://doi.org/10.21037/tau.2017.04.38). They support internal/external pudendal contributions and preservation of the inferior internal-pudendal pedicle in the commonly used inferiorly based flap. These were selected source sections, not another complete manuscript/figure review.
- **Arterial interruption/ischemia:** prior independent full ESVS 2025 main-source review is recorded in [the ESVS source report](esvs-vascular-trauma-source-review.md), including its specific supplemental-access limits. The anatomical warning that collateral circulation does not abolish ischemic risk is reasonable; this does not extend that trauma guideline into an authoritative technical obstetric ligation manual.
- **Urethral and penile lymphatics:** directly read current EAU Primary Urethral Carcinoma §5.6 and Penile Cancer §6.2.1 regional-drainage descriptions. The final table matches these descriptions, distinguishes anterior/posterior male and proximal/distal female urethra, and does not turn drainage into an oncologic dissection template. [Urethral guideline](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/primary-urethral-carcinoma/chapter/diagnostic-evaluation-and-staging), [penile guideline](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/penile-cancer/chapter/disease-management). Additionally read Long 2015's full primary main text, DOI [10.1002/ar.23094](https://doi.org/10.1002/ar.23094): its 13-volunteer foreskin-injection MRL study supports interconnected superficial networks and inguinal pathways, but does not establish every deep corporal or urethral drainage route. Actual Long figures and the full bibliography were not inspected, and the study was not added to the page.

## WHO obstetric section check

The section and added bullets agree with the directly read WHO/FIGO/ICM 2025 Recommendations 21–41 and selected full evidence-annex sections. Exact access, **21 main pages, 140 annex pages, 18 rendered pages**, source hashes and exclusions are recorded in [the WHO review](who-pph2025-source-review.md).

Specifically checked: concurrent initial interventions and cause assessment; prompt bundle initiation; TXA 1 g IV over ten minutes with its three-hour clock anchored to **birth**, and the postpartum repeat-dose criteria; all five balloon prerequisites; the distinction between intrauterine gauze and packing for another bleeding source; parallel surgical preparation when embolization is considered; fibrinogen goal at least 2 g/L; and lack of a proven optimal universal obstetric transfusion ratio. The instruction not to delay necessary surgery merely to exhaust every conservative option is a defensible clinical interpretation of the guideline's escalation and context requirements, rather than a verbatim standalone WHO recommendation.

No introduced clinical dosing, denominator, anatomical or escalation error remained in these reviewed versions. Clinical sign-off and exhaustive independent verification of all retained background anatomy remain outside this peer report.
