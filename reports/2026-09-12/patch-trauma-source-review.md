# PATCH-Trauma 2023: primary-paper review

Reviewed 2026-09-12 by root for vascular damage control. [Gruen et al., NEJM 2023](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2215457), PMID 37314244. Complete ten-page publisher paper retrieved from [the Handtevy-hosted copy](https://www.handtevy.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/TXA-NEJM-2023.pdf), not an abstract or summary. All text, both tables/footnotes, all 15 references and the affiliation appendix read. All three actual figures and both tables visually inspected on physical PDF pages 5–9. The publisher protocol, supplementary appendix, disclosures and data statement were identified but their four retrieval attempts returned HTTP 403; none is represented as read. No patient-level data audited.

1310 randomized (661 TXA, 646 placebo, three allocation unknown); intention-to-treat groups 657 and 643. Primary six-month functional outcome available for 572 and 559, with approximately 13% missing. The regimen was 1 g IV over ten minutes prehospital plus 1 g over eight hours in adults with COAST score at least three, within three hours of injury, in advanced trauma systems; 92% had blunt injury.

Favorable six-month functional survival: 53.7% versus 53.5%, RR 1.00 (95% CI 0.90–1.12), absolute difference 0.2 percentage points (−5.6 to 6.0). This is a null primary result, not equivalence. Day-28 mortality 17.3% versus 21.8%, RR 0.79 (0.63–0.99), is a secondary result with unadjusted multiplicity; six-month mortality RR 0.83 (0.67–1.03). Any vascular occlusive event was 23.6% versus 19.7%, RR 1.20 (0.97–1.48), not proof of no thrombotic risk. More severely disabled survivors do not prove TXA caused disability. Approximately 16% in both arms received open-label TXA, and protocol deviations were common. Recruitment stopped at 1310 of a planned 1316 because study agent expired.

Table 2's cause-specific six-month mortality rows use hazard ratios; the all-cause six-month row is the reported risk ratio, as specified in Methods. Do not conflate them. This supports retaining appropriately scoped early trauma TXA guidance while acknowledging a null functional primary endpoint; it does not establish universal TXA use for iatrogenic GU bleeding. Antecedent references and unavailable supplements were not all independently read.
