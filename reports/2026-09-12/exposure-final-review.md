## Principal corrections

The page now separates positioning, incision selection, tissue planes and vascular-control decisions. It replaces maximal hip flexion/abduction and a supposedly safe two-hour cutoff with the least positioning required and a link to the updated positioning workflow. It distinguishes hand-held retractors from weighted specula and sends medication selection to the hydrodissection hub instead of prescribing routine vasopressin.

Vaginal exposure no longer conflates Burch colposuspension with an anterior vaginal incision, posterior colporrhaphy with obligatory levator/perineal repair, or one fixed distance with ureteral safety. Sacrospinous approaches and uterosacral ureteral assessment are described separately. Perineal access no longer requires universal muscle division, circumferential urethral dissection or maximum lithotomy. Symphysiotomy is distinguished from pubectomy; the favorable 46-patient historical series is not presented as universal safety.

Abdominal routes retain useful selection detail without promises that extraperitoneal surgery eliminates bowel, adhesion or leak complications, or that bilateral ureteral work invariably requires a transperitoneal route. Sato's denominator is corrected to 23 operations in 22 patients. A new 2026 JSES consensus is used narrowly to explain inconsistent fascial terminology and variable operative planes, not as new histological proof or an outcome study.

Kocher mobilization, complete right medial visceral rotation, full left rotation behind the kidney and modified left rotation anterior to it are now distinct. The page removes the unrelated Frey-procedure citation and claims of automatic whole-ureter, renal or retrohepatic-caval exposure. It separates direct infrarenal retroperitoneal access from indiscriminate mesenteric incision. Historical MVR and pancreaticosplenic series retain their actual selected populations and cannot establish zero morbidity or comparative superiority.

The old always/never hematoma-zone algorithm is replaced by ESVS 2025 recommendations 61–63: shock with ongoing suspected major vascular bleeding requires immediate control; at trauma laparotomy, an unstable patient **or** unstable hematoma supports exploration; a stable patient **and** stable hematoma supports leaving it unopened with subsequent CTA. The Class I/Level C evidence and guideline exclusion of iatrogenic injury are explicit. Temporary containment does not excuse assessment of known vascular, bowel, ureteral or ischemic injury.
