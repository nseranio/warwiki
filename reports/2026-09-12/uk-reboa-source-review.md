## Findings to preserve in the site

1. **This randomizes a treatment strategy, not actual balloon inflation.** Ninety patients were assigned 46 to standard care plus a REBOA strategy and 44 to standard care. One control participant withdrew at day 4, leaving 89 for the 90-day primary analysis. Only 19/46 assigned REBOA actually had a balloon inflated. Seventeen improved before full inflation, two deteriorated before arterial access, and eight had unsuccessful access. The actual flow figure also records two control-arm balloon-inflation crossovers. Failure to inflate in an improving patient is not automatically failure to follow the assigned strategy.
2. **No demonstrated survival benefit, with a concerning early mortality signal.** At 90 days, deaths were 25/46 versus 18/43; Bayesian OR 1.58, 95% credible interval 0.72–3.52, posterior probability of increased mortality 86.9%. The main model's absolute difference is 11.3 percentage points (credible interval −8.1 to 30.1), distinct from the simple raw difference. Avoid claiming a conventionally statistically significant 90-day increase or equivalence. At three hours the corresponding counts were 11/46 versus 2/44, OR 4.25 (1.33–15.99), posterior harm probability 99.3%; at six hours 13/46 versus 4/44, OR 3.14 (1.13–9.76), 98.6%. These secondary analyses have no multiplicity adjustment. Keep the 44-control denominator at early time points.
3. **Timing cannot prove a mechanism.** Among patients who underwent a qualifying definitive hemorrhage-control procedure, the reported medians were 83 versus 64 minutes from randomization. Only 14/46 versus 19/44 underwent such a procedure, with timing available for 12 of the 14 intervention patients and all 19 controls. This is a selected, competing-risk-affected subset, not a demonstrated average 19-minute delay for every randomized patient. The authors' delay explanation is plausible interpretation, not randomized mediation evidence.
4. **The no-device-adverse-event statement is narrow.** Protocol pages 42–47 explicitly treat deaths and expected complications as trial outcomes rather than separately reportable SAE/SADE events. Six versus ten participants had recorded complications (eTable 6); these small counts, unequal early survival and selected event definitions cannot establish safety. Access injuries, ischemia, renal replacement and multiorgan failure are listed. Avoid a zero-complication or zero-harm claim.
5. **Population limits matter.** This was an emergency-department UK major-trauma-center trial, overwhelmingly blunt injury (97%), median ISS 41. Sixteen sites participated but only twelve recruited patients. Prehospital REBOA was excluded. It does not establish efficacy in elective or iatrogenic GU hemorrhage, all penetrating trauma, or a different high-volume/pre-positioned-access protocol. Standard care could include thoracotomy/open aortic occlusion; it was not one uniform comparator technique.
6. **Early stopping and uncertainty remain material.** The second interim review at 80 patients crossed the prespecified 90% posterior harm threshold (90.1%); ten more patients had accrued before recruitment stopped, so the final 86.9% estimate need not itself exceed that threshold. The small unblinded trial had baseline hemodynamic/head-injury imbalances. Adjusted analyses also favor harm but have wide credible intervals; adjustment does not remove all uncertainty.
7. **Do not turn historical protocol technique details into contemporary safety guarantees.** The old catheter illustrations and suggested zone-I/III occlusion durations describe the trial approach. The protocol itself says precise maximum safe durations cannot be given and labels its rough durations animal-derived. They are not validated safe ceilings or a substitute for current device instructions and specialist protocols.

## Internal source discrepancies and analysis limits

- The main narrative's approximately 23% receiving CPR on emergency-department arrival does not match its table: prehospital CPR is 10/43 and 11/44, whereas CPR on ED arrival is 4/40 and 4/43. Do not reproduce the narrative percentage as an ED-arrival rate.
- eAppendix 1 calls its specific analyses post hoc, while SAP page 14 anticipated a broad CACE analysis for unsuccessful arterial access (R2). Distinguish this broad prespecification from the published detailed Q1/Q2 analyses.
- **Visually confirmed, not an extraction error:** the Q2 CACE table (results PDF page 22) lists 36 compliers and 10 noncompliers despite the stated exclusion of technical access failures numbering eight in the main flow. Its 24-hour death cells 24 + 5 total 29 rather than the main trial's 17; its inpatient deaths 17 + 7 total 24 rather than 25. A control inpatient-death cell is blank in both Q1 and Q2 tables. No patient-level reconciliation was available. Do not repair these values by guess or use them for a definitive causal device-effect claim.
- The Q1 90-day counts among actual balloon recipients (13/19) and nonrecipients (12/27) are selected post-randomization groups. Their raw difference is not a randomized balloon effect. CACE estimates are highly imprecise (Q1 OR 4.25, credible interval 0.41–45.07) and require assumptions beyond random allocation.
- The learning analysis removes the first intervention patient at each recruiting site, leaving 34 versus 44. This post hoc asymmetric selection cannot establish that training or learning has no effect.
- SAP pages 8–10 disclose an original variance-input error: the stated probability of detecting a true survival OR 1.3 fell from 90% to 16% when corrected. The revision relaxed the final success criterion from 95% to 80% and explored informative priors. The displayed equation on page 9 still shows 0.95 despite the narrative and table specifying 0.80. The report's minimally informative analysis and harm stopping rule should not be conflated with unreported informative-prior sensitivity plans.
- The SAP is dated 30 March 2022, after recruitment ended 16 March 2022; do not call it a document finalized before enrollment. The paper says it preceded analysis, which was not independently audited here. Protocol/SAP survival-OR orientation is opposite to the main report's mortality OR; keep the direction explicit.
- The trial's main reference list and funding/conflict statements were read, but all cited antecedent studies were not independently retrieved as part of this bounded source task. No current guideline recommendation is established solely by this trial-source review.
