## Completed methods findings

- This is a German adaptation of EuroGuiDerm, accepted in June 2025 and subsequently published in abridged journal form in 2026. The German adaptation **did not update the systematic search or evidence report**. Authors could add newer references to background text. The underlying search was September 9, 2021, updating BAD 2018 with studies from 2017 onward. The cover dates are not an evidence-search cutoff.
- Figure 1 shows 951 deduplicated records, 362 full-text screenings, 62 eligible new reports, five new prospective comparative studies and nine carried forward from BAD, yielding 14 in the quantitative review. The separate descriptive analysis comprises 57 newer and 74 earlier reports. These are review-selection counts, not necessarily unique patient cohorts.
- RCTs were assessed with RoB 2; earlier BAD RCT assessments retained RoB 1. Clinical heterogeneity prevented pooling. No pooled meta-analysis was performed despite the generic methods description of planned fixed-effect analysis. The stated GRADE approach did not downgrade for inconsistency/publication bias when pooling was impossible; confidence must still be assessed per comparison.
- Surgery, emollients and pregnancy recommendations are consensus based because of insufficient comparative evidence. A strong consensus recommendation is not high-certainty randomized evidence. Recommendations were reconsidered in January/February 2025: 83 strong-consensus and nine consensus recommendations.
- The open category translated as “may be considered” explicitly means **no recommendation for or against**. It should not be presented as an established effective treatment.
- Changes during adaptation include stronger recommendations for circumcision after unsuccessful appropriate medical treatment and oral-mucosal urethroplasty for functionally consequential LS strictures; addition of meatoplasty; dilation restricted to palliation; and a pediatric vulvar surgical exception for functional impairment. These do not establish a universal operative recipe or comparative superiority.
- Adult female intralesional corticosteroid and genital UVA1 wording was changed to an open recommendation; childhood/genital treatment distinctions matter. PRP and laser wording must be read in full before describing them as guideline-endorsed standard care. The topical-steroid companion has now been reconciled in the thirteenth batch.
- External review justified avoiding pediatric genital UV therapy because direct LS evidence is lacking and topical corticosteroids are usually effective; evidence from other childhood dermatoses was not accepted as proof for LS.
- COI declarations, assessment and mitigation were read. Reported relevant conflicts were classified as low; affected participants were not to serve as sole leaders. This is the guideline's disclosure process, not a reason to invent either absence of conflicts or invalidity of its conclusions.

## Completed long-version findings

Pages 1–16 independently confirm the unchanged evidence report and allowance for newer background citations. The overview distinguishes women, girls, men, boys and extragenital disease, including off-label systemic therapy. Potent/ultrapotent corticosteroids remain first line; calcineurin inhibitors are a second-line/additional option when corticosteroids are contraindicated or insufficient. Female reconstruction follows persistent mechanical symptoms despite appropriate medical care. All detailed treatment, surgical, follow-up, patient-education and research sections have now been read. Recommendations must retain their population, anatomical and evidence-strength limits.

The PDF contains a minor validity-date discrepancy (cover June 15, 2030; page 8 June 14, 2030). Do not turn either date into a claim that evidence has been searched through 2030.

### Interpretation and source-error checks

- The long version offers no single universal steroid regimen. It distinguishes treatment of inflammatory activity from irreversible scarring and retains long-term care and surveillance. A three-week treatment pause discussed for diagnostic LS histology is not a reason to delay biopsy of a suspicious neoplastic lesion.
- PRP in adult genital LS, adult genital laser and PDT carry open recommendations, not proven efficacy. Childhood PRP/laser/genital UV/PDT are recommended against. Symptomatic improvement and histological inflammatory control must remain separate.
- The background laser discussion uses stronger language than its formal open recommendations; its statement that laser benefits GSM must be checked against current sham-controlled GSM evidence rather than imported as settled fact. The n=20 Salgado feasibility study's nonsignificant difference does not establish equivalence.
- Page 72 initially presents 20.5% versus 7.5% recurrence as though comparing meatotomy with meatoplasty, then correctly describes an LS versus non-LS comparison from Meeks. Verify the primary paper before using those numbers. The physical page was inspected, excluding an extraction artifact.
- Reference 172 on page 106 is an unrelated, explicitly retracted pumpkin-alkaloid paper (DOI 10.1111/jfbc.13839), cited in the outcomes discussion on page 29. It cannot support LS outcome measurement; the adjacent Simpson review/CORALS source is relevant. The physical reference page was inspected.
- Page 71 reports 276 of 287 as 92%, an arithmetic mismatch; do not reuse this as a verified circumcision estimate. Reference/case-series denominators need primary-source checks.
- Page 38 names mometasone 0.05% in the historical pediatric trial while the therapeutic discussion generally uses 0.1%; verify the original Kiss report before giving a trial-specific concentration.
- The surgery discussion retains highly selected historical age/caliber criteria and acknowledges case-selection differences between staged and single-stage urethroplasty. Do not present those historical thresholds as universal contraindications or a randomized procedure comparison.

### New high-impact evidence identified

Hecken et al., *Obstetrics & Gynecology*, published May 28, 2026, DOI [10.1097/AOG.0000000000006326](https://doi.org/10.1097/AOG.0000000000006326), PMID [42208069](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42208069/): complete indexed and publisher abstracts read. The publisher redirects to an Ovid **abstract** page; the full main, graphics and supplement have not been accessed. Search found no accessible author manuscript. The complete current [NCT05010421 registry record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05010421) was read via its public API.

- Open-label randomized trial, 245 women in the publication (123 laser/122 clobetasol), three-month BIP symptom endpoint. Median change was −8 versus −5; adjusted between-group coefficient −2.90 (95% CI −4.64 to −1.15). The abstract reports no serious treatment-related adverse events; detailed ascertainment and full event counts remain unverified.
- Subsequent crossover was chosen by participants and exploratory, not a randomized comparison of treatment sequences. No claim of histological remission, cancer prevention or durable steroid replacement is supported by the abstract.
- Registry states single center, no masking, exclusion of prior vulvar cancer/dysplasia/surgery, actual enrollment 246 and a 14-day laser interval. The published abstract reports 245 randomized and 21-day intervals. These discrepancies require the full report/protocol; do not silently harmonize them. The registry contains no posted results or downloadable protocol.
- PEARLS has a 2026 protocol publication and a July revision. It remains a maintenance-strategy trial to track, not completed outcome evidence. The complete 21-page version-2 protocol publication and its actual figures/reviewer responses are now read; separate operational documents remain pending.

## Completed evidence-report findings

- The report contains 14 prospective comparative trials (631 participants) and the earlier seven-trial Cochrane review (249 participants). These are not a contemporary 2026 search or a single pooled treatment estimate. Each displayed forest plot represents one study; the summary diamond is not proof of pooled independent replication.
- GRADE certainty varies by outcome, even within a trial. Most laser, PDT and regenerative comparisons have low or very low certainty. Short-term patient satisfaction, symptoms, clinical signs, histology, scarring and cancer prevention are different outcomes.
- The descriptive appendix includes abstracts, uncontrolled studies, mixed anatomical populations and repeated entries for combination therapies. Do not treat the number of table rows as unique trials or sum their participants as independent cohorts.
- Several quantitative displays are internally inconsistent. The Terras burning/pain plot on page 35 repeats the itching plot (−2.50), whereas pages 12/59 report −1.00. The Nd:YAG physician-score table on page 62 gives 36 patients and a confidence interval excluding zero, while the plot on page 46 has 31 patients and an interval crossing zero. The Nd:YAG satisfaction plot gives RR 2.73 (1.53–4.87), versus 2.79 (1.93–2.85) in pages 16/62. The zero-control-event row converts 8/20 versus 0/20 into a misleading zero absolute difference. These displays must not supply clinical effect estimates without original-study reconciliation.
- The male intraurethral-clobetasol series explicitly reports ongoing treatment in most patients. Avoiding another intervention during follow-up is not permanent cure or evidence that dilation reverses LS fibrosis.
