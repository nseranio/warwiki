## Completed methods findings

- This is a German adaptation of EuroGuiDerm, accepted in June 2025 and subsequently published in abridged journal form in 2026. The German adaptation **did not update the systematic search or evidence report**. Authors could add newer references to background text. The underlying search was September 9, 2021, updating BAD 2018 with studies from 2017 onward. The cover dates are not an evidence-search cutoff.
- Figure 1 shows 951 deduplicated records, 362 full-text screenings, 62 eligible new reports, five new prospective comparative studies and nine carried forward from BAD, yielding 14 in the quantitative review. The separate descriptive analysis comprises 57 newer and 74 earlier reports. These are review-selection counts, not necessarily unique patient cohorts.
- RCTs were assessed with RoB 2; earlier BAD RCT assessments retained RoB 1. Clinical heterogeneity prevented pooling. No pooled meta-analysis was performed despite the generic methods description of planned fixed-effect analysis. The stated GRADE approach did not downgrade for inconsistency/publication bias when pooling was impossible; confidence must still be assessed per comparison.
- Surgery, emollients and pregnancy recommendations are consensus based because of insufficient comparative evidence. A strong consensus recommendation is not high-certainty randomized evidence. Recommendations were reconsidered in January/February 2025: 83 strong-consensus and nine consensus recommendations.
- The open category translated as “may be considered” explicitly means **no recommendation for or against**. It should not be presented as an established effective treatment.
- Changes during adaptation include stronger recommendations for circumcision after unsuccessful appropriate medical treatment and oral-mucosal urethroplasty for functionally consequential LS strictures; addition of meatoplasty; dilation restricted to palliation; and a pediatric vulvar surgical exception for functional impairment. These do not establish a universal operative recipe or comparative superiority.
- Adult female intralesional corticosteroid and genital UVA1 wording was changed to an open recommendation; childhood/genital treatment distinctions matter. PRP and laser wording must be read in full before describing them as guideline-endorsed standard care. The topical-steroid companion remains queued for reconciliation.
- External review justified avoiding pediatric genital UV therapy because direct LS evidence is lacking and topical corticosteroids are usually effective; evidence from other childhood dermatoses was not accepted as proof for LS.
- COI declarations, assessment and mitigation were read. Reported relevant conflicts were classified as low; affected participants were not to serve as sole leaders. This is the guideline's disclosure process, not a reason to invent either absence of conflicts or invalidity of its conclusions.

## Long-version findings so far

Pages 1–16 independently confirm the unchanged evidence report and allowance for newer background citations. The overview distinguishes women, girls, men, boys and extragenital disease, including off-label systemic therapy. Potent/ultrapotent corticosteroids remain first line; calcineurin inhibitors are a second-line/additional option when corticosteroids are contraindicated or insufficient. Female reconstruction follows persistent mechanical symptoms despite appropriate medical care. Full recommendation qualifiers and evidence discussions remain to be read before updating companion pages.

The PDF contains a minor validity-date discrepancy (cover June 15, 2030; page 8 June 14, 2030). Do not turn either date into a claim that evidence has been searched through 2030.
