## Consequential application to WARWIKI

| Topic | Verified recommendation/context | Editorial implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scope | §1.6 explicitly excludes iatrogenic vascular injuries, while allowing application of relevant principles. | Label use in intraoperative GU vessel injury as extrapolation. Trauma-specific outcome rates and clocks are not direct GU evidence. |
| Hemorrhage and imaging | Recs 1–5, 9–11, 61 prioritize appropriate expertise, hemorrhage control/resuscitation and CTA when the patient can safely undergo it. Potential renal impairment alone should not delay life/limb-saving CTA. | Imaging must not delay control of unstable active bleeding. TXA statements apply to severe traumatic bleeding, with dosing/timing governed by the trauma protocol. |
| REBOA | Rec 8 is Class IIIb/B against routine use; the UK randomized strategy did not improve survival and raised concern for harm. | Do not present routine balloon occlusion as a proven survival intervention or delay definitive hemorrhage control to place it. |
| Retroperitoneal hematoma | Recs 62–63, Class I/C: instability of the patient **or** an expanding/pulsatile/actively bleeding hematoma favors exploration; a stable patient **and** stable hematoma permits leaving it unopened with subsequent CTA. | A rigid zone-only rule is insufficient. The narrative retains packing as temporary control and escalation when packing fails. |
| Exposure | §5.3 and actual Figure 4: right rotation accesses the IVC, aorta to the SMA base, iliac/right renal structures; left rotation accesses the celiac/mesenteric aorta and left renal structures. | Match exposure to the injured vessel and preserve the distinction between trauma mobilization and elective GU approaches. |
| Shunts | Recs 12–15 favor rapid restoration of flow, definitive repair when feasible and temporary shunting when physiology/injury prevents it. Conversion should occur as soon as possible, ideally in the initial operation. | The narrative's 12-hour practical target is not a demonstrated safe dwell-time guarantee. Routine postoperative heparin benefit is unproven. |
| Conduits | Recs 16–18 prefer short interposition when appropriate; synthetic repair is an option and vein is favored for longer/small-caliber reconstructions. Rec 66 permits synthetic emergency aortic reconstruction despite concomitant bowel injury. | No randomized comparison establishes a universal best conduit. Emergency feasibility is not proof that contamination and coverage are irrelevant. |
| Iliac arteries | Recs 69–71 support primary/interposition repair or shunting and selected endovascular treatment; internal-iliac branch bleeding can be embolized. | Preserve common/external iliac limb flow. Bilateral internal-iliac sacrifice carries pelvic/bowel ischemia risk and is rarely required. |
| Major veins | Recs 79–81 and §5.8 allow ligation when repair is not readily achievable, selected observation for stable contained IVC injury, and reject atriocaval shunting. | Ligation is a damage-control option, not consequence-free. Left renal venous collateral drainage differs from right renal venous drainage; anatomy and remaining collaterals matter. |
| Ischemia | Rec 85 seeks extremity revascularization immediately, ideally within one hour of admission. The text explicitly rejects a safe ischemic waiting window. | Do not teach six hours as permission to wait. Acute compartment syndrome needs urgent treatment; routine prophylactic fasciotomy remains a separate, unsettled question. |
| Antithrombotics and surveillance | Recs 94 and 98 are individualized/optional; the text warns against automatically applying atherosclerotic-disease regimens. Rec 97 permits clinical/DUS follow-up at one month or earlier for symptoms. | No universal therapeutic heparin, aspirin duration or surveillance schedule follows for every repaired GU vessel. |

Most relevant abdominal/exposure recommendations are Level C; a strong recommendation class does not turn its evidence into a randomized trial. The literature search was updated in February 2024, with selected important later publications included. The document itself identifies renal salvage, mesenteric injury, conduit selection, surveillance and antithrombotics as unresolved areas.

## Source interpretation cautions

- The ESVS arterial grades are **1, 2, 3 and X**. They are not interchangeable with SVS thoracic grades: ESVS 1 combines SVS I/II, ESVS 2 corresponds to SVS III and ESVS 3 to SVS IV. Actual Figures 1–2 and Table 5 were checked.
- Renal recommendations 74–78 distinguish a devascularized unilateral kidney detected after trauma from bilateral injury or a solitary salvageable kidney. The narrative's absolute warm-ischemia wording must not become a rule to abandon a newly recognized, potentially salvageable intraoperative renal arterial injury.
- Table 11's abbreviated SMA-occlusion cell begins with nonoperative management but specifically retains restoration for salvageable ischemic bowel/inadequate collaterals; rec 72 also permits endovascular reperfusion. Do not detach the first word from that qualification or from the time-critical perfusion principles.
- Internal bibliography signals require caution before copying numerical claims: reference 162 is titled as an **MRA** diagnostic-accuracy review although used in CTA boxes; reference 450 duplicates a MESS/amputation paper despite the nearby heparinization narrative; reference 393 is titled a **systematic review**, while the narrative describes its 35 patients as a series. These are source-identity concerns observed within the full guideline, not a claim that the underlying studies have been independently adjudicated here. The formal recommendations should not be replaced with guessed references.
- Section 1.3 says single case reports/animal studies were excluded, while later bibliography/narrative includes such evidence. This reinforces the need to retain each recommendation's actual grade and study limitations rather than represent every cited item as high-level evidence.

Main-document, table and actual-figure reading is complete. Supplement access and independent underlying-study verification remain explicitly incomplete. The root reviewer retains responsibility for the vascular/exposure page revisions and their final companion checks.
