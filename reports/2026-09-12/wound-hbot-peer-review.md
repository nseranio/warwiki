# Independent wound-healing / HBOT peer review

September 12, 2026. Both revised MDX files were read completely, including tables, cautions and references. No shared source-page edits were made by this reviewer.

## Result

No blocking introduced clinical-inference error was found in the HBOT rewrite. It appropriately separates the randomized RICH-ART urinary-symptom endpoint from the uncontrolled five-year follow-up, distinguishes HOT2 from HORTIS, and treats Toppen's NSTI finding as an observational odds ratio rather than a causal Fournier mortality reduction. The hypospadias graft-area contraction, fistula type, IC/BPS and ED endpoint distinctions agree with the checked primary records.

One retained wound-healing error was flagged to the author at line110: the infection row says infection “reduces tissue oxygen tension and pH.” A universal fall in pH is unsupported. The cited Guo infection section instead supports prolonged inflammation and protease-mediated matrix damage. Suggested replacement: **“Prolongs inflammation and can increase protease-mediated matrix damage, impairing repair.”** This can use the existing Guo reference; no additional small-study clinical recommendation is needed. The primary prospective human pH study [PMID41896289](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41896289/) was read as its complete indexed abstract; it describes higher pH in chronic/nonhealing wounds and an association with infection, not a general infection-induced fall in pH. Its results were used to challenge the blanket statement, not to recommend pH-directed treatment.

The wound draft's overlapping phases, skin-specific strength limit, absence of a universal repair calendar, macrophage-model qualification and procedure-specific protection advice have no blocking issue within this scoped check.
