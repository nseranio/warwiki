## Main interpretation safeguards

- Many analysis cells show `0.0 [0.0, 0.0]` while the parent row says “Totals not selected”, with empty sample-size cells. These are unavailable summary displays, **not evidence of zero risk, zero events, equivalence or perfect precision**. Some zero-study rows also have the same display. Do not use them as effects.
- “Traditional sling” is materially heterogeneous here. Explicit examples include autologous rectus fascia, cadaveric fascia lata, porcine dermis/Pelvicol, lyophilised dura mater, Goretex/Teflon, vaginal skin, and an antimicrobial PTFE patch. These are not interchangeable evidence for modern autologous fascial slings. The material-comparison tables also contain Fortaperm and Vypro/Ultrapro/Prolene variants.
- Cure, failure, improvement and satisfaction are not interchangeable. Several trials use composite subjective/objective definitions, some substitute quality-of-life improvement or pad counts, and the review occasionally assumes the outcome type. Avoid presenting one universal “cure rate”.
- Outcomes use different denominators and follow-up windows; never divide every event by total randomized or add sample sizes across related analyses as independent patients.

## Numerical cross-checks that matter

**SISTEr:** the table's 2-year composite symptom/diary/retreatment failures are 101/265 sling and 130/255 Burch; objective pad-test failures 37/265 and 38/255. Safety uses randomized denominators: any complication 206/326 vs 156/329; any voiding dysfunction 46/326 vs 7/329; surgical revision for voiding dysfunction 20/326 vs 0/329. These distinguish patient-reported/composite continence from objective pad testing and urinary morbidity. Five-year follow-up enrolled 482 (243/239), but completed visits were 183/174; self-report, composite, retreatment and satisfaction each use different observed denominators. The review explicitly assumes some denominators from subjective responders.

**Guerrero:** 12-month incontinence 35/67 autologous, 36/46 Pelvicol, 31/69 TVT; not improved 7/67, 18/46, 5/69; reoperation 0/67, 9/46, 0/69. At 10 years incontinence 30/61, 32/38, 43/63; not improved 15/61, 16/38, 17/63; satisfaction 43/61, 20/38, 44/63. Reported reoperation 0/61, 5/38, 2/63 at 10 years is lower for Pelvicol than its earlier 9/46; **cannot interpret that as a cumulative reduction** without the original follow-up definition and ascertainment. Attrition and early arm termination matter.

Analysis table 15 (traditional sling vs open retropubic colposuspension) reports different effects by endpoint: women's incontinence at 1–5 years RR 0.73 (0.61–0.89), 4 studies/687 women; objective incontinence at 1–5 years RR 0.90 (0.60–1.34), 2/592; voiding dysfunction after 3 months RR 6.08 (3.10–11.95), 5/853. Those are **different subsets/endpoints**, and mixed historical sling materials preclude treating them as personalized contemporary autologous-fascia risks.

Analysis table 16 (traditional vs midurethral sling/tape) reports women's incontinence within 1 year RR 1.04 (0.85–1.28), 11/841, and 1–5 years RR 1.29 (0.98–1.68), 6/458; the intervals are not proof of equivalence. Voiding dysfunction RR 1.34 (0.85–2.12), 8/629; urodynamic de novo DO RR 2.61 (1.17–5.84), 4/325; urge symptoms RR 1.62 (0.66–3.99), 5/348. Do not merge urodynamic DO and symptoms into a single proven adverse effect.

## Source inconsistencies / points needing original-report follow-through

1. Guerrero characteristics explicitly list 12-month dropouts 12/79, 4/50, 3/72 and say unexplained differential loss from the autologous arm, but the bias table says “No differential dropout” and low attrition risk. The stated 85% follow-up also differs from the shown outcome denominators 182/201 (90.5%). Record the discrepancy rather than silently adopting the low-risk label.
2. Albo's attrition justification says 243/239 women “followed up” at 5 years while Outcomes distinguishes these enrollment counts from 183/174 completed visits. Its low attrition judgment does not remove the visible missing-data problem; recruiting extra women for power is not by itself proof of unbiased loss.
3. Helmy's abstract data (482 women, adverse-event counts 22/23 and approximately 83%/73% satisfaction) conspicuously match numbers in the SISTEr extended follow-up table. **Possible overlap/duplicate-reporting signal only**; original abstract/authorship must be checked before concluding identity. Cochrane extracts no usable data from Helmy, so do not add its 482 to efficacy totals.
4. Enzelsberger states 5/36 versus 1/36 voiding difficulty and 1/36 versus 5/36 prolapse outcomes were both statistically significant; no test or P value is supplied in this table. Small sparse counts warrant original-report confirmation rather than repeating that significance claim.
5. Comparison 18 outcome 6 is headed “CURE” but says “number of women with urinary incontinence >1 year”; the label is internally contradictory. Do not reverse event direction from this display alone.
6. Choe's arm totals are 20 each but transient de novo urgency uses 8/7 denominators (apparently women without baseline urgency). This is another reason to preserve risk-set denominators and not substitute 20.
