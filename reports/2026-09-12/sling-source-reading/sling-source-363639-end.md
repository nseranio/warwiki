## Findings useful for WARWIKI

### Publication date and incorporated evidence

The review explicitly calls this the **2020 update**, but its awaiting-assessment notes repeatedly state that studies found in the **January 23, 2019 search have not been fully incorporated**. Do not describe it as fully incorporating all January 2019 evidence or as current 2026 evidence.

The unresolved records are Abou Hashem 2017 (appears identical to the already included Abouhashem 2014 conference abstract), Hassan 2018 (registry, NCT03473041), Kajbafzadeh 2017 (IRCT201611278554N3, acellular skin versus synthetic mesh), and **Sharifiaghdas 2017 (10-year follow-up of the included 2008 trial, explicitly not added)**. Hilton 2000 and Zhu 2014 remain in the review's historical ongoing-study list; this does not establish that either is still ongoing in 2026. Zhu is Regen **PVDF**, compared with TVT-O, rather than a polypropylene-versus-autologous trial.

The other published review versions listed are 1999, 2000, 2001, 2005 (pub2), 2011 (pub3), and 2017 (pub4). A bibliography with 2017 companion Cochrane reviews and NICE CG171 (2013) should not be exported as present-day guideline currency.

### Material identities and limits of generalization

- The tables and reference titles mix **autologous rectus fascia, autologous fascia lata, autologous dermal patch, vaginal-wall tissue, cadaveric fascia lata, porcine collagen/dermis, PTFE and different synthetic sling constructs**. An overall traditional-sling result cannot be represented as a result for autologous fascia alone.
- **Lucas 2000** is long versus short autologous fascial sling (material confirmed by its linked publication title in the review reference list); six-year follow-up and historical hospital stays do not establish modern recovery rules.
- **Pacetta 2005** compares autologous fascia with **FortaPerm**, described as laminated purified porcine collagen matrix. It is an abstract-only trial in this review, with 34 randomized, and should not support broad allograft/autograft equivalence.
- **Sand 2000** uses a **PTFE sling**, not an autologous sling. Its favorable long-term objective results involve 13 sling and 15 Burch participants, with subjective and objective definitions differing.
- **Shin 2001** compares **autologous dermal graft patch with cadaveric fascia lata**, not autologous rectus fascia with polypropylene; abstract-only provenance and conflicting denominators prevent confident numerical reuse.
- **Silva Filho 2006** compares rectus-fascia pubovaginal sling with **SAFYRE TOT**, described as a polypropylene central mesh and silicone anchoring columns. Findings are not interchangeable with every contemporary transobturator sling.
- **Okulu 2013** compares tailored broad-based double-forced slings fixed to rectus fascia: Vypro, Ultrapro and Prolene light. The review describes differing absorbability/filament construction. This does not establish superiority of one polymer across all sling devices; verify actual manufacturer construction before repeating the review's compressed material description.
- **Teleb 2011** compares anterior rectus sheath, tailored Prolene strip, and an anterior vaginal-wall patch, using the same retropubic midurethral placement. Its 32 participants and 11 with 36-month follow-up should not become a durable material-equivalence claim.
- **Zargham 2013** involves combined anterior prolapse/incontinence procedures, with vaginal-wall wrap versus a synthetic sling/mesh construct. It is not an isolated material-only randomized comparison, and its allocation is explicitly quasi-random by file number.
- **Teixeira 2008** evaluates inflammatory markers and 90-day biopsy findings, with no review-relevant outcome measure; it cannot establish continence success or comparative patient safety.

### Internal discrepancies and extraction cautions

These are inconsistencies in the supplied review text, not resolved errors in the original trials. Verify original reports before reusing affected numbers; do not guess corrections.

1. **Kondo 2006:** comparison is PVS versus TVT, but the attrition-bias note calls the comparator the “Burch arm.” Birth-date allocation is high risk. Women having concomitant/revision surgery were excluded from the six-parameter analysis, reducing groups from 29/31 to 21/23; different outcome denominators cannot be treated as one clean intention-to-treat sample.
2. **Maher 2005:** table gives normal-activity time in **weeks as 4 versus 28**, alongside ranges 0–42 and 0–35, and hospital stay 4 days with range **3–81**. These are suspicious unit/transcription presentations; no numerical correction inferred. Six-month “patient satisfaction” wording also requires original-source validation. Five-year analyzed samples shrink to 13 and 14, versus 22/23 randomized.
3. **Sharifiaghdas 2008:** 39/100 participants lost by one year, including 11 dissatisfied with surgery, yet attrition judged low risk because group losses were similar. That label does not remove informative attrition concerns. The 2017 ten-year report was not incorporated.
4. **Sharifiaghdas 2015:** participants say 72, arms/outcomes say 35+35; no-loss note does not reconcile the difference. “Cure defined as woman report of some degree of SUI” appears inverted. Adverse-event percentage summary does not map cleanly to the detailed 8/35 versus 1/35 hematoma/infection row. Do not reproduce as a verified definition or risk estimate.
5. **Shin 2001:** 30/33 and 22/24 are labeled 91.6% and 93.2%, respectively; arithmetic differs. Comparator denominators change to 27 for improvement and 20 for de novo instability, despite 24 allocated. These discrepancies remain unresolved.
6. **Tcherniakovsky 2009:** arms are 20 and 21 but retention row uses 2/21 and 3/20, reversing denominators. Avoid quoting that row without the original trial.
7. **Wadie 2005:** 63 randomized, 12 lost, yet early intervention/outcome denominators are 25+28=53; later UDI/IIQ denominators become 39+24. Review itself warns that denominators differ. Sexual-function data are withheld by reviewers because the instrument was uncertain. The differential-dropout judgment is high risk at two years.
8. **Zargham 2013:** intervention allocation 26/30 changes to 25/25 outcome denominators. Attrition note calls actual loss 4/5, inconsistent with arithmetic from those allocations; “28 women (56%)” also uses an implied denominator of 50 rather than 56 recruited. Abstract, subjective graph and objective graph cure estimates differ. Material and concomitant-procedure descriptions are complex enough to require the original report before simplified comparisons.
9. **Excluded Kuo 2001:** reason merely states rectus fascia versus polypropylene mesh, which by itself does not explain exclusion from a traditional-sling review. Do not infer a methodological reason absent from this text.
10. Abouhashem/Abou Hashem and Arunkalaivanan/Aurunkalaivanan/Barrington records include suspected duplicate publications; do not count report-level references as independent trials. Multiple SISTEr reports, the Guerrero/Khan publications and abstract reports are similarly grouped under one study.

### Methods changes and disclosures

The 2020 changes add the mini-sling comparison, planned primary/recurrent-SUI and prolapse subgroups, redefined primary dry/continence and repeat-surgery outcomes, woman-reported cure, GRADE and an economic commentary. Presence of a prespecified subgroup does not prove adequate subgroup evidence.

Authors report no known competing interests (LS, HR, MIO, JDC, PA, CG). No internal funding supplied; NIHR support is declared, with funder-view disclaimer. The update status says new search/content updated **without change to conclusions**. No new conclusion should be invented from that status label.
