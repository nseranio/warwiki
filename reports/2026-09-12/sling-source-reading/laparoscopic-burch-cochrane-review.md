## Findings relevant to WARWIKI

- Search date May22,2019; 26 trials/2,271 participants in review, many small and seven available only as abstracts. Do not label this current-to-2026 trial evidence.
- The clinically useful short-term comparison is **suture-based** laparoscopic versus open colposuspension, subjective cure within18months: RR1.04 (0.99–1.08), six trials,755 participants. Actual forest lap5 confirms totals380/375 and cure312/296. The high-certainty label concerns this specific endpoint, not every complication, long-term outcome or robotic approach.
- Mesh/staples laparoscopic techniques are a different intervention: lap5 subjective-cure RR0.75 (0.61–0.93), totals203/159. A pooled lap-versus-open number blends those operations with suture-only Burch. Do not describe all laparoscopic colposuspension trials as mesh-free.
- Actual objective-cure forest lap22 shows pooled lap-versus-MUS RR0.88 (0.81–0.95), favouring MUS; suture subgroup RR0.93 (0.86–1.01), mesh subgroup RR0.66 (0.51–0.86). The discussion sentence saying objective cure favours laparoscopic colposuspension reverses the result and must not be repeated.
- Lap-versus-open actual short-term bladder-perforation forest lap16 is RR1.72 (0.90–3.29),36/688 versus11/623; suture subgroup RR2.15 (0.97–4.77). Uncertainty does not establish equal injury risk or absence of injury. Perioperative-complication forest counts **events**, not necessarily unique women, and cannot become a patient-specific complication percentage without original data.
- Trial outcome definitions vary: satisfaction, improvement and dryness are not interchangeable. Several trials exclude ISD or substantial voiding dysfunction; the review does not justify recommending Burch to a fixed, poorly functioning urethra.
- Long-term lap-versus-open objective data are very small and heterogeneous: lap9 RR0.89 (0.28–2.80),53/54 total, I²92%. They cannot support a universal 5–14year cure promise.
- Operative-time, length-of-stay and return-to-activity comparisons largely reflect historical hospital pathways and heterogeneous surgery; they should not be prescribed as modern recovery schedules.

## Important internal source discrepancies — do not silently reproduce

These are observations about the review's own text/tables/figures. They are not allegations about original trial reports, which were not independently read here.

1. **One versus two sutures Summary of Findings arm reversal.** The summary table labelled one-versus-two places the RR1.37 benefit under one suture, but the main gives72/81 cured with two sutures and50/77 with one; the ratio is two/one. Similarly3/81 versus1/77 voiding difficulty underlies RR2.82; directions must be checked against original allocation before quoting. The one-versus-two comparison has no additional actual figure in the33-figure inventory; its in-article tables were fully read.
2. **Objective-cure discussion reversal.** Actual lap22 and results favour MUS for the pooled endpoint; the discussion incorrectly describes a lap advantage.
3. **MUS bladder-perforation reporting conflict.** The review says no included MUS-comparison trial reported bladder perforations, while its own characteristics describe bladder injuries/suture or needle perforations in Adile, Foote, Paraiso, Ustun and Valpas. An unpooled endpoint is not evidence of zero harm.
4. **Comparator labels.** Actual lap19 and lap22 subgroup headings say sutures versusTVT but contain Foote's SPARC comparison; lap21 separately labels Foote as SPARC. A results paragraph also calls the mesh-lap-versusTVT comparator “open colposuspension.”
5. **Foote denominators need original-source resolution.** Characteristics describe80 randomized (40/40); actual lap21 uses43/44 for incontinence episodes and lap24 uses48/49 for return to activities. Follow-up reports or expanded recruitment may explain this, but the extracted review does not reconcile it. Do not mix these denominators or call it confirmed primary-data error.
6. **Fixed versus random model.** Actual lap29 suture-versus-mesh subjective cure displays a fixed-effect RR1.28 (1.11–1.47), I²73%; the main/SoF uses random-effects RR1.24 (0.96–1.59). Preserve the model and uncertainty, not just the significant estimate.
7. **Quality-of-life direction mismatch.** The extracted footnote broadly says lower scores are better; EQ-5D health utility is higher-is-better, while symptom distress/impact is lower-is-better. Actual lap31 combines EQ-5D and SUDI/SIIQ with a shared direction; interpretation requires verifying any reversal/sign handling in primary data. Do not copy a pooled benefit from this plot without that check.
8. **Plain summary follow-up.** It says all studies followed patients at least18months, whereas the main/study tables include6- and12month outcomes. Elsewhere open-comparison sample totals differ (1,304 abstract versus1,396 main).
9. **Timing label.** A SoF repeat-surgery row is headed1year but its comment reports a5year3/30 versus0/30 comparison.
10. **Risk-of-bias narrative inconsistencies.** Some study characteristics/blinding details conflict with summary labels or narrative lists (e.g., Ustun included in both low and high reporting-bias descriptions). Actual risk matrix has Ustun high selective-reporting risk. Do not equate every green marker with a fully verified original trial.
11. **Retracted study.** The review explicitly excludes Prezioso2013 as retracted and cites the2016 retraction. Veit-Rubin2019 still cites the original in its bibliography. Do not carry an outcome estimate across that citation without checking its provenance.
