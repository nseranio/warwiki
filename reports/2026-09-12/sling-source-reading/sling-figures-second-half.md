## Findings affecting interpretation on fascia pages

1. **Apparent reversed Shin 2001 arms in analysis18.6 (ordinal188).** The subgroup label reads autologous dermal patch versus cadaveric fascia lata, but cure is plotted **19/24 versus25/33**, OR1.22 (0.34–4.32). Analysis18.3 (ordinal185) plots failure in that same labeled arm order as **8/33 versus5/24**. Their cure complements are therefore **25/33 versus19/24**, the reverse of the actual cure plot. This reverses the point-estimate direction; do not infer a material advantage without the original trial's arm labels. Additionally the figure literally combines the heading CURE with wording about women *with incontinence*, an internally contradictory event label.

2. **Catheter-removal estimate dominated by historical dura mater, not autologous fascia.** Analysis15.13 (ordinal133) reports +8.01 days, but **99.3% weight** comes from Enzelsberger's lyophilised dura-mater sling (15 vs7 days), compared with modified Burch. Only0.7% comes from Sand. It would be inappropriate to use this pooled number as a modern autologous-fascial-sling recovery estimate.

3. **“Wound pain at6 months” is not a uniform six-month pain endpoint.** Analysis16.25 (ordinal170) plots Guerrero **2/61 versus0/63**, which the study-characteristics table identifies as **10-year scar pain**. It also plots Al-Azzawi **8/40 versus0/40**, described in that table as a composite of wound pain/ooze/hematoma/infection. Wade7/25 versus2/28 provides65.5% weight. The pooled RR6.40 (1.94–21.12) must not be presented as an isolated contemporary six-month chronic-pain risk.

4. **The urodynamic DO signal is driven by cadaveric fascia.** In analysis16.23 (ordinal168), Basok's **cadaveric fascia lata** versus intravaginal slingplasty (15/67 versus5/72) provides **65.9% weight**. Al-Azzawi's autologous rectus fascia versus TOT has2/40 versus2/40. Thus the overall RR2.61 (1.17–5.84) does not establish that modern autologous fascia uniformly produces excess de novo urgency. Analysis16.22 (ordinal167) assesses **urge symptoms separately**, RR1.62 (0.66–3.99).

5. **The >5-year “clinician” label contains a composite endpoint.** Analysis15.11 (ordinal131) uses Albo153/221 versus161/212, contributing98.6% weight. The study table describes these as the self-report/diary/retreatment **composite failures**, not isolated pad-test failure. Do not call RR0.90 (0.80–1.01) an exclusively objective-pad-test result.

6. **Follow-up windows are mixed in broad “>1-year cure” analyses.** Analysis16.7 (ordinal152) includes Guerrero31/61 versus20/63, corresponding to ten-year cure, alongside shorter trial follow-up. Its overall OR1.06 (0.65–1.72) is not a uniform fixed-year cure effect. The separate Guerrero >5-year incontinence result in16.5 (ordinal150) is RR0.72 (0.53–0.98), while satisfaction is a distinct endpoint in16.9 (ordinal154).

7. **Strong heterogeneity undermines an exact generic time estimate.** Analysis16.13 (ordinal158) reports a fixed-effect operative-time difference +57.08 minutes despite **I²98%** and opposite direction in Zargham (-14 minutes). Hospital stay16.14 (ordinal159) has **I²88%**. Historical operations, materials and settings differ. They support discussing procedural burden, not promising a precise extra hour or stay duration for today's patient.

8. **Safety categories require their original definitions.** Analysis15.16 (ordinal135), titled perioperative surgical complications, uses Albo42/326 versus32/329: these are the study table's **serious-adverse-event counts**, not its any-complication206/326 versus156/329. The definitions and timeframes should accompany a comparative-harm statement. Analysis18.13 (ordinal195) also uses Barbalias0/32 versus5/16 under perioperative complications, whereas the characteristics list the five Goretex events as two erosions and three recurrent UTIs without timing; do not assume these are all intraoperative or immediate postoperative events.

9. **An apparent urgency-figure mismatch was checked and ruled out.** Ordinals100 (10.6) and179 (17.6) both visibly show Sharifiaghdas2015 **5/35 versus1/35**, RR5.00 (0.62–40.64). The adjacent bladder-perforation figure178 (17.5) shows1/35 versus0/35, RR3.00 (0.13–71.22). Do not log a discrepancy between the two urgency figures; it was an initial adjacent-image reading concern, resolved by viewing both actual assets together.

## Other visual interpretation checks

- Blank/non-estimable HTML table summaries are not zero-effect findings: the actual images contain nonzero single-study effects and wide CIs. For example11.17 (ordinal104) vaginal bleeding1/20 versus0/20 givesRR3.00 (0.13–69.52), and11.22 (ordinal106) sling release2/61 versus1/38 givesRR1.25 (0.12–13.28).
- Cure forests use **odds ratios** and reverse the favored-direction labels relative to incontinence/failure risk-ratio forests. Read effect measure and event direction anew for each graph.
- Analysis15.3 (ordinal126) is92.5% weighted by SISTEr, and15.5 (ordinal127) is99.4% weighted by its extension. The many smaller historical trials do not make these many independent large confirmations.
- Analysis16.8 (ordinal153) has no repeat operations in either arm at the plotted early assessment and is **not estimable**. It does not establish absence of later retreatment; longer-term Guerrero outcomes exist separately.
- Comparison17 (ordinals174–182) comes from one70-woman Sharifiaghdas2015 comparison. Wide intervals and a historical particular mini-sling/material cannot be treated as the modern large SIMS evidence base or class equivalence.
- Comparison18 keeps material/technique alternatives as separate, predominantly tiny single-study contrasts. There is no defensible pooled “all materials equally safe/effective” result. Several groups reuse the same multiarm participants across pairwise comparisons.
- Analysis13.6 (ordinal114), a sling-versus-injectable comparison, labels its second column “Minimally Invasive Sling”. This appears to be a column-header carryover; the assigned trial/comparison must govern material interpretation.
- Analysis14.4 (ordinal120) labels hospital stay “hours” for20 versus7 (MD13). The study-characteristics table gives20 and7 without a unit. This alone does not resolve the unit; do not turn the displayed number into a contemporary stay recommendation without the original Hilton report.
