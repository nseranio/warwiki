## Version and applicability limits

The final incorporated clinical search was **27 February 2017**. A **23 January 2019** search was not fully incorporated: four eligible reports were placed in awaiting-classification, including a separate Sharifiaghdas 2008 trial's 2017 ten-year update. Thus “2020 review” must not be described as a search current through 2020 or 2019. Economics searched August 2018.

There are **34 randomized/quasi-randomized trials, 3,244 women**, samples 20–655; three trials were quasi-randomized. Fifteen sling materials were represented: autologous tissues, cadaveric/xenograft materials, and historic synthetic materials. This is **not an autologous-rectus-fascia-only meta-analysis**. Most trials did not separate primary from recurrent SUI, or outcomes by concomitant prolapse; ten included some stress-predominant MUI. Definitions of cure varied substantially; the primary pooled outcome accepts patient report, clinician observation, quantitative or composite definitions. Only 14 trials used women's report of cure/incontinence. Long-term data were sparse.

Seven trials clearly concealed allocation; blinding was generally absent and no third-party outcome assessment was reported. Selective reporting was not formally assessed. An RCT-only review does not characterize all uncommon or delayed device harms.

## Errors, contradictions or misleading passages to keep out of WARWIKI

1. **Certainty mismatch:** medium-term traditional vs colposuspension dryness is moderate in abstract main results, body and SOF7, but low in abstract conclusion. Perioperative complications vs colposuspension are very low in abstract/SOF7 but low in the detailed narrative; SOF7 footnote describes only two downgrades. IIQ row says “low” while its symbol and later narrative say very low and footnote describes four downgrades. Preserve a note internally; do not invent a reconciled GRADE.
2. **Denominators:** medium-term traditional-vs-MUS n458 vs SOF445; Macroplastique late dryness n27 vs SOF43; Maher table even describes 45 women but lists21+22. Exact outcome/available-case denominators matter.
3. **Follow-up:** Guerrero late traditional-vs-MUS continence says10y in abstract vs5y in body. Sharifiaghdas's distinct ten-year update is explicitly awaiting incorporation; do not mix these two trials.
4. **Obvious numeric errors:** SOF10 describes IIQ as “50.2 higher” with CI2.23–12.77; later detailed table gives correct arithmetic **MD7.50**, with mean control42.7 (50.2 likely treatment mean). Mini-sling satisfaction table says10/35 vs7/35 satisfied but RR0.89, mathematically incompatible as stated. Macroplastique clinician-observed incontinence4/21 vs20/22 has RR0.21 with CI0.09–0.21, a suspicious upper bound; requires actual source/analysis. SOF6 complications excess is labelled “56 fewer” though lower relative CI implies56 more, and upper absolute interval exceeds possible event counts. These should not be reproduced.
5. **Source-attribution slips:** the Sharifiaghdas2015 mini-sling comparison cites Sharifiaghdas2008 for several harms despite being a different study. Do not carry these attribution errors.
6. **Historic background is not current clinical guidance:** UUI is described as necessarily caused by forceful bladder contraction; UUI symptoms and demonstrable DO are not interchangeable. The text frames USI as requiring exclusion of DO, even while admitting mixed cases. Pharmacology mentions estrogens/alpha-adrenergic agents as principal SUI drug treatment; do not transfer this old generic framing into current practice recommendations. The materials description initially includes lyodura among synthetics, then correctly treats lyophilized dura as other biological tissue.
7. Hospital stay/catheter duration data derive from small old studies and local policy; the review itself warns about hospital-policy confounding. The phrase women “used a sling for eight days longer” is evidently a catheter-wording error. Not an operative catheter protocol.

## Appropriate editorial use

Use this as an important, dated synthesis supporting the tradeoff of better continence/reintervention outcomes versus voiding dysfunction/UTI for traditional slings against open colposuspension, and uncertain comparative efficacy against MUS. Pair with current guidelines and original pivotal trials for present-day counseling. Do not use it to justify material-specific tensile strength, safe generic clamp placement/pressure, instrument size, suture tension, or nerve/vascular handling claims: it addresses operations, not those instrument mechanics.
