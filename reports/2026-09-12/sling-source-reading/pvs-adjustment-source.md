## Findings for the PVS rewrite

This single-centre trial randomized53 women;52 completed12weeks. It compared3cm versus6cm knot-to-aponeurosis height for a **midurethral** rectus-fascial graft (1.5×8cm), using a no.17 cystoscope sheath during tying. Prior anti-incontinence surgery and baseline retention/BOO were excluded;75% had mixed symptoms. Power assumed33% versus0% retention.

At2weeks retention was9/27 versus5/25; difference13.3percentage points,95%CI−10.8to35.2,P=.28. Five later underwent sling division. The trial found no optimal adjustment interval;12-week PVR was lower with6cm, of uncertain clinical importance.

This does **not** validate a particular clamp under the urethra, fingerbreadth/spacer rule, or universally safe “loose” tension. It does not establish equivalence, long-term durability, or applicability to recurrent/complex PVS patients. The small study belongs in a narrowly framed technical-evidence note, not as a major practice-changing trial.

## Reviewer cautions and source discrepancies

These are audit observations, not corrected patient-level results:

- The stated effect and confidence interval leave meaningful benefit/harm unresolved; lack of statistical significance cannot establish equal safety. The unusually large planning effect limits sensitivity to more plausible differences.
- The12-week endpoint follows sling divisions; its favorable later retention counts should not be described as spontaneous resolution from the original adjustment alone.
- The paper's “double-blind” description explains assessor/analyst blinding; do not expand that automatically to every participant and operative team.
- Excluding incomplete follow-up after randomization needs to be disclosed when characterizing the analysis; this is not a demonstrated strict intention-to-treat analysis.
- Methods describe Cochran's Q for a between-group primary comparison and Wilcoxon signed-rank for between-group score comparisons. These are reporting concerns requiring statistical clarification; the description alone cannot prove exactly how analyses were implemented.
- Baseline recurrent-UTI prose and Table1 disagree (61.5% versus20/52,38.5%). Avoid reusing that incidental figure.
- Table2 distinguishes one12-week case by elevated PVR whereas prose calls both catheter dependent. Do not manufacture a resolution to this discrepancy.
- “De novo urgency” includes worsening storage symptoms/medication use in an already predominantly mixed-symptom cohort. That label should not be taken as a precise incidence among previously urgency-free women.
- Several reported differences omit the direction's sign. Preserve group counts rather than transplanting ambiguous signed differences.
- The paper's exact postoperative timing and BOO thresholds describe its protocol; they are not universal clinical-management requirements. The cited management guidelines/trials need their own independent review before adoption.

## Suggested restrained editorial conclusion

Adjustment should follow a described operative technique and individual anatomy; no universal spacer or knot distance has been established by this small short-term trial. Emphasize avoiding urethral obstruction, documenting the method, planning postoperative emptying assessment, and counseling about catheterization or revision. Keep a plain statement that optimal fascial-sling tension remains incompletely standardized, rather than converting one measured gap into a mandatory rule.
