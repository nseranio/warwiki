## Recommended edits

1. **Add one Cochrane evidence-table row to rectus fascia**, using the completed parent source read before final integration: 2020 synthesis, 34 trials/3,244 women, evidence search fully incorporated only through February 2017. Traditional sling materials were heterogeneous. The broad synthesis favors traditional slings over open colposuspension for some continence outcomes, with increased voiding morbidity; it does not establish that autologous fascia is universally more effective than MUS or that rectus and thigh fascia are equivalent. Keep original SISTEr outcomes as the more interpretable autologous-rectus comparison. Avoid disputed denominator/follow-up details documented in the earlier report.
2. **Make the urethral-surgery distinction explicit** in the rectus page. Suggested replacement for the current broad “may be particularly useful” paragraph: “Autologous fascia PVS is an option for index SUI patients. AUA/SUFU advises against a synthetic MUS during concomitant urethral diverticulectomy, urethrovaginal fistula repair or urethral mesh excision with SUI surgery; if a concomitant sling is appropriate, autologous fascia is preferred over other biological substitutes. A poorly mobile urethra or compromised tissue requires individualized selection; radiation and steroid exposure still affect autologous healing.” Preserve the statement numbers 12 and 16–18 internally. This distinguishes a clinical-principle restriction from preference, without claiming every patient needs concurrent continence surgery.
3. **Clarify the E-SISTEr row**: add that its five-year continence values are cumulative survival estimates using a “once failed, remains failed” classification, not a cross-sectional proportion of all 655 randomized women. Optionally replace unqualified satisfaction percentages with **148/179 sling respondents versus126/172 Burch respondents**. The existing statement about preferential enrollment of incontinent women is correct. Do not replace the current five-year numbers with the different unadjusted bars in Figure3.
4. **Add one short paragraph under fascia-lata donor choice on direct comparative evidence.** Peng2020 was retrospective (21FL/84RF), and a much larger overall 2024 registry still contained only81FL/160RF cases. These studies do not establish an intrinsically safer or more durable donor tissue. If including the registry, report only30-day outcomes and retain the absent prior-SUI-surgery data, coding and selection limitations. Cross-link this material-choice paragraph from rectus rather than duplicating it.
5. **Optional—not a necessary practice-changing addition:** Mangir2025 belongs beside the existing2024 review if the page should show newer synthesis. It pooled only three randomized studies of primary surgery, found no clear cure difference, and reported longer autologous operative time. It substantially overlaps the older trial evidence and does not prove equivalence. Prefer one sentence or a combined synthesis row over expanding the page with several overlapping meta-analyses.
6. **Keep the existing2024 Grigoryan row cautious.** Add its scope (20RCTs in review/10 in meta-analysis, search before September2023) if useful, but do not add its abstract's questionable “RR” values for continuous operating-time/hospital-stay outcomes. Do not describe lower reported long-term complications as no autologous complications.

No new pivotal randomized donor-site trial or newer Cochrane CD001754 version was identified in this targeted search. That is a search result, not proof that none exists. Small2026 transobturator pilot/case-series reports and a2026 conference scoping review were not selected as practice-changing additions.

### Additional AUA scope and selection wording

Complete text pages4,25,26 of the same official2023 PDF were subsequently read, completing statement22 and its discussion. With previously read pages15,16,21,22,23, this is a scoped guideline section read, not the entire guideline.

- Statement16 concerns a fixed/immobile urethra. Do not substitute an isolated leak-point/closure-pressure threshold or treat urethral mobility and sphincter function as interchangeable. The discussion favors PVS for a nonmobile urethra but lists several options; it is expert guidance, not a randomized proof of universal superiority in ISD.
- Statement17's synthetic-sling prohibition applies when performing **concurrent** diverticulectomy, urethrovaginal fistula repair or urethral mesh excision with SUI surgery. A remote history alone is not the same prohibition. If a simultaneous sling is chosen, autologous fascia is the preferred biological material in that discussion.
- Statement22 permits an intentionally obstructing autologous PVS for selected severe outlet dysfunction/refractory cases, with counseling and an appropriate bladder-emptying plan. That salvage concept must remain distinct from ordinary tension-free sling technique. Bladder-neck closure requires drainage; neither strategy should be offered as a routine shortcut.
