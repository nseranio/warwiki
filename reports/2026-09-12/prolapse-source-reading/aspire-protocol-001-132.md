## Findings that matter for the site

1. **This is a trial of symptomatic post-hysterectomy vault prolapse.** Entry requires prior total hysterectomy with no cervix, age at least 21, bothersome bulge, at least one Ba/C/Bp point beyond the hymen and C greater than −2/3 TVL. It excludes known prior mesh/biologic-graft POP repair, known prior SSLS, unresolved active chronic pelvic pain, prior abdominal/pelvic radiation, known material complications and selected anatomic contraindications. Prior midurethral sling is allowed. Prior USLS is allowed, but the native-tissue operation must then be SSLS. Do not extend its result unqualified to uterine prolapse, concomitant hysterectomy, extensive mesh salvage, radiated tissue or chronic pelvic-pain populations (original eligibility; final pages 111–118).

2. **The formal primary failure endpoint is composite and time dependent.** Any bothersome bulge, prolapse retreatment with surgery/pessary, or any Ba/C/Bp greater than 0 cm constitutes failure. Loss of apical support still above the hymen deliberately does not constitute primary anatomic failure. C greater than −2/3 TVL is a secondary endpoint. This differs from older CARE/OPTIMAL composites and precludes direct comparison of unqualified success percentages across trials. A primary anatomic failure does not itself mean symptoms, dissatisfaction or a need for another operation (final pages 112–114, 131–132).

3. **The randomization is between three treatment strategies, not every operative variant.** SCP could be open, laparoscopic or robotic; native tissue could be unilateral SSLS or USLS, chosen according to surgeon experience/practice. Mesh configuration, many suturing details and concomitant procedures were discretionary. The trial cannot establish robotic-versus-laparoscopic superiority, equivalence of fixation materials, or USLS-versus-SSLS superiority from those nonrandomized subgroups.

4. **The TVM intervention and recruitment changed before completion.** The original protocol allowed Uphold LITE or Elevate AA. The final protocol explicitly states Elevate production halted in March 2016 and the kit was not to be used; its TVM arm is Uphold LITE. After the FDA's 16 April 2019 order, enrollment and randomization into TVM halted. At that point 344 surgeries were complete and 18 consented or randomized participants awaited surgery. Remaining TVM assignments were to be rerandomized 1:1 to SCP/native tissue. Preserve these facts when reporting allocation, arm denominators and applicability; the publication/SAP must provide the final analytic handling (final pages 106, 109, 120).

5. **Masking is limited.** Participants are masked only preoperatively; surgeons, coordinators and telephone interviewers are not masked. Anatomic evaluators at 6–60 months are masked. The narrative claim that postoperative unmasking should not bias outcomes is too strong because the primary composite includes participant-reported bulge. Allocation is web based with permuted blocks, concealed block size and site/age stratification. The final protocol permits discontinuation if surgery is not scheduled within six months for patient-specific reasons unrelated to masked assignment; evaluate the actual publication denominators rather than assuming every randomized participant had surgery (final pages 119–121).

6. **Noninferiority does not mean identical efficacy.** The original planning assumptions were 80% two-year success in each mesh arm and 60% in native tissue, with a planned 363 participants (121 per arm), 5% annual loss and a 15-percentage-point noninferiority margin corresponding to HR 1.93. The original NI test used one-sided alpha .03; each mesh-versus-native-tissue superiority comparison used two-sided alpha .01. These are design assumptions, not observed outcomes. The final protocol adds a closed test of TVM superiority if noninferiority is established (pages 101, 114–117). The original SAP and final SAP still need the other readers' reconciliation.

7. **The SCP technique rules support precise description, not universal mandates.** The final protocol uses knitted, large-pore (at least 1800 microns), lightweight (at most 42 g/m²), high-porosity (at least 60%) monofilament polypropylene; no xenograft, allograft or collagen-coated mesh. Y mesh or separate arms are allowed, with no fixed minimum arm length. At least four vaginal attachment sites per arm are required. Self-locking sutures are excluded. Delayed-absorbable or permanent monofilament 0/2-0 sutures are allowed; Gore-Tex and braided permanent vaginal fixation are excluded. At least two permanent sacral sutures **or** permanent anchors/tacks are allowed. The text explicitly does not establish superior efficacy/safety of either sacral-fixation method. The trial protocol therefore cannot justify a claim that tacks and sutures are equally safe or that one suture type is universally best (actual pages 33–34 and 124–125).

8. **Native-tissue standardization is specific.** SSLS is unilateral modified Michigan four-wall suspension with two permanent and two delayed-absorbable sutures. USLS uses one permanent plus one delayed-absorbable suture on each side. Permanent sutures are buried without exposed vaginal epithelium; delayed-absorbable sutures traverse the wall. Bilateral SSLS and USLS midline plication/culdoplasty are prohibited in the protocol. These standardization choices do not establish comparative superiority over every other accepted variant (final pages 125–126).

9. **Concomitant continence surgery was discretionary and not the primary endpoint.** Full-length retropubic or transobturator MUS is allowed; Burch urethropexy is not part of this protocol. The rationale cites an E-CARE association involving support, which is not an ASPIRe randomized comparison establishing Burch harm. Anterior/posterior repair is discretionary, with a protocol anatomic target of at least 1 cm above the hymen. Cystoscopy assessing ureteral function follows all suspension ties and mesh tensioning (final page 127).

10. **Adverse-event ascertainment is bounded and changes over time.** The original protocol collects all grade II-or-higher events initially; from six weeks to six months it adds all deaths/hospital/ER events plus qualifying possibly related pelvic/surgical events, and later focuses on deaths and possibly related grade II-or-higher events. Primary efficacy failure is not separately entered into the AE log. Thus an AE rate must retain the source's period, severity and attribution rules; it is not every all-cause event over a lifetime. The protocol's important-complication pain cutoff beyond six weeks differs from PPAR's pelvic-pain symptom description beyond 12 weeks. Its clinical/empirically treated UTI definition is broader than strictly culture-confirmed UTI (original safety and appendices; final page 121).

11. **The PPAR and FASt appendices are protocols, not outcome reports.** PPAR compares patient versus clinician AE rankings, blinded to each other's ratings, with a preselected symptom/event inventory and reassessment over time. It does not fully capture every possible patient concern or exact event-resolution timing. FASt is the prospective older-adult frailty cohort using six Robinson components plus Timed Up and Go, with baseline assessments masked from surgeons. Hypotheses, expected sample sizes and suggested cutoffs in these appendices are not validated outcome estimates from ASPIRe.

## Internal source discrepancies and extraction traps

These findings concern the supplied protocol's wording or historical background summaries. They do **not** demonstrate an error in ASPIRe's eventual trial results or in an original paper that was not read in this assignment.

- **Mesh units:** original actual PDF page 33 prints a pore size of at least 1800 **mm**. Actual final page 124 corrects this to 1800 **microns** (1.8 mm), consistent with the mesh table. This is a real original-protocol unit typo, not merely text extraction.
- **At-the-hymen criterion:** the background success list uses Ba/C/Bp less than 0 cm (final page 114), but formal primary failure uses greater than 0 cm (page 131). The latter permits 0 cm anatomically. Quote the formal endpoint and reconcile to the publication/SAP rather than importing the background inequality.
- **Historical Table 2:** actual original page 19/final page 110 gives Lo dyspareunia 1/52 (9%) versus 7/66 (39%); Maher repeats 1/52 (9%) and 7/66 (58%) despite a different total sample. Counts and percentages cannot all be correct. The caption/text also inconsistently labels subjective/objective criteria. Do not reproduce these cells without the original reports.
- **Historical cohort counts:** the Stanford paragraph gives 142 total but 61 prior hysterectomy + 19 concurrent hysterectomy + 83 hysteropexy, totaling 163 (original page 16/final page 108). That is internally inconsistent.
- **Success/failure mixing:** original actual appendix tables place some failure definitions or failure percentages under success headings: Hilger/Culligan in the SCP summary, Vu 0–4.2% in the TVM summary, Rapp's greater-than −1 criterion, and Hefni's vault “distal to” the hymen. These cannot become universal cure ranges or surgical targets.
- **Table 7 extraction correction:** actual page 59 shows Brubaker 56% for stage less than 2 and 98% for stage **less than or equal to 2**. Text extraction dropped the latter inequality; that specific apparent contradiction is resolved by the actual image. The column headed number lost to follow-up containing 322 (302) still should not be interpreted as 302 lost without checking the original source.
- **Other background numerical issues:** the USLS table includes De Boer's anterior recurrence of 47.9% and Doumouchtsis reoperation of 11.9%, inconsistent with adjacent universal low ranges. The Huang table contains 110/200 (67.5%) and 53/200 (32.5%), which do not agree arithmetically. The frailty planning table's cells and printed totals differ (134+127+106+182=549, printed 550; other combined totals also differ). These are background/planning tables, not ASPIRe outcomes.
- **Instrument/technique references:** the original USLS technique citation points to a Shull SSLS paper; PISQ-IR is paired with a PISQ-12 reference and SF-12 with an SF-36 reference. Several frailty references contain unclean PubMed metadata. The embedded Katz ADL description substitutes walking for the usual continence domain, and the FCI description has an unusual 16-item/0–33 specification. Do not create clinical scoring tools from these secondary descriptions without the original validated instruments.
- **PPAR inventory:** narrative text says seven events, while the actual inventory contains eight event rows, in addition to 11 symptom rows. Keep the exact inventory when describing study collection, or avoid a misleading event count.
- **Final internal recall-period mismatch:** page 121 describes pain-medication use over 72 hours; page 132 specifies 24 hours. Do not infer a single recall period without the case-report forms/SAP.
- **Historical context stays historical:** much of the 2021 protocol background retains 2015-era sales figures, “ongoing” trials and device-market language. Page 120 contains the later FDA-related stop. It must not be used as a current device-availability source.
