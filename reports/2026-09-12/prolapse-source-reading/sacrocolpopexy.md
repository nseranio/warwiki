## Findings requiring correction in the page

1. The current reference 4 is Yeung 2024 **transvaginal mesh** versus native-tissue Cochrane, DOI CD012079.pub2. It does not establish apical sacrocolpopexy outcomes, fixation technique, hysterectomy comparisons or bladder injury rates. Numerous citations using it must be removed/replaced with actually applicable sources. The applicable apical Cochrane is a different review; do not silently substitute without reading its relevant source.
2. Remove claims that absorbable sutures eliminate erosion. Tagliaferri showed mesh exposures 0/75 versus 3/75, P=.245, and was not powered for rare complications. Pollack's SCP appendix reports mesh exposure 3.1% with permanent versus 3.3% with absorbable vaginal fixation, without a detected difference. Suture exposure may be lower with absorbable material; mesh exposure remains possible.
3. Separate vaginal mesh fixation from sacral attachment. Giannini's public abstract describes three permanent cervical OR three absorbable vault sutures, six anterior and six posterior sutures, and permanent sacral attachment. Its 60-patient retrospective series cannot establish an obligatory stitch count or prevention of mesh exposure. Full main and its two actual figures remain inaccessible. Do not turn its technical preference into a universal recipe.
4. Remove the single-port randomized trial claim (n=70, 157 versus 181 minutes, P=.018): its attached Menefee ASPIRe citation concerns three prolapse procedures, not this single-port comparison. Remove unverified barbed-suture minutes and fixation-anchor timing comparisons pending primary sources.
5. Explain that dissection and vaginal attachment are tailored to the defect and tissue. A uterine manipulator, precise port diameters, bladder-neck/trigone endpoint and posterior levator attachment are not universally required. Culligan describes identifying the right ureter and sacral vessels/iliac vein, exposing anterior longitudinal ligament over the sacrum and avoiding L5–S1 disc penetration, urinary integrity inspection, and covering/burying exposed mesh or barbs. Spondylodiscitis is a recognized rare complication, not merely a theoretical risk limited to tackers. Avoid a rigid ischial-spine tension target or universal 3-mm-depth instruction without context.
6. Remove BMI >=26 as a stand-alone surgical indication and impaired healing as an automatic indication for biologic mesh. Counsel on alternatives, tissue/medical factors, mesh-related risk and patient goals. Do not generalize one fascia-lata trial's five-year rates to all biologic materials.
7. Do not universally prefer supracervical hysterectomy or prohibit total hysterectomy. Sources include conflicting observational findings with different mesh eras, exposure definitions and ascertainment. Retained uterus/cervix, uterine disease, screening and patient preference require individualized planning. Root is checking these sources separately.
8. Rebuild long-term counseling with explicit denominators and outcome definitions. e-PACT II's 95% success is in 82 returnees; cumulative 9.9% mesh exposure is 18/182 with prior-event carryforward. CARE extension mixes anatomical, symptomatic and composite failure; the current 27–48% range must not be presented as one endpoint or a general prediction.
9. Preserve the already corrected CARE 2006 three-month composite SUI values **33.6% versus 57.4%**, linked to the 2016 correction. The original 23.8% versus 44.1% figures must not recur. Contemporary continence-procedure counseling should distinguish prophylaxis, overt SUI and staged treatment.
10. Do not call laparoscopic and robotic techniques proven equivalent. Ferrari 2026 source review by root identifies heterogeneous mixed-design studies and internal reporting problems. State no clear detected difference in major clinical outcomes, with uncertainty, and omit causal advantages in complex cases not tested in those data.
11. Reframe learning curves as platform/surgeon/team dependent. Van Zanten's public abstract describes 372 cases by two surgeons and approximately 78 procedures for risk-adjusted proficiency in that cohort; it is not a minimum-case credential or guarantee. Current unrelated citations for 20–30 or 55–84 cases are not adequate.
12. Activity evidence is mixed in the page: the 2023 randomized recovery study cannot be described as having two-year follow-up using Morciano 2025. Morciano is a retrospective cohort with activity assessed at three months and outcomes to two years, not randomized early unrestricted activity. Avoid unsupported fixed postoperative restrictions, but use correctly identified recovery evidence.
13. Figure source is `static/img/diagrams/sacrocolpopexy.svg`. Initial source-audit phase did not render it; subsequent independent visual inspection of original and corrected renders is recorded below. Fix the self-referential sacrohysteropexy link or link the real companion page. Preserve video section immediately before references.

## Source discrepancies: do not copy into the site

- Shahid's complication summary and tables contain inconsistent totals, cited-study/category mismatches and CARE follow-up denominator/endpoint problems. Its mesh-related suture comparison is 4.2% versus 0.4%, not the page's 0.04%; do not retain this comparison without the original source.
- Tagliaferri Table 2 postoperative POP-Q IQRs include positive points despite reported 100% success using no point reaching the hymen. This unresolved internal inconsistency warrants restraint about the perfect success estimate. Study-specific prolonged prophylaxis and routine adnexal removal are not modern universal instructions.
- Pollack actual Figure 4 uses absorbable/permanent success ratios but the side labels appear reversed. Supplement Appendix 7 adverse-event forest likewise has direction labels inconsistent with the plotted risk ratio. Appendix 4's native-tissue vaginal adverse-event table columns contradict the narrative direction for exposure/granulation. Its methods incorrectly list poliglecaprone-25 among permanent materials. The direct SCP meta-analysis has only three comparative studies, is dominated by a nonrandomized comparison, and mostly short follow-up. Do not republish the graphics or treat pooled single-arm percentages as randomized comparisons.
- e-PACT II has small internal table totals, smoking and PGI-I mismatches and inconsistent labeling of four interval events; use only consistent headline endpoints with attrition stated.
- Culligan includes author preference and device-related disclosures; not all confident technical assertions are comparative evidence. Some reference DOIs and mesh-weight units are misprinted. Avoid importing these errors.
