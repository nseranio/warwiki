Confirms extensive cross-pelvic uterine/ovarian and iliac communications; original site no-cross-communication statement is false. Distinguish pelvic venous reflux/obstruction from uterine-artery embolization; diagnose clinically rather than treating a compression image alone. Variable collateral flow requires planning before sacrifice/embolization. Presacral basivertebral veins retract into foramina and may be difficult to control; avoid a rigid thumbtack sequence. Venous and arterial corona mortis are distinct and prevalence is by hemipelvis, not interchangeable with patient prevalence.

Do not copy source errors: body calls superficial epigastric veins an internal-thoracic draining pathway where superior epigastric veins are intended (the table distinguishes them); inferior-gluteal narrative says inferior sciatic notch although the route is the infrapiriform greater sciatic foramen; a right renal-vein-variant phrase means right gonadal drainage variation; and its portosystemic section conflates rectal varices with internal hemorrhoids. The review's vessel-size and compression thresholds are not stand-alone diagnostic or intervention criteria. Underlying49references were not all separately read.
