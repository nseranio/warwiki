## Clinically material findings

- **Empiric add-on PCC in trauma.** Protocol specifies 25 IU factor IX/kg of four-factor PCC (Kanokad), compared with equal-volume saline, added to standard resuscitation. Both arms could receive early ratio-based red cells/plasma, tranexamic acid, fibrinogen for deficiency, platelets and definitive hemorrhage control. This differs from FiiRST-2's early combined FC/PCC replacement of plasma. Recent anticoagulant treatment was excluded: PROCOAG does not test anticoagulant reversal or justify prohibiting PCC for every indication.
- **Superiority design.** Planned 162 patients per arm provided 80% power for a three-unit/25% reduction from a control mean of 12 units, with two-sided alpha .05; 350 envelopes allowed for exclusions/nonenrollment. No noninferiority margin and no planned interim efficacy analysis. Main reported 327 randomized and 324 analyzed after consent withdrawals. Failure to show the expected benefit cannot be called equivalence.
- **Interpret the main endpoint correctly.** Main Table 2 explicitly defines continuous absolute differences as mean differences with bootstrap confidence intervals. Its 0.2 units (95% CI −2.99 to 3.33) is not the subtraction of reported medians 12 versus 11. The nonparametric P value and bootstrap mean CI are different summaries. Avoid labeling this a one-unit causal effect or a proven clinically equivalent result. Platelet units here follow the main paper's own component convention; do not transfer FiiRST-2's four-unit platelet-dose conversion.
- **The safety signal remains relevant.** Main Table 3 has at least one thromboembolic event in 56/161 versus 37/157 (RR 1.48, 95% CI 1.04–2.10). Those are outcome-available denominators, not the randomized totals and not the count of all events. The composite includes venous and arterial events, including superficial thrombosis and other ischemic events. Safety ascertainment followed clinical suspicion and confirmatory imaging, with thromboprophylaxis at clinician discretion; it was not a universal scheduled scan program.
- **Supplementary subsets do not establish a safer subgroup.** Per-protocol patients treated within one hour: 159/149 in eTable 2, median blood products 12/11, P=.889; eTable 4 has TEE outcomes for 156/146, with 53/36 patients affected, P=.076. Observed massive-transfusion subset: 83/77 in eTable 5, median blood products 18.5/19, P=.929; eTable 7 has 30/82 versus 18/75 with TEE, P=.087. Smaller nonsignificant comparisons are not evidence of equivalent safety. Massive transfusion is partly defined after randomization and treatment may affect membership; this is not a validated baseline responder phenotype.
- **The post-hoc PTr typo is unresolved.** Main reports 6/49 as 33%; it is approximately 12%. None of the seven eTables or three eFigures supplies corrected PTr-subgroup event counts. Do not guess that 16 was intended or claim PCC is safe in patients without coagulopathy. No interaction test establishes differential benefit/harm.

## Protocol/SAP chronology and source defects

1. Protocol page 1 lists amendments in September 2017, March/October 2018 and April 2019: outcome clarification, an ancillary study, sampling windows/patient information, a center replacement and extension. The document identifies version 6/February 6, 2019, although the amendment list includes a later April date and the bibliography includes 2020 publications. It is a supplied/reported protocol, not evidence that every sentence was locked before enrollment in December 2017. No earlier complete versions were retrieved.
2. SAP version 1 is dated May 9, 2022; its history table gives database freezing as the reason, while the footer carries April 8, 2022. Both follow August 2021 trial completion. The SAP is therefore not demonstrably pre-enrollment. It largely supports the planned primary/per-protocol/massive-transfusion analyses, but does not independently verify when all analysis decisions were made or when unblinding occurred.
3. Neither complete protocol nor SAP specifies multiplicity adjustment across the many secondary/subgroup tests. The clinical safety signal is worth reporting with its exact endpoint and uncertainty; do not promote every unadjusted subgroup result into a separate confirmatory finding.
4. Protocol/SAP require testing whether time spent within the first 24 hours differs before choosing totals versus hourly blood consumption. eTable 1 shows 24 (24–24) for both groups but labels those values mean (SD), an apparent summary-label mismatch. A nonsignificant between-arm exposure-time test does not prove absence of survivor bias. Early deaths' actual transfusions were included in the main analysis.
5. The main regression description swaps the plausible dependent/independent roles of time and PTr. SAP is also imprecise about this model. The actual eFigures show descriptive PTr trajectories; they cannot resolve the fitted model from its code. eFigure 1 states 324 participants and plots a cumulative incidence that already starts around 0.6 at time zero; it is not a plot restricted solely to those initially above 1.5. Do not infer new treatment thresholds or effects from it.
6. eFigures 2–3 show group trajectories with error bars but do not supply per-timepoint available sample counts or a self-contained error-bar definition. No new numeric effects were extracted from them.
7. Several supplement difference columns use placebo minus PCC, including negative TEE differences despite more events in PCC, whereas main Table 3 uses PCC minus placebo. Do not copy a negative sign as a protective PCC effect. Some continuous differences also do not equal displayed medians; avoid relabeling them as median differences.
8. Secondary outcome labels/denominators remain imperfect. Supplement eTables 3/6 label disposition at 30 days while protocol specifies day 28; reported death percentages are not always generated by their table-header denominators. Main hospital-free-days values duplicate its ICU-free-days values, whereas supplement hospital-free medians are zero. Without data or a correction, do not invent reconciled denominators, recalculate a comparative effect or choose one conflicting value as definitively correct.
9. Main baseline/flow GOS terminology actually describes the 3–15 Glasgow Coma Scale and reverses its direction. Protocol/SAP correctly distinguish later GOS-E as a neurological outcome but do not repair the main label. Preserve normal GCS direction in clinical content.
10. Public funding, free study product from LFB, sharing of aggregated safety data with the manufacturer, and several investigators' manufacturer lecture payments are explicitly disclosed. They should remain part of internal appraisal, not be mistaken for proof of either bias or independence beyond the stated roles.

The attachments support a concise high-yield conclusion: routine early empiric PCC added to this trauma-resuscitation strategy did not reduce blood-product use and was associated with more patients experiencing thromboembolic events. They do not support broad equivalence, a reassuring coagulopathy subgroup, or direct extrapolation to anticoagulant reversal and elective GU surgery.
