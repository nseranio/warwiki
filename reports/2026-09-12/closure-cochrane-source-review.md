## Clinical interpretation

The review included 55 studies and 19,174 participants, with heterogeneous populations, incisions, material combinations and techniques. It compared five broad components: absorption, filament structure, absorption speed, continuous/interrupted closure and mass/layered closure. It deliberately did not select one optimal combined technique.

It **excluded trials comparing only small versus large bites**, including STITCH and the Millbourn stitch-length studies, because those comparisons were outside its defined protocol. It also excluded laparoscopic/laparoscopic-assisted surgery and studies of skin-only, peritoneum-only or retention-suture-only closure. Consequently, it cannot be used as negative evidence against modern small-bites closure, as a trocar-closure review or as direct evidence for routine separate peritoneal closure.

The reported monofilament hernia signal was RR0.76 (95% CI0.59–0.98; 16 studies, 4,520 participants). However, the subgroup retaining the same closure method and technique crossed the null, RR0.80 (0.55–1.15), as did the sensitivity analysis excluding high-risk studies and mixed-technique comparisons, RR0.65 (0.42–1.01). The study/material classification problems below further limit treating the pooled result as definitive isolated-material evidence.

Other primary hernia comparisons did not establish superiority. That is not proof of equivalence. The mass-versus-layered estimate was particularly imprecise and rated very low certainty. Its apparently significant single same-technique trial, Ausobsky1985, had major differences in incision distribution between groups; it does not establish that mass closure is harmful for a comparable midline incision.

Absorbable sutures were associated with fewer sinus/fistula outcomes in the reported overall pool, RR0.49 (0.26–0.94), rated low certainty. Infection and dehiscence results did not establish a reliable advantage for the major component comparisons. Only one trial involved an exclusively emergency population; contamination and emergency/elective effect modification could not be adequately assessed. Many included reports had unclear methods, quasi-random allocation, attrition or more than one changed component.

## Source discrepancies found on actual table/figure inspection

These findings are kept in the audit record. They are not silently corrected pooled estimates and do not justify reversing clinical recommendations based on newer guidelines and trials.

1. **Polyglyconate misclassification affects the actual monofilament analysis.** The Carlson1995 study table labels Maxon/polyglyconate multifilament. Analysis4.1 includes nylon4/91 as monofilament versus polyglyconate7/80 as multifilament; the same classification appears in other outcome plots. The [complete original indexed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7486431/) independently read in this check confirms those materials and event counts. [Medtronic's primary Maxon description](https://www.medtronic.com/en-us/healthcare-professionals/products/wound-closure/sutures/absorbable-sutures/maxon-monofilament-absorbable-suture.html) identifies it as monofilament. Both arms therefore belong to the monofilament category. The original full Carlson manuscript was not obtained. Colombo1997 is likewise labeled as multifilament polyglyconate in the included-study table and is absent from Analysis4.1 despite its comparison with PGA. That table inconsistency was checked against the review's own material definitions; the original full Colombo manuscript was not independently read here.
2. **Bresler1995 arm orientation is inconsistent.** Its included-study table duplicates the group2 label and says groups1/2 were pooled as monofilament despite assigning polyglactin to group1. Analysis4.1 has monofilament15/141 versus multifilament7/62, whereas Analysis5.1 reverses these data to slow7/62 versus fast15/141. The complete [English indexed abstract of the original French trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8526449/) was separately read: it confirms polyglactin910, PDSI and PDSII, 235 early assessments and 203 at one year, but does not supply a consistent full arm-count table. Its percentages also do not straightforwardly reconcile with the review's cell counts. The original full French article was not obtained; the correct reconstruction remains unresolved. No manufacturer secondary summary was used to manufacture a corrected estimate.
3. **Absorbable/nonabsorbable infection and dehiscence numbers disagree internally.** Abstract/summary text gives infection29 studies/8,457 participants, RR0.99 (0.84–1.17); actual Analysis1.2 gives28/8,304, RR1.01 (0.86–1.19). Abstract/summary text gives dehiscence34/9,004, RR0.78 (0.55–1.10); actual Analysis1.3 gives33/8,851, RR0.82 (0.58–1.17). Both discrepancies are153 participants. Neither version establishes superiority; do not combine their denominators and effects.
4. **A subgroup interpretation is overstated.** Discussion describes evidence of a midline-specific monofilament effect, but Analysis4.5 shows a nonsignificant interaction, P=.24. A significant result within one subgroup does not establish a significant difference between subgroups.
5. **Risk-of-bias and sensitivity counts are inconsistent.** The text describes only Bloemen2011 as low risk in all domains, whereas the complete Figure3 and the Colombo1997 table also display all-low judgments. The monofilament low-risk sensitivity figure contains five comparison rows from four distinct studies, explaining why a five-study narrative count should not be copied as a distinct-study count. Some historical trials allocated by calendar period, birthday or hospital number despite the broad randomized-trial label.
6. **Other summary discrepancies are not clinically decisive.** Monofilament sinus heterogeneity is51% overall in Analysis4.4;77% applies to its different-technique subgroup. Several missing-data sensitivity estimates differ between narrative and figures, including absorbable/nonabsorbable and continuous/interrupted hernia comparisons. All figures were read; no new sensitivity meta-analysis was calculated from them.
