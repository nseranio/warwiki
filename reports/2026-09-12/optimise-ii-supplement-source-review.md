## Consequential interpretation

1. **This is a specific treatment strategy.** The protocol/SOP tests cardiac-output-guided fluid challenges plus a fixed low-dose inotrope during surgery and four hours afterward, with a maximum total intervention duration of 24 hours. Inotrope options were dobutamine 2.5 micrograms/kg/min or dopexamine 0.5 micrograms/kg/min; these doses describe the trial and are not a new general treatment recommendation. Fluid challenges were 250 mL and responsiveness was a stroke-volume increase of at least 10%. The protocol retained clinical discretion and required reducing/stopping the inotrope for sustained tachycardia. It does not isolate the effect of monitoring alone or establish that every individualized haemodynamic strategy is ineffective.
2. **Population and denominators matter.** The actual participant-flow graphic shows 2,502 randomized, four removed for incorrect age eligibility, and 2,498 eligible allocated participants (1,251 intervention, 1,247 usual care). Four intervention participants lacked the primary outcome, leaving 1,247 in each primary-outcome denominator. The trial enrolled older adults undergoing major elective gastrointestinal surgery; it is not a direct reconstructive-urology or emergency-haemorrhage trial. Patients whose clinicians already intended cardiac-output monitoring irrespective of allocation were excluded. Rescue monitoring remained possible in critically ill control participants.
3. **The primary endpoint is a composite of clinically treated infections.** It includes surgical-site, pulmonary, urinary, bloodstream and uncertain-source infections, with Clavien–Dindo grade II or higher. It is not simply SSI. Death before infection was treated as no primary infection; the prespecified competing-risk sensitivity analysis also found no benefit (S3: subhazard ratio 1.06, 95% CI 0.88–1.27).
4. **Keep the early cardiac signal and its scope.** The main abstract gives 38/1,250 versus 21/1,247 patients with acute cardiac events within 24 hours, adjusted OR 1.82 (95% CI 1.06–3.13). S5 shows the principal component difference was arrhythmia (33 versus 17); most were grade II, although severe events also occurred. The composite also includes infarction, myocardial injury, successfully resuscitated arrest and cardiogenic pulmonary oedema. It should not be renamed myocardial injury alone, and overlapping event categories must not be summed as unique patients. The 30-day composite was 85/1,249 versus 79/1,247 and did not show a statistically clear difference.
5. **Do not generalize the renal endpoint.** The study's AKI outcome was KDIGO stage 2 or greater, with morbidity requiring clinical treatment. A null result here is not evidence that all stage-1 AKI or all renal injury is unaffected.
6. **Subgroups do not identify a proven beneficiary or superior device.** The planned surgical-category interaction was not significant (S2, p=0.520). Surgical approach was a post hoc analysis (interaction p=0.229). FloTrac and ClearSight groups were separately compared against the entire control arm in an explicitly post hoc pseudo-subgroup analysis; sensor choice was not randomized. No device superiority claim follows from their different raw infection rates. Very small obesity-surgery groups were removed from the interaction calculation because one had no events.
7. **Additional safety tables have separate counting rules.** S9 reports 17 versus 12 separately submitted SAE events, in addition to routinely collected trial endpoints. These are not the total number of all complications or all affected patients. S4–S8 permit multiple components per patient. S10 records confirmed outcome-assessor blinding in only about 71% of assessments and definite allocation knowledge in about 18% in each arm; do not describe the trial as fully double-blind.
8. **The SOP is a historical research document.** Its fluid options include starch-based colloids, its control description includes a CVP-response option, and its shared general-care targets include haemoglobin above 80 g/L and MAP 60–100 mm Hg. These should not be copied into WARWIKI as universal current bedside instructions merely because the trial is high quality. A study's protocol, its measured effect and contemporary treatment guidance are distinct.

## Source caveats retained internally

- The appendix's SAP-deviation section correctly labels several post hoc analyses and omitted conditional analyses. Its date refers to 17 January 2022, whereas the actual signed SAP is 17 January 2023. Its parenthetical description of a risk difference as a logit coefficient is mathematically imprecise. These are reporting caveats, not evidence that the primary analysis was incorrectly implemented; no participant data or executed code was independently available here.
- Figure S3 displays an unadjusted composite OR and component ORs; the primary article reports the prespecified adjusted result. Do not silently substitute one confidence interval for the other. The bloodstream-infection component estimate should not displace the primary endpoint or be presented as a separately confirmed practice-changing conclusion without multiplicity and component-outcome context.
- SAP example-code and blank-template labels contain minor inconsistencies with their accompanying prose. No unexecuted example was used to overrule the prespecified main outcome or the reported result. The full appendix, including its departures from the SAP, remains the record for interpretation.
