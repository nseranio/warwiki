# Hydrogel donor-site review: independent source reading

Reviewed September 12, 2026. This is a source-access and critical-appraisal record, not clinical clearance of a product or every wound-care claim. No wound-page source was edited by this reviewer.

## What the review can support

The review searched through July 20, 2022 and included **two trials totaling 162 randomized participants**. It is a narrowly defined hydrogel review, not a comparison of every donor-site dressing. The trials could not be pooled. Its low/very-low certainty conclusions do not establish a preferred hydrogel, a class-wide healing benefit, or equivalence for infection, pain and scarring.

The pediatric comparison involved **Sorbact (DACC-coated mesh plus amorphous hydrogel), Algisite M and Cuticerin**, with 36, 33 and 32 randomized participants respectively. The review derived mean differences of about 1.67 days from medians using the Wan conversion. The original trial's unadjusted medians were 10, 7 and 7 days, but the adjusted comparison was **not statistically significant (P = .0678)**. Baseline donor-site area and measured graft thickness differed. All 101 had healing data; later scar/pain data had losses. The review's derived difference should not be presented as a definitive class-wide delay or superiority claim. The primary trial was pediatric, single-center and predominantly small/medium thigh wounds; neither it nor the review establishes results for adult large radial-forearm/flap donor sites.

The octenidine trial randomized 61 people (31 versus 30), but the per-protocol analysis included **19 versus 22**. It compared octenidine-containing and identical octenidine-free hydrogel within a multistage dressing regimen, not hydrogel against no treatment. The original report found no significant healing-time difference; bacterial counts are not clinical infection outcomes. Small numbers and losses prevent confident safety/effectiveness conclusions.

**Appropriate site-level summary:** evidence for hydrogel donor-site dressings remains insufficient; one low-certainty, derived comparison suggested longer healing, while the original adjusted pediatric analysis did not establish a difference. Choose a dressing for the particular wound's moisture/exudate needs, atraumatic removal, comfort and follow-up. Do not use a cooling sensation as proof of improved healing. The rewritten adjunct page's brief cautious wording is compatible with these limits.

## Extraction/reporting discrepancies: do not propagate

1. **Bacterial-colonization direction:** the review says more octenidine wounds were colonized at day 4, dropping the original qualifier **“lower than 300 cfu.”** The original abstract, Results and Figure 4 show a shift toward lower bacterial counts in the octenidine group (P = .014). There was no clear group difference on day 6. This remains a microbiological surrogate, not proof that clinical infections are prevented.
2. **Adverse-event group counts:** review prose says two intervention participants and one placebo participant, yet RR 0.58 corresponds to the original **one of 19 octenidine versus two of 22 placebo** participants. Four events occurred in those three participants. The original Results identifies both placebo patients and the single octenidine patient. Do not reverse the counts or call this a demonstrated safety advantage.
3. **Healing confidence interval:** the review's MD 0.40 days (95% CI 0.28–0.52) conflicts with the primary report's 7.3 ± 0.2 versus 6.9 ± 0.2 days and **P = .236**. Treating the displayed 0.2 values as independent sample SDs reproduces the narrow review interval, but the primary distribution graph shows a substantially wider spread in observed healing days. The primary Methods generically calls variability SD; therefore the precise dispersion-label error cannot be settled from the text alone. Do not relabel it as definitely SEM or silently replace the confidence interval. The original data/author clarification would be needed for a sound recalculation.
4. **Pediatric percentages:** 33/57 is approximately **58%**, correctly stated in McBride, but becomes **38%** in the review. A separate pain fraction, 69/91 labeled 85.2%, is arithmetically inconsistent in the original and is inherited by the review; 69/91 is about 75.8%. Neither needs reproducing on WARWIKI.
5. **Outcome-specific bias:** the review's McBride study table explicitly calls healing attrition low risk but other outcomes high risk, while its healing evidence downgrade cites incomplete outcomes more generally. Its blinding summary is likewise simplified; the original describes only partial masking for healing photographs and full masking for later staff scar ratings. Keep the overall low certainty rather than asserting uniformly complete or uniformly absent masking.
6. **Units and scope:** the review labels the Australian trial's dressing costs USD, whereas the original describes Australian hospital prices without a conversion. The original permits separate randomization of bilateral thigh wounds; the review says no split-body studies were found. The actual number of bilateral participants is not clear in the accessible narrative, so do not assert the unit-of-analysis concern has been disproven. These do not establish that the central cautious conclusion is wrong.
7. The octenidine original itself has denominator/percentage and day-6 wording imperfections (for example eight AEs patients labeled 12.3% despite 61 randomized, and a statement that all wounds exceeded 2300 CFU despite its plotted lower-count categories). Distinguish the original from the later extraction; neither should be copied unquestioningly.

No independent meta-analysis was performed. These observations are retained in this maintenance report rather than burdening the public clinical page with source-audit details.

## Brölmann 2013: bounded full-text attempt

The [Amsterdam UMC author-repository record](https://pure.amsterdamumc.nl/en/publications/randomized-clinical-trial-of-donor-site-wound-dressings-after-spl/) supplied the complete abstract, independently read here, but its only document link was DOI [10.1002/bjs.9045](https://doi.org/10.1002/bjs.9045). The DOI open failed in the browser tool; one Utrecht repository-path attempt returned 404. The previously reported blocked thesis route was not repeatedly retried. **The full trial article, tables, figures and supplement were not retrieved or read by this reviewer.**

The abstract supports 289 randomized/288 analyzed in 14 centers and six arms, hydrocolloid median healing 16 versus 23 days, and gauze infection 18% versus 7.6% (RR 2.38, 95% CI 1.14–4.99). It does not by itself establish the exact gauze product/regimen or all subgroup details. The site appropriately limits its recommendation to the studied donor-site strategy and does not ban gauze for every wound purpose.
