## Consequential application to the pending page revision

- Restrictive resuscitation with SBP80–90mmHg is limited to early uncontrolled traumatic bleeding without clinical brain injury; the rationale also excludes spinal injury and cautions about older people/chronic hypertension. Severe TBI guidance specifies MAP≥80mmHg. These are not a universal pressure target for elective GU bleeding.
- A negative FAST/POCUS cannot exclude abdominal injury or major haemorrhage. Stable/stabilised patients may need contrast CT; an unstable patient needing immediate control should not undergo a delaying diagnostic sequence.
- Damage-control surgery is selected for adverse physiology and injuries that make definitive repair unsuitable. The recommendation does not establish a universal90-minute operation limit or mandatory fixed timing for definitive reconstruction.
- Pelvic closure, embolisation and preperitoneal packing are complementary. Packing is recommended when bleeding continues and/or angiography cannot be achieved promptly, rather than obliging every patient through one linear sequence. The2023REBOA suggestion predates UK-REBOA and must be interpreted with later trial and guideline evidence.
- Initial massive-haemorrhage resuscitation can use a factor-based strategy or plasma with RBCs and a high platelet ratio; ongoing treatment should follow laboratory/viscoelastic findings. PROPPR is not evidence that1:1:1 improves every mortality endpoint. A normal initial haemoglobin does not rule out bleeding or justify delaying resuscitation.
- TXA is recommended as early as possible and within3hours of injury for bleeding/significant bleeding risk; the1g/10min plus1g/8h schedule is a trauma regimen, not a default for all operative GU bleeding. Later PATCH-Trauma adds important functional-outcome context.
- Low fibrinogen treatment is distinct from empirical high-dose cryoprecipitate for all trauma patients. The latter requires CRYOSTAT-2 reconciliation. Monitor and correct ionised hypocalcaemia; do not claim a mortality effect from calcium replacement that this guidance explicitly says has not been demonstrated.
- Do not copy the2023andexanet recommendation as current US availability guidance or reproduce generic trauma transfusion/neuraxial/VTE cutoffs as universal reconstructive instructions. Existing current pharmacology/antithrombotic companion pages remain the appropriate destinations.

## Source inconsistencies and limitations not propagated

The source itself contains errors and requires critical interpretation. These are recorded without claiming to have reanalysed the underlying datasets:

- R11 describes iTACTIC as690adults; R26 describes396patients. R26 calls a subgroup estimate OR2.12(0.84–5.34) significant, although its displayed interval includes1. The different outcome/timepoint/subgroup claims require the primary study; neither disputed statement is being copied.
- R23 repeatedly cites reference49 for a prehospital TXA trial, but the bibliography identifies49 as a PAMPer blood-product secondary analysis. The source's CRASH-3 severe-injury interval ends1.7, which also needs primary verification.
- The PROPPR platelet-substudy paragraph prints significance thresholds p<0.5 and p<0.1. Do not repeat these as conventional statistical significance or substitute them for primary-trial results.
- Reference9 is a review protocol despite the corresponding paragraph describing completed pooled results; reference266 is the FEISTY protocol despite a results claim. Reference391 is a police/AED cardiac-arrest study despite being cited for trauma-guideline adherence. Source citations need reconciliation before individual quantitative claims are retained.
- The calcium paragraph's citation323 is a microfluidic recalcification paper; the claimed calcium-gluconate dependence on hepatic metabolism should not be copied without a suitable primary pharmacologic source. No calcium preparation/dose rule is being inferred from that passage.
- The39recommendations are predominantly low-certainty or consensus-supported. Industry-supported writing/publication and declared author relationships were read; they do not invalidate the guideline but should not disappear from the internal appraisal.
- Supplement1 explicitly omits PICO2/24 from the search owing to result volume. Supplement2 lists bundle11 as omitted, maps PICO17 to bundle12 whereas Supplement1 calls it13, and repeats a CENTRAL#54 row where#65would be expected. Its screened subtotals do not consistently reconcile with de-duplicated counts. These are reproducibility/reporting caveats, not grounds to invent corrected searches.
- Fibrinogen R29 remains1C after disagreement; the main rationale explains why its proposed grading was not retained and Supplement3 records67%agreement. Do not describe the recommendation as unanimous high-certainty trial evidence.
