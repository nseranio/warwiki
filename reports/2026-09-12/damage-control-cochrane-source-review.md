# Damage-control laparotomy: independent source review

Reviewed September 12, 2026 by operative-review agent. No public MDX edits.

## Complete Cochrane source scope

[Cirocchi et al., Damage control surgery for abdominal trauma](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7202128/), DOI **10.1002/14651858.CD007438.pub3**, PMID 23543551.

Actually read the entire available PMC main text: abstract and plain-language summary, background, all methods and planned analyses, results/discussion/conclusions, update history, complete search-strategy appendix, all 18 excluded-study table rows, support/conflict declarations and all 21 listed references. Inspected the actual sole PRISMA figure. There are no included studies, pooled analyses, forest plots or listed supplementary files. The PMC PDF link returned HTML, so a separate PDF was not read; the complete accessible HTML was read in two untruncated text chunks.

The review found no eligible randomized trials through **December 2012**. That is a historical evidence gap, not proof of ineffectiveness or a current claim that trials do not exist. Its planned statistical methods describe no performed meta-analysis. The actual PRISMA figure has an unexplained 2441-to-2411 step; narrative screening totals and the 18-row exclusion table also do not fully reconcile. These discrepancies do not change the zero-included-trial result.

The [current Cochrane public summary](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD007438_surgery-unstable-trauma-patients) displays April 1, 2022 and a 2022 citation but retains the December 2012 search. PMC/PubMed identify the 2013 version. No withdrawal notice was visible in the sources accessed; the publisher full review returned 403. Do not describe the page stamp as a refreshed 2022 search or claim comprehensive verification of its present editorial status.

## Currency check: later randomized evidence

[Harvin et al., the DCL pilot trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8323393/), DOI **10.1136/tsaco-2021-000777**, PMID 34423135, was published in 2021. Independently read its entire main XML narrative, all five tables/footnotes, 20 references, funding/conflicts and the actual CONSORT figure. The article lists no supplemental files; its separately published protocol and registry protocol/SAP were identified but not read in full. The registry overview was read.

Forty patients were randomized; 39 were analyzed after one consent withdrawal (18 DCL, 21 definitive closure). It studied surgeon-equipoise cases and excluded ongoing instability, required hemorrhage packing, planned hemostatic interventional radiology and unsafe fascial closure. The primary composite was imprecise, with baseline imbalance, crossover and inadequate recruitment. It neither proves equivalence nor supports abandoning rescue DCL. The authors' plan to liberalize local indications is not a general guideline. No contemporary multicenter definitive DCL-versus-closure trial was established in this bounded search.
