# Prehospital whole blood: independent primary-source review

Reviewed September 12, 2026, for the vascular damage-control/exposure revision. This is a source-access and interpretation report, not a guideline or approval of all trauma care. No clinical source page was edited for this task.

## Clinical implication

Neither 2026 trial demonstrated superiority of its initial prehospital whole-blood strategy over the trial's component-blood strategy for its prespecified primary outcome. The results do not establish equivalence, noninferiority, or identical safety. They also do not directly test an exclusively whole-blood hospital resuscitation protocol, whole blood versus crystalloid alone, or elective reconstructive-urology hemorrhage. Both interventions covered an initial maximum of two whole-blood units; subsequent treatment followed usual care. Local massive-hemorrhage protocols and prompt hemorrhage control remain necessary regardless of product selection.

A suitable short clinical summary is: “Two large 2026 prehospital trauma trials did not demonstrate superiority of initial whole blood over component-blood resuscitation. TOWAR assessed 30-day mortality, whereas SWiFT assessed death or massive transfusion within 24 hours. These findings do not establish equivalence or determine the optimal subsequent hospital transfusion regimen.”

## TOWAR: design, outcome and limits

- Cluster-randomized crossover strategy at 44 air-medical bases associated with 11 trauma centers in the United States and Canada, with allocation in a 2:1 whole-blood/component-blood schedule. Patients were not individually randomized. Recruitment ran May 2022–June 2025; the older protocol anticipated ten centers and a longer recruitment period.
- Eligible adults were 18–90 years with specified hypotension/tachycardia and initiation of transfusion. Transport could start at the scene or a referring hospital; 722/1,020 came from the scene. Prior transfusion was allowed. The detailed arrest exclusion was more than five consecutive minutes of CPR without return of vital signs, rather than exclusion of every patient who ever had traumatic arrest. Women of childbearing potential were not categorically excluded; known pregnancy was excluded.
- Whole blood was low-titer group O. The actual age comparison covered 1–14 versus 15–21 storage days. Although the protocol discussed product-specific shelf lives up to 35 days, the reported comparison does not establish efficacy or safety of every product stored that long. Product leukoreduction and later transfusion practice varied across sites.
- Of 1,020 enrolled participants (715 whole blood, 305 components), 27 withdrew, leaving **993 in the primary analysis: 695 versus 298**. Thirty-day status was observed in **972**; 21 missing outcomes (13 versus eight) were imputed as survival. Keep observed and analyzed populations separate.
- Thirty-day mortality was **180/695 (25.9%) versus 61/298 (20.5%); adjusted odds ratio 1.24, 95% CI 0.87–1.76; P=.24**. The point estimate favored components, but the interval does not establish either benefit or harm from whole blood. Missing-data sensitivity analyses remained nonsuperior.
- The original protocol specified a cluster-adjusted comparison of proportions; the article foregrounds adjusted mixed logistic regression and also reports the original comparison (P=.08). The common mortality endpoint remained unchanged, but not every detail of the reported adjusted model should be described as prospectively fixed. The planned effect was a large ten-percentage-point reduction (26% to 16%); absence of that demonstrated benefit is not proof of no smaller effect.
- The reported per-protocol analysis reclassifies treatment received and is no longer randomized; its supplement explicitly acknowledges residual confounding. The storage-age comparison is also observational, not a randomized noninferiority trial of older versus younger blood. Exploratory subgroups were underpowered and explicitly not intended to support treatment recommendations.
- Laboratory analyses have substantial missingness: the supplement describes about 24% missing prothrombin-time measures, 45% thromboelastography measures, and 83.8% platelet-mapping measures. Apparent laboratory similarities do not establish mechanistic or clinical equivalence.
- Table S10 reports only possibly related adverse events: three patients/four events versus one patient/one event. It is not a count of all adverse events after severe trauma. The storage-age safety table uses a different, broader event scope. Neither small related-event count demonstrates equal safety.

## SWiFT: design, outcome and limits

- Phase 3 pragmatic trial using individually randomized blood boxes, 1:1, across ten English air-ambulance services and 19 receiving hospitals; recruitment December 2022–September 2024. Clinicians identified major traumatic hemorrhage. Patients already transfused before the trial intervention were excluded, unlike TOWAR. All ages could enter; this was not an adults-only eligibility rule.
- The intervention provided up to two units of platelet-preserving, leukocyte-depleted low-titer group O whole blood; the comparator provided up to two red-cell units and two plasma units. The registry protocol specifies O RhD-negative, Kell-negative whole blood stored at 2–6°C for no more than 21 days. Component types and volumes should not be assumed identical to TOWAR.
- **942 were randomized (470 versus 472); 641 entered the modified intention-to-treat population (327 versus 314); 616 had the primary composite determined (314 versus 302).** Prespecified exclusions included traumatic cardiac arrest at randomization and subsequently established nontraumatic hemorrhage; consent withdrawals and unavailable arrest status also affected inclusion. Describing every excluded participant as a cardiac-arrest case would be incorrect.
- The primary outcome was all-cause death **or** massive transfusion within 24 hours: **153/314 (48.7%) versus 144/302 (47.7%); risk ratio 1.02, 95% CI 0.80–1.31; P=.84**. This differs fundamentally from TOWAR's 30-day mortality primary outcome.
- Massive transfusion was defined using a trial-specific unit/weight rule: generally at least ten units, with a 40 mL/kg threshold for children younger than 16 weighing under 50 kg. For this endpoint the trial counted two whole-blood units as four component units. That analytic convention is not a universal clinical conversion or proof that one whole-blood unit supplies a full therapeutic 1:1:1 component dose.
- The composite can be determined in a patient with known death even if transfusion-volume data are missing, explaining different denominators for composite, mortality and volume outcomes. The whole-blood group could and often did subsequently receive red cells and plasma; seven component-assigned patients received whole blood.
- Secondary 24-hour/30-day mortality and thrombotic comparisons did not establish superiority. Prolonged prothrombin time was more frequent after whole blood in a secondary comparison, with appreciable missing data and no multiplicity adjustment; this is not definitive evidence of clinical harm. Likewise, similar thrombotic estimates do not establish equal safety.
- Reported serious adverse events were 31 versus 37 events in 24 versus 31 patients. The protocol exempts several expected consequences of major trauma from expedited SAE reporting and captures such outcomes separately. Those SAE counts must not be represented as total morbidity.
- The original power target was 68% versus 56%, a 12-percentage-point absolute reduction. The blinded review increased the recruitment target to accommodate greater dropout. The main article explicitly explains that risk-ratio reporting replaced the originally planned odds ratios at journal request. Neither feature makes this an equivalence trial.
- The protocol warns that subgroups defined by post-randomization transport time or anesthesia are descriptive associations rather than causal treatment-effect tests. The v3.1 amendment history also documents data-withdrawal/opt-out clarification, a recruitment halt for a consent issue and the recruitment-target change. Final appendix-based sensitivity results remain an access limitation.

## Internal source and extraction cautions

These issues affect precise reuse but do not overturn either negative primary superiority result. They should remain in the audit rather than be turned into a catalogue on the clinical page.

1. TOWAR main Figure 1 appears to print “82” for imputed control outcomes, whereas the article, Table S4 and arithmetic consistently require **eight**. Main Figure 2's overall control denominator is 297 rather than the primary table's 298. Use the consistent main/supplement primary analysis (695/298, 21 total imputations), not a copied figure denominator.
2. TOWAR Table S13 is headed as a leukocyte-status comparison among whole-blood randomized patients, but its mortality denominators total 993 (745+248), the entire retained trial population. Non-leukoreduced blood was used at one site. This table is unsuitable for a causal leukoreduction claim. Storage-age model terminology also varies (log-binomial methods versus odds-ratio labels); do not rebrand the estimate as a proven equivalence result.
3. TOWAR Table S21 reports 60 whole-blood hemorrhage/exsanguination deaths, whereas the main table reports 61. Cause-of-death data are missing for 13 whole-blood and three component deaths and allow two causes. This discrepancy remains unresolved. Table S19's ionized-calcium unit/distribution and several other descriptive rows should not be copied into clinical normal-range guidance.
4. TOWAR PDF extraction adds invisible alignment characters to some counts: for example, a visible 69/594 can extract as 169/594, and a visible 87 as 187. Actual rendered tables were checked; these particular anomalies are extraction artifacts, not evidence that the printed table reported those inflated values.
5. SWiFT's accepted-manuscript flow figure has 31 whole-blood deaths by 24 hours, while Table 3 has 32/317. The primary composite remains consistently 153/314. The phrase describing 68% to 56% as a 38% relative reduction is not arithmetically a relative risk reduction; the unambiguous planned absolute reduction is 12 percentage points. The registry protocol cover also has a typographical final digit in its ISRCTN number; the trial registration is ISRCTN23657907.
6. Europe PMC indexes SWiFT with an “equivalence trial” publication-type label, but the actual protocol and manuscript test superiority. Metadata classifications are not a substitute for reading the design. No site language should claim proven equivalence from that index term.
