Removed universal flap preferences, zero-morbidity claims, guaranteed lubrication/depth and extrapolation from animal urinary reconstruction. Added the major lymphocele randomized trials, their conflicting findings, the 2026 IPD review and the relevant current EAU discussion. A Cochrane prolapse/continence review did not address peritonealization and was removed from that bibliography; removal does not imply source verification.

## Source inconsistencies that changed the interpretation

- **Baumann:** pooled estimates and sample sizes differ between the Results, GRADE table, discussion and aggregate/IPD forest plots. Some operative-time confidence intervals do not contain the reported point estimate. The review's Pose reference is a 2023 conference abstract even though the full 2025 trial is available. The page therefore reports the synthesis direction and uncertainty, not a silently reconstructed numerical estimate. A March 2026 correspondence exists (DOI 10.1007/s00345-026-06366-5), but its main is paywalled/unread; it is not being called a correction or used to infer its authors' objections.
- **Wang:** Methods and Results give different average follow-up durations. Its “free” vaginal flaps are mobilized local tissue, not free peritoneal grafts. The inherited peritoneal attribution was wrong.
- **Yang:** prose labels 2.6 days as catheter duration, whereas Table 2 lists drainage at 2.6 days and urinary catheterization at 3.6 weeks. The site does not export that contradictory timing as a protocol.
- **Oge abstract:** the printed compliance unit is inverted relative to conventional compliance, and the small-capacity failure observation is not a validated selection threshold. Neither has been promoted into a clinical rule.
- **Ratanalert:** three-month follow-up, incomplete baseline urinary assessments, one partial scrotal graft and one closure after stopping dilation limit claims. Serous secretion is not evidence of arousal-related lubrication. One supplementary donor-site photograph caption concerns a previous free-graft patient, not necessarily this pedicled-flap cohort.

## Omental corrections and additional complete mains

The revised page separates selected interposition from routine omentoplasty. Corrected gastric-vessel anatomy; removed claims of universal first choice, negligible morbidity, passive urinary drainage and guaranteed prevention of re-encasement. Separated breast-harvest series from GU outcomes, mixed omental/peritoneal repairs from omental-only cohorts, and the 27 total patients in Miyamoto from the ten who received omentum. Distinguished historical silicone/augmentation operations from an independent omental continence effect. Added the focused APR synthesis and broader 2024 review; selected fistula observations do not establish routine benefit after APR.

Escandón's 56-patient retrospective USF series reports sepsis of 3% with omentum, 27% with VRAM and 18% with primary repair. A preliminary review comment had reversed the comparator; direct publisher and indexed-abstract rechecking corrected that comment. The inherited 27% figure is retained. Selection and operation differences prevent a universal superiority inference; the adjusted recurrence comparison is against primary repair, not demonstrated against VRAM.

## Dedicated peritoneal vaginoplasty page

Corrected the hybrid peritoneal/skin description and hair-removal implications, removed guaranteed depth/lubrication and impossible-Xi-dual-team claims, and separated the actual cohorts. Acar's blood loss/bowel-recovery figures had been assigned to the hypoplasia paper; the 125-minute learning-curve median describes the entire cohort, not specifically the expert phase. The complication table had offered bowel rest generically despite two incarcerated internal hernias requiring surgery. The separate Dy peritoneal and Sljivich sigmoid series are not a direct comparative trial; the latter contains 36 total patients including 25 revisions, and the aggregate depth cannot be assigned to the revision subgroup.

The 500-case report and June 2026 systematic review are incorporated with their verified abstract-level limits. Neither supplies a concurrent randomized comparison with PIV. All 35 original bibliographic identities and complete available abstracts were read; print details for Celis, Ratanalert, Dhami, Zucchi and Sljivich were updated. Detailed main-paper access remains incomplete for the other original sources.
