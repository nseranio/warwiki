# Foreign bodies and tissue scaffolds — source review, September 12, 2026

## Scope

All five original MDX files and their final revisions were fully read: free silicone, penile pearls, decellularized ECM, composite scaffolds and synthetic polymer scaffolds. The [reference register](foreign-bodies-scaffolds-reference-access.json) preserves all ten original reference entries, maps the 18 current entries and records access to 11 distinct cited sources. Full MDX reading is not full-paper reading or clinical clearance.

## Corrections

- **Free silicone:** replaced unsupported worldwide illegality and unspecified exceptionally high complication-rate assertions with specific FDA safety/authorization information. Removed assumptions about every patient's circumstances; distinguished exposure-based testing, infection, granulomatous inflammation and acute systemic symptoms. Removed the imiquimod/minocycline/steroid treatment menu, universal radical-excision/flap implication and categorical safety claims about implanted silicone devices. Treatment extent is individualized. The 2023 systematic review supports a low-certainty management discussion, not comparative cure rates.
- **Penile pearls:** corrected blanket radiopacity, routine imaging implications and universally straightforward removal. Distinguished intact asymptomatic objects, exposed objects, infection, and deep/ventral placement or suspected structural injury. The original seven-patient series is used to clarify an existing practical claim, not as a new practice-changing study. Do not transfer its old antibiotic combinations or local-anesthetic doses into a universal protocol.
- **ECM:** removed claims of complete antigen removal, fully preserved architecture, guaranteed native-tissue remodeling and absent immune response. Distinguished cell-free matrices from cultured oral-mucosa grafts and added exact EAU recommendations. Commercial wound/soft-tissue matrices are not automatically authorized or proven organ replacements.
- **Composite scaffolds:** broadened the definition to multiple material components, including natural/natural combinations; corrected the conflation of cultured urothelial cells with stem cells. Replaced organ-scale printing implications with a comparison of research aims and unproven functional outcomes.
- **Synthetic scaffolds:** removed a universal PLA degradation timescale and separated the existing PLA/PBSu cell-culture study from implanted-human evidence. Material, surface-marker and product-clearance claims cannot substitute for clinical patency or organ function.

## Complete original-source reads

**Duan et al., 2023, DOI 10.3389/fbioe.2023.1258666.** Complete main, table 1, all 189 references, funding/disclosures and all seven actual figures read. The full XML text was re-extracted without omitting parent paragraphs containing tables or figures, then read in contiguous chunks. The Europe PMC supplementary archive contains the seven figures in JPG/GIF formats; no separate supplement was listed in the XML. This is a narrative engineering review, not a clinical trial or guideline. Its cited originals were not all read.

Several errors or overextensions in this review were explicitly excluded: autologous tissue described as having immune rejection; rabbit results generalized to patients; mechanically incompatible units for ureter properties; the figure-1 PU abbreviation; and figure-5 attribution mismatching a corneal paper. The claimed clinical scope of a Chinese commercial scaffold is not verified by the cited inguinal-hernia trial. Only basic scaffold concepts and translational limitations were retained; no quantitative clinical outcome, registration scope or operative recommendation was inferred from those passages.

**Kirkham et al., 2019, DOI 10.1186/s12245-019-0241-z.** Complete abstract, all seven cases, discussion, all 16 references and disclosures read. All three actual figures inspected: asymptomatic implants, a visible CT foreign body, and a removed plastic object. No separate supplement was listed. The case descriptions support individualized management and the limitations of bedside removal; this selected series cannot estimate prevalence or complication incidence. The abstract and case descriptions are not perfectly aligned on who had pain or required removal, so those aggregate counts were not adopted.

**Mustin et al., 2023, DOI 10.1016/j.jdin.2022.12.006.** Complete main, table I, five references and disclosures read. An initial paragraph extractor dropped the results paragraph surrounding the table; a complete body/back extraction was read to correct that omission. The paper includes 93 studies, 89 of them case reports/series, with nonuniform outcomes and no patient-reported outcome studies. These limitations, rather than treatment-response percentages, are the clinically useful point.

The separate [Mendeley version-2 supplement](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/nvmxbjdnbn/2), DOI 10.17632/nvmxbjdnbn.2, was obtained via its public file-view URL after the landing/download routes failed. Its SHA256 matches the published API value `aefac85df865c35a4758615b7cc858862305b67f840407d34c2b5b9dc1a5546f`. Complete expanded methods, five-database search table, PRISMA text, treatment/outcome table and all footnotes were read. All five rendered supplement pages were inspected, including the actual vector PRISMA and both treatment-table pages. Rendered section labels overlap parts of the PRISMA, but counts remain readable and agree with the original OOXML text.

The supplement states that no meta-analysis, formal risk-of-bias assessment or certainty assessment was performed. It exposes very small medical-treatment denominators (e.g. five minocycline-monotherapy patients). Its PRISMA initial count differs from the main report (1,161 versus 901); some treatment subrow allocations do not agree with their parent totals. These discrepancies reinforce the decision against adopting comparative response percentages; they do not justify declaring every included observation invalid.

## Guidelines and access limits

- **EAU 2026:** complete online tissue-transfer chapter 9.1–9.5, including displayed tables, read. Cell-free grafts: avoid with extensive spongiofibrosis, failed prior urethroplasty or stricture length greater than 4 cm, weak recommendation. Cultured autologous oral-mucosa grafts: clinical trials only, strong recommendation. Separate supplement tables and the entire guideline were not read in this batch. The online 20–110% patency range appears erroneous and was not reproduced.
- **FDA:** complete main text of the 2017 injectable-silicone safety announcement and current dermal-filler consumer page read. No global legal claim, genital filler approval or numerical complication risk inferred.
- **Chung 2026:** complete indexed abstract read; publisher identifies OA, but actual PDF/API requests returned 403. Full imaging text and figures remain unread. This prevents claiming that the whole imaging review has been verified.
- **Sharma 2022, Keshel 2020 and Davis 2018:** complete indexed abstracts read; originals were not obtained. Publisher errors/challenges and the limits of abstract-level support are recorded individually. They remain source-access gaps.
- **Sartoneva 2023:** complete abstract plus the beginning of the official 13-page university PDF, web lines 0–164 (first two pages and part of the third), read. Continued retrieval returned access denials. The result is a partial original read, not a full PDF read; actual figures, later methods/results/discussion and references remain unread. A downloaded file bearing a PDF suffix contained an HTML denial page and was not credited as a document.

The recent-literature search found narrative reviews, animal studies and a conference abstract, but established no new large human practice-changing trial for these scaffold pages. This bounded search is not proof that none exists. Duan 2025 was downloaded but only its opening sections were read; it was not added. Selected ICSM/SMSNA recommendation snippets were seen but the complete statements were not accessed in this batch, and are not counted as complete reads.
