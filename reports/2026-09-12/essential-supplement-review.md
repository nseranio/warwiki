## Primary endpoint and analysis

The planned primary endpoint was **all-grade anastomotic leakage (A+B+C)**. Grade B+C leakage was secondary. The protocol/SAP use a two-sided chi-square test of independence at alpha 0.05; the primary RR confidence interval uses a log-normal approximation.

The assumed control leak rate of 13% and intervention rate of 7% — a six-percentage-point reduction — were **power/sample-size assumptions**. They led to 391 per group (782 total), inflated to 850 for exclusions. They were not a six-point superiority margin. The reported 4.2-point difference with P=.041 is a statistically positive primary comparison under the stated analysis; failure to reach the anticipated six-point effect does not make the trial statistically negative. The main article's reported phrase “not superior” should not be adopted as the primary statistical verdict without explaining this internal inconsistency.

The planned primary population was **modified intention to treat (mITT)**, not all 850 randomized participants. The results are 422 ICG and 417 control patients, total 839. Supplement 5 documents the 11 exclusions after randomization:

| Reason | ICG | Control |
|---|---:|---:|
| No anastomosis | 1 | 3 |
| Severe protocol/inclusion violation | 1 (platelets below eligibility threshold) | 2 (recorded iodine allergy; no blood sample within four weeks) |
| Intraoperative T4b involvement | 1 | 1 |
| Withdrew consent before surgery | 0 | 2 |
| **Total** | **3** | **8** |

The SAP excludes missing values/outliers without special processing and assigns handling of discontinuations/deviations to case review. This supplement does not provide an all-randomized sensitivity analysis resolving the consequences of the post-randomization exclusions. Do not attach the event-rate denominators to 850 or call this a strict all-randomized ITT result.

## Verified outcome cells

These were checked against the rendered Supplement 8 table and its footnotes, not only the abstract:

| Outcome | ICG, n=422 | Control, n=417 | Reported estimate |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Grade A+B+C leakage, primary | 32 (7.6%) | 49 (11.8%) | RR 0.645 (95% CI 0.422–0.987); RD −0.042 (−0.082 to −0.002); P=.041 |
| Grade B+C leakage, secondary | 20 (4.7%) | 34 (8.2%) | RR 0.581 (0.340–0.993); P=.044 |
| Reoperation, secondary | 2 (0.5%) | 10 (2.4%) | RR 0.198 (0.044–0.897); P=.021 |
| Postoperative complications | 82 (19.4%) | 89 (21.3%) | RR 0.910 (0.697–1.190); P=.492 |

The secondary analyses are unadjusted for multiple comparisons. Reoperation was decided by individual surgeons; the protocol did not specify re-laparotomy indications. Neither these cells nor the Clavien table establish a reduction in all postoperative morbidity or mortality. There were no Clavien V events and one IVa event in the ICG group.

ICG assessments were good in 411, poor in five, and absent in six. **Ten patients** had additional resection, not all 11 with poor/absent perfusion; median additional length was 4 cm (IQR 1.5–10). The study's 60-second fluorescence rule, camera settings and 12.5-mg ICG regimen are specific protocol elements, not a universal validated tissue-viability threshold, device rule or current drug-label dosing recommendation.

## Ascertainment, timing and external validity

- Asymptomatic patients with a diverting stoma underwent contrast enema; asymptomatic patients without a stoma did not require the same routine imaging. Suspected clinical leakage prompted imaging, with CT allowed if contrast examination was not possible. Thus the opportunity to discover grade A leakage depended on diversion status. Stomas occurred in 213/422 (50.5%) versus 229/417 (54.9%); this numerical difference was not statistically significant (P=.213). This is a potential ascertainment limitation, not proof that it explains the treatment effect.
- The latest protocol permits the 30-day assessment window to extend **five days**; the amendment changed earlier “±5” language to “+5.” Some plain-text passages say 30 days. The protocol permits the scheduled evaluation to end at discharge if pre-discharge contrast examination has already been performed and discharge occurs within the allowed period. It also directs recording of postoperative adverse events through 30 days in another section. Do not assume every subject received identical post-discharge 30-day surveillance; the document contains these differing instructions.
- The SAP version is dated August 27, 2021, after the recorded last patient/last visit (March 26, 2021), but before database closure (September 22, 2021). It should not be described as an SAP prospectively fixed before enrollment. No claim is made here that investigators had seen unblinded outcome analyses before preparing it.
- The SAP defines the age subgroup as **75 years or older versus younger than 75**. This agrees with the main forest-plot threshold reported by the parent reviewer, but conflicts with the main text's 70-year threshold. Subgroup analyses are explicitly exploratory. Do not infer a validated age-based treatment selection rule or interaction from nominal subgroup estimates.
- Enrollment was restricted to rectal cancer within 12 cm of the anal verge, clinical stage 0–III, ECOG 0–2 and planned anastomosis, in Japanese centers using standardized Stryker 1588/1688 equipment and experienced surgeons. Planned combined organ resection, multiple anastomoses, severe comorbidity, obstruction and intra-abdominal infection were among exclusions. The trial is not direct evidence for ileal urinary diversion, cystectomy bowel anastomoses, bowel re-harvest or multivisceral irradiated reconstruction.
- Most patients had no preoperative oncological therapy (89.1% and 86.8%); only 21 and 30 had chemoradiation, plus one short-course radiation patient per arm. Broad conclusions about heavily irradiated populations are unsupported.
- The study was investigator led and funded by Stryker Japan. The protocol describes separation of the company from conduct/analysis/reporting; this is an author-reported role statement, not an independent funding audit.

## Source inconsistencies kept internal

Beyond the main conclusion/age-threshold conflicts, the supplementary file contains staging-definition/translation mistakes and minor numerical ambiguities. The intraperitoneal-drain row visually prints 397/422 versus 406/417, an RD interval excluding zero, but P=.185. Pathological stage cells total 421 in the ICG group rather than 422 without an explanatory missing-value footnote. These are not needed for the site summary and should not be copied. The protocol's “non-allergenic” or “hypoallergenic” characterization of ICG should also not be repeated; the current pharmacology hub supplies label-based hypersensitivity precautions.

## Editorial disposition

A concise site summary can state that EssentiAL found lower 30-day all-grade leak rates in its 839-patient mITT rectal-cancer population (7.6% versus 11.8%; RR 0.65, 95% CI 0.42–0.99), while keeping its rectal setting and exclusions distinct from urinary reconstruction. Preserve the overall randomized evidence context. No need to reproduce the internal source inconsistencies or every secondary endpoint in the clinical page. The six-point power target must not be presented as an unmet superiority margin.
