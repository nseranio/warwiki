## Corrections

- Removed the false assertion that an inferior-vesical artery contains the cavernous nerves and that nerve-sparing cystectomy preserves the superior-vesical pedicle. Replaced fixed ureter distances/crossings and incision rules with variable anatomical relationships.
- Corrected internal-pudendal course, separated ovarian/testicular arterial routes, and corrected the dual pudendal supply and usual inferior pedicle of the Martius flap.
- Separated arterial/venous corona mortis; the systematic-review denominator is hemipelves, not patients. Removed a fixed-distance safety implication and the obsolete 20–30% estimate for all crossing vessels.
- Corrected pelvic/ovarian vein valve and cross-communication claims; distinguished venous reflux/obstruction from uterine-artery embolization. Removed unsupported left-predominant postpartum ovarian-vein thrombosis, routine left-only treatment and image-only diagnostic implications.
- Removed universal DVC timing, blind foraminal tack instructions, an assurance of negligible internal-iliac ischemia, and a single-mechanism assertion for vertebral metastasis. Corrected external-iliac tributary cadaver versus venous-system denominators.
- Replaced the unsupported multi-organ oncologic dissection-template table with a bounded regional orientation and current EAU penile/urethral drainage descriptions. The original glans-to-pelvis shortcut was misleading.
- Removed inappropriate clinical extrapolations from the small cornual-temperature experiment, a Cochrane **protocol** for rectal-artery embolization, and a 30-man single-arm zotarolimus-stent feasibility study used as support for penile bypass.

## Complete sources actually read

### Corona mortis systematic review

[Noussios et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.14740/jocmr4062), [PMC7011932](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7011932/). Root read the entire 22,457-character article, both complete tables, all 24 references and disclosures, and inspected all five actual figures: anatomical drawing, selection flowchart, overall forest plot and separate arterial/venous forests. No separate attachment was identified in the article. No original participant data or all antecedent anatomy studies were independently read.

Thirteen studies comprise 1,455 **hemipelves**, including cadaveric, imaging and operative observations. Overall pooled prevalence is approximately 46%, with I² 95%; use an approximate orientation rather than a precise population prediction. Actual forest plots support arterial approximately 25% and venous approximately 42% in different study subsets; they are not complementary percentages from one denominator and are not used as a bedside probability table.

Source defects: Table 1's “patients” column mixes units actually described as hemipelves; the flowchart labels 41 as records after duplicate removal while the next box has 424 screened (465 identified minus 41 duplicates); the bilateral estimate/CI is internally inconsistent between narrative and table; study years and some geographic summaries differ; cited sling papers do not support a universally safe retropubic distance. These source defects were not copied. Correct authors are **Noussios, Galanis, Chatzis et al.**; the initial draft's incorrect coauthors were caught and corrected by independent metadata checks.

### Vulvovaginal reconstructive anatomy

[Yavagal et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0031-1281481), [PMC3312145](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3312145/). Root read all 38,798 extracted characters, all 32 references and four actual figures, including the deep-perineal artery diagram. No separate appendix/supplement was identified. Used for stable pudendal and vulvovaginal vascular relationships, not as current treatment guidance.

Do not reproduce historical claims that a labial width alone indicates surgery, that perineal repair restores every levator defect, or that named fascial layers have one universally agreed interpretation. Its introductory obturator-foramen bone attribution and some neural/fascial wording are imprecise; these were not adopted. No cosmetic recommendation or outcome estimate was imported.

### Martius technique

[Wilson, Pillay and Greenwell, 2017](https://doi.org/10.21037/tau.2017.04.38), [PMC5522801](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5522801/). Root read all 23,576 extracted characters, all 35 references and **all 13 actual operative/follow-up photographs**. No separate supplement was identified. Supports superior external-pudendal/inferior internal-pudendal supply and preservation of the inferior base in the commonly described flap.

The article is an expert technical review, not proof of routine interposition benefit or zero donor morbidity. Its observational comparisons do not establish equivalence or improvement from adding a flap; complication and sexual-function results have selection/follow-up limitations. References 7/30 and 8/32 duplicate sources, rather than adding independent evidence. Its strong claims that the flap definitely improves outcomes and that complications are extremely low were not copied into the anatomy page.
