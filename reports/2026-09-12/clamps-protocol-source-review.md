# Clamp handling, protocol attribution and related clinical corrections

September 12, 2026. This is a scoped correction batch within the continuing whole-site review. Nine original/current MDX files were fully read: six clamps, female testosterone, genital lymphedema and anal anatomy. Gracilis, omental and peritoneal received targeted companion corrections only; their whole-page reads remain open. A full MDX read does not mean every cited paper is read or the page is clinically certified.

## Changes

- **Allis, Babcock, Kocher, Kelly, Crile and Halsted mosquito:** corrected recognizable jaw features and model variation. Removed unsupported force rankings, safe ratchet notches, pressure-free fenestrations, blind spreading, tubing/suture handling and open/robotic or hemostat/microvascular equivalence. The Allis IUD trial remains limited to its actual cervical-stabilization setting. Preserved and visually checked the three existing public-domain instrument photographs; no new images or source PDFs enter the build.
- **Female testosterone:** corrected APHRODITE's three groups and efficacy/safety windows, Braunstein's single four-arm trial, applied versus delivered dose, monitoring intervals, contributor treatment and safety certainty. Replaced contraindications attributed to a Cochrane protocol with the applicable ISSWSH guidance. Removed an uncited older review from the public bibliography and renumbered consistently; the original entry remains in the access register.
- **Genital lymphedema:** the exercise publication is a protocol and cannot support liposuction effectiveness. Removed unvalidated GLS bands assigning operations, clarified that limb evidence is not genital comparative evidence, and removed causal rankings from heterogeneous surgical series. Previous CHASCIP/BLOOM full-method gaps remain open.
- **Anal anatomy:** corrected the anorectal junction versus peritoneal reflection, overly absolute sensory/RAIR statements, simplified EAS nerve map, additive pressure percentages and tumor-workup shortcuts. Corrected LIFT's transsphincteric indication, automatic repeat sphincteroplasty, indiscriminate first-line trigger-point injections, hemorrhoid grading and stapled-hemorrhoidopexy positioning. The page remains an anatomical overview; detailed imaging, neonatal surgery, physiological norms and all inherited source claims are not completely revalidated. Removed one now-uncited cancer review and renumbered; its original reference is preserved.
- **Three flap companions:** explicitly identified the VVF Cochrane protocol. Omental also no longer calls itself the universal first-line flap for USF based on Escandón's 56-patient retrospective series. These are paragraph-level corrections, not full-page reviews.

## Source limitations that affected interpretation

Wu used six young cadavers and proposed a different subdivision of the sphincter complex; its functional interpretation is not a validated in-vivo surgical rule. Its correction changes supplement filenames/captions. Chiarioni randomized 157 patients, with stronger benefit in the tender-levator subgroup; no sham arm, unblinded patients/therapist, and only six-month responders contacted at 12 months limit generalization. It does not establish an injection or proctalgia-fugax treatment algorithm. ASCRS recommendations distinguish strong/conditional recommendations and evidence quality; the site does not turn them into universal procedure mandates. Testosterone trial safety cannot settle uncommon long-term breast or cardiovascular events, and applied milligrams must not be equated with patch-delivered micrograms.

References removed from public pages are not considered reviewed or resolved by deletion. Entries without new source credit explicitly remain in the verification queue; earlier domain reports may document narrower reads.

## Quarterly screening repair

The [protocol classification record](cochrane-protocol-classification.json) logs seven confirmed protocols among 20 candidate Cochrane identifiers. Absence of a `.pub` suffix was only a search cue. The confirmation used complete indexed abstracts, not title guessing. VVF's 14 uses include three corrected flap paragraphs and 11 already-qualified references; antibiotic and endometrial protocol references were already identified and did not need blanket replacement.

The collector now retains a boolean when the indexed abstract explicitly declares a Cochrane review protocol. The signal survives normalization and later shortlist/Markdown screening without retaining or publishing the abstract. A real CD016320-shaped regression verifies exclusion, preserves correction/retraction override, and avoids excluding completed reviews merely mentioning a protocol. Missing abstracts remain a documented limitation; screening is not editorial verification. Evidence remains quarterly, with priority for major guidelines, large trials and high-yield systematic reviews.
