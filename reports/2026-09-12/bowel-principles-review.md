# Bowel reconstruction principles: continuing source review

Status: in progress, September 12, 2026. Four original MDX pages were read completely: bowel handling/injury management, reoperative bowel harvest, bowel anastomosis, and stoma site marking. This does not certify every clinical claim or cited paper. The original bibliography contains 98 entries. Sources downloaded to temporary storage are not automatically counted as read.

## Completed source reads

- **WSES bowel injury 2022**, DOI 10.1186/s13017-022-00418-y: complete 15-page main manuscript, six extracted tables and 125 bibliography entries; Table 4 additionally visually checked. Original studies cited by the guideline are not thereby fully read. Table 4's Schnueriger percentages appear to use the 12 leaks as denominator rather than each anastomosis group; avoid repeating them as location-specific leak incidence. Other inconsistencies include a Witzke total not equalling its displayed groups and differing recommendation grades between body and summary.
- **ASRM adhesions 2019**, DOI 10.1016/j.fertnstert.2019.06.027: complete six-page committee opinion, 62 bibliography entries and sole Figure 1 visually checked. Adhesion-score benefits cannot be presented as demonstrated prevention of pain, infertility or all bowel obstruction.
- **Seprafilm IFU, 2023-09-28**: complete text, four extracted tables and five references; graphics not separately inspected. Contraindication is wrapping directly around a fresh intestinal suture/staple line; do not soften this to merely caution. Reoperation for adhesive SBO and all SBO are different endpoints/denominators.
- **AGA short bowel syndrome 2022**, DOI 10.1016/j.cgh.2022.05.032: complete 12-page PDF including main text, two main tables, 64 references, supplementary nutrition table, all three main figures and supplementary algorithm. Table 1 visually checked: PDF text extraction changes micrograms to milligrams (B12, calcitriol, selenium, chromium); never use the extracted units. Supplementary algorithm has mismatched table-number pointers; use the content rather than reproduce those labels. Anatomic SBS (usually 200 cm or less) and functional intestinal failure requiring intravenous support are distinct. Residual anatomy, colon continuity and disease matter; no 180-cm guarantee or fixed ileocecal-valve centimetre equivalence.
- **IntAct 2025**, DOI 10.1016/S2468-1253(25)00101-3: complete 12-page primary manuscript, three tables, 31 bibliography entries and both figures visually checked. Complete 46-page supplemental PDF now read: all 20 supplementary tables, all nine supplementary figures visually inspected, and the health-economics analysis plan including its analysis table and references. 766 randomized, 698 complete cases in primary analysis; clinical grades B/C leak 36/343 versus 54/355, adjusted OR 0.667 (0.419–1.060), P=.087. Significant all-grade secondary endpoint does not convert primary endpoint into positive superiority. Postrandomization exclusions include permanent stomas, with unequal numbers. Rectal-cancer population excluded multivisceral/extended resections, prior unrelated pelvic radiation and immunosuppression; avoid claiming proven benefit for ileoileal reconstruction in urinary diversion.
- **EAST 2016 penetrating extraperitoneal rectal guideline**: complete online main text, all 19 bibliography entries and four tables read; figure remains unviewed. Table 4 appears to repeat the presacral-drainage header despite presenting washout data; some table/body percentages differ, so avoid repeating those exact pooled percentages. Page says update in process, not a published 2026 replacement. Conditional diversion and avoiding routine presacral drainage/distal washout apply to nondestructive penetrating extraperitoneal injury.

## Original-reference abstract review

All available indexed abstracts from all four original pages (98 references: 37 handling, 24 repeat harvest, 27 anastomosis, 10 stoma) have been read. Several DOI lookups intermittently failed or references lacked identifiers; those gaps are not declared absent publications. No abstract-only item is marked full text. Important checks: Cotter 2017 is a small retrospective comparison (33 vs 10), and Hebert 2026 found no worse 30-day outcomes with concurrent colonic anastomosis; albumin 3.2 is an observational risk association, not a treat-to nutritional target. Somani 2007's 47% incontinence and 24% regret belong to the small clam-enterocystoplasty subgroup, not all urinary diversions. Fung 2011 excluded radiation/neurogenic cases and cannot guarantee normal bowel function after repeat harvest.

Removed unsupported absolutes on thermal injury, peritonitis, forced diversion, mesenteric adjacency, prior radiation and short bowel thresholds. Corrected rectal peritoneal anatomy and mismatched citations (adhesion barrier study used for serosal repair; abdominal fascial closure Cochrane used for intestinal leak). Distinguished colorectal ICG findings from urinary-diversion bowel anastomosis. Clinician sign-off remains unchanged; public metadata stays simple.

## Further completed reads

- **WOCN/AUA/ASCRS 2021 stoma-positioning statement**, DOI 10.1097/WON.0000000000000820: complete four-page main text, bibliography, all 13 key points and both figures visually read. The separate quick-reference guide was not read. Replaces the old 2015 statement in the page.
- **WSES open abdomen 2018**, DOI 10.1186/s13017-018-0167-4: complete main text, two tables and 157 references read. Two figure graphics remain unviewed; attempted image downloads returned HTML. Re-exploration 24–48 hours, protected viscera, early closure and staged source control verified. Old biological-mesh recommendations should not be generalized beyond their setting or ignore newer RCTs.
- **WTA rectal injury 2023**, DOI 10.1097/TA.0000000000004093: complete 25-page accepted-manuscript text, all 45 references and sole table read through the AAST-hosted PDF. Figure 1 graphic not visible in tool screenshot output; caption only. Published 2023, not a 2026 update despite search crawl metadata. Accessible nondestructive extraperitoneal transanal repair without diversion differs from EAST's earlier conditional diversion recommendation; evidence is largely observational/expert guidance.
- **ICG-COLORAL 2025**, DOI 10.1001/jamasurg.2025.0006: complete eight-page primary manuscript, three tables, all 19 bibliography entries and sole CONSORT graphic visually read. All three supplemental files now fully read: 14-page original protocol, 12-page supplement with all ten tables, and one-page data sharing statement. Separate visual abstract not inspected. Overall 33/567 versus 45/569 leak, OR .73 (.48–1.13), P=.16; did not demonstrate primary superiority. The as-treated left-side signal is not a prespecified randomized whole-cohort benefit. Excludes planned anastomoses below peritoneal fold and several complex settings.
- **Slieker 2013**, DOI 10.1001/2013.jamasurg.33: complete author-thesis Chapter 4 (printed pp45–70), all seven parts of Table 1, 148 bibliography entries and both figure texts/captions read; both actual figure graphics separately inspected. Table lists 2.9% vs28.6% for Goligher1970 while narrative says fivefold; do not copy an exact multiplier without original verification. Narrative claims no stricture difference in Cochrane while that Cochrane reports more strictures with circular stapling. Continuous-suture level1b table claim in WARWIKI was false: the source specifically states no RCT for this isolated comparison. Replaced universal ideal recipe with technique principles and explicit uncertainty.

## Additional completed primary reads and limits

- **AVOID 2024**, DOI 10.1016/S2468-1253(24)00198-5: all 11 published main pages plus repository cover, three tables, 40 references and sole CONSORT figure read. The separate appendix remains unread. Primary clinical leak comparison was nonsignificant (32/463 vs 42/468; RR 0.77, 95% CI 0.50–1.20). Subgroup findings remain labeled. Its reference 7 concerns thyroid/parathyroid imaging, not intestinal perfusion; no intestinal claim was based on that reference.
- **EssentiAL 2023**, DOI 10.1097/SLA.0000000000005907: complete main article, 43 references and both actual figures read. Figure 2 uses age 75 while the main text describes age 70; no subgroup claim based on that mismatch was imported. The observed positive primary comparison must not be mistaken for failure merely because it did not achieve the six-percentage-point difference assumed for power. The complete 256,829-character supplementary document has now been independently read, including both English protocols, all amendment tables, SAP and Supplements 3–9. All unique embedded figure content and final result tables were visually checked. See [the complete supplementary review](essential-supplement-review.md) for exact access, analysis timing, exclusions, ascertainment and source discrepancies.

- **Burch 2000:** complete abstract and selected original methods/results read. Entire original tables/figure and main paper still need access. The unit is 125 patients and 132 anastomoses, not 132 patients.
- **Cochrane colorectal 2012 and ileocolic 2011:** complete available abstracts read; the former also has selected main-text passages read. Entire included-study tables, forest plots, appendices and full reviews remain gaps. Their populations are not interchangeable with isolated ileoileal harvest.

## Primary-source internal inconsistencies retained in the audit only

ICG-COLORAL's main article and CONSORT use 567/569 randomized participants, while supplemental tables variously print 567 or 568 control/other denominators. The site uses the main result and makes no new estimate from the inconsistent supplement. IntAct's supplement has several descriptive label/percentage discrepancies and economic estimates that should not be re-presented without clarification; these were not imported. Slieker's composite preferred technique is not separate randomized proof for each individual component. These checks are reasons to retain source-specific uncertainty rather than reproduce a published table automatically.
