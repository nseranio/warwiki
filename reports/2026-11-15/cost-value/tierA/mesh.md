## Cost and Value

Economic evaluations of mesh-augmented anterior repair found native-tissue repair to have the highest probability of being cost-effective, and a Medicare-based decision analysis found mesh kit repair more costly than anterior colporrhaphy. In the economic evaluation of the PROSPECT trials (35 UK hospitals, trial-based incremental costs and a 5-year Markov model, published 2016), incremental costs versus standard repair were £363 (95% CI −£32 to £758) with mesh inlay and £565 (95% CI £180 to £950) with biologic graft, with incremental QALYs of 0.071 (95% CI −0.004 to 0.145) and 0.039 (95% CI −0.041 to 0.120); the Markov model showed standard repair with the highest probability of cost-effectiveness, with considerable uncertainty.<sup>[[34]](#ref34)</sup> A Markov model of primary anterior prolapse repair from the UK National Health Service perspective (published 2020) likewise found non-mesh repair to have the highest probability of being cost-effective, and its conclusions were robust to the effectiveness, cost and utility inputs.<sup>[[35]](#ref35)</sup> A 5-year Markov model of mesh-augmented versus non-mesh anterior repair (published 2013) estimated an ICER of £15 million per QALY gained with mesh-augmented versus non-mesh repair and found no plausible input that made mesh cost-effective by conventional criteria.<sup>[[36]](#ref36)</sup> A decision analysis using Medicare reimbursement and hospital costs (published 2011) estimated $3,380 for non-kit mesh repair, $3,461 for anterior colporrhaphy and $4,678 for mesh kit repair; anterior colporrhaphy was the more cost-effective option when its recurrence was below 20% or mesh extrusion was above 25%.<sup>[[37]](#ref37)</sup>

Against abdominal mesh, a single-institution cohort of 120 transvaginal mesh repairs and 106 robotic sacrocolpopexies (cases from January 2012 to December 2013, total direct and indirect institutional costs, amortized robot purchase and maintenance included, published 2016) found mean total costs of $9,675 with robotic sacrocolpopexy and $6,718 with transvaginal mesh (P &lt; .001). Robotic patients more often had concomitant hysterectomy (58.5% versus 26.7%), and operative time was longer with the robot.<sup>[[38]](#ref38)</sup>

For uterovaginal prolapse, the economic evaluation alongside the SUPeR trial (168 postmenopausal women, US health care sector perspective, 5-year horizon, 2020 US dollars, published 2026) found costs of $11,631 (95% CI $8,257 to $15,005) with vaginal mesh sacrospinous hysteropexy and $12,622 (95% CI $10,172 to $15,072) with vaginal hysterectomy and uterosacral ligament suspension (P = 0.64), with 3.77 QALYs in each group (P = 0.99). The point estimate was dominant for hysteropexy (lower cost and more QALYs), but the probability that hysteropexy was cost-effective compared with hysterectomy was 53% at a willingness-to-pay threshold of $150,000 per QALY.<sup>[[39]](#ref39)</sup>
