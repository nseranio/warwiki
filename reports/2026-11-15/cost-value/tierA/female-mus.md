## 2. Section text (home page)

## Cost and Value

Models comparing retropubic and transobturator slings reached different conclusions. A US Markov model using Medicare resource-based relative value scale costs (published 2014), built from 21 randomized trials, gave average costs of $9,579 for the retropubic sling and $9,017 for the transobturator sling, with 6.275 and 6.272 QALYs; cost-effectiveness ratios were $1,527 and $1,438 per QALY, and the authors judged the transobturator sling more cost-effective, with results sensitive to device cost, surgeon fee, efficacy, operative time and length of stay.<sup>[[46]](#ref46)</sup> The UK ESTER evidence synthesis and Markov microsimulation of nine operations, from the NHS and personal social services perspective (published 2019 and 2020), found the retropubic sling the most cost-effective over 10-year and lifetime horizons; over a lifetime it was on average the least costly and most effective, with substantial uncertainty, and the retropubic sling and the traditional (autologous) sling had the highest probabilities of cost-effectiveness across willingness-to-pay thresholds.<sup>[[47]](#ref47)[[48]](#ref48)</sup>

For single-incision mini-slings, the SIMS trial's economic evaluation (600 women, 21 UK hospitals, published 2022) found no difference in costs (−£6, 95% CI −£228 to £208) or QALYs (0.005, 95% CI −0.068 to 0.073) between adjustable anchored mini-slings and standard midurethral slings, with a 56% probability that mini-slings were cost-effective at £20,000 per QALY; follow-up beyond 3 years was not available.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> An earlier UK within-trial analysis (137 women randomized to the Ajust mini-sling under local anaesthesia or the TVT-O under general anaesthesia, published 2013) found the mini-sling £142.41 less costly (95% CI −£316.99 to £32.17), a saving of £48,419 per QALY lost, with a 94% probability of cost savings to the health service up to 1 year after placement. Including the earlier return to work in the mini-sling group, the saving was £477 (95% CI −£823.65 to −£129.63).<sup>[[49]](#ref49)</sup>

Against other operations, a US Markov model with Medicare resource-based relative value scale costs (published 2013) found the tension-free vaginal tape more cost-effective than Burch colposuspension at 10 years, $1,495 versus $1,824 per QALY; Burch became the more cost-effective option if the probability of success after the tape fell below 42%.<sup>[[50]](#ref50)</sup> A 10-year Markov model from the health care system perspective (2005 US dollars) estimated $9,320 for Burch and $8,081 for the tape, with 7.260 and 7.248 QALYs, an incremental cost-effectiveness ratio of $98,755 per QALY gained with Burch versus the tape, against a threshold of $50,000.<sup>[[51]](#ref51)</sup> A 1-year decision tree comparing autologous rectus fascial sling with retropubic midurethral sling (published 2023) found the midurethral sling cost $2,348.94 against $2,114.06 from the hospital perspective, with 0.82 versus 0.80 QALYs and an ICER of $17,452 per QALY gained with the midurethral sling versus the fascial sling; from the health care perspective the fascial sling cost $4,656.63 against $4,630.47 and was less effective, so the midurethral sling had lower cost and more QALYs.<sup>[[52]](#ref52)</sup> A US model of stress incontinence treatment pathways over 2 years (2019 US dollars) found pessary, pelvic floor muscle therapy and midurethral sling to be the cost-effective strategies; no other surgical option was cost-effective, and if midurethral slings were unavailable, every other surgical option was dominated by pelvic floor muscle therapy.<sup>[[53]](#ref53)</sup>

## 2b. Sibling page text (SIMS)

### Cost and Value

In the SIMS trial's economic evaluation (600 women, 21 UK hospitals, published 2022), adjustable anchored mini-slings and standard midurethral slings did not differ in costs (−£6, 95% CI −£228 to £208) or QALYs (0.005, 95% CI −0.068 to 0.073), and the probability that mini-slings were cost-effective was 56% at £20,000 per QALY.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Other cost comparisons of midurethral slings are on the [female slings page](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/female-slings-suspensions#cost-and-value).

## 6. Notes

- Retraction check (efetch XML) on 23946119, 30929658, 32532771, 36520097, 24053310, 23773373, 17618760, 36735421, 34314554: no retraction, expression of concern or erratum flags. Crossref returned 200 for every DOI used (Jia 36735421 was checked separately and is also clean; its DOI returned 200).
- Industry ties: no affiliation with Bard, Ethicon, Coloplast or Boston Scientific in any record. SIMS and Boyers studies carry UK public (NIHR HTA, Chief Scientist Office) grant fields only; Boyers tested a Bard device (Ajust) but its record names no maker funding, so none is stated. Chang, Jia, Laudano, Seklehner and Wu records show no funding statements; Wu and Jia COI statements empty or "no conflicts".
- Seklehner and Laudano abstracts give no price year or explicit perspective, so "published 2014" and "published 2013" are used; Seklehner's horizon is not in the abstract and is omitted. Jia's abstract gives "$" but no country, so none is named. Wu's abstract gives "2005 US dollars" so the year and country are stated.
- Seklehner and Laudano ratios are average cost-effectiveness ratios (cost per QALY for each option), not ICERs, and are written as such.
- Rejected: Wallace 2026 cost-minimization (PFMT, pessary, sling; Medicare claims; PMID 42849022), because its DOI 10.1097/SPV.0000000000001931 returns 404 on Crossref and doi.org (online-ahead-of-print); revisit once the DOI registers, it would suit a sentence on sling versus conservative first-line cost. Ross 2011 (TOT vs TVT cost-utility) is a protocol, no results. Sassani 2024, Wang 2023 and Richardson 2013 (concomitant or timed sling at prolapse surgery) belong with the hub's "Concomitant Sling at Prolapse Repair" decision, which already carries Mou 2022; left out per the brief. Kunkle 2015 (bulking agent vs MUS), Casteleijn 2023 (bulking vs MUS) and Harvie 2021 (behavioral therapy with sling, MUA) address other decisions. Cody 2003 and Kilonzo 2004 (TVT cost-effectiveness) are old and not comparative among the sling routes. PORTRET (TVT vs PFMT) returned only the 2009 protocol. ESTER and Javanbakht are one model, reported twice; they are cited together as a single study.
- The hub does not discuss PFMT or pessary as comparators, only Burch, fascial sling and bulking in "Mesh-Free and Lower-Mesh Alternatives"; Chang's sentence names them because the model includes them. Owner does not offer Burch, but the public page keeps it, so the Burch sentences are kept.
