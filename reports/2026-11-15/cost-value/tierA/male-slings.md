## 2. Section text (home page)

## Cost and Value

The two economic analyses comparing a male sling with an AUS reached opposite conclusions on cost, in different settings and populations. In the MASTER trial (380 men with urodynamic stress incontinence after prostate surgery, 28 UK NHS centres, economic outcome at 24 months after randomisation, published 2022), the male sling cost less than the AUS but gave a smaller QALY gain, an incremental cost-effectiveness ratio equal to a saving of £425,870 for each QALY lost with sling versus AUS. The probability that the sling was cost-effective was 99% at a willingness-to-pay threshold of £30,000 per QALY.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

For severe post-prostatectomy stress incontinence, a Markov model with Monte Carlo simulation from a Canadian provincial publicly funded health care perspective (2016 Canadian dollars, 1-year cycles, horizon up to 10 years, 1.5% annual discount rate, transition probabilities and utilities from published literature and expert opinion) gave a 10-year mean cost of $14,228 and 7.58 QALYs for the AUS and $18,938 and 6.43 QALYs for the AdVance sling. The AUS saved $4,710 with 1.15 additional QALYs (lower cost and more QALYs), and it remained the most cost-effective option at a willingness-to-pay threshold of $50,000.<sup>[[41]](#ref41)</sup>
