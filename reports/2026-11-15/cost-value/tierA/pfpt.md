## 2. Section text (pelvic-floor-pt.mdx)

## Cost and Value

Group-based PFMT cost less than individual PFMT with similar effect in older women. In an economic evaluation alongside a Canadian noninferiority trial (362 women aged 60 years or older with stress or mixed incontinence, 12 weekly 1-hour sessions with one physiotherapist per woman or per eight women, 1-year horizon, 2019 Canadian dollars), group PFMT cost CA$914 less per woman from the participant perspective (95% CI −970 to −863) and CA$509 less from the provider perspective (95% CI −523 to −496), at least 60% less than individual treatment, with minor differences in leakage reduction and quality-adjusted life years.<sup>[[59]](#ref59)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

Adding biofeedback to PFMT did not change cost or quality-adjusted life years significantly. In the OPAL trial (600 women with stress or mixed incontinence randomized to biofeedback-mediated or basic PFMT in UK community and outpatient care), biofeedback PFMT was not significantly more expensive at 24 months and was not associated with significantly more quality-adjusted life years, with a 48% probability of cost-effectiveness at a threshold of £20,000 per quality-adjusted life year.<sup>[[60]](#ref60)[[29]](#ref29)</sup>

For initial treatment of stress incontinence, a US decision tree model compared continence pessary, PFMT and midurethral sling over 12 months (Medicare costs, 2012 US dollars). The sling cost $32,132 per QALY gained versus PFMT against an ICER threshold of $50,000; PFMT remained acceptable when subjective cure exceeded 35%, and the sling stopped being the preferred strategy when subjective cure exceeded 40.5% with PFMT or 43.5% with a pessary.<sup>[[61]](#ref61)</sup>

Two Swedish cost-utility analyses alongside randomized trials (1-year societal perspective) evaluated app-delivered PFMT. In 123 women with stress incontinence, annual costs were €547.0 with the app and €482.4 with no treatment, with annual QALY gains of 0.0101 and 0.0016; the extra cost per QALY gained with the app versus no treatment ranged from −€2,425.7 to €14,870.6 across the analyses.<sup>[[62]](#ref62)</sup> In 122 women with mixed or urgency incontinence, an app combining PFMT and bladder training cost €738.42 a year against €605.82 for an information app, with annual QALY gains of 0.0152 and 0.0037; the base-case ICER was €11,770.52, ranging from −€9,303.78 to €22,307.67 in sensitivity analyses. Two authors of that analysis co-founded and hold shares in the company that markets the app.<sup>[[63]](#ref63)</sup>

A 2024 Ontario health technology assessment identified six cost-utility analyses of PFMT for prolapse or urinary incontinence. For women with stress incontinence, PFMT was likely cost-effective compared with other nonsurgical interventions; for men with incontinence after prostate surgery, it was likely not cost-effective compared with standard care; for women with prolapse, cost-effectiveness compared with no active treatment was uncertain. The average cost of PFMT was approximately $763 per patient.<sup>[[64]](#ref64)</sup>
