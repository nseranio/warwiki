## 2. Section text

## Cost and Value

Trial-based economic evaluations found methenamine hippurate and daily antibiotic prophylaxis similar in cost and QALYs, and cranberry more costly than trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole. In the economic evaluation alongside ALTAR (205 women in the analysis, 8 UK centres, NHS perspective, 18-month within-trial analysis, 2019 pounds), methenamine hippurate cost £40 less than daily antibiotic prophylaxis (95% CI −£684 to £603) and added 0.014 QALYs (95% CI −0.05 to 0.07); the probability that methenamine hippurate was cost-effective ranged from 51% to 67% over the values considered for an additional QALY. A sensitivity analysis that added a cost for antimicrobial resistance (£741 per antibiotic prescription, inflated to 2019 prices; £1,471 per participant with antibiotic prophylaxis and £1,111 with methenamine hippurate) gave a probability of 69% with no value placed on a QALY and 76% at £20,000 per QALY.<sup>[[49]](#ref49)</sup> In the Dutch trial of cranberry extract 500 mg twice daily versus trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole 480 mg nightly in premenopausal women (109 and 98 women in the analysis, economic evaluation alongside the trial over 12 months, 2009 euros), total costs were €627 with cranberry and €378 with trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (mean difference €249, 95% CI 70 to 516), with 4.3 versus 2.7 UTIs and 0.76 versus 0.80 QALYs, neither difference statistically significant. The authors concluded that cranberry was less effective and more expensive than trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole and not cost-effective, and that the trial could not include the costs of antibiotic resistance.<sup>[[50]](#ref50)</sup>

Across strategies, a Markov chain Monte Carlo model of women with 3 UTIs per year (published 2014; outcomes were treatment efficacy, patient and payer cost, and quality of life) found daily nitrofurantoin the most effective strategy, reducing the rate to 0.4 UTIs per year, and the most expensive to the payer ($821 per year). Daily estrogen, daily cranberry, acupuncture and symptomatic self-treatment produced payer cost savings but were less efficacious; symptomatic self-treatment was the only strategy that saved costs for patients and had the most favorable cost per QALY gained.<sup>[[51]](#ref51)</sup>

For increased water intake, a 10-year Markov model of women with recurrent UTI and low fluid intake (payer perspective, 2019 US dollars; three authors were Danone employees and Danone paid for the modeling) found 1.5 L of additional water daily cost less and added QALYs compared with usual intake in all seven countries modeled. In the US, 10-year costs were $6,937 with usual intake and $4,169 with increased intake, with 7.08 and 7.11 QALYs, which is lower cost and more QALYs. The model took its effect size from a single randomized trial, which reduced cystitis risk by 48%.<sup>[[52]](#ref52)</sup>

- Considered and rejected: van den Hout 2014 (PMID 25180379, cranberry capsules in long-term-care residents, not women with rUTI); Joshi 2025 (PMID 40605628, six-month UK drug-cost comparison from list prices; §15 excludes list prices); Carrión-López 2022 (PMID 35130558, uncontrolled pretest-posttest of Uromune; the abstract does not equate Uromune with the page's MV140) and Ramírez-Sevilla 2023 (PMID 36470711, nonrandomized MV140 groups, total group costs unclear); Wang and LaSala 2021 (PMID 34418350, testing strategies for UTI, not prevention); Hendrickson 2022 (bacteriuria screening before surgery); Stamm 1981 (1981 narrative); NICE evidence review (PMID 39937944, abstract has no figures); IQWiG HTA of herbal remedies (PMID 37023241, German, abstract summarises cranberry as costlier than antibiotics without citable figures).
