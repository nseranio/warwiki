### β3-agonists page

## Cost and Value

Models comparing β3 agonists with antimuscarinics estimated favorable value for the β3 agonists, with results dependent on modeled persistence and adverse effects. A health-care-perspective Monte Carlo microsimulation of 10,000 treatment pathways over 10 years (published 2026) estimated an ICER of $9,132.26 per QALY gained with β3 agonists versus anticholinergics; its dementia-risk inputs projected 1.9% more dementia among anticholinergic users.<sup>[[33]](#ref33)</sup> In an Astellas-affiliated 3-year US Markov model (published 2016), the ICER for mirabegron versus tolterodine immediate release was $59,690 per QALY gained from a commercial health-plan perspective and $66,347 per QALY gained from a Medicare Advantage perspective.<sup>[[34]](#ref34)</sup> An Astellas-affiliated UK NHS 5-year Markov model (published 2015) estimated £367 per QALY gained with mirabegron 50 mg versus solifenacin 10 mg and £15,593 per QALY gained with mirabegron 50 mg versus oxybutynin immediate release 10 mg; at a £20,000-per-QALY willingness-to-pay threshold, the modeled probability of cost-effectiveness ranged from 70.2% versus oxybutynin immediate release 10 mg to 97.8% versus darifenacin 15 mg.<sup>[[35]](#ref35)</sup>

Vibegron was cost-effective against mirabegron in a Urovant-affiliated US lifetime semi-Markov model (published 2022): from the commercial-payer perspective, the ICER was $9,311 per QALY gained with vibegron 75 mg versus mirabegron 50 mg and $118,121 per QALY gained with vibegron versus a market-share anticholinergic basket. From the Medicare perspective, the ICER was $12,154 per QALY gained with vibegron versus mirabegron 50 mg and $60,756 per QALY gained with vibegron versus the anticholinergic basket. At a $50,000-per-QALY willingness-to-pay threshold, vibegron had a 98.6% probability of cost-effectiveness versus mirabegron 50 mg for commercial payers and 100% for Medicare; the model assumed equal persistence for vibegron and mirabegron 50 mg because real-world vibegron persistence data were unavailable.<sup>[[36]](#ref36)</sup>

Against oral drugs after inadequate anticholinergic treatment, an AbbVie-affiliated US Medicare-plus-societal 1-year Markov cost-per-responder model (published 2026) estimated complete dryness in 23.5% with onabotulinumtoxinA versus 10.9% to 12.1% with vibegron, mirabegron, or generic anticholinergics, at $18,646 versus $21,941 to $49,524 per complete responder.<sup>[[37]](#ref37)</sup> The [intradetrusor onabotulinumtoxinA cost section](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/intradetrusor-botox#cost-and-value) covers procedural comparisons with neuromodulation.

### Desmopressin page

### Cost and Value

Models of low-dose desmopressin for nocturia due to nocturnal polyuria estimated additional QALYs at additional cost. A Polish 30-year hybrid partitioned-survival and state-transition model, including public-payer and patient costs (Polish zloty, published 2018), estimated PLN 56.1 thousand per QALY gained with desmopressin oral lyophilisate plus behavioral best supportive care versus supportive care alone, below its PLN 135.5-thousand-per-QALY threshold.<sup>[[40]](#ref40)</sup> In a Ferring-submitted NHS Wales lifetime Markov model of older adults with nocturnal polyuria (pounds sterling, published 2017), low-dose sublingual desmopressin cost an additional £1,278 and produced more QALYs than no treatment, an ICER of £6,989 per QALY gained with desmopressin versus no treatment; the appraisal reported a 95% probability of cost-effectiveness at a £20,000-per-QALY threshold.<sup>[[41]](#ref41)</sup> The Welsh appraisal identified extrapolation of treatment efficacy beyond the observed follow-up and judged the submission's separate cost-minimization comparison with off-label desmopressin preparations inappropriate because it assumed equivalent effects and adverse events.<sup>[[41]](#ref41)</sup>

### Anticholinergics page

## Cost and Value

A 10-year health-care-perspective microsimulation (published 2026) estimated $9,132.26 per QALY gained with β3 agonists versus anticholinergics after modeling dementia risk and treatment sequencing; the [β3-agonist cost section](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/storage-oab/beta3-agonists#cost-and-value) gives the drug comparisons.<sup>[[53]](#ref53)</sup>

## 6. Notes and verification status

A web search did not surface retraction or erratum notices; this is not a substitute for the XML check.
- St Martin (PMID 42149638) reports Foundation of Female Health Awareness support and no maker employment in PubMed affiliations; its dementia and cost effects are model projections, not observed treatment effects. Wielage and Nazir have Astellas-affiliated authors; Chen has Urovant-affiliated authors; Chung has an AbbVie-affiliated author. These ties are visible in PubMed records. The Polish article is not PubMed indexed; its open-access full text names Ferring employees and states Ferring financed the study. The Welsh report explicitly describes a Ferring submission.
- Polish and Welsh analyses use different populations, jurisdictions, comparators, and models; their ratios should stay in separate sentences. The Polish article's abstract gives a PLN 135.5-thousand threshold, but its body says PLN 134.5 thousand; the draft uses the abstract value. The journal page identifies 2018, issue 2, without volume or page range. Neither report states a price year in the inspected text, so the section uses publication years.
- Considered but not cited: Aballéa 2015 (PMID 25491433), a narrower UK mirabegron–tolterodine comparison overlapping Nazir 2015; Murray 2019 (PMID 30511584) and Murray 2023 (PMID 36655745), both Allergan/AbbVie-affiliated best-supportive-care models centered on onabotulinumtoxinA and better suited to the procedure page; Herschorn 2017 (PMID 28515812), a Canada mirabegron–tolterodine model less relevant to the US choice; vibegron budget-impact model (PMID 35881325), which reports plan-level budget rather than comparative patient value; Norwegian ICS 2004 desmopressin poster, conference abstract only; Scottish Medicines Consortium Noqdirna appraisal, which rejected the submitted economic case and provides no competing ICER in its public advice; Noctiva US model, none found in the PubMed search results.
