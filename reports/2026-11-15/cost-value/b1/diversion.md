## 2. Section text

### Principles of Urinary Diversion

## Cost and Value

In the UK NHS iROC within-trial economic analysis (2020–2021 pounds, health-service perspective, 305 patients over 90 days), robotic cystectomy with ICUD cost £1,124 more per patient (95% CI −£576 to £2,824) and gained 0.01124 QALYs (95% CI 0.00391–0.01857) versus open cystectomy, an ICER of £100,008 per QALY gained with robotic cystectomy with ICUD versus open cystectomy.<sup>[[57]](#ref57)</sup> In a single-center Italian randomized trial of 116 patients with a hospital cost analysis (published 2024), robotic cystectomy with ICUD cost more than open cystectomy.<sup>[[58]](#ref58)</sup> In a US private-insurance claims cohort (published 2022), adjusted total health-care costs over 90 days were $67,915 for open cystectomy with ileal conduit, $67,371 for open cystectomy with neobladder, $70,677 for robotic cystectomy with ileal conduit and $70,818 for robotic cystectomy with neobladder.<sup>[[59]](#ref59)</sup>

In US and Israeli 90-day decision-tree models using US literature costs and Israeli hospital administrative costs (published 2022), robotic ICUD with ERAS had lower cost and more QALYs than robotic ECUD with ERAS in both settings.<sup>[[60]](#ref60)</sup>

In a US cohort cost analysis across three integrated health systems (2022 US dollars, 821 ileal conduits and 181 neobladders, 12 months after cystectomy), adjusted mean monthly medical costs by quarter did not differ significantly between ileal conduit and neobladder recipients.<sup>[[61]](#ref61)</sup>

### Intracorporeal Urinary Diversion

### Cost and Value

In a single-center Italian randomized trial with a hospital cost analysis (published 2024), robotic cystectomy with ICUD cost more than open cystectomy.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> See [Cost and Value](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion/urinary-diversion-principles#cost-and-value) for the trial-based iROC economic analysis and comparisons involving ECUD, ileal conduit and neobladder.

- **Verification gate pending:** `pubmed.py` and `curl` could not resolve NCBI/Crossref hosts in this sandbox, and browser access was unavailable. Before applying, run the required PubMed `efetch` XML check for PMIDs 37389878, 38336579, 37145811, 36143218 and 39406639; inspect `Retracted Publication`, `RetractionIn`, `ExpressionOfConcernIn` and `ErratumIn`. No retraction or erratum status is certified here. Resolve all five new DOIs on Crossref and run `npm run refs:new` after applying. No repo file was edited for this draft.
- [iROC economic analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10314306/) specifies UK NHS perspective, 90-day horizon and 2020–2021 price levels; its PubMed abstract supplies the cost and QALY estimates. Its PubMed COI statement names foundation support and unrelated pharmaceutical consulting, not a robotic-device maker.
- [Kord full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9502617/) specifies a 90-day decision tree and QALY outcome; its abstract reports ICUD as less costly and more effective than ECUD. Its PubMed record lists no maker affiliation or manufacturer funding.
- RAZOR primary trial (PMID 29976469): rejected as cost evidence; the trial did not report a usable cost analysis, and its robotic diversion was extracorporeal.
- Kukreja decision model (PMID 30033071): considered but omitted because its PubMed abstract reports an ICER of $2,969 while also reporting $17,000 higher cost and 0.32 additional QALYs, requiring full-text reconciliation.
- Leow US population-based cost analysis (PMID 24491306): considered but omitted because the newer private-insurance cohort reports costs by both approach and diversion type.
- No comparative cost study of continent cutaneous diversion versus ileal conduit with verified usable figures was identified; the neobladder comparison is the available diversion-type evidence here.
