## 2. Section text, ready to paste

## Cost and Value

Economic evaluations of hydrophilic-coated versus uncoated intermittent catheters in spinal cord injury reached different conclusions: a systematic review of eight evaluations found five that judged hydrophilic-coated catheters cost-effective, two that did not, and one that projected lower long-term healthcare costs.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> In a UK NHS lifetime probabilistic Markov model of community self-catheterization (published 2013), the ICER exceeded £54,350 per QALY gained with gel-reservoir versus clean non-coated self-catheterization; the authors judged clean non-coated catheters most cost-effective but advised offering hydrophilic or gel-reservoir catheters as a precaution.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> A lifetime UK NHS Markov model of single-use catheters for people with spinal cord injury or multiple sclerosis, with Coloplast-affiliated authors (published 2023), estimated £3,183 higher cost and 0.55 additional QALYs with hydrophilic-coated versus uncoated catheters, or £5,755 per QALY gained with hydrophilic-coated versus uncoated catheters.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>

Modeled reuse of non-coated intermittent catheters was judged most cost-effective in a 5-year Ontario Ministry of Health and Long-Term Care economic evaluation (2017 Canadian dollars); for patients clinically advised against reuse, the authors judged single-use non-coated catheters most cost-effective.<sup>[[27]](#ref27)</sup> Mixed reusable and single-use catheterization cost £1,348.82 less per participant than single-use only in a 12-month UK payer within-trial cost-utility analysis of MultICath (published 2026), with a QALY difference of −0.001 and an incremental net monetary benefit of £1,328.82 at a willingness-to-pay threshold of £20,000 per QALY.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>

CIC had lower cost and more QALYs than indwelling drainage in a lifetime Canadian public-healthcare Markov model of spinal cord injury-related NLUTD (published 2023): for a modeled 40-year-old, CIC saved CA$2,496 and added 1.96 QALYs versus an indwelling urethral catheter, and saved CA$330 and added 1.77 QALYs versus a suprapubic catheter.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup>
