## Cost and Value

Compared with nonsurgical treatment, sacral neuromodulation (SNM) added QALYs at additional cost in a UK decision analysis using data from 70 patients and 2005/06 national-tariff costs: the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio was £25,070 per QALY gained with SNM versus nonsurgical treatment using direct medical and nonmedical costs, and £12,959 per QALY when indirect nonmedical costs were included.<sup>[[57]](#ref57)</sup> In a Spanish public-health micro-costing evaluation based on a cohort of 93 patients with severe fecal incontinence (published 2023), the long-term ratios for SNM versus symptomatic management were €14,347 per QALY gained from the societal perspective and €28,523 per QALY gained from the health-service-provider perspective.<sup>[[58]](#ref58)</sup>

Offering percutaneous tibial nerve stimulation (PTNS) before SNM was more effective and less costly than immediate SNM in a 5-year UK NHS and Personal Social Services decision model (2012–2013 pounds): savings were £662 to £5,697 per patient across modeled scenarios, and the probability of cost-effectiveness was about 80% at willingness-to-pay thresholds of £20,000 to £30,000 per QALY.<sup>[[59]](#ref59)</sup>

A Swiss cost analysis found higher first-year costs for SNM than anterior sphincter repair and lower estimated 5-year costs than colostomy: from a hospital perspective (2005 euros), SNM cost €15,345 in a prospective series of 36 candidates, compared with €5,327 for anterior sphincter repair in a separate 13-patient departmental series; estimated 5-year cumulative costs were €22,150 for SNM and €33,996 for colostomy.<sup>[[60]](#ref60)</sup> A pathway using SNM more often and dynamic graciloplasty or an artificial bowel sphincter less often had lower modeled costs and more QALYs in a 5-year Dutch Markov analysis using Maastricht University Medical Centre costs (published 2012): the pathway without SNM cost €22,651 and produced 4.14 QALYs per patient, compared with €16,473 and 4.21 QALYs for the SNM pathway; sphincteroplasty use was held equal in both pathways.<sup>[[61]](#ref61)</sup>

Dextranomer-hyaluronate injection cost more than anorectal biofeedback after failed medical and behavioral treatment in the US FIT randomized trial at five centers (published 2026): at 3 months, at least 75% fewer fecal-incontinence episodes occurred in 28/101 injection patients and 29/99 biofeedback patients, while injection cost $1,563 more per patient from the payer perspective and $1,579 more from the societal perspective.<sup>[[62]](#ref62)</sup>
