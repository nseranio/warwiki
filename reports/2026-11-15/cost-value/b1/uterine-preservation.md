## Section text: Sacrospinous Hysteropexy

## Cost and Value

Mixed uterine-preservation procedures cost more than vaginal hysterectomy in the UK VUE Uterine trial: its 12-month within-trial analysis of 563 women from the NHS perspective (2015–16 pounds) found £235 higher cost (95% CI £6 to £464) and a nonsignificant QALY difference of −0.004 (95% CI −0.026 to 0.019) with preservation, with a 15% probability of cost-effectiveness at a willingness-to-pay threshold of £30,000 per QALY.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup> A 5-year Markov model (published 2024) found both sacrospinous and uterosacral vaginal hysteropexy cost-effective at a willingness-to-pay threshold below $100,000 per QALY; uterosacral hysteropexy cost $90,738.14 per QALY gained versus sacrospinous hysteropexy, while vaginal hysterectomy with either apical suspension had higher cost and fewer QALYs than a hysteropexy strategy.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup> A separate decision model of native-tissue sacrospinous hysteropexy versus vaginal hysterectomy with uterosacral ligament suspension (published 2021, US dollars) estimated $587.61 higher cost per prolapse case with hysterectomy and $2,698,677 per endometrial cancer death prevented; the authors judged hysterectomy cost-effective for cancer prevention.<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup>

Manchester repair cost less than vaginal hysterectomy with uterosacral ligament suspension in a Danish activity-based hospital-cost analysis of 590 matched patients (published 2018): total hospital costs over 20 months were €2,318 versus €3,514 per patient, a €1,196 difference (95% CI €927 to €1,465).<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup> Manchester repair also cost less than sacrospinous hysteropexy in the 2-year economic evaluation alongside the Dutch SAM trial (434 women, 2019 euros, societal perspective): it cost €1,458.34 less, with 1.68 versus 1.67 QALYs, respectively, and no significant QALY difference.<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup>

Mesh-augmented sacrospinous hysteropexy and vaginal hysterectomy with uterosacral ligament suspension had similar costs and QALYs in SUPeR's 5-year within-trial US health care sector evaluation of 168 postmenopausal women (2020 US dollars): costs were $11,631 (95% CI $8,257 to $15,005) versus $12,622 (95% CI $10,172 to $15,072; P = 0.64), and both groups had 3.77 QALYs (P = 0.99).<sup>[[33]](#ref33)</sup> The cost-effectiveness point estimate for mesh hysteropexy was lower cost and more QALYs, but its probability of cost-effectiveness was 53% at a willingness-to-pay threshold of $150,000 per QALY.<sup>[[33]](#ref33)</sup>

## Section text: Vaginal Hysterectomy

## Cost and Value

In the UK VUE Uterine trial's 12-month NHS analysis of mixed uterine-preservation procedures versus vaginal hysterectomy (2015–16 pounds), preservation cost £235 more per patient (95% CI £6 to £464) and had a 15% probability of cost-effectiveness at £30,000 per QALY.<sup>[[35]](#ref35)</sup> The [Sacrospinous Hysteropexy Cost and Value section](/docs/surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/sacrospinous-hysteropexy#cost-and-value) also compares native-tissue and Manchester evidence.

## Notes

- Retraction check: PubMed efetch XML for all six cited PMIDs showed no `Retracted Publication`, `RetractionIn`, `ExpressionOfConcernIn`, or `ErratumIn` flags. Chang 2021 (PMID 32576734) has a `CommentIn` letter (PMID 34397609), not an erratum.
- DOI check: Crossref returned `status: ok` for Hemming, Wallace, Chang, Husby, and Schulten. Crossref returned `Resource not found` for Harvie's PubMed-listed DOI `10.1097/SPV.0000000000001924`; its reference therefore uses the PMID, matching the existing mesh page. The reviewer should recheck Crossref and run `npm run refs:new` after applying the draft.
- VUE allowed a mix of uterine-preservation operations, including mesh use; its aggregate result is not a native-tissue sacrospinous estimate. The 2015–16 pound price basis and NHS perspective are stated in its open-access scientific summary.
- Schulten's open full text labels its costs as 2019 euros. The abstract gives the trial design, societal perspective, two-year horizon, cost difference, and QALYs.
- Wallace does not state payer, perspective, or price year in its abstract; Chang does not state perspective, price year, or time horizon; Husby does not state a price year. Those fields are omitted from the section.
- Industry ties: no cited PubMed record identifies an author as employed by a maker of the compared procedure or names manufacturer funding. SUPeR lists unrelated author COIs, and VUE lists a Bard speaker fee, but neither is identified as manufacturer funding for these analyses.
- Considered and rejected: Syan/Wallace 2020 AUA abstract (conference-only predecessor of Wallace 2024); Husby and Klarskov 2021 letter and Chang et al. 2021 reply (commentary, not economic evaluations); Griswold 2026 sacrohysteropexy versus sacrocolpopexy (outside this vaginal decision group); the 2026 Manchester systematic review (less direct than the included economic studies).
