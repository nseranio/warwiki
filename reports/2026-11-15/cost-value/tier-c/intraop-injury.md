## 2. Section text, ready to paste

### Hysterectomy

## Cost and Value

Routine cystoscopy became cost-saving at higher modeled ureteral-injury rates: a US hospital-perspective decision analysis (published 2001) found thresholds of 1.5% for abdominal hysterectomy and 2% for vaginal or laparoscopically assisted vaginal hysterectomy.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> A US third-party-payer decision analysis (published 2019) found that selective cystoscopy added $13.20 to $26.13 per benign hysterectomy compared with no cystoscopy, while routine cystoscopy added another $51.39 to $57.86 compared with selective cystoscopy.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> In a US Medicare-cost decision tree for benign laparoscopic hysterectomy (published 2021), universal cystoscopy added $111 to $131 per case at a modeled 0.44% lower urinary tract injury rate and became cost-effective when the rate exceeded 0.80%.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

Universal ureteral catheterization became cost-saving only above a modeled ureteral-injury rate of 3.2% in a US Medicare-based decision tree for benign abdominal or radical hysterectomy (published 2008).<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup> Prophylactic cystoscopic ureteral stenting added a median $1,580 in actual hospital costs in a US retrospective cohort of 18 stented cases among 792 major inpatient gynecologic operations (published 2011).<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

### Radical Hysterectomy and Pelvic Lymphadenectomy

### Cost and Value

Universal ureteral catheterization became cost-saving above a modeled ureteral-injury rate of 3.2% in a US Medicare-based decision tree that included radical hysterectomy (published 2008).<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> In a Chinese single-hospital retrospective cohort of 304 women with cervical cancer undergoing abdominal radical hysterectomy (published 2025), prophylactic stenting was associated with higher surgery and hospitalization costs and more urinary complications, without a significant difference in ureteral or bladder injury rates.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

### Ureteral Trauma

## Cost and Value

Iatrogenic ureteral injury was associated with higher one-year payer spending in a US matched claims cohort by authors including Astellas Pharma employees (published 2024): annual costs attributable to injury were $38,859 per patient (95% CI $28,142 to $49,576) compared with matched operations without injury.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup> Delayed rather than immediate recognition was associated with higher total health care costs in a population-based retrospective cohort of 1,193 iatrogenic ureteral injuries (published 2021), with an incidence rate ratio of 2.06 (95% CI 1.89 to 2.24).<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup>

## 6. Notes

- PubMed efetch XML was checked for every cited PMID (11339916, 33310171, 30685289, 18518875, 22182952, 40752273, 37145504, 37961772): no `Retracted Publication`, `RetractionIn`, `ExpressionOfConcernIn`, or `ErratumIn` notices were present.
- All seven cited DOIs returned HTTP 200 from Crossref `/works/<doi>`; Fanning 2011 has no DOI in its PubMed record.
- McCarus 2024 has Astellas Pharma employee affiliations in PubMed; the section labels this tie. No maker affiliation or maker-named funding was identified in the PubMed records for the other cited studies. Wang 2025 reports no competing interests.
- The source abstracts do not identify a currency price year. Publication years are supplied instead. No probability of cost-effectiveness is quoted from Luchristt 2021 because the abstract does not give a willingness-to-pay threshold. No numeric Wang 2025 cost difference is given in its abstract.
- `cesarean-section.mdx`: no section. Economic models of general benign or radical hysterectomy do not address cesarean delivery or placenta accreta spectrum cesarean hysterectomy; no two procedure-specific economic studies of urinary-injury detection or prevention were found.
- `pelvic-tumor-resection.mdx`: no section or cost pointer. Alexandre 2023 (PMID 37829173) compares colorectal stenting and hospital cost, and Chahin 2002 (PMID 12002296) reports a lighted-stent price in laparoscopic colectomy; neither is specific to the gynecologic pelvic tumor resections discussed on that page.
- Muresan 2026 (PMID 42283953) was considered for the ureteral trauma page but describes German costs of intraoperative injury versus no injury across surgical types, not delayed versus immediate diagnosis; the US matched payer analysis was preferred. Its PubMed record shows Astellas employees and funding.
- Blackwell 2018 (PMID 29408429) reports morbidity after recognized versus unrecognized hysterectomy injury but no monetary cost result; it is already reference 25 on `ureteral-trauma.mdx` and was not used as economic evidence. Ravlo 2022 (PMID 34766333) reports compensation claims but no claim amounts and was not used.
