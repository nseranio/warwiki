## Section text

### Cost and Value

Replacing renal ultrasound with CT urography during initial evaluation of asymptomatic microscopic hematuria added one detected cancer per 10,000 patients at $6,480,484 per additional cancer detected in a US payer-perspective decision model (2016 US dollars, diagnostic horizon under one year); both strategies included cystoscopy.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup> Uniform CT urography increased modeled initial-evaluation costs in a US Medicare-payer microsimulation (2017 US dollars, with lifetime radiation-risk modeling): the historical AUA strategy cost $939 per person versus $443 for a Dutch cystoscopy-and-ultrasound strategy, and $1,034,374 per additional urinary-tract cancer detected versus a risk-index strategy.<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup> Retrospective modeling of the 2012 and 2020 AUA microhematuria protocols in 3,789 patients at a US public health system (2020 US Medicare fee schedules, modeled initial workup) estimated total costs of $1,905,236 and $1,260,677, respectively, and $100,276 versus $61,760 per cancer detected.<sup>[[33]](#ref33)</sup>

In US privately insured patients evaluated for hematuria, a retrospective claims cost analysis of 318,680 patients (imaging within three months of diagnosis, published 2024) found median total evaluation costs of $504 with contrast-enhanced CT and $163 with ultrasound.<sup>[[34]](#ref34)</sup>

## Notes

- PubMed efetch XML was checked for all four cited PMIDs. None had `Retracted Publication`, `RetractionIn`, `ExpressionOfConcernIn`, or `ErratumIn`; no errata were listed. The records carried comments or replies only. All four DOI records returned Crossref HTTP 200 on November 15, 2026.
- Full-text checks: Halpern reports the payer perspective, 2016 US dollars, Medicare fee schedules, and a horizon under one year in [PMC5818826](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5818826/). Georgieva reports a US national Medicare-payer perspective and 2017 US dollars in [PMC6664383](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6664383/). Hannemann's [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11648274/) identifies $504 and $163 as median *total hematuria-evaluation costs* by imaging type; it records contrast-enhanced CT, not a separately identified CT urography protocol.
- The Halpern, Georgieva, and Gold models evaluate older guideline strategies, not the 2025 AUA/SUFU risk categories presently described on the page. The section names their historical comparators and does not transfer their estimates to the current guideline.
- `cystography.mdx`: no section. CT versus conventional cystography studies found addressed diagnostic accuracy or workflow, not comparative economic outcomes; the pediatric VUR imaging cost lead was an out-of-pocket analysis, outside the requested payer/economic decision evidence.
- `rug-vcug.mdx`: no section. RUG, VCUG, sonourethrography, and MRI comparisons found assessed anatomy or diagnostic accuracy; descriptive claims that ultrasound is inexpensive are not economic evaluations.
- `ultrasound.mdx`: no section. Its SUG-versus-RUG, pelvic-floor ultrasound-versus-MRI, and renal surveillance choices did not yield two relevant economic studies or a trial-based evaluation. Hematuria imaging belongs to the CT urogram page because this page does not discuss that workup.
- `mri.mdx`: no section. Reported cost differences were descriptive; the claims study above combines MRI rather than a specified MR urography protocol and does not economically evaluate the page's reconstructive MRI choices.
- `mag3-renal-scintigraphy.mdx`: no section. The MAG3-versus-DTPA and functional-imaging hierarchy lacked qualifying published economic evaluations; the SNMMI/EANM practice standard calls for future cost-effectiveness research.
- `nutrition.mdx`: no section. SWOG S1600 (PMID 42475098) reported clinical outcomes, not an economic evaluation. Roth's cystectomy RCT (PMID 22695241) reported €614 per TPN patient as a secondary product-cost outcome, without comparative total costs or a cost-effectiveness analysis; it is not a trial-based economic evaluation of preoperative immunonutrition.
- `screening-tools.mdx`: no section. The preoperative screening cost model found for total knee arthroplasty (PMID 36257507) is outside cystectomy or major GU reconstruction and cannot support this page's screening decision.
- Sridalla 2025 (PMID 40358418) was considered but omitted: it models a different RBC-threshold decision against older AUA guidance, and its abstract does not identify the comparator for each reported ICER clearly enough for a concise section here.
