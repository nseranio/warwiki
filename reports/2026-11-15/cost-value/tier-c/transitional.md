## Section text

## Cost and Value

Estimated total health care costs declined after transfer from pediatric to adult care in an Ontario population-based cohort of 299 people with spina bifida followed from age 16 into ages 19 to 21 (published 2024), while unplanned emergency visits and emergency surgery increased (P = 0.004 and P = 0.02, respectively).<sup>[[74]](#ref74)</sup> Among adults aged 18 to 25 with spina bifida in a 1-year US integrated-system analysis, those who had not fully transitioned to adult care used more inpatient and emergency care than those who had transitioned.<sup>[[58]](#ref58)</sup> In a 2002 US inpatient-discharge analysis of adults with childhood-onset diseases, case-mix-adjusted hospital charges for the spina bifida subgroup were higher at pediatric hospitals than at adult or mixed hospitals (P < 0.01).<sup>[[75]](#ref75)</sup>

Adults with spina bifida had higher medical expenditures than adults without spina bifida in a 1-year US cohort cost analysis of employer-sponsored fee-for-service paid medical and prescription claims: mean incremental expenditures in 2003 US dollars were $13,339 at ages 18 to 44 and $10,134 at ages 45 to 64, and total expenditures were three to six times as high.<sup>[[55]](#ref55)</sup> In a retrospective US tertiary-center audit of 353 admissions for adults with spina bifida from 1982 to 1992, 166 admissions (47.0%) were for potentially preventable secondary conditions, including serious urologic infections and renal calculi; total hospital costs excluding professional fees were $437,262 in 1992 (published 1996).<sup>[[76]](#ref76)</sup>
