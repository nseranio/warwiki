## 2. Section text, ready to paste

### Sutures page

### Cost and Value

Savings from triclosan-coated versus uncoated sutures were uncertain in a worldwide healthcare-perspective model of abdominal surgery: using 2022 US dollars, the modeled SSI-related cost difference per patient ranged from −$466 to +$171 in high-HDI countries.<sup>[[75]](#ref75)</sup> An earlier UK NHS decision-tree model (published 2017) estimated mean savings of £91.25 per operation across wound types; one author was affiliated with Johnson & Johnson.<sup>[[76]](#ref76)</sup> A US decision model of abdominal incisions (published 2014) estimated savings of $4,109 to $13,975 per SSI prevented from the hospital perspective and $4,133 to $14,297 from the third-party payer perspective when baseline SSI risk was 15%, depending on the assumed efficacy of coated sutures.<sup>[[77]](#ref77)</sup>

### Barbed Sutures page

### Cost and Value

In a US single-surgeon randomized trial of vesicourethral anastomosis during robot-assisted radical prostatectomy (published 2010), barbed suture had higher per-case suture costs than polyglactin 910, $51.52 versus $8.44, and more day-8 cystogram extravasation, 20.0% versus 2.8%.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> A Canadian single-surgeon randomized trial of posterior reconstruction and vesicourethral anastomosis (published 2012) recorded lower institutional material costs with barbed suture than with conventional monofilament suture and clips, CA$48.05 versus CA$70.25 per case, alongside shorter reconstruction time, 13.1 versus 20.8 minutes.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

### Hemostatic Agents page

## Cost and Value

Topical hemostat use was associated with higher total encounter costs in a US retrospective cohort of benign hysterectomy, prolapse repair and anti-incontinence surgery: across 184,070 encounters in the Vizient Clinical Database, encounters with an agent cost $6,271.10 versus $4,572.00 without one (published 2022).<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

Oxidized regenerated cellulose had lower hemostat acquisition costs than other adjunctive hemostats in an Ethicon-affiliated US matched hospital cohort (published 2015): across four procedure groups including hysterectomy, agent costs were 28% to 56% lower with oxidized regenerated cellulose than with flowables, gelatins or topical thrombins.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> In a US two-institution cohort of laparoscopic partial nephrectomy (40 cases, published 2009), surgeon-prepared Gelfoam was estimated to save $200 to $450 per case compared with FloSeal; median blood loss was 100 versus 150 mL (P = 0.09).<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
