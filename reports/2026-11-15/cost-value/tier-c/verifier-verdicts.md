# Tier C verdicts (no repo files edited)

Sources: PubMed abstracts and XML affiliations/COI; PMC full text (Halpern, Georgieva, Mortensen, Eray); NICE evidence review B1 PDF (downloaded, read); CADTH Reimbursement Review PDF (canjhealthtechnol.ca, read). Not openable: Wallace 2020 full text (Elsevier, paywalled); NCBI Bookshelf NBK603397 (captcha; the same CADTH report was read from the journal site instead); Annemans 2005 full text.

## 1. radical-hysterectomy-pelvic-lymphadenectomy.mdx, Wang 2025 (ref 14)
SUPPORTED. 304 women, Qilu Hospital (Shandong University, Qingdao), abdominal radical hysterectomy; abstract: "Operation time, surgery cost and hospitalization expenses were higher in the stent group"; urinary complications higher; ureteral and bladder injury P > 0.05. No competing interests.

## 2. ct-urogram.mdx, Cost and Value (refs 31-34)
- Halpern 2017 sentence: SUPPORTED. Full text: "updated to 2016 US dollars", payer perspective, "model time horizon was less than 1 year"; $6,480,484 per additional cancer, 1 additional cancer per 10,000; no COI.
- Georgieva 2019 sentence: SUPPORTED. Full text: "US national payer (Medicare) perspective using 2017 US dollars"; lifetime radiation-risk modeling; $939 vs $443 (Dutch); $1,034,374 vs HRI.
- Gold 2022 sentence: SUPPORTED (numbers, n = 3,789, 2020 Medicare fee schedules). Note: it compares the 2012 and 2020 AUA protocols, not CT urography against ultrasound directly, so it only loosely fits the opener.
- Hannemann 2024 sentence: CORRECT-AS: "In US privately insured patients evaluated for hematuria, a retrospective claims cost analysis of 318,680 patients who had cystoscopy and upper tract imaging within three months of diagnosis (published 2024) found median imaging costs of $504 for contrast-enhanced CT and $163 for ultrasound.<sup>[[34]](#ref34)</sup>"
  Reason: the $504 and $163 are costs of the imaging study, not "total evaluation costs". Quote: "Median costs associated with upper tract imaging were $362 overall, $504 for CT with contrast, $163 for US".
- Opener: SUPPORTED for Halpern, Georgieva, Hannemann; see Gold note.

## 3. ic-pbs.mdx (refs 65-69)
- Opener "favored on cost in two European analyses": SUPPORTED with a caveat. Cervigni says HA/CS "showed a more acceptable cost-effectiveness profile" (an ICER, not lower cost); Riedl found lowest total costs. Optional wording: "favored on cost-effectiveness".
- Cervigni 2017: SUPPORTED. 110 women, 6 months, ICER 3735 to 8003 euros per QALY vs DMSO, direct healthcare costs; two authors (Trevisan, Frangione) at IBSA R&D.
- Riedl 2013: SUPPORTED. Austria, 1/5/10 years, hyaluronan lowest total costs at all three timepoints, cost saving "in standard assumptions and in all sensitivity analyses"; comparators hyaluronan, pentosan polysulfate, amitriptyline, nonspecific therapy. No industry affiliation in the PubMed record.
- Mikl 2026: SUPPORTED. Affiliations: Mikl, Purdue Pharma L.P.; Pascale, Knoa Pharma LLC; others Genesis Research Group; Dmochowski, Vanderbilt. $20,288 vs $10,222 PPPY, RR 1.98; MarketScan Commercial and Medicare Supplemental; "billed expenses".
- Tung 2017: SUPPORTED. "Funding for this study was contributed by Allergan"; $7,223 (95% CI 6,650-7,796); Hunner $6,895; first 12 months after diagnosis.
- Wu 2006: SUPPORTED. n = 749 under 65, 152 with disability data, direct 130% and indirect 84% higher, first year after diagnosis. (Abstract says "average", the page says "mean"; harmless.)

## 4. vaginal-laser-therapy.mdx (refs 67, 68, 32)
- NICE sentence: CORRECT-AS: "A 1-year UK NHS and personal social services Markov model (published 2024) favored estriol at a £20,000-per-QALY threshold: the ICER was £183,260 per QALY gained with CO₂ laser versus estriol, with the laser effectiveness estimate drawn from two studies of 88 women in total and the three-session laser cost (median £2,495) estimated from private-sector prices and a guideline committee estimate.<sup>[[68]](#ref68)</sup>"
  Reason: NICE Table 12 gives £183,260 per QALY laser vs estriol; "only two studies of 88 patients in total"; Markov, 1-year horizon, NHS+PSS confirmed. The cost was the median of three private-sector prices (£1,500, £1,990, £3,600) and a committee estimate (£3,000). Quote: "A median value for laser treatment of £2,495 was estimated for the model."
- Wallace sentence: UNVERIFIABLE in part. Confirmed from the abstract: decision tree (TreeAge), moderate GSM dyspareunia, all three therapies cost-effective below $50,000 per QALY, laser ICER $16,372.01 (ospemifene $5,711.14). Not confirmed (full text paywalled): "patient-cost perspective", "2017 US dollars", "1-year" horizon, that the comparator for the $16,372 ICER is oral ospemifene, and "Stanford". Ref 32 (Mortensen 2022, PMC9564803) says only "estimated an out-of-pocket cost at US$2733 for three sessions of LASER"; it does not say Stanford. Wallace's abstract also gives sensitivity figures of 200% = $5,554 and 20% = $555.40, which imply a model laser cost of about $2,777, not $2,733. Suggest keeping $16,372, the $50,000 threshold and the dyspareunia population, and dropping or sourcing the perspective, price year, comparator and "Stanford" wording once the full text is in hand.
- Opener: SUPPORTED as a summary of the two models once the Wallace details are settled.

## 5. gsm.mdx (refs 25-28)
- Faught 2019: SUPPORTED with a small trim. Authors from Duchesnay Inc. (4 of 7). Abstract: "Total all-cause healthcare costs were also significantly less for ospemifene patients compared with all other LETs." "Mean" is not stated for total costs; use CORRECT-AS: "A US retrospective claims analysis by Duchesnay-affiliated authors (published 2019) reported lower all-cause health care costs among ospemifene users than users of local vaginal estrogen formulations.<sup>[[26]](#ref26)</sup>"
- NICE 2024 sentence: CORRECT-AS: "In NICE's 1-year UK NHS and personal social services Markov model (published 2024), estriol had lower cost and more QALYs than nonhormonal moisturizer/lubricant and a 60.0% probability of being preferred at £20,000 per QALY; prasterone ranked second overall (31.4%) and was the preferred option for people who could not or did not wish to use local vaginal estrogen, at £2,548 per QALY gained versus moisturizer/lubricant.<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup>"
  Reason: the £2,548 is the base-case ICER (Table 20), where prasterone ranked 2nd behind estriol. NICE does not report a separate "local estrogen excluded" run; it states "Prasterone was the preferred option for those who were contraindicated or wished not to take local vaginal oestrogen." The 60.0% and estriol -£3 with +0.0171 QALY are correct.
- CADTH 2022: SUPPORTED on the figures. Report: "Canadian publicly funded health care payer", "Time horizon 10 years", "ospemifene was dominated (i.e., more costly [incremental costs: $175] and less effective [incremental QALYs: 0.001])" in CADTH reanalyses against a mixed basket of local estrogens. The word "Markov" was not confirmed; safer: "CADTH's 10-year reanalysis from the Canadian publicly funded payer perspective (published 2022) estimated ospemifene to cost $175 more and yield 0.001 fewer QALYs than a weighted mix of local vaginal estrogen formulations." (Sponsor was Duchesnay, not mentioned on the page.)
- Dymond 2021: SUPPORTED. Affiliation "Shionogi B.V." (Perard); NHS Scotland, lifetime, cohort Markov, £14,138 per QALY, 89% at £20,000; population not candidates for local estrogen. NICE table also notes "Model funded by manufacturer of ospemifene".

## 6. sutures.mdx (refs 75-77)
SUPPORTED, all three sentences.

## 7. transitional-urology.mdx (refs 74-76)
SUPPORTED all three.
- Playfair 2024: 299 patients, Ontario, age 16 baseline vs ages 19-21; ER visits P = 0.004, emergency surgery P = 0.02, total cost declined P = 0.03. (The abstract says "unplanned ER visits"; page says "unplanned emergency visits"; fine.)

## 8. fourniers-gangrene.mdx (refs 28-30)
- Ganapathy 2023: SUPPORTED. 76 patients, $130,000 ± $102,000 vs $142,000 ± $152,000, p = 0.37, time to surgery not different.
- Eray 2015: CORRECT-AS: "For patients needing fecal diversion after debridement who had preserved sphincters and no rectal injury, a 48-patient comparison (published 2015) associated a bowel management catheter with lower hospital expense and fewer additional operations than colostomy.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup>"
  Reason: neither the abstract nor the PMC full text calls the study retrospective. Quote: "Decreased duration of total hospital stay, additional surgery requirements, and hospital expense in bowel management catheter group". (Optional: the catheter group also had a shorter total stay.)
- Sarofim 2021 sentence: SUPPORTED. 27 studies, n = 1482; "Significantly more surgical procedures ... and the total hospital cost was also higher in this group". (The same paper found higher mortality with stoma, OR 1.71, not mentioned here.)

## 9. wound-healing-adjuncts.mdx (refs 36-40, 13)
- Ker 2019: SUPPORTED. NZ hospital health-care buyer, 49 randomized, 3-month follow-up, saving $3903.28 per treatment, 73.15% at NZ$25,000. The abstract writes "$3903.28" without a currency label; add "NZ" only if the full text confirms it.
- Kwon 2018: SUPPORTED. $30,492 vs $36,537, savings $6045, P = .11, 30 days, variable hospital costs, single institution, high-risk incisions.
- INVIPS: SUPPORTED. €16,621 vs €16,285 at 90 days, healthcare perspective, SSI 11.9% vs 30.0%, €1,853 per SSI avoided.
- DRESSING: SUPPORTED. AUD$162 (95% CI -$170 to $494) higher, no detectable QALY difference, 20% at $50,000 per QALY, health service perspective, four weeks post-discharge.
- SWHSI-2 sentence (ref 13): SUPPORTED. Lancet abstract: "NPWT was found not to be cost-effective compared with usual care."
- Saramago 2025 (ref 40): it is a separate analysis, not the trial's economic evaluation: an economic model that "extrapolate[d] the effectiveness results of a meta-analysis over a patient's lifetime", by the SWHSI-2 investigators, probability of cost-effectiveness under 30%. So the two sentences do not describe one study twice, but the second is unclear. CORRECT-AS: "A UK healthcare-system lifetime economic model that extrapolated a meta-analysis of NPWT for these wounds (published 2025) projected higher costs and marginally higher QALYs with NPWT than with standard dressings, driven mainly by intervention costs, with a probability of cost-effectiveness below 30%.<sup>[[40]](#ref40)</sup>"

## 12. hysterectomy.mdx (refs 20-24) and ureteral-trauma.mdx (refs 28, 29)
SUPPORTED all.
- Visco 2001: hospital perspective, 1.5% abdominal and 2% vaginal/LAVH thresholds.
- Cadish 2019: third-party payer; selective cystoscopy +$13.20-26.13 vs none; routine +$51.39-57.86 vs selective.
- Luchristt 2021: Medicare, +$111 to $131 at 0.44% LUTI, cost-effective above 0.80%.
- Schimpf 2008: Medicare DRG and CPT, cost saving above 3.2%, benign abdominal or radical hysterectomy.
- Fanning 2011: 18 prophylactic stent cases among 792 major inpatient gynecologic procedures (Summa Health System), median additional cost $1,580.
- McCarus 2024: US claims, $38,859 (95% CI 28,142-49,576); affiliations include Astellas Pharma Global Development (Shortridge, Kimura, Feng, Han, Jiang). "Employees" is an inference from the affiliation; "authors affiliated with Astellas Pharma" is the safer wording.
- Locke 2021: 1,193 patients, 2003-2018, total health care cost IRR 2.06 (1.89-2.24) for delayed vs immediate recognition.

## Summary
1. 12 items checked; 24 sentence groups read against abstracts, PMC full text, NICE B1 and the CADTH report.
2. Corrections needed: Hannemann (imaging cost, not total evaluation cost); NICE prasterone sentence in gsm (£2,548 is the base-case ICER, rank 2, no "estrogen excluded" run); NICE laser sentence (committee estimate also used); Eray ("retrospective" unsupported); Saramago (model extrapolating a meta-analysis, probability under 30%); Faught ("mean" unsupported); CADTH ("Markov" unconfirmed).
3. Unverifiable: Wallace 2020 perspective, 2017 dollars, 1-year horizon, ospemifene comparator and "Stanford" (full text paywalled; the $2,733 price is from Mortensen without "Stanford", and Wallace's sensitivity figures imply about $2,777); Annemans funding.
4. Industry ties confirmed: IBSA (Cervigni), Purdue and Knoa (Mikl), Allergan (Tung), Duchesnay (Faught), Shionogi (Dymond), J&J (Leaper), Astellas (McCarus).
5. No arithmetic or direction-of-result errors found beyond those above; all other figures, currencies, price years and horizons matched.
