## 2. Section text

### Wound Healing Adjuncts

## Cost and Value

NPWT with same-day discharge cost less than a conventional bolster with five days of inpatient bed rest after lower-limb split-skin grafting in a New Zealand hospital-buyer decision model using data from a 49-patient randomized trial (three-month follow-up; published 2019).<sup>[[36]](#ref36)</sup> Mean savings were $3,903.28 per treatment; the probability of cost-effectiveness was 73.15% at a willingness-to-pay threshold of 25,000 New Zealand dollars.<sup>[[36]](#ref36)</sup>

For high-risk vascular groin incisions, a US single-center randomized trial-based hospital-cost analysis over 30 days (published 2018) found mean variable costs of $30,492 with NPWT and $36,537 with gauze; the $6,045 difference was not statistically significant (P = .11).<sup>[[37]](#ref37)</sup> In the Swedish INVIPS randomized trial, a healthcare-perspective analysis over 90 days (published 2021) found procedure-related costs of €16,621 with NPWT and €16,285 with standard dressings, while surgical-site infection occurred in 11.9% versus 30.0% of patients; the incremental cost was €1,853 per infection avoided.<sup>[[38]](#ref38)</sup> Among obese women undergoing caesarean delivery, the Australian DRESSING within-trial analysis from the health-service perspective through four weeks after discharge (published 2023) found AUD$162 higher cost per patient with closed-incision NPWT than with standard dressings and no detectable QALY difference; the probability of cost-effectiveness was 20% at AUD$50,000 per QALY.<sup>[[39]](#ref39)</sup>

For surgical wounds healing by secondary intention, the UK SWHSI-2 randomized trial found NPWT not cost-effective against usual care.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> A UK healthcare-system lifetime economic model (published 2025) projected higher costs and marginally higher QALYs with NPWT than with standard dressings, driven mainly by intervention costs.<sup>[[40]](#ref40)</sup> After Fournier debridement, a Turkish 10-patient comparative study (published 2009) found similar total costs with VAC and conventional dressings; VAC required fewer dressing changes and was associated with less pain.<sup>[[41]](#ref41)</sup>

### Penile Skin Grafting

### Cost and Value

After lower-limb split-skin grafting, a New Zealand trial-based analysis found lower hospital costs with NPWT plus same-day discharge than with a conventional bolster plus inpatient bed rest.<sup>[[37]](#ref37)</sup> See [Wound Healing Adjuncts](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/wound-healing-adjuncts#cost-and-value) for the economic results.

### Scrotal Skin Grafting

### Cost and Value

After lower-limb split-skin grafting, a New Zealand trial-based analysis found lower hospital costs with NPWT plus same-day discharge than with a conventional bolster plus inpatient bed rest.<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> See [Wound Healing Adjuncts](/docs/foundations/surgical-principles/wound-healing-adjuncts#cost-and-value) for the economic results.

### Penile Grafting With Tissue Substitutes

## Cost and Value

Adding a dermal substitute and topical negative pressure to split-skin grafting raised initial treatment cost in a Dutch randomized burn trial-based analysis from the societal perspective (86 patients, 12 months; published 2014): €2,912 versus €1,703 with grafting alone.<sup>[[33]](#ref33)</sup> Mean total patient costs did not differ significantly across the four graft, substitute and negative-pressure strategies (€29,097–€43,774).<sup>[[33]](#ref33)</sup>

## 6. Notes

- eFetch XML for all eight cited PMIDs, including existing SWHSI-2 PMID 40250455, showed no `Retracted Publication`, `RetractionIn`, `ExpressionOfConcernIn`, or `ErratumIn`. No errata were found.
- Crossref returned HTTP 200 for all eight cited DOIs, including the existing SWHSI-2 DOI. The reviewer should still run `npm run refs:new` after applying the draft.
- No manufacturer affiliation or manufacturer-named funding statement appeared in the PubMed records of the seven new studies. Device names in those studies are not treated as proof of industry funding.
- The WOUND comparison bundles dressing choice with discharge timing and inpatient bed rest. Its economic result should not be attributed to dressing cost alone.
- PMID 35392664, Bloom 2023: Medicare-based decision-tree model for a vascular groin incision; omitted in favor of randomized analyses with observed costs.
- PMID 27320010, Heard 2017: small caesarean pilot economic evaluation; superseded for this question by the larger DRESSING trial.
- PMID 35288248, Cooper 2022: trial-linked knee arthroplasty economic model; lower decision relevance than vascular groin and caesarean studies.
- PMID 28256293, Mushin 2017: home VAC versus inpatient VAC after burn STSG; cost comparison is principally discharge setting, without a conventional bolster arm.
- PMID 25595397, Yang 2015: NPWT with instillation, grafting and inpatient bolster versus historical compression therapy for venous ulcers; not a graft-fixation comparison.
- PMID 27699461, Schiavon 2016: Integra versus flaps for scalp defects; retrospective, with a different recipient bed and no direct matrix-versus-STSG comparison.
- PMID 39224413, Jou 2024: BTM versus Integra in upper-extremity wounds; no penile comparison or trial-based evaluation.
- PMID 18789410, Ozturk 2009: small Fournier comparison with qualitative cost reporting only; no currency amount, price year or formal perspective reported in its abstract.
- No hyperbaric-oxygen cost text is proposed.
