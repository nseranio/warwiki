## Section text

## Cost and Value

Plication cost less than collagenase clostridium histolyticum (CCH) in a US decision-tree model using institutional billing data (published 2017): $3,039 per patient for plication versus $25,856 for a CCH pathway in moderate curvature and $26,375 in severe curvature.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup> In a US MarketScan commercial claims analysis of treatment from 2009 to 2019 (published 2022), median per-patient costs rose from $1,856 to $3,196 for plication and from $2,233 to $3,631 for plaque grafting, compared with $6,940 to $8,895 per CCH cycle.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup> A UK NHS protocol cost comparison (published 2020) included expected repeat surgery and found that CCH cost £3,832.77 more per patient than primary surgery under the IMPRESS protocol and £70.77 more under the London protocol; the modeled London-protocol break-even price was £548.41 per CCH vial.<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup>

RestoreX traction had the lowest mean cost in a 10-year US Markov model using 2017 Medicare reimbursements and single-institution prospective outcomes: $883 per patient versus $11,419 for surgery (plication or plaque incision and grafting) and $33,628 for CCH, with 9.27, 9.36 and 9.44 QALYs, respectively.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup> At a willingness-to-pay threshold of $100,000 per QALY, traction was the most cost-effective strategy in 49% of simulations, surgery in 48% and CCH in 3%; at $150,000 per QALY, the corresponding probabilities were 33%, 55% and 12%.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup>

## Notes

- The four cited studies are English-language economic analyses, including three US analyses. No economic evaluation alongside the 2023 CCH-versus-surgery randomized trial was found; that trial reports clinical outcomes only (PMID [37811752](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37811752/)).
- Walton et al. (PMID 35461065) has an erratum (PMID [36028462](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36028462/)) correcting “Histiolyticum” to “Histolyticum” in the title only; it does not change cost data. The new reference line uses the corrected title.
- PubMed records for the four cited papers show no retraction or expression-of-concern notice. The requested EFetch XML check could not be completed because this environment could not resolve `eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov`; repeat that check before applying the draft.
- Crossref API DOI checks could not be completed because this environment could not resolve `api.crossref.org` (and its web view was unavailable). All four DOI strings match the papers' PubMed records; repeat direct Crossref resolution and `npm run refs:new` after applying the draft.
- The abstracts do not state price years for Cordon, Walton or Harvey, or a named analytic perspective or time horizon for those studies. Publication years are used as required; no perspective or horizon is inferred. Wymer states the 2017 Medicare reimbursement basis and 10-year horizon but does not explicitly name a perspective.
- Cordon's model defined success as residual curvature of 30° or less and assumed secondary plication after 50% of CCH failures. Walton's claims data did not measure treatment outcomes or postprocedural costs; its per-cycle CCH figure is not a whole-course cost.
- PubMed affiliations list academic or NHS institutions and do not name Endo, Auxilium or another maker; the displayed PubMed records provide no maker funding statement. No manufacturer tie is asserted. RestoreX-related author interests should not be inferred from the device studied.
- Considered, rejected: Parkinson et al., PMID [41364316](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41364316/), narrative review rather than an original economic evaluation.
- Considered, rejected: Loftus et al., PMID [33036960](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33036960/), large claims trend analysis of overall PD spending, less directly tied to the treatment comparisons on the algorithm page.
- Considered, rejected: Green et al., PMID [37811752](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37811752/), randomized comparison of clinical outcomes without a reported economic evaluation.
