## 2. Section text for absorbent-products.mdx

## Cost and Value

Absorbent-product cost-effectiveness differed by sex and setting in a UK health technology assessment of three randomized crossover trials (published 2008): disposable diapers were the most cost-effective disposable design for men with moderate-to-heavy incontinence, while disposable inserts were a cheaper alternative to preferred pull-ups for community women; washable diapers were the least expensive design but were unacceptable to 27/36 community women and highly acceptable at night to 31/49 community men.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Among 85 community women with light incontinence in the same assessment, menstrual pads and washable pants cost less than disposable inserts but leaked more; 6/85 and 13/85 women respectively preferred them.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)</sup> The authors suggested that combining more effective products when out with cheaper designs at home might be less expensive than using one design throughout.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

Reusable underwear cost slightly less than disposable pads in a consumer-perspective, 2-year cost model of products purchased online (published 2020) that assumed two pads per day and equal laundering costs: Icon underwear cost US $380.80 versus US $392.40 for regular underwear with disposable pads; the ranking reversed at a pad price of US $0.15 with three pads per day or US $0.24 with fewer than two pads per day.<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup>

Personal product spending increased with symptom severity in a cross-sectional questionnaire analysis of 702 women recruited through ResearchMatch and using incontinence products weekly (published 2022): mean spending was $5.42 per week (median $3), and women reported using a mean of 1.8 products per day.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup> In an earlier diary-based study of 315 community-dwelling women in three southeastern US cities, median annual pad spending among pad users was $76 (interquartile range $36–$177) in 1995 US dollars.<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup>
