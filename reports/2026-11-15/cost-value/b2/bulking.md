## 2. Section text for the home page

## Cost and Value

Among women with stress urinary incontinence without urethral hypermobility, a 1-year decision tree (published 2015) found that midurethral slings cost $436,465 more than bulking agents per 100 women treated and yielded an ICER of $70,400 per utility gained with the sling versus bulking; at a $50,000 willingness-to-pay threshold, the authors did not judge the sling cost-effective as first-line treatment in many modeled situations.<sup>[[35]](#ref35)</sup> A separate decision model (published 2024) estimated 1.82 QALYs for PAHG versus 1.86 for the midurethral sling, an annualized difference of 0.02 QALY/year below the stated minimally important utility difference of 0.03 per year; PAHG was cost-effective when more than 58% of injections were performed in the office.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

An international prospective cohort of 284 women, partly funded by Urolastic manufacturer Urogyn BV, recorded lower costs with Urolastic than with a midurethral sling in a 12-month economic analysis.<sup>[[36]](#ref36)</sup> Using Dutch unit costs (2021 euros) and including direct medical costs, out-of-pocket expenses and productivity loss, mean total costs were €3,567 with Urolastic and €6,688 with a sling; the ICER was €15,598 per IIQ QALY gained and €37,408 per EQ-5D-5L QALY gained with the sling versus Urolastic.<sup>[[36]](#ref36)</sup> At a willingness-to-pay threshold of €25,000 per QALY, the sling was cost-effective in 84% of adjusted analyses using IIQ QALYs, whereas Urolastic was cost-effective in 99% using EQ-5D-5L QALYs.<sup>[[36]](#ref36)</sup>

Macroplastique was more expensive than a pubovaginal sling in an Australian randomized trial of 45 women with SUI and intrinsic sphincter deficiency that measured costs at 6 and 12 months (published 2005; P &lt; 0.001).<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

For comparisons among sling operations, see [Female Slings and Suspensions: Cost and Value](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/female-slings-suspensions#cost-and-value).

The PubMed article pages consulted showed no visible retraction or erratum notice; that is not an XML check. The reviewer must run these checks and `npm run refs:new` before applying/publishing. No erratum was found in the accessible records; its absence remains unconfirmed.
- The Urolastic cohort's PubMed abstract lists a ZonMw grant. Its [open-access full text](https://obgyn.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/1471-0528.17396) also names a nonrestricted Urogyn BV grant; the existing WARWIKI Urolastic page identifies Urogyn BV as the manufacturer. No maker affiliation or maker funding was found in the accessible PubMed records for Kunkle or Chang. Chang's disclosed institutional funding from Cook-MyoSite and Watson-Conti does not name the maker of a compared option.
- No completed economic evaluation alongside the Helsinki PAHG-versus-TVT randomized trial was found. The 2025 PURSUIT randomized trial of recurrent SUI closed early and did not produce a usable comparative economic result ([PMID 40613491](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40613491/)).
- Direct Bulkamid-versus-Macroplastique cost evidence was not found. Chang 2024 modeled non-PAHG agents, but its PubMed abstract gives no agent-to-agent economic result. Therefore no cost ranking of injectable agents is drafted.
- Chang 2022 / ESTER analyses already appear in the [female slings hub](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/female-slings-suspensions#cost-and-value), so their figures are linked rather than repeated here.
- Kunkle 2015 does not report a price year or explicit perspective in the abstract; `published 2015` is used. It reports an ICER per utility gained, so the draft preserves that unit rather than relabeling it per QALY. Chang 2024's abstract does not report a horizon, price year or perspective, so these are omitted.
- Considered and rejected: [PMID 34314554](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34314554/) (Chang 2022), already used on the sling hub; [PMID 32312310](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32312310/) (Brazzelli 2020), systematic review with no agent-specific decision figure needed here; [PMID 28738443](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28738443/) (Kirchin 2017), brief economic commentary rather than a new economic evaluation; [PMID 42681922](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42681922/) (Pfeuti 2026), comparative retreatment cohort without costs; [PMID 40613491](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40613491/) (PURSUIT), trial closed too early for comparative economic conclusions.
