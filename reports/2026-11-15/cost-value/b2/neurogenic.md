## 2. Section text, ready to paste

### Spinal Cord Injury

## Cost and Value

A Canadian public-payer lifetime Markov model (published 2023) found CIC had lower cost and more QALYs than suprapubic or indwelling urethral catheters in SCI-related NLUTD; the [Intermittent Catheter Cost and Value section](/docs/foundations/tools/biomaterials/urinary-catheters/intermittent-catheter#cost-and-value) reports the figures.<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

OnabotulinumtoxinA carried an ICER of $2,182 per QALY gained versus best supportive care in the SCI subgroup of a US payer 3-year Markov model (published 2013) of anticholinergic-refractory NDO.<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup> OnabotulinumtoxinA plus best supportive care carried an ICER of £1,767 per QALY gained versus best supportive care alone in the SCI subgroup of a UK NHS 5-year Markov model using 2013 drug costs and 2011–2012 NHS administration costs (published 2015).<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup>

Botulinum toxin A injections cost $28,065 over five years and were less costly than augmentation cystoplasty when their effect lasted longer than 5.1 months in a US health-care payer 5-year decision model based on insurance reimbursements (published 2011) for antimuscarinic-refractory NDO with decreased bladder compliance; augmentation was cheaper when its modeled complication rate fell below 14%.<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup> Augmentation cystoplasty was the cost-effective option versus botulinum toxin A when both were viable in a UK NHS and Personal Social Services lifetime decision model published by NICE in 2012; toxin was cost-effective against no treatment when augmentation was unsuitable.<sup>[[33]](#ref33)</sup>

### Multiple Sclerosis

## Cost and Value

OnabotulinumtoxinA carried an ICER of $32,268 per QALY gained versus best supportive care in the MS subgroup of a US payer 3-year Markov model (published 2013) of anticholinergic-refractory NDO.<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> OnabotulinumtoxinA plus best supportive care carried an ICER of £6,422 per QALY gained versus best supportive care alone in the MS subgroup of a UK NHS 5-year Markov model using 2013 drug costs and 2011–2012 NHS administration costs (published 2015).<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

The [SCI Cost and Value section](/docs/clinical-conditions/03d-nlutd/nlutd-spinal-cord-injury#cost-and-value) covers the catheter-strategy and augmentation-versus-toxin comparisons.

- **Industry ties:** The [Carlson publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0149291813000763) says Allergan funded the manuscript and employed two authors; the [Hamid open-access full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40273-014-0245-8) says Allergan funded the study and employed five authors. Because EFetch affiliation and COI fields could not be checked, the paste text omits industry labels under the task's PubMed-record-only rule. Add those labels if EFetch confirms them. The [Padmanabhan publisher record](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00345-010-0618-3) reports Vanderbilt funding and one author consulting for Allergan; it is not labeled manufacturer-funded.
- **Model distinction:** Hamid adapted Carlson's US model for UK costs. These are two publications, not independent model structures. The NICE lifetime model and Padmanabhan five-year model use different assumptions and reach different preferred strategies. Neither is a randomized head-to-head economic evaluation of augmentation versus toxin.
- **Adult scope:** The NICE model uses adult toxin trial data and adult assumptions for augmentation complications. Padmanabhan's abstract describes an NDO base case without specifying age; the paper does not identify a pediatric population, but the reviewer should check the full text before application.
- **Surveillance:** No adult NLUTD urodynamic-surveillance economic evaluation with a quantified comparison was found. [PMID 23941789](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23941789/) measures management changes after annual urodynamics, without costs. [PMID 30526805](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30526805/) reviews surveillance effectiveness, without an economic comparison. NICE's selective-testing cost judgment is an unquantified guideline statement, not a surveillance model. No surveillance cost sentence is drafted.
- **Spina bifida:** [PMID 39281356](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39281356/) is a Canadian transition cohort with total healthcare costs, but no bladder-management strategy comparison. [PMID 17335056](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17335056/) estimates general adult spina-bifida expenditure versus people without spina bifida; [PMID 9008813](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9008813/) reports admissions for potentially preventable conditions. These do not meet the two-study threshold for a decision-relevant bladder Cost and Value section.
- **Rejected:** [PMID 35216926](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35216926/) models toxin versus augmentation in children; [PMID 35305930](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35305930/) is commentary on that pediatric model; [PMID 27201347](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27201347/) is a conference abstract of the later Hamid model; [PMID 27988174](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27988174/) is a non-English narrative review; [PMID 18665974](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18665974/) is a narrative review; [PMID 40620147](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40620147/) is an economic RCT of non-neurogenic OAB that excluded neurogenic bladder.
