## 2. Section text for the home page

## Cost and Value

HBOT was associated with lower follow-up Medicare payments than conventional therapy for radiation cystitis in a US retrospective cohort of 3,309 patients (2014–2019 claims, published 2024): unadjusted payments were $5,059 lower per patient during the first year after HBOT completion, and the reported difference was $11,548 per patient among those who received at least 40 sessions.<sup>[[42]](#ref42)</sup> A radiation-injury course commonly means several weeks of repeated visits.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

In a single-patient Australian purchaser-perspective cost analysis of radiation cystitis (published 2012; three years of observation), 38 HBOT sessions cost AUD12,014.95; recorded healthcare costs were AUD32,571.42 over 139 days before HBOT and AUD17,113.42 over 897 days afterward, with a projected saving of AUD187,483.96 over 2.5 years.<sup>[[43]](#ref43)</sup> In a German retrospective series of 15 patients with radiation- or chemotherapy-related hemorrhagic cystitis (published 2015), HBOT cost approximately €200 per session and €6,800 per patient for a median 34-session course.<sup>[[44]](#ref44)</sup>

HBOT was associated with higher hospital costs for surgically treated necrotizing soft-tissue infection in a US retrospective inpatient cohort (2012–2020 National Inpatient Sample, published 2024): 600 of 60,481 patients received HBOT; unadjusted hospital costs were $54,000 versus $46,000, and the adjusted difference was $7,800 (95% CI $2,200–$13,300) with HBOT versus no HBOT.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## 6. Notes for review

- **Source hierarchy:** No trial-based economic evaluation was found for RICH-ART; its 2025 follow-up calls for evaluation of health economic impact. HOT2 concerns bowel dysfunction, not cystitis, and did not yield a relevant economic evaluation in this search. The radiation-cystitis cost evidence is a US retrospective Medicare comparison, a single-patient before/after analysis, and a small German series reporting treatment-course costs. The latter two do not establish comparative cost-effectiveness.
- **Interpretation:** Feldmeier et al. report first-year *post-treatment* Medicare payments. Their abstract does not establish a net saving including the HBOT course. Smart and Wallington's projected 2.5-year saving extrapolates one patient's pre-HBOT costs.
- **Full-text cost source:** The German course-cost amounts are absent from the abstract but present in the [open-access article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4423090/): “The costs amount to approximately €200 per HBOT session. Accordingly, the median costs per patient in this study were €6,800”.
- **Retraction/erratum check:** PubMed records for PMIDs 38985151, 22828818, 25953493 and 38512943 showed no retraction or erratum notice in the pages accessible here. The required EFetch XML check could not run because the repo shell could not resolve `eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov`; the web tool also could not open the EFetch URL. Recheck all four PMIDs with EFetch before applying. No erratum was found in the accessible records.
- **DOI check:** The new DOI for Feldmeier et al. appears on the [publisher DOI page](https://doi.org/10.22462/704) and [Washington University publication record](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/controlled-cms-data-demonstrates-a-cost-and-clinical-advantage-fo/). Degener et al.'s DOI appears on [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25953493/) and [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4423090/). Direct Crossref API requests could not run because the shell could not resolve `api.crossref.org` and the web tool could not open the API records. Smart and Wallington have no DOI listed on PubMed. Run Crossref resolution and `npm run refs:new` on the applied pages before publication.
- **Industry ties:** The accessible PubMed affiliations and conflict statements did not identify a device-maker affiliation or manufacturer funding for the cited papers. No industry tie is asserted in the section.
- **Existing citation check:** The moved visit-burden sentence retains references 12 and 22 exactly. Reference 22 is a Fournier's gangrene study rather than a radiation-injury course study; review whether it should remain attached to that sentence when applying the draft.
- **Considered, not used:** RICH-ART 2019 (PMID 31537473) and its 2025 follow-up (PMID 40291346): clinical outcomes, no economic evaluation in the abstracts.
- **Considered, not used:** HOT2 (PMID 26703894): bowel-symptom trial, no relevant economic outcome in the trial abstract.
- **Considered, not used:** Shaw et al. 2014 (PMID 24786980): US necrotizing-infection cohort with no direct-cost difference in three lower-severity strata, but no numeric cost estimate in its abstract; the larger contemporary Toppen cohort was selected.
- **Considered, not used:** Gilmartin et al. 2025 (PMID 40910326) and Handmer et al. 2020 (PMID 32194089): inpatient burden of radiation cystitis or mixed pelvic-radiation urologic complications, without an HBOT treatment comparison. They could support a separate condition-level cost section if that page is later in scope.
- **Considered, not used:** A 1993 14-patient HBOT series (PMID 8326555) reports $10,000–$15,000 per patient without a formal comparative analysis; the later cost reports were selected.
