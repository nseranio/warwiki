# B2 unextracted items: verdicts

Sources: PubMed abstracts, PMC full texts where open (Moses, Hamid, Memokath MTG, Moretto, Epiu, Baker, Degener), Casteleijn chapter in the author's UvA thesis, NICE CG148 full guideline PDF. No repo file was edited.

## 1. Inflatable penile prosthesis, Moses 2019 (ref 53): SUPPORTED
Abstract: IPP 10-year cost $22,009 vs alternate PDE5-I $52,883, ICI alprostadil $62,890, ICI trimix $48,617; QALY 7.82 (IPP) vs 7.73 (PDE5-I), 8.51 and 8.47 (injections). Full text: "The health care provider's perspective was used"; "A Markov transition model that uses repetitive decision trees" (abstract calls it a repetitive decision tree model). Both injection strategies yield more QALYs than IPP: correct. Currency and price year are not stated in the paper, and the page does not claim them.

## 2. Urethral bulking agents
Casteleijn 2023 (ref 36): SUPPORTED, with one optional wording fix.
- 284 women (131 PDMS-U, 153 MUS), international multicentre prospective two-arm cohort, 12-month cost-effectiveness analysis; EUR 3,567 vs 6,688; ICER 15,598 per IIQ QALY and 37,408 per EQ-5D-5L QALY; WTP 25,000: 84% (IIQ) and 99% (EQ-5D-5L). All match.
- Thesis chapter (same text): "nonrestricted grant from Urogyn Bv ... and a grant from ZonMW" (so "partly funded" is right). "Costs were expressed in Euros in 2021." "The Dutch costing guideline was used to determine the relevant unit costs." "direct medical costs, out-of-pocket expenses and the indirect non-medical costs of productivity loss." 
- The 84% and 99% come from the adjusted net-benefit regression, as "% of bootstrap samples". Optional exact wording: CORRECT-AS: "At a willingness-to-pay threshold of EUR 25,000 per QALY, the sling had the higher net benefit in 84% of bootstrap samples of the adjusted analysis using IIQ QALYs, whereas Urolastic did in 99% using EQ-5D-5L QALYs.<sup>[[36]](#ref36)</sup>" Reason: the source reports "in 84% of bootstrap samples, MUS yielded a higher net benefit"; the page's "cost-effective in 84% of adjusted analyses" is a loose paraphrase.
Kunkle 2015 (ref 35): SUPPORTED. Abstract: "MUS cost $436,465 more than BA for every 100 women treated in 1 year ... ICER of $70,400 per utility gained"; "decision tree"; "1-year time horizon"; WTP $50,000; patients without urethral hypermobility. The unit is literally "per utility gained"; the paper does not say "per QALY", so keep "per utility gained". Perspective and currency year are not in the abstract; page does not claim them.

## 3. PFUI opener: Tausch SUPPORTED; Johnsen "US" UNVERIFIABLE; direction OK
- Tausch 2017 (ref 72): SPT + delayed urethroplasty $9,743 vs PER $11,043; PER preferred only at success of 40% or better; "hospital and operating room costs and Medicare reimbursement rates"; institutional database (UT Southwestern authors). Supported.
- Johnsen 2017 (ref 73): abstract gives five strategies, 2-year follow-up, "PER was preferred over SPT placement in all iterations of the model", PER + single DVIU least costly at "$17,493 per unobstructed voider". Opposite conclusion to Tausch: confirmed. The abstract does not state the country, payer or cost source (dollars only; Vanderbilt and Texas affiliations). "US" for Johnsen could not be confirmed from an openly readable text (Springer blocked).
- CORRECT-AS: "Two decision models of pelvic fracture urethral injury reached opposite conclusions on primary endoscopic realignment (PER)." Reason: country of the Johnsen model is not stated in the abstract. Keep "US" only if the Springer full text is checked. Quote: "PER was preferred over SPT placement in all iterations of the model."

## 4. Peyronie's Cost and Value (refs 28, 29, 30): SUPPORTED
- Cordon 2017: TreeAge decision tree; "billing department using real-world patient data"; plication $3,039 vs injection pathway $25,856 (moderate) and $26,375 (severe). Country not stated in the abstract (US institutional authors); "US" is an inference but consistent with Medicare-era billing data.
- Walton 2022: MarketScan Commercial Claims 2009-2019; medians $1,856 to $3,196 plication, $2,233 to $3,631 grafting, $6,940 to $8,895 per CCH cycle. All match. Note: an erratum exists (Sex Med 2022;10(5):100551); its content was not readable; the PubMed abstract figures match the page.
- Harvey 2020: NHS; IMPRESS +£3,832.77 (143.7%), London +£70.77 (2.7%); "£548.41 per vial" cost-neutral; repeat surgery included. Match.
- Opener "Surgery cost less than CCH in the three analyses" holds (London protocol is only 2.7% more for CCH, still more).

## 6. NLUTD spinal cord injury
(a) Shamout 2023 (ref 15): SUPPORTED. "Markov model ... lifetime horizon", "public healthcare perspective", Montreal, Canadian dollars; CIC cost savings $330 vs SPC and $2,496 vs UC with more QALYs (1.77, 1.96).
(b) Carlson 2013 and Hamid 2015 (refs 30, 31): PARTLY UNVERIFIABLE.
- Hamid (PMC): SCI ICER "£1,767/QALY"; MS "£6,422"; "adapted from the US model by Carlson et al."; "This study was funded by Allergan Limited (UK)"; abstract: "Drug costs (2013) and administration costs (NHS Reference Costs 2011-2012)", 5-year horizon, NHS perspective, 3.5% discounting. All supported.
- Carlson (abstract): Markov, "time horizon of the model was 3 years", direct medical costs, SCI "$2182" (unit unlabeled, ICER context), MS $32,268/QALY. Not stated in any text I could open: Allergan funding, payer perspective. Hamid's text does not describe Carlson's funding or perspective.
- CORRECT-AS: "OnabotulinumtoxinA was cost-effective against best supportive care in the SCI subgroups of two Markov models of anticholinergic-refractory NDO: the ICER was $2,182 in a US 3-year model (published 2013) and £1,767 per QALY gained with onabotulinumtoxinA plus best supportive care versus best supportive care alone in an Allergan-funded UK NHS 5-year adaptation using 2013 drug costs and 2011 to 2012 NHS administration costs (published 2015).<sup>[[30]](#ref30)[[31]](#ref31)</sup>" Reason: Allergan funding is confirmed only for the UK model; US "payer" and funding of Carlson not confirmed (drop "payer"). Restore "Allergan-funded" for Carlson only if the Clin Ther funding statement is read.
(c) Padmanabhan 2011 (ref 32): SUPPORTED. "health care payor perspective", "Reimbursements were derived from the average of insurance carriers", "BTX-A treatment was less expensive over 5 years, costing $28,065", "effect lasted for >5.1 months", "AC complication rate<14%". NICE CG148 calls it "a five year study from a US payer perspective", confirming "US".
(d) NICE 2012 (ref 33): MOSTLY SUPPORTED; perspective UNVERIFIABLE. CG148 full guideline: "AC is the cost-effective option when compared to botulinum toxin (BTX) and no treatment ... using a lifetime horizon"; evidence statements: "Augmentation cystoplasty is cost effective compared to botulinum toxin type A in patients where it is suitable"; "Botulinum toxin type A is cost effective when compared to no treatment" (base case 2 for patients unsuitable for AC). The appendix text I could read states no perspective for this model ("NHS and PSS" appears only for a different model, UTI prevention).
- CORRECT-AS: "A UK lifetime decision model published by NICE in 2012 found augmentation cystoplasty cost-effective against botulinum toxin A in patients for whom it was suitable, and toxin cost-effective against no treatment when augmentation was unsuitable.<sup>[[33]](#ref33)</sup>" Reason: "NHS and Personal Social Services" is not stated for this model. Quote: "AC is the cost-effective option ... using a lifetime horizon."

## 8. Metal long-term stents
- Singapore, Law 2025 (ref 3): SUPPORTED. "247 stents were placed in 63 patients with CUO over the 4-year study period"; "cost per dwelling day SGD 7.82 ± 10.44 vs. SGD 8.23 ± 20.50, P = 0.888"; malignant and benign; published 2025;66(1). Country not stated in the abstract (journal and SGD indicate Singapore).
- Memokath, Eaton Turner 2018 (refs 2, 21): MOSTLY SUPPORTED. "EAC's model estimated ... savings of at least £1619 per patient over 5 years compared with double-J stents"; "Overall, Memokath-051 is likely to be cost saving in patients not indicated for reconstructive surgery and those expected to require a ureteral stent for at least 30 months". Full text says the scope requires an NHS and PSS perspective and that the company model took a hospital perspective; it does not say what the EAC model used.
- CORRECT-AS: "NICE's 2018 medical-technology evaluation described Memokath-051 as likely to be cost saving in patients not indicated for reconstructive surgery who are expected to need a stent for at least 30 months; the cost model revised by its External Assessment Centre estimated savings of at least £1,619 per patient over 5 years versus double-J stents.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>" Reason: the EAC model's NHS and PSS perspective is not stated. Also note ref 2 is the 2022 HTG651 page; the 2018 wording is from ref 21.
- Cooper 2024 (ref 41): SUPPORTED. "Markov model ... parameterised to 5 years ... UK care system perspective, with 3.5% discounting"; "cost-saving of £2164.74 and a health gain of +0.046 QALYs"; "89.3% probability ... at £20 000"; "DC is a salaried employee of Cook Medical". A journal-wide erratum (bco2.482, 2024-12-30) lists this article; it appears to be a blanket correction, content not readable.

## 9. Pyeloplasty (refs 43, 76, 77, 78): SUPPORTED
- Seideman 2012: eight studies, 181 RP and 145 LP; mean direct costs $10,635 vs $9,065; cost data "from our institution" (hospital cost centers). Country not stated in the abstract (US inferred from dollars and authors). The page does not claim "adult" for it; the abstract does not give the age of the included studies' patients.
- Moretto 2023: full text confirms age 18 or older, Rome, "our urological referral center", 43 robotic vs 48 open; direct cost per procedure and hospital stay 2,373 EUR higher for robotic; year of prices not stated; page does not claim it.
- Gettman 2003: decision tree with "our institution" costs: retrograde ureteroscopic endopyelotomy $3,842, lap pyeloplasty $7,026, open $7,119. Matches. Age and country not stated in the abstract; the page claims neither adults nor a price year ("US institutional" is inferred).
- Jacobs 2017: MarketScan adults 18 to 64 (data 2002 to 2010); adjusted mean $16,379, $22,421, $22,843 for the episode from 30 days before to 30 days after surgery. Match.
- Optional strictness: replace "at a US institution" and "in a US institutional decision tree" with "at one institution" if the country must be source-stated; consistent with the author affiliations (UT Southwestern) so low risk.

## 12. HBOT (refs 42, 43): PARTLY SUPPORTED
- Feldmeier 2024 abstract: "reduced unadjusted Medicare costs of $5,059 per patient within the first year after completion of HBO2 treatment"; "When at least 40 treatments were provided, cost savings per patient increased to $11,548". Only the $5,059 figure is labeled unadjusted; the $11,548 is not described as adjusted or unadjusted in the abstract (full text not openly available). 3,309 patients, Oct 2014 to Dec 2019, public-access Medicare data: match.
- CORRECT-AS: "HBOT was associated with lower follow-up Medicare costs than conventional therapy for radiation cystitis in a US retrospective cohort of 3,309 patients (2014 to 2019 claims, published 2024): unadjusted costs were $5,059 lower per patient during the first year after HBOT completion, and savings rose to $11,548 per patient among those who received at least 40 sessions.<sup>[[42]](#ref42)</sup>" Reason: the source calls the first figure unadjusted and does not label the second; also "Medicare costs" is the source's word.
- Degener 2015 (full text): "approximately €200 per HBOT session"; "median costs per patient in this study were €6,800 (34 HBOT sessions)"; 15 patients, 12 after radiotherapy, 2 chemotherapy, 1 both; median 34 treatments. Match. Country and price year are not stated in the text (author affiliations are German). Optional: "a retrospective series of 15 patients from German centers" is safer than "German retrospective series"; also "median" applies to the per-patient cost.
