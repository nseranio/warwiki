## 2. Section text, ready to paste

### Metal & Long-Term Ureteral Stents

```mdx
### Cost and Value

Resonance had lower calculated 12-month maintenance costs than polymer exchanges in a US single-center retrospective analysis of 13 patients with benign obstruction: $11,183 versus $23,999 per patient (published 2010).<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> Memokath-051 had lower modeled costs than double-J stents in a UK NHS and Personal Social Services cost model revised by NICE's External Assessment Centre: at least £1,619 saved per patient over 5 years (published 2018).<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

Metallic and polymer stents had no significant difference in mean cost per dwell day in a Singapore retrospective cohort of 247 stent placements in 63 patients with benign or malignant chronic ureteric obstruction: SGD 7.82 versus SGD 8.23 over a 4-year study period (p = 0.888; published 2025).<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> For malignant ureteral obstruction, Resonance had lower modeled cost and more QALYs than polymer stents in a 5-year UK care-system Markov model by a Cook Medical-affiliated author: £2,164.74 saved and 0.046 QALYs gained per patient, with an 89.3% probability of cost-effectiveness at £20,000 per QALY (published 2024).<sup>[[41]](#ref41)</sup>
```

### Pyeloplasty

```mdx
## Cost and Value

Robotic pyeloplasty had higher modeled direct hospital costs than laparoscopic pyeloplasty at a US institution: a decision model using outcomes from eight studies (181 robotic and 145 laparoscopic patients) estimated $10,635 versus $9,065 per procedure (published 2012).<sup>[[76]](#ref76)</sup> At an Italian referral center, a retrospective comparison of 43 adults undergoing robotic and 48 undergoing open pyeloplasty found €2,373 higher mean direct cost for the robotic procedure and hospital stay (published 2023).<sup>[[43]](#ref43)</sup>

Endopyelotomy cost less than pyeloplasty in a US institutional decision tree (published 2003): modeled treatment and follow-up costs were $3,842 for retrograde ureteroscopic endopyelotomy, $7,026 for laparoscopic pyeloplasty and $7,119 for open pyeloplasty.<sup>[[77]](#ref77)</sup> In a US MarketScan retrospective claims analysis of commercially insured adults aged 18–64 (published 2017), adjusted mean expenditures from 30 days before to 30 days after surgery were $16,379 after endopyelotomy, $22,421 after open pyeloplasty and $22,843 after minimally invasive pyeloplasty.<sup>[[78]](#ref78)</sup>
```

No claim of a negative formal XML check is made. Run both checks before applying/publishing; then run `npm run refs:new` on the applied pages.
- The four new DOIs above were transcribed from PubMed records and the journal version-of-record metadata, but **Crossref resolution remains unverified**. Existing reference 43 (Moretto) and existing metal references 3, 14 and 21 are reused.
- López-Huertas 2010's abstract says $23,999 versus $11,183, then calls the difference $10,394 (43%); those numbers are arithmetically inconsistent. The draft uses the two reported totals and does not repeat the reported difference or percentage.
- Cooper 2024 is explicitly maker-affiliated: the PubMed affiliation lists Cook Medical for Cooper, and the COI statement calls her a salaried Cook employee. The Memokath paper describes a PNN Medical company submission subsequently revised by NICE's External Assessment Centre; the section reports the latter model.
- The stricture home page has no directly applicable published economic comparison of chronic stent versus nephrostomy versus reconstruction in this search. The NICE Memokath assessment compared surgery in a model but chiefly addresses patients who are not reconstruction candidates; it did not evaluate nephrostomy. No Cost and Value text is drafted for the home page.
- **Considered, not used:** Taylor 2012, PMID [22360415](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22360415/), US single-center metal versus polymer charges, overlaps the US Resonance estimates already represented by López-Huertas; Frederick 2017, PMID [37592614](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37592614/), US multicenter estimate, useful sensitivity to 3- versus 6-month polymer exchange but would overload this short device section; Baumgarten 2014, PMID [24856490](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24856490/), retrospective estimate with mixed malignancy/diversion indications; Schuil 2026, PMID [41830115](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41830115/), trial-based nephrostomy versus JJ stent economics in acute obstructing stones rather than chronic stricture; pediatric pyeloplasty cost studies, including PMID [23017518](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23017518/) and [29530407](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29530407/), excluded by the adult scope; Gettman et al. 2006, PMID [16552199](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16552199/), small prospective robotic versus laparoscopic comparison superseded for cost selection by the larger Seideman decision model.
- Jacobs 2017 pools laparoscopic and robotic surgery into “minimally invasive pyeloplasty,” so its cost estimate must not be labeled as a robotic-specific figure. The Gettman decision tree does not specify a numeric follow-up horizon in the abstract. Price years are absent for most studies, so the section uses publication years as instructed.
