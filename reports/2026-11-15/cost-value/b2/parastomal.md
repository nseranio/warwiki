## 2. Section text

## Cost and Value

Prophylactic mesh had lower mean costs and fewer clinical parastomal hernias than no mesh in a Swedish health care provider analysis of 159 ileal-conduit patients from a randomized trial (2020 euros, within-trial follow-up until death or August 2020): the mean total cost difference was −€2,047 (95% CI −€16,441 to €12,348), with clinical hernia in 6/80 (7.5%) versus 17/79 (21.5%).<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup> A Markov model of prophylactic mesh at radical cystectomy and ileal conduit creation (2022 US dollars, published 2023) estimated an additional $897 and 0.49 QALYs with mesh versus no mesh across bladder cancer stages I–IV, an ICER of $2,114.71 per QALY gained with mesh versus no mesh; results were sensitive to the probability of mesh infection.<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

For permanent end colostomy after abdominoperineal resection for rectal cancer, a Canadian 5-year cohort Markov model (2012 Canadian dollars, published 2014) found prophylactic mesh lower cost and more QALYs than no mesh in stage I–III disease; in stage IV disease, mesh cost CAD$495 more and gained 0.05 QALYs, an ICER of CAD$10,818 per QALY gained with mesh versus no mesh.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup> A UK NHS lifetime decision model of end colostomy (published 2021), informed by 13 randomized trials, found synthetic mesh lower cost and more QALYs than biologic or no mesh across rectal cancer stages I–IV; at a willingness-to-pay threshold of £20,000 per QALY, incremental net monetary benefit with synthetic versus no mesh ranged from £1,706 in stage I to £684 in stage IV.<sup>[[32]](#ref32)</sup>

For parastomal hernia repair, a post hoc analysis of 108 patients in a multicenter randomized biologic-versus-synthetic mesh trial (published 2022) found higher median hospital and mesh costs through 30 days with biologic mesh, while 2-year recurrence and quality of life were similar between mesh types.<sup>[[33]](#ref33)</sup>

## 6. Notes

- The ileal-conduit evidence meets the two-study threshold: one trial-based Swedish analysis and one US-dollar model. The latter does not state a perspective or horizon in its abstract; those fields are omitted. Its assumed preventive benefit predates the negative 2024 and 2025 conduit trials already discussed on the page.
- The Swedish trial measured hernias prevented, not QALYs. Its mean cost difference was imprecise, with a 95% CI crossing zero; the section reports that interval and does not call mesh “dominant.”
- The Canadian colostomy model does not name a perspective in its abstract; the section omits one. The UK model does not state a price year; the section uses “published 2021.” The repair analysis does not give numerical costs, a price year, or a named perspective in its abstract; no amounts or currency are added.
- Mohiuddin et al. reports a correction to estimated QALYs for all three interventions, added October 5, 2021 after first online publication. The accessible PubMed abstract includes the correction notice; check the formal erratum relation during XML review.
- PREVENT's early report ([PMID 26511879](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26511879/)) names cost-effectiveness as a secondary endpoint but reports no economic result; excluded. STOMA-CONST's protocol and efficacy report do not supply a published economic analysis; excluded. The [CIPHER model](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34331840/) is included, not treated as a completed trial-based CIPHER evaluation.
- Findlay et al. ([PMID 29732505](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29732505/)) modeled stoma costs from older randomized trials, mostly colostomies; omitted because the later CIPHER model addresses the same prevention choices. Odensten et al. ([PMID 37563916](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37563916/)) compared appliance costs by hernia status after colostomy, not total costs of mesh versus no mesh; omitted. No comparative cost evaluation of Sugarbaker versus keyhole, resiting versus in-situ repair, or open versus minimally invasive conduit repair was identified.
