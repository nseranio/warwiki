## Things to know about the data

- The WHO commentary gives no separate strength label for the recommendation ("is recommended as an alternative to longer duration"); only the overall certainty of evidence (low, GRADE) is stated. The `<Rec>` badge therefore carries the certainty statement, worded as closely as possible to "The overall certainty of evidence ... was graded as low." The commentary does not name Barone 2015 explicitly; it says the review found two randomized trials in about 700 women, and the Torloni abstract gives 684 combined patients (524 here plus a second trial). The page says only that the guideline panel's review found two trials; it does not claim the guideline "cites" Barone by name.
- Primary denominators: Table 2 shows 10/250 and 8/251 for the primary outcome, i.e. randomized minus those lost to follow-up (and one ineligible). The secondary breakdown figures (23/261 and 13/261 versus 20/262 and 12/262) show that 13 and 12 women already had a positive dye test 7 days after catheter removal, and 23 minus 13 = 10 and 20 minus 12 = 8 equals the primary events. So the primary denominators appear to include women whose repair had already failed at the day-7 post-removal test, who could not have a primary event. The paper does not explain this. I reported the paper's numbers as written and gave the all-breakdown secondary result beside it; a clinical reviewer may want to look at this.
- The results sentence says the 12 women with multiple fistulas "were excluded", but the flow diagram's analysed numbers (250 = 261 - 11, 251 = 263 - 11 - 1) and the Discussion limitation ("included ... 2% of our participants") indicate they stayed in the analysis. The page follows the Discussion and flow diagram.
- Percentages in the file are computed from n/N to one decimal (the paper rounds to whole numbers: 4% versus 3% for the primary outcome). Risk differences, relative risks and CIs are quoted from Table 2. No P values are reported for the noninferiority test, so `effect.p` is absent.
- Not entered: antibiotic prophylaxis "194 (74%)" (the paper's wording leaves the denominator ambiguous), vaginal pack duration (ambiguous group), the interim analysis detail beyond one sentence, and subgroup data (none reported).
- Relations: none. No other trial on the site's list addresses fistula drainage or catheter duration.

## WARWIKI pages that cite this trial (grep for the DOI and "Barone")

All of the sentences below agree with the source unless a disagreement is listed.

1. `docs/04-surgical-techniques/04h-fistula-repair/female/conservative-vvf-management.mdx` line 51: 524 women, simple fistula closed at day 7, 7 versus 14 days, 10/250 (4.0%) versus 8/251 (3.2%), dye test from 8 days after removal to 3 months, noninferiority met. 2. `docs/03-clinical-conditions/03f-fistulas/in-females/vesicovaginal.mdx` line 147 (refs 39, 40): "WHO recommends 7-10 days after repair of a simple obstetric urinary fistula". The WHO wording is that 7-10 days is "recommended as an alternative to longer duration of catheterization" (BJOG commentary), a softer statement; consider "recommends 7 to 10 days as an alternative to longer drainage". Numbers in the trial sentence (524, 501 per-protocol, 10/250, 8/251, radiation and complex fistulas excluded, "mostly obstetric") agree with the source (obstetric cause 93.1% and 96.2%). Same line: "A 2026 IUGA/ICS Delphi consensus advises a minimum of 10-14 days of catheter drainage ... and a dye test at catheter removal" (ref 58): I could not open the full text and a search of its public description found no catheter-duration statement, so this is unverified here (it is not about Barone, but it sits beside it and appears to be in tension with a 7-day WHO option). Same line, last sentence: "a cystogram can miss a persistent fistula if it has no voiding phase or if the fistula is at the bladder neck", cited in part to Barone (ref 40). The Barone paper does not mention cystography (its only occurrence is a reference title in the bibliography); the trial used dye tests. The statement needs a different source or ref 40 should come off that sentence.
3. `docs/03-clinical-conditions/03f-fistulas/in-females/obstetric.mdx` line 180 (ref 39): 524 women randomized after a simple repair stayed closed on day 7; "multiple, radiation-related and cancer-related fistulas were excluded" (multiple fistula was added only on July 31, 2012; 12 women with multiple fistulas were randomized before then and remain in the groups, PDF pp2, 5, 6-7); 10/250 (4%) versus 8/251 (3%); 10% margin. Numbers agree. The same line gives "Delphi ... statement 37 ... noting that 7 days may suffice for simple cases" (ref 36): not verified. Also note that ref37 on this page is the WHO labour care guide, unrelated to the line above.
4. `docs/04-surgical-techniques/04h-fistula-repair/female/urethrovaginal-fistula-repair.mdx` line 148 (ref 18): "524 women with simple genital fistulas already closed at postoperative day seven supported seven rather than fourteen days in that selected population". Agrees; the trial did not include urethral-fistula-specific analyses (urethral fistulas were 8/261 and 14/262, PDF p4, Table 1), which the page does not claim.

Other "Barone" hits in `docs` (Shurtleff and Barone urethral prolapse; Barone B diversion review; Peyronie's; Leto Barone) are different authors. The Frajzyngier, Ruminjo and Barone 2012 AJOG review in `vesicovaginal.mdx` (ref 19) is not this trial.
