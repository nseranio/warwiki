# BMG Harvest Closure Meta-analysis: Closure versus Non-closure of the Buccal Mucosa Graft Harvest Site

<TrialBottomLine id="bmg-closure-meta" />

The **BMG Harvest Closure Meta-analysis** by Güler pooled seven trials (373 patients, four of them quasi-randomized) that compared nonclosure with primary closure of the intraoral graft harvest site in adults having urethroplasty, with oral pain, difficult mouth opening, perioral numbness, salivation change and diet tolerance as outcomes. Nonclosure lowered pain on day 0–1 (standardized mean difference −0.67, 95% CI −1.08 to −0.26), and the overall pooled estimates for all five outcomes showed no difference.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

<TrialQuickHit id="bmg-closure-meta" />

## Design

The review searched PubMed, Embase, Scopus, the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials, Web of Science, Global Index Medicus, ClinicalTrials.gov and Google Scholar in January 2022. Of 1,150 records, 670 were screened by title and abstract, 29 were assessed in full text and 7 were included. Eligible patients were over 18 years old with urethral stricture and unilateral intraoral graft harvest for urethroplasty; studies in women, adolescents and children were excluded. One reviewer screened the records, and extraction by one reviewer was checked by a second.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The authors named six primary outcomes: difficult mouth opening and oral pain (time points to 6 months), oral numbness (to 12 months), salivation change (to 6 months), liquid tolerance (to 7 days) and solid diet tolerance (to 6 months). Pain scores on different scales were pooled as standardized mean differences, and dichotomous outcomes as risk ratios. A fixed-effect model was used when heterogeneity was below 50% (I²) and a random-effects model above it, with sensitivity analyses that removed the trials driving heterogeneity.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The seven trials were from Canada (Rourke 2012), the United Kingdom (Wong 2014), Germany (Soave 2018), India (Muruganandam 2009 and Gulani 2019), Egypt (Abdallah 2021) and Bangladesh (Kabir 2017), and ranged from 30 to 135 patients. Soave 2018 was the largest, with 72 patients in the nonclosure arm and 63 in the closure arm. Across the seven trials, 191 patients had nonclosure and 182 had closure.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Results

For oral pain, the pooled estimate across all time points and all seven trials was a standardized mean difference of −0.20 (95% CI −0.41 to 0.01; I² 64%) for nonclosure, which was not significant. On day 0–1, nonclosure had less pain (−0.67, 95% CI −1.08 to −0.26; P = 0.001; I² 70%). After removing Muruganandam 2009 and Gulani 2019 as the sources of heterogeneity, the day 0–1 estimate remained significant (−0.42, 95% CI −0.80 to −0.04; P = 0.03; I² 53%). On day 3–7 (0.10, 95% CI −0.15 to 0.34) and at 1–6 months (−0.05, 95% CI −0.26 to 0.17), pain did not differ.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

For difficult mouth opening, the pooled risk ratio across time points was 0.89 for nonclosure (95% CI 0.76 to 1.03; P = 0.11; 205/426 versus 230/415 events). The one significant time point was 6 months, with difficulty in 15/90 (17%) of patients with nonclosure and 26/93 (28%) with closure (risk ratio 0.59, 95% CI 0.36 to 0.97; P = 0.04). That estimate rests on Soave 2018 (14/41 versus 24/42); in the other two trials, one had 1/25 versus 2/25 and one had no events in either arm. At day 5–7 the ratio was 0.87 (95% CI 0.75 to 1.00; P = 0.06).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

The pooled risk ratio for perioral numbness was 0.88 (95% CI 0.72 to 1.08; 99/314 versus 104/300 events), for alteration of salivation 1.05 (95% CI 0.82 to 1.35; 75/237 versus 68/232) and for tolerance of liquid or solid diet 1.01 (95% CI 0.95 to 1.08; 321/532 versus 330/500). None differed by time point.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Surgery and treatment delivery

The pooled trials harvested the graft from a unilateral cheek in three trials, from a unilateral cheek with bilateral cheek or lower-lip harvest for some patients in two, from the tongue in one (Gulani 2019) and from an unreported site in one. The graft was ovoid in two trials, rectangular in two and not described in three. Five trials infiltrated a local anesthetic with epinephrine before harvest, and hemostasis was by bipolar cautery or diathermy, often with epinephrine-soaked gauze, in the trials that reported it.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

Closure used continuous suture in most trials and interrupted suture in two; one trial did not report the technique. The nonclosure arm was left to heal by secondary intention. Pain was scored on a 10- or 11-point scale in three trials and a 5-point scale in one; two did not define the scale. Some trials added oral rinses (chlorhexidine or chamomile) after surgery.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Adverse events

The review measured oral morbidity rather than surgical complications. Perioral numbness was reported in 99/314 (32%) of patient assessments with nonclosure and 104/300 (35%) with closure, pooled across day 3–7 and 1–12 months, and salivation change in 75/237 (32%) and 68/232 (29%) across its time points. Patients can be counted at more than one time point in these totals. The report lists increased salivation, parotid sialadenitis and mucous retention cysts as possible salivary complications and advises identifying and preserving the Stenson duct at the level of the second molar during harvest.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Limitations

Four of the seven trials used quasi-randomization, and the risk-of-bias assessment rated all seven as high risk for blinding of outcome assessment and six of seven as high risk for blinding of participants and personnel. Donor sites were mixed (unilateral cheek, bilateral cheek, lower lip and, in one trial, tongue), as were graft shapes, closure techniques and pain scales, and the review had no subgroup analyses by graft size, shape, suture or donor site. Funnel plots were not fully symmetrical. The report's text and forest plots differ for the pooled numbness, salivation, diet and total pain estimates; the forest-plot values are reported here. The 6-month mouth-opening result came from one trial, and the authors state that more studies are needed to confirm it.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## In conversation

<InConversation id="bmg-closure-meta" />

A systematic review of five randomized trials (346 randomized patients) rated the evidence as low or very low certainty and estimated day 1 pain from three trials (199 participants) as 0.24 standard deviations lower with nonclosure (95% CI 0.61 lower to 0.12 higher), an interval that includes no difference.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Güler’s analysis found lower day 0–1 pain with nonclosure (standardized mean difference −0.67, 95% CI −1.08 to −0.26).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup> That review also reported a possible increase in cosmetic defects at 6 months in one trial with 84 participants (risk ratio 2.40; 95% CI 0.93 to 6.22; low certainty), an outcome not analyzed here.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
