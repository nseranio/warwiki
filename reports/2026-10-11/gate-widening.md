# Claim gate widening — October 11, 2026

## Scope and method

Scanned all 1,102 content pages used by the claim gate (sections 01–05, 07, 08) at the current working-tree state, including the already-modified pages. A new classification means the old `GUIDE` expression did **not** tag the unit as `guideline`, but the widened detector does. The scan uses the gate's sentence/table-row extraction, citation binding, and v2 keys. The October 9 sweep's 760 ungated AUA/EAU/SUFU/AUGS sentences motivated this change; its count is a prior-day, sections-01–05 cohort and is not directly comparable with this whole-site, current-tree inventory.

*Incremental counts assign each unit to its first matching new pattern in table order; patterns overlap. Total: **1,505 newly guideline-classified units on 555 pages**. Of these, **1,235 units were entirely absent from the previous gate extraction**; 270 already had a number, dose, or absolute classification. The 20 examples per pattern below are a fixed-seed random sample (`20261011` plus the pattern's table index) from its incremental set, so they do not repeat across patterns.

The old society regex limited the name-to-verb span to 80 characters and stopped at a period or table pipe. The new society/action rule searches the entire extracted sentence or row, including parenthetical society names. Society acronyms are case sensitive: otherwise ordinary “who” was mistaken for WHO. The strength rule accepts named recommendation grades and `level 1b`-style evidence labels without a named body. It excludes phrases such as “grade A leaks.” The generic rule requires a recommendation verb, excluding the noun phrase “guideline recommendations.” The statement rule recognizes numbered statements. A broad bare `should` pattern and unrestricted `expert opinion` pattern were dropped during calibration because they matched ordinary advice and commentary without a guideline attribution.

The sample flags **3/80 (3.75%)** as off-target for a *standalone substantive guideline statement*: one general white-paper operations suggestion, one grade-only fragment, and one navigation description. Per-pattern rates appear above. These are screening estimates from 20 random examples per pattern, not a measured site-wide precision guarantee. None of the retained patterns was mostly non-claims in its sample. Other potential false positives can remain, especially long table rows that mix source guidance with trial results.
