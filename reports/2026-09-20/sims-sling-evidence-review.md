# Single-incision sling: SIMS/Cochrane evidence check (20 September 2026)

The complete current [mini-sling page](../../docs/04-surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/single-incision-mini-sling.mdx) was reread, including technique, outcomes, references and its video entry. The earlier [NICE/AUA route check](female-sling-route-guidance-review.md) is reused, not repeated. The current [EAU sling recommendations](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/non-neurogenic-female-luts/chapter/disease-management) directly support intraoperative cystoscopy, now included in the operative sequence. This is a **partial page audit**, not device-wide clinical clearance.

Primary reading: the open [NIHR SIMS HTA](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK587586/) PDF, especially the scientific summary, chapter 2 methods/eligibility, chapter 3 baseline/operative results, chapter 4 Table 7 and Figure 8, chapter 5 Tables 13–14, and discussion of safety/limitations. It randomized **600**, with **596 after four post-randomization exclusions**. Ajust/Altis were compared with a mix of retropubic/transobturator slings in selected women. Patient-reported success met the 10% noninferiority margin at 15 and 36 months; this is not an all-device or complex-patient conclusion. Three-year dyspareunia was 17/145 versus 7/145 among direct-question respondents, with a baseline imbalance; exposure was 9/276 versus 5/261 (p=0.373), and further SUI surgery 7/276 versus 3/261. Shorter hospital stay and lower early pain were shown; return to normal activities within 28 days was not significantly different. The trial's general operative sequence supports the page's broad steps, but not a substitute for a product IFU.

The full accessible [2023 Cochrane review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10604512/) was checked in the abstract, Summary-of-Findings tables 2–3, discussion/applicability and practice conclusion. Its primary analysis excludes withdrawn TVT-Secur. Twelve-month subjective outcomes are similar, strongest versus transobturator slings; long-term harms/durability and device heterogeneity remain material limitations. The [2025 Zhou meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40465798/) was available only as a PubMed abstract in this pass (24 RCTs, 3,687 women, mean 13.42-month follow-up); publisher/Ovid full text returned payment/access errors. Its nonsignificant cure comparison does **not** itself prove noninferiority. Its organ-injury signal was kept as a comparator-dependent pooled finding, not an absolute safety claim. The [Altis manufacturer procedural guide](https://www.coloplastprofessional.us/globalassets/hcp/interventional-urology/pdfs/female-continence/altis-procedural-technique_pm-04640.pdf) was indexed but direct full-PDF retrieval returned 403; it explicitly says it does not replace the IFU. No IFU review is claimed.

Narrow page changes correct the enrollment denominator, make trial device/population/endpoints explicit, contextualize harms and early recovery, and distinguish trial noninferiority from pooled nonsignificance. No indication section, operative step, table, reference, link, pearl or video was removed. The remaining exact actions are to obtain current model-specific IFUs/availability for named devices and the full 2025 meta-analysis/supplements where legitimately accessible, then check any consequential device-selection or safety claims; shared TOMUS/Cochrane MUS remains queued for the companion route pages.
