## Source and claim findings

- [Toronto's authored operative case](https://pie.med.utoronto.ca/TVASurg/project/vaginal-hysterectomy/) (case description, uterosacral/cardinal/vascular sequence, written Heaney-step description) and the [*Atlas of Pelvic Surgery* vaginal-hysterectomy technique](https://atlasofpelvicsurgery.org/5Uterus/9TotalVaginalHysterectomy/chap5sec9.html) (text/figure captions describing uterosacral clamp-tip and midpoint passes, distinct simple versus fixation and separate double ties for certain adnexal pedicles) were read at the relevant text. The **videos and actual atlas figure images were not independently viewed**. These descriptions support a transfixing Heaney-type stitch for selected pedicles, not the page's former universal second wrap, universal vascular application, or “no single technique is as reliable” claim. The [authored prolapse-technique chapter excerpt](https://abdominalkey.com/techniques-for-vaginal-hysterectomy-and-vaginal-trachelectomy-in-patients-with-pelvic-organ-prolapse/) corroborates upper-pedicle and midpoint passes and distinguishes later vascular-pedicle fixation; its figure/video content and gated continuation were not read. Heaney's 1948 original [PubMed bibliographic record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18872872/) was previously indexed only, not read in full. The page now preserves a usable operative explanation while distinguishing a separate second ligature, laparoscopic energy/clip use, and formal prolapse suspension.
- The human urinary-diversion [DeCastro/Large/Steinberg authored chapter excerpt](https://oncohemakey.com/urinary-diversions-and-reconstructions/) was read at section 25A ileal-conduit construction and Fig. 25A.1 **caption text** (not artwork). It describes a two-layer Lembert **or** Parker-Kerr proximal-conduit closure. The [NCBI Bookshelf operative overview](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK560483/) was read at the ileal-conduit technique and leak-assessment passages. Neither establishes Parker-Kerr as the closure of every Studer afferent limb or neobladder configuration, nor superiority in irradiated/redo bowel. The [BSAVA technical chapter search-index excerpt](https://www.bsavalibrary.com/content/chapter/10.22233/9781905319756.chap22) identifies a Cushing-over-forceps and Lembert-oversew pattern; the **full veterinary chapter and Fig. 22.13 art were inaccessible**, and veterinary technique is not human outcomes evidence. The accessible [1909 original case opening](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/article-abstract/429344) independently calls this a Cushing right-angled basting stitch over clamps; the rest is gated. These checks demonstrated that the page's full-thickness “baseball” bites and mandatory 5–7 interrupted second-layer stitches were too specific or technically mistaken. Corrected to a non-luminal Cushing first layer and Lembert second layer, keeping variant wording where full atlas access is lacking.
- [Later surgical-journal bibliography](https://www.americanjournalofsurgery.com/article/S0002-9610%2842%2990664-0/abstract) identifies **Parker EM and Kerr HH**, *Bull Johns Hopkins Hosp* 1908;19:132–137, not “Walter Parker and Harry Kerr.” The 1908 **original full article and illustrations were not accessed**. The claims that the method predates Connell/Lembert, arose due to stapler costs, dominated training, takes 8–15 minutes, has a universal 3–4-mm bite/leak threshold, or is safest for irradiated bowel have no applicable sourced package here. Those particular assertions were removed or qualified; the history, technical steps, use table, cross-links and original two references were retained. [Zollinger 9th-edition metadata](https://books.google.com/books/about/Zollinger_s_Atlas_of_Surgical_Operations.html?id=9on8IiReVxoC) identifies Zollinger Jr **and Ellison**, correcting an inherited bibliographic error. [Emory's bibliography](https://med.emory.edu/departments/surgery/education/center-surgical-anatomy-and-technique/publications.html) confirms Skandalakis 2004. Neither book has been inspected at page/figure level; their exact applicability remains open. No new OpenEvidence query was used.
