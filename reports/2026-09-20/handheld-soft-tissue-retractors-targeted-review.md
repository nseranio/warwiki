# Handheld soft-tissue retractors — 2026-09-21

The complete current MDX, tables and references for Richardson, S retractor, Senn and skin hooks were read. Each remains a **partial claim review, not a clinical clearance**. Practical exposure and instrument-selection teaching was preserved; no OpenEvidence query or publication occurred.

## Focused source/diff findings

- **Richardson:** current Sklar records distinguish a single-ended Richardson from a double-ended Richardson-Eastman. The prior page conflated them and supplied one universal dimension range. Current model dimensions, nonsterile supply and material are now explicit examples rather than class-wide rules. Dillavou and Kvist-Poulsen/Borel concern self-retaining pelvic retraction and cannot establish handheld Richardson neuropathy rates. The instrument eponym remains plausible, but the reviewed historical references do not document a modern-pattern patent or original design account.
- **S retractor:** current Teleflex/Pilling catalog 054620 is a small 13.5 cm double-ended device with 6 × 10 mm and 13.5 × 15 mm blades. The former page instead described a 25–30 cm intermediate-depth abdominal retractor and treated Morris/Eastman as synonyms. Current Morris catalogs show a distinct, much larger abdominal family. The page now makes model identity and actual blade dimensions primary and no longer claims comparative ergonomics, pressure distribution, a mandatory depth hierarchy or S-specific neurovascular outcomes.
- **Senn:** current sharp- and blunt-prong Senn and Miller-Senn product records support the basic double-ended design, small-site/surface-tissue use and model-specific dimensions. No reviewed source establishes that blunt Senn-Miller is the universal reconstructive default, that the instrument distributes force without injury or that every listed genitourinary procedure requires it. Practical examples remain labeled as such.
- **Skin hooks:** the Clark survey supports 85.1% use among 571 dermatologic-surgery respondents, not superior cosmetic results. Talebi-Liasi's 60-member Mohs survey supports 56.7% past-year injury and 14.7% bloodborne exposure among those reporting injury; Donnelly's separate 336-respondent survey supports 85.1% career injury, 40.6% past-year injury among those affected and 64% ever-unreported injury. LoPiccolo found no reported exposures among respondents using blunt hooks but explicitly could not establish firm associations. “Atraumatic,” no microvascular disruption, universal 45-degree/2–3 mm insertion and “always hook, never forceps” claims were removed. A current Sklar urethroplasty set verifies that Joseph and Guthrie hooks are used in that instrument domain.
