## Source access and claim decisions

- [WHO 2025 FGM/C guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK614880/), Recommendations 4–5, counseling and reinfibulation remarks read: Type III deinfibulation is a conditional/low-certainty recommendation; antepartum or intrapartum timing is conditional/very-low-certainty; partner/family involvement is only at the woman's request. No new universal timing/anesthesia rule was added. Annex/GRADE tables and full source package remain unread.
- [Okusanya 2026 original systematic review](https://obgyn.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ijgo.70759), methods, comparison results, Table 2 and discussion read; rendered forest plots and supplement not inspected. Eight studies/3,166 total is **not** the denominator for each endpoint. Emergency cesarean OR 0.16 came from **one n=250** study (45/230 versus 12/20); genital laceration OR 0.48 from three studies/n=1,067. All had serious bias/very-low certainty. No eligible gynecologic/urologic/sexual-health comparative study; no established PPH, episiotomy, prolonged-stage or neonatal-resuscitation reduction. The page now makes these boundaries explicit.
- [Nour 2006 original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16816056/) read: 40 Type III patients operated, **32 telephone respondents**, 94% of respondents recommended; the prior n=40 satisfaction denominator was wrong. Full original article unavailable here; respondent selection and long-term outcomes remain open. Earlier [defibulation recovery review](../2026-09-19/defibulation-recovery-review.md) was reused for Anand/Jones/Berg context without upgrading abstract-level access.
- [Lauber 2021 original five-page PDF](https://verein-lichensclerosus.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/EuropeanJournalperineoplasty16Dec2020-1.pdf), full text, Tables 1–2 and figure legend read; figure graphics not inspected. Of 70 invited, 41 replied, 18 had additional de-adhesion, 37/41 satisfied or very satisfied, 39/41 would recommend, 10/27 answering the dyspareunia item still had pain, and 23/41 needed intensified steroid treatment for LS activity. The text reports dyspareunia p=.02 while Table 2 lists <.001; site retains the text result and flags this internal source discrepancy for clinical clearance. All 70 excised specimens were examined under that study's protocol, not a universal guideline mandate. Dilators from four weeks and maintenance twice weekly were its protocols, not proven universal rules.
- [Rouzier 2002 original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11810083/) read: 64 operated, 50 evaluable; 45/50 dyspareunia and 43/50 sexual-quality improvements. The risk-factor analysis belongs to Rouzier, **not Lauber**. Original full text/methods and adverse-event tables remain open.
- [Burger 2016 original publisher article](https://obgyn.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/aogs.12852), technique/results text and figure legends read, image panels not inspected: 23 posterior operations, four de-hoodings, five anterior grafts; graft donor may be **anterior or posterior vaginal wall**; one graft contracted/keratinized. Steroids were interrupted in that case-series protocol and one LS flare with bullae occurred; causal generalization is not justified. [Gurumurthy 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22397746/) and [Breech 2000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10742669/) original abstracts confirm 25/80% initial improvement and a three-adolescent Surgicel report, respectively. The old table's “ten-year follow-up” was actually Gurumurthy's study **period**.
- [Rangatchew's authored 2017 meeting abstract](https://dspr.dk/Content/uploads/d185b56d-2301-47dc-8b8b-b73ad2620c9a.pdf) gives n38, 33 baseline dyspareunia, five short-term (seven unknown), 75% reported benefit among those with prior dyspareunia, 74% satisfaction, mean 8.4-year clinical follow-up at 78% follow-up; 50% minor and 38% severe LS-related recurrent symptoms are among its long-term assessed patients, not a universal risk. Original journal full text/denominators and flap-specific tables remain open.
- [2026 LS consensus guideline](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ddg.70000), diagnosis/biopsy, steroid, surgery and follow-up sections read (figures/evidence report not inspected). It suggests surgery for persistent function-limiting stenosis after adequate steroids, not routine surgery in girls; pre/postoperative corticosteroid plan and multidisciplinary counseling matter. Suspicious new hyperkeratotic/eroded lesions require biopsy; individualized maintenance and surveillance replace a fixed indefinite clobetasol dose. ACOG 2020 Practice Bulletin remains abstract/index only; its full recommendations were not represented as read. The new guideline is a later high-yield source; no new OpenEvidence query was made.
- The genital-principles page was checked against the [FDA Integra SSED, contraindication p2](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf/p900033s042b.pdf) and [EAU 2026 penile-cancer local-treatment recommendations](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/penile-cancer/chapter/disease-management). The infected-wound contraindication, selected organ-sparing treatment and limited Mohs evidence remain correctly bounded, so **no edit** was needed. Its Alammar mixed-Fournier estimate was reused from the [earlier flap-source review](igap-singapore-labia-targeted-review.md), not reread as a full review. Original Shahzad, Höckel, Brodbeck and Alwaal complete packages and the Alammar study-level table/figures remain open.
