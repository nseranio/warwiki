The full current NCCN text remained inaccessible.
- **Original Shaeer report:** the [original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16422895/) describes one patient after circumcision-related glans amputation, a 12 × 4-cm infraumbilical rectus myofascial flap based on inferior epigastric vessels, paramedian harvest, partial degloving 1 cm below the summit, distal-skin urethral elongation, subcutaneous tunneling, neoglans sculpting and corona-by-tucking. At six months, the authors reported consistency, color and shape similar to a normal glans. The abstract does not report sensation, sexual/voiding function, donor morbidity, complications, histology or comparative outcomes. The full paper, figures and supplement were unavailable.
- **Later modified case:** the [open Nasr/Traboulsi 2013 report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3860129/) was read through the case, procedure, results, discussion, conclusion and figure captions (actual image panels not independently inspected). It used a modified approach with Z-plasties, suspensory-ligament release, mons liposuction, Pfannenstiel harvest and STSG coverage. It reported about 40% shrinkage at six weeks, a small ventral fistula and meatal stenosis; the fistula ultimately closed with twice-daily dilation, and cosmetic satisfaction was reported at 90 days. This cannot be merged with the original protocol, but it directly refutes inherited zero-event and purely theoretical-complication language.
- **Context sources:** inherited rectus anatomy, other rectus-flap applications, Hage glans sculpturing and Gobbo phalloplasty references remain useful contextual sources at their previously recorded access levels. They cannot supply Shaeer-specific survival, abdominal-wall safety, superiority or validated phalloplasty use. The page now separates the vascular background from one-operation outcomes.
