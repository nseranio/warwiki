## Focused source/diff findings

- **GCIPF:** [Commenge's original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41455451/) (online December 2025, print February 2026) describes a retrospective single-center **27-patient/46-flap** cohort: 19 bilateral patients, 78.3% vulvar healing before four weeks, 69.6% donor healing before four weeks, 4-day median stay, complications in 33.3% of patients, dehiscence 17.4%, infection 21.7%, and no reported total flap necrosis or adjuvant-radiotherapy delay. Several percentages lack an explicit patient-versus-flap denominator in the abstract. The publisher's search-indexed methods/results excerpts (not full article access) describe **27/128 vulvectomies (21.1%)** receiving GCIPF, 61.7% primary closure and 22 patients receiving other flaps. The journal full text returned 403. The page now distinguishes a center's selected experience from first-line comparative efficacy and scopes its generic operative sequence because the original technique figures were unavailable.
- **Separate anatomy and comparisons:** [O'Dey's aOAP abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20869760/) and the [prior aOAP review](aoap-scrotal-cecil-targeted-review.md) support **10 bodies/20 cadaveric specimens**, 16 musculocutaneous and four septocutaneous perforators, the ~1.3-cm landmark and ~7 × 15-cm related territory—not a fixed GCIPF pedicle, guaranteed sensation or size ceiling. [Han's original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37489969/) compares **31 IPAP** with **16 PAP/TUG** patients (12.9% versus 37.5% wound complications) in a retrospective cohort, not GCIPF. [Huang's original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25596518/) covers 16 patients/27 mixed perforator flaps without a musculocutaneous comparator. [Shin's original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35717498/) covers a different 16-patient/27-flap PPSF series and a nonrandom historical aesthetic comparison. [Wendelspiess's original abstract/indexed text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11202299/) reports 49 studies/1,840 people, with 276 perforator and 1,564 non-perforator reconstructions and no significant overall short-term complication difference; the full tables/figures were not read. The page now avoids cross-series survival rankings and a mandatory musculocutaneous-flap rule for dead space.
- **Toulouse:** [Ricotta's original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39966025/) proposes a location- and size-based author algorithm favoring perforator options; it is not a guideline or comparative outcome trial. Both the journal and ScienceDirect full-text routes returned 403, so its actual figure, branch labels and source details remain unverified. Its title and authors' proposal are retained without asserting universal first-line use.
- **LS local flaps:** The [prior focused LS review](defibulation-perineoplasty-principles-targeted-review.md) already read relevant 2026 S3 guideline sections and the full original Lauber PDF/Tables 1–2: 41/70 invited responded, while **10/27** answering the dyspareunia item still had pain. Rangatchew, Rouzier, Brauer, Reid and Lee findings were reused at their documented source-access levels; no full-source status was upgraded. A new directly relevant [Caretto 2024 original open article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11204669/) was read through methods, technique, results, Table 1, discussion and figure legends (**not image panels**): 13 selected stenosis patients/29 flaps, 10 LS and three after cancer surgery; the authors reported no complications or recurrence at one year. This uncontrolled one-center series does not rank techniques or make its Doppler/tunnel protocol mandatory. The page retains LS treatment and surveillance alongside reconstruction.
