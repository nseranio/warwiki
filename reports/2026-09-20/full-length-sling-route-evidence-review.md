# Full-length sling routes: TOMUS/Cochrane safety check (20 September 2026)

The complete current retropubic and transobturator procedure pages were read, including all indications, operative steps, outcome tables, references and the retropubic video listing. This is a shared-source **partial** audit, not full device/clinical clearance. The earlier [NICE/AUA selection check](female-sling-route-guidance-review.md) was reused.

Read the accessible [TOMUS primary trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2962585/) methods, outcomes, Table 2 and discussion: 597 randomized; 12-month objective composite 80.8% retropubic versus 77.7% transobturator met its equivalence margin, while subjective composite 62.2% versus 55.8% **did not** meet prespecified equivalence despite no significant difference. Trocar bladder perforation was 15/298 (5.0%) versus 0/299. TOMUS's nonrandomized inside-/outside-out subgroup had more vaginal epithelial perforations inside-out, unlike the direction in the larger pooled review; neither supports a universal route-safety ranking. Its supplement was not read. The full accessible [2017 Cochrane MUS review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6483329/) was checked at abstract, Summary-of-Findings tables 1/3 and discussion/applicability: short-term cure overlaps, pooled bladder perforation 4.5% retropubic versus 0.6% transobturator, lower voiding dysfunction but more groin pain with transobturator, and limited long-term comparisons. It does not include single-incision slings.

The current [EAU online recommendations](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/non-neurogenic-female-luts/chapter/disease-management) were read for synthetic MUS: **perform intraoperative cystoscopy**, including transobturator placement; counsel on route-specific harms and longer-term retropubic advantage. The official [AUA/SUFU 2023 unabridged guideline](https://www.auanet.org/documents/Guidelines/PDF/2023%20Guidelines/SUI%20Unabridged%20FINAL%20080223.pdf) indexed text independently agrees about intraoperative cystoscopy and favors autologous pubovaginal sling for a fixed immobile urethra; direct PDF retrieval returned 403, so no new full-AUA read is claimed. The [Schierlitz ISD trial follow-up](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22270284/) was abstract-level only; it supports considering route differences in selected ISD, not a blanket superiority rule.

For inherited bibliography, the accessible [Imamura 2019 network-review abstract/results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6549286/) supports a near-12-month ranking of RMUS above TMUS, not a universal patient-level rule; the BMJ publisher page returned 403 in this pass and the full review was not reread. The [Fusco 2017 PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28479203/) supports the broader comparison of MUS with other SUI operations, not a definitive route choice; its full paper remains unreviewed. These are narrow abstract/indexed checks, not new full-source clearances.

Corrections: transobturator cystoscopy is no longer optional; the pages now distinguish TOMUS objective from subjective equivalence, state perforation denominators, qualify inside-/outside-out heterogeneity, and separate selected ISD from fixed-urethra decisions. The retropubic Cochrane cure ranges were retained with endpoint/follow-up context. No operative step, table, reference, video or useful route teaching was removed. Exact remaining work: current TVT/TVT-O/Monarc IFUs and named video/technique verification; long-term cohort/mesh-removal and ISD full-source appraisal; full Fusco/Imamura source packages if any stronger comparison is proposed; other inherited outcome/selection claims. The focused diff review matched corrected figures to source tables and checked preservation.
