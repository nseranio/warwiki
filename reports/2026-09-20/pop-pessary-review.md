# POP pessaries: targeted evidence correction (20 September 2026)

The complete current [pessary page](../../docs/04-surgical-techniques/04g-prolapse-repair/obliterative-pessary/prolapse-pessaries.mdx), including both device tables, outcomes, follow-up, special populations, 12 inherited references and two video listings, was read before and after editing. It has no imported clinical dataset or figure. This is a **partial audit**, not clearance of its remaining fitting, complication, device or emerging-technology claims. Video metadata were inspected; the videos were not played.

- The official [IUC 2025 combined chapter PDF](https://www.iuga.org/images/content/IUJ/IUJ_IUC_Chapter_3.1_3.2_3.3.pdf) was read in its pessary abstract and relevant fitting, type, continuation, symptom, complication and maintenance sections (chapter 3.1, PDF pp 0–11). Its 41–96.6% fitting range varies by definition/time; the page no longer promises 92% fitting or stage-specific 100%/71%/64% rates. Practical ring-to-space-filling selection remains. The standalone publisher article was not obtained; the official IUGA PDF contains the relevant article.
- Current [NICE NG123 pessary recommendations 1.7.5–1.7.9](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng123/chapter/recommendations) were read. Pessary is an option, alone or with PFMT; supervised PFMT for at least 16 weeks is a first option in symptomatic stage 1–2 POP, not a compulsory pessary-before-surgery pathway. NICE advises removing a pessary at least every six months, with six-month clinic appointments for people at risk because they cannot manage care. A fixed ACOG annual/3–4-month schedule and a mandatory clinician inspection for every self-manager are not asserted.
- The earlier full-main/all-table/two-figure [PEOPLE trial reading](../2026-09-12/prolapse-source-reading/pop-overview-sources.md) is reused without repeating it. The page distinguishes 440 randomized (one excluded afterward), 118/218 crossover to surgery, 74/173 pessary-arm primary responders still using one at 24 months, and failure to establish noninferiority. Trial supplements remained access-blocked in the earlier report. The 24–49% generic discontinuation sentence was removed because these denominators do not support it.
- The [TOPSY primary trial abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38078194/) and [NIHR scientific summary](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK603983/) were read for eligibility, 340 randomized, 18-month PFIQ-7 result, adherence and the approximately 40% clinic-arm self-removal crossover. Self-management is offered without claiming superior quality of life or guaranteed fewer visits. The later four-year follow-up was identified but its full source package was not adjudicated here.
- The [2025 Zhou placebo-controlled trial](https://www.bmj.com/content/389/bmj-2025-084418) uses the documented full-main/tables reading in [the prior principles record](../2026-09-12/prolapse-source-reading/principles.md): 420 randomized, 411 modified-ITT after successful ring fitting, no improvement in the primary 12-month continuation-plus-symptom endpoint, and some exploratory local adverse-event differences. Estrogen remains an option for menopause-associated genitourinary symptoms, not a promised pessary-continuation aid. The [official Cochrane pessary review](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD004010_pessaries-mechanical-devices-managing-pelvic-organ-prolapse-women) was checked for the PFMT-combination statement; its full review tables were not reopened.

The classifications, selection advice, complications, special populations, related links, all inherited references and both media listings were preserved. Exact remaining work: reconcile the inherited 627-patient QoL meta-analysis and symptom/complication percentages with original methods and denominators; check ACOG 214, the tailored older-adult cohort, device-specific fitting/cleaning instructions and emerging-design evidence; assess the TOPSY four-year full main/tables before any long-term inference. No source package or video is claimed read merely because it is cited.
