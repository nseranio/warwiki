# Laparotomy pads and Ray-Tec sponges — 21 September 2026

Both complete original/revised MDX pages, tables, 23 inherited references and related links were read. Neither page has a local clinical-data import, figure or video. Both are **partial claim reviews**, not clinical clearances.

- [RF Surgical catalog Rev C](https://www.medline.com/media/catalog/Docs/MKT/RF%20SURG.PDF), PDF text pp 2–3, lists distinct 4 × 18 and 18 × 18 pads, 4 × 4 16-/32-ply gauze, sterile and nonsterile codes, X-ray/RF tags, and a *no-loop* pad variant. Product-list entries are **not** a full model IFU or guarantee that every gauze pad is marked/sterile/RF-compatible. Product photos and IFUs were not obtained.
- [ACS retained-item statement](https://www.facs.org/about-acs/statements/prevention-of-unintentionally-retained-surgical-items-after-surgery/) (2016, full visible statement) recommends standardized counts, wound exploration, detectable items, and additional X-ray or detection technology *as indicated*. It expressly does not define a universal standard of care. [WHO 2009 safe-surgery PDF](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/44185/9789241598552_eng.pdf?sequence=1) surfaced indexed count/discrepancy excerpts, but direct fetch returned 403; do not claim its full text was read. The old categorical RFD-mandate, BMI≥30 imaging mandate and absolute intra-abdominal 4 × 4 prohibition are not supported by the cited SIR/interventional or sponge-case sources. Preserve larger-pad preference for deep packing and meticulous count/retrieval of any small gauze.
- [Inaba 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27433911/) primary abstract: 2,051 emergency patients/2,148 operations, eleven preclosure RFD detections and no missed sponge *reported*; $0.17 additional cost applies to one 4 × 18 model in that series, not an invariant current price or proven 100% sensitivity. [Rupp 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22770865/) primary abstract: one near miss and 35 miscounts/2,285; BMI was **not** associated with miscounts in that cohort, whereas Gawande's distinct [case-control study](https://www.nejm.org/doi/abs/10.1056/NEJMsa021721) found increasing BMI associated with retained objects. Gawande's 88% pertains to counts *reported correct among retained-object cases with counts performed*, not the false-negative rate of all counts. Inaba/Rupp full tables and detection-test specificity were not obtained.
- [Bezhentseva 2022](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0267293) relevant accessible main-text methods, results and limitations plus figure/supplement captions: veterinary-context **in-vitro** E. coli-water assay, growth under 192/192 inoculated plates; 6 versus 8 layers not statistically different. Full Table 1 cells, figure images and S1–S3 supplement pixels were not interpreted. Retained human operative barrier use, removed the implication of demonstrated human infection prevention.
- [Holmes 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4144356/) indexed article excerpts give 46 mixed procedures, 758 **18 × 18 pads**, r=.93 hemoglobin correlation and 466-mL mean gravimetric bias per case, not a correction factor for every surgery or a Ray-Tec. Direct PMC full-text fetch prompted CAPTCHA; full Table 1/2 and figure pixels not read here. [ACOG 2019 opinion abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31764759/) supports weighing as one obstetric QBL method and explicitly says no single method is proven superior or that QBL itself improves clinical outcomes. Full opinion remains inaccessible. [Stone 1983](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6847272/) primary abstract documents 1/14 versus 11/17 historical nonrandomized series; it is no instruction to use 4–17 pads or close under tension in a contemporary patient.
