## Source access and findings

- Almadori et al. 2025 [open original full article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00266-025-04895-9): abstract, introduction, eligibility, methods, operative text, results, discussion/limitations and figure captions 1–7 read. Tables 1–5 were identified but not opened as separate rendered tables; figure image panels were not visually inspected. Thirteen women (types I/II/III = 2/5/6); mean 9.8 mL injected, centrifuged macro-fat deeper and micro-fat at the dermal junction/in scar, sedation or general anesthesia, same-day discharge. Mean follow-up 12.23 months, one self-resolving donor-site bruise. No comparator, histology, processing comparison, cost or low-resource implementation study. The page now distinguishes this technique from nanofat/lipoconcentrate and labels experimental scar mechanisms and scalability as unproven in FGM/C.
- Foldès et al. 2012 [open original Lancet PDF](https://www.endfgm.eu/editor/files/2016/08/Pierre_Foldes_et_al_Reconstructive_surgery_FGM_The_Lancet.pdf): full study text relevant methods, operative sequence, outcomes, adverse events, limitations, Table 2 and Figure legend read; figure image panel and online appendix not inspected. Its cohort included type II and type III **with clitoral excision**, not all FGM types. The original text describes a buttonhole incision, graded suspensory-ligament release while preserving dorsal neurovasculature, fibrous-tunica-to-vestibular fixation and superior periosteal closure. It does **not** establish wedge plasty, a ≥5-mm projection target or bulbocavernosus anchoring as Foldès requirements. Of 2,938 operated women, 866 returned at one year; 430/841 respondents reported orgasm. Claims now use exact denominators.
- Meremikwu et al. 2026 [open original systematic review](https://obgyn.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ijgo.70760): abstract, included-study Table 1, risk-of-bias text and six-study pain analysis read; figure image and supplementary files not inspected. Twelve of 13 included studies used Foldès-style reconstruction; the other used a vaginal mucosal graft. Pain OR 79.67 (six studies, 1,116 participants, I² 82%) is a **before–after** result, not surgery versus control; certainty very low.
- Almadori et al. 2024 [open scoping review](https://obgyn.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1471-0528.17886): relevant method/results text and Tables 3–4 read; supplements not inspected. The “95%” is **38/40 studies** with clitoral reconstruction, mostly Foldès or variations, not 95% of worldwide cases. Reported 6,858/7,274 “improved” outcomes mix definitions; 56% of improvements lack a specified domain, and only 14/40 studies reported complications. Neither figure is an individual Foldès prognosis.
- [WHO 2025 guideline, Recommendation 8](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK614880/): recommendation, evidence summary and implementation remarks read (annex/GRADE tables not read). It **conditionally suggests** clitoral reconstruction for selected women, with very-low-certainty evidence, multidisciplinary care and sexual-health counseling before or alongside surgery. It does not select a preferred graft or operative variant.
- Mañero/Labanca 2018 [original abstract mirrored with DOI/PMID](https://openalex.org/W2793691011) (PMID **29528921**, not the earlier recovery note's 29528906): n32, FSFI 16→29 and Female Genital Self-Image 11→23, both **P<.05**; no separate quality-of-life outcome. The publisher/original full operative article was not accessible. [Sigurjonsson/Jordal 2018 open technical review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11930-018-0147-4), full relevant technique/outcomes and Figure 1 caption read (image not inspected), describes Mañero's **posterior** vaginal graft approximately 3–4×2 cm, 27/32 six-month attendees and two partial graft necroses; the **lateral** vaginal graft elsewhere in that review is the Swedish team's distinct modification. These details remain secondary-source attribution, not a claim of primary full-text verification.
