## Newly checked sources

- [Abufaraj et al, *AJOG* 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33727114/): read the accessible 12-page author-hosted original PDF, specifically its NHANES population, question definitions, analysis denominator, Table 2 (including age strata), discussion and limitations. The 2017–2018 weighted estimates are for **all surveyed US women aged ≥20**, not only respondents with UI: 45.9% reported stress leakage and 18.1% mixed leakage in the prior year. MUI was 31.4% among women aged ≥60. The study defines any small-volume episode from symptom questions; these are not rates of bothersome or care-seeking disease. The MUI page's “18.1% of women with UI” denominator was wrong and is corrected. The SUI page now identifies its 45.9% as a broad NHANES symptom estimate, with the primary citation, rather than an unspecified clinical prevalence.
- [Ippolito et al, 2025 Cochrane review's full official abstract and public summary](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD013643_what-are-benefits-and-risks-vaginal-lasers-treating-stress-urinary-incontinence-women): read all reported methods, comparisons, results and conclusions. Nine studies included 689 women; the short-term sham/control continence estimate was uncertain (RR 1.50, 95% CI 0.72–3.10), and the average ICIQ-UI SF change (−1.42 points) did not meet the review's prespecified clinically important difference. Certainty was very low; no major adverse events were reported in the included trials. One 76-person medium-term study suggested more objective continence without clear patient-reported benefit. The full review link returned 403, so its detailed risk-of-bias tables and appendices were **not** read. The SUI laser paragraph now conveys the uncertainty and clinically limited short-term result without declaring class-wide harm or erasing the option.
