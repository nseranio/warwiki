# Glans and scrotal named flaps — 2026-09-20

All four complete current MDX pages, their tables and references were read. They remain **partial claim reviews, not clinical clearances**. The named operations, practical anatomy, operative teaching and references were preserved. No new OpenEvidence query or publication occurred.

## Focused source/diff findings

- **Yao butterfly flap:** the [original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36468711/) reports seven selected patients, bilateral scrotal flaps on the anterior scrotal arteries, satisfactory outcomes, significant postoperative increases in flaccid and erect length, and small necrotic areas in 2/7 that healed with wound care. It does not report the magnitude of length change, a comparator or a complete outcome table. The JoVE methods/video were blocked by the site's JavaScript/bot gate, so the inherited operative sequence is retained as a pending practical outline rather than a verified universal protocol. Cross-series tables no longer claim that the design proves lower necrosis or superiority.
- **Mazza/Cheliz:** the [original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11490239/) reports 34 patients, mean age 43.2 years and mean follow-up 73.2 months. It supports the two-stage transfer, urethra through a flap opening, fixation to albuginea, pedicle division at four to six weeks, normal-appearing penis, unobstructed flow, depilation in 6/34, partial necrosis requiring grafts in 2/34, potency in 7/34 and meatal stenosis requiring a minor procedure in 1/34. The full paper and figures were unavailable. Speculative mechanisms, an inferred institutional evolution and rankings against urethral-flap or STSG cohorts were removed.
- **IUF:** the [Belinky abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21074797/) supports n=10, mean follow-up 11 months, no neomeatal stenosis or flap necrosis, one recurrence and one ventral curvature without penetration difficulty. The [Chavarriaga abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35260297/) supports n=74, median follow-up 72 months, six-year OS 86.5%, RFS 90.5%, PFS 85.1%, EQ-5D VAS 84.6±10.4, ICIQ-MLUTS 1.7±3.2 and IIEF-5 17.3±7. It does **not** report stenosis, necrosis, curvature or sensation. Full methods/figures were unavailable, so the inherited 2–3-cm urethral length, suture, catheter and meatal details remain explicitly pending rather than being erased.
- **Gulino:** the [2007 abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17632177/) supports 14 patients, thermal and tactile sensibility in 14/14, rigid erections in 10/14, no significant change in ejaculation/orgasm domains, and no reported recurrence or retraction at mean 13 months. It does not report stenosis, necrosis or curvature. The [open 2013 follow-up](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3737667/) was read through abstract, methods, operative description, results/table text and discussion (actual image panels not inspected). Its technique mobilizes the penile urethra toward the base, obtains at least 3 cm beyond the corpora, opens it ventrally for 3 cm and spreads the mucosa over the corporal apices; it is not the inherited rolled/everted sleeve. The paper includes a mixed 42-patient cohort, 25 glanduloplasties and 19/25 reporting normal ejaculation/orgasm at six months. The page and related comparisons now use the actual accessible configuration and keep denominators separate.
- **Current guideline context:** previously documented relevant sections of the current EAU disease-management chapter were reused for selected organ-sparing reconstruction and frozen section when completeness is in doubt. The [current EAU follow-up chapter](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/penile-cancer/chapter/followup) was read through its recommendations and schedule table: partial penectomy/amputation and penile-preserving pathways are not interchangeable, and nodal follow-up depends on status. The full current NCCN text remained inaccessible and was not used to impose a protocol.
