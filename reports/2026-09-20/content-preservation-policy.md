# Preserve useful content while checking evidence

The user corrected the review approach on September 20, 2026: manufacturer information and practical surgical teaching are valuable even when they have not been tested in a clinical trial. This policy supersedes the blanket-removal approach in the September 19–20 audit reports.

## Match the source to the claim

- Manufacturer IFUs, labels and model-specific catalogs support specifications, assembly, intended use, compatibility, warnings and processing. Cite the model/version. Marketing claims of superiority need separate comparative support.
- Anatomy texts, atlases, original anatomy studies, technical papers, operative videos and established teaching support descriptions of anatomy and technique. Label a particular author's technique or preference as such.
- Case reports, small series, bench and animal studies can be useful for a named technique, mechanism or safety signal. Preserve their population and design; do not convert them into comparative efficacy, incidence or standard-of-care claims.
- Guidelines, major trials and systematic/Cochrane reviews are preferred for treatment recommendations and practice-changing updates. That quarterly-update priority is not an inclusion criterion for every educational fact on the site.

## Correct claims without emptying pages

Read the full selected page. Preserve useful descriptions, operative steps, specifications, historical context, references and media. A source that has not yet been rechecked is not thereby false. Lack of an RCT or inaccessible full text alone does not justify deleting the section or replacing it with an audit notice.

Make the smallest correction supported by the finding: fix a number or citation, qualify a model-specific instruction, distinguish personal technique, retain a safety signal in its original setting, or remove a specific disproven/dangerously misleading claim. Record the exact reason and source. When a consequential claim cannot be supported, qualify or hold that claim alone and keep the rest of the page useful.

Put incomplete source-review work in the internal ledger and reports. Public pages should teach the subject, not narrate the audit. Preserve ordinary page-last-updated display. Do not add a blanket evidence banner or imply clinician approval.

Before any major reduction, inspect the actual diff and account for every substantive lost section. No whole-page replacement with “not adjudicated,” “removed inherited content,” or equivalent notices. User-requested deletions and specific demonstrated errors remain valid exceptions, with their reasons recorded.

## Honest review records

Editorial restoration is not fresh clinical verification. Preserve source-access history, use current content hashes, and distinguish complete page reading from reading the cited sources. Never inflate completion counts by reviewing a stub after deleting the substantive content. Reuse detailed prior source records with attribution; do not claim a personal full-text read of someone else's work.

The whole-site audit remains open. Work in coherent small batches and retain the unfinished queue. Use the restored content as the starting point; do not repeat the destructive approach.
