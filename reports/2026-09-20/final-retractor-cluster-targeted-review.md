The prior page used one universal perineal geometry, unsupported 360-degree and low-profile advantages, broad procedure lists and Bookwalter complications as if Turner-Warwick-specific. Reid's approximately 90% result applies to a selected 60-patient urethroplasty technique, not the retractor. Richard Turner-Warwick's death year was corrected from 2010 to 2020.
- **FISH/Glassman viscera retainer:** the prior page incorrectly treated FISH as a flexible plastic/silicone device and Glassman as a separate metal alternative. Current FDA records identify Glassman/FISH as the same sterile, single-use flexible synthetic-polymer paddle family. Removal and operative counting are now explicit; an FDA retained-device report is framed as a failure mode, not an incidence estimate. Hernia/adhesiolysis enterotomy rates remain useful background but are no longer presented as device efficacy. The CC-DHA barrier remains preclinical murine/mechanical work.
- **Volkmann:** current catalogs show a family spanning one to six sharp or blunt prongs, with materially different lengths and handles. The former universal 20–22 cm flat-handle specification, fixed depth hierarchy, force-distribution/tear reduction and insertion-angle algorithms were removed. Reconstructive examples remain as practical uses rather than comparative or required-instrument claims.
- **Weitlaner:** current Sklar and Teleflex catalogs support finger rings, curved shanks, ratchet locking, interdigitating prongs and multiple sharp/blunt sizes. The prior page asserted unsupported fatigue, stability and outcome advantages, interchangeability, a 30–60-minute release interval and fixed size-by-incision rules. Older cited articles describe other proposed self-retaining devices or extensions and cannot establish standard Weitlaner performance. Historical design provenance remains unresolved.
