# Ospemifene

**Ospemifene (Osphena)** is the only oral, non-estrogen FDA-approved treatment for moderate-to-severe dyspareunia and vaginal dryness due to menopause. As a third-generation selective estrogen receptor modulator (SERM), it acts as an agonist on vaginal and bone tissue, an antagonist on breast tissue, and a weak agonist on endometrium, which makes it a unique option for GSM patients who cannot or prefer not to use vaginal preparations.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup> Small observational studies have explored bladder symptoms in women with VVA, but OAB and UUI are not approved indications or established treatment roles.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[5]](#ref5)[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

For related classes, see [Vaginal and topical estrogen](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/hormonal-therapies/vaginal-topical-estrogen), [Vaginal DHEA](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/hormonal-therapies/vaginal-dhea), [Preoperative hormonal priming](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/hormonal-therapies/preoperative-hormonal-priming), and the clinical [GSM](/docs/special-populations/05e-womens-health/gsm) article.

## Mechanism and tissue-selective SERM profile

Ospemifene is a triphenylethylene derivative (structurally related to tamoxifen and toremifene) with tissue-selective ER modulation:<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

| Tissue | ER effect | Clinical implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Vaginal epithelium** | **Agonist** | Epithelial maturation, ↑ superficial cells, ↓ pH, lubrication<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> |
| **Bone** | **Agonist** | ↓ bone-turnover markers; bone-biomarker profile comparable to raloxifene<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup> |
| **Breast** | **Antagonist** | Preclinical anti-proliferative on breast tissue<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> |
| **Endometrium** | **Weak agonist** | Endometrial thickening; one simple hyperplasia without atypia and no endometrial cancer in trials up to 52 weeks<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup> |
| **Cardiovascular / lipids** | Mixed | ↑ HDL, ↓ LDL; neutral on triglycerides<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup> |

### Pharmacokinetics<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

- **Absorption:** must be taken with food; a high-fat meal increases Cmax and AUC 2.3× and 1.7× compared with fasting
- **Protein binding** &gt;99% (clinically relevant for drug interactions)
- **Volume of distribution** 448 L
- **Metabolism:** primarily CYP3A4 and CYP2C9 (CYP2C19 minor); major metabolite 4-hydroxyospemifene
- **Half-life** about 26 hours; steady state after about 9 days
- **Elimination** about 75% fecal, about 7% urinary

## Contraindications and boxed warning

### FDA boxed warning<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

- **Endometrial cancer:** estrogen-agonist effect on the endometrium; increased risk of endometrial cancer with unopposed estrogens; evaluate any undiagnosed persistent or recurring abnormal genital bleeding
- **Cardiovascular disorders:** in ospemifene trials (up to 15 months), the label reports:
  - Thromboembolic stroke: 1.13 per 1,000 woman-years (versus 3.15 placebo)
  - Hemorrhagic stroke: 3.39 per 1,000 woman-years (versus 0 placebo)
  - DVT: 2.26 per 1,000 woman-years (2 cases) versus 3.15 per 1,000 woman-years (1 case) placebo

### Contraindications<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

- Undiagnosed abnormal genital bleeding
- Estrogen-dependent neoplasia
- **Active DVT / PE** or history of these
- **Active arterial thromboembolic disease** (stroke, MI) or history
- Hypersensitivity to ospemifene
- Known or suspected pregnancy

The label also states that ospemifene should not be used in severe hepatic impairment; no dose adjustment is needed for mild or moderate (Child-Pugh A or B) impairment.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Drug interactions

| Interacting drug | Mechanism | Effect | Clinical action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fluconazole** | Moderate CYP3A / strong CYP2C9 / moderate CYP2C19 inhibitor | **↑ AUC 2.7×, ↑ Cmax 1.7×** | **Do not co-administer**<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Rifampin** | Strong CYP3A / moderate CYP2C9-2C19 inducer | **↓ AUC 58%, ↓ Cmax 51%** | Avoid: exposure falls substantially, so the therapeutic effect may be lost<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Ketoconazole** | Strong CYP3A inhibitor | ↑ AUC 1.4×, ↑ Cmax 1.5× | Use with caution |
| **Omeprazole** | Moderate CYP2C19 inhibitor | ↑ AUC 1.17×, ↑ Cmax 1.20× | No dose adjustment needed |
| **Warfarin** | Protein-binding displacement (ospemifene &gt;99% bound) | No effect on single-dose warfarin PK; chronic INR/PT not studied | Monitor if co-administered |
| **Estrogens / other SERMs** | Pharmacodynamic | Safety not studied | **Do not co-administer** |
| **Highly protein-bound drugs** | Displacement | May alter exposure of either drug | Use with caution |

The fluconazole interaction is particularly relevant in urogynecologic practice; fluconazole is commonly prescribed for VVC in the same patient population.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Pivotal efficacy data

Approval rested on three 12-week efficacy trials and one 52-week safety trial, total 2,516 postmenopausal women (1,416 ospemifene and 1,100 placebo).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> All four coprimary endpoints met (superficial cells, parabasal cells, vaginal pH, most bothersome symptom).

### Di Donato 2019 meta-analysis (6 RCTs)<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup>

Ospemifene 60 mg versus placebo at 12 weeks:

- **Vaginal pH:** SMD −0.96 (95% CI −1.12 to −0.81; p &lt; 0.0001)
- **Parabasal cells:** SMD −36.84 (95% CI −46.95 to −26.72; p &lt; 0.0001)
- **Superficial cells:** SMD 8.23 (95% CI 3.73 to 12.74; p &lt; 0.0003)
- **Dyspareunia:** SMD −2.70 (95% CI −2.88 to −2.52; p &lt; 0.0001)

The abstract reports standardized mean differences on differing scales; direction and consistency across trials are the dependable message.

### Portman 2013 phase 3 trial<sup>[[30]](#ref30)</sup>

605 women aged 40–80 with dyspareunia as the most bothersome symptom were randomized to ospemifene 60 mg (n = 303) or placebo (n = 302) for 12 weeks. Ospemifene was superior on all coprimary endpoints (parabasal and superficial cells, vaginal pH, dyspareunia severity; p &lt; 0.0001 except dyspareunia p = 0.0001). Hot flushes were the most frequent treatment-related adverse event (6.6% versus 3.6%); one participant per arm discontinued.

### Simon 2023 network meta-analysis<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup>

The network meta-analysis summarized mean endometrial thickness; this does not mean every patient remained below a diagnostic threshold. The FDA label reports endometrial thickening ≥5 mm and one simple hyperplasia without atypia, with no endometrial cancer during trials through 52 weeks.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Urogynecologic applications beyond dyspareunia

### Overactive bladder and urge urinary incontinence — exploratory evidence

| Study | n | Design | Finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Schiavi 2017** | 46 | Retrospective 12 wk | Detrusor overactivity **39% → 13%** (p = 0.04); voids/day 9.57 → 6.63; urgency episodes 5.63 → 1.44; nocturia 3.17 → 1.11; UUI 0.85 → 0.33 (all p ≤ 0.003)<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> |
| **Schiavi 2018** | 105 | Prospective 12 wk | OAB-Q 55.34 → 23.22 (p &lt; 0.0001); FSFI significantly improved; **PGI-I 90.5% total success rate**<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> |
| **Novara 2020** | 25 | Prospective 12 wk (**first-line-refractory OAB**) | Significant reduction in micturition, nocturia, urgency, incontinence; bladder-wall thickness decreased; UDI-6 and OAB-Q improved<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> |
| **Russo 2023** | 40 | Prospective 12 wk with urodynamics | Cystometric capacity, compliance, and verbal sensory threshold improved; daily voids, UUI, and nocturia decreased; ICIQ-UI SF and ICIQ-OAB improved<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |

The 25-patient Novara study is hypothesis-generating. It does not establish ospemifene as a second-line OAB treatment or justify delaying guideline-supported OAB therapies.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>

**Proposed mechanism:** estrogen-receptor activity in lower urinary tract tissues may affect bladder symptoms, but these small studies do not establish the mechanism or clinical equivalence to vaginal estrogen.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)[[15]](#ref15)</sup>

### Recurrent UTI prevention — not a primary indication

Unlike vaginal estrogen, ospemifene has not been specifically studied for rUTI prevention. The 2023 *JAMA* review notes that none of the FDA-approved GSM therapies (including ospemifene) are approved for reducing rUTI frequency.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

**PEONY observational signal** (breast cancer survivors, 6 months of ospemifene): rUTIs decreased by 80% and postcoital cystitis decreased by 90% as secondary outcomes.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> Hypothesis-generating.

### Sexual function

Schiavi 2018 showed significant improvement in total FSFI and decreased sexual distress in women with VVA and OAB or UUI.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> The 12-month PEONY study confirmed sustained improvements in lubrication and pain domains; overall FSFI total did not reach significance, likely reflecting the multifactorial nature of sexual dysfunction in this population.<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

### Guideline positioning

- **AUA/SUFU/AUGS 2025 GSM guideline:** ospemifene may be offered for vulvovaginal dryness and/or dyspareunia (conditional recommendation, Grade C); this does not establish an OAB indication.<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup>

  - Patients can be counseled that neither vaginal DHEA nor ospemifene increases breast cancer risk (statement 21, expert opinion) or the risk of atypical hyperplasia or endometrial cancer (statement 23, Moderate Recommendation, Grade C). No endometrial surveillance is needed solely because of ospemifene use (statement 24). For OAB with GSM, the guideline names local low-dose vaginal estrogen, not ospemifene (statement 12).<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup> The FDA label still advises against ospemifene in women with a history of breast cancer.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Menopause Society (NAMS) 2020 GSM position statement:** oral ospemifene is one of the effective prescription options for moderate-to-severe GSM, alongside low-dose vaginal estrogen and vaginal DHEA.<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup>
- **ACOG 2021 clinical consensus** addresses hormonal and nonhormonal therapy for urogenital symptoms in breast cancer survivors (see below).<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
- **AGS Beers Criteria 2023:** lists ospemifene as a GSM-dyspareunia alternative alongside vaginal estrogen, DHEA, and hyaluronic acid<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup>
- **AAFP 2023:** FDA-approved non-estrogen therapy for vaginal atrophy and dyspareunia<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

## Adverse effects and safety

Pooled safety (6 Phase 2/3 trials; n = 1,242 ospemifene versus n = 958 placebo):<sup>[[12]](#ref12)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

| AE | Ospemifene 60 mg | Placebo |
|---|---|---|
| **Hot flush** | **8.5%** | 3.3% |
| UTI | 6.5% | 4.8% |
| Vaginal discharge | 3.8% | 0.3% |
| Muscle spasms | 3.2% | 0.9% |
| Headache | 2.4% | 2.3% |
| Hyperhidrosis | 1.6% | 0.3% |
| Vaginal hemorrhage | 1.0% | 0.2% |
| Night sweats | 1.0% | 0.0% |
| **Discontinuation for AEs** | **7.6%** | 3.8% |
| Serious AEs | 2.6% | 1.8% |

Hot flashes (about 8.5%) are the most clinically meaningful AE. Counsel patients with pre-existing vasomotor symptoms that ospemifene may worsen hot flashes.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>

## Endometrial safety

**Constantine 2015** Phase 2/3 pooled analysis (n = 1,242 ospemifene / n = 924 placebo):<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

- **No endometrial cancer** in any ospemifene trial
- Endometrial hyperplasia: &lt;1% of ospemifene-treated women over up to 52 weeks
- Proliferative endometrium: 26.3 per 1,000 women (ospemifene) versus 0 (placebo)
- Uterine polyps: 19.6 versus 8.3 per 1,000 women

Endometrial thickening of 5 mm or more occurred in 101.4 per 1,000 women on ospemifene versus 20.9 per 1,000 on placebo (label, up to 52 weeks).<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> Mean-thickness summaries should not obscure individual thickening or bleeding that needs assessment.<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> Progestogen co-administration was not evaluated in trials; the FDA label does not mandate it. Long-term (&gt;1 y) endometrial data are limited, and patients should be counseled to report any postmenopausal bleeding.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## VTE risk — real-world signal favorable

| Source | n | VTE signal |
|---|---|---|
| Clinical trials (up to 15 mo) | Pooled | DVT 2.26 / 1,000 woman-y (2 cases) vs 3.15 / 1,000 (1 case) placebo — trials not powered for VTE<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Nordstrom 2020 MarketScan 2013–2017** | 8,188 ospemifene | **3.7 per 1,000 person-years** VTE |
| Other SERMs | 11,777 | 11.5 per 1,000 person-years |
| Untreated VVA | 220,242 | 11.3 per 1,000 person-years |

Lower observed VTE rates in a claims cohort do not establish a protective effect or comparative safety because treatment selection and confounding may differ. The FDA contraindication for active or prior DVT/PE/stroke/MI remains.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>

### Perioperative

Per FDA label, discontinue ≥4–6 weeks before high-VTE-risk surgery or during prolonged immobilization.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>

## Bone and lipid effects

### Bone

- Phase 3 data show significantly greater decreases in 7 of 9 bone biomarkers versus placebo at 12 weeks, comparable to raloxifene<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
- **Maffei 2022 observational 12-month study** (n = 128): stable BMD at total femur and lumbar spine on ospemifene; control group showed significant decline at all sites; bone alkaline phosphatase decreased<sup>[[23]](#ref23)</sup>
- **AYSEX real-world 12-month study** (n = 100): no significant change in DXA or bone-resorption markers<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>
- **Not FDA-approved for osteoporosis:** no fracture-outcome data; biomarker-level evidence only<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>

### Lipids

Archer 2017 post-hoc analysis (n = 2,166):<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

- **HDL** increased 4.4% at 3 mo and 5.1% at 6 mo (versus 0.2% and 1.5% with placebo; p &lt; 0.001)
- **LDL** decrease (p &lt; 0.05)
- **Triglycerides** neutral (unlike oral estrogens)

## Use in breast cancer survivors — regulatory divergence

| Source | Position |
|---|---|
| **FDA label** | Advises against use with known/suspected **or a history of breast cancer**; estrogen-dependent neoplasia is contraindicated<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **EMA (Europe)** | European labeling permits consideration for VVA only after all breast-cancer treatment, including adjuvant therapy, is completed; this is not proof of recurrence safety<sup>[[28]](#ref28)</sup> |
| **AUA/SUFU/AUGS 2025** | Ospemifene is not known to increase breast-cancer risk (statement 21), but few studies address breast-cancer survivors; the guideline text notes the FDA warning and says clinicians may recommend it in a patient with a breast-cancer history using informed shared decision-making with the treating oncologist<sup>[[29]](#ref29)</sup> |
| **NAMS 2020** | Not recommended for GSM in women with known or suspected breast cancer because of the label<sup>[[31]](#ref31)</sup> |
| **NCCN Survivorship 2026** | "Currently ospemifene is contraindicated in survivors with a history of estrogen-dependent cancers"<sup>[[25]](#ref25)</sup> |
| **ACOG 2021 clinical consensus** | FDA warning is "controversial"; post-approval data have not demonstrated increased recurrence; long-term safety data are limited; clinicians should discuss the label warning with the patient and use clinical judgment (2021 consensus lists ospemifene as an option to consider in survivors)<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |

### Supporting clinical data

- **Preclinical:** antagonist activity in breast tissue; anti-proliferative effects<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[26]](#ref26)</sup>
- **PEONY 6-month** (n = 64 breast cancer survivors): significant improvement in VVA symptoms and treatment satisfaction; decreased rUTIs and cystitis; no safety signals<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **PEONY 12-month** (n = 385 overall; 145 ospemifene incl. breast cancer survivors): sustained efficacy; ospemifene users had about 3× higher odds of clinically relevant satisfaction improvement versus local estrogen (OR 2.85; 95% CI 1.33–6.11)<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

The US label advises against use in women with known, suspected or prior breast cancer. European labeling differs after completion of cancer treatment, but small uncontrolled studies and preclinical breast antagonism do not establish recurrence safety. Any discussion requires oncology involvement.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)[[18]](#ref18)[[27]](#ref27)</sup>

## Long-term efficacy and adherence

### AYSEX 12-month real-world (n = 100)<sup>[[24]](#ref24)</sup>

- Significant improvement in all VHI domains sustained at 12 months
- Only 1 patient remained with vaginal atrophy at 12 months (versus 100% at baseline)
- Significant improvement in sexual function and QoL
- No significant change in endometrial thickness, mammography, or bone health

### PEONY 12-month (n = 385; 145 ospemifene / 240 local estrogen)<sup>[[17]](#ref17)</sup>

- 75.9% ospemifene completion at 12 months (versus 78.7% local estrogen), comparable adherence
- Treatment satisfaction 7.02 at 3 mo, rising to 8.32 at 12 mo (p &lt; 0.001)
- Favorable OR versus local estrogen for satisfaction improvement

## Practical prescribing

| Parameter | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Ideal candidate** | Postmenopausal woman with moderate-to-severe dyspareunia or dryness who prefers oral therapy over vaginal application<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Dose** | 60 mg PO daily **with food** (absorption is food-dependent)<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Onset** | Significant improvement by 4–6 wk; full effect by 12 wk<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> |
| **Duration** | Shortest duration consistent with treatment goals and risks; reassess periodically whether treatment is still needed<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Progestogen** | Not mandated; one simple hyperplasia without atypia and no cancer in trials through 52 weeks; evaluate abnormal bleeding<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Monitoring** | Evaluate postmenopausal bleeding; no routine endometrial surveillance needed unless symptomatic<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Avoid in** | Active / history of DVT-PE-stroke-MI; estrogen-dependent neoplasia; known/suspected or prior breast cancer; severe hepatic impairment; concurrent fluconazole or rifampin; pregnancy<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Perioperative** | **Discontinue 4–6 wk before high-VTE-risk surgery**<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Counsel about** | Hot flashes ~8.5%; vaginal discharge ~4%; **take with food**<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup> |
| **Breast cancer survivors** | US label advises against use; European restrictions differ after completion of all cancer treatment; oncology discussion<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup> |
| **Cost** | Brand-name only; ~$200–300/month uninsured |

## Clinical Positioning

- **Ospemifene 60 mg daily with food is the only oral, non-estrogen FDA-approved GSM therapy.** Take-with-food matters; absorption nearly doubles with a fatty meal.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Avoid with fluconazole (2.7× AUC) and rifampin (58% AUC loss).** The fluconazole interaction collides with common VVC management in the same patient population.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Ospemifene is not an established OAB or UUI treatment.** Small uncontrolled bladder-symptom studies do not justify using it as a standard step before botulinum toxin or neuromodulation.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
- **Not an rUTI-prevention agent.** Vaginal estrogen retains the AUA-endorsed indication (RR 0.42). The PEONY observational signal is hypothesis-generating only.<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup>
- **Hot flashes are the dominant tolerance issue (about 8.5%)** and drive most discontinuations; counsel preemptively, particularly in women with pre-existing vasomotor symptoms.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- **Endometrial safety through 52 weeks is reassuring:** no cancer, &lt;1% hyperplasia; some patients developed thickening ≥5 mm. Long-term (&gt;1 y) endometrial data remain limited; evaluate any postmenopausal bleeding.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)[[14]](#ref14)</sup>
- **Observational VTE comparisons do not establish protection or comparative safety.** The active or prior DVT, PE, stroke and MI contraindications remain.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup>
- **Discontinue 4–6 weeks before high-VTE-risk surgery** or during prolonged immobilization per label.<sup>[[12]](#ref12)</sup>
- **Breast-cancer recommendations differ by jurisdiction.** The US label advises against use with known, suspected or prior breast cancer; European labeling permits consideration only after all treatment is complete. PEONY cannot establish long-term recurrence safety.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)[[17]](#ref17)</sup>
- **Bone and lipid effects are favorable but off-label.** Do not select ospemifene to treat dyslipidemia or osteoporosis on the basis of surrogate biomarkers.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
- **Counsel about 4–6-week onset** and 12-week full effect; realistic expectations improve adherence.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
