# Surgicel — Oxidized Regenerated Cellulose

**Surgicel** (oxidized regenerated cellulose, ORC) is a plant-cellulose-derived hemostatic matrix: a sterile, absorbable, knitted fabric labeled as an adjunct for capillary, venous and small-arterial bleeding when ligation or conventional control is impractical or ineffective.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> It works through an acidic blood-cellulose clot and mechanical tamponade, and it is one of the oldest and most widely used passive hemostatic agents in surgery (Ethicon, a Johnson & Johnson company).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

---

## Mechanism

- **Cellulose fibers** are chemically oxidized to introduce carboxylic acid groups, producing a low-pH, locally acidic microenvironment when hydrated.
- The acidic pH promotes a gel-like, brown blood-cellulose coagulum as the matrix absorbs blood; the low pH also destroys thrombin (see below).<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Mechanical tamponade** provides a secondary hemostatic effect.
- The low pH is also bactericidal in vitro, including against MRSA and VRE, but bactericidal activity is not a substitute for systemic prophylaxis or treatment of infection.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- The label gives no fixed absorption time: it states absorption depends on the amount used, degree of blood saturation and the tissue bed, and may be prevented in chemically cauterized areas. About 7–14 days is the commonly quoted teaching figure.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

Surgicel is a **passive matrix**. It enhances local clot formation but contains no thrombin or other active coagulation factors, so efficacy depends on the patient's native clotting capacity. It is not for large-artery bleeding, non-hemorrhagic serous oozing, or use as an adhesion barrier.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Product Forms

| Form | Texture | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Surgicel Original** | Knitted fabric (pad) | Sheet placement on bleeding surface |
| **Surgicel Fibrillar** | Fluffy cotton-like | Packing into crevices or irregular cavities |
| **Surgicel SNoW** | Structured non-woven fabric | Endoscopic and laparoscopic use; the label says it may be more convenient than other forms |
| **Surgicel Nu-Knit** | Knitted fabric | Sheet placement; can be cut to size for endoscopic use |
| **Surgicel Powder** | Fine powder (with an endoscopic applicator for a 5 mm or larger trocar) | Broad diffuse oozing; apply dry, use minimal amounts and remove excess by irrigation and aspiration once hemostasis is achieved. It is dry, can scatter or migrate, and dislodged powder can plug the urethra, ureter or a catheter |

The manufacturer lists Original, Fibrillar, Nu-Knit and SNoW as cuttable for endoscopic use.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> Handling and removal instructions differ by form. The package insert for the form in hand should be read.

## GU Applications

- **Partial nephrectomy renal bed.** Surgicel is placed after sliding-clip renorrhaphy for diffuse capillary oozing. It should be kept out of the collecting system, where fragments can plug the ureter or a catheter; the label reports a blocked ureter after kidney resection.
- **Pelvic sidewall.** Surgicel is placed after lymphadenectomy for residual oozing.
- **Bladder-neck dissection.** Surgicel controls diffuse oozing after radical prostatectomy or bladder-neck reconstruction.
- **Urethroplasty donor bed.** Surgicel controls bed bleeding at graft harvest sites, including buccal mucosa and penile skin.
- **Retropubic space.** Surgicel is used after closure of large pelvic dissections.
- **Neurovascular-bundle protection during RARP.** Some surgeons place a thin strip over the NVB for hemostasis without cautery-related thermal injury; this is an operative technique, not a labeled use.
- **Scrotal or perineal wounds.** Closing material into an infected or contaminated wound should be avoided, and Surgicel is not routine packing after Fournier debridement.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Technique Pearls

- **The sheet should be pre-cut** to the size of the bleeding area. Oversizing increases residual foreign-body volume.
- **Surgicel should be applied dry.** The hemostatic effect is greater when dry, so it should not be moistened with water or saline. The site should not be pre-treated with silver nitrate or other escharotics, which can prevent absorption.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Surgicel should be held firmly in place until bleeding stops** (commonly a few minutes). It should not be wiped or rinsed off afterward.
- **The minimum amount should be used, and excess should be removed** before closure. Retained bulk can encapsulate and mimic artifacts or masses on imaging. In urologic surgery, minimal amounts should be used to avoid plugging the urethra, ureter or catheter.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Surgicel should not be applied too tightly as a vascular wrap.** Stenosis has been reported.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Surgicel is not a substitute for a suture or clip.** Arterial sources should be controlled first; Surgicel is not indicated for large-artery bleeding.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

---

## Limitations and Cautions

Surgicel should not be combined with topical thrombin: the low pH destroys thrombin activity, and the manufacturer states that hemostasis is not enhanced. It should not be impregnated with antibiotics or other agents. Only the minimum needed should be used; product forms have different handling and removal instructions.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

- **Surgicel is not for packing or wadding** unless it is removed once hemostasis is achieved, and it is not for implantation in bone defects.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Contaminated or infected fields.** Closing Surgicel inside a contaminated wound can cause complications. It is bactericidal in vitro but does not replace drainage or antimicrobials.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
- **Urinary tract.** Fragments can plug the urethra, ureter or catheter. The label reports difficulty passing urine after prostatectomy and one blocked ureter after kidney resection, both needing catheterization. It should not be placed within the collecting system. The stone-nidus warning appears in the gelatin and Arista labels, not in Surgicel's.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Foreign-body reaction and imaging mimicry.** Retained Surgicel can encapsulate and mimic tumor recurrence or an abscess on later imaging; this has been reported in case series and case reports. Placement should be documented in the operative note.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
- **Heparinized or coagulopathic patients.** As a passive matrix, Surgicel depends on native clotting. An active, thrombin-containing agent should be considered instead, and thrombin should never be added to Surgicel.
- **Surgicel should be removed after hemostasis in bony confines or near structures threatened by swelling.** The IFU requires removal near foramina in bone, the spinal cord, the optic nerve or chiasm, and tubular structures that swelling could constrict. Dislodgement and migration have been reported.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Surgicel is not for non-hemorrhagic serous oozing.** Serum and other non-blood fluids do not produce a satisfactory hemostatic effect.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
- **Surgicel is not ideal for high-flow bleeding.** The source should be controlled first.

## Surgicel vs. Alternatives

| Agent | Mechanism | Best for vs. Surgicel |
|---|---|---|
| **Surgicel** | Passive matrix and acid-pH platelet activation | Broad or sheet-like raw surfaces; no coagulopathy |
| **[Gelfoam](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/intraoperative-adjuncts/hemostatic-agents/gelfoam)** | Passive matrix, neutral pH | Bone bleeding; can combine with thrombin |
| **[Arista AH](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/intraoperative-adjuncts/hemostatic-agents/arista)** | Passive, absorbs water and concentrates clotting factors | Diffuse oozing; rapid absorption but excess must still be removed |
| **[FloSeal](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/intraoperative-adjuncts/hemostatic-agents/floseal)** | Active: contains thrombin | Coagulopathic patient; high-volume oozing |
| **[Topical thrombin](/docs/foundations/pharmacology/intraoperative-adjuncts/hemostatic-agents/topical-thrombin)** | Active: pure thrombin | Combined with Gelfoam for targeted activity |
