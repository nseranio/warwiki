# Male Urethral Slings & Implant Devices — Chooser

Male SUI implants divide into **fixed slings, adjustable slings, periurethral balloons, and the AUS**. Each device has its own canonical page; this article is the **decision-and-comparison hub** that ties them together.

The historical **bone-anchored InVance sling** (silicone-coated mesh secured to the inferior pubic rami with titanium screws) is mentioned in many older guidelines but has been **largely abandoned** due to mesh-infection rates of 2–12%; it is not covered as a standalone device here.

## Mechanism comparison

| Device | Mechanism | Key clinical implication |
|---|---|---|
| **AdVance / XP** | Repositioning of the lax membranous urethra proximally — non-obstructive | Requires **residual sphincter function**; a positive repositioning test on cystoscopy is the usual selection step, although controlled studies have not shown that it predicts success<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> |
| **Virtue (quadratic)** | Ventral elevation (transobturator arms) + compression (prepubic arms) | Dual mechanism in theory; long-term durability disputed (one single-institution series with 68% failure at 55 months led its authors to abandon the device)<sup>[[19]](#ref19)</sup> |
| **ATOMS** | Adjustable ventral compression of the bulbar urethra against the urogenital diaphragm | Reported effective across severity strata in cohorts, though severity, radiation and diabetes predict failure; office-based percutaneous saline adjustment |
| **Argus** | Compression + mechanical washer adjustment | Pain, erosion and explantation reported in some cohorts; rates depend on series and route |
| **REMEEX** | Compression + suprapubic varitensor adjustment | Re-tensioned percutaneously through a suprapubic varitensor; retropubic passage carries bladder + bowel-injury risk |
| **ProACT** | Periurethral balloon compression at the bladder neck (focal, non-circumferential) | Adjustable scrotal port; high explantation rate (all-cause 27%, range 7–55%, in the guideline review); offered only to non-irradiated men<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> |
| **AUS (AMS-800)** | Hydraulic circumferential cuff at bulbar urethra; patient-activated pump | Most predictable and reliable option for mild through severe SUI (AUA/GURS/SUFU 2024)<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>; manual-dexterity prerequisite |

A dynamic-MRI study demonstrated that the transobturator sling works by **lengthening the vesicourethral-anastomosis-to-bulbar-urethra distance** — at rest, functional urethral length increased from 1.27 cm pre-sling to 1.53 cm post-sling, approaching the 1.92 cm seen in continent post-prostatectomy controls (P = 0.09).<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>

## Decision matrix — which device for which patient

The AUA/GURS/SUFU 2024 guideline asks clinicians to discuss male slings for mild-to-moderate post-prostate-treatment SUI, not to routinely implant them in severe SUI, and to offer adjustable balloons only to non-radiated men.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> A sling is reasonable when the patient has not been irradiated and has minimal nocturnal leakage or isolated climacturia, or cannot operate an AUS because of hand function or cognition.

| Clinical scenario | First choice | Alternative(s) | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mild SUI, no radiation, positive repositioning test** | [AdVance / XP](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/advance-sling) | [ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling), [ProACT](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/proact-balloons) | — |
| **Moderate SUI, no radiation** | [AUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) (Sacco 2021: 94% vs sling 69%) | [ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling), [AdVance XP](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/advance-sling) | Fixed sling alone if radiation history |
| **Severe SUI** | [AUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) | [ProACT](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/proact-balloons) in non-irradiated men (AUA 2024 conditional recommendation covers mild to severe); [ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling) in selected cases (observational data) | Fixed sling (AUA 2024: do not routinely implant in severe SUI) |
| **Mild–moderate, declines AUS pump but accepts an implant** | Consider [ProACT](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/proact-balloons) in selected non-irradiated men, with repeat-adjustment and explant counseling | Fixed sling if residual sphincter function; [urethral bulking](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/urethral-bulking-agents) only in limited circumstances | Do not describe ProACT as non-implant treatment |
| **SUI with prior pelvic radiation** | Offer [AUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) over a sling or adjustable balloon (AUA/GURS/SUFU 2024, moderate recommendation)<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>, with higher tissue-risk counseling | Selected [ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling) series report lower success than non-irradiated cohorts | [ProACT](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/proact-balloons) after radiation (AUA 2024 limits it to non-irradiated men; EAU advises against); counsel poorer fixed-sling outcomes |
| **Recurrent SUI after failed AdVance** | Re-evaluate the cause, then recommend [AUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) (AUA/GURS/SUFU 2024)<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>; selected [ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling) salvage is an alternative | Queissert's 88-man ATOMS cohort reported 76.1% social continence at mean 42.5 mo; no randomized comparison to salvage AUS | Do not assume repeat fixed sling is effective |
| **Recurrent SUI after failed ATOMS** | [AUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) (AUA 2024 recommends AUS after sling failure) **or** repeat ATOMS (Angulo 2021 favors repeat ATOMS over AUS in observational data) | — | — |
| **Recurrent SUI after failed AUS** | Evaluate cause, then consider cause-specific AUS revision | Component replacement, cuff revision or selected tandem-cuff approach | Do not infer the best revision from a cohort's procedure frequency |
| **Concomitant ED + SUI** | [AUS + IPP](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction), concomitant or staged (AUA 2024 conditional recommendation; counsel on longer operation and possible higher reoperation risk)<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> | Sling + IPP | — |
| **Devastated outlet, all options exhausted** | [Bladder-neck closure + Mitrofanoff](/docs/surgical-techniques/04ab-bladder-neck-reconstruction/outlet-continence/bladder-neck-closure) | [Urinary diversion](/docs/surgical-techniques/04c-urinary-diversion) | Continued failed reconstruction |

## Comparative-meta snapshot

| Comparison | Source | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Sling vs AUS (overall)** | MASTER RCT, Abrams 2021 (n = 380, 12 mo)<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> | Sling **noninferior** on strict 12-mo leakage endpoint; several symptom/satisfaction secondary results favored AUS |
| **Sling vs AUS (moderate SUI)** | Sacco 2021 propensity-matched, retrourethral TiLOOP sling vs AMS 800<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup> | **AUS 94.3% vs sling 68.6%** "much improved" (p &lt; 0.001) |
| **Sling vs AUS (mild vs moderate)** | Khouri 2020<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> | Single-surgeon AdVance series: mild — sling 78%; moderate — AUS 80% vs sling 63% (p = 0.02) |
| **Adjustable sling vs AUS (subjective dry)** | Grabbert 2019 multicenter<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> | **AUS 57.3% vs adjustable sling 22.0%** (p &lt; 0.001); retrospective, and AUS patients had more severe baseline incontinence |
| **ATOMS vs ProACT** | Angulo 2019 PLoS meta<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> | Observational studies only; ATOMS higher dryness (68% vs 55%), satisfaction and explant-free survival (explant 5% vs 24%); complication rate did not differ significantly (17% vs 26%, p = 0.07); working device 3 yr **81% vs 58%** |
| **ATOMS vs REMEEX** | Angulo 2021 SR/meta (n = 1,919)<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> | Observational studies only; ATOMS higher dryness (69.3% vs 53.4%, p = 0.008) and lower explantation (5.5% vs 13.9%, p = 0.027); complication rates 18.9% vs 35.8% (p = 0.096) |
| **ATOMS vs AUS — durability** | Geretto 2023 propensity-matched<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> | 49 matched pairs: AUS higher dryness (44.9% vs 22.5%) but more high-grade complications, reinterventions and explantations; **device survival at 60 mo favors ATOMS (82% vs 67%, p = 0.03)** |

## MASTER trial — the definitive sling-vs-AUS RCT

The **MASTER trial** is the only RCT comparing male transobturator sling (predominantly AdVance) to AUS — 380 men across 27 UK centers.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

- **Primary outcome (continence at 12 mo):** male sling **noninferior** to AUS (87.0% vs 84.2% still incontinent by strict definition; risk difference 3.6%, 95% CI −11.6 to 4.6; non-inferiority p = 0.003).
- **ICIQ-UI SF:** both groups improved; AUS slightly greater (mean difference 1.4, p = 0.02).
- **24-month follow-up:** sling remained noninferior on the continence outcome; symptom scores again favored AUS (mean difference 1.4), and further surgery was more frequent after sling (11%) than after AUS (2%).<sup>[[21]](#ref21)</sup>
- **Serious adverse events:** six sling recipients and eleven AUS recipients had such events (13 AUS events); patient and event counts differ.
- **All secondary outcomes** with statistically significant differences favored AUS.
- **Cost-effectiveness:** sling more cost-effective (99% probability at £30,000 / QALY threshold), though slightly lower QALY gain.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Predictors of failure — common across implants

| Risk factor | Effect | Strength of evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Prior pelvic radiation** | OR 0.66–0.68 for sling success; ~ 3× higher explantation (meta-analysis)<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>; ATOMS explant HR 2.7 and failure HR 2.3<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> | Meta-analysis; multicenter cohort |
| **Severe baseline SUI** | Lower cure after male sling; slings are not routinely recommended in severe SUI | Multiple prospective<sup>[[11]](#ref11)[[12]](#ref12)</sup> |
| **Detrusor overactivity** | Independent mid-term failure predictor | Prospective<sup>[[13]](#ref13)</sup> |
| **Weak residual sphincter function / incomplete closure on the repositioning test** | OR 29.0 for retrourethral sling failure; resorbable sutures with ≤ 4 stitches OR 8.4; no sling tunnelling OR 22.6 | Prospective<sup>[[14]](#ref14)</sup> |
| **Diabetes** | HR 2.2 ATOMS failure | Multicenter<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> |
| **Prior urethral stricture surgery, 24-h pad test &gt; 200 g** | Higher failure after AdVance (ATOMS: prior urethral stenosis not associated with failure)<sup>[[15]](#ref15)</sup> | Prospective<sup>[[16]](#ref16)</sup> |

**Radiation is the single most important predictor of failure.** Ghaffar 2023 meta — irradiated patients OR 0.68, p &lt; 0.001 for sling success.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Salvage pathways

A failed male sling does **not preclude** subsequent [AUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter).<sup>[[17]](#ref17)[[20]](#ref20)</sup> Repeat sling after failure has a cure rate of about 45% at experienced centers; in one 61-man cohort, 55% of repeat slings failed versus 6% of salvage AUS.<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup>

- **After failed AdVance** → re-evaluate the cause, then recommend [AUS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/artificial-urinary-sphincter) (AUA/GURS/SUFU 2024).<sup>[[20]](#ref20)</sup> About 20% of sling recipients in a California cohort needed salvage AUS by 8 years, with durability comparable to primary AUS.<sup>[[22]](#ref22)</sup> Selected [ATOMS](/docs/surgical-techniques/04f-incontinence-procedures/procedures/atoms-sling) salvage is an alternative; Queissert's observational ATOMS cohort reported 76.1% social continence without proving superiority to AUS.
- **After failed ATOMS** → repeat ATOMS or AUS; Angulo 2021 (n = 902) — repeat ATOMS outperforms AUS for repeat implant on pad test, pad count, satisfaction, and PGI-I.
- **After failed Argus or REMEEX** → AUS (Hübner: 7/13, Kim: 12/18).

Simultaneous male sling + [penile prosthesis](/docs/surgical-techniques/04j-sexual-dysfunction) implantation is feasible and safe — pooled social continence 72–100% and prosthesis satisfaction 84–100%.<sup>[[18]](#ref18)</sup>
