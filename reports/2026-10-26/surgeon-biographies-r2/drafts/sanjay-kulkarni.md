## Biography

When Sanjay Kulkarni began introducing urethroplasty in Pune in the 1980s, he faced the problem of building a reconstructive practice where the operation was not yet established locally.[^4] He opened a small hospital with two operating theatres and 20 beds in 1995; by 2021 it had grown into a 100-bed urology centre.[^4] That institution became the base for the Kulkarni School of Urethral Surgery, which has trained visiting surgeons from other countries.[^4]

His operative work moved from buccal mucosa urethroplasty in 1997 to its use in the penile urethra in 2000 and a one-sided dissection approach in 2009.[^4] He describes his mentors and observation in the operating theatre as important to how he developed those techniques.[^4] The school turned that same approach into a way of teaching reconstruction: surgeons travel to observe operations and workshops before adapting the methods in their own practice.[^4]
