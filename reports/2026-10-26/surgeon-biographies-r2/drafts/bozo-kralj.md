BIOGRAPHY:

## Biography

Božo Kralj came to urogynecology after work on cervical insufficiency and the urinary consequences of cervical cancer treatment.[^2] From 1969 he developed an interdisciplinary urogynecology service in Ljubljana, introduced functional electrical stimulation for incontinence, and brought urodynamic testing into hospitals across Slovenia.[^2] His work responded to the number of women living with urinary incontinence and to the need for more systematic assessment and conservative treatment.[^2]

That concern also drew him into the group that founded IUGA in 1976.[^2] He later chaired its 1987 annual meeting in Ljubljana and served as its president, connecting the local service to an international professional network.[^2] He continued to write about pelvic floor dysfunction as a growing area of women's health, including a 2010 editorial on its social and therapeutic challenges.[^2]

Colleagues remembered Kralj as a teacher who shared knowledge freely and gave younger clinicians room to develop their own work.[^2] His teaching continued after he left clinical leadership, when he served as dean of two health-sciences faculties.[^2]

The Ljubljana service was only one part of his route into international work.[^2] He broadened his own training through a Fulbright scholarship in the United States, a British Council scholarship in London, and research visits.[^2] Those exchanges accompanied his later roles in Slovenian and Italian gynecology organizations.[^2] The memorial's authors describe perseverance, continual learning, and warmth beneath a resolute manner, traits they also saw in how he taught their generation.[^2]
