## Biography

Donald Ostergard's work in urogynecology began during obstetrics and gynecology residency, when Jack Robertson brought new tools for examining the female urethra to Harbor General Hospital.[^6] Their female urology clinic used those tools to evaluate women with incontinence.[^6] On a 1975 sabbatical, Ostergard visited clinicians developing urodynamics in Oslo, London, and San Francisco and reviewed a field whose literature was still sparse.[^6]

He brought microtransducer testing into the female urology clinic he opened in 1977 and began training a fellow the following year.[^6] Postgraduate courses then brought physicians from other centers to learn incontinence evaluation.[^6] With colleagues, he helped establish both IUGA and the Gynecologic Urology Society, creating places for that developing work to be discussed and taught.[^6]

Ostergard later collaborated with Oscar Contreras Ortiz to establish the International Urogynecology Journal and took on its managing editorship.[^6] In his retrospective, he describes reviewing manuscripts and finding reviewers before computers and the Internet eased that work.[^6] He also traces subsequent fellowship programs and society leaders to his trainees, showing how a clinic, courses, a journal, and mentorship carried the field beyond one institution.[^6]

He was willing to challenge surgical practice when follow-up data conflicted with surgeons' impressions.[^6] In a talk on stress incontinence, he contrasted reported results of anterior repair and Burch colposuspension and asked his audience to review its own patients' outcomes.[^6] His later account also describes longstanding concern about vaginal mesh complications, based partly on his earlier experience with prosthetic material.[^6] The same insistence on tracking results shaped his work from urodynamics through later debates over implants.[^6]
