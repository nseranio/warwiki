BIOGRAPHY:

## Biography

Harold Drutz entered pelvic medicine when women with pelvic floor disorders often had to see separate urology, gynecology, and colorectal services.[^1] During a travelling fellowship he worked with clinicians in Africa and Europe, then returned to Toronto in 1976 to build a urogynecology service at Mount Sinai Hospital.[^1] His approach joined care for the whole pelvic floor with investigation of the reasons behind established treatments.[^1]

He trained his first urogynecology fellow in the early 1980s and later led the University of Toronto division.[^1] At Baycrest he helped establish a pelvic floor service for older women, bringing that care into a geriatric hospital.[^1] Across his career he mentored 44 fellows, whose subsequent practices and training programs extended the work beyond Toronto.[^1]

Drutz also edited a multidisciplinary textbook and helped found the Canadian Society for Pelvic Medicine.[^1] His teaching and society work developed alongside clinical care, as urogynecology became a defined subspecialty in Canada.[^1]

He argued for care organized around the patient rather than around three separate departments.[^1] In his recollection, the older arrangement treated the pelvis as a set of isolated organs; the later program brought the needed specialties together.[^1] He also supported research into lower urinary tract pharmacology, thyroid function, and pelvic procedures.[^1] His textbook with Sender Herschorn and Nicholas Diamant gave learners from both urology and gynecology a common account of the field.[^1]
