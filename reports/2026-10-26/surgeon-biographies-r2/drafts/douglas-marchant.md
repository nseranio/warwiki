BIOGRAPHY:

## Biography

Douglas Marchant treated women's health as a problem that crossed the usual boundaries between specialties.[^2] At Tufts he established a Breast Health Center in 1978 that brought specialists together around patients with breast disease, instead of sending them through separate services.[^2] Colleagues described its shared approach to diagnosis and treatment as a break from the practice of the time.[^2]

Marchant had trained in both surgery and obstetrics, and his teaching at Tufts began in gynecology before he directed its cancer institute.[^2] Robert Kennison, one of his former residents, remembered a demanding surgical teacher who expected precision.[^2] Radiologist Marc Homer recalled that Marchant welcomed changes that could improve care or make it less harmful.[^2]

After leaving Tufts in 1992, Marchant established another Breast Health Center at Women and Infants Hospital in Providence.[^2] The move carried a model of coordinated care beyond the institution where he had developed it.[^2]

The breast center also changed when a concern reached specialists.[^2] Homer recalled an era when women might delay telling a doctor about a breast lump until routine imaging brought it to attention.[^2] Within the center, radiology, surgery, and other services could work toward the same diagnosis and treatment plan.[^2] Marchant's colleagues remembered him as meticulous with surgery and receptive to ideas from other specialties when those ideas helped patients.[^2]
