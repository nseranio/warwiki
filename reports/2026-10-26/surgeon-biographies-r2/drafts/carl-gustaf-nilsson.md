BIOGRAPHY:

## Biography

Carl-Gustaf Nilsson's early research was in family planning rather than incontinence surgery.[^3] In Tapani Luukkainen's steroid laboratory he studied hormone-releasing intrauterine systems and completed a thesis on that work in 1977.[^3] He later worked with Ulf Ulmsten on tension-free vaginal tape (TVT) for stress urinary incontinence.[^3]

Nilsson used TVT from an early stage and followed patients for years after surgery.[^3] That long follow-up produced evidence on the procedure's durability, linking the development of a new operation to assessment of its later results.[^3] He remained involved in assessing newer urogynecologic operations after formally retiring in 2012.[^3]

Away from medicine, he trained the Finnish sailing team at the 1976 and 1980 Olympic Games.[^3] The obituary by Ian Milsom remembers both his work on TVT and a colleague active in the wider urogynecology community.[^3]

The transition from contraceptive research to an incontinence operation also changed the kind of evidence Nilsson could gather.[^3] His early participation in TVT meant that he could observe the same patients well beyond initial recovery.[^3] He published those long-term observations and supervised six theses, giving younger researchers a part in evaluating the procedure.[^3] His continued involvement after retirement kept that focus on assessing operations rather than simply introducing them.[^3]
