BIOGRAPHY:

## Biography

Paul Riss helped build the organizations and teaching routes through which urogynecology spread internationally.[^1] Working with Engelbert Hanzel, he developed an IUGA exchange program for countries where the specialty was still developing; at least half the course speakers were to come from the host country.[^1] That design gave local clinicians a place in the teaching rather than treating them only as an audience.[^1]

Riss and Peter Dwyer later led the International Urogynecology Journal together.[^1] During their editorship the journal added video articles and other formats and changed its manuscript workflow.[^1] Colleagues also recalled Riss teaching pelvic floor anatomy and prolapse assessment in workshops, and advocating conservative treatment and careful research.[^1]

The tributes describe a soft-spoken colleague who made time for younger editors and trainees.[^1] One colleague remembered an operative workshop in India where Riss declined travel support and contributed materials to the host department.[^1] His editorial and society work thus sat alongside direct instruction and help to clinicians in developing programs.[^1]

Riss's approach to education was also visible in the details of his lectures.[^1] Manidip Pal recalled using Riss's way of teaching pelvic floor anatomy in a textbook chapter and receiving comments from him on the manuscript.[^1] Pal also described Riss's visual explanation of POP-Q measurement.[^1] Other colleagues remembered his preference for conservative treatment as a first step and his repeated invitations to discuss its evidence.[^1]
