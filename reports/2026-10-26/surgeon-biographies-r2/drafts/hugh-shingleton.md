BIOGRAPHY:

## Biography

Hugh Shingleton built a gynecologic oncology program at the University of Alabama at Birmingham after moving there in 1969.[^1] He established its division and directed it while the specialty's organizations and research groups were taking shape.[^1] He was a founding member of both the Society of Gynecologic Oncology and the Gynecologic Oncology Group, as well as the society that became AUGS.[^1]

Shingleton's work on cervical cancer extended into textbooks used by gynecologic oncology trainees.[^1] The colleagues who wrote his society memorial described him as focused on improving women's cancer care and on making diagnosis and treatment more systematic.[^1] His surgical and teaching work met in a division that trained later leaders of the specialty.[^1]

His former colleagues also recalled how he developed people: he concentrated on their strengths, pushed them to improve, and expected them to take on needed work.[^1] The memorial traces later society presidents and board leaders among his trainees.[^1]

Shingleton's institutional work extended beyond the division he founded.[^1] He later chaired obstetrics and gynecology at UAB while remaining active in the national gynecologic oncology societies.[^1] Editorial service on *Gynecologic Oncology* and work with the subspecialty board gave him a role in how the field published research and trained specialists.[^1] The account by James Orr and Ronald Alvarez places his surgical skill in the context of that larger program of research, teaching, and professional organization.[^1]
