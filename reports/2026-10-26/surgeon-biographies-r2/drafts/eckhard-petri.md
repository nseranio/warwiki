BIOGRAPHY:

## Biography

Eckhard Petri trained in both urology and gynecology in Mainz and worked at the boundary between those disciplines.[^2] He began organizing scientific meetings on urogynecology in Mainz, continued them in Idar-Oberstein, and carried them to Schwerin when he moved there as a department chief.[^2] The Schwerin meetings drew specialists from across the German-speaking field and became a recurring forum for operative and research exchange.[^2]

Petri also helped found IUGA and later served as its president.[^4] He chaired its 1983 meeting in Mainz, co-chaired the 1985 Budapest meeting, and served on the International Urogynecology Journal editorial board.[^4] His work as a visiting surgeon and teacher extended beyond Germany; IUGA's memorial records trainees from countries across Asia, Europe, and the Americas.[^4]

Colleagues in the German urogynecology society remembered him as open to new developments while asking for evidence, and as a clinician who continued to train others throughout his career.[^2] The meetings and fellows were two ways he spread a cross-specialty practice beyond his own operating room.[^2][^4]

Petri's move to Schwerin became an important test of that network.[^2] His colleagues recall that the hospital and region faced difficulties, yet he continued the scientific meeting there and brought established urogynecologists to it.[^2] He wrote and revised *Gynäkologische Urologie* across four editions while maintaining a broad surgical practice that included oncology and breast surgery.[^2][^4] The society's obituary names fellows from Argentina, Mexico, Korea, India, Nepal, Jordan, Syria, and several European countries as part of the reach of his teaching.[^4]
