## Biography

Tom Lue arrived at UCSF expecting a one-year research fellowship before private practice.[^3] He turned from a long-term bladder neuroprosthesis project to penile physiology after finding that stimulation of certain pelvic nerve branches produced erection without the bladder and bowel responses of broader sacral stimulation.[^3] Emil Tanagho offered him a clinical instructor position at the end of the year.[^3]

Experiments in dogs and monkeys, along with studies of fresh human cadaver tissue, helped Lue and colleagues describe cavernous smooth muscle relaxation, arterial inflow, and venous compression in erection.[^3] After Giles Brindley's demonstration of injection-induced erection, Lue and Richard Schmidt explored intracavernous papaverine.[^3] Lue's account describes an early prolonged erection and acute hypertension after an attempted reversal.[^3]

The physiology work led to clinical studies of penile blood flow and to operations for Peyronie's disease and priapism.[^3] In 1998, he and colleagues established a molecular urology laboratory at UCSF with visiting fellows and basic scientists.[^3]
