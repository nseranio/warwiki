## Biography

Alan Wein entered urology when its broad mix of surgery and medical care appealed to him.[^11] With Paul Abrams, he coined “overactive bladder” in place of terms such as “unstable bladder,” which they thought misleading.[^11]

The change began when they agreed to lead a symposium only if its proposed title could be changed.[^11] Wein says the International Continence Society later adopted the new term.[^11] He also organized lower urinary tract dysfunction around failure to store or failure to empty, using those functions to guide diagnostic testing and treatment.[^11]

Wein developed that framework after observing that older terms largely described neurologic disorders.[^11] He described storage as filling at low pressure with a closed bladder neck and urethra and no involuntary contractions; emptying required a sustained bladder contraction and an open, unobstructed outlet.[^11] After decades at the University of Pennsylvania, he continued seeing patients and mentoring at the University of Miami.[^11]
