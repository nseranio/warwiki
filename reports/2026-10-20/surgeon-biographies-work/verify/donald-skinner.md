## Corrected BIOGRAPHY

## Biography

At UCLA, Donald Skinner developed his work in urologic oncology.[^3] There he performed post-chemotherapy retroperitoneal lymph node dissections for patients with testicular cancer, many of whom were then considered incurable.[^3]

When Skinner arrived at USC in 1980, its urology division had few faculty, little infrastructure, and no federal research funding.[^3] He recruited faculty, grew a fellowship program, and helped plan the Norris Comprehensive Cancer Center.[^3] In bladder cancer he adapted the continent Kock pouch to an orthotopic neobladder and advocated meticulous pelvic lymph node dissection during cystectomy.[^3] He also maintained prospective clinical databases so outcomes from large operative series could be studied and reported.[^3] His team used those records for a major cystectomy outcomes paper, and he paired surgical specimens with laboratory research into bladder cancer biology.[^3]

Former trainees Anne Schuckman and David Ginsberg remembered daily rounds attended by the full urology team, with Skinner speaking directly and calmly to patients while demanding precision from trainees.[^3] The same memorial describes how he linked operative specimens with molecular research by working with Peter Jones on genetic and epigenetic studies of bladder cancer.[^3] He trained surgeons who went on to lead oncology programs, and he later described attention to technique, patient commitment, and communication as the lessons he most wanted them to carry.[^3] Colleagues also recalled classical music in his operating room, a small part of the working atmosphere around complex cases.[^3]
