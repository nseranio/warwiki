## Corrected BIOGRAPHY

## Biography

Stuart Stanton developed a urogynecology service for female urinary incontinence and pelvic floor disorders at St George’s Hospital.[^2] Under the influence of Richard Turner-Warwick, he learned Burch colposuspension.[^2] His research and influence helped make colposuspension widely used for stress urinary incontinence during the 1970s to 1990s.[^2]

Stanton believed research was vital to the development of urogynecology.[^2] He helped found the International Continence Society and International Urogynecological Association and worked on ICS standardisation committees.[^2] His department studied female incontinence and pelvic floor disorders while attracting trainees from Britain and abroad; many of those trainees went on to establish their own departments and research programs in other institutions.[^2] Dwyer described Stanton’s department as a model for units later developed by his trainees.[^2] Stanton also convened the ICS annual scientific meeting in London in 1985.[^2]

Peter Dwyer, a former trainee, remembered a demanding mentor who expected high standards and was known for his humor and humanity.[^2] Dwyer described a busy theatre with observers and open discussion, where surgical work and teaching happened together.[^2] In later life Stanton moved to Jerusalem, continued to work in medicine, and regularly returned to London to visit family.[^2] His career linked a local urogynecology unit to an international training network through the people who learned in it.[^2]
