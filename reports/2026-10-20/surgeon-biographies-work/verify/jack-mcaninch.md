## Corrected BIOGRAPHY

## Biography

Jack McAninch arrived in urology when genitourinary trauma and reconstruction had few dedicated training programs.[^2] During residency at Letterman Army Hospital, he encountered the complex pelvic wounds of servicemen returning from Vietnam.[^2] He later taught residents and fellows how to make decisions when the injury found at surgery differed from what they had expected.[^2] Before medicine, he had expected to study animal husbandry; his interest in basic science led him from a West Texas ranch to medical school.[^2]

At San Francisco General Hospital, McAninch built a clinical record of trauma cases and later a reconstruction database.[^2] By 2014 the trauma database contained 4,000 cases, and the reconstruction database begun in the 1980s remained a research tool.[^2] He continued to work at the county hospital after retirement, advising trainees and conducting outcomes research.[^2]

From 1989 onward he trained a fellow each year in male genitourinary trauma and reconstruction, and former fellows went on to lead programs in other cities.[^2] At a 2014 gathering of those fellows, 23 of the 25 he had trained returned to San Francisco.[^2]
