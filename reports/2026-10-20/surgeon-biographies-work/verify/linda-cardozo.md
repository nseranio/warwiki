## Corrected BIOGRAPHY

## Biography

Linda Cardozo began in obstetrics, but a research fellowship with Stuart Stanton turned her attention to urodynamics and lower urinary tract dysfunction.[^4] Her work on detrusor instability became an MD thesis, and the research questions she met at St George's shaped the remainder of her career in urogynecology.[^4] At King's College Hospital she led the Urogynaecology Unit as the subspecialty gained formal recognition in Britain.[^4]

She supervised more than 60 research fellows who later became consultants across the world and welcomed visitors and observers whose time at King's developed into lasting collaborations.[^4] When King's became an accredited UK urogynecology subspecialty training centre in 1996, she trained eight subspecialists there.[^4] She also co-edited the *Textbook of Female Urology and Urogynaecology*.[^4] She supervised 15 MD theses and one PhD.[^4]

Her teaching reached professional societies as well as hospital trainees.[^4] She helped establish the British Society of Urogynaecology, chaired its founding period, and served in education roles with the International Continence Society.[^4] Colleagues described her as a gifted communicator who lectured widely and advocated for women's health.[^4] Robinson also recalled that she earned a pilot's licence and trained in aerobatic flying as a junior doctor.[^4]
