## Corrected BIOGRAPHY

## Biography

In a Norfolk operating room, Charles Devine and Charles Horton reconsidered how a hypospadias repair was being done.[^2] Gerald Jordan's memorial account describes that encounter as the start of years of reconstructive work together.[^2] The two surgeons developed operations for hypospadias, epispadias, Peyronie disease, genital trauma, and phallic reconstruction.[^2]

Devine worked with his brother Patrick on urethral stricture reconstruction, including patch graft techniques.[^2] Their work developed in a local practice that also built a urology residency and helped establish Eastern Virginia Medical School, where Charles became the first department chair.[^2]

Devine, Patrick Devine, and Horton held surgical teaching clinics in Norfolk where visiting urologists and plastic surgeons watched operations over periods that could last weeks.[^2] Devine carried those lessons to postgraduate courses and international meetings, explaining tissue handling across reconstructive problems.[^2] The clinics showed primary and complex hypospadias operations as well as repairs after genital trauma, giving visitors a range of operative problems to observe.[^2] Devine founded the Society for Genitourinary Reconstructive Surgery.[^2] A separate Norfolk Academy memorial resolution recalled Devine's love of sailing.[^2]
