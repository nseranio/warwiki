## Corrected BIOGRAPHY

## Biography

Axel Ingelman-Sundberg developed operations for both stress and urge urinary incontinence.[^6] His work ranged from a pubococcygeus muscle repair for stress incontinence to an operation intended to reduce urge incontinence through partial bladder denervation.[^6]

During the 1939 war between Finland and Russia, he was sent to Sweden's northern border and organized a fully equipped military hospital.[^6] Years later, his department at Sabbatsberg in Stockholm combined clinical work, medical education, and research; Harold Drutz remembered rounds that included staff, fellows, residents, students, and nurses.[^6] The teaching reached trainees who carried related work forward, including Ulf Ulmsten, whom Drutz identifies as his protégé.[^6]

In 1976 Ingelman-Sundberg and Jack Robertson gathered an international group at the FIGO meeting in Mexico City to establish the International Urogynecological Association.[^6] Its stated purpose joined education with clinical and basic research on female pelvic floor disorders across countries, and Ingelman-Sundberg became its first president.[^6] Drutz later remembered him at annual scientific sessions, seated in the front row and asking detailed questions.[^6] With his wife Mirjam Furuhjelm and collaborators, he also published *A Child is Born*, using photographs to explain life before birth to expectant parents.[^6]
