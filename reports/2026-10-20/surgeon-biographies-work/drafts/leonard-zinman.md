BIOGRAPHY:

## Biography

Leonard Zinman's interest in genitourinary reconstruction grew during urology training at Massachusetts General Hospital, where Wyland Leadbetter and W. Hardy Hendren encouraged that direction.[^1] He later worked with Richard Turner-Warwick in London and returned to Lahey Clinic to establish its Center for Reconstructive Urologic Surgery in 1973.[^1] Over the following decades that center became the base for a practice focused on problems that often required more than a standard urethral repair.[^1]

Zinman described his group's work as addressing radiation injuries and long, high-risk strictures with combinations of oral mucosa grafts, muscle or myocutaneous flaps, and other graft materials.[^1] The same obituary records reconstructive work after male genital cancer and experiments with injectable agents intended to limit fibrosis.[^1] These were techniques for damaged tissue and difficult fields, where a surgeon needed more than one reconstructive material or route.[^1]

Teaching and institution building ran alongside the operations.[^1] Zinman directed Lahey's residency program, later chaired urology, and helped establish the Society of Genitourinary Reconstructive Surgeons with Gerald Jordan, Charles Devine, and Charles Horton.[^1] Colleagues remembered his persistent inquiry and affection for both trainees and patients, while the program he built gave residents and fellows repeated exposure to complex reconstruction.[^1] His professional network joined the London school that influenced him to a North American reconstructive community with its own teaching society.[^1] He remained associated with Lahey for more than five decades, creating a center devoted to reconstructive surgery and training many residents, whom colleagues later described as a central part of his influence on the next generation.[^1]
