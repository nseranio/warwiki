BIOGRAPHY:

## Biography

Stuart Stanton helped bring urinary incontinence and pelvic floor disorders into a dedicated gynecologic service when the work was still divided between specialties.[^2] At St George's Hospital he learned Burch colposuspension under the influence of Richard Turner-Warwick and developed a urogynecology service around careful investigation and surgery.[^2] His research and teaching helped make colposuspension widely used for stress urinary incontinence during the following decades.[^2]

Stanton believed that a subspecialty needed common language and sound research as much as operative skill.[^2] He helped found the International Continence Society and International Urogynecological Association and worked on ICS standardisation committees.[^2] His department studied female incontinence and pelvic floor disorders while attracting trainees from Britain and abroad; many of those trainees went on to establish their own departments and research programs in other institutions.[^2] The clinical service thus became a route for spreading both techniques and a model of specialist training.[^2] Stanton also convened the ICS annual scientific meeting in London in 1985, giving that developing research community another forum for exchanging results.[^2]

Peter Dwyer, a former trainee, remembered a demanding mentor who expected high standards but also brought humor and humanity to the operating theatre.[^2] Dwyer described a busy theatre with observers and open discussion, where surgical work and teaching happened together.[^2] In later life Stanton moved to Jerusalem, continued to work in medicine, and remained connected with colleagues and family in London.[^2] His career linked a local urogynecology unit to an international training network through the people who learned in it.[^2]
