BIOGRAPHY:

## Biography

Linda Cardozo began in obstetrics, but a research fellowship with Stuart Stanton turned her attention to urodynamics and lower urinary tract dysfunction.[^4] Her work on detrusor instability became an MD thesis, and the research questions she met at St George's shaped the remainder of her career in urogynecology.[^4] At King's College Hospital she built a unit that joined clinical practice to research and training as the subspecialty gained formal recognition in Britain.[^4]

Cardozo's influence travelled chiefly through people and shared methods.[^4] She supervised more than 60 research fellows who later became consultants across the world and welcomed visitors and observers whose time at King's developed into lasting collaborations.[^4] When King's became an accredited UK urogynecology subspecialty training centre in 1996, she trained eight subspecialists there.[^4] She also co-edited the *Textbook of Female Urology and Urogynaecology*, bringing clinical and research work into a reference used beyond her own department.[^4] She guided numerous advanced research projects, including 15 MD theses and a PhD, while building the department's link between study and specialist training.[^4]

Her teaching reached professional societies as well as hospital trainees.[^4] She helped establish the British Society of Urogynaecology, chaired its founding period, and served in education roles with the International Continence Society.[^4] Colleagues described her as a direct, energetic communicator who lectured widely and kept women's health at the centre of the work.[^4] A fellow clinician's memorial also records the pilot's licence and aerobatic flying she pursued as a junior doctor, a pursuit outside medicine that her colleagues recalled.[^4]
