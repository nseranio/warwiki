BIOGRAPHY:

## Biography

Charles Devine's reconstructive work began in a Norfolk operating room where he and plastic surgeon Charles Horton reconsidered how a hypospadias repair was being done.[^2] Gerald Jordan's memorial account describes that encounter as the start of a long collaboration rather than a single technique.[^2] The two surgeons applied principles of tissue handling to hypospadias and epispadias, then extended their work to Peyronie disease, genital trauma, and phallic reconstruction.[^2]

Devine worked with his brother Patrick on urethral stricture reconstruction, including patch graft techniques.[^2] Their work developed in a local practice that also built a urology residency and helped establish Eastern Virginia Medical School, where Charles became the first department chair.[^2] The residency and visiting clinics gave the operative work a place to be taught and passed to subsequent surgeons.[^2]

The group taught through direct observation as well as papers.[^2] Devine, Patrick Devine, and Horton held surgical teaching clinics in Norfolk where visiting urologists and plastic surgeons watched operations over periods that could last weeks.[^2] Devine carried those lessons to postgraduate courses and international meetings, explaining tissue handling across reconstructive problems rather than presenting a single signature operation.[^2] The clinics showed primary and complex hypospadias operations as well as repairs after genital trauma, giving visitors a range of operative problems to observe.[^2] Devine founded a professional society for genitourinary reconstruction, extending the teaching network beyond Norfolk.[^2] Jordan also recalled Devine's love of sailing, a detail that sits alongside the demanding surgical and teaching schedule in colleagues' account of his life.[^2]
