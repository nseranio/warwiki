BIOGRAPHY:

## Biography

Frank Hinman Jr. grew up in a family already closely tied to urology, then made his own career at the intersection of surgery, research, and medical illustration.[^1][^4] After wartime Navy service, he returned to California for urology training, joined his father's practice, and became deeply involved in university research and education.[^1][^4] His work on bladder defense mechanisms received prolonged research support and helped shape clinical approaches to urinary infection.[^4]

Hinman also described a form of bladder dysfunction that came to bear his name.[^4] His writing reached surgeons through atlases of urologic and pediatric urologic operations and a volume on surgical anatomy, placing his surgical writing beside his lifelong work in visual art.[^4] He helped found the Society of Pediatric Urology in 1951, then used leadership and teaching roles to connect operative practice with a growing research community.[^4]

Drawing was not simply a professional tool for him; he began painting during quieter periods of wartime service and continued through the end of his life.[^4] His obituary records a large body of paintings, including work published in *Life* and used on covers of the *Journal of the American Medical Association*.[^4] The visual skill visible in his atlases thus belonged to a broader artistic practice that continued outside medicine.[^4] He and his wife Marion were also devoted sailors, and their memorial account describes trainees and friends as a central part of the community they left behind.[^4] The San Francisco Yacht Club named him Yachtsman of the Year in 1998, and his obituary records more than 250 scientific articles alongside the books and paintings.[^4]
