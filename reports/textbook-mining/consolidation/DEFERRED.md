# Questions for the user (all resolved September 30, 2026)

The user asked Claude to resolve these with best judgment and the most
reliable source.

1. **Q-flap (Morey 2000) follow-up numbers** (`q-flap.mdx`; AMUGRS-017-002,
   TRUS08-057-004). The publisher's full text was not reachable (Wiley 403;
   the user's Chrome session did not respond). The abstract, retrieved from
   UT Southwestern's research repository, reports 15 men, excellent results in
   10, and complications including recurrent stricture in 2; it gives no
   follow-up duration. The page already states exactly these abstract figures.
   **Resolution:** no change; neither book's follow-up figures (13 of 15 at a
   mean 42.6 months versus 10 of 13 at a mean 42 months) is added, since
   neither can be verified against the paper. Revisit if the full text becomes
   available.

2. **Proximal dilator size** (`revision-scenarios.mdx`; PIS-013-002). The
   "avoid dilators under 9 mm" advice comes from one chapter (Alnajjar and
   Muneer, *Penile Implant Surgery*), whose supporting citation (Karpman 2016)
   is a pictorial piece about crossover, not crural perforation; standard
   sequences on the site start at 8 mm. **Resolution:** added as a short
   prevention note labelled expert opinion without comparative data, alongside
   the shared principle of dilating the crura without force; no numeric rule.

3. **Manual modeling hand position** (`manual-modeling.mdx`; PIS-005-001).
   The most reliable source is Lucas 2020 (*Urology*; Wilson, who introduced
   modeling, is a co-author), which describes bending "while maintaining
   glanular pressure to prevent urethral injury". The chapter (Haile, Bajic and
   Levine) places the bending hand on the distal shaft, not the glans, with
   digital pressure over the corporotomies, and warns against downward pressure
   on the cylinder tips. Both describe the same mechanism (a cylinder tip
   driven through the fossa navicularis) and are compatible. **Resolution:**
   the page now combines them (protect the glans; bend from the distal shaft;
   avoid pressing down on the tips) and drops an unsupported explanation
   (urethral compression against the pubic symphysis).
