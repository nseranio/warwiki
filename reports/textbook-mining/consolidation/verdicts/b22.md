## Deferred questions

1. **revision-scenarios.mdx — PIS-013-002 (proximal dilator caliber).** PIS ch.13 (Alnajjar & Muneer) states that avoiding narrow-calibre dilators (&lt;9 mm) during proximal corporal dilation reduces crural-perforation risk, citing Karpman E. *J Sex Med* 2016;13(6):1008-12 (PMID 27215691). That article is a short "Surgical Techniques" pictorial/video piece with no indexed abstract text, so the &lt;9 mm threshold's evidentiary basis could not be independently confirmed. The page's existing fibrosis-escalation sequence starts Hegar dilation at 8 mm (a different clinical context — progressive whole-corpus dilation in fibrosis, not a single perforation-risk threshold for routine proximal dilation), so no existing text was changed. Needs a specialist read of the full Karpman article (or a better primary source) before deciding whether to add a caliber caution anywhere on the site.

2. **manual-modeling.mdx — PIS-005-001 (modeling hand position).** PIS ch.5 (Haile, Bajic, Levine) describes grasping the distal shaft (not the glans) during manual modeling, with digital pressure over the corporotomy sites, and specifically cautions that downward pressure on the corporal tips may promote distal urethral perforation. This differs from the page's current instruction that sustained glanular pressure is "the main safeguard against urethral injury," cited to Wilson & Delk 1994 and Lucas 2020 (both already on the page). This is a safety-critical technique instruction for preventing the defining complication of modeling. I did not edit the page: reconciling it requires reading Wilson & Delk 1994 and Lucas 2020 directly to determine whether "glanular pressure" and "avoiding downward pressure on the corporal tips while supporting the distal shaft" are the same maneuver described differently, or a genuine disagreement on hand position, before touching this instruction.
