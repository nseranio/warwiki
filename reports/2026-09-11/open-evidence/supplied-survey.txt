## Storage, Incontinence & Neuromodulation

The **MUSA trial** (JAMA, June 2025) is the landmark storage/SUI publication: in 137 women with moderate-to-severe mixed incontinence, onabotulinumtoxinA 100U was **not superior** to midurethral sling for UDI change at 6 months (−66.8 vs −84.9; difference 18.1, 95% CI −4.6 to 40.7, p=0.12), with the sling better for stress symptoms and botulinum toxin better early for irritative symptoms; 22.6% ultimately received both, underscoring that MUI often needs multimodal therapy.[1] The **FUTURE trial** (Lancet, March 2025) — the largest RCT of routine urodynamics in refractory OAB (n=1,099) — found no benefit of urodynamics + clinical assessment over assessment alone (success 23.6% vs 22.7%; adjusted OR 1.12) and that urodynamics is unlikely cost-effective (£42,643/QALY), though critics note it changed the diagnosis in ≥13% and reduced invasive procedures.[2][3][4]

For implantable tibial neuromodulation, the **Revi OASIS** pivotal trial now has durable 2- and 3-year data (79% responder rate at 36 months, 95% satisfaction, no serious AEs), and Revi is the first FDA-cleared iTNM device usable without prior conservative-therapy failure.[5][6] A systematic review of 1,766 patients found SNM and iTNM have nearly identical efficacy (UUI response ~71–72%; OAB response 74–79%) but iTNM had far fewer serious device-related AEs (0.3% vs 3.3%).[7]

On the pharmacologic side, vibegron accumulated substantial evidence: the **COURAGE** responder analysis (men with OAB+BPH), a comprehensive review, and a network meta-analysis showing vibegron 100 mg outperformed mirabegron and antimuscarinics for micturition frequency.[8][9][10] Botulinum toxin technique evolved as well, with a scoping review supporting fewer injection sites (1–10) maintaining efficacy and safe trigone inclusion.[11]

## Neurogenic LUTD & Voiding/Outlet Disorders

The **NEUROGED Guidelines** (EAN/EFAS/INUS, April 2025) are the first neuro-urology guidelines tailored for neurologists, providing screening, workup, and a risk-stratified referral algorithm.[12] A major therapeutic debate emerged over first-line storage therapy in NLUTD — a narrative review argued β3-agonists should be first-line given cognitive concerns with antimuscarinics, and the first head-to-head crossover RCT of[13] **vibegron vs fesoterodine** for neurogenic detrusor overactivity showed comparable urodynamics but strong patient preference for vibegron.[14] The 2024 EAU guideline now positions SNM as equivalent to intradetrusor botulinum toxin for NLUTD, a status reviewed in the ICI-RS 2025 report.[15] For the underactive detrusor, the two-part **ICI-RS 2024** think-tank concluded no therapy reliably augments detrusor contractility, though intravesical electrical stimulation reduced PVR (MD −108.7 mL) in a 2025 meta-analysis.[16][17][18] The **JAMA review of male LUTS** (Wei et al., Sept 2025) is a useful synthesis across BPH, OAB, and voiding dysfunction.[19]

## Female Stress Incontinence & BPH/Male LUTS Procedures

Two practice-shaping SUI publications appeared. The Finnish **TVT vs Bulkamid 5-year RCT** (NEJM Evidence, 2025) confirmed polyacrylamide hydrogel is **not noninferior** to retropubic TVT for satisfaction/cure (objective cure 78% vs 96% at 3 yr) but has roughly half the complications — a genuine, safer alternative for appropriately counseled women.[20] A meta-analysis of 24 RCTs (3,687 women) found single-incision mini-slings noninferior to standard MUS with faster recovery and less pain, though a separate 5-year RCT flagged declining SIMS objective cure over time.[21][22] An England HES analysis (2013–2024) documented the near-total cessation of mesh tape after 2019 and the rise of bulking agents as mainstay, only partially compensating and suggesting an undertreated SUI cohort.[23]

In BPH, the **2026 AUA guideline** (Part III, procedural) upgraded HoLEP/ThuLEP to Strong Recommendation as size-independent options and PAE to Conditional for prostates ≥50 cc, with a new volume-stratified algorithm.[24][25] The **WATER III trial** (Eur Urol Focus, March 2026) was the first comparison of Aquablation vs laser enucleation in large glands (80–180 mL): noninferior IPSS at 3 months with dramatically better ejaculatory preservation (retrograde ejaculation 14.8% vs 77.1%) and no persistent SUI (0% vs 9.3%), albeit only 3-month follow-up.[26] A new Cochrane HoLEP review and pooled Aquablation analyses across the WATER series round out the evidence.[25][27]

## Urethral & Bladder Neck Reconstruction

The **EAU 2026 urethral stricture guideline** update positions the Optilume drug-coated balloon (DCB) as a promising second-line endoluminal option for bulbar strictures.[29] DCB evidence expanded substantially: **ROBUST I** 5-year (58% functional success, 72% freedom from reintervention) and **ROBUST III** 3-year data (71% freedom from reintervention), plus the first comparative data for posterior/sphincteric stenosis showing improved recurrence-free survival with no de novo incontinence, and the **FIRST-CARE** RCT protocol testing DCB as first-line in treatment-naïve bulbar strictures.[30][31][32][33][34] A meta-analysis found dorsal and ventral onlay BMG equivalent for success, with less transient ED using ventral onlay.[35] For bladder neck/vesicourethral stenosis, novel techniques emerged — transurethral incision with transverse mucosal realignment (93.5% success) and robotic transvesical bladder neck reconstruction (91% success).[36][37]

For post-prostatectomy incontinence, the **AUSCO trial** (J Urol, Oct 2025) is the first prospective multicenter AMS 800 study: 94% achieved >50% pad-weight reduction, 60% pad-free, 7.8% revision, zero infections, with parallel data showing improved emotional health.[28][38] The **AdVance/AdVance XP 10-year** follow-up showed 67.8% early cure but 28.8% late loss of continence, and French national data quantified AUS reintervention-free survival at 40% at 10 years.[39][40]

## Fistula Repair

The **IUGA-ICS consensus** (2026) redefined surgical success as anatomical closure **plus** absence of residual incontinence, with an "incurable" label reserved for three independently assessed failed repairs.[41] A **FIGO expert opinion** (July 2025) standardized evaluation of post-obstetric-fistula-repair incontinence.[42] Robotic VVF repair showed low recurrence (~2.9%) in a 14-study review, and a 25-year single-center series (n=638) documented the shift to minimally invasive repair with hysterectomy now surpassing obstetric causes as the leading etiology; prior radiotherapy consistently predicted failure.[43][44][45] For rectovaginal fistula, the GRECCAR multicenter cohort reported 81% success with delayed coloanal anastomosis.[46]

## Pelvic Pain & IC/BPS

A **global consensus on IC/BPS therapeutics** (2026) and the **ICI-RS 2025** consensus (favoring the term "Primary Bladder Pain Syndrome") reframed the field toward phenotype-directed care.[47][48] A Nature Reviews Urology review proposed a phenotype-based multimodal algorithm distinguishing Hunner (fulguration first-line) from non-Hunner disease, with BoNT-A the only therapy with strong guideline support.[49] The **MAPP Network** analysis in Nature Reviews Urology explained repeated trial failures and advocated phenotyping-based trial design.[50] PPS maculopathy evidence strengthened toward causality, including a Korean cohort of 103,553 users (adjusted HR 1.34).[51][52] For chronic pelvic pain, a high-certainty systematic review found multimodal physical therapy reduced pain substantially (short-term SMD −1.69).[53]

## Defecatory Disorders

The **Rome V criteria** (Gastroenterology, 2026) revised anorectal disorder definitions — removing "functional" from dyssynergic defecation, requiring only one abnormal test, and lowering the fecal incontinence threshold to ≥1 episode/month (raising prevalence to 4.2%).[54][55] A cross-society **Pelvic Floor Consortium consensus** (2026) addressed pelvic floor PT for obstructed defecation.[56] Two contrasting neuromodulation trials appeared: the positive sham-controlled **TNT trial** for fecal incontinence (responder rates 66–81% vs 32% sham) and the negative **SUBSoNIC** crossover RCT that failed to confirm SNM efficacy, raising real questions about this widely used therapy.[57][58]

## Urinary Diversion, Augmentation, Catheterizable Channels & Upper Tract

Contemporary practice is shifting: a 6,469-patient international analysis documented declining neobladder use (41%→19%) and rising cutaneous ureterostomy.[59] A meta-analysis found no long-term renal-function difference between ileal conduit and neobladder, but higher ureteroenteric stricture risk with neobladder.[60] Robotic intracorporeal diversion matured with the largest intracorporeal neobladder series (n=410) and first long-term (60-month) urodynamic/metabolic data.[61][62] Continent diversion for neurogenic bladder generated a structured pro/con debate in Neurourology & Urodynamics.[63][64][65] Upper-tract reconstruction saw major series: robotic **BMG ureteroplasty** with 92% success across 163 patients over a decade, the largest robotic **ileal ureter replacement** series (n=48, 91.7% success, worse with radiation), and single-port BMG and ileal ureter feasibility series.[66][67][68][69] A robotic ureteral stricture treatment algorithm was proposed for the robotic era.[70] For catheterizable channels, a 175-child, 27-year series found 97% durable channel utility with the appendix favored, and the largest adult CCC series (n=44) reported 75.7% continued use.[71][72]

## Genital Reconstruction & Gender-Affirming Surgery

The **GenderCOS project** delivered the first international core outcome sets for both feminizing and masculinizing genital gender-affirming surgery, with validated measurement instruments in a 2026 follow-up — a major standardization advance.[73][74][75] The largest **robotic peritoneal flap vaginoplasty** series (500 cases) reported low procedural-intervention rates and superior depth outcomes, and a prospective study confirmed quality-of-life gains.[76][77] The first **international Delphi consensus on feminizing genital surgery** reached agreement on 63/68 perioperative statements but not on hormone-therapy timing.[78] Phalloplasty evidence grew with the "Big Ben" urethral-lengthening series (96% standing urination) and systematic reviews of sexual outcomes and pALT vs RFFF.[79][80][81] For non-gender-affirming reconstruction, the updated **ACS GU injuries guideline** addresses penile reconstruction and replantation, and an acellular collagen mesh advanced to first-in-man urethral reconstruction.[82][83]

## Male Sexual Dysfunction, Peyronie & Cosmetic Genital Surgery

The definitive **Cochrane review of LiSWT for ED** (21 RCTs, 1,357 men) rated evidence low-certainty, with a short-term IIEF gain (MD 3.89) that falls just below the MCID.[84][85]

A phase 2 RCT of **intracavernosal botulinum toxin** added to sildenafil was negative on intention-to-treat, and a PRP meta-analysis showed no consistent clinically meaningful benefit — reinforcing that restorative ED therapies remain investigational.[86][87] An EAU-commissioned systematic review of penile prosthesis complications (92,777 patients) quantified infection, erosion, and mechanical-failure rates.[88] In Peyronie disease, a "Guideline of Guidelines" compared AUA/EAU/CUA/ISSM, and hyaluronic-acid intralesional therapy was approved for the acute phase, alongside a translational PDE5i+SERM combination review.[89][90] Cosmetic genital surgery meta-analyses showed moderate FSFI improvement after labiaplasty with no clear technique superiority, against the backdrop of ACOG's continued caution on evidence quality.[91][92][93]

## Trauma & Emergencies

The **ACS Best Practices Guideline for GU Injuries** (Aug 2025) is the single most comprehensive new document, covering renal (NOM for all stable grades, embolization preferred), bladder, PFUI (SPT first; open realignment contraindicated), penile fracture, and testicular trauma.[82] The **AAST Kidney Organ Injury Scaling 2025 update** and a German S3 polytrauma guideline update (upgrading renal preservation and extraperitoneal bladder NOM to Grade B) further shaped practice.[94][95] A meta-analysis of **primary realignment vs delayed urethroplasty for PFUI** found no difference in stricture, ED, or incontinence.[96] Large registries (German TraumaRegister, GRAND, TQIP penetrating-renal-salvage) and the Israeli war-trauma analysis added epidemiologic and management data.[97][98][99][100]

## Cancer Survivorship, Women's Health & Lifelong Care

The **NeuroSAFE PROOF trial** (Lancet Oncology, 2025) showed frozen-section-guided nerve-sparing RARP significantly improved 12-month erectile function.[101] The **SPIRIT trial** (JAMA Internal Medicine, 2026) was the first RCT of testosterone replacement in hypogonadal prostate cancer survivors, improving sexual and physical function with no biochemical recurrence over the study window — directly challenging longstanding contraindications.[102] In bladder cancer, **IMvigor011** (NEJM, Dec 2025) established ctDNA-guided adjuvant atezolizumab, now category 1 in NCCN v3.2026.[103][104] Multiple systematic reviews quantified pelvic floor dysfunction after gynecologic cancer therapy, and updated **NCCN Survivorship** and gynecologic guidelines standardized sexual-health screening and management.[105][106][107][108] The **EMBRACE-I** analysis confirmed regular vaginal dilation reduces stenosis, and a colorectal survivor cohort (JAMA Netw Open) linked higher dietary fiber to fewer bowel symptoms.[109][110]

The **NEUROGED algorithm** is a practical decision tool worth having on hand for the lifelong neuro-urologic care population.
