# Focused renal/metabolic source corrections

This is a bounded correction of the renal-function/metabolic-surveillance page, not a full clinical review of every recommendation or reference on that page. The page's evidence note states that limit.

- Corrected reference 24 to McDougal WS, Koch MO. **Effect of sulfate on calcium and magnesium homeostasis following urinary diversion.** *Kidney Int.* 1989;35(1):105–115. DOI `10.1038/ki.1989.15`, PMID 2709657. The reference inventory found the same DOI under incompatible titles on two pages. The colon-conduit title was correct; the renal-surveillance title was wrong. The primary abstract describes patient mineral findings plus rat mechanistic experiments, not a clinical prevention trial. Removed the unsupported statement that long-term demineralization is “fully preventable” with alkali. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2709657/)
- Corrected the reversed creatinine mechanism. Returning urinary creatinine to the circulation does not explain a falsely lowered serum concentration. Bowel exchange can distort serum interpretation and timed urinary clearance, and the direction/magnitude are not a uniform correction factor. The 1985 canine ileal-interposition experiment demonstrated flow-dependent solute reabsorption; the 2005 study of 30 human ileal neobladders reported both resorptive and excretory patterns. Added those direct references and removed the assertion that a 24-hour collection necessarily resolves the uncertainty. [Koch and McDougal 1985](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3974007/), [Rinnab et al. 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15892819/)
- Corrected the automatic bicarbonate rule. KDIGO 2024 Practice Points 3.10.1–3.10.2 concern people with CKD and advise considering treatment of acidosis with potential clinical implications, with adult bicarbonate below 18 mmol/L as an example, while monitoring blood pressure, potassium, fluid state and overcorrection. This is not an automatic universal threshold of 22 mmol/L or a diversion-specific cutoff. Added a link to the agent-specific pharmacology companion. [KDIGO 2024, section 3.10](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/KDIGO-2024-CKD-Guideline.pdf)

The source checks used the PubMed primary-study abstracts and the official KDIGO guideline. They do not validate every historical surveillance interval, epidemiologic statistic, or other reference elsewhere on the page.
